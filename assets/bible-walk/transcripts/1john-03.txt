A little girl stands outside a big house, in the rain. She has no family, and no home. The door opens, and a man and a woman kneel down in front of her. We want you to come and live with us, they say. Not as a visitor. As our daughter. You can call us mum and dad. Everything we have is yours. John says God has done that for us, and even more.
In chapter two, John told us that when we sin, we have an advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the righteous. He told us to walk as Jesus walked, and to love our brothers and sisters. He warned us not to love the world, because the world passes away, but he that doeth the will of God abideth for ever.
Now, in chapter three, John is amazed at God’s love. Listen for what God calls us. And listen for what real love looks like.
John begins with a cry of wonder.
John goes on. 'therefore the world knoweth us not, because it knew him not.' If people do not understand us, it should not surprise us.
Then John looks ahead. 'Beloved, now are we the sons of God, and it doth not yet appear what we shall be: but we know that, when he shall appear, we shall be like him; for we shall see him as he is.' Right now, we are already God’s children. But one day it will be even better. When Jesus comes, we will see him face to face, and we will be changed to be like him.
And that hope changes how we live today. 'And every man that hath this hope in him purifieth himself, even as he is pure.' If you knew a very special guest was coming to your house, you would get ready. You would clean up. In the same way, if we are waiting to see Jesus, we want our lives to be clean.
Then John speaks about sin. 'for sin is the transgression of the law.' Sin is breaking God’s law, going against what he says. And Jesus came to deal with it. 'And ye know that he was manifested to take away our sins; and in him is no sin.' Manifested means he appeared, he came to us. And 'For this purpose the Son of God was manifested, that he might destroy the works of the devil.' Jesus came to undo all the damage that evil has done.
John then says something that can sound puzzling. Whoever is born of God does not commit sin, because God’s life is in him. But John has already told us, if we say we have no sin, we deceive ourselves. So what does he mean? He means that God’s children do not keep on living in sin, as if it does not matter. We still stumble. But we do not make our home in sin. God’s life inside us pulls us back towards what is right. Like a compass needle, we may be shaken, but we keep turning back to point north.
Then John comes back to love. 'For this is the message that ye heard from the beginning, that we should love one another.' Not like Cain, John says. Do you remember Cain, at the beginning of Genesis? He took his brother Abel’s life, because his own deeds were evil, and his brother’s were good. Cain was jealous of goodness.
So, John says, do not be surprised if the world hates you. 'We know that we have passed from death unto life, because we love the brethren.' One of the clearest signs that God has given us new life is that we love other believers. Whoever does not love is still living in death.
John says that hating a brother is like taking his life in your heart. Jesus taught the same thing in the Sermon on the Mount. Hatred is where it starts. God cares about our hearts, not only our hands.
So what does real love look like? John points us to the cross.
And John makes it very practical. Suppose someone has plenty, and sees a brother or sister in need, but closes his heart against them. How can God’s love be in him?
So John says.
Then John speaks to those who struggle with a guilty conscience. Even when our hearts accuse us, and we feel we are not good enough, John says, 'For if our heart condemn us, God is greater than our heart, and knoweth all things.' Our feelings are not the final judge. God knows everything, our failures and our faith, and he is greater than our fears.
And when our hearts are at peace with God, we can come to him with confidence, and ask him for what we need. 'And this is his commandment, That we should believe on the name of his Son Jesus Christ, and love one another'. Believe, and love. Those two words sum up the whole Christian life.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 1. 'Behold, what manner of love the Father hath bestowed upon us, that we should be called the sons of God: therefore the world knoweth us not, because it knew him not.' What manner of love means, what kind of love is this? Where did it come from? It is so amazing that John can hardly believe it. Bestowed means given as a gift. God did not just forgive us and leave us outside. He brought us into his family. Sons of God here means all God’s children, girls and boys alike. Whoever you are, if you belong to Jesus, God calls you his child.
Verse 16. 'Hereby perceive we the love of God, because he laid down his life for us: and we ought to lay down our lives for the brethren.' If you want to know what love is, look at Jesus on the cross. He laid down his life for us. And because he did, we should be ready to give ourselves for others. Most of us will never be asked to die for someone. But we can lay down our lives in smaller ways every day: our time, our comfort, our own way, for the good of others.
Verse 18. 'My little children, let us not love in word, neither in tongue; but in deed and in truth.' It is easy to say, I love you, or, I will pray for you. But real love does something. It shares a meal. It visits someone who is lonely. It gives to someone in need. Love is not just a feeling, or a word. It is an action. And it is real, in truth, not pretending.
Jesus said, 'For God so loved the world, that he gave his only begotten Son'. And he said, 'Greater love hath no man than this, that a man lay down his life for his friends.'
Remind yourself today that if you belong to Jesus, you are God’s child. Then look for one way to love someone in deed, not just in words. Is there someone in need you could help?
Here is today’s question. John says, behold, what manner of love the Father hath bestowed upon us. How does it change the way you see yourself, to know that God calls you his child?
Let us pray. Father God, what manner of love you have given me, that I should be called your child. Thank you that Jesus laid down his life for me. When my heart condemns me, remind me that you are greater than my heart. Help me to love others, not just in words, but in deed and in truth. Amen.
