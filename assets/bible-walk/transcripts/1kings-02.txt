An old man lies on his bed, too weak to stand. His son sits beside him, a young man with a crown that still feels heavy. The old man takes his hand, and speaks slowly. My son, I am going the way of all the earth. Be strong. Be a man. And keep the ways of the LORD. They are the last words a father gives his son.
In chapter one, king David was old. His son Adonijah declared, I will be king. But Nathan the prophet and Bathsheba reminded David of his promise. Solomon was anointed king at Gihon, and the people shouted, God save king Solomon. Adonijah ran to the altar, and Solomon let him go home in peace.
Now, in chapter two, David gives Solomon his last charge, and then he dies. Listen for what David tells his son matters most. And listen for how Solomon’s kingdom is made secure.
As the days of David come near to their end, he calls Solomon to him, and gives him his charge.
The charge goes on. Keep God’s laws, as Moses wrote them. Then, David says, the LORD will keep his promise to me, that if my children walk before him in truth, with all their heart and soul, there shall not fail a man on the throne of Israel.
Then David speaks about some unfinished business. He reminds Solomon what Joab did. In the second book of Samuel, we heard how Joab, the army commander, took the lives of two other commanders, Abner and Amasa, in a time of peace. David never punished him. Now he tells Solomon to act wisely.
But David also remembers kindness. 'But shew kindness unto the sons of Barzillai the Gileadite, and let them be of those that eat at thy table'. Barzillai was the old man who fed David and his men when they fled from Absalom. David never forgot it.
And he speaks of Shimei, the man who cursed David and threw stones at him as he fled from Absalom. David had promised not to harm him. But he tells Solomon not to treat him as innocent.
'So David slept with his fathers, and was buried in the city of David.' The shepherd boy, the giant killer, the sweet psalmist of Israel, is gone. 'And the days that David reigned over Israel were forty years'.
And Solomon takes his father’s place.
But Adonijah has not given up. He comes to Bathsheba, the king’s mother, with a request. You know the kingdom should have been mine, he says. But it has gone to my brother, for it was his from the LORD. Now I have one small request. Ask king Solomon to give me Abishag, the young woman who cared for king David, to be my wife.
Bathsheba agrees to ask. Solomon rises to meet his mother, bows to her, and sets a throne for her at his right hand. But when he hears the request, he understands at once what it really means. In those days, a man who took the old king’s household was claiming the old king’s throne. Why not ask for the kingdom too? Solomon says. Adonijah had been warned, and now he had shown that he still wanted the crown. So Solomon has him put to death.
Next, Solomon turns to Abiathar, the priest who had sided with Adonijah. Abiathar had carried the ark for David, and suffered with him, so Solomon spares his life. But he sends him home to his fields, and removes him from being priest. And the writer tells us why this matters. It happened 'that he might fulfil the word of the LORD, which he spake concerning the house of Eli in Shiloh.' Long before, in the first book of Samuel, God had told the boy Samuel that Eli’s family would lose the priesthood. Abiathar was from Eli’s family. God’s word had come true.
When Joab hears the news, he too runs to the tabernacle, and takes hold of the horns of the altar. But Joab had shed innocent blood, and the altar was never meant to protect a man guilty of that. So Solomon has Joab put to death, as justice for the lives he had taken. And Benaiah becomes commander of the army, and Zadok becomes priest.
Last of all, Solomon calls Shimei. Build a house in Jerusalem, he says, and live there. But if you ever leave and cross the brook Kidron, you will die. Shimei agrees. For a long time, he keeps his word. Then two of his servants run away to the city of Gath, and Shimei saddles his donkey and goes after them. When Solomon hears, he calls Shimei, and reminds him of his oath. Shimei had broken his promise, and he too is put to death.
These are hard events, and the Bible tells them plainly. Some were justice for real crimes. Others were about keeping the kingdom safe. The chapter ends, 'the kingdom was established in the hand of Solomon.'
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 2. 'I go the way of all the earth: be thou strong therefore, and shew thyself a man'. The way of all the earth means death. Every person on earth will one day walk that road. Knowing that, David tells his son to be strong. Not strong in muscles, but strong in character. A real man, in God’s eyes, is not the loudest or the toughest. It is the one who stands firm, does what is right, and walks with God.
Verse 3. 'And keep the charge of the LORD thy God, to walk in his ways, to keep his statutes, and his commandments, and his judgments, and his testimonies, as it is written in the law of Moses, that thou mayest prosper in all that thou doest, and whithersoever thou turnest thyself'. Of all the things David could have told Solomon, about wars, or money, or politics, the most important was this: walk in God’s ways. The best gift a parent can give a child is not wealth, but a life pointed towards God.
Verse 12. 'Then sat Solomon upon the throne of David his father; and his kingdom was established greatly.' Established means made firm and secure. God had promised David a son on his throne, and here he is. But notice, it is still David’s throne. Solomon sits on a throne that God promised to David’s family for ever. And many years later, an angel would tell Mary that her son, Jesus, would be given 'the throne of his father David'.
The LORD told Joshua, 'Only be thou strong and very courageous, that thou mayest observe to do according to all the law'. And Paul wrote, 'Watch ye, stand fast in the faith, quit you like men, be strong.'
Think of the most important thing you want to pass on to the people who come after you, your children, younger friends, or family. Is it walking with God? Ask him to help you show it, not only by your words, but by your life.
Here is today’s question. David’s last words to Solomon were, be strong, and walk in God’s ways. If you could leave one charge to someone you love, what would it be?
Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that your promises to David came true, and that his throne belongs for ever to Jesus. Make me strong, not in pride, but in faithfulness. Help me to walk in your ways, all my days, and to pass on a love for you to those who come after me. Amen.
