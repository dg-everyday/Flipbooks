Imagine a building site with no noise. No hammers banging. No saws screeching. No one shouting over the din. Huge stones arrive, already cut and shaped far away in the quarry. One by one, they are lifted into place, and they fit perfectly. Day after day, the walls rise, in a hush, as if the whole city is holding its breath. That is how the house of the LORD was built.
In chapter five, Solomon sent word to Hiram, king of Tyre. God had given him rest on every side, so now he would build a house for the name of the LORD, just as God had promised David. Great cedars from Lebanon came floating down the sea, and workers prepared great, costly stones for the foundation.
Now, in chapter six, the temple is built. Listen for its beauty. And listen for what God says matters even more than the building.
The chapter begins by telling us exactly when the work started. 'And it came to pass in the four hundred and eightieth year after the children of Israel were come out of the land of Egypt, in the fourth year of Solomon’s reign over Israel'. The writer connects the temple all the way back to the Exodus. The God who brought them out of Egypt was now coming to dwell in a house among them.
The temple was not huge by today’s standards. A cubit was about the length from a man’s elbow to the tip of his fingers. The main house was sixty cubits long, twenty cubits wide, and thirty cubits high. In front was a porch. It had narrow windows, set high in the walls. And all around the outside, against the walls, were rooms built in three storeys, for storage and for the priests.
And there was something special about how it was built.
While Solomon was building, the word of the LORD came to him. Concerning this house you are building, God says, 'if thou wilt walk in my statutes, and execute my judgments, and keep all my commandments to walk in them; then will I perform my word with thee, which I spake unto David thy father'.
And then God makes a promise.
So Solomon builds the house, and finishes it. Inside, the stone walls are lined with boards of cedar, from the floor to the ceiling, and the floor is covered with fir. The cedar is carved with gourds and open flowers. 'all was cedar; there was no stone seen.'
At the far end of the temple was a special room, called the oracle, or the most holy place.
The most holy place was a perfect cube, twenty cubits long, twenty wide, and twenty high. Solomon covered it with pure gold. In fact, he covered the whole house inside with gold, until everything shone. Even the altar by the most holy place was covered with gold. And there were chains of gold across the front of the most holy place.
Inside the most holy place, Solomon made two cherubims, carved from olive wood and covered with gold. Cherubims are mighty angels. Each was ten cubits high, and each had two wings, five cubits long. They stood side by side, with their wings spread out, so that one wing of each touched the side walls, and their other wings touched each other in the middle of the room. They would watch over the ark of the covenant.
All over the walls, inside and out, Solomon carved cherubims, palm trees and open flowers. The doors were made of olive wood and fir, and they too were carved with cherubims, palm trees and flowers, all covered with gold.
Think about that. Cherubims, trees and flowers. Do you remember the garden of Eden, at the start of Genesis? When Adam and Eve sinned, God placed cherubims at the entrance, to guard the way to the tree of life. Now, in the temple, there are cherubims and trees and flowers everywhere. It was like a picture of Eden, a garden where God and people could meet again.
And how long did it take? 'And in the eleventh year, in the month Bul, which is the eighth month, was the house finished throughout all the parts thereof, and according to all the fashion of it. So was he seven years in building it.'
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 7. 'And the house, when it was in building, was built of stone made ready before it was brought thither: so that there was neither hammer nor axe nor any tool of iron heard in the house, while it was in building.' Every stone was shaped at the quarry, so that it fitted exactly in its place. The building site was quiet, out of reverence for God. The New Testament says that God’s people are like living stones, being built into a spiritual house. God is shaping each of us now, sometimes in ways that hurt, so that we fit perfectly into the place he has for us.
Verse 13. 'And I will dwell among the children of Israel, and will not forsake my people Israel.' Here is the heart of it all. The gold and the cedar were beautiful. But the most wonderful thing about the temple was this promise. God would live among his people. And notice, God tells Solomon that what matters most is not the building, but walking in his ways. A beautiful building cannot take the place of an obedient heart.
Verse 19. 'And the oracle he prepared in the house within, to set there the ark of the covenant of the LORD.' The ark was the golden chest that held the tablets of the Ten Commandments. It was the sign of God’s presence. Only the high priest could enter the most holy place, and only once a year. When Jesus died on the cross, the Gospels tell us that the veil of the temple, the curtain in front of the most holy place, was torn in two, from top to bottom. Through Jesus, the way into God’s presence is open to all.
John wrote, 'And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us'. And Peter wrote, 'Ye also, as lively stones, are built up a spiritual house'.
Is God shaping you right now, through something hard? Ask him to help you trust the stonemason. And remember that through Jesus, you can come into God’s presence today, anywhere, at any time, in prayer.
Here is today’s question. God told Solomon that walking in his ways mattered more than the beautiful building. What matters more to you, how things look on the outside, or what is happening in your heart?
Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that you promised to dwell among your people, and that in Jesus you came to live among us. Shape me like a stone made ready, so that I fit where you want me. Thank you that the way into your presence is open. Help me to walk in your ways, from the heart. Amen.
