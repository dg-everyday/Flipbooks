Two roads lead out from a crossroads. One climbs up into the sunlight, through green fields and vineyards. The other winds down into a dark valley, where the houses are in ruins. A traveller stands at the crossroads, and someone he trusts points to each road in turn, and tells him exactly where each one leads. The choice is his. That is what God does for Solomon in this chapter.
In chapter eight, the ark was carried into the temple, and the cloud of God’s glory filled the house. Solomon spread out his hands to heaven and prayed, there is no God like thee. He asked God to hear and forgive, for his own people and for strangers from far away. And he blessed the LORD, because not one word of all his good promise had failed.
Now, in chapter nine, God answers Solomon’s prayer. Listen for God’s promise. And listen for God’s warning, which sets out the two roads for Solomon, and for all Israel.
The building work is finished. The temple, the palace, and everything Solomon wanted to build. And then the LORD appears to Solomon a second time, just as he had at Gibeon, when Solomon asked for wisdom.
God says, I have heard your prayer.
Then God sets out the first road. If you walk before me as David your father walked, with an honest heart, and keep my commandments, I will establish your throne over Israel for ever, as I promised David.
And then the second road. 'But if ye shall at all turn from following me, ye or your children, and will not keep my commandments and my statutes which I have set before you, but go and serve other gods, and worship them'. Then, God says, I will remove Israel from the land I gave them. And even this house, which I have made holy for my name, I will cast out of my sight. Israel will become a proverb, a byword, an example people talk about. The temple, so high and splendid now, will become a ruin, and people passing by will be shocked, and ask, why has the LORD done this to this land, and to this house?
And God gives the answer they will hear.
That is a sobering word, at the very moment when everything looks so bright. God is warning Solomon, because he loves him.
Then the chapter tells us about the rest of Solomon’s reign. Hiram, king of Tyre, had supplied Solomon with cedar, fir and gold. So Solomon gave Hiram twenty towns in the land of Galilee. But when Hiram came to see them, he was not pleased. What towns are these, my brother? he asked. And he called the area Cabul, which sounds like a word meaning, good for nothing. Even the friendship of kings can have its disappointments.
We also hear about Solomon’s great building works. Besides the temple and the palace, he rebuilt the wall of Jerusalem, and strong cities at Hazor, Megiddo and Gezer. Gezer had been captured by Pharaoh, and given to his daughter, Solomon’s wife, as a wedding gift. Solomon built store cities, and cities for his chariots and horsemen, all over his kingdom.
Who did all this work? Do you remember from Joshua and Judges, how Israel did not drive out all the peoples of the land? Their descendants were still living there. Solomon made them into a permanent workforce. The Israelites themselves were not made slaves. They served as soldiers, officials, and commanders. But we will see later that they too felt the weight of Solomon’s demands.
Pharaoh’s daughter moved into the house Solomon had built for her. And Solomon kept worshipping at the temple. 'And three times in a year did Solomon offer burnt offerings and peace offerings upon the altar which he built unto the LORD'.
At the end of the chapter, Solomon builds a fleet of ships at Ezion-geber, on the shore of the Red Sea. Hiram sends his experienced sailors to work with Solomon’s men. They sail to a faraway land called Ophir, and come back with a great load of gold.
So the chapter ends with gold, and ships, and great cities. But God’s words still echo. Two roads. Which one will Solomon choose?
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 3. 'And the LORD said unto him, I have heard thy prayer and thy supplication, that thou hast made before me: I have hallowed this house, which thou hast built, to put my name there for ever; and mine eyes and mine heart shall be there perpetually.' Hallowed means made holy. Perpetually means always. What a promise. God’s eyes, watching over his people. God’s heart, caring for them. God hears our prayers. And his eyes and heart are turned towards those who seek him, always.
Verse 4. 'And if thou wilt walk before me, as David thy father walked, in integrity of heart, and in uprightness, to do according to all that I have commanded thee, and wilt keep my statutes and my judgments'. Integrity of heart means a whole heart, an honest heart, not divided. David was not perfect. He sinned badly. But his heart belonged to God, and when he sinned, he came back. That is what God asked of Solomon. And it is what he asks of us. Not perfection, but a whole and honest heart.
Verse 9. 'And they shall answer, Because they forsook the LORD their God, who brought forth their fathers out of the land of Egypt, and have taken hold upon other gods, and have worshipped them, and served them: therefore hath the LORD brought upon them all this evil.' Forsook means abandoned, left behind. The temple would not be ruined because God was weak, or because enemies were too strong. It would happen because the people left the God who had rescued them. A beautiful building cannot protect a people whose hearts have turned away.
Moses said to Israel, 'I have set before you life and death, blessing and cursing: therefore choose life'. And Jesus said, 'Enter ye in at the strait gate: for wide is the gate, and broad is the way, that leadeth to destruction'.
God sets two roads before every one of us. Is there a choice in front of you today where you know which road leads towards God? Ask him for an honest and whole heart, to choose his way.
Here is today’s question. God told Solomon that his eyes and his heart would be on his house always. How does it change your day to know that God’s eyes and heart are turned towards you?
Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that you hear my prayers, and that your eyes and your heart are always turned towards your people. Give me integrity of heart, a whole and honest heart, like David’s. Keep me on the road that leads to you, and never let me forsake you, the God who rescued me. Amen.
