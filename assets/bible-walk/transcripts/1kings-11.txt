A great ship drifts on a calm sea. The captain does not notice that the ship is turning, very slowly. Just one degree at a time. There is no storm, no crash, no sudden alarm. But hours later, the ship is far off course, heading towards the rocks. Solomon’s heart turned away from God like that. Not all at once. Slowly, over many years.
In chapter ten, the queen of Sheba came to test Solomon with hard questions. She saw his wisdom, his house and his riches, and said, the half was not told me. And she blessed the LORD. But we also heard about Solomon’s many horses from Egypt, and silver as common as stones, just what God had warned Israel’s kings not to gather.
Now, in chapter eleven, we come to the saddest part of Solomon’s story. Listen for what turned his heart. And listen for God’s faithfulness to David, even in judgment.
The chapter begins with one word that changes everything. 'But king Solomon loved many strange women, together with the daughter of Pharaoh'. Strange here means foreign. Solomon married women from the Moabites, the Ammonites, the Edomites, the Sidonians and the Hittites.
These were the very nations God had warned about. He had told Israel, do not marry them, 'for surely they will turn away your heart after their gods'. But Solomon held on to them in love. He had hundreds of wives, many of them princesses from other kingdoms. Many of these marriages sealed alliances with other nations. And slowly, they did exactly what God had said they would.
The writer tells us how it happened.
Solomon began to follow Ashtoreth, the goddess of the Sidonians, and Milcom, the god of the Ammonites. 'And Solomon did evil in the sight of the LORD, and went not fully after the LORD, as did David his father.' On a hill just outside Jerusalem, within sight of the temple he had built for the LORD, Solomon built high places for Chemosh, the god of Moab, and Molech, the god of Ammon. And he did the same for all his foreign wives, so they could burn incense and sacrifice to their gods.
Think of it. The man who prayed, there is no God like thee, now building altars to idols.
'And the LORD was angry with Solomon, because his heart was turned from the LORD God of Israel, which had appeared unto him twice'. Twice. Once at Gibeon, and once after the temple was built. Solomon had heard God’s voice, more clearly than almost anyone. And still his heart turned away.
So God speaks to Solomon.
But even in judgment, God remembers his promise to David. It will not happen in your lifetime, God says, for David your father’s sake. It will happen in the days of your son. And I will not take away the whole kingdom. I will leave one tribe for your son, for David’s sake, and for Jerusalem’s sake, which I have chosen.
Then trouble begins. God raises up enemies against Solomon. Hadad, a prince of Edom, who had fled to Egypt as a child when David conquered Edom, comes back to fight. And Rezon, who becomes king in Damascus, in Syria, is an enemy of Israel for the rest of Solomon’s reign. The peace on every side is coming to an end.
And then there is Jeroboam. He was one of Solomon’s own officials, a capable young man, so hard working that Solomon put him in charge of all the workers from the tribes of Joseph.
One day, as Jeroboam is leaving Jerusalem, a prophet named Ahijah meets him on the road. Ahijah is wearing a brand new coat. The two of them are alone in a field. And Ahijah takes off his new coat, and tears it into twelve pieces. 'And he said to Jeroboam, Take thee ten pieces: for thus saith the LORD, the God of Israel, Behold, I will rend the kingdom out of the hand of Solomon, and will give ten tribes to thee'.
Twelve pieces, for the twelve tribes of Israel. Ten for Jeroboam. And one tribe kept for the family of David. Ahijah explains it is because the people have turned to other gods. And God makes Jeroboam a promise too. If you will listen to me, and walk in my ways, as David did, I will be with you, and build you a lasting house, as I did for David.
When Solomon hears about it, he tries to have Jeroboam put to death. So Jeroboam runs away to Egypt, and stays there until Solomon dies.
And then the end. 'And the time that Solomon reigned in Jerusalem over all Israel was forty years. And Solomon slept with his fathers, and was buried in the city of David his father: and Rehoboam his son reigned in his stead.'
The wisest man who ever lived, who had everything, who heard God speak twice, ended his life with his heart turned away. It is one of the most sobering stories in the whole Bible.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 4. 'For it came to pass, when Solomon was old, that his wives turned away his heart after other gods: and his heart was not perfect with the LORD his God, as was the heart of David his father.' When Solomon was old. He did not start badly. He began by loving the LORD. But over many years, little by little, his heart drifted. Perfect here means whole, fully given. It is not only young people who can turn away from God. We need to guard our hearts all the way to the end.
Verse 11. 'Wherefore the LORD said unto Solomon, Forasmuch as this is done of thee, and thou hast not kept my covenant and my statutes, which I have commanded thee, I will surely rend the kingdom from thee, and will give it to thy servant.' Rend means to tear. Forasmuch as means because. Sin has consequences. Solomon’s choices would tear his nation apart, and his family would suffer for it. God is patient and merciful, but he does not pretend that sin does not matter.
Verse 36. 'And unto his son will I give one tribe, that David my servant may have a light alway before me in Jerusalem, the city which I have chosen me to put my name there.' A light alway. God promised that David’s family would never go out like a lamp in the dark. Even when kings failed, God kept that light burning. And many years later, from David’s family in Bethlehem, came the one who said, I am the light of the world.
Solomon himself wrote, 'Keep thy heart with all diligence; for out of it are the issues of life.' And Paul wrote, 'Wherefore let him that thinketh he standeth take heed lest he fall.'
Is there anything slowly pulling your heart away from God? A friendship, a habit, a love of something good that is taking first place? Small turns matter. Bring it to God today, and ask him to turn your heart back to him.
Here is today’s question. Solomon’s heart turned away slowly, when he was old. What can you do to guard your heart, so that you stay close to God all your life?
Let us pray. Lord God, Solomon’s story makes me humble. If the wisest man could turn away, so could I. Keep my heart whole and true to you, all my days, to the very end. Show me anything that is slowly turning me away from you. And thank you that you kept a light burning for David, and that Jesus is the light of the world. Amen.
