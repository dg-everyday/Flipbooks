A crowd of tired, angry people stands before a new king. They have worked hard for years, building, carrying, paying. Now they have one request. Please, make our load lighter, and we will serve you gladly. The young king could win their hearts with one kind word. Instead, he chooses a cruel one. And in a single day, a kingdom splits in two.
In chapter eleven, Solomon’s heart was turned away by his many foreign wives, and he built altars to other gods. God said he would tear the kingdom from Solomon’s son, but would keep one tribe for David’s sake. The prophet Ahijah tore his new coat into twelve pieces, and gave ten to Jeroboam. Then Solomon died, and his son Rehoboam became king.
Now, in chapter twelve, the kingdom is torn apart. Listen for two kinds of advice. And listen for what Jeroboam does to keep the people away from Jerusalem.
Rehoboam goes to Shechem, where all Israel has gathered to make him king. Jeroboam hears the news in Egypt, and comes home. And Jeroboam and the people come to Rehoboam with a request. 'Thy father made our yoke grievous: now therefore make thou the grievous service of thy father, and his heavy yoke which he put upon us, lighter, and we will serve thee.'
A yoke is a wooden bar laid across the necks of oxen, so they can pull a heavy load. The people felt like oxen, worked too hard by Solomon’s building projects and taxes. Rehoboam tells them to come back in three days.
First, Rehoboam asks the old men, the wise counsellors who had served his father Solomon. What should I say to these people?
And they give him good advice.
But Rehoboam does not like it. So he turns to the young men he grew up with, his friends. And they tell him the opposite. Tell them you will be even tougher than your father. Make their yoke heavier.
On the third day, the people come back. 'And the king answered the people roughly, and forsook the old men’s counsel that they gave him'. He says, 'My father made your yoke heavy, and I will add to your yoke: my father also chastised you with whips, but I will chastise you with scorpions.' Scorpions here probably means whips with sharp barbs, far more painful.
The writer tells us that behind it all, God was at work, to fulfil what he had said through the prophet Ahijah. Rehoboam made his own foolish choice. But God was still in control of the story.
And the people answer. 'What portion have we in David? neither have we inheritance in the son of Jesse: to your tents, O Israel: now see to thine own house, David.' What do we have to do with David’s family? Go home, everyone! And they walk away.
Rehoboam sends Adoram, the man in charge of the forced labour, to talk to them. It is exactly the wrong man to send. The people attack him, and he dies. And Rehoboam jumps into his chariot, and flees to Jerusalem.
The ten northern tribes make Jeroboam their king. From now on, they are called Israel. Only the tribe of Judah, with the small tribe of Benjamin, stays loyal to David’s family. They are called Judah.
Rehoboam is furious. He gathers a huge army from Judah and Benjamin, to fight Israel and win back the kingdom. But God sends a prophet named Shemaiah, with a message.
And to their credit, they listen. They go home.
Meanwhile, Jeroboam, the new king of Israel, has a worry. He thinks to himself, if my people keep going up to Jerusalem to worship at the temple, their hearts will turn back to Rehoboam, and they will turn against me. Jeroboam was afraid. And fear made him do something terrible.
He sets up one golden calf at Bethel, in the south of his kingdom, and another at Dan, in the far north. 'And this thing became a sin: for the people went to worship before the one, even unto Dan.'
Jeroboam goes further. He builds shrines on high places, and makes priests from anyone he likes, not from the tribe of Levi, as God had commanded. He even makes up his own feast, a month later than the feast in Judah, 'even in the month which he had devised of his own heart'. And he stands at the altar in Bethel, and offers sacrifices himself.
God had promised Jeroboam a lasting house, if he would walk in his ways like David. But instead of trusting God, Jeroboam trusted his own plans. And for the rest of the book, the kings of Israel will be measured against him. Again and again we will hear, he walked in the way of Jeroboam, who made Israel to sin.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 7. 'And they spake unto him, saying, If thou wilt be a servant unto this people this day, and wilt serve them, and answer them, and speak good words to them, then they will be thy servants for ever.' What wise advice. Be a servant, and they will serve you. Speak kindly, and they will follow you. The old men understood that true leadership means serving others. Rehoboam thought leadership meant being harsh. He lost most of his kingdom because he would not speak a few good words.
Verse 24. 'Thus saith the LORD, Ye shall not go up, nor fight against your brethren the children of Israel: return every man to his house; for this thing is from me. They hearkened therefore to the word of the LORD, and returned to depart, according to the word of the LORD.' Your brethren. Even divided, they were still one family. And for once, Rehoboam listened. It takes courage to stand down, and humility to accept that God has allowed something we do not like. Sometimes the bravest thing is not to fight.
Verse 28. 'Whereupon the king took counsel, and made two calves of gold, and said unto them, It is too much for you to go up to Jerusalem: behold thy gods, O Israel, which brought thee up out of the land of Egypt.' Do you remember those words from Exodus? At Mount Sinai, Aaron made a golden calf, and the people said exactly the same thing. Jeroboam made worship easy and convenient. It is too much for you, he said. But easy worship that God has not asked for is not worship at all.
Jesus said, 'Take my yoke upon you, and learn of me; for I am meek and lowly in heart: and ye shall find rest unto your souls. For my yoke is easy, and my burden is light.' And Solomon wrote, 'A soft answer turneth away wrath: but grievous words stir up anger.'
Is there someone who needs a soft answer from you today, instead of a harsh one? Choose good words. And if you are a leader at home, at school or at work, ask God to help you lead by serving.
Here is today’s question. Rehoboam listened to the advice he wanted to hear, instead of the advice that was wise. Whose advice do you listen to, and how can you tell when it is wise?
Let us pray. Lord Jesus, thank you that your yoke is easy, and your burden is light. Make me a servant, like you. Give me soft answers and good words. Help me to listen to wise counsel, even when I do not like it. And keep me from making worship easy for myself, instead of worshipping you in the way you ask. Amen.
