A king stands at his new altar, ready to burn incense to his golden calf. The crowd is watching. Suddenly a stranger steps forward, a man from the south, and cries out, not to the king, but to the altar itself. O altar, altar! The king stretches out his arm and shouts, seize him! And his arm freezes, stiff and dry, and he cannot pull it back.
In chapter twelve, Rehoboam refused to lighten the people’s load, and spoke to them roughly. The ten northern tribes cried, to your tents, O Israel, and made Jeroboam their king. Then Jeroboam, afraid that his people would go back to Jerusalem, made two golden calves, and set them at Bethel and Dan, and said, behold thy gods, O Israel.
Now, in chapter thirteen, God sends a prophet to speak against Jeroboam’s altar. Listen for an astonishing prophecy. And listen for how the prophet himself is tested.
'And, behold, there came a man of God out of Judah by the word of the LORD unto Bethel: and Jeroboam stood by the altar to burn incense.' We are not told his name. He is simply called the man of God.
And he cries out against the altar.
Then he gives a sign that this word is from God. This altar will split apart, and the ashes on it will be poured out.
Jeroboam is furious. He stretches out his hand from the altar and shouts, lay hold on him! And his hand dries up, so that he cannot draw it back. At the same moment, the altar splits apart, and the ashes pour out, just as the man of God had said.
Now the king is frightened. 'Intreat now the face of the LORD thy God, and pray for me, that my hand may be restored me again.' And the man of God prays. And the king’s hand is healed, and becomes as it was before. Even for an enemy, the man of God prays, and God shows mercy.
Then Jeroboam says, come home with me, and I will give you a reward. But the man of God refuses. 'For so was it charged me by the word of the LORD, saying, Eat no bread, nor drink water, nor turn again by the same way that thou camest.' God did not want his prophet to share a meal in that place, as if all was well. So he goes home by another road.
Now, in Bethel, there lives an old prophet. When his sons tell him what happened, he rides after the man of God, and finds him resting under an oak tree. Come home with me and eat bread, he says.
Again, the man of God refuses. God told me not to.
But then the old prophet says, 'I am a prophet also as thou art; and an angel spake unto me by the word of the LORD, saying, Bring him back with thee into thine house, that he may eat bread and drink water.'
And then come four short, terrible words.
The man of God believes him, and goes back to eat in his house. And as they sit at the table, the word of the LORD really does come to the old prophet. He cries out, because you disobeyed the word of the LORD, you will not be buried in the tomb of your fathers.
The man of God sets off for home. And on the road, a lion meets him, and he dies. But the writer tells us something strange. The lion does not eat him, or attack the donkey. The lion and the donkey simply stand beside his body, by the road, until people pass by and see them. Everyone could see this was no accident. It was a sign from God.
The old prophet goes and finds the body, still guarded by the lion and the donkey, and buries it in his own tomb. And they mourn over him, saying, 'Alas, my brother!' And the old prophet tells his sons, when I die, bury me beside him. Because what he said against the altar at Bethel will surely come true.
This is a hard story, and Christians have understood it in different ways. Why was the old prophet’s lie not punished, while the man of God died? The Bible does not explain everything. But one lesson is clear. The man of God knew what God had told him. And he let someone else’s claim to speak for God talk him out of it.
And Jeroboam? Even after a dried hand, a split altar, and a lion on the road, he does not change.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 2. 'And he cried against the altar in the word of the LORD, and said, O altar, altar, thus saith the LORD; Behold, a child shall be born unto the house of David, Josiah by name; and upon thee shall he offer the priests of the high places that burn incense upon thee, and men’s bones shall be burnt upon thee.' This is amazing. The man of God names a king, Josiah, who would not be born for a very long time. And in the second book of Kings, we will hear that it happened, exactly as he said. God knows the future, and his word always comes true.
Verse 18. 'He said unto him, I am a prophet also as thou art; and an angel spake unto me by the word of the LORD, saying, Bring him back with thee into thine house, that he may eat bread and drink water. But he lied unto him.' Not every voice that says, God told me, is telling the truth. The old prophet sounded religious. He even mentioned an angel. But what he said went against what God had already clearly said. When anyone tells us something that goes against God’s Word, we must hold on to what God has said.
Verse 33. 'After this thing Jeroboam returned not from his evil way, but made again of the lowest of the people priests of the high places: whosoever would, he consecrated him, and he became one of the priests of the high places.' Jeroboam saw God’s power with his own eyes. His hand was dried up and healed. And still he did not turn back. Seeing miracles does not change a heart that does not want to change. Anyone who wanted to be a priest, he made a priest. He did whatever he pleased.
John wrote, 'Beloved, believe not every spirit, but try the spirits whether they are of God'. And Paul wrote, 'But though we, or an angel from heaven, preach any other gospel unto you than that which we have preached unto you, let him be accursed.'
Is there something God has clearly said in his Word that someone is trying to talk you out of? Hold on to what God has said. And when someone claims a word from God, test it against the Bible.
Here is today’s question. The man of God knew what God had told him, but he believed someone who claimed to have a newer message. How can you stay faithful to God’s Word when other voices tell you something different?
Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that your word always comes true, even the things you say long before they happen. Help me to know your Word, and to hold on to it. Give me wisdom to test every voice. And keep my heart soft, so that when you speak, I turn back to you, and do not harden my heart like Jeroboam. Amen.
