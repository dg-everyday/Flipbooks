A woman pulls a plain shawl over her head, and puts on old clothes. She does not want anyone to know who she is. She is the queen of Israel, but today she wants to look like an ordinary farmer’s wife. She carries a simple gift of bread and honey, and walks to the house of an old prophet who is blind. Surely he will never know. But before she even says a word, he calls her by name.
In chapter thirteen, a man of God from Judah cried against Jeroboam’s altar at Bethel, and named a king, Josiah, who would one day be born. The altar split, and Jeroboam’s hand dried up, and was healed. Then the man of God was deceived by an old prophet, and disobeyed the word of the LORD, and a lion met him on the road. And Jeroboam did not turn from his evil way.
Now, in chapter fourteen, trouble comes to both kingdoms. Listen for a mother who cannot hide from God. And listen for what happens to Solomon’s golden shields.
Jeroboam’s son, a boy named Abijah, becomes very sick. And Jeroboam thinks of the prophet Ahijah, who first told him he would be king. So he says to his wife, disguise yourself, so no one will know you are my wife, and go to Shiloh. Take ten loaves, some cakes and a jar of honey. Ahijah will tell you what will happen to the boy.
Why the disguise? Perhaps Jeroboam was ashamed. He had turned away from the God of Ahijah, and set up golden calves. But he still wanted God’s help when trouble came.
Ahijah is old now, and his eyes are blind. But God tells him, Jeroboam’s wife is coming, pretending to be someone else. And as soon as Ahijah hears her footsteps at the door, he says, 'Come in, thou wife of Jeroboam; why feignest thou thyself to be another? for I am sent to thee with heavy tidings.' Feignest means pretending. Heavy tidings means bad news.
And Ahijah gives her a message for Jeroboam. God says, I lifted you up from among the people, and made you ruler over my people Israel. I tore the kingdom away from David’s family and gave it to you. But you have not been like my servant David, who kept my commandments and followed me with all his heart.
And God says more.
Because of this, Ahijah says, disaster will come on Jeroboam’s family. It will be swept away completely. Then he tells her the hardest news of all. When you get home, as soon as you step into the city, the child will die.
But even in this sorrow, there is a gentle word.
And Ahijah looks further ahead. One day, he says, the LORD will shake Israel like a reed in the water, and uproot them from this good land, and scatter them far away, because of the sins of Jeroboam, who sinned, and made Israel to sin.
Jeroboam’s wife goes home. And just as she reaches the doorway of her house, the boy dies. And all Israel mourns for him, as the LORD had said. Later, Jeroboam himself dies, and his son Nadab becomes king.
Then the story turns south, to Judah. Rehoboam, Solomon’s son, is king in Jerusalem. And Judah does no better than Israel. They build high places and idols on every high hill and under every green tree. They do all the wicked things of the nations that God had driven out of the land.
And then, an invasion. Shishak, king of Egypt, comes up against Jerusalem. 'And he took away the treasures of the house of the LORD, and the treasures of the king’s house; he even took away all: and he took away all the shields of gold which Solomon had made.' Just a few years after Solomon’s death, the golden treasures of his reign are carried off to Egypt.
So what does Rehoboam do? He makes new shields, not of gold, but of bronze. And whenever he goes into the house of the LORD, the guards carry the bronze shields before him, as if nothing had changed. Then they put them back in the guardroom.
And the chapter ends, 'And there was war between Rehoboam and Jeroboam all their days.' Brothers, from one family, at war with each other.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 9. 'But hast done evil above all that were before thee: for thou hast gone and made thee other gods, and molten images, to provoke me to anger, and hast cast me behind thy back'. Cast me behind thy back. What a picture. God had given Jeroboam everything, and Jeroboam turned his back on him, and threw God behind him, like something he no longer wanted. But here is the wonderful thing. When we turn back to God, he does the opposite with our sins. He casts them behind his back, and remembers them no more.
Verse 13. 'And all Israel shall mourn for him, and bury him: for he only of Jeroboam shall come to the grave, because in him there is found some good thing toward the LORD God of Israel in the house of Jeroboam.' In the middle of all the darkness of Jeroboam’s family, there was one boy whose heart was turned towards God. God saw it. Even a little faith in a young heart is precious to God. Children are never too young for God to see the good in them.
Verse 27. 'And king Rehoboam made in their stead brasen shields, and committed them unto the hands of the chief of the guard, which kept the door of the king’s house.' Gold replaced by bronze. From a distance, perhaps, they still looked shiny. Rehoboam kept up appearances, as if nothing had been lost. But the glory was gone. We can do the same, keeping up the outward look of faith while our hearts have drifted. God is not fooled by bronze shields. He wants the real thing.
King Hezekiah prayed, 'for thou hast cast all my sins behind thy back.' And Jeremiah heard God say, 'For my people have committed two evils; they have forsaken me the fountain of living waters, and hewed them out cisterns, broken cisterns, that can hold no water.'
Is there any part of your life where you are pretending, wearing a disguise before God, or keeping up bronze shields? God sees and knows. Come to him honestly today. He welcomes those who come to him as they really are.
Here is today’s question. Jeroboam’s wife tried to hide who she was, but God knew her before she spoke. How does it feel to know that God sees you as you really are, and still invites you to come to him?
Let us pray. Lord God, you see me as I really am. I cannot hide from you, and I do not want to. Forgive me for the times I have turned my back on you. Thank you that when I turn to you, you cast my sins behind your back. Take away my bronze shields, and give me a real and honest faith. Amen.
