Two altars stand on a mountain top. On each lies a sacrifice, on a pile of wood. No one is allowed to light a fire. Hundreds of prophets dance around one altar, shouting to their god for hours. On the other side, one man stands alone, waiting. And the whole nation of Israel is watching, to see which God will answer.
In chapter seventeen, Elijah told king Ahab, there shall not be dew nor rain these years, but according to my word. God fed Elijah by the brook Cherith through ravens, and then through a poor widow in Zarephath, whose jar of flour and jug of oil did not run out. And when her son died, God heard Elijah’s prayer, and the boy lived again.
Now, in chapter eighteen, Elijah comes out of hiding. Listen for the question Elijah asks the people. And listen for what happens after the fire.
After a long time, in the third year of the drought, God tells Elijah, go and show yourself to Ahab, and I will send rain.
The famine in Samaria is terrible. Ahab calls Obadiah, the man in charge of his palace. And the writer tells us something about Obadiah. 'Now Obadiah feared the LORD greatly'. When Jezebel was trying to wipe out the prophets of the LORD, Obadiah hid a hundred of them in two caves, and fed them with bread and water. A faithful believer, working right inside the palace of the wicked king.
Out searching for grass for the horses, Obadiah meets Elijah. Go, Elijah says, and tell your master, Elijah is here. Obadiah is terrified that Ahab will put him to death if Elijah disappears again. But Elijah promises, I will show myself to him today.
So Ahab comes to meet Elijah. 'Art thou he that troubleth Israel?' Is that you, you troublemaker? But Elijah answers, 'I have not troubled Israel; but thou, and thy father’s house, in that ye have forsaken the commandments of the LORD, and thou hast followed Baalim.' Baalim means the Baals.
Then Elijah sets the challenge. Gather all Israel to Mount Carmel, with the prophets of Baal, and the prophets of Asherah who eat at Jezebel’s table.
So the people gather on Carmel. And Elijah steps forward and says to them, 'How long halt ye between two opinions? if the LORD be God, follow him: but if Baal, then follow him.' To halt means to limp, to hobble from one side to the other. The people say nothing.
So Elijah says, let us each take a young bull, and lay it on wood, but light no fire.
And all the people say, it is well spoken.
The prophets of Baal go first. They prepare their bull, and call on Baal 'from morning even until noon, saying, O Baal, hear us. But there was no voice, nor any that answered.' They leap around their altar.
And Elijah begins to tease them. Shout louder! He is a god, after all. Perhaps he is busy, or gone on a journey, or asleep and needs waking up!
So they shout louder, and even hurt themselves, as was their custom, hoping their god would notice. They keep it up for hours. 'that there was neither voice, nor any to answer, nor any that regarded.'
Then Elijah says to the people, come near to me. He repairs the broken altar of the LORD with twelve stones, one for each tribe of Israel. He digs a trench around it, lays the wood and the sacrifice on it, and then he says, fill four jars with water, and pour it over the sacrifice and the wood. Do it again. Do it a third time. The water runs down, and fills the trench.
Then Elijah prays a simple prayer. 'Hear me, O LORD, hear me, that this people may know that thou art the LORD God, and that thou hast turned their heart back again.'
And God answers.
'And when all the people saw it, they fell on their faces: and they said, The LORD, he is the God; the LORD, he is the God.'
Then Elijah has the prophets of Baal, who had led the whole nation away from God, put to death at the brook Kishon, as the law of Moses commanded for false prophets.
Then Elijah tells Ahab, go and eat, for there is a sound of a great rain. And Elijah climbs to the top of Carmel, and bows down to the ground, with his face between his knees, and prays. He tells his servant, go and look towards the sea. The servant comes back. There is nothing. Go again, Elijah says. Seven times.
And then, the seventh time.
The sky turns black, and a great rain falls. And the hand of the LORD is on Elijah, and he runs ahead of Ahab’s chariot, all the way to Jezreel.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 24. 'And call ye on the name of your gods, and I will call on the name of the LORD: and the God that answereth by fire, let him be God. And all the people answered and said, It is well spoken.' Baal was supposed to be the god of lightning and storms. If any god could send fire from the sky, it should be him. Elijah chose a test where Baal should have been strongest. A real God answers. A false god cannot. And the people agreed, this was the fair way to find out.
Verse 38. 'Then the fire of the LORD fell, and consumed the burnt sacrifice, and the wood, and the stones, and the dust, and licked up the water that was in the trench.' Not just the sacrifice. The wood, the stones, the dust, and even the water. Nothing could stand in the way of God’s fire. After hours of shouting with no answer, one short prayer, and God answered at once. Our God is not asleep, or busy, or far away. He hears.
Verse 44. 'And it came to pass at the seventh time, that he said, Behold, there ariseth a little cloud out of the sea, like a man’s hand. And he said, Go up, say unto Ahab, Prepare thy chariot, and get thee down, that the rain stop thee not.' Elijah prayed, and nothing happened. He kept praying. Six times, nothing. On the seventh, just a tiny cloud, the size of a man’s hand. But Elijah knew what it meant. Sometimes God’s answers begin very small. Keep praying, and watch for the little cloud.
James wrote, 'And he prayed again, and the heaven gave rain, and the earth brought forth her fruit.' And Joshua said, 'choose you this day whom ye will serve'.
Is there something you have been praying about for a long time, with no answer yet? Keep praying, like Elijah, and watch for the small signs of God at work. And is there any area of your life where you are limping between two opinions? Choose the LORD today.
Here is today’s question. Elijah asked, how long halt ye between two opinions? Where are you tempted to follow God halfway, and what would it look like to follow him fully?
Let us pray. Lord God, you are the God who answers by fire. You are not asleep, or busy, or far away. You hear when I pray. Forgive me for limping between two opinions. I choose you. Help me to keep praying, even when nothing seems to happen, and to watch for the little cloud. The LORD, he is the God. Amen.
