A man sits alone under a small tree in the desert. He is so tired he can hardly move. Only a few days ago, he saw fire fall from heaven, and rain end a long drought. Now he is running for his life, and he wants to give up. He lies down and falls asleep. And then someone touches him gently, and says, get up and eat.
In chapter eighteen, Elijah faced the prophets of Baal on Mount Carmel. Baal did not answer. But when Elijah prayed, the fire of the LORD fell, and the people cried, the LORD, he is the God. Then Elijah prayed seven times, until a little cloud like a man’s hand rose out of the sea, and a great rain came.
Now, in chapter nineteen, we see a very different Elijah. Listen for how God cares for his tired prophet. And listen for how God speaks to him.
Ahab goes home, and tells Jezebel everything Elijah has done, and what happened to the prophets of Baal. Jezebel is furious. She sends a messenger to Elijah, with a threat. May the gods punish me, if I do not take your life within a day.
And Elijah, who stood fearless before hundreds of false prophets, is suddenly afraid. He runs for his life, all the way south to Beer-sheba, at the edge of the desert. He leaves his servant there, and goes on alone, a whole day’s journey into the wilderness. He sits down under a juniper tree, a kind of desert bush. And he prays that he might die. 'It is enough; now, O LORD, take away my life; for I am not better than my fathers.'
This was a great man of God. And he was completely worn out, discouraged, and alone. The Bible is honest about that. Even strong believers can reach a point where they feel they cannot go on. If you ever feel like that, you are not the first. Tell God, as Elijah did. And tell someone you trust. God does not turn away from people who feel that way. Watch what he does for Elijah.
Elijah lies down and falls asleep under the tree. And an angel touches him, and says, 'Arise and eat.' Elijah looks, and there by his head is a cake of bread, baked on hot stones, and a jar of water. He eats and drinks, and lies down again.
God did not scold him. He did not give him a lecture. He let him sleep, and he gave him food.
And then the angel comes again.
So Elijah gets up, and eats and drinks, and 'went in the strength of that meat forty days and forty nights unto Horeb the mount of God.' Horeb is Mount Sinai, where God gave Moses the Ten Commandments.
There Elijah goes into a cave, and stays there. And the word of the LORD comes to him. 'What doest thou here, Elijah?'
And Elijah pours out his heart. I have been very zealous for the LORD. But the people of Israel have broken your covenant, torn down your altars, and taken the lives of your prophets. And I, only I, am left. And now they want my life too.
God says, go out, and stand on the mountain before the LORD. And the LORD passes by. First, a great and strong wind tears at the mountains, and breaks the rocks in pieces. But the LORD is not in the wind. After the wind, an earthquake. But the LORD is not in the earthquake. After the earthquake, a fire. But the LORD is not in the fire.
And then comes something else.
When Elijah hears it, he wraps his face in his cloak, and goes out, and stands at the mouth of the cave. And the voice asks again, 'What doest thou here, Elijah?' And Elijah gives the same answer. I only am left.
So God gives him work to do. Go back. Anoint Hazael to be king of Syria. Anoint Jehu to be king of Israel. And anoint Elisha, the son of Shaphat, to be prophet in your place. God was not finished with Elijah. And he was not finished with Israel.
And then God corrects Elijah, gently.
So Elijah leaves, and finds Elisha. Elisha is ploughing a field, with twelve pairs of oxen. He is driving the last pair himself. A wealthy farmer’s son. As Elijah passes, he throws his cloak, his mantle, over Elisha’s shoulders. Everyone knew what that meant. You are called to follow me.
Elisha leaves the oxen, and runs after Elijah. Let me kiss my father and mother goodbye, he says, and then I will follow you. And Elisha goes back, and takes his own pair of oxen, and uses the wood of the plough to cook their meat, and gives it to the people to eat. A farewell feast. He was not planning to come back to farming. Then he sets off after Elijah, and becomes his helper.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 7. 'And the angel of the LORD came again the second time, and touched him, and said, Arise and eat; because the journey is too great for thee.' The journey is too great for thee. God knows our limits. He does not expect us to be stronger than we are. When Elijah was exhausted, God gave him rest, food, and a gentle touch. Sometimes the most spiritual thing we can do is to eat, and sleep, and let God care for us. God cares for our bodies, not only our souls.
Verse 12. 'And after the earthquake a fire; but the LORD was not in the fire: and after the fire a still small voice.' After all the noise, a quiet whisper. Elijah had seen God in the fire on Carmel. But God wanted him to know that he also works quietly. Not always in great and dramatic ways, but often in a still small voice. To hear it, we need to be still, and listen.
Verse 18. 'Yet I have left me seven thousand in Israel, all the knees which have not bowed unto Baal, and every mouth which hath not kissed him.' Elijah thought he was the only one left. He was wrong. There were thousands of faithful people he did not know about. When we feel alone in following God, we are not. God always has his people, often hidden, quietly faithful. You are part of a much bigger family than you can see.
Jesus said, 'Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest.' And the psalmist wrote, 'Be still, and know that I am God'.
Are you tired, or discouraged, or feeling alone? Tell God about it honestly, as Elijah did. Take the rest you need. And find a quiet moment today to be still, turn off the noise, and listen for God.
Here is today’s question. Elijah thought he was the only faithful one left, but God had seven thousand. When have you felt alone in following God, and how does this story encourage you?
Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that you care for your tired servants, and that you know when the journey is too great for me. When I am worn out, give me rest. When I feel alone, remind me of your people. Help me to be still, and to hear your still small voice. And thank you that you are not finished with me yet. Amen.
