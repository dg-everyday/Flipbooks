A king lies on his bed in his palace, with his face turned to the wall. He will not eat. He will not talk. Is he sick? Is the kingdom in danger? No. He wanted his neighbour’s little vineyard, and his neighbour said no. The most powerful man in Israel is sulking like a child who cannot have a sweet. And his wife says, leave it to me.
In chapter twenty, the Syrians surrounded Samaria, and God gave Ahab victory, not once but twice, to show that he is the LORD of the hills and the valleys. But Ahab let the Syrian king go free for a trade deal. And when a prophet rebuked him, Ahab went home heavy and displeased.
Now, in chapter twenty one, we see what Ahab’s heart is really like. Listen for a man who stands firm. And listen for a surprising change in Ahab at the end.
Naboth lives in Jezreel. He owns a vineyard, right next to Ahab’s palace there. And Ahab wants it. Give me your vineyard, he says, so I can have a vegetable garden, because it is close to my house. I will give you a better vineyard for it, or, if you like, I will pay you what it is worth.
That sounds fair. But Naboth says no.
So Ahab goes home, heavy and displeased, just as he did at the end of the last chapter. He lies down on his bed, turns his face away, and will not eat.
Jezebel comes in. Why are you so sad? Why are you not eating? And Ahab tells her. Naboth will not give me his vineyard.
And Jezebel says, 'Dost thou now govern the kingdom of Israel? arise, and eat bread, and let thine heart be merry: I will give thee the vineyard of Naboth the Jezreelite.' Are you the king or not? Get up and eat. I will get you that vineyard.
Jezebel writes letters in Ahab’s name, and seals them with his royal seal. She sends them to the leaders of Naboth’s town. Call a special gathering, she writes, and seat Naboth in a place of honour. Then put two worthless men opposite him, and have them accuse him of cursing God and the king. Then take him out and put him to death.
And the leaders of the town do exactly what she says. Not one of them stands up for Naboth. The two false witnesses accuse him in front of everyone. Naboth is taken outside the city, and put to death. And they send word to Jezebel, Naboth is dead.
So Jezebel says to Ahab, get up, and take the vineyard. Naboth is not alive. He is dead. And Ahab gets up, and goes down to the vineyard, to take it for himself.
But someone was watching. 'And the word of the LORD came to Elijah the Tishbite'. Go down to meet Ahab. He is in Naboth’s vineyard, taking it for himself. And tell him, thus saith the LORD, have you taken a man’s life, and also taken his land?
So Elijah goes. And when Ahab sees him, standing in the stolen vineyard, he says something that shows his guilty conscience.
Then Elijah gives Ahab God’s judgment. Disaster will come on Ahab and on his whole family, just like the families of Jeroboam and Baasha. And Jezebel too will face God’s judgment, in Jezreel, the very place where Naboth died.
The writer pauses to sum up Ahab’s life. 'But there was none like unto Ahab, which did sell himself to work wickedness in the sight of the LORD, whom Jezebel his wife stirred up.' He followed idols, just like the nations God had driven out of the land.
And then, something unexpected happens. When Ahab hears Elijah’s words, he tears his clothes, and puts on sackcloth, rough cloth worn in grief. He goes without food, sleeps in sackcloth, and walks around quietly, humbled. For once, the proud king is broken.
And God notices.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 3. 'And Naboth said to Ahab, The LORD forbid it me, that I should give the inheritance of my fathers unto thee.' To us, it might seem that Naboth was just being stubborn. But in Israel, the land was God’s gift to each family, to be passed down from generation to generation. God’s law said it must not be sold away for ever. Naboth would not give up what God had given his family, even to a king. He chose to obey God rather than please a powerful man. And it cost him his life.
Verse 20. 'And Ahab said to Elijah, Hast thou found me, O mine enemy? And he answered, I have found thee: because thou hast sold thyself to work evil in the sight of the LORD.' Hast thou found me? Ahab thought he had got away with it. Jezebel had made it all look legal. But God saw everything. And Elijah’s words are striking: thou hast sold thyself. Ahab got a vineyard. But he sold his own soul to get it. That is always a terrible trade.
Verse 29. 'Seest thou how Ahab humbleth himself before me? because he humbleth himself before me, I will not bring the evil in his days: but in his son’s days will I bring the evil upon his house.' Even Ahab, the worst king Israel had known. When he humbled himself, God saw it, and showed mercy. God is far more willing to forgive than we imagine. No one is too far gone for God to notice when they turn to him.
Jesus said, 'Take heed, and beware of covetousness: for a man’s life consisteth not in the abundance of the things which he possesseth.' And Moses warned, 'be sure your sin will find you out.'
Is there something you want so badly that it is making you unhappy, or tempting you to do wrong? Bring it to God, and let it go. And if you know someone who is being treated unfairly, ask God for the courage to stand up for them, as no one did for Naboth.
Here is today’s question. Ahab got what he wanted, but sold himself to do evil. What things are you tempted to want so much that you might lose something more important?
Let us pray. Lord God, you see everything. Nothing is hidden from you. Keep me from wanting what belongs to others. Give me the courage of Naboth, to obey you rather than please powerful people. And thank you that when anyone humbles themselves before you, even someone like Ahab, you see it, and you show mercy. Amen.
