A woman weeps at the house of God, praying so hard that her lips move but no sound comes out. A boy wakes in the dark, hearing his name called. A tall young man goes looking for his father’s lost donkeys, and comes home a king. And a shepherd boy, the youngest in his family, is called in from the fields, and anointed with oil. The first book of Samuel is full of people whose lives are turned upside down by God.
Welcome to the first book of Samuel, on The Living Voice. First Samuel has thirty one chapters, so on this walk it is thirty one steps, one chapter at a time.
At the end of the book of Judges, we heard the words, in those days there was no king in Israel: every man did that which was right in his own eyes. The first book of Samuel tells how that changed. It tells how Israel moved from the time of the judges to the time of the kings.
First and second Samuel were once a single book, named after Samuel, the great prophet who anoints Israel’s first two kings. The book follows three men.
The first is Samuel himself. He is born in answer to his mother’s prayer, grows up at the house of God, and hears God speak to him as a boy. He becomes the last of the judges, and a prophet whose words never fall to the ground.
The second is Saul. The people ask for a king, like the nations around them, and God gives them Saul, tall and handsome and impressive. But Saul does not obey God from the heart, and his story becomes one of the saddest in the Bible.
The third is David. He is a shepherd boy, the youngest of his brothers, and God chooses him to be king. He defeats a giant, becomes a hero, and then spends years running for his life from Saul. Through it all, he learns to trust God, and refuses to take the throne by force.
Here are three things to watch for as we walk.
Watch for how God lifts up the lowly. Hannah, mocked because she has no children, sings that the LORD brings low, and lifts up. Again and again in this book, God turns things upside down. The proud fall, and the humble are raised.
Watch for the difference between doing religious things and truly obeying God. Saul offers sacrifices, and carries out half of what God asks. But Samuel tells him, to obey is better than sacrifice. God is not impressed by outward show.
And watch for the heart. When Samuel goes to anoint a new king, he looks at the tallest and strongest of the brothers. But God says, man looketh on the outward appearance, but the LORD looketh on the heart. That is the key to the whole book.
There are some hard chapters in this book too, with battles, jealousy, and a king who loses his way. We will tell them honestly and gently. And we will see that God is at work through all of it, preparing a king after his own heart. A king from Bethlehem, whose family line would one day lead to Jesus, the King of kings.
Here is a question to carry with you. God looks on the heart, not the outward appearance. What do you think God sees when he looks at your heart today?
Let us pray. Lord God, as I walk through the first book of Samuel, teach me to listen to your voice, as Samuel did. Give me a heart that obeys you, not just outwardly, but from within. Thank you that you lift up the lowly. Make my heart a heart after yours. Amen.
