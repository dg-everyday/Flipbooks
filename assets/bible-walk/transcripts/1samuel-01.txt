Every year, the family makes the same journey to the house of God. And every year, it is the same painful scene. One wife has children around her, laughing and eating. The other has none. And the one with children never lets her forget it. Year after year, until one day, the woman with no children can bear it no longer. She gets up from the table, and goes to pray.
In the introduction, we heard that the first book of Samuel tells how Israel moved from the time of the judges to the time of the kings. We heard about three men, Samuel, Saul and David, and we heard that God lifts up the lowly, and looks on the heart.
Now, in chapter one, the story begins not with a king or a battle, but with a woman praying. Listen for how Hannah prays. And listen for what she does when God answers.
There is a man from the hill country of Ephraim named Elkanah. He has two wives, Hannah and Peninnah. Peninnah has children, but Hannah has none.
Every year, Elkanah goes up from his town to worship and offer sacrifices to the LORD of hosts at Shiloh, where the tabernacle stands. The priests there are the two sons of Eli, Hophni and Phinehas. Remember those names.
When Elkanah offers his sacrifice, he gives portions of the meat to Peninnah and all her children. But to Hannah he gives a double portion, because he loves her.
But Peninnah, her rival, provokes her bitterly, to upset her, because Hannah has no children. Year after year, whenever they go up to the house of the LORD, Peninnah taunts her. And Hannah weeps, and will not eat.
Elkanah tries to comfort her. Hannah, why are you weeping? Why will you not eat? Why is your heart so sad? Am I not better to you than ten sons?
He means well. But he does not really understand. Sometimes the people who love us most cannot fix our deepest sorrow. Only God can.
So after they have eaten at Shiloh, Hannah gets up. Eli the priest is sitting on a seat by the doorpost of the house of the LORD. And Hannah, in bitterness of soul, prays to the LORD, and weeps and weeps.
And she makes a promise.
As she keeps on praying, Eli watches her mouth. Hannah is praying in her heart. Her lips are moving, but no sound comes out. And Eli thinks she has had too much wine. He tells her, put away your wine.
Imagine how that felt. Hannah is pouring out her heart to God, and the priest thinks she is behaving badly at the house of God.
But Hannah answers him gently. No, my lord, she says. 'I am a woman of a sorrowful spirit:' I have had no wine, but I have 'poured out my soul before the LORD.' Do not think I am a wicked woman. I have been speaking out of my great sorrow and grief.
Then Eli says, 'Go in peace: and the God of Israel grant thee thy petition that thou hast asked of him.'
And Hannah goes her way, and eats, and her face is no longer sad.
Notice that. Nothing has changed yet. She is not yet expecting a child. But she has poured out her heart to God, and left it with him. And she has peace.
The family rise early, and worship the LORD, and go home to Ramah. And the LORD remembers Hannah.
In time, Hannah has a son. She names him Samuel, saying, because I have asked him of the LORD. The name Samuel sounds like the Hebrew words for heard of God, or asked of God.
The next year, when Elkanah goes up to Shiloh, Hannah stays at home. I will not go up until the child is weaned, she says. Then I will bring him, so that he may appear before the LORD, and stay there for ever.
When Samuel is old enough, Hannah takes him up to Shiloh, with an offering of a bull, flour and wine. He is still very young. And she brings him to Eli.
Oh my lord, she says, I am the woman who stood here beside you, praying to the LORD.
Think of what this cost Hannah. She had waited and prayed for so long for this child. And now, with a full heart, she gives him back to God. She keeps her promise.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 11. 'And she vowed a vow, and said, O LORD of hosts, if thou wilt indeed look on the affliction of thine handmaid, and remember me, and not forget thine handmaid, but wilt give unto thine handmaid a man child, then I will give him unto the LORD all the days of his life, and there shall no razor come upon his head.' Look on me. Remember me. Do not forget me. Hannah felt overlooked and forgotten. But she brought that feeling straight to God. And notice what she promises. Not to keep the child for herself, but to give him back to God. Hannah wanted a son, but she wanted God even more.
Verse 27. 'For this child I prayed; and the LORD hath given me my petition which I asked of him:' For this child I prayed. Hannah stands with her little boy before Eli, and points to him as living proof that God answers prayer. Every time she looked at Samuel, she would remember that God had heard her. Our answered prayers become our testimony, the story we tell of God’s goodness.
Verse 28. 'Therefore also I have lent him to the LORD; as long as he liveth he shall be lent to the LORD. And he worshipped the LORD there.' 'I have lent him to the LORD' Hannah understood that her son was a gift from God, and belonged to God. She did not hold on to him tightly. Everything good we have, our children, our gifts, our lives, comes from God. The best thing we can do with his gifts is to give them back to him.
Paul wrote, 'Be careful for nothing; but in every thing by prayer and supplication with thanksgiving let your requests be made known unto God.' And Peter wrote, 'Casting all your care upon him; for he careth for you.'
Is there a sorrow you have been carrying, that others do not understand? Pour out your soul before the LORD today, as Hannah did. Tell him everything. And then leave it with him, and go your way in peace.
Here is today’s question. Hannah’s face was no longer sad, even before God answered her prayer. What do you think gave her that peace?
Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that you see those who feel overlooked and forgotten. Thank you that I can pour out my soul before you. I bring you my deepest longings now. And whatever you give me, help me to hold it with open hands, and to give it back to you. Amen.
