It is dark inside the house of God. The lamp is still burning, flickering low. An old priest is asleep in his room. And a boy is asleep near the ark of God. Then a voice calls his name. Samuel. The boy jumps up and runs to the old priest. Here I am, you called me. But the old man says, I did not call you. Go back to bed.
In chapter two, Hannah sang, there is none holy as the LORD, he bringeth low, and lifteth up. Eli’s sons, the priests, despised the LORD’s offering, and Eli did not stop them. But the child Samuel grew on, in favour with the LORD and with men. And a man of God told Eli, them that honour me I will honour.
Now, in chapter three, God speaks to Samuel for the first time. Listen for how many times God calls. And listen for what Samuel learns to say.
The boy Samuel serves the LORD under Eli.
In those days, God seldom spoke. There were few visions. The priests were wicked, and the people had drifted far from God.
Eli’s eyes have grown so dim that he can hardly see. He is lying down in his place. The lamp of God has not yet gone out in the house of the LORD, where the ark of God is. And Samuel is lying down to sleep.
The lamp of God has not yet gone out. That is true in more ways than one. Israel was in darkness, but God had not let the light go out completely. He was about to speak again.
Then the LORD calls Samuel. And Samuel answers, here I am. He runs to Eli and says, here I am, for you called me. And Eli says, I did not call. Lie down again. So he goes and lies down.
The LORD calls again, Samuel. And Samuel gets up and goes to Eli. Here I am, for you called me. And Eli says, I did not call, my son. Lie down again.
The writer explains. Samuel did not yet know the LORD. The word of the LORD had not yet been revealed to him. He had served in God’s house all his life. But he had never heard God speak.
Then the LORD calls Samuel a third time. And again he goes to Eli. Here I am, for you called me.
And now Eli understands. It is the LORD who is calling the boy.
So Eli says to Samuel, go and lie down. And if he calls you, say, speak, LORD, for thy servant heareth.
There is something moving about this. Eli had failed as a father, and his sons were wicked. But here, at the end of his life, he points the boy to God. He teaches Samuel how to listen. Even a failing leader can still help someone else to hear God.
So Samuel goes and lies down in his place.
Then the LORD tells Samuel something terrible. I am about to do something in Israel that will make the ears of everyone who hears it tingle. I will carry out against Eli everything I have spoken about his family. I told him I would judge his house, because his sons made themselves vile, and he did not stop them.
What a hard first message for a young boy to receive. Samuel lies there until it is light. Then he gets up and opens the doors of the house of the LORD, as he does every day. But he is afraid to tell Eli what God said.
Eli calls him. Samuel, my son. And Samuel says, here I am.
What did God say to you, Eli asks. Do not hide it from me. May God deal with you if you hide anything.
And Samuel tells him everything, and hides nothing.
And Eli answers, 'It is the LORD: let him do what seemeth him good.'
Eli accepts God’s judgment. He does not argue. He knows he deserves it. It is a sad moment, but an honest one.
Then the chapter moves forward through the years.
All Israel, from Dan in the north to Beer-sheba in the south, knows that Samuel is a true prophet of the LORD. And the LORD appears again at Shiloh, and reveals himself to Samuel through his word.
The chapter began with the word of the LORD rare and precious. It ends with God speaking again, through a boy who learned to listen.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 1. 'And the child Samuel ministered unto the LORD before Eli. And the word of the LORD was precious in those days; there was no open vision.' Precious here means rare. God was hardly speaking at all. Why? Perhaps because the people had stopped listening. God’s word was available in the law of Moses, but it was ignored. When God’s people stop listening to his word, his voice can seem very far away.
Verse 10. 'And the LORD came, and stood, and called as at other times, Samuel, Samuel. Then Samuel answered, Speak; for thy servant heareth.' Speak; for thy servant heareth. Samuel does not say, listen, Lord, for your servant is speaking. He says, speak, I am listening. So often our prayers are all talking. Samuel teaches us to stop, and be still, and listen to what God wants to say. And notice that God called him by name, twice. God knows each of us by name.
Verse 19. 'And Samuel grew, and the LORD was with him, and did let none of his words fall to the ground.' None of his words fell to the ground. That means everything Samuel said in God’s name came true. He did not add his own ideas, or soften God’s message. He spoke what God said, and God stood behind it. A person who listens carefully to God can speak with God’s authority.
Jesus said, 'My sheep hear my voice, and I know them, and they follow me'. And James wrote, 'let every man be swift to hear, slow to speak'.
Spend a few quiet minutes today simply listening to God. Read a short passage of the Bible slowly, and pray Samuel’s prayer: speak, LORD, for thy servant heareth. Then be still, and listen.
Here is today’s question. Samuel did not recognise God’s voice at first, and needed Eli to help him. Who has helped you to hear God, and how could you help someone else?
Let us pray. Speak, LORD, for thy servant heareth. Thank you that you know me by name, and that you still speak through your word. Forgive me for the times I have done all the talking. Teach me to be still and listen, and give me courage to obey what you say. Amen.
