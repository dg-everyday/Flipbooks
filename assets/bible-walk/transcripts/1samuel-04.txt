An old man sits on a seat by the side of the road, straining his blind eyes toward the battlefield. He is not worried most about the soldiers, or even about his own two sons who are fighting there. He is worried about a golden box. The ark of God. And then he hears a runner coming, and the whole town begins to cry out.
In chapter three, the LORD called Samuel in the dark, three times, until Eli understood and told him to say, speak, LORD, for thy servant heareth. God gave Samuel a hard message about Eli’s house. Samuel grew, and the LORD let none of his words fall to the ground. And all Israel knew that Samuel was a prophet of the LORD.
Now, in chapter four, the judgment God spoke of begins to come. Listen for why Israel takes the ark into battle. And listen for the name a mother gives her baby.
Israel goes out to battle against the Philistines. Israel camps at a place called Eben-ezer, and the Philistines camp at Aphek. When the battle begins, Israel is defeated, and many men fall in the field.
The army comes back to the camp. And the elders ask a question. And then they come up with an answer.
It sounds like faith. But it is not. They do not ask God what to do. They do not repent of their sin. They simply decide that if they bring the ark, God will have to fight for them. They are treating the ark like a lucky charm, a magic box that will win the battle.
So they send to Shiloh, and bring the ark of the covenant of the LORD of hosts. And Eli’s two sons, Hophni and Phinehas, come with it.
When the ark comes into the camp, all Israel gives a great shout, so loud that the ground shakes.
The Philistines hear the shout, and ask, what does this great shouting in the Hebrews’ camp mean? And they learn that the ark of the LORD has come.
And the Philistines are terrified. 'God is come into the camp.' Woe to us, they say. Who will save us from these mighty gods? These are the gods who struck the Egyptians with all the plagues in the wilderness.
The Philistines had heard the stories of the Exodus. They knew what the God of Israel had done. And yet they encourage each other. Be strong, and fight like men, or you will become slaves to the Hebrews, as they have been to you.
So the Philistines fight. And Israel is defeated, and every man runs home. It is a terrible defeat. The ark of God is captured. And Hophni and Phinehas, the two sons of Eli, both fall on that one day, just as the man of God had said.
A man from the tribe of Benjamin runs from the battle all the way to Shiloh, the same day, with his clothes torn and dust on his head, the signs of grief.
When he arrives, Eli is waiting.
The man comes into the town and tells the news, and the whole town cries out. Eli hears the noise and asks, what does this uproar mean? And the man hurries to tell him.
Eli is very old now, and blind. The man says, I have come from the battle. And Eli says, what happened, my son?
The messenger answers. Israel has fled before the Philistines. There has been a great defeat. Your two sons, Hophni and Phinehas, are dead. And the ark of God has been taken.
When he hears about the ark of God, Eli falls backward from his seat by the gate. He is old and heavy, and the fall takes his life. He had judged Israel for many years.
Notice what finally broke Eli’s heart. Not the defeat, and not even the news of his sons, terrible as that was. It was the ark of God. For all his failures, Eli still cared about the presence of God among his people.
There is one more sad scene. Eli’s daughter in law, the wife of Phinehas, is expecting a baby. When she hears that the ark is taken, and that her husband and father in law are dead, she goes into labour. And as she is dying, the women with her say, do not be afraid, you have given birth to a son. But she does not answer, or take any notice.
She names the boy Ichabod, which means no glory. Because, she says, the glory is departed from Israel.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 3. 'And when the people were come into the camp, the elders of Israel said, Wherefore hath the LORD smitten us to day before the Philistines? Let us fetch the ark of the covenant of the LORD out of Shiloh unto us, that, when it cometh among us, it may save us out of the hand of our enemies.' They asked a good question: why has the LORD let us be defeated? But they did not wait for God’s answer. And notice what they said: that it may save us. Not that he may save us. They trusted in the box, not in the God of the box. It is possible to trust religious things, and forget the living God.
Verse 13. 'And when he came, lo, Eli sat upon a seat by the wayside watching: for his heart trembled for the ark of God. And when the man came into the city, and told it, all the city cried out.' His heart trembled for the ark of God. Eli had been weak and had failed badly. But there was still something in him that loved God’s presence. Perhaps he knew that the ark should never have been taken into battle like that. God sees even the trembling hearts of people who have failed.
Verse 22. 'And she said, The glory is departed from Israel: for the ark of God is taken.' The glory of God was the visible sign of his presence with his people. Now, it seemed, God had left them. Not because the Philistines were stronger than God, but because Israel had treated him as something to use, rather than someone to obey. But this is not the end of the story. As we will see in the next chapter, God can take care of his own glory.
Jeremiah warned the people, 'Trust ye not in lying words, saying, The temple of the LORD, The temple of the LORD'. And a psalm says, 'Some trust in chariots, and some in horses: but we will remember the name of the LORD our God.'
Is there anything you treat like a lucky charm, a religious habit or object that you hope will protect you, while your heart is far from God? Turn to the living God himself today, not just the things that remind you of him.
Here is today’s question. Israel trusted the ark to save them, instead of trusting God. What is the difference between trusting God, and trusting religious things?
Let us pray. Lord God, you are not a lucky charm to be used. You are the living God, to be loved and obeyed. Forgive me for the times I have trusted religious things instead of you. Do not let your glory depart from my life. Draw me close to you, and let your presence be with me. Amen.
