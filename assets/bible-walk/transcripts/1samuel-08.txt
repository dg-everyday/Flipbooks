A group of old men walk into the house of an even older man. They have been talking it over for a long time. And now they say what is on their minds. You are getting old. Your sons are no good. We want a king, like everyone else has. It sounds reasonable. But behind their request is something they do not see.
In chapter seven, all Israel mourned after the LORD. Samuel said, return unto the LORD with all your hearts, and they put away their idols. At Mizpeh the Philistines attacked, but the LORD thundered against them. And Samuel set up a stone and called it Eben-ezer, saying, hitherto hath the LORD helped us. He judged Israel all the days of his life.
Now, in chapter eight, the people ask for a king. Listen for why they want one. And listen for what God says the request really means.
When Samuel is old, he makes his sons judges over Israel. Their names are Joel and Abiah, and they judge at Beer-sheba.
But his sons do not walk in his ways. They turn aside for money. They take bribes, and twist justice.
Does this sound familiar? Eli’s sons were wicked too. Samuel was a faithful man, but his sons did not follow his example. Faith is not automatically passed on, even in the best families.
So all the elders of Israel gather and come to Samuel at Ramah.
But it displeases Samuel when they say, give us a king to judge us. And Samuel prays to the LORD.
Notice what Samuel does when he is upset. He does not shout at the elders, or storm out. He takes it to God.
And the LORD answers him.
Then God says, they are doing to you what they have done to me ever since I brought them out of Egypt. They have forsaken me and served other gods. Now listen to them. But warn them solemnly. Tell them what a king will do.
So Samuel tells the people everything the LORD has said. And he describes what a king will be like.
He will take your sons, and make them run before his chariots, and serve as his horsemen. He will make some of them captains in his army, and others will plough his fields and reap his harvest, and make his weapons. He will take your daughters to be his cooks and bakers and perfume makers. He will take your best fields, vineyards and olive groves, and give them to his officials. He will take a tenth of your grain and your wine. He will take your servants, your best young men, and your donkeys, and put them to work for himself. He will take a tenth of your flocks. And you yourselves will become his servants.
Listen to the word that keeps coming. He will take. He will take. He will take. A king like the kings of the nations would be a taker.
And Samuel warns them. When that day comes, you will cry out because of the king you have chosen. And the LORD will not answer you then.
But the people refuse to listen. No, they say, we want a king over us.
There it is. The heart of the matter. Israel was meant to be different from the nations. They were God’s own people, with God as their King. But they wanted to be like everyone else. They wanted a king they could see, a hero to lead them into battle. They forgot that the LORD had fought their battles again and again, at the Red Sea, at Jericho, at Mizpeh only a short time before.
Samuel hears all that the people say, and repeats it to the LORD.
And the LORD says, listen to them, and give them a king.
Why would God agree? Sometimes, when people insist on their own way, God lets them have it, and lets them learn from it. And God was also able to work through this request. A king was part of his plan. Long before, God had told Abraham that kings would come from him. And Moses had given rules for a king who would follow God’s law. The problem was not having a king. The problem was why they wanted one: to be like the nations, and to stop depending on God.
Samuel sends the people home, every man to his own town. And the story of Israel’s first king is about to begin.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 5. 'And said unto him, Behold, thou art old, and thy sons walk not in thy ways: now make us a king to judge us like all the nations.' The elders had real concerns. Samuel was old. His sons were corrupt. Those were real problems. But instead of asking God to solve them, they decided for themselves what the solution should be: a king like all the nations. We can have real problems, and still choose the wrong answer, if we do not seek God first.
Verse 7. 'And the LORD said unto Samuel, Hearken unto the voice of the people in all that they say unto thee: for they have not rejected thee, but they have rejected me, that I should not reign over them.' Samuel took it personally. But God said, it is not you they are rejecting. It is me. Israel had a King all along, the LORD himself. Wanting a human king to replace him was turning away from God. And notice God’s kindness to Samuel. He comforts him. Do not take this as rejection of you.
Verse 20. 'That we also may be like all the nations; and that our king may judge us, and go out before us, and fight our battles.' That we also may be like all the nations. God had called Israel to be holy, set apart, different. But they wanted to fit in. And they wanted someone else to fight their battles, forgetting that God had always fought for them. The pressure to be like everyone else is just as strong today. But God still calls his people to be different.
Paul wrote, 'And be not conformed to this world: but be ye transformed by the renewing of your mind'. And Peter wrote, 'But ye are a chosen generation, a royal priesthood, an holy nation, a peculiar people'.
In what ways are you tempted to be like everyone else, instead of living as God’s person? Ask God today for courage to be different, and to let him be your King.
Here is today’s question. Israel wanted a king so they could be like all the nations. Why do you think the desire to fit in is so strong, and how can we resist it when it pulls us away from God?
Let us pray. Lord God, you are my King. Forgive me for the times I have wanted to be like everyone else, instead of being who you made me to be. Forgive me for seeking my own solutions instead of seeking you. Help me to let you reign over every part of my life, and to trust you to fight my battles. Amen.
