Every day, twice a day, for forty days, a giant walks out into the valley and shouts at Israel’s army. Send me a man to fight! And every day, the soldiers of Israel run and hide. Then one day, a shepherd boy arrives with bread and cheese for his brothers. He hears the giant’s words. And instead of running away, he asks, why is no one doing anything about this?
In chapter sixteen, Samuel went to Bethlehem, and looked at Jesse’s tall eldest son. But the LORD said, man looketh on the outward appearance, but the LORD looketh on the heart. The youngest, David, was called in from the sheep and anointed, and the Spirit of the LORD came upon him from that day forward. Then David came to play the harp for Saul.
Now, in chapter seventeen, comes one of the most famous stories in the world. Listen for why everyone else is afraid. And listen for what David sees that no one else sees.
The Philistines gather their armies for battle, on a hillside on one side of the valley of Elah. And Saul and the men of Israel camp on a hillside on the other side, with the valley between them.
And out of the Philistine camp comes a champion. His name is Goliath, from the city of Gath. He is about three metres tall, covered in heavy bronze armour, with a spear like a weaver’s beam, and a man carrying his shield before him.
Remember Gath? In Joshua, we heard that a few of the giants were left there. Now one of them stands in the valley.
Goliath shouts, choose a man to fight me. If he wins, we will be your servants. If I win, you will be ours. 'I defy the armies of Israel this day'.
When Saul and all Israel hear him, they are dismayed and terrified.
Saul was the tallest man in Israel. If anyone should have gone out to face the giant, it was the king. But Saul is as afraid as everyone else.
David is the youngest son of Jesse. His three eldest brothers have gone to fight with Saul. But David goes back and forth between Saul and his father’s sheep at Bethlehem.
For forty days, the giant comes out to make his challenge.
One day, Jesse sends David to the camp with grain, bread and cheese for his brothers. David arrives just as the armies are going out to their battle lines, and runs to greet his brothers.
As he is talking with them, out comes Goliath, shouting the same words. And David hears him. And all the men of Israel run from him, terrified.
The soldiers say the king will richly reward whoever defeats him.
David asks what will be done for the man who takes away this shame from Israel. Who is this Philistine, he asks, that he should defy the armies of the living God?
The armies of the living God. That is what David sees. Everyone else sees a giant. David sees a man insulting the living God.
David’s eldest brother Eliab hears him, and is angry. Why have you come down here? Who is looking after those few sheep in the wilderness? I know your pride, and the wickedness of your heart. You have only come to watch the battle!
And David says, 'What have I now done? Is there not a cause?'
David’s words are reported to Saul, and Saul sends for him.
Saul says, you cannot fight this Philistine. You are only a young man, and he has been a warrior since his youth.
But David tells Saul how, as a shepherd, he rescued lambs from a lion and a bear. 'The LORD that delivered me out of the paw of the lion, and out of the paw of the bear, he will deliver me out of the hand of this Philistine.'
And Saul says, go, and the LORD be with you.
Saul dresses David in his own armour and sword. He tries to walk, but he is not used to it. I cannot go in these, he says, because I have not tested them. And he takes them off.
David would not fight Saul’s way. He would fight the way God had trained him, on the hillsides with the sheep.
The giant comes closer, with his shield bearer in front of him. When he sees David, he looks down on him with scorn, a boy, ruddy and handsome. Am I a dog, that you come at me with sticks? And he curses David by his gods.
And David answers that he comes in the name of the LORD of hosts. And the battle is the LORD’s.
The giant moves in to attack, and David runs quickly toward the battle line to meet him.
The Philistine falls. David stands over him. And when the Philistines see that their champion has fallen, they turn and run. And the men of Israel and Judah rise up with a shout, and chase them.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 32. 'And David said to Saul, Let no man’s heart fail because of him; thy servant will go and fight with this Philistine.' Let no man’s heart fail. While everyone else was losing heart, David encouraged them. Courage is catching, just as fear is. One person who trusts God can lift the hearts of a whole army. And notice that David offers himself. He does not say, someone should do something. He says, I will go.
Verse 40. 'And he took his staff in his hand, and chose him five smooth stones out of the brook, and put them in a shepherd’s bag which he had, even in a scrip; and his sling was in his hand: and he drew near to the Philistine.' A shepherd’s staff, a sling, and five smooth stones. Ordinary tools, from an ordinary life. David did not need Saul’s armour. He used what God had already given him. God often uses the ordinary skills and experiences of our everyday lives to prepare us for the big moments.
Verse 49. 'And David put his hand in his bag, and took thence a stone, and slang it, and smote the Philistine in his forehead, that the stone sunk into his forehead; and he fell upon his face to the earth.' One stone. The giant who had terrified a whole army for forty days fell on his face before a shepherd boy. Not because David was strong, but because the battle was the LORD’s. Our giants may be fears, or problems, or temptations. But none of them is too big for God.
Paul wrote, 'Nay, in all these things we are more than conquerors through him that loved us.' And John wrote, 'greater is he that is in you, than he that is in the world.'
What giant are you facing right now? Name it before God today. Then remember what he has already brought you through, your lion and your bear. And trust him with this one too.
Here is today’s question. Everyone else saw a giant, but David saw a man defying the living God. How might seeing your problems in the light of who God is change the way you face them?
Let us pray. Lord God, you are the living God, and the battle is yours. Thank you for every lion and bear you have already brought me through. When I face giants, help me not to lose heart. Use the ordinary things you have given me, and give me courage to go out in your name. Amen.
