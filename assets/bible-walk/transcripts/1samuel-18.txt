Two young men stand facing each other. One is a prince, the son of the king, the man everyone expects to be the next ruler of Israel. The other is a shepherd boy who has just become a national hero. They should be rivals. Instead, the prince takes off his royal robe, and his sword, and his bow, and gives them to the shepherd. And they make a promise of friendship that will last the rest of their lives.
In chapter seventeen, Goliath of Gath defied the armies of Israel for forty days, and everyone was afraid. But David said, let no man’s heart fail because of him. He refused Saul’s armour, chose five smooth stones out of the brook, and went out in the name of the LORD. With one stone, the giant fell on his face.
Now, in chapter eighteen, David is a hero. Listen for the two very different ways people respond to him: love, and jealousy.
When David has finished speaking with Saul after the battle, something wonderful happens.
Saul takes David into his service from that day, and does not let him go home to his father’s house.
And Jonathan makes a covenant with David, because he loves him as his own soul. He takes off the robe he is wearing and gives it to David, along with his armour, and even his sword, his bow and his belt.
Think about what this means. Jonathan was the king’s son. By rights, he should be the next king. His robe and his sword were signs of that. By giving them to David, Jonathan was saying, I see that God is with you. I will not stand in your way. Jonathan put God’s plan above his own ambitions.
David goes out wherever Saul sends him, and does well. Saul puts him in charge of the soldiers, and everyone is pleased, even Saul’s own officers.
But then something happens that changes everything.
As the army comes home after David’s victory over the Philistine, the women come out of all the towns of Israel, singing and dancing to meet King Saul, with tambourines and joy and music. And as they dance, they sing to one another. Saul has defeated his thousands, and David his tens of thousands.
Saul is very angry. The song displeases him. They give David tens of thousands, he says, and me only thousands. What more can he have, but the kingdom?
Jealousy is a terrible thing. It turns a song of joy into a cause of anger. Saul could have rejoiced that God had given Israel such a victory. Instead, all he could think about was himself.
The next day, a troubling spirit from God comes upon Saul, and he raves in his house. David is playing the harp, as he usually does. And Saul has a spear in his hand. Saul hurls the spear, thinking, I will pin David to the wall. But David escapes from him, twice.
Saul is afraid of David, because the LORD is with David, and has left Saul. So he sends David away from him, and makes him a commander of a thousand men. And David leads the soldiers out to battle and back.
Saul thought that sending David into battle would cost him his life, or at least out of sight. But it only made the people love David more.
When Saul sees how successful David is, he is even more afraid of him. But all Israel and Judah love David, because he leads them out and brings them home.
Then Saul offers David his elder daughter Merab as his wife, if he will fight bravely in the LORD’s battles. But Saul is thinking, I will not lift my hand against him. Let the Philistines do it.
David says humbly, who am I, and what is my family, that I should be the king’s son in law? But when the time comes, Merab is given to someone else.
Then Saul’s younger daughter, Michal, falls in love with David. Saul is pleased, because he thinks she can be a trap for David. He sends his servants to tell David, the king delights in you, so become his son in law. David says, it is no small thing to be the king’s son in law, and I am a poor man, of no importance.
So Saul sets a dangerous price. Instead of a bride price of money, he asks David to defeat a hundred Philistines. He is hoping David will fall in battle. But David and his men go out and defeat twice that many. And Saul gives him Michal as his wife.
Saul sees clearly that the LORD is with David, and that his own daughter Michal loves him. And Saul becomes even more afraid of David, and is his enemy for the rest of his life.
And the chapter ends with David behaving more wisely than all of Saul’s officers, so that his name becomes greatly honoured.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 1. 'And it came to pass, when he had made an end of speaking unto Saul, that the soul of Jonathan was knit with the soul of David, and Jonathan loved him as his own soul.' Knit together, like two threads woven into one cloth. This is one of the greatest friendships in the Bible. Jonathan loved David as his own soul, wanting the best for him, even above himself. True friendship is not jealous. It rejoices when a friend is blessed.
Verse 9. 'And Saul eyed David from that day and forward.' Saul eyed David. He watched him with suspicion and jealousy. From one song, a seed of envy was planted, and it grew until it took over Saul’s whole life. Jealousy always starts small. That is why we must deal with it the moment we notice it, before it takes root.
Verse 14. 'And David behaved himself wisely in all his ways; and the LORD was with him.' Four times in this chapter, we hear that David behaved wisely. He did not fight back against Saul. He did not grab for power. He simply did his work well, and trusted God. And the LORD was with him. That was the secret. David did not need to push himself forward. God was with him.
James wrote, 'For where envying and strife is, there is confusion and every evil work.' And a proverb says, 'A friend loveth at all times'.
Is there someone whose success you find hard to celebrate? Ask God to take away any jealousy in your heart today. And think of a friend you could encourage, the way Jonathan encouraged David.
Here is today’s question. Jonathan loved David even though David might take his place as king, while Saul was consumed with jealousy. What do you think made the difference between them?
Let us pray. Lord God, thank you for the gift of true friends. Make me a friend like Jonathan, who rejoices when others are blessed. Take away any jealousy in my heart before it grows. Help me to behave wisely in all my ways, and to trust that you are with me. Amen.
