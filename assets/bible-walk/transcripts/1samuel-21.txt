A man walks alone into a small town of priests. He has no food, no weapons, no soldiers with him. Just days ago, he was a national hero, the king’s own son in law. Now he is a fugitive, running for his life. And the only sword he can find is the one that once belonged to the giant he defeated.
In chapter twenty, David and Jonathan made a plan, with arrows shot in a field. Saul raged at Jonathan and hurled a spear at him, and Jonathan knew his father meant to take David’s life. The two friends wept together, and Jonathan said, go in peace. The LORD be between me and thee, and between my seed and thy seed for ever.
Now, in chapter twenty one, David is on his own. Listen for how desperate he becomes. And listen for the man who is watching in the background.
David comes to the town of Nob, where the priests live, to Ahimelech the priest.
David tells him a story. The king has sent me on a secret mission, he says. No one is to know about it. I have told my men to meet me at a certain place.
It is not true. David is afraid, and he lies. The Bible records it honestly, without praising it. And as we will see in the next chapter, that lie would have terrible consequences.
David asks, what do you have here? Give me five loaves of bread, or whatever you can find.
The priest says, I have no ordinary bread. There is only the holy bread, the bread of the presence. This was the bread that was placed on a table in the holy place, before the LORD, and replaced with fresh bread each week. Only the priests were supposed to eat it. Ahimelech asks whether David’s men have kept themselves ceremonially clean, and David says they have.
Then the writer mentions a man who happens to be there that day. One of Saul’s servants, named Doeg, an Edomite, the chief of Saul’s shepherds. He is at Nob, detained before the LORD for some reason. And he sees everything.
Remember that name. Doeg the Edomite.
David asks, do you have a spear or a sword here? I did not bring my sword or my weapons, because the king’s business was urgent.
And the priest says, the sword of Goliath the Philistine, the one you defeated in the valley of Elah, is here, wrapped in a cloth behind the ephod. If you want it, take it. There is no other sword here.
And David says, 'There is none like that; give it me.'
The sword of Goliath. A reminder of the day God gave David his greatest victory. David takes it with him as he runs.
Then David runs again, and goes to Achish, the king of Gath.
Gath. Goliath’s home town. David goes into enemy territory, carrying the giant’s own sword. Perhaps he thought no one would recognise him. Perhaps he thought the Philistines would welcome an enemy of Saul. But it was a dangerous mistake.
The servants of Achish recognise him at once. Is this not David, the king of the land? Is this not the one they sang about in their dances, the one who defeated tens of thousands?
David hears what they say.
So David pretends to be mad. He scratches marks on the doors of the city gate, and lets saliva run down his beard.
And Achish says to his servants, look, the man is mad. Why have you brought him to me? Do I not have enough madmen already, that you bring this fellow to act like a madman in front of me? Is this man going to come into my house?
And so David escapes from Gath.
Think about how far David has fallen. A few chapters ago, he faced a giant with total confidence in God. Now he is lying to a priest, running into enemy territory, and pretending to be mad to save his life. Even the bravest believers have times of fear and weakness.
But this is not the end of the story. The heading of the thirty fourth psalm tells us that David wrote it when he pretended to be mad before the king. And in that psalm, he says, I sought the LORD, and he heard me, and delivered me from all my fears. In his weakest moment, David still cried out to God. And God heard him.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 1. 'Then came David to Nob to Ahimelech the priest: and Ahimelech was afraid at the meeting of David, and said unto him, Why art thou alone, and no man with thee?' Why art thou alone? David, who had led armies, now had no one. His friend Jonathan was gone. His wife Michal was gone. His mentor Samuel was far away. Sometimes following God leads us through lonely places. But we are never truly alone. David would later write, though I walk through the valley of the shadow of death, I will fear no evil: for thou art with me.
Verse 6. 'So the priest gave him hallowed bread: for there was no bread there but the shewbread, that was taken from before the LORD, to put hot bread in the day when it was taken away.' Hallowed means holy. The shewbread was the bread of God’s presence. Normally only priests could eat it. But Ahimelech gave it to a hungry man. Many years later, Jesus pointed to this story. He taught that God’s laws were given for people’s good, and that mercy to those in need matters to God.
Verse 12. 'And David laid up these words in his heart, and was sore afraid of Achish the king of Gath.' Sore afraid. The one who faced the giant was terrified. David was a real man, with real fears. The Bible does not hide the weaknesses of its heroes. That is a comfort to us. God does not only use people who are never afraid. He uses people who are afraid, and who bring their fear to him.
Jesus said, 'Have ye not read what David did, when he was an hungred, and they that were with him; How he entered into the house of God, and did eat the shewbread'. And David wrote, 'I sought the LORD, and he heard me, and delivered me from all my fears.'
Is fear driving you to make choices you know are not right? Stop, and bring that fear to God today. Pray the words of David: I sought the LORD, and he heard me, and delivered me from all my fears.
Here is today’s question. David went from facing Goliath with confidence to pretending to be mad out of fear. Why do you think even strong believers go through times of fear and weakness?
Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that you do not only use people who are never afraid. When I am afraid and alone, help me to seek you, and not to make foolish choices out of fear. Thank you that you hear me, and deliver me from all my fears. Amen.
