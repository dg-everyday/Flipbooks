Four hundred angry men are marching down a mountain path, swords strapped on, ready to take revenge. At their head is David, furious at an insult. And coming up the path toward them is a single woman on a donkey, with servants leading a long line of animals loaded with food. She is about to stand in the way of an army, armed with nothing but wisdom and courage.
In chapter twenty four, Saul walked alone into a cave where David was hiding. David’s men said, this is your chance. David cut off a corner of Saul’s robe, but his heart smote him, and he said, the LORD forbid that I should stretch forth mine hand against the LORD’s anointed. Saul wept and said, thou art more righteous than I.
Now, in chapter twenty five, David faces a different kind of test. Not a king, but a fool. Listen for how close David comes to doing something terrible. And listen for the woman God sends to stop him.
The chapter begins with sad news. Samuel dies. All Israel gathers to mourn him, and they bury him at his home in Ramah.
Samuel, the voice of God in Israel since he was a boy, is gone. And David goes down to the wilderness.
There is a very rich man named Nabal, with thousands of sheep and goats. His wife’s name is Abigail.
It is sheep shearing time, a time of feasting and plenty. And David sends ten young men to Nabal with a polite message. Peace to you, and your house, and all you have. While your shepherds were with us in the wilderness, we did not harm them, and nothing went missing. Ask your young men, and they will tell you. So please be kind to us, and give us whatever you can spare.
David’s men had protected Nabal’s flocks. It was fair to ask.
But Nabal answers rudely. Who is David? Who is the son of Jesse? There are plenty of servants running away from their masters these days. Why should I take my bread and water and meat, and give it to men I do not know?
When David hears this, he is furious. Every man, strap on your sword, he says. And he sets off with four hundred men, determined to destroy Nabal and all the men of his household.
David had been so patient with Saul. But this insult from a fool makes him lose his temper. Sometimes it is not the big trials that trip us up, but the small insults.
But one of Nabal’s servants runs to Abigail. David sent messengers to greet our master, and he insulted them. But these men were very good to us. They never harmed us, and nothing went missing. They were a wall around us all the time we were with them, looking after the sheep. Disaster is coming, and no one can talk to our master.
Abigail acts fast. She gathers two hundred loaves, wine, five sheep ready to cook, roasted grain, a hundred clusters of raisins, and two hundred cakes of figs, and loads them on donkeys. But she does not tell her husband.
On the mountain path, she meets David and his men.
When Abigail sees David, she quickly gets off her donkey, and bows to the ground at his feet. Let the blame be on me, my lord, she says. Please listen to me. Do not pay attention to this worthless man Nabal. His name means fool, and he is a fool. But I did not see the men you sent.
And Abigail speaks with remarkable wisdom. The LORD has kept you from shedding blood, and from taking revenge with your own hand. Please accept this gift for your men. And forgive me. For the LORD will certainly give you a lasting house, because you fight the LORD’s battles, and no evil has been found in you all your days.
Then she says something beautiful.
And Abigail reminds David of his future. When the LORD has made you ruler over Israel, you will not want this on your conscience, that you shed blood for no reason, or took revenge yourself.
David is stopped in his tracks.
David accepts her gift, and says, go home in peace. I have listened to you.
When Abigail gets home, Nabal is holding a great feast, like a king, merry and full of wine. So she tells him nothing until the next day. When the wine has worn off, she tells him what happened. And his heart fails him, and he becomes like a stone. About ten days later, the LORD strikes Nabal, and he dies.
When David hears, he says, blessed be the LORD, who has taken up my cause, and kept his servant from doing evil. God had dealt with Nabal. David did not need to.
Then David sends for Abigail, and asks her to be his wife. And she agrees, and goes with him.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 3. 'Now the name of the man was Nabal; and the name of his wife Abigail: and she was a woman of good understanding, and of a beautiful countenance: but the man was churlish and evil in his doings; and he was of the house of Caleb.' Churlish means rude and harsh. What a contrast. Abigail, wise and beautiful. Nabal, rude and evil. Remarkably, he was from the family of Caleb, the great man of faith. A godly ancestor does not make a godly person. Each of us must choose our own way.
Verse 29. 'Yet a man is risen to pursue thee, and to seek thy soul: but the soul of my lord shall be bound in the bundle of life with the LORD thy God; and the souls of thine enemies, them shall he sling out, as out of the middle of a sling.' Bound in the bundle of life. A bundle was where precious things were wrapped and kept safe. Abigail tells David that his life is wrapped up safely with God. And his enemies? God will sling them away, like a stone from a sling. Perhaps David remembered Goliath. God takes care of his own. David did not need to take revenge.
Verse 33. 'And blessed be thy advice, and blessed be thou, which hast kept me this day from coming to shed blood, and from avenging myself with mine own hand.' David was humble enough to listen. He was a great warrior, the future king, leading four hundred men. And he stopped, and listened to wise advice from a woman on a donkey. It is a mark of true greatness to be able to say, you are right, and I was wrong.
A proverb says, 'A soft answer turneth away wrath'. And James wrote, 'For the wrath of man worketh not the righteousness of God.'
Is there someone who needs a wise and gentle word from you, to keep them from doing something they will regret? Be an Abigail today. And if someone gives you wise advice, be humble enough to listen, as David did.
Here is today’s question. David was patient with Saul, but lost his temper with Nabal. Why do you think small insults can sometimes make us angrier than big trials?
Let us pray. Lord God, thank you for Abigail, and for all the wise people you send into my life. Keep me from taking revenge with my own hand. Give me humility to listen to good advice, and wisdom to give it gently to others. Thank you that my life is bound in the bundle of life with you. Amen.
