A king in disguise creeps through the dark to a small house in a village. He is looking for a woman whom he himself has banned from the land. He has tried everything else. He has prayed, and heard nothing. He has looked for dreams and prophets, and found silence. And now, terrified of the battle ahead, he has come to the one place God told his people never to go.
In chapter twenty seven, David said in his heart, I shall now perish one day by the hand of Saul. He fled to Achish, king of Gath, and lived among the Philistines in the town of Ziklag. He deceived Achish, and Achish trusted him, saying, he shall be my servant for ever.
Now, in chapter twenty eight, the Philistines gather for war. Listen for what happens when Saul seeks God. And listen for where Saul goes when God is silent. This is a strange and solemn chapter.
The Philistines gather their armies to fight Israel. And Achish says to David, you know, of course, that you and your men will go out with me to battle.
David’s lie has caught up with him. Now he is expected to fight against his own people. David gives a clever, unclear answer. Then you will see what your servant can do. And Achish says, then I will make you my bodyguard for life.
Then the writer reminds us of two things. Samuel is dead, and all Israel has mourned him and buried him in Ramah. And Saul had driven out of the land the mediums and those who claimed to speak with the dead. God’s law had forbidden these things, and Saul had obeyed.
The Philistines gather and camp at Shunem. Saul gathers all Israel and camps at Gilboa. And when Saul sees the Philistine army, he is terrified, and his heart trembles greatly.
Saul tries to ask God for help.
Saul had rejected God’s word again and again. He had disobeyed at Gilgal, disobeyed with Amalek, hunted David, and destroyed the priests of Nob. Now, in his hour of need, God is silent. It is a terrible thing to turn away from God for so long, that when we finally call, there seems to be no answer.
And so Saul says to his servants, find me a woman who is a medium, so that I can go and ask her. They tell him, there is one at Endor.
So Saul disguises himself in other clothes, and goes with two men, in the dark, to the woman. And he says, please call up for me the one I name.
The woman says, you know what Saul has done, how he has driven the mediums out of the land. Why are you setting a trap for my life?
And Saul swears to her by the LORD that she will not be punished. Saul swears by the very God whose law he is breaking.
The woman asks, whom shall I bring up for you? And Saul says, bring up Samuel.
When the woman sees Samuel, she cries out with a loud voice, terrified. She says, why have you deceived me? You are Saul!
The king says, do not be afraid. What do you see? She says, I see a spirit coming up out of the earth. He asks, what does he look like? She says, an old man is coming up, wrapped in a robe. And Saul knows it is Samuel, and bows with his face to the ground.
Christians have understood this scene in different ways. Some believe that God truly allowed Samuel to appear, one last time, to speak judgment to Saul. Others believe it was not really Samuel, but a deception. The Bible tells us what happened, and the message given was true.
Then Samuel speaks.
Then Samuel speaks again.
Samuel says that Saul did not obey the voice of the LORD, or carry out his judgment on Amalek. And that the LORD will give Israel into the hand of the Philistines, and that Saul and his sons will soon join Samuel among the dead.
Saul falls full length on the ground, terrified by Samuel’s words. There is no strength left in him. He has eaten nothing all day.
The woman comes to Saul, and sees how frightened he is. She says, I risked my life to do what you asked. Now please listen to me. Let me give you some food, so that you have strength for your journey. At last he agrees. The woman quickly prepares a calf and bakes bread, and sets it before Saul and his servants. They eat. And then they get up and go away into the dark.
What a sad picture. Israel’s first king, the tallest and most impressive man in the land, eating his last meal in the house of a medium, with no word from God but judgment.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 6. 'And when Saul enquired of the LORD, the LORD answered him not, neither by dreams nor by Urim, nor by prophets.' Urim were a means the priests used to seek God’s answer. Saul tried every way to hear from God, and heard nothing. This is the sad end of a life of disobedience. But we should not read this as a rule that God will never answer those who have strayed. Throughout the Bible, God welcomes back those who truly return to him. Saul wanted answers. But he never truly wanted God.
Verse 15. 'And Samuel said to Saul, Why hast thou disquieted me, to bring me up? And Saul answered, I am sore distressed; for the Philistines make war against me, and God is departed from me, and answereth me no more, neither by prophets, nor by dreams: therefore I have called thee, that thou mayest make known unto me what I shall do.' God is departed from me. Saul knew it. And instead of falling on his face before God in repentance, he went looking for another way to get answers. When God feels far away, the answer is not to turn to forbidden things, but to turn back to God with all our heart.
Verse 17. 'And the LORD hath done to him, as he spake by me: for the LORD hath rent the kingdom out of thine hand, and given it to thy neighbour, even to David:' The LORD hath done as he spake. Everything Samuel had said years before, at Gilgal, was coming true. God’s word does not fail. His promises to David would come true, and so would his warnings to Saul. What God says, he does.
Isaiah wrote, 'And when they shall say unto you, Seek unto them that have familiar spirits, and unto wizards that peep, and that mutter: should not a people seek unto their God?' And James wrote, 'Draw nigh to God, and he will draw nigh to you.'
When God seems silent, where do you turn? Do not look for answers in forbidden places. Draw near to God today, in honest prayer, and in his word. He has promised to draw near to you.
Here is today’s question. Saul sought answers from a medium when God was silent. What are some ways people today look for guidance outside of God, and why can that be dangerous?
Let us pray. Lord God, keep me from turning away from you, little by little, until your voice seems far away. When you seem silent, help me to draw near to you, and not to look for answers anywhere else. Thank you that you draw near to all who truly return to you. Amen.
