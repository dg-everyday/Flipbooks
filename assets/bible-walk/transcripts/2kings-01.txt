A king lies in bed, badly hurt. He fell through the wooden lattice of an upstairs window, and crashed to the ground below. Will he get better? He wants to know. So he sends messengers on a long journey, to ask a foreign god. But on the road, a strange figure steps out in front of them, wearing a rough hairy cloak and a leather belt.
In the introduction, we heard that second Kings begins with Elijah being taken up to heaven, and Elisha taking his place. It tells how Israel fell to Assyria, and later Judah fell to Babylon. And we heard three things to watch for: Elisha’s mercies, God’s word coming true, and God’s patience.
Now, in chapter one, we meet the son of Ahab. Listen for where he turns for help. And listen for Elijah’s last great stand.
After Ahab died, his son Ahaziah became king of Israel. And at the very start of the book, the land of Moab, which had paid tribute to Israel, rebels and breaks free.
Then Ahaziah has an accident. He falls through a lattice in his upper room in Samaria, and is badly injured. So he sends messengers. 'Go, enquire of Baal-zebub the god of Ekron whether I shall recover of this disease.' Ekron was a Philistine city. Baal-zebub means lord of the flies. In the Gospels, the enemies of Jesus would call the prince of demons Beelzebub, a name that comes from this god.
Think about that. The king of Israel, God’s own people, does not pray to the LORD. He does not send for a prophet of the LORD. He sends to a Philistine idol.
But God sees. And he sends the angel of the LORD to Elijah.
So Elijah goes and meets the messengers on the road. He gives them a message to take back. Because the king has asked a foreign god instead of the LORD, he will not get up from his bed. He will die.
The messengers turn around and go straight back to the king. Why have you come back? he asks. And they tell him. A man came up to meet us, and gave us this message.
What did this man look like? the king asks. 'He was an hairy man, and girt with a girdle of leather about his loins.' A hairy man probably means he wore a cloak of animal hair. Girt with a girdle of leather means he wore a leather belt. And the king knows at once. 'It is Elijah the Tishbite.' The same prophet who had stood against his father Ahab.
So the king sends a captain with fifty soldiers to arrest Elijah. They find him sitting on top of a hill. And the captain shouts up at him, man of God, the king says, come down!
Elijah answers, if I am a man of God, let fire come down from heaven and consume you and your fifty. And fire falls from heaven on them.
The king sends a second captain, with another fifty. This one is even ruder. Man of God, the king says, come down quickly! And again, fire falls from heaven.
These were soldiers sent to seize God’s prophet by force, and to mock the God he served. It was the king and his men against the LORD himself. And God showed who was really in charge.
Then the king sends a third captain, with a third fifty. But this captain is different. He goes up the hill, falls on his knees before Elijah, and begs.
He had seen what happened to the others. And he came humbly, asking for mercy.
And God answers.
Elijah goes to the king himself, and tells him to his face. Because you sent to ask Baal-zebub, the god of Ekron, as if there were no God in Israel to ask, you will not get up from that bed. You will surely die.
'So he died according to the word of the LORD which Elijah had spoken.' And because Ahaziah had no son, his brother Jehoram became king instead.
This is the last story of Elijah before he is taken up to heaven. And it shows the same thing his whole life showed. His name means, the LORD is my God. And through him, God kept asking his people the same question. Why do you turn to idols, when there is a God in Israel?
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 3. 'But the angel of the LORD said to Elijah the Tishbite, Arise, go up to meet the messengers of the king of Samaria, and say unto them, Is it not because there is not a God in Israel, that ye go to enquire of Baal-zebub the god of Ekron?' Is it because there is no God in Israel? Of course there was. The living God, who had sent fire on Carmel, and rain after the drought. But the king acted as if God were not there. When we are in trouble, where do we turn first? To God, or to anything and everything else?
Verse 13. 'And he sent again a captain of the third fifty with his fifty. And the third captain of fifty went up, and came and fell on his knees before Elijah, and besought him, and said unto him, O man of God, I pray thee, let my life, and the life of these fifty thy servants, be precious in thy sight.' Besought means begged. The first two captains came with pride and orders. The third came with humility and a plea for mercy. And he was spared. It is the same lesson we have seen again and again. God resists the proud, but gives grace to the humble.
Verse 15. 'And the angel of the LORD said unto Elijah, Go down with him: be not afraid of him. And he arose, and went down with him unto the king.' Be not afraid. Elijah was about to walk into the palace of a king who wanted him arrested. But God told him not to be afraid, and he went. Do you remember when Elijah ran from Jezebel in fear? Now he walks straight in to face the king. God had restored his courage. When God says, go, and be not afraid, we can go.
When some villagers would not welcome Jesus, James and John asked, 'Lord, wilt thou that we command fire to come down from heaven, and consume them, even as Elias did? But he turned, and rebuked them'. And Solomon wrote, 'Trust in the LORD with all thine heart; and lean not unto thine own understanding.'
When trouble comes today, where will you turn first? Make God your first call, not your last. And if you have been proud towards God, come to him humbly, like the third captain. He will have mercy.
Here is today’s question. Ahaziah acted as though there were no God in Israel to ask. When do you find yourself turning to other things for help before turning to God?
Let us pray. Lord God, you are the living God, and there is no one like you. Forgive me for the times I have turned to other things for help before turning to you. Make me humble, like the third captain. And when you send me, help me to go without fear, knowing that you are with me. Amen.
