Three armies are lost in the desert. They have been marching for a week, and the water is gone. The soldiers’ lips are cracked. The horses and cattle are stumbling. There is no well, no stream, not a cloud in the sky. One of the kings throws up his hands and says, the LORD has brought us out here to die! And then a prophet tells them to do something very strange. Start digging.
In chapter two, Elijah was taken up into heaven by a whirlwind, in a chariot of fire. Elisha asked for a double portion of his spirit, and picked up Elijah’s mantle. He struck the Jordan, and said, where is the LORD God of Elijah? And the waters parted. Then Elisha healed the waters of Jericho.
Now, in chapter three, Elisha’s help is needed by three kings. Listen for how God provides water. And listen for how God gives more than they asked.
Jehoram, another son of Ahab, is now king of Israel. He does evil in the sight of the LORD, but not as much as his father and mother. He takes down the image of Baal that Ahab had made. But he still holds on to the sins of Jeroboam, the golden calves.
Now, Mesha, the king of Moab, had been paying Israel a huge tribute every year, in lambs and in wool. But after Ahab died, he rebelled and stopped paying. So Jehoram decides to go to war. He asks Jehoshaphat, the good king of Judah, to come with him, just as Ahab once did. And Jehoshaphat says yes, my people are as your people, my horses as your horses.
They decide to go the long way round, through the wilderness of Edom, and the king of Edom joins them. But after marching for seven days, there is no water left, for the soldiers, or for their animals.
Jehoram panics. Alas! he cries. The LORD has called these three kings together, to hand us over to Moab!
But Jehoshaphat asks the right question.
Elisha poured water on Elijah’s hands. That means he had been Elijah’s servant. Jehoshaphat says, the word of the LORD is with him. So the three kings go down to see Elisha.
But Elisha is not impressed with Jehoram. What do I have to do with you? Go to the prophets of your father and mother! If it were not for Jehoshaphat, the king of Judah, I would not even look at you.
Then Elisha says, bring me a minstrel, a musician. And as the musician plays, the hand of the LORD comes on Elisha. Music can quiet our hearts, so that we are ready to listen to God.
And Elisha says, thus saith the LORD, make this valley full of ditches.
And there is more.
But God’s plan also included judgment on Moab. The Israelites would destroy its cities, cut down its good trees, block up its wells, and fill its good fields with stones.
So the soldiers dig. All over the dry valley, they dig ditches. Can you imagine? Thousands of thirsty men digging holes in the hot dry ground, with no rain in sight, just because God said so.
'And it came to pass in the morning, when the meat offering was offered, that, behold, there came water by the way of Edom, and the country was filled with water.' Rain had fallen far away, perhaps in the hills of Edom, and the water came rushing down into the valley, and filled every ditch.
Meanwhile, the Moabites have heard the kings are coming, and they gather every man who can carry a weapon, and wait at the border. Early in the day, they look across the valley. The sun is shining on the water in the ditches, and it looks red. The Moabites had never seen water in that valley. So they think, it is blood! The kings must have fought each other! Come on, Moab, let us go and take the spoil!
They run straight into the camp of Israel. But the Israelites are ready. They rise up and drive the Moabites back, and march into Moab itself. They break down the cities, fill the good fields with stones, block the wells, and cut down the good trees, just as Elisha had said.
The king of Moab, trapped in his last city, tries to break out with seven hundred swordsmen, and fails. Then, in his despair, he does a dreadful thing. He offers his own son as a sacrifice to his god, on the city wall. It was horrifying. And the armies of Israel and Judah turn away, and go home.
Moab’s gods could only demand terrible things. The LORD, who filled the valley with water, gives life.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 11. 'But Jehoshaphat said, Is there not here a prophet of the LORD, that we may enquire of the LORD by him? And one of the king of Israel’s servants answered and said, Here is Elisha the son of Shaphat, which poured water on the hands of Elijah.' Jehoshaphat should have asked first, before marching into the desert. But at least, when trouble came, he asked. And notice how Elisha is described. Not as a great prophet, but as a servant who poured water on his master’s hands. Elisha learned to lead by first learning to serve.
Verse 17. 'For thus saith the LORD, Ye shall not see wind, neither shall ye see rain; yet that valley shall be filled with water, that ye may drink, both ye, and your cattle, and your beasts.' No wind. No rain. And yet water. God does not need the usual ways to do what he promises. Sometimes he asks us to dig the ditches first, to prepare for a blessing we cannot yet see. Digging was an act of faith. Every ditch said, we believe God will fill this.
Verse 18. 'And this is but a light thing in the sight of the LORD: he will deliver the Moabites also into your hand.' A light thing means an easy thing. Filling a desert valley with water was easy for God. And he would do even more than they asked. They asked for water. God gave them water, and victory too. Nothing is too hard for him.
The psalmist wrote, 'There is a river, the streams whereof shall make glad the city of God'. And Paul wrote, 'Now unto him that is able to do exceeding abundantly above all that we ask or think'.
Is there a dry valley in your life, a place where you need God to work? Ask him what ditches you could dig. A step of faith, a prayer, an act of obedience, getting ready for the blessing you are trusting him for.
Here is today’s question. The soldiers dug ditches before they saw any water. What would it look like for you to prepare in faith for something you are trusting God to do?
Let us pray. Lord God, nothing is too hard for you. You can fill a dry valley without wind or rain. Forgive me for the times I have panicked instead of asking you first. Give me faith to dig the ditches, and to trust you for what I cannot yet see. Thank you that you are able to do far more than I ask or think. Amen.
