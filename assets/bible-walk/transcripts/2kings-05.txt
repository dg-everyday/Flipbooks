A powerful general rides up to a small house in his gleaming chariot, with his horses, his servants, and a fortune in silver and gold. He waits for the famous prophet to come out and bow, and wave his hand over him, and heal him. Instead, a servant opens the door, and gives him a message. Go and wash in the river seven times. Then the door closes. And the general is furious.
In chapter four, a widow’s little jar of oil filled jar after jar until there were no more. The woman of Shunem built a room for Elisha, and when her son died, she said, it is well, and God raised him to life through Elisha’s prayer. And twenty loaves fed a hundred men, with some left over.
Now, in chapter five, Elisha’s help reaches a foreigner, even an enemy. Listen for a young girl who speaks up. And listen for a great man who has to become small.
'Now Naaman, captain of the host of the king of Syria, was a great man with his master, and honourable, because by him the LORD had given deliverance unto Syria: he was also a mighty man in valour, but he was a leper.'
Naaman had everything. Fame, power, victories. But he had leprosy, a skin disease that could not be cured.
On one of their raids into Israel, the Syrians had captured a young girl, and she became a servant to Naaman’s wife. And this little girl says something remarkable.
Someone tells Naaman what the girl said, and he goes to his king. The king of Syria says, go, and I will send a letter to the king of Israel. So Naaman sets off with a huge gift, silver, gold, and fine clothes.
The letter asks the king of Israel to cure Naaman. The king tears his clothes in panic. Am I God? he says. He is trying to start a quarrel!
But Elisha hears about it, and sends a message to the king. Why have you torn your clothes? Let him come to me, 'and he shall know that there is a prophet in Israel.'
So Naaman comes, with his horses and chariots, and stops at the door of Elisha’s house. And Elisha does not even come out. He sends a messenger. 'Go and wash in the Jordan seven times, and thy flesh shall come again to thee, and thou shalt be clean.'
Naaman is furious. I thought he would surely come out to me, he says, and stand and call on the name of the LORD his God, and wave his hand over the place, and cure me. 'Are not Abana and Pharpar, rivers of Damascus, better than all the waters of Israel? may I not wash in them, and be clean?' And he turns, and storms off in a rage.
But then his servants come to him, and gently say something very wise.
Naaman stops. And he swallows his pride. He goes down to the Jordan, and dips himself in the river, once, twice, three times. Seven times, just as the man of God had said. 'and his flesh came again like unto the flesh of a little child, and he was clean.'
The great general came out of the water with skin like a little child’s. He had to become humble, like a child, to be healed.
Naaman goes back to Elisha, with all his company, and stands before him. And now his heart has been healed too.
He begs Elisha to accept a gift. But Elisha says, as the LORD lives, before whom I stand, I will take nothing. God’s healing could not be bought.
Then Naaman asks for two mules’ load of earth from Israel to take home, because from now on he will worship only the LORD. And Elisha says, 'Go in peace.'
But the story does not end there. Gehazi, Elisha’s servant, has been watching. My master let this Syrian go without taking anything, he thinks. I will run after him and get something.
So Gehazi runs after Naaman, and lies. My master has sent me, he says. Please give some silver and clothes for two young prophets. Naaman gladly gives him twice as much. Gehazi hides it in his house.
Elisha asks, where have you been, Gehazi? Nowhere, he says. And Elisha says, did not my heart go with you? Is this the time to take money? Naaman’s leprosy will cling to you. And Gehazi goes out from his presence, a leper, as white as snow.
Naaman, the foreigner, went home clean, believing in the LORD. Gehazi, the servant of the prophet, went home a leper, because of his greed and his lie.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 3. 'And she said unto her mistress, Would God my lord were with the prophet that is in Samaria! for he would recover him of his leprosy.' This girl had been taken from her home and her family, and made a servant in a foreign land. She could have been bitter. Instead, she cared about her master, and pointed him to God’s prophet. We do not even know her name. But because she spoke up, a great man was healed and came to know the true God. No one is too young or too small to point someone to God.
Verse 13. 'And his servants came near, and spake unto him, and said, My father, if the prophet had bid thee do some great thing, wouldest thou not have done it? how much rather then, when he saith to thee, Wash, and be clean?' If he had been asked to do something great and difficult, Naaman would have done it gladly. It was the simple thing that offended his pride. God’s way of salvation is like that. We cannot earn it by doing great things. We simply have to humble ourselves and receive it.
Verse 15. 'And he returned to the man of God, he and all his company, and came, and stood before him: and he said, Behold, now I know that there is no God in all earth, but in Israel: now therefore, I pray thee, take a blessing of thy servant.' Now I know. Naaman’s skin was healed, but even better, his heart was changed. He came looking for a cure. He went home knowing the living God. God’s greatest gift is not what he does for us, but knowing him.
Jesus said, 'And many lepers were in Israel in the time of Eliseus the prophet; and none of them was cleansed, saving Naaman the Syrian.' And David prayed, 'wash me, and I shall be whiter than snow.'
Is pride keeping you from doing a simple thing God is asking of you? Humble yourself, and obey. And like the servant girl, look for a chance today to point someone towards God.
Here is today’s question. Naaman almost missed his healing because the answer was too simple. Are there simple things God is asking you to do that you have been resisting?
Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that your healing cannot be bought, and that you offer it freely to all who humbly come. Take away my pride. Make me like a little child before you. Give me the courage of the servant girl to point others to you. And keep me from the greed and lies of Gehazi. Now I know that there is no God like you. Amen.
