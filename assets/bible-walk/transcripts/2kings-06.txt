A young man wakes up early, steps outside, and freezes. All around the town, as far as he can see, are enemy soldiers. Horses. Chariots. Spears glinting in the sun. They have come for his master. He runs back inside, shaking. Alas, my master! What shall we do? And his master looks at him calmly, and says, do not be afraid.
In chapter five, Naaman, the great Syrian general, was told to wash in the Jordan seven times. His pride nearly stopped him, but his servants persuaded him, and his skin became like a little child’s. Now I know, he said, that there is no God in all the earth but in Israel. But Gehazi lied to get Naaman’s silver, and went out a leper.
Now, in chapter six, we see more of Elisha’s miracles. Listen for what Elisha’s servant cannot see. And listen for how Elisha treats his enemies.
The chapter begins with a small, everyday problem. The prophets who live with Elisha say, our meeting place is too small. Let us go down to the Jordan, and each of us cut a beam, and build a new one. Elisha says, go. And one of them says, please come with us. So Elisha goes.
As one man is cutting down a tree, his iron axe head flies off the handle, and falls into the river. 'Alas, master! for it was borrowed.' Iron was very valuable then. He could never afford to pay it back.
Elisha asks, where did it fall? The man shows him. Elisha cuts a stick, and throws it into the water, 'and the iron did swim.' The heavy iron axe head floats up to the surface. Pick it up, Elisha says. And the man reaches out and takes it.
A small miracle, for a small problem. But it shows that God cares about the little things that worry us, even a borrowed axe.
Then the story turns to war. The king of Syria keeps planning secret ambushes against Israel. But every time, Elisha sends word to the king of Israel. Do not go near that place, the Syrians are hiding there. And again and again, the king of Israel escapes.
The king of Syria thinks there is a spy. But an officer says, it is Elisha, the prophet in Israel. He tells the king of Israel the very words you speak in your bedroom. So the king sends a great army, with horses and chariots, and they surround the city of Dothan, where Elisha is staying.
Early the next day, Elisha’s servant goes outside, and sees them. Horses and chariots everywhere. 'Alas, my master! how shall we do?'
And Elisha answers.
Then Elisha prays.
The Syrian army comes down towards Elisha. And Elisha prays again. LORD, strike these people with blindness. And God does. Then Elisha walks out to the blinded soldiers and says, this is not the right road, and this is not the right city. Follow me, and I will take you to the man you are looking for. And he leads them, all the way to Samaria, the capital city of Israel.
When they are inside the city, Elisha prays, LORD, open their eyes. And they look around, and find themselves surrounded by the army of Israel, in the middle of Samaria.
The king of Israel is excited. 'My father, shall I smite them? shall I smite them?' Shall I kill them?
But Elisha says something surprising.
So the king prepares a great feast for them. They eat and drink, and he sends them home to their master. 'So the bands of Syria came no more into the land of Israel.' Kindness did what swords could not. The raiders stopped coming.
But later, Ben-hadad, king of Syria, gathers his whole army, and surrounds Samaria. This time it is a long siege. No food can get into the city, and a terrible famine follows. Food becomes so scarce that even the worst scraps cost a fortune. People became so desperate that some did unthinkable things. The Bible does not hide how terrible it was.
As the king of Israel walks along the city wall, a woman cries out, help me, my lord, O king! And the king says bitterly, if the LORD does not help you, how can I? When he hears her story, he tears his clothes in grief. And the people see that underneath his royal robe, he is wearing sackcloth, rough cloth worn in mourning.
But instead of turning to God, the king blames God’s prophet. May God punish me, he says, if Elisha is still alive at the end of today! He sends a man to arrest Elisha. And the king’s messenger arrives, saying, this trouble is from the LORD. Why should I wait for the LORD any longer?
The chapter ends there, in the darkest moment. A starving city, an angry king, and a prophet in danger. But God has not finished. The next chapter will show what he is about to do.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 16. 'And he answered, Fear not: for they that be with us are more than they that be with them.' Elisha’s servant could count the enemy. He could see their horses and chariots. But he could not see what Elisha knew was there. Those who are with us are more. When we feel surrounded by trouble, and outnumbered, it is good to remember that God and his armies are on the side of his people. We are never as alone as we feel.
Verse 17. 'And Elisha prayed, and said, LORD, I pray thee, open his eyes, that he may see. And the LORD opened the eyes of the young man; and he saw: and, behold, the mountain was full of horses and chariots of fire round about Elisha.' God’s help had been there all along. The servant just could not see it. Sometimes what we need most is not for our situation to change, but for our eyes to be opened, to see that God is already there. This is a good prayer to pray for ourselves and others: LORD, open my eyes, that I may see.
Verse 22. 'And he answered, Thou shalt not smite them: wouldest thou smite those whom thou hast taken captive with thy sword and with thy bow? set bread and water before them, that they may eat and drink, and go to their master.' Elisha had the enemy army completely in his power. And he gave them dinner. He showed mercy instead of revenge. Many years later, Jesus told his followers, love your enemies, do good to them that hate you. And here, long before, Elisha did exactly that. And it brought peace.
The psalmist wrote, 'The angel of the LORD encampeth round about them that fear him, and delivereth them.' And Paul wrote, 'Therefore if thine enemy hunger, feed him'.
Is there something making you afraid, where you feel outnumbered? Pray Elisha’s prayer for yourself: LORD, open my eyes, that I may see. And is there someone who has treated you badly? Ask God how you could show them kindness instead.
Here is today’s question. Elisha’s servant could see the enemy, but not God’s army around them. What helps you to see God’s presence when you are afraid?
Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that those who are with us are more than those who are against us. When I am afraid, open my eyes, that I may see you at work. Thank you that you care even about small things, like a borrowed axe head. And give me a heart like Elisha’s, that shows mercy to enemies. Amen.
