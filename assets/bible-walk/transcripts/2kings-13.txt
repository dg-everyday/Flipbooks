An old man lies on his bed, very weak. A young king kneels beside him, weeping. Then the old man whispers, get a bow and some arrows. Open the window. Put your hands on the bow. And the old man reaches up, and puts his own frail hands over the king’s hands. Shoot! The arrow flies out of the window. And the old prophet says, that is the arrow of the LORD’s victory.
In chapter twelve, the boy king Joash of Judah did right all the days Jehoiada the priest instructed him. Jehoiada set a chest with a hole in its lid beside the altar, and the people gave to repair the house of the LORD, and the workmen dealt faithfully. But Joash later emptied the treasury for Hazael, and turned away after Jehoiada died.
Now, in chapter thirteen, we go back to the northern kingdom of Israel. Listen for God answering a king who did not deserve it. And listen for Elisha’s last words.
In Israel, Jehu’s son Jehoahaz is king. He does evil, and follows the sins of Jeroboam, the golden calves. So God allows Hazael, king of Syria, and then Hazael’s son Ben-hadad, to oppress Israel for years.
And then something surprising happens. 'And Jehoahaz besought the LORD, and the LORD hearkened unto him: for he saw the oppression of Israel, because the king of Syria oppressed them.' Besought means begged. Hearkened means listened. A king who did evil cried out to God. And God listened. Not because the king was good, but because God saw how his people were suffering.
So the LORD gives Israel a saviour, a deliverer, and they are freed from the Syrians, and live in peace in their homes again. And yet, sadly, they still do not turn away from Jeroboam’s sins. Even after God rescued them, they kept their idols.
By the end of Jehoahaz’s reign, Israel’s army has been reduced to almost nothing, only a handful of horsemen and chariots. Hazael had crushed them like dust on a threshing floor.
Then Jehoahaz dies, and his son Joash becomes king of Israel. This is a different Joash from the king of Judah. He too does evil in the sight of the LORD.
And then, Elisha falls ill. He has been a prophet in Israel for many, many years, through the reigns of several kings. Now he is old, and dying.
King Joash comes down to see him, and weeps over him. 'O my father, my father, the chariot of Israel, and the horsemen thereof.' Those were the very words Elisha had cried out when Elijah was taken up to heaven. Now the king says them over Elisha. The prophet was worth more to Israel than all its army.
And then Elisha gives the king a strange lesson.
Then Elisha says, take the arrows, and strike the ground with them. The king picks up the arrows, and strikes the ground. Once. Twice. Three times. And then he stops.
And Elisha is angry.
Then Elisha dies, and they bury him.
But even after his death, God has one more surprise. Raiders from Moab used to come into the land every spring. One day, some Israelites are burying a man, when they see a band of raiders coming. In a panic, they throw the man’s body into the nearest tomb, which happens to be Elisha’s. 'and when the man was let down, and touched the bones of Elisha, he revived, and stood up on his feet.'
Even in death, Elisha’s story points to life. God, not Elisha, was the one who gave life. And many years later, from another tomb, someone would rise again, not for a while, but for ever.
The chapter ends with good news for Israel, though they did not deserve it.
Hazael, king of Syria, dies, and his son Ben-hadad becomes king. And Joash of Israel defeats Ben-hadad three times, and wins back the cities that Syria had taken. Three times. Just as many times as he struck the ground.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 17. 'And he said, Open the window eastward. And he opened it. Then Elisha said, Shoot. And he shot. And he said, The arrow of the LORD’S deliverance, and the arrow of deliverance from Syria: for thou shalt smite the Syrians in Aphek, till thou have consumed them.' Notice Elisha’s hands over the king’s hands. The king held the bow, but the strength and the victory came from God. That is how God works with us. We do our part, but his hands are over ours. Every victory we have is really the LORD’s deliverance.
Verse 19. 'And the man of God was wroth with him, and said, Thou shouldest have smitten five or six times; then hadst thou smitten Syria till thou hadst consumed it: whereas now thou shalt smite Syria but thrice.' Wroth means angry. The king struck the ground half-heartedly, and stopped too soon. He did not really expect much. And so he received only part of what God was ready to give. Sometimes we receive little from God because we expect little, and ask for little. Do not stop at three. Keep on believing, and keep on asking.
Verse 23. 'And the LORD was gracious unto them, and had compassion on them, and had respect unto them, because of his covenant with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, and would not destroy them, neither cast he them from his presence as yet.' Gracious. Compassion. Israel had done nothing to deserve this. They were still worshipping golden calves. But God remembered his promises to Abraham, Isaac and Jacob, long, long before. God’s faithfulness does not depend on ours. But notice the words, as yet. God’s patience is great, but it is not endless.
Jeremiah wrote, 'It is of the LORD’S mercies that we are not consumed, because his compassions fail not. They are new every morning: great is thy faithfulness.' And Paul wrote, 'And let us not be weary in well doing: for in due season we shall reap, if we faint not.'
Is there something you have stopped praying for, after just a few tries? Pick up the arrows again. Keep asking, keep trusting. And thank God that his compassion does not depend on how good you have been, but on his promises.
Here is today’s question. The king struck the ground three times and stopped. In what area of your life might you be expecting too little from God?
Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that you are gracious and full of compassion, and that you remember your promises. Forgive me for the times I have expected too little from you, and stopped too soon. Put your hands over mine, and give me faith to keep on asking. Every victory is yours. Amen.
