A man in fine armour stands outside the walls of Jerusalem, shouting up at the people on top of the wall. Behind him stands a vast army. He shouts in their own language, so that everyone can understand. Do not let your king fool you! Has any god of any nation ever saved its people from the king of Assyria? Where are they now? And on the wall, the people stand in silence. Their king has told them, do not answer him.
In chapter seventeen, the northern kingdom of Israel fell to Assyria, and its people were carried away. The writer explained why: they would not listen to the prophets, they hardened their necks, and they served idols. New peoples were brought into Samaria, who feared the LORD, and served their own gods.
Now, in chapter eighteen, we meet one of the greatest kings of Judah. Listen for what made Hezekiah different. And listen for the question the Assyrians shout at Jerusalem.
Hezekiah, the son of Ahaz, becomes king of Judah. His father had been one of the worst kings. But Hezekiah is one of the best. 'And he did that which was right in the sight of the LORD, according to all that David his father did.'
And Hezekiah does what no king before him had done.
The writer tells us what made Hezekiah special.
Hezekiah held on to the LORD, and kept his commandments. 'And the LORD was with him; and he prospered whithersoever he went forth'. He stopped paying tribute to the king of Assyria. And he defeated the Philistines.
Then the writer reminds us of what had happened in the north. In Hezekiah’s early years, Assyria besieged Samaria, and took it, and carried Israel away, 'Because they obeyed not the voice of the LORD their God'. Hezekiah had seen with his own eyes what happens to a people who turn from God.
And then the Assyrians come for Judah. Sennacherib, the king of Assyria, marches in, and captures all the fortified cities of Judah, one after another. Only Jerusalem is left.
For a moment, Hezekiah’s courage fails. He sends a message to Sennacherib: I have done wrong. Go away, and I will pay whatever you ask. Sennacherib demands a huge amount of silver and gold. Hezekiah gives him all the silver in the temple and the palace. He even strips the gold from the doors and doorposts of the temple, which he himself had covered, and sends it to the king of Assyria.
But it is not enough. Sennacherib sends three of his top officials, with a great army, to Jerusalem. One of them is called the Rabshakeh, a title meaning chief officer. They stand by the water channel outside the city, and call for king Hezekiah. Three of Hezekiah’s officials go out to meet them.
And the Rabshakeh begins to speak.
Do you think words are enough to win a war? he says. Who are you trusting in? Egypt? Egypt is a broken reed. If you lean on it, it will snap, and stab your hand. And if you say, we trust in the LORD our God, is he not the one whose altars Hezekiah has torn down? He did not understand that Hezekiah had removed the false shrines, to worship the LORD in the right way. And then he even claims, the LORD himself told me to come and destroy this land!
Hezekiah’s officials ask him to speak in Aramaic, the Syrian language, not in Hebrew, where the people on the wall can hear. But the Rabshakeh wants everyone to hear. He stands up and shouts in Hebrew to the people on the wall.
Do not let Hezekiah deceive you. He cannot save you. Do not let him make you trust in the LORD, saying, the LORD will surely save us. Make peace with me, and come out. Then each of you can eat from your own vine and fig tree, until I come and take you away to a land like your own. Do not listen to Hezekiah.
And then he asks his proudest question. Has any of the gods of the nations ever saved its land from the king of Assyria? Where are the gods of Hamath and Arpad? Where are the gods of Sepharvaim? Did they save Samaria from me? Who among all the gods of the nations has saved his land, that the LORD should save Jerusalem?
He thought the LORD was just like all the other gods, idols of wood and stone.
'But the people held their peace, and answered him not a word: for the king’s commandment was, saying, Answer him not.' They did not argue. They did not shout back. They simply stayed silent, and trusted.
Hezekiah’s officials come back to the king, with their clothes torn in distress, and tell him everything the Rabshakeh said.
What will Hezekiah do? That is the next chapter.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 4. 'He removed the high places, and brake the images, and cut down the groves, and brake in pieces the brasen serpent that Moses had made: for unto those days the children of Israel did burn incense to it: and he called it Nehushtan.' Do you remember? In the wilderness, when the people were bitten by snakes, God told Moses to make a bronze serpent and lift it up, and everyone who looked at it lived. That was a good gift from God. But over the years, people began to worship it. So Hezekiah broke it, and called it Nehushtan, which sounds like, just a piece of bronze. Even good things can become idols, if we worship them instead of God.
Verse 5. 'He trusted in the LORD God of Israel; so that after him was none like him among all the kings of Judah, nor any that were before him.' What made Hezekiah stand out among all the kings? Not his army, or his riches, or his cleverness. He trusted in the LORD. That was the thing God valued most. Trust is the heart of a life with God.
Verse 19. 'And Rab- shakeh said unto them, Speak ye now to Hezekiah, Thus saith the great king, the king of Assyria, What confidence is this wherein thou trustest?' Confidence means trust. The Rabshakeh asked the right question, but with the wrong answer in mind. What are you trusting in? It is a question everyone has to answer. When the enemy mocks our trust in God, the best answer is not to argue, but to keep on trusting, and take it to God in prayer.
Jesus said, 'And as Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, even so must the Son of man be lifted up'. And the psalmist wrote, 'They that trust in the LORD shall be as mount Zion, which cannot be removed, but abideth for ever.'
Is there a voice in your life, perhaps even inside your own head, saying, God cannot help you? Do not argue with it. Take it to God. And is there anything good in your life that has become more important to you than God himself?
Here is today’s question. The Rabshakeh asked, what confidence is this wherein thou trustest? How would you answer that question about your own life?
Let us pray. Lord God, I want to trust you, like Hezekiah. When others mock my faith, help me to stay calm, and to bring it all to you. Show me anything good that has become an idol in my life, and help me to break it. And thank you for Jesus, who was lifted up on the cross, so that all who look to him may live. Amen.
