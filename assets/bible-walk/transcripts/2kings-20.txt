A king lies in bed, very sick. The prophet has just told him, put your affairs in order. You are going to die. The king turns his face to the wall, so no one can see, and he weeps, and prays. The prophet walks out of the room, across the courtyard. And before he has even reached the middle of the courtyard, God stops him. Go back. I have heard his prayer.
In chapter nineteen, Hezekiah spread the threatening letter from the king of Assyria before the LORD, and prayed, thou art the God, even thou alone. God answered through Isaiah: I will defend this city, for mine own sake, and for my servant David’s sake. The angel of the LORD struck the Assyrian camp, and Sennacherib went home to Nineveh.
Now, in chapter twenty, we see two more moments in Hezekiah’s life. Listen for a prayer with tears. And listen for a visit from a faraway land, that will matter very much later.
'In those days was Hezekiah sick unto death.' He had a dangerous sore, a boil, and it was going to take his life. And the prophet Isaiah comes to him with hard news. 'Thus saith the LORD, Set thine house in order; for thou shalt die, and not live.'
Hezekiah turns his face to the wall, and prays.
And Hezekiah weeps bitterly. He was still young. He did not yet have a son to take the throne. And he poured out his heart to God.
Isaiah leaves the room. But before he has even reached the middle courtyard of the palace, the word of the LORD comes to him.
And God adds, 'And I will add unto thy days fifteen years; and I will deliver thee and this city out of the hand of the king of Assyria'.
Then Isaiah says, bring a lump of figs, and lay it on the boil. They do, and Hezekiah recovers. God used an ordinary remedy, and his own healing power, together.
Hezekiah asks, what sign will there be that the LORD will heal me, and that I will go up to the house of the LORD on the third day?
Isaiah answers, here is your sign. The shadow on the steps of Ahaz, a kind of sundial where the shadow moved down the stairs as the sun moved across the sky, shall it go forward ten steps, or back ten steps?
Hezekiah says, it is easy for the shadow to go forward ten steps. That happens every day. Let it go back ten steps. 'And Isaiah the prophet cried unto the LORD: and he brought the shadow ten degrees backward, by which it had gone down in the dial of Ahaz.' The God who made the sun can make a shadow go backwards.
Then comes a visit. The king of Babylon, a land far to the east, sends messengers with letters and a gift, because he has heard that Hezekiah has been sick. At that time, Babylon was a small kingdom, looking for friends against Assyria.
And Hezekiah is flattered. He welcomes them, and shows them everything. His treasure house, the silver and the gold, the spices and the precious oil, his whole armoury, and everything in his storehouses. There is nothing in his palace or in his whole kingdom that he does not show them.
Then Isaiah comes to the king. What did these men say? Where did they come from? From a faraway land, Hezekiah says. From Babylon.
And Isaiah asks a searching question.
Then Isaiah says, hear the word of the LORD. The days are coming when everything in your palace, everything your fathers have stored up, will be carried off to Babylon. Nothing will be left. And some of your own descendants will be taken away, to serve in the palace of the king of Babylon.
Do you remember the book of Daniel? Daniel and his friends were young men from the royal family of Judah, taken to serve in the palace of the king of Babylon. Isaiah’s word came true, many years later.
And Hezekiah’s answer is surprising. 'Good is the word of the LORD which thou hast spoken. And he said, Is it not good, if peace and truth be in my days?' He accepted God’s word. But he seemed relieved that the trouble would not come in his own lifetime. It is a little sad. He did not seem to grieve for the generations to come.
The chapter ends by mentioning something Hezekiah built. He 'made a pool, and a conduit, and brought water into the city'. A conduit is a channel. Hezekiah cut a long tunnel through solid rock, to bring water into Jerusalem, so the city would have water during a siege. That tunnel can still be walked through in Jerusalem today.
Then Hezekiah dies, and his son Manasseh becomes king.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 3. 'I beseech thee, O LORD, remember now how I have walked before thee in truth and with a perfect heart, and have done that which is good in thy sight. And Hezekiah wept sore.' Hezekiah did not hide his feelings from God. He wept, and he told God honestly what was in his heart. God is not offended by our tears. He invites us to pour out our hearts to him, just as we are.
Verse 5. 'Turn again, and tell Hezekiah the captain of my people, Thus saith the LORD, the God of David thy father, I have heard thy prayer, I have seen thy tears: behold, I will heal thee: on the third day thou shalt go up unto the house of the LORD.' I have heard thy prayer. I have seen thy tears. What tender words. God does not just hear our words. He sees our tears. Not one of them goes unnoticed. And notice, God answered before Isaiah had even left the palace. Sometimes God’s answer is already on its way.
Verse 15. 'And he said, What have they seen in thine house? And Hezekiah answered, All the things that are in mine house have they seen: there is nothing among my treasures that I have not shewed them.' Hezekiah showed the visitors his riches, his weapons, his treasures. But did he show them his God? The God who had just healed him, and made the shadow go back? He had a wonderful story to tell. Instead, he showed off his possessions. When people visit our lives, what do they see? Our things, or our God?
David wrote, 'put thou my tears into thy bottle: are they not in thy book?' And Solomon wrote, 'Let another man praise thee, and not thine own mouth'.
Is there something making you sad today? Tell God about it, tears and all. He sees. And think about the people who will visit your home or your life this week. Look for a chance to show them, not your treasures, but your God.
Here is today’s question. Isaiah asked, what have they seen in thine house? If someone spent a day with you, what would they see about God in your life?
Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that you hear my prayers, and see my tears. Not one is wasted. Thank you that you are the God who heals, and who rules even the sun and its shadow. Keep me from showing off what I have, and help me instead to show others what you have done. Amen.
