A young king stands by a pillar in the temple, holding an old scroll. Before him stands the whole nation, rich and poor, old and young, priests and prophets. He begins to read aloud, every word, so that everyone can hear what God has said. When he finishes, the king makes a solemn promise. From now on, we will follow the LORD, with all our heart and soul. And all the people stand, and promise with him.
In chapter twenty two, the boy king Josiah grew up walking in the ways of David. While the temple was being repaired, the high priest found the book of the law. When Josiah heard it read, he tore his clothes. The prophetess Huldah said that judgment would still come on Jerusalem, but because Josiah’s heart was tender, it would not come in his days.
Now, in chapter twenty three, Josiah acts on what he has heard. Listen for how thoroughly he cleans out the idols. And listen for a prophecy from long ago that comes true.
Josiah gathers all the leaders of Judah and Jerusalem. Then he goes up to the house of the LORD with all the people, the priests, the prophets, small and great. And he reads in their ears all the words of the book of the covenant that was found in the house of the LORD.
Then Josiah makes a covenant.
And then Josiah gets to work. And he does not do it halfway.
He brings out of the temple everything made for Baal, for Asherah, and for the stars, and burns it outside Jerusalem. He removes the idolatrous priests. He tears down the high places throughout Judah. He defiles Topheth, where people had offered their children to the god Molech, so it could never be used again. He burns the chariots of the sun.
He smashes the altars of Ahaz and Manasseh. He even destroys the high places Solomon had built for the gods of his foreign wives, all those years before.
Then Josiah goes north, to Bethel, where Jeroboam had set up his golden calf and his altar, the place where Israel’s great sin began. And Josiah breaks down that altar and its high place.
And then something remarkable happens. As Josiah looks around, he sees tombs on the hillside. He sends men to take the bones out of the tombs, and he burns them on the altar at Bethel, to defile it for ever. And the writer says, this was 'according to the word of the LORD which the man of God proclaimed, who proclaimed these words.'
Do you remember? In the first book of Kings, chapter thirteen, a man of God came from Judah and cried out against Jeroboam’s altar at Bethel. O altar, altar! A child shall be born to the house of David, Josiah by name, and he shall burn men’s bones upon you. That prophecy was spoken long, long before Josiah was born. And it named him. Now it had come true, exactly.
Then Josiah sees a gravestone.
So they leave his bones alone, together with the bones of the old prophet who had been buried beside him.
Josiah does the same in all the towns of Samaria, removing the shrines the kings of Israel had built.
And then he commands all the people, 'Keep the passover unto the LORD your God, as it is written in the book of this covenant.' The passover remembered how God rescued his people from Egypt. And the writer says that there had not been a passover like it since the days of the judges. For years, the people had forgotten it. Now they remembered.
Josiah also removes the fortune tellers and those who claimed to speak to the dead, and every idol in the land, to do what was written in the book Hilkiah had found.
And the writer gives him the highest praise.
And yet, there is a sad word. 'Notwithstanding the LORD turned not from the fierceness of his great wrath'. Because of all that Manasseh had done, the judgment would still come. Josiah’s reforms were wonderful, but they came too late to save the nation. The people’s hearts had not truly changed.
Josiah’s life ends suddenly. Pharaoh Necho, king of Egypt, marches north to fight. Josiah goes out to meet him at Megiddo, and falls in battle there. His servants carry him back to Jerusalem in a chariot, and bury him.
The people make his son Jehoahaz king. But he does evil, and after only a few months, Pharaoh takes him away to Egypt in chains, where he dies. Pharaoh makes another of Josiah’s sons king, and changes his name to Jehoiakim. And Jehoiakim too does evil in the sight of the LORD.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 3. 'And the king stood by a pillar, and made a covenant before the LORD, to walk after the LORD, and to keep his commandments and his testimonies and his statutes with all their heart and all their soul, to perform the words of this covenant that were written in this book. And all the people stood to the covenant.' Josiah did not just read God’s Word. He promised to obey it, and he led his people to promise too. Hearing God’s Word is good. But God’s Word is meant to be obeyed. It is not enough to know what God says. We must do it.
Verse 17. 'Then he said, What title is that that I see? And the men of the city told him, It is the sepulchre of the man of God, which came from Judah, and proclaimed these things that thou hast done against the altar of Bethel.' A title here means a gravestone or marker. The man of God had died long ago. But his words did not die. Everything he said came true. God’s Word outlives those who speak it. What God says always comes to pass, even if it takes a very long time.
Verse 25. 'And like unto him was there no king before him, that turned to the LORD with all his heart, and with all his soul, and with all his might, according to all the law of Moses; neither after him arose there any like him.' With all his heart, soul and might. Those are the very words of the great commandment, in the book that Josiah had found. Josiah did not just admire them. He lived them. That is the kind of love God wants from each of us.
Moses wrote, 'And thou shalt love the LORD thy God with all thine heart, and with all thy soul, and with all thy might.' And the man of God had said, 'Behold, a child shall be born unto the house of David, Josiah by name'.
Is there one thing in God’s Word that you know you need to obey, but have not yet? Make your own covenant with God today. And is there anything in your life, like Josiah’s idols, that needs to be cleared out? Do not do it halfway.
Here is today’s question. Josiah turned to the LORD with all his heart, soul and might. What would it look like for you to love God with everything you have?
Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that your Word always comes true, even after many years. Like Josiah, I want to turn to you with all my heart, all my soul, and all my might. Show me what needs to be cleared out of my life, and give me courage to do it completely. Help me not only to hear your Word, but to keep it. Amen.
