A young king walks out of the gates of Jerusalem, with his mother, his officials and his princes. He has been king for only a few months. The army of Babylon surrounds the city. He knows he cannot win. So he goes out, and gives himself up. And behind him, soldiers begin carrying out the treasures of the temple, the golden vessels that Solomon made. They are all going to Babylon.
In chapter twenty three, Josiah read the book of the law to all the people, and made a covenant to follow the LORD with all their heart. He cleared out the idols from the whole land, and burned bones on the altar at Bethel, just as the man of God had said long before. But the LORD did not turn from his anger, because of Manasseh. Josiah fell at Megiddo, and his sons did evil.
Now, in chapter twenty four, Babylon arrives. Listen for how often the writer says this happened because of God’s word. And listen for names you may remember from the book of Daniel.
Jehoiakim, the son of Josiah, is king of Judah. He does evil in the sight of the LORD. And a new power rises in the east. Babylon has overthrown Assyria, and its king, Nebuchadnezzar, is the most powerful ruler in the world.
'In his days Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon came up, and Jehoiakim became his servant three years: then he turned and rebelled against him.'
Do you remember how the book of Daniel begins? In the reign of Jehoiakim, king of Judah, Nebuchadnezzar came to Jerusalem, and took some of the young men of the royal family to Babylon, to serve in his palace. Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael and Azariah were among them. Isaiah’s words to Hezekiah were already coming true.
When Jehoiakim rebels, trouble comes from every side.
And the writer makes sure we understand why.
The sins of Manasseh. His idols, and the innocent blood he had shed. Josiah’s reforms had been wonderful, but the people’s hearts had never truly turned. And the judgment that had been delayed was now coming.
Jehoiakim dies, and his son Jehoiachin becomes king. He is only a young man, and he too does evil. And he reigns for just a few months.
Then Nebuchadnezzar’s army comes and surrounds Jerusalem. And Nebuchadnezzar himself arrives. Jehoiachin knows there is no hope. So he goes out and surrenders, with his mother, his servants, his princes and his officers. And the king of Babylon takes him prisoner.
Then the Babylonians strip the city.
And they carry away the people too. All the leaders, all the mighty men of valour, all the skilled craftsmen and metalworkers. Everyone who might be able to lead a rebellion, or make weapons, is taken to Babylon. 'none remained, save the poorest sort of the people of the land.' The king himself, his mother, his wives, his officers and the leading men of the land, are all carried away to Babylon.
Among those taken to Babylon at this time was a young priest named Ezekiel. Far away, by a river in Babylon, God would give him amazing visions, and he would become a prophet to the exiles.
Then Nebuchadnezzar makes Jehoiachin’s uncle, Mattaniah, king in Jerusalem, and changes his name to Zedekiah. Changing someone’s name showed that you were their master. Zedekiah was a king, but only under Babylon’s control.
Zedekiah does evil, just like Jehoiakim. And then he does something very foolish.
The prophet Jeremiah lived in Jerusalem at this time. His book tells us that he warned Zedekiah again and again: do not rebel against Babylon. Submit, and you will live. But Zedekiah would not listen. And the next chapter tells what happened.
All through this chapter, the writer keeps pointing to God. At the commandment of the LORD. According to the word of the LORD. As the LORD had said. Babylon was powerful. But Babylon was not in charge. God was. Even in judgment, God was still in control of history.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 2. 'And the LORD sent against him bands of the Chaldees, and bands of the Syrians, and bands of the Moabites, and bands of the children of Ammon, and sent them against Judah to destroy it, according to the word of the LORD, which he spake by his servants the prophets.' The Chaldees are the Babylonians. Notice who sent them: the LORD. The enemies of Judah thought they were acting on their own. But they were carrying out what God’s prophets had warned for generations. God is never surprised by history. Even the armies of nations are in his hands.
Verse 13. 'And he carried out thence all the treasures of the house of the LORD, and the treasures of the king’s house, and cut in pieces all the vessels of gold which Solomon king of Israel had made in the temple of the LORD, as the LORD had said.' As the LORD had said. Isaiah had told Hezekiah that all the treasures would be carried to Babylon. Now it happened. The golden vessels that Solomon had made for the glory of God were cut up and taken away. What a sad end to the golden age. And yet, the book of Daniel tells us those vessels were kept in Babylon. And the book of Ezra tells us they came home again.
Verse 20. 'For through the anger of the LORD it came to pass in Jerusalem and Judah, until he had cast them out from his presence, that Zedekiah rebelled against the king of Babylon.' Zedekiah’s foolish rebellion was part of God’s judgment. When people keep turning away from God, he may let them follow their own foolish choices, even when those choices lead to disaster. That is a sobering thought. It is always better to turn back to God early, while we still can.
Daniel’s book begins, 'In the third year of the reign of Jehoiakim king of Judah came Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon unto Jerusalem, and besieged it.' And Jeremiah wrote to the exiles, 'For I know the thoughts that I think toward you, saith the LORD, thoughts of peace, and not of evil, to give you an expected end.'
When the news is full of powerful nations and frightening events, remember that God is still in control of history. Pray for your country and its leaders. And if God is warning you about something in your life, turn back to him now, while you can.
Here is today’s question. Again and again, the writer says, according to the word of the LORD. How does it help you to know that God is in control, even when things are going badly?
Let us pray. Lord God, you are in control of all nations and all of history. Even when things fall apart, you have not lost control. Thank you for your thoughts of peace towards your people, even in exile. Help me to listen to your warnings, and to turn back to you early. And thank you that you always keep a remnant, and always have a plan to bring your people home. Amen.
