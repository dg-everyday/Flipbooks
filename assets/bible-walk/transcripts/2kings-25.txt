In a dark prison cell in Babylon, a man sits, as he has sat for many, many years. Once he was a king. Now his clothes are prison rags, and he has almost forgotten what the sky looks like. Then one day, the door opens. A guard says, the new king of Babylon wants to see you. He is led out, blinking in the light. And the king of Babylon looks at him, and speaks to him kindly.
In chapter twenty four, Nebuchadnezzar, king of Babylon, came to Jerusalem. Jehoiachin surrendered, and was carried away to Babylon, with the princes, the mighty men and the craftsmen. The golden vessels of Solomon were taken, as the LORD had said. And Nebuchadnezzar made Zedekiah king. But Zedekiah rebelled.
Now we come to the last chapter of the second book of Kings. It is a sad chapter, and we will tell it gently. Listen for what happens to the city and the temple. And listen very carefully to the very last verses.
Because Zedekiah rebelled, Nebuchadnezzar comes back with his whole army. 'And it came to pass in the ninth year of his reign, in the tenth month, in the tenth day of the month, that Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon came, he, and all his host, against Jerusalem, and pitched against it; and they built forts against it round about.'
The siege lasts a long time, and a terrible famine grips the city. There is no bread left for the people.
Then the Babylonians break through the city wall. Zedekiah and his soldiers try to escape, slipping out through a gate between two walls, near the king’s garden, while the enemy surrounds the city. They head for the Jordan valley.
But the Babylonian army chases them, and catches Zedekiah on the plains of Jericho. His soldiers scatter and leave him. They bring him to Nebuchadnezzar, who passes judgment on him. Zedekiah suffers terribly for his rebellion. He loses his sons, and his sight, and he is taken to Babylon in chains. Jeremiah had begged him to submit to Babylon. He had not listened.
Then Nebuzar-adan, the captain of Nebuchadnezzar’s guard, comes to Jerusalem. And he burns the house of the LORD. The temple that Solomon built, where the glory of the LORD had filled the house, is set on fire. He burns the king’s palace, and all the houses of Jerusalem, every great house. And the army breaks down the walls of Jerusalem all around.
The rest of the people left in the city are carried away to Babylon.
But not everyone.
Then the Babylonians break up the bronze of the temple.
The two great pillars, Jachin and Boaz, broken into pieces. The great bronze sea, broken. The gold and the silver, all taken away. There was so much bronze, it could not be weighed, just as when Solomon made it.
The chief priest and other leaders are taken to the king of Babylon, and they do not come back. 'So Judah was carried away out of their land.'
Nebuchadnezzar makes a man named Gedaliah governor over the poor people left in Judah. Gedaliah tells them, do not be afraid. Live in the land, serve the king of Babylon, and it will be well with you. But a man from the royal family, named Ishmael, comes with ten men, and takes Gedaliah’s life. Then all the people, afraid of what Babylon will do, run away to Egypt.
It seems like the end of everything. The temple burned. The city in ruins. The people scattered. The king of David’s family in prison in Babylon. Is God’s promise to David finished?
And then come the last four verses of the book.
Many years later, a new king comes to the throne in Babylon, named Evil-merodach. And in the year he becomes king, he lets Jehoiachin, the captive king of Judah, out of prison.
He gives Jehoiachin a seat of honour above the other captive kings in Babylon. Jehoiachin takes off his prison clothes, and eats at the king’s table, for the rest of his life. And he is given a regular allowance, every day, all his days.
And that is where the second book of Kings ends. Not with the fire, but with a door opening. A king of David’s family, still alive, out of prison, eating at the king’s table. It is just a glimmer. But it is a glimmer of hope. God had not forgotten his promise to David.
And in the very first chapter of the New Testament, Matthew lists the family line of Jesus. And there, in that line, is Jehoiachin, also called Jechonias. The line of David went on, through exile, all the way to Bethlehem.
From Elijah’s chariot of fire to Jerusalem in flames, second Kings has shown us God’s patience, God’s warnings, and God’s faithfulness. The people turned away again and again. But God never stopped keeping his promises.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 12. 'But the captain of the guard left of the poor of the land to be vinedressers and husbandmen.' Vinedressers look after vineyards. Husbandmen are farmers. Even in the middle of judgment, the poorest people were left in the land, to care for it. Throughout the Bible, God cares for the poor. Even when everything else was being taken away, the land was not left empty. God was keeping a small seed alive.
Verse 13. 'And the pillars of brass that were in the house of the LORD, and the bases, and the brasen sea that was in the house of the LORD, did the Chaldees break in pieces, and carried the brass of them to Babylon.' The pillars named, he shall establish, and in him is strength, were broken and carried away. It might seem that God’s strength had failed. But the pillars were only bronze. They were signs, not God himself. When the signs were gone, God was still there. Buildings can fall. God never does.
Verse 28. 'And he spake kindly to him, and set his throne above the throne of the kings that were with him in Babylon'. He spake kindly to him. After all the years in prison, a word of kindness. The book of Kings, so full of sorrow, ends with kindness. And that kindness pointed to a greater kindness still. God would bring his people home. And one day, he would send the true King, the Son of David, to set all prisoners free.
Matthew wrote, 'And after they were brought to Babylon, Jechonias begat Salathiel'. And Jeremiah heard God say, 'after seventy years be accomplished at Babylon I will visit you, and perform my good word toward you, in causing you to return to this place.'
Is there something in your life that feels like ruins, broken and burned? Bring it to God. The story is not over. Look for the glimmer of hope, the door that God is opening, however small. He speaks kindly to those who are in prison.
Here is today’s question. Second Kings ends, not with the fire, but with a captive king lifted out of prison. Why do you think God wanted the book to end with that small sign of hope?

Even when everything fell apart, you did not forget your promise. Thank you that you speak kindly to prisoners, and that Jesus, the Son of David, came to set us free. Amen.
