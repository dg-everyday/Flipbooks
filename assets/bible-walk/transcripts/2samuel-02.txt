Two groups of soldiers sit facing each other across a pool of water. They are all Israelites. They worship the same God. They are brothers, cousins, neighbours. But they follow two different kings. And what starts as a contest at the pool will end in a bitter battle between brothers.
In chapter one, an Amalekite brought David the crown of Saul, claiming he had ended Saul’s life. David tore his clothes and mourned, and sang the song of the bow: Saul and Jonathan were lovely and pleasant in their lives. O Jonathan, thy love to me was wonderful. How are the mighty fallen.
Now, in chapter two, David becomes king, but only of part of Israel. Listen for what David does first. And listen for the question Abner asks Joab.
After this, David asks the LORD.
Notice that David does not rush to grab the throne. He asks God first, even about which town to go to. This is the David who encouraged himself in the LORD at Ziklag. He has learned to wait for God’s direction.
So David goes up to Hebron, with his two wives, and all his men and their families. And the men of Judah come, and there they anoint David king over the house of Judah.
At last. Many years before, Samuel had anointed David in secret, in his father’s house in Bethlehem. Now the people of his own tribe anoint him publicly as their king. But it is only Judah. The rest of Israel is not yet his.
Then David is told that the men of Jabesh-gilead buried Saul. And David sends them a message.
David goes on. May the LORD show you kindness and faithfulness. I too will treat you well, because you have done this. And now be strong, for your master Saul is dead, and the house of Judah has anointed me king.
David reaches out to Saul’s loyal supporters with kindness, not threats. He honours their loyalty to Saul, and invites them to be loyal to him.
But Abner, the commander of Saul’s army, takes Ish-bosheth, a son of Saul, and makes him king over Gilead, Jezreel, Ephraim, Benjamin, and all the rest of Israel. Only the house of Judah follows David.
So now there are two kings in Israel. David in Hebron, over Judah. And Ish-bosheth, over the rest. And David reigns in Hebron for more than seven years.
One day, Abner and the servants of Ish-bosheth go out to Gibeon. And Joab, David’s nephew and the commander of his army, goes out with David’s servants. They meet at the pool of Gibeon, one group on one side, and the other on the other side.
Abner says to Joab, let the young men get up and hold a contest before us. And Joab says, let them. So twelve men from Benjamin, for Ish-bosheth, and twelve of David’s men face each other. But the contest turns deadly, and all twenty four fall together.
And then a fierce battle breaks out that day. Abner and the men of Israel are defeated by David’s men.
Joab has two brothers with him, Abishai and Asahel. Asahel is as fast as a wild gazelle. And he chases after Abner, turning neither to the right nor to the left.
Abner looks back. Is that you, Asahel? I am, he says.
Abner says, turn aside, and go after one of the young men instead, and take his armour. But Asahel will not stop chasing him. Abner says again, stop following me. Why should I strike you down? How could I face your brother Joab then?
But Asahel will not turn aside. So Abner, with the back end of his spear, strikes him, and Asahel falls and dies there. And everyone who comes to the place where Asahel fell stops still.
Abner did not want to harm Asahel. He warned him twice. But Asahel would not listen. And his death will lead to more sorrow, as we will see.
Joab and Abishai chase after Abner. As the sun goes down, they reach a hill. And the men of Benjamin gather behind Abner, and stand on the top of the hill.
Then Abner calls out to Joab.
And Joab answers, as God lives, if you had not spoken, the men would have kept chasing their brothers until it was light. So Joab blows the trumpet, and all the people stop. They chase Israel no more, and fight no more.
Abner and his men march all through the dark hours, across the Jordan, back to Mahanaim. And Joab gathers his men. They take up Asahel, and bury him in his father’s tomb in Bethlehem. Then they march all the way back to Hebron, arriving at daybreak.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 1. 'And it came to pass after this, that David enquired of the LORD, saying, Shall I go up into any of the cities of Judah? And the LORD said unto him, Go up. And David said, Whither shall I go up? And he said, Unto Hebron.' Shall I go up? Whither? David asked God even the details. Hebron was the city Caleb had asked for, long before, the old city of the giants, where Abraham was buried. God led David to a place full of history and promise. When we ask God, he guides us, step by step.
Verse 5. 'And David sent messengers unto the men of Jabesh-gilead, and said unto them, Blessed be ye of the LORD, that ye have shewed this kindness unto your lord, even unto Saul, and have buried him.' Blessed be ye of the LORD. David could have treated Saul’s supporters as enemies. Instead, he blessed them for their loyalty and kindness. It is a mark of a good leader to honour faithfulness wherever he sees it, even among those who were not on his side.
Verse 26. 'Then Abner called to Joab, and said, Shall the sword devour for ever? knowest thou not that it will be bitterness in the latter end? how long shall it be then, ere thou bid the people return from following their brethren?' Shall the sword devour for ever? It is a cry from the heart. Brothers fighting brothers can only end in bitterness. Fighting within families, within churches, within nations, always leaves wounds. Someone has to say, enough. Someone has to blow the trumpet and call for peace.
Jesus said, 'Blessed are the peacemakers: for they shall be called the children of God.' And Paul wrote, 'If it be possible, as much as lieth in you, live peaceably with all men.'
Is there a conflict in your life that has gone on too long? Ask God today for the courage to say, enough. Be the one who blows the trumpet for peace.
Here is today’s question. Abner asked, shall the sword devour for ever? Why do you think conflicts between people who should be close, like family or friends, can be so hard to stop?
Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that you guide those who ask you, step by step. Make me a peacemaker. Where there is fighting in my family, my church, or my community, give me courage to call for peace. And help me, like David, to honour kindness and faithfulness wherever I see it. Amen.
