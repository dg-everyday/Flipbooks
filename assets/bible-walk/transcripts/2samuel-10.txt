A king sends messengers on a mission of kindness, to comfort a neighbour whose father has just died. But the messengers are seized, and sent home humiliated, with half their beards shaved off and their clothes cut short. They are too ashamed to come home. And an act of kindness turns into a war.
In chapter nine, David asked, is there yet any of the house of Saul, that I may shew him kindness for Jonathan’s sake? He found Mephibosheth, Jonathan’s lame son, in a poor town far away, and said, fear not, thou shalt eat bread at my table continually. And Mephibosheth ate at the king’s table, as one of the king’s sons.
Now, in chapter ten, David tries to show kindness again, but this time it is rejected. Listen for how David cares for his humiliated men. And listen for Joab’s words before the battle.
The king of the Ammonites, Nahash, dies, and his son Hanun becomes king in his place.
And David says, I will show kindness to Hanun, as his father showed kindness to me. So David sends his servants to comfort Hanun about his father.
We do not know exactly what kindness Nahash had shown David. Perhaps he had helped David when he was on the run from Saul. Whatever it was, David remembered it.
But when David’s servants arrive, the princes of the Ammonites say to Hanun, do you really think David is honouring your father, by sending people to comfort you? Has he not sent his servants to spy out the city, so that he can conquer it?
Suspicion. The princes could not imagine that David was simply being kind. They assumed the worst.
So Hanun seizes David’s servants. He shaves off half of each man’s beard, and cuts their clothes off in the middle, and sends them away.
In that culture, a man’s beard was a sign of honour and dignity. To shave half of it off was a terrible insult. And cutting their clothes was meant to shame them completely.
When David hears what has happened, he sends people to meet his men, because they are deeply ashamed.
When the Ammonites realise that they have made David their enemy, they hire armies from Syria, many thousands of soldiers, to fight with them.
When David hears of it, he sends Joab with the whole army of mighty men.
The Ammonites come out and line up for battle at the gate of their city. And the Syrian armies line up separately, out in the open field.
Joab sees that the battle lines are against him, in front and behind. So he takes the best of Israel’s soldiers, and lines them up against the Syrians. And he puts the rest of the army under his brother Abishai, and lines them up against the Ammonites.
And Joab says to Abishai, if the Syrians are too strong for me, then you come and help me. But if the Ammonites are too strong for you, then I will come and help you.
And then Joab says something remarkable.
So Joab and his men advance against the Syrians, and the Syrians run away from him. When the Ammonites see the Syrians running, they also run from Abishai, and go back into their city. And Joab returns to Jerusalem.
But the Syrians do not give up. They gather again. Hadarezer, their king, sends for more Syrian armies from beyond the river Euphrates. They come to a place called Helam, led by Shobach, the commander of Hadarezer’s army.
When David hears about it, he gathers all Israel, crosses the Jordan, and comes to Helam. The Syrians line up against David and fight him. But the Syrians flee before Israel. David defeats their chariots and horsemen, and their commander Shobach falls there.
When all the kings who served Hadarezer see that they have been defeated, they make peace with Israel, and serve them. And the Syrians are afraid to help the Ammonites any more.
The war that began with a rejected kindness ends with peace, and Israel stronger than ever. But the war with the Ammonites is not quite over. And in the next chapter, while that war goes on, David will make the worst mistake of his life.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 2. 'Then said David, I will shew kindness unto Hanun the son of Nahash, as his father shewed kindness unto me. And David sent to comfort him by the hand of his servants for his father. And David’s servants came into the land of the children of Ammon.' David remembered kindness. In chapter nine, he remembered Jonathan’s kindness. Here, he remembers Nahash’s kindness. A grateful heart remembers those who were good to us, and looks for ways to repay them. Even when our kindness is rejected, it is still right to offer it.
Verse 5. 'When they told it unto David, he sent to meet them, because the men were greatly ashamed: and the king said, Tarry at Jericho until your beards be grown, and then return.' David did not make his humiliated men walk into Jerusalem in shame. He sent people to meet them, and told them to rest at Jericho until their beards grew back. It is a small, tender detail. David cared about his people’s dignity. God also cares when we are ashamed, and covers our shame with his kindness.
Verse 12. 'Be of good courage, and let us play the men for our people, and for the cities of our God: and the LORD do that which seemeth him good.' Be of good courage. Fight bravely, for our people and for the cities of our God. And then, leave the outcome to the LORD. That is real faith. We do our part with all our strength. And then we trust God with the result, whatever it may be.
Paul wrote, 'Watch ye, stand fast in the faith, quit you like men, be strong.' And a psalm says, 'Be of good courage, and he shall strengthen your heart, all ye that hope in the LORD.'
Is there something difficult you must face? Do your part with courage, and then leave the result with God. And think of someone who has been shamed or humiliated. How could you help protect their dignity today?
Here is today’s question. Joab said, be of good courage, and the LORD do that which seemeth him good. What does it look like to do our best, and still leave the outcome to God?
Let us pray. Lord God, give me good courage for the things I must face. Help me to do my part with all my strength, and then to trust you with the result. Thank you that you care when I am ashamed, and cover me with your kindness. Help me to treat others with kindness and dignity too. Amen.
