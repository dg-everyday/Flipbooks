A prophet comes to see the king, with a story about injustice in the kingdom. A rich man, with flocks and herds beyond counting, has stolen a poor man’s only lamb, a lamb the poor man loved like a daughter. The king is outraged. That man deserves to die, he says. And the prophet looks him in the eye, and says four words that change everything.
In chapter eleven, David stayed in Jerusalem while his army went to war. From his roof he saw Bathsheba, the wife of Uriah, and he took her. To cover his sin, he arranged for faithful Uriah to fall in battle. He married Bathsheba, and it seemed no one knew. But the thing that David had done displeased the LORD.
Now, in chapter twelve, God confronts David. Listen for how Nathan reaches David’s heart. And listen for the difference between David’s response and Saul’s.
The LORD sends Nathan the prophet to David.
Nathan does not begin with an accusation. He tells a story.
There were two men in a certain city, Nathan says. One was rich, and the other poor. The rich man had a great many flocks and herds. But the poor man had nothing except one little ewe lamb, which he had bought. He raised it, and it grew up with him and his children. It ate from his plate, and drank from his cup, and slept in his arms. It was like a daughter to him.
Then a traveller came to visit the rich man. And the rich man did not want to take one of his own sheep or cattle to prepare a meal for his guest. So he took the poor man’s lamb, and prepared it for the man who had come.
David is furious with the rich man. He says to Nathan, as the LORD lives, the man who did this deserves to die! He must pay back four times over for the lamb, because he did this, and because he had no pity.
David, the old shepherd, could see the injustice clearly, in someone else.
And then Nathan says it.
And Nathan goes on, with God’s words. I anointed you king over Israel. I rescued you from Saul. I gave you your master’s house, and the kingdoms of Israel and Judah. And if that had been too little, I would have given you much more. Why have you despised the word of the LORD, by doing what is evil in his sight? You struck down Uriah the Hittite, and took his wife to be your wife.
David had everything, and still he took what was not his.
Then Nathan tells David the consequences. The sword will never leave your house, because you despised me, and took the wife of Uriah. Trouble will rise up against you from your own family. You did it in secret, but what happens will happen before all Israel.
And here is the moment that shows David’s heart.
No excuses. No blaming others. No saying, the people made me do it, as Saul did. Just, I have sinned against the LORD.
And Nathan answers at once. The LORD also hath put away thy sin; thou shalt not die. But because by this deed you have given the enemies of the LORD great cause to mock him, the child that has been born to you will die.
Forgiveness came at once. But the consequences of the sin remained.
Nathan goes home. And the LORD strikes the child that Uriah’s wife bore to David, and he becomes very ill.
David pleads with God for the child. He fasts, and goes in and lies on the ground through the dark hours. The elders of his house try to raise him up, but he will not get up, or eat with them.
On the seventh day, the child dies. And David’s servants are afraid to tell him. They say, while the child was alive, he would not listen to us. How can we tell him the child is dead? He may do something desperate.
But David sees them whispering, and understands. Is the child dead? Yes, they say, he is dead.
Then David gets up from the ground. He washes, and puts on lotion, and changes his clothes. He goes into the house of the LORD and worships. Then he goes home, and asks for food, and eats.
His servants are amazed.
And David says, while the child was alive, I fasted and wept, because I thought, who knows? Perhaps the LORD will be gracious to me, and let the child live.
David comforts Bathsheba, his wife. And in time she has another son, and David names him Solomon. And the LORD loves him. God sends word through Nathan the prophet, and gives the child another name, Jedidiah, which means loved by the LORD.
Out of the sorrow, God brings a new beginning. This child, Solomon, will be the next king, and will build the temple of God.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 7. 'And Nathan said to David, Thou art the man. Thus saith the LORD God of Israel, I anointed thee king over Israel, and I delivered thee out of the hand of Saul;' Thou art the man. It took great courage for Nathan to say this to the king. And it took God’s wisdom to say it through a story, so that David would judge himself before he could defend himself. We are often quick to see sin in others, and slow to see it in ourselves. God loves us enough to show us the truth.
Verse 13. 'And David said unto Nathan, I have sinned against the LORD. And Nathan said unto David, The LORD also hath put away thy sin; thou shalt not die.' Six words of confession, and immediate forgiveness. This is why David was a man after God’s own heart. Not because he never sinned, but because when he was confronted, he did not make excuses. He confessed. And God forgave. David’s prayer of repentance is in the fifty first psalm: have mercy upon me, O God.
Verse 23. 'But now he is dead, wherefore should I fast? can I bring him back again? I shall go to him, but he shall not return to me.' I shall go to him. In his grief, David had hope. He could not bring the child back. But one day, he would go to where the child was. Many Christians have found comfort in these words when they have lost a little one. David trusted that his child was safe in God’s hands.
David prayed, 'Have mercy upon me, O God, according to thy lovingkindness: according unto the multitude of thy tender mercies blot out my transgressions.' And John wrote, 'If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins'.
Is there something God has been showing you, through his word, or through someone else, that you need to confess? Do not make excuses. Say, as David did, I have sinned against the LORD. And receive his forgiveness.
Here is today’s question. Both Saul and David sinned, but David said, I have sinned against the LORD, without excuses. Why do you think it is so hard to admit our sin without blaming others?
Let us pray. Have mercy upon me, O God, according to your lovingkindness. I have sinned against you. Thank you that when I confess, you put away my sin. Give me a clean heart, and help me to see my own sin as clearly as I see the sin of others. Amen.
