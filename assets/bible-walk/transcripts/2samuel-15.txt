A king walks barefoot up a hill, with his head covered, weeping as he goes. All around him, his people are weeping too. He is fleeing his own city, his own palace. And the man he is running from is his own son, the son he loved and longed for, and finally kissed.
In chapter fourteen, a wise woman of Tekoah told David a story, and said, God devises means, that his banished be not expelled from him. David brought Absalom home, but would not see him for two years. At last, Absalom forced his way back, and the king kissed Absalom.
Now, in chapter fifteen, we find out what was really in Absalom’s heart. Listen for how Absalom wins the people over. And listen for the loyal friends who stand by David in his darkest hour.
After this, Absalom gets himself a chariot and horses, and fifty men to run ahead of him. He wants to look like a king.
Absalom gets up early, and stands beside the road at the city gate. Whenever anyone comes with a complaint for the king to judge, Absalom calls him over. Where are you from? And then he says, your case is good and right, but there is no one from the king to hear you. If only I were judge in the land! Then everyone with a complaint could come to me, and I would give them justice.
And when anyone comes near to bow down to him, Absalom reaches out, takes hold of him, and kisses him.
Absalom made promises he never meant to keep. He criticised his father, and flattered the people. And slowly, day by day, he turned their hearts.
Then Absalom says to the king, let me go to Hebron to keep a vow I made to the LORD. When I was in Geshur, I vowed that if the LORD brought me back to Jerusalem, I would worship him.
And the king says, go in peace. So Absalom goes to Hebron.
But Absalom sends secret messengers to all the tribes of Israel. As soon as you hear the trumpet, shout, Absalom is king in Hebron! And Absalom sends for Ahithophel, David’s own wise counsellor, who joins him. The conspiracy grows stronger and stronger.
A messenger comes to David. The hearts of the men of Israel are with Absalom.
And David says to his servants, get up, and let us flee, or none of us will escape from Absalom. Hurry, before he comes and brings disaster on us, and attacks the city.
So the king leaves, with his whole household. They stop at the last house of the city, and all his soldiers march past him. Among them are six hundred men from Gath, led by a man named Ittai.
David says to Ittai, why are you coming with us? Go back and stay with the new king. You are a foreigner, an exile from your own land. You only arrived recently. Should I make you wander with us? Go back, and take your men with you. May the LORD show you kindness and faithfulness.
But Ittai answers.
A foreigner, from Gath, Goliath’s city, showing more loyalty to David than his own son. Like Ruth, who said to Naomi, whither thou goest, I will go.
So David says, go on, then. And Ittai and all his men and their families go on.
The whole countryside weeps aloud as the people pass by. The king crosses the brook Kidron, and all the people head toward the wilderness.
Zadok the priest is there too, with the Levites carrying the ark of the covenant. But David says to Zadok, take the ark of God back into the city. If I find favour with the LORD, he will bring me back, and let me see it and his dwelling place again.
David had learned not to treat the ark like a lucky charm, as Israel did long before. He would not use God for his own protection. He would trust God, wherever God led.
So Zadok and Abiathar take the ark back to Jerusalem.
Then David goes up the Mount of Olives, weeping as he goes, with his head covered and his feet bare. And all the people with him cover their heads, and go up weeping.
Someone tells David, Ahithophel is among the conspirators with Absalom. And David prays a short, urgent prayer. O LORD, I pray thee, turn the counsel of Ahithophel into foolishness.
At the top of the hill, where people used to worship God, David’s friend Hushai comes to meet him, with his robe torn and dust on his head. And David sees the answer to his prayer. Go back to the city, he says. Tell Absalom you will serve him. Then you can defeat the advice of Ahithophel. And send me word of everything you hear, through the priests and their sons.
So Hushai, David’s friend, goes back into the city, just as Absalom arrives in Jerusalem.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 6. 'And on this manner did Absalom to all Israel that came to the king for judgment: so Absalom stole the hearts of the men of Israel.' Stole their hearts. With flattery, with promises, with a kiss. Absalom looked good and sounded good. But his heart was set on taking what was not his. Be careful of anyone who wins your heart by criticising others, and promising everything you want to hear.
Verse 21. 'And Ittai answered the king, and said, As the LORD liveth, and as my lord the king liveth, surely in what place my lord the king shall be, whether in death or life, even there also will thy servant be.' Whether in death or life. Ittai chose to follow David when David had nothing to offer him, when David was running for his life. That is true loyalty. And it is the kind of loyalty Jesus asks of those who follow him. Not only when things go well, but in death or life.
Verse 26. 'But if he thus say, I have no delight in thee; behold, here am I, let him do to me as seemeth good unto him.' Behold, here am I. David put himself entirely in God’s hands. If God wanted to bring him back, he would. If not, David would accept it. This is the humble faith of a man who knows he has sinned, and who trusts God with his future, whatever it may be.
Jesus said, 'If any man serve me, let him follow me; and where I am, there shall also my servant be'. And many years later, Jesus too would cross the brook Kidron, and go to the Mount of Olives to pray, saying, 'nevertheless not my will, but thine, be done.'
Is there a situation you cannot control? Pray as David did: here am I, let him do to me as seemeth good unto him. And think of a friend who is going through hard times. Stand by them, as Ittai and Hushai stood by David.
Here is today’s question. Ittai said he would stay with David whether in death or life. What does it mean to follow Jesus with that kind of loyalty?
Let us pray. Lord God, when I do not know what will happen, help me to say, here am I, do to me as seems good to you. Keep me from being deceived by flattery. Make me a loyal friend, and a loyal follower of Jesus, whether in death or life. Amen.
