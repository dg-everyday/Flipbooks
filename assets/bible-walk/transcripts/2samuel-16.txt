A king is walking along a dusty road, fleeing for his life. And on the hillside above him, a man is walking alongside, shouting curses, throwing stones and dust at him. The king’s soldiers draw their swords. One of them says, let me go and silence him for good. And the king says, no. Leave him alone. Perhaps God has sent him.
In chapter fifteen, Absalom stole the hearts of the men of Israel, and made himself king in Hebron. David fled from Jerusalem, weeping up the Mount of Olives, with his head covered and his feet bare. Ittai the Gittite said, whether in death or life, there will thy servant be. David sent the ark back, saying, let him do to me as seemeth good unto him. And he sent his friend Hushai back to defeat the counsel of Ahithophel.
Now, in chapter sixteen, David meets three people on the road. Listen for how David responds to insult. And listen for what is happening back in Jerusalem.
When David is a little past the top of the hill, Ziba, the servant of Mephibosheth, meets him. He has two donkeys, loaded with bread, raisins, summer fruit and wine.
The king asks, what are these for? Ziba says, the donkeys are for the king’s family to ride, the bread and fruit for the young men to eat, and the wine for those who grow faint in the wilderness.
The king asks, where is your master’s grandson, Mephibosheth? And Ziba says, he is staying in Jerusalem. He says, today the house of Israel will give me back my grandfather’s kingdom.
The king says, then everything that belonged to Mephibosheth is now yours. And Ziba bows and thanks him.
But was Ziba telling the truth? We will find out later. David, in his distress, believed him without asking Mephibosheth.
Then, at a place called Bahurim, a man from Saul’s family comes out. His name is Shimei. And as he comes, he curses David. He throws stones at David and all his servants, even though the soldiers are all around the king. He shouts that David is a man of violence, that the LORD is paying him back for taking Saul’s throne, and that now the LORD has given the kingdom to Absalom.
Shimei blamed David for things he had not done. David had never harmed Saul. He had spared his life twice. But Shimei did not care about the truth.
Abishai, Joab’s brother, says to the king, why should this dead dog curse my lord the king? Let me go over and take off his head.
But the king says no.
And David says to Abishai and all his servants, my own son, my own flesh and blood, is trying to take my life. How much more this man from Saul’s tribe. Leave him alone, and let him curse.
And then David says something deeply humble.
So David and his men go on along the road. And Shimei goes along the hillside opposite him, cursing as he goes, throwing stones and dirt.
The king and all the people with him arrive at their destination exhausted. And there they rest and are refreshed.
Meanwhile, back in Jerusalem, Absalom arrives with all the men of Israel, and Ahithophel is with him.
David’s friend Hushai comes to Absalom, and says, long live the king! Long live the king!
Absalom is suspicious. Is this your loyalty to your friend? Why did you not go with him?
Hushai answers cleverly. No, he says. I will serve the one whom the LORD and this people and all the men of Israel have chosen. And besides, whom should I serve? Should it not be the son? As I served your father, so I will serve you.
Hushai’s words are carefully chosen. Whom the LORD has chosen. Absalom heard it as himself. But Hushai knew that the LORD had chosen David.
Then Absalom says to Ahithophel, give us your advice. What should we do?
And Ahithophel gives Absalom shameful advice. He tells him to do something openly, before all Israel, to show that he has broken completely with his father, and that there can be no going back. And Absalom does it, on the roof of the palace, in the sight of all Israel.
It was a terrible act of disrespect and shame against his father. And it was exactly what Nathan had warned David, after his sin with Bathsheba. You did it secretly, but this will happen before all Israel. The sword had come into David’s house, just as God had said.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 10. 'And the king said, What have I to do with you, ye sons of Zeruiah? so let him curse, because the LORD hath said unto him, Curse David. Who shall then say, Wherefore hast thou done so?' David did not take revenge. He believed God was in control even of this. Perhaps God was using Shimei’s curses to humble him. When people insult us, it is tempting to strike back. David chose to accept it, and to leave it with God.
Verse 12. 'It may be that the LORD will look on mine affliction, and that the LORD will requite me good for his cursing this day.' Affliction means suffering. David hoped that God would see his suffering, and turn the curses into blessing. That is the faith of someone who has learned humility. David knew he had sinned. He accepted the consequences. And he still trusted in God’s mercy.
Verse 23. 'And the counsel of Ahithophel, which he counselled in those days, was as if a man had enquired at the oracle of God: so was all the counsel of Ahithophel both with David and with Absalom.' Ahithophel’s advice was so good that people treated it as if it came from God. But clever advice is not always godly advice. Ahithophel was brilliant, but he used his wisdom to serve a rebellion. Wisdom without faithfulness is dangerous. And David had prayed that God would turn Ahithophel’s counsel into foolishness.
Jesus said, 'Bless them that curse you, and pray for them which despitefully use you.' And Peter wrote of Jesus, 'when he was reviled, reviled not again'.
Has someone spoken against you unfairly? Instead of striking back, bring it to God today. Ask him to look on your affliction, and to turn it to good.
Here is today’s question. David said of Shimei, let him alone, and let him curse. What do you think helped David to respond to insults without taking revenge?
Let us pray. Lord God, when people speak against me, help me not to strike back, but to leave it with you. Look on my affliction, and turn it to good. Give me humility, like David’s. And keep me from using any wisdom you give me for wrong purposes. Amen.
