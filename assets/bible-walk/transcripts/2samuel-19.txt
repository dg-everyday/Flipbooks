A young man comes limping down the road to meet the returning king. His feet are uncared for. His beard is untrimmed. His clothes are unwashed. He has not taken care of himself since the day the king left. He has been mourning. And he has been accused of betraying the king. Now he has a chance to clear his name, and win back everything he lost. And his answer surprises everyone.
In chapter eighteen, David begged his commanders, deal gently for my sake with the young man Absalom. But Absalom was caught in an oak, and Joab ended his life. When the runners came, David asked, is the young man Absalom safe? And when he heard the answer, he wept: O my son Absalom, would God I had died for thee.
Now, in chapter nineteen, David must return to his kingdom. Listen for how he treats those who hurt him, and those who helped him. And listen for Mephibosheth’s answer.
Joab is told, the king is weeping and mourning for Absalom. And the victory that day is turned into mourning for all the people, because they hear how the king is grieving for his son. The soldiers creep back into the city quietly, like soldiers who are ashamed because they have run away from battle.
Joab goes to the king, and speaks bluntly. You have shamed your servants, who saved your life. You love those who hate you, and hate those who love you. Now get up, and speak kindly to your servants, or not one man will stay with you.
Joab was harsh. But he was right. David’s grief was real, but he had a duty to the people who had risked their lives for him. Sometimes, even in deep grief, we must still care for those around us.
So the king gets up, and sits in the gate. And all the people come before him.
Meanwhile, the tribes of Israel argue about bringing the king back.
David sends word to the priests. Ask the elders of Judah, my own tribe, why are you the last to bring the king back? And he offers to make Amasa, Absalom’s commander, the commander of his army instead of Joab. And David wins the hearts of all the men of Judah, and they send word, come back, you and all your servants.
So the king comes back to the Jordan. And there, waiting to meet him, are three men.
The first is Shimei, the man who cursed David and threw stones at him. He hurries down with a thousand men of Benjamin, and falls down before the king. Do not hold this against me, he says. Do not remember the wrong I did the day you left Jerusalem. I know I have sinned. That is why I have come first today, to meet my lord the king.
Abishai says, should not Shimei be put to death for this, because he cursed the LORD’s anointed?
But David says, what have I to do with you, sons of Zeruiah? Should anyone be put to death in Israel today? Do I not know that today I am king over Israel?
Then Mephibosheth comes down to meet the king. He has not cared for his feet, or trimmed his beard, or washed his clothes, from the day the king left until the day he came home safely.
The king says, why did you not come with me, Mephibosheth?
And Mephibosheth explains. My lord, my servant deceived me. I said, I will saddle my donkey and go with the king, because I am lame. But Ziba left without me, and slandered me to my lord the king. But you are like an angel of God. Do whatever seems good to you. All my grandfather’s family deserved nothing but death from you. Yet you set me among those who eat at your own table. What right do I have to ask anything more?
So Ziba had lied about Mephibosheth. Mephibosheth had stayed loyal all along.
The king says, why say any more? You and Ziba divide the land.
And Mephibosheth answers.
Then old Barzillai comes down from Gilead, to escort the king across the Jordan. He is eighty years old, and had provided for the king at Mahanaim.
The king says, come with me, and I will provide for you in Jerusalem.
But Barzillai says, how long do I have left to live, that I should go up with the king to Jerusalem? I am eighty years old. Can I still tell what is good? Can I taste what I eat and drink? Can I still hear the voices of singers? Let me go back and die in my own town, near the grave of my father and mother. But here is my son Chimham. Let him go with you.
The king says, Chimham shall come with me, and I will do for him whatever you wish. So the king kisses Barzillai and blesses him, and he goes home.
But the chapter ends with a quarrel. The men of Israel complain that the men of Judah have taken the king for themselves. And the men of Judah answer back. And the quarrel grows fierce. The kingdom has been restored, but it is not united.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 4. 'But the king covered his face, and the king cried with a loud voice, O my son Absalom, O Absalom, my son, my son!' David’s grief was deep and real. The Bible does not hide it, or tell him to stop feeling it. Grief is not a lack of faith. But even in grief, David eventually got up and returned to his people. God gives us strength to carry our sorrow, and still care for others.
Verse 23. 'Therefore the king said unto Shimei, Thou shalt not die. And the king sware unto him.' Shimei had cursed David, thrown stones at him, and called him terrible names. And David forgave him, on the day of his return. He chose mercy over revenge. A day of victory is a good day to forgive.
Verse 30. 'And Mephibosheth said unto the king, Yea, let him take all, forasmuch as my lord the king is come again in peace unto his own house.' Let him take all. Mephibosheth did not care about the land. He cared about the king. The king was home safe, and that was all that mattered. It is a picture of a heart that loves the giver more than the gifts. When we truly love God, we can say, whatever I lose, it is enough that you are with me.
Paul wrote, 'I count all things but loss for the excellency of the knowledge of Christ Jesus my Lord'. And a psalm says, 'Whom have I in heaven but thee? and there is none upon earth that I desire beside thee.'
Is there something you are holding on to tightly, that matters more to you than it should? Ask God to help you love him more than his gifts. And is there someone you need to forgive, like Shimei? Choose mercy today.
Here is today’s question. Mephibosheth said, let him take all, because the king was home. What would it look like for you to love God more than anything he gives you?
Let us pray. Lord God, you are better than all your gifts. Help me to love you more than anything you give me. Give me a heart like Mephibosheth’s, that says, it is enough that you are with me. And help me to forgive, as David forgave Shimei. Amen.
