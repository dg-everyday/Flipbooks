An old king looks back over his whole life. The lion and the bear. The giant in the valley. The spears thrown at him in the palace. The caves and the wilderness. The betrayals, the battles, the son who turned against him. So many times he could have died. And now, at the end of it all, he does not boast about his own courage or skill. He sings a song about God.
In chapter twenty one, there was a famine because Saul had broken Israel’s oath to the Gibeonites. David put it right, but spared Mephibosheth because of the LORD’s oath. Rizpah kept watch over her sons. David grew faint in battle, and his men called him the light of Israel. And the giants of Gath fell by the hand of David, and by the hand of his servants.
Now, in chapter twenty two, David sings. This song is almost exactly the same as the eighteenth psalm. Listen for all the pictures David uses to describe God. And listen for what David says made him great.
David speaks to the LORD the words of this song, on the day when the LORD rescued him from all his enemies, and from the hand of Saul.
The song begins with a great list of pictures.
A rock. A fortress. A shield. A high tower. A refuge. Every picture David uses comes from his life on the run. He hid among the rocks of the wilderness. He took shelter in strongholds and caves. He knew what it meant to need a safe place. And now he says, God himself was my safe place.
David goes on. I will call on the LORD, who is worthy to be praised, and so I will be saved from my enemies.
Then David remembers the times when he nearly died. The waves of death surrounded me. The floods of ungodly men made me afraid. The cords of the grave wrapped around me. The snares of death were in front of me.
And then David tells us what he did.
And then the song describes God coming to rescue David, in a mighty storm. The earth shook and trembled. The foundations of heaven moved. God bowed the heavens and came down, with darkness under his feet. He rode upon a cherub, and flew on the wings of the wind. The LORD thundered from heaven, and the Most High raised his voice. He sent out arrows of lightning, and scattered the enemy.
This is poetry. David did not literally see God riding through the sky. But he knew that when he cried for help, the whole power of heaven came to rescue him.
And then, the most tender picture. 'He sent from above, he took me; he drew me out of many waters;' Like a drowning man pulled out of a flood, God reached down and lifted David out.
He delivered me from my strong enemy, from those who hated me, for they were too strong for me. They confronted me in the day of my disaster, but the LORD was my support. He brought me out into a wide, open place. He rescued me, because he delighted in me.
Because he delighted in me. Not because David was strong, or good. Because God loved him.
David sings about God’s faithfulness to those who are faithful. With the merciful, you show yourself merciful. With the upright, you show yourself upright. You save the humble, but you bring down the proud.
Then comes a beautiful line. 'For thou art my lamp, O LORD: and the LORD will lighten my darkness.' In chapter twenty one, the people called David the lamp of Israel. But David knows that God is his lamp.
With your help I can run against an army, David sings. With my God I can leap over a wall.
David sings that God makes his feet like the feet of a deer, sure footed on the high places. God teaches his hands to fight. God gives him the shield of salvation.
And then David says something surprising.
Not thy power, or thy strength. Thy gentleness. God’s gentle care, his patience, his kindness to David through all his failures, that is what made David great.
The song ends with praise. The LORD lives! Blessed be my rock! Exalted be God, the rock of my salvation! I will give thanks to you, O LORD, among the nations, and sing praises to your name.
And the very last line points forward. God gives great victories to his king, and shows mercy to his anointed, to David and to his descendants for ever.
To his descendants for ever. The promise of chapter seven. A King from David’s family, who will reign for ever.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 2. 'And he said, The LORD is my rock, and my fortress, and my deliverer;' My rock, my fortress, my deliverer. Notice the word my, again and again. David did not just know about God. He knew God personally. God was his rock, his fortress, his deliverer. Faith is not just believing that God is strong. It is trusting him to be strong for me.
Verse 7. 'In my distress I called upon the LORD, and cried to my God: and he did hear my voice out of his temple, and my cry did enter into his ears.' In my distress I called. That is the simple secret of David’s life. Whenever he was in trouble, he cried out to God. And God heard. Not always at once, not always in the way David expected. But God always heard. The same God hears us when we cry out to him.
Verse 31. 'As for God, his way is perfect; the word of the LORD is tried: he is a buckler to all them that trust in him.' His way is perfect. David had walked a long and hard road. He had made terrible mistakes. And yet, looking back, he could say that God’s way was perfect. The word of the LORD is tried, tested and proved true. A buckler is a shield. God shields all who trust in him.
The eighteenth psalm begins with almost the same words: 'I will love thee, O LORD, my strength. The LORD is my rock, and my fortress, and my deliverer'. And Jesus said, 'whosoever heareth these sayings of mine, and doeth them, I will liken him unto a wise man, which built his house upon a rock'.
Look back over your own life, as David did. Where has God been your rock, your fortress, your deliverer? Write your own short song or prayer of thanks today, using David’s pictures, or your own.
Here is today’s question. David said, thy gentleness hath made me great. How has God’s gentleness, his patience and kindness, shaped your life?
Let us pray. Lord, you are my rock, my fortress and my deliverer. In my distress I call upon you, and you hear me. Thank you for drawing me out of deep waters, and for your gentleness, which has made me what I am. Your way is perfect, and you are a shield to all who trust in you. Amen.
