An old man, near the end of his life, gathers his strength to say his last words. He was once a shepherd boy who played the harp on the hillsides. He became a warrior, a king, a writer of songs that people would sing for thousands of years. And in his last words, he does not talk about his victories. He talks about God’s promise.
In chapter twenty two, David sang a song of praise. The LORD is my rock, and my fortress, and my deliverer. In my distress I called upon the LORD, and he did hear my voice. Thou art my lamp, O LORD. Thy gentleness hath made me great. As for God, his way is perfect.
Now, in chapter twenty three, we hear David’s last words, and the names of his mighty men. Listen for what David says a good ruler should be like. And listen for the story of the water from Bethlehem.
These are the last words of David.
David, the son of Jesse, says. The man who was raised up on high, the anointed of the God of Jacob, the sweet psalmist of Israel, says.
The sweet psalmist of Israel. Of all the things David was, this is one of the most beautiful. He gave God’s people songs. Many of the psalms we still read and sing were written by David.
'The Spirit of the LORD spake by me, and his word was in my tongue.'
David knew that his psalms were not just his own words. God had spoken through him.
Then David describes what God says a good ruler should be.
And David goes on, using a lovely picture from nature. Such a ruler 'shall be as the light of the morning, when the sun riseth, even a morning without clouds; as the tender grass springing out of the earth by clear shining after rain.'
A good, just leader is like the sun rising on a clear day, like fresh green grass after the rain. He brings light and life to his people.
But David knows he has not always been that kind of king.
Then the chapter lists David’s mighty men, the bravest warriors who stood with him.
There was Adino, the chief of the captains, who raised his spear against hundreds in one battle.
There was Eleazar, one of the three mighty men. When the men of Israel had run away from the Philistines, he stood his ground, and fought until his hand was so weary that it stuck to his sword. And the LORD brought about a great victory that day.
There was Shammah. The Philistines gathered in a field full of lentils, and the people ran away. But Shammah stood in the middle of the field and defended it. And the LORD brought about a great victory.
Notice the words, the LORD brought about a great victory. The mighty men were brave. But the writer makes sure we know who really won.
Then comes a lovely story. Once, when David was hiding in the cave of Adullam, the Philistines had a garrison in Bethlehem, David’s home town. And David was homesick.
David said, oh, that someone would give me a drink of water from the well of Bethlehem, by the gate!
David was not giving an order. He was just remembering, longing for the water of his childhood home.
But three of his mighty men heard him.
David said, far be it from me, O LORD, to do this. Is this not the very lives of the men who risked everything? And he would not drink it.
David saw that the water was too precious to drink. It was worth the lives of his men. So he gave it to God.
The list goes on. Abishai, Joab’s brother, who was chief of the three. Benaiah, a brave man who did many great deeds. He went down into a pit on a snowy day, and defeated a lion. He faced a huge Egyptian armed with a spear, with only a staff, and won. David put him in charge of his bodyguard.
Then comes a list of the thirty, the next rank of mighty men. Asahel, Joab’s brother. Elhanan of Bethlehem. And many others, from many places, even some from other nations.
And the very last name on the list is Uriah the Hittite.
Uriah. The faithful soldier who would not go home while his comrades were in the field. The man David wronged so terribly. His name is written here, among David’s mighty men, honoured for ever. The Bible does not let us forget him.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 3. 'The God of Israel said, the Rock of Israel spake to me, He that ruleth over men must be just, ruling in the fear of God.' Just, and ruling in the fear of God. That is what God wants from every leader. Not just strong, or clever, or popular. Just and fair, and humble before God. And it is true for all of us, in whatever small area we have influence: our homes, our work, our friendships.
Verse 5. 'Although my house be not so with God; yet he hath made with me an everlasting covenant, ordered in all things, and sure: for this is all my salvation, and all my desire, although he make it not to grow.' David knew his family had not lived up to God’s standard. His own failures were great. But his hope was not in himself. It was in God’s everlasting covenant, ordered in all things, and sure. When we look at our failures, our hope is the same. Not our goodness, but God’s faithful promise.
Verse 16. 'And the three mighty men brake through the host of the Philistines, and drew water out of the well of Bethlehem, that was by the gate, and took it, and brought it to David: nevertheless he would not drink thereof, but poured it out unto the LORD.' Three men risked their lives for their king’s wish. And David, instead of drinking it, poured it out to God. Their love was too precious to use for himself. It is a picture of worship: giving to God the most precious things we have.
Jesus, the Son of David, born in Bethlehem, said, 'whosoever drinketh of the water that I shall give him shall never thirst'. And Paul wrote, 'I am now ready to be offered'.
Is there something precious you could pour out to God today? Your time, your gifts, your love? And think of someone who has gone out of their way for you. Thank them, and thank God for them.
Here is today’s question. David put his hope in God’s everlasting covenant, even though his own house had failed. Why is it so important that our hope rests on God’s promise, and not on our own goodness?
Let us pray. Lord God, thank you for your everlasting covenant, ordered in all things, and sure. My hope is not in my own goodness, but in your faithful promise. Help me to be just and to live in the fear of God. And receive the precious things I pour out to you. Amen.
