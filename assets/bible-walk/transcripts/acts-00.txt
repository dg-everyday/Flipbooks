A small group of ordinary people stands on a hill outside Jerusalem. Their teacher has just been carried up into heaven before their eyes. They have no army, no money, no buildings and no power. But he has given them a task: to tell the whole world about him. By the end of this book, their message will have reached the capital of the Roman Empire.
Welcome to the book of Acts, on The Living Voice. Its full name is the Acts of the Apostles. It has twenty-eight chapters, so on this walk it is twenty-eight steps, one chapter at a time.
Who wrote it? The same writer who gave us the Gospel of Luke. Acts begins, 'The former treatise have I made, O Theophilus, of all that Jesus began both to do and teach'. The former treatise is the Gospel of Luke. Notice the word began. The Gospel told what Jesus began to do. Acts tells what he goes on doing, by his Spirit, through his people. And in some places Luke stops saying they, and starts saying we. He was there, travelling with the apostle Paul.
What is it about? Acts is the only history we have of the first followers of Jesus after he rose from the dead. It tells how the good news spread, just as Jesus said it would. One verse gives the plan of the whole book: 'ye shall be witnesses unto me both in Jerusalem, and in all Judaea, and in Samaria, and unto the uttermost part of the earth.'
So the book moves outward, like ripples in a pond. In chapters one to seven, the witness is in Jerusalem. Peter preaches, thousands believe, and Stephen gives his life for Jesus. In chapters eight to twelve, the good news goes out into Judaea and Samaria, to an official from Ethiopia, and to a Roman soldier named Cornelius. And a fierce enemy of the church, Saul, meets the risen Jesus on the road to Damascus. In chapters thirteen to twenty, Saul, now called Paul, travels across the lands we now call Turkey and Greece, telling Jews and Gentiles about Jesus. And in chapters twenty-one to twenty-eight, Paul is arrested, stands trial before governors and a king, survives a shipwreck, and reaches Rome.
Luke was a careful writer. He names real cities, real rulers and real officials, and many of those details have been found again in old inscriptions. Acts is history, and it is also the story of God at work.
Here are four things to watch for as we walk.
Watch for the Holy Ghost, the Spirit of God. Some people call Acts the Acts of the Holy Spirit, because he is the one who gives power, who guides, who sends, and who opens doors.
Watch for witnesses. Again and again, ordinary people speak about Jesus: fishermen, a deacon, a tentmaker, a woman who sold purple cloth, a jailer. They tell what they have seen and heard.
Watch for opposition. The message is opposed at every step, by councils, crowds, kings and storms. And every time, the word of God keeps growing.
And watch for the name of Jesus. Peter says, 'there is none other name under heaven given among men, whereby we must be saved.'
The book of Acts does not really end. Paul is still in Rome, still preaching, still waiting for his trial. The story is unfinished, because it is still going on, and everyone who follows Jesus is part of it.
Here is a question to carry with you. Jesus told his followers, ye shall be witnesses unto me. Who in your life might hear about Jesus from you as we walk through this book?
Let us pray. Lord Jesus, thank you that your work did not stop when you returned to heaven. As I walk through the book of Acts, fill me with your Spirit, make me bold, and make me your witness, wherever you send me. Amen.
