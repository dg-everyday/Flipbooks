A crowd gathers in the streets of Jerusalem, drawn by a strange sound like a rushing mighty wind. People from many nations are there, and each of them hears a group of men from Galilee speaking in his own language. Some are amazed. Others laugh and say, they are drunk. Then a fisherman stands up, raises his voice, and begins to speak.
In chapter one, the risen Jesus promised his disciples power when the Holy Ghost came upon them, and told them they would be his witnesses. Then he was taken up into heaven. About a hundred and twenty believers waited in Jerusalem, praying with one accord, and Matthias was chosen to take Judas’ place among the twelve.
Now, in chapter two, the waiting is over. It is the feast of Pentecost, a harvest feast, when Jews from many lands came to Jerusalem. This is the day the Spirit comes, and the first time the good news of the risen Jesus is preached to a crowd.
When the day of Pentecost has fully come, the believers are all together in one place. Suddenly there comes a sound from heaven, as of a rushing mighty wind, and it fills the house where they are sitting. Something like tongues of fire appears, and rests on each of them. They are all filled with the Holy Ghost, and begin to speak in other languages, as the Spirit gives them the words.
Devout Jews from every nation under heaven are staying in Jerusalem. When they hear the sound, they come together, and they are confused, because every one of them hears the believers speaking in his own language. Are not all these men from Galilee? they ask. How do we each hear them in the language we were born with? People from Parthia, Media and Mesopotamia, from Egypt and Libya, visitors from Rome, Cretes and Arabians: 'we do hear them speak in our tongues the wonderful works of God.' They are amazed, and ask, 'What meaneth this?' But others mock, and say, these men are full of new wine.
Then Peter stands up with the eleven, and raises his voice. These men are not drunk, as you suppose, he says. It is far too early in the day for that. This is what the prophet Joel spoke about. In the last days, says God, I will pour out my Spirit on all flesh. Your sons and your daughters will prophesy. Your young men will see visions, and your old men will dream dreams. And Joel ends with a promise.
Then Peter speaks about Jesus of Nazareth. He was a man approved by God among you, by miracles and wonders and signs, as you yourselves know. He was handed over by the plan and foreknowledge of God, and you took him, and by wicked hands crucified and killed him. But God raised him up, 'because it was not possible that he should be holden of it.' Death could not hold him.
Peter quotes King David, who wrote in a psalm, thou wilt not leave my soul in hell, neither wilt thou suffer thine Holy One to see corruption. David died and was buried, Peter says, and his tomb is here with us. So David was not speaking about himself. He was a prophet, and he spoke about the resurrection of the Christ. 'This Jesus hath God raised up, whereof we all are witnesses.' Now Jesus is exalted at the right hand of God, and he has poured out what you now see and hear.
'Therefore let all the house of Israel know assuredly, that God hath made that same Jesus, whom ye have crucified, both Lord and Christ.'
When the people hear this, they are pricked in their heart, cut to the heart. They say to Peter and the other apostles, 'Men and brethren, what shall we do?' And Peter answers them. The promise is unto you, he says, and to your children, and to all that are afar off, even as many as the Lord our God shall call. With many other words he pleads with them, 'Save yourselves from this untoward generation.'
That day, those who gladly receive his word are baptized, and about three thousand people are added to them.
Then Luke shows us what the first believers were like. Fear comes upon every soul, and many wonders and signs are done by the apostles. All who believe are together, and share what they have. They sell their possessions, and give to anyone who has need. They meet with one accord in the temple, and break bread in their homes, eating with gladness and singleness of heart, praising God, and having favour with all the people. 'And the Lord added to the church daily such as should be saved.'
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 21. 'And it shall come to pass, that whosoever shall call on the name of the Lord shall be saved.' Peter quotes the prophet Joel, and then shows that the Lord to call on is Jesus. Notice the word whosoever. Anyone. Any nation, any language, any past. There were people from all over the world in that crowd, and some of them had shouted for Jesus to be crucified. Even they could call on him and be saved. So can you.
Verse 38. 'Then Peter said unto them, Repent, and be baptized every one of you in the name of Jesus Christ for the remission of sins, and ye shall receive the gift of the Holy Ghost.' To repent means to turn around, to change your mind and your direction, away from sin and toward God. Remission of sins means forgiveness, sins sent away. And notice the last part: a gift. The same Holy Ghost who filled the apostles is given to everyone who turns to Jesus. He is not a reward for the best believers. He is a gift for all.
Verse 42. 'And they continued stedfastly in the apostles’ doctrine and fellowship, and in breaking of bread, and in prayers.' Doctrine means teaching. The new believers kept on in four things: learning God’s Word, sharing life together, eating together and remembering Jesus, and praying. Notice the word stedfastly. It was not a burst of excitement that faded. They kept going. The Spirit came in a moment, but the Christian life is lived day after day, together.
Joel had written, 'And it shall come to pass afterward, that I will pour out my spirit upon all flesh'. In the Old Testament, the Spirit came on a few people for special tasks: judges, kings, prophets. Moses once said, 'would God that all the LORD’S people were prophets, and that the LORD would put his spirit upon them!' At Pentecost, Moses’ wish came true.
Have you ever called on the name of the Lord? If not, you can do it now, in your own words. And if you have, look at the four things the first believers kept doing. Which one do you need to come back to?
Here is today’s question. The crowd asked, Men and brethren, what shall we do? What is God asking you to do in answer to Peter’s message today?
Let us pray. Lord Jesus, I call on your name. Thank you that whosoever calls on you shall be saved. Forgive my sins, fill me with your Holy Spirit, and keep me steadfast in your Word, in fellowship, in remembering you, and in prayer. Amen.
