A complaint is growing in the church. Some widows are being missed when food is handed out each day. The widows who speak Greek feel forgotten, while those who speak Hebrew are cared for. It is the kind of problem that could split a church in two. The apostles call everyone together, and suggest a wise way forward.
In chapter five, Ananias and Sapphira lied to God and died, and great fear came on the church. The apostles were thrown into prison, set free by an angel, and beaten by the council. But they rejoiced that they were counted worthy to suffer shame for Jesus’ name, and kept on teaching and preaching Jesus Christ.
The trouble so far has come from outside the church. In chapter six, the trouble comes from inside: a quarrel about fairness. Watch how the church solves it, and meet a man named Stephen, who will be at the centre of the next chapter too.
In those days, as the number of disciples is growing, a murmuring arises. The Grecians, Jewish believers who grew up speaking Greek in other lands, complain against the Hebrews, the believers from Judaea, because their widows are being neglected in the daily serving of food.
The twelve call the whole multitude of the disciples together. They say, 'It is not reason that we should leave the word of God, and serve tables.' It would not be right for the apostles to stop teaching God’s Word in order to run the meals. So they make a proposal.
'But we will give ourselves continually to prayer, and to the ministry of the word.'
The whole multitude is pleased with the idea. They choose seven men. Stephen, a man full of faith and of the Holy Ghost, and Philip, and Prochorus, Nicanor, Timon, Parmenas, and Nicolas, a convert from Antioch. Notice that every one of the seven has a Greek name. The church has chosen men from the group that felt left out, to make sure their widows are cared for. They set these men before the apostles, and the apostles pray and lay their hands on them.
And then Luke gives a report on how the church is doing.
Stephen, full of faith and power, does great wonders and miracles among the people. But some men rise up to argue with him, from a synagogue called the synagogue of the Libertines, freed slaves, with others from Cyrene, Alexandria, Cilicia and Asia. 'And they were not able to resist the wisdom and the spirit by which he spake.'
When they cannot win the argument, they turn to lies. They secretly persuade men to say, we have heard him speak blasphemous words against Moses, and against God. They stir up the people, the elders and the scribes. They come upon Stephen, seize him, and bring him to the council. And they set up false witnesses, who say, this man never stops speaking against this holy place and the law. We have heard him say that this Jesus of Nazareth will destroy this place, and change the customs Moses gave us.
It is the same kind of charge that was brought against Jesus at his trial. And everyone in the council turns to look at Stephen.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 3. 'Wherefore, brethren, look ye out among you seven men of honest report, full of the Holy Ghost and wisdom, whom we may appoint over this business.' Notice what the apostles look for in men who will hand out food: honest report, which means a good reputation, and full of the Holy Ghost and wisdom. Serving tables is not less spiritual than preaching. Every kind of service in God’s family needs people who are honest, wise and full of the Spirit. And notice that the church listened to the complaint, and dealt with it fairly.
Verse 7. 'And the word of God increased; and the number of the disciples multiplied in Jerusalem greatly; and a great company of the priests were obedient to the faith.' When the problem was solved well, the church grew even more. And notice who joined: a great company of priests, men who served in the very temple whose leaders opposed the apostles. Luke gives reports like this all through Acts, little summaries that say, the word keeps growing. Nothing can stop it, not threats from outside, and not quarrels from within, when they are handled with wisdom and love.
Verse 15. 'And all that sat in the council, looking stedfastly on him, saw his face as it had been the face of an angel.' Stephen is surrounded by enemies and accused by liars. And yet his face shines with peace. Long before, when Moses came down from the mountain after speaking with God, his face shone. Now Stephen, accused of speaking against Moses, has a face shining like an angel’s. God was with him, and everyone in the room could see it.
The apostles gave themselves to prayer and the word, and the seven served the widows. God had always cared for widows. The law of Moses said, 'Ye shall not afflict any widow, or fatherless child.' And a psalm calls God 'A father of the fatherless, and a judge of the widows'. Caring for widows was not a side issue. It was the heart of God.
Is there someone being overlooked around you, someone left out or forgotten? Ask God how you can help. And whatever your part is in God’s family, whether it is speaking or serving, do it full of faith and full of the Holy Ghost.
Here is today’s question. Stephen was a man full of faith and of the Holy Ghost, chosen to serve. What is one way God might be asking you to serve someone today?
Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that you see the widow and the forgotten. Make me honest, wise and full of your Holy Spirit. Show me who is being overlooked, and help me to serve them with love. And when I face opposition, give me Stephen’s peace. Amen.
