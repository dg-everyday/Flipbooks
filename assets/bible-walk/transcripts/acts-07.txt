Stones are flying. A man is on his knees outside the city wall, bleeding, and the crowd keeps throwing. At the edge of the crowd, a young man stands guard over a pile of coats that the stone throwers have taken off. He approves of what is happening. His name is Saul. And the dying man on his knees looks up and prays, not for himself, but for the people killing him.
In chapter six, the church chose seven men full of the Holy Ghost and wisdom to care for the widows. One of them, Stephen, did great wonders, and no one could resist the wisdom with which he spoke. So his enemies brought false witnesses against him, and dragged him before the council. As they all stared at him, his face was like the face of an angel.
Now the high priest asks Stephen, 'Are these things so?' Chapter seven is Stephen’s answer. It is the longest speech in the book of Acts. Instead of defending himself, Stephen tells the story of Israel. Listen for a pattern that runs all the way through it.
Stephen begins with Abraham. The God of glory appeared to our father Abraham in Mesopotamia, and told him to leave his country and go to a land God would show him. God promised that land to Abraham and to his children, when he had no child yet. Abraham believed, and went.
Then Stephen tells of Joseph. The patriarchs, Joseph’s own brothers, were jealous of him and sold him into Egypt. 'but God was with him'. God rescued Joseph, gave him wisdom, and made him governor over Egypt. And when famine came, Joseph saved the very brothers who had rejected him.
Then Stephen tells of Moses. When Israel grew into a great people in Egypt, a new king made them slaves. Moses was born in that hard time, and was raised in Pharaoh’s palace, learned in all the wisdom of Egypt. When he was grown, he went to visit his people, and defended one of them. 'For he supposed his brethren would have understood how that God by his hand would deliver them: but they understood not.' They pushed him away, saying, who made you a ruler and a judge over us? So Moses fled.
Long afterward, in the wilderness near Mount Sinai, God spoke to Moses from a burning bush. I am the God of thy fathers. I have seen the affliction of my people in Egypt, and I am come down to deliver them. Stephen says, 'This Moses whom they refused,' this same Moses God sent to be a ruler and a deliverer. He led them out with wonders and signs. And Moses himself told Israel that God would raise up a prophet like him.
Yet even then, our fathers would not obey him. In their hearts they turned back to Egypt. They made a golden calf and worshipped it. Stephen tells how they carried the tabernacle, the tent of meeting, through the wilderness, until the days of David. Solomon built God a house. 'Howbeit the most High dwelleth not in temples made with hands'. Heaven is God’s throne, and earth is his footstool.
Then Stephen turns to the council, and speaks to them directly. You stiffnecked people, you always resist the Holy Ghost. As your fathers did, so do you. Which of the prophets did your fathers not persecute? They killed those who foretold the coming of the Just One, and now you have betrayed and murdered him. You received the law given by angels, and have not kept it.
When they hear these things, they are cut to the heart, and they grind their teeth at him. But Stephen, full of the Holy Ghost, looks up, and sees something no one else in the room can see. 'Behold, I see the heavens opened, and the Son of man standing on the right hand of God.'
Then they cry out with a loud voice, cover their ears, and rush at him together. They throw him out of the city, and stone him. The witnesses lay down their clothes at the feet of a young man named Saul. And as the stones fall, Stephen prays.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 55. 'But he, being full of the Holy Ghost, looked up stedfastly into heaven, and saw the glory of God, and Jesus standing on the right hand of God,' Everywhere else in the Bible, the risen Jesus is described as seated at God’s right hand, his work complete. Here, he is standing. It is as if Jesus rose to his feet to welcome his faithful witness home. The council had condemned Stephen. But heaven stood up for him.
Verse 59. 'And they stoned Stephen, calling upon God, and saying, Lord Jesus, receive my spirit.' Do you remember the last words of Jesus on the cross, in Luke’s Gospel? Father, into thy hands I commend my spirit. Stephen dies with almost the same words, but now he prays them to Jesus. He trusts Jesus with his soul, just as Jesus trusted the Father. For those who belong to Jesus, death is not falling into darkness. It is being received by him.
Verse 60. 'And he kneeled down, and cried with a loud voice, Lord, lay not this sin to their charge. And when he had said this, he fell asleep.' Again, Stephen echoes Jesus, who prayed, Father, forgive them. Lay not this sin to their charge means, do not hold this sin against them. And then Luke says, he fell asleep. A violent death, described as gently as a child falling asleep. That is how the Bible speaks of the death of a believer, resting until the day of resurrection. And standing there, holding the coats, was Saul, who would one day become a forgiven man himself.
The pattern in Stephen’s speech is clear. Joseph was rejected by his brothers, and became their saviour. Moses was rejected by his people, and became their deliverer. Long before, Isaiah had written of the coming Servant, 'He is despised and rejected of men'. Jesus was rejected too, and he is the greatest Saviour of all.
Is there someone who has hurt you, someone you find hard to forgive? Pray Stephen’s prayer for them: Lord, lay not this sin to their charge. And if you fear death, remember that Jesus stands ready to receive the spirit of everyone who trusts in him.
Here is today’s question. Stephen prayed for the people who were stoning him. Who is one person you need to pray for, and forgive, today?
Let us pray. Lord Jesus, thank you that you stand at the right hand of God, and that you receive everyone who trusts in you. Fill me with your Holy Spirit, as you filled Stephen. Give me his courage, and his forgiving heart. Lord Jesus, receive my spirit. Amen.
