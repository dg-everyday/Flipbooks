A chariot rolls slowly along a desert road. Inside sits an important man from Ethiopia, the treasurer of a queen. He is reading aloud from a scroll of the prophet Isaiah, and he is puzzled by the words. Then a stranger comes running up beside the chariot, and asks him a simple question: do you understand what you are reading?
In chapter seven, Stephen told the story of Israel before the council. He saw Jesus standing at the right hand of God, and he was stoned to death. As he died, he prayed, Lord, lay not this sin to their charge. And a young man named Saul stood by, guarding the coats of those who threw the stones.
Now, in chapter eight, the map of Acts begins to unfold. Jesus said his followers would be his witnesses in Jerusalem, and in all Judaea, and in Samaria. The witness in Jerusalem has been given. Now, through hard times, the good news begins to travel.
Saul agrees with Stephen’s death. That same day, great trouble begins for the church in Jerusalem. All the believers, except the apostles, are scattered through the lands of Judaea and Samaria. Godly men bury Stephen, and weep greatly for him. As for Saul, he goes from house to house, takes men and women away, and puts them in prison.
But the scattering does not stop the message. It spreads it.
Philip, one of the seven men chosen to care for the widows, goes to a city of Samaria, and preaches Christ there. The Samaritans and the Jews had been enemies for hundreds of years. But the people listen gladly to Philip, and they see the miracles he does. Many who are troubled by unclean spirits are set free. Many who cannot walk are healed. 'And there was great joy in that city.'
In that city lives a man named Simon. For a long time he has amazed the people with magic tricks, and they say, this man is the great power of God. But when the people believe Philip, who preaches the kingdom of God and the name of Jesus Christ, they are baptized, both men and women. Simon himself believes too, and is baptized. He follows Philip everywhere, amazed at the signs he sees.
The apostles in Jerusalem hear that Samaria has received the word of God, so they send Peter and John. The two apostles pray for the new believers, that they might receive the Holy Ghost, because he had not yet come upon any of them. They lay their hands on them, and they receive the Holy Ghost.
When Simon sees this, he offers the apostles money. Give me this power too, he says. But Peter tells him that the gift of God cannot be bought with money. Your heart is not right with God, Peter says. Turn away from this, and pray that God will forgive you. Simon answers, pray to the Lord for me. Then Peter and John go back toward Jerusalem, preaching the good news in many Samaritan villages along the way.
Then an angel of the Lord speaks to Philip. Get up, and go south, to the road that goes down from Jerusalem to Gaza, through the desert. So Philip leaves a city full of joy, and goes out to an empty road. And there he meets the man from Ethiopia, a great officer of Candace, queen of the Ethiopians, who is in charge of all her treasure. He has come all the way to Jerusalem to worship God. Now he is going home, sitting in his chariot, reading the prophet Isaiah.
The Spirit says to Philip, 'Go near, and join thyself to this chariot.' So Philip runs to him. The officer answers, 'How can I, except some man should guide me?' And he invites Philip to come up and sit with him.
The passage he is reading is about someone who was led away like a quiet lamb, who suffered and did not open his mouth. The officer asks Philip, who is the prophet talking about? Himself, or someone else? And Philip begins to explain.
As they travel along the road, they come to some water. The officer says, 'See, here is water; what doth hinder me to be baptized?' Philip says, 'If thou believest with all thine heart, thou mayest.' And he answers, 'I believe that Jesus Christ is the Son of God.' He tells the driver to stop the chariot. They both go down into the water, and Philip baptizes him. When they come up out of the water, the Spirit of the Lord takes Philip away, and the officer does not see him again. 'and he went on his way rejoicing.' Philip appears at a town called Azotus, and preaches in all the towns along the coast, until he comes to Caesarea.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 4. 'Therefore they that were scattered abroad went every where preaching the word.' The trouble in Jerusalem was meant to destroy the church. Instead, it scattered the believers like seeds. Wherever they landed, they talked about Jesus. Notice who did the preaching. Not the apostles, who stayed in Jerusalem, but ordinary believers. God can use even our hardest seasons to spread his good news.
Verse 30. 'And Philip ran thither to him, and heard him read the prophet Esaias, and said, Understandest thou what thou readest?' Esaias is the Greek form of the name Isaiah. Notice two things. First, Philip ran. He was eager to obey the Spirit. Second, he asked a simple question. Many people today read or hear parts of the Bible, and do not understand them. Sometimes the kindest thing we can do is simply ask, and offer to help.
Verse 35. 'Then Philip opened his mouth, and began at the same scripture, and preached unto him Jesus.' Philip did not change the subject. He started right where the man was, in Isaiah chapter fifty-three, and showed him that the quiet lamb who suffered is Jesus. Any page of the Bible can lead us to Jesus, because the whole Bible points to him. Philip preached unto him Jesus. That is the heart of every witness.
The officer was reading Isaiah’s song about the suffering servant, who 'was wounded for our transgressions, he was bruised for our iniquities'. And a little further on, the same prophet promised that God’s house would be open to people from far away: 'mine house shall be called an house of prayer for all people.' On that desert road, both promises came true.
Has a hard season taken you somewhere you did not expect to be? Ask God who you can share him with there. And be ready to start where people are, with their own questions, and to lead them gently to Jesus.
Here is today’s question. Philip asked, understandest thou what thou readest? Is there someone you could help to understand the Bible, or someone who could help you?
Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that nothing can stop your word, not even hard times. Make me ready, like Philip, to run when your Spirit calls, to ask good questions, and to begin wherever people are and lead them to Jesus. Send me on my way rejoicing. Amen.
