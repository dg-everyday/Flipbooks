Peter comes home to Jerusalem, and some of the believers are waiting for him, and they are not happy. You went into the house of uncircumcised men, they say, and you ate with them. They are shocked. For a Jew, this was simply not done. Peter does not argue or get angry. He starts at the beginning, and tells them exactly what happened.
In chapter ten, God gave Peter a vision of a sheet full of unclean animals, and taught him not to call any person common or unclean. Peter went to the house of Cornelius, a Roman centurion, and preached Jesus. While he was still speaking, the Holy Ghost fell on the Gentiles, and they were baptized.
Now, in chapter eleven, the news reaches Jerusalem. The church has to decide what to think about it. And then Luke shows us a new church growing in a great city to the north, where the good news reaches Greeks.
The apostles and believers in Judaea hear that the Gentiles have also received the word of God. When Peter comes up to Jerusalem, those of the circumcision, Jewish believers who keep the law of Moses strictly, find fault with him.
So Peter explains it all to them, in order. He tells them how he was praying in the city of Joppa, and saw the sheet let down from heaven, full of animals, and heard the voice: rise, Peter; kill, and eat. He told the Lord he had never eaten anything common or unclean. And the voice answered, what God hath cleansed, that call not thou common. This happened three times.
At that very moment, three men arrived from Caesarea. The Spirit told Peter to go with them, doubting nothing. Six brothers went with him, and these six are here with him now, as witnesses. Cornelius told them how an angel had told him to send for Peter, who would tell him words by which he and all his house would be saved.
'And as I began to speak, the Holy Ghost fell on them, as on us at the beginning.' Then Peter remembered the words of the Lord Jesus, John indeed baptized with water, but you shall be baptized with the Holy Ghost. 'Forasmuch then as God gave them the like gift as he did unto us, who believed on the Lord Jesus Christ; what was I, that I could withstand God?'
When they hear these things, their complaint falls silent. They give glory to God.
Meanwhile, the believers who were scattered by the persecution after Stephen’s death have travelled as far as Phenice, Cyprus and Antioch. At first they speak the word only to Jews. But some of them, men from Cyprus and Cyrene, come to Antioch and speak to the Grecians, the Greeks, preaching the Lord Jesus. 'And the hand of the Lord was with them: and a great number believed, and turned unto the Lord.'
Antioch is one of the greatest cities of the Roman world, far to the north of Jerusalem. When news of what is happening there reaches the church in Jerusalem, they send Barnabas. Luke tells us why he was the right man: 'For he was a good man, and full of the Holy Ghost and of faith'. When Barnabas arrives and sees the grace of God at work, he is glad, and he encourages them all. And many more people are added to the Lord.
Then Barnabas does something wise. The work is growing too big for him alone. He remembers Saul, the former persecutor, who has gone home to Tarsus. So Barnabas travels to Tarsus to look for him. When he finds him, he brings him back to Antioch. For a whole year the two of them meet with the church, and teach many people. And it is in Antioch that the disciples are first called Christians.
In those days, prophets come down from Jerusalem to Antioch. One of them, named Agabus, stands up and foretells by the Spirit that there will be a great famine throughout the world. It happens, Luke says, in the days of the emperor Claudius. The disciples in Antioch decide that each of them, according to his ability, will send relief to the believers living in Judaea. And they do it, sending their gift to the elders by the hands of Barnabas and Saul.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 18. 'When they heard these things, they held their peace, and glorified God, saying, Then hath God also to the Gentiles granted repentance unto life.' The believers in Jerusalem had a choice. They could hold on to their old ideas, or they could follow what God was clearly doing. They chose to praise God. Notice the words granted repentance unto life. Even turning to God is his gift. He opened the door for the nations, and they walked through.
Verse 23. 'Who, when he came, and had seen the grace of God, was glad, and exhorted them all, that with purpose of heart they would cleave unto the Lord.' Barnabas looked at a new church full of people very different from him, and he saw the grace of God. Not problems, not differences, but grace. And he was glad. To cleave unto the Lord means to hold on tight to him, like glue. With purpose of heart means on purpose, wholeheartedly. That is good advice for every new believer, and every old one too.
Verse 26. 'And when he had found him, he brought him unto Antioch. And it came to pass, that a whole year they assembled themselves with the church, and taught much people. And the disciples were called Christians first in Antioch.' The name Christian means belonging to Christ, Christ’s people. It may first have been a nickname given by outsiders. But it stuck, because the believers in Antioch talked about Christ so much that people named them after him. And notice how they got there: Barnabas went looking for Saul, and a whole year of patient teaching followed.
The prophet Isaiah had heard God say of his Servant, 'I will also give thee for a light to the Gentiles, that thou mayest be my salvation unto the end of the earth.' In Antioch, that light reached the Greeks. And Jesus had said, 'other sheep I have, which are not of this fold'. In this chapter the church begins to understand what he meant.
Is there someone you could encourage today, as Barnabas did, someone new in faith, or someone who needs a friend to stand beside them? And think about the name you carry. Would the people around you know, from how you live and talk, that you belong to Christ?
Here is today’s question. Barnabas encouraged the new believers to cleave unto the Lord with purpose of heart. What would it look like for you to hold on to Jesus on purpose today?
Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that you have granted repentance unto life to people of every nation, even me. Help me to see your grace in others, as Barnabas did, and to be glad. Make me an encourager, and help me to cleave to you with all my heart. Amen.
