A group of church leaders is gathered in the great city of Antioch, worshipping God and fasting. They are not making plans or setting goals. They are simply seeking God together. And as they worship, the Holy Ghost speaks. He has work for two of them, and it will take them far from home. Two men are about to set out on a journey that will change the world.
In chapter twelve, King Herod killed James and put Peter in prison, but the church prayed without ceasing, and an angel set Peter free. Herod, who accepted praise as a god, died, but the word of God grew and multiplied. Barnabas and Saul came back to Antioch, bringing John Mark with them.
Now, in chapter thirteen, the story turns. From here to the end of the book, Luke follows Saul, who from this chapter on is called Paul. The good news is about to travel to the uttermost part of the earth.
In the church at Antioch there are prophets and teachers from many backgrounds, Barnabas and Saul among them. As they minister to the Lord and fast, the Holy Ghost speaks. So when they have fasted and prayed, they lay their hands on Barnabas and Saul, and send them away.
Sent out by the Holy Ghost, they sail to the island of Cyprus, where Barnabas grew up. John Mark goes with them as their helper. They preach across the whole island until they come to Paphos. There the governor, Sergius Paulus, a wise man, wants to hear the word of God. But a sorcerer named Elymas opposes them, trying to turn the governor away from the faith.
Then Saul, 'who also is called Paul,' filled with the Holy Ghost, fixes his eyes on him. You child of the devil, you enemy of all righteousness, will you not stop perverting the right ways of the Lord? Now the hand of the Lord is upon you, and you will be blind for a time. Immediately a mist and a darkness falls on Elymas, and he goes about looking for someone to lead him by the hand. When the governor sees what has happened, he believes, astonished at the teaching of the Lord.
Paul and his company sail on to the mainland. There John Mark leaves them and returns to Jerusalem. Paul and Barnabas travel inland to another city called Antioch, in the region of Pisidia. On the sabbath day they go into the synagogue and sit down. After the reading of the law and the prophets, the rulers of the synagogue send them a message: if you have any word of encouragement for the people, say it.
Paul stands up, motions with his hand, and speaks. Like Stephen, he begins with the story of Israel: how God chose their fathers, brought them out of Egypt, gave them the land, and then gave them King David, a man after his own heart.
From David’s family, Paul says, God has brought to Israel a Saviour, Jesus, as he promised. The people of Jerusalem and their rulers did not know him. They did not understand the voices of the prophets, which are read every sabbath day, and yet they fulfilled them by condemning him. They asked Pilate to put him to death, took him down from the tree, and laid him in a tomb. 'But God raised him from the dead'. He was seen for many days by those who came with him from Galilee, who are now his witnesses.
So we bring you good news, Paul says. The promise God made to the fathers, he has fulfilled for us their children, by raising up Jesus. Then Paul makes his great announcement. And by him, all who believe are justified, made right with God, from everything the law of Moses could never set you free from. So beware, he warns them, lest what the prophets spoke about comes upon you, that you see God’s work and refuse to believe it.
As they leave, the people beg them to speak again the next sabbath. Many Jews and devout converts follow Paul and Barnabas, who encourage them to continue in the grace of God.
The next sabbath, almost the whole city comes to hear the word of God. But some of the Jewish leaders, filled with envy, speak against Paul. Then Paul and Barnabas grow bold. The word had to be spoken to you first, they say, but since you push it away, 'lo, we turn to the Gentiles.'
When the Gentiles hear this, they are glad, and glorify the word of the Lord, 'and as many as were ordained to eternal life believed.' The word of the Lord spreads through the whole region. But the leaders stir up important women and the chief men of the city, raise persecution against Paul and Barnabas, and drive them out. So they shake off the dust of their feet against them, and go to Iconium. 'And the disciples were filled with joy, and with the Holy Ghost.'
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 2. 'As they ministered to the Lord, and fasted, the Holy Ghost said, Separate me Barnabas and Saul for the work whereunto I have called them.' The great missionary journeys of Paul did not begin with a strategy meeting. They began with worship. As they ministered to the Lord, seeking him, the Holy Ghost spoke. Notice too that the church sent its two best teachers. When God calls, he often asks for our very best.
Verse 38. 'Be it known unto you therefore, men and brethren, that through this man is preached unto you the forgiveness of sins:' This is the heart of Paul’s message, then and always. Through this man, Jesus, forgiveness of sins is offered. Not through keeping the law, which no one could keep perfectly. Not through being born into the right people. Through Jesus. And the next verse says it is for all that believe.
Verse 47. 'For so hath the Lord commanded us, saying, I have set thee to be a light of the Gentiles, that thou shouldest be for salvation unto the ends of the earth.' Paul quotes the prophet Isaiah. In Isaiah, those words were spoken to God’s Servant, the Christ. Now Paul says they are a command for Christ’s people too. Jesus is the light of the world. And those who follow him carry his light to the ends of the earth.
The words Paul quoted come from Isaiah chapter forty-nine. God says it is too small a thing for his Servant only to restore Israel: 'I will also give thee for a light to the Gentiles, that thou mayest be my salvation unto the end of the earth.' What God promised through Isaiah, Paul and Barnabas carried across the sea.
Make time to worship God and listen to him, as the leaders at Antioch did. He may have something to say to you. And remember that you carry the light of Jesus. Where is one dark place in your world where that light could shine today?
Here is today’s question. Through Jesus is preached unto you the forgiveness of sins. Who in your life most needs to hear that message, and how could you share it?
Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that through Jesus there is forgiveness of sins for all who believe. As I worship you, speak to me, and send me where you want me to go. Make me a light to those around me, so that your salvation reaches the ends of the earth. Amen.
