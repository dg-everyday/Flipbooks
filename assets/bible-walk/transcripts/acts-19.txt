A huge bonfire is burning in the middle of Ephesus. People are carrying armfuls of books and scrolls and throwing them into the flames. These are books of magic, spells and charms, worth a fortune. Nobody is forcing them. They are the owners of the books, and they are burning them gladly, in front of everyone, because they have come to know Jesus.
In chapter eighteen, Paul worked as a tentmaker in Corinth with Aquila and Priscilla, and the Lord told him in a vision, be not afraid, but speak. Paul stopped briefly in Ephesus and promised to return. Then Aquila and Priscilla taught a gifted preacher named Apollos the way of God more perfectly.
Now, in chapter nineteen, Paul keeps his promise and comes back to Ephesus. It is one of the greatest cities in the Roman world, home to the enormous temple of the goddess Diana. Paul stays here longer than anywhere else on his journeys, and the whole region hears the word of the Lord.
While Apollos is at Corinth, Paul passes through the inland regions and comes to Ephesus. There he finds about twelve disciples, and asks them a question. They answer that they have not so much as heard whether there is any Holy Ghost. Then into what were you baptized? Paul asks. They say, into John’s baptism. Paul explains that John baptized with the baptism of repentance, telling the people to believe on the one who would come after him, that is, on Christ Jesus. When they hear this, they are baptized in the name of the Lord Jesus. Paul lays his hands on them, the Holy Ghost comes on them, and they speak with tongues and prophesy.
Paul goes into the synagogue and speaks boldly for three months, reasoning and persuading about the kingdom of God. But when some harden their hearts and speak evil of the Way before the crowd, he leaves them, and takes the disciples with him. Every day he teaches in the school of a man named Tyrannus. This goes on for a long time, so that everyone who lives in the province of Asia, both Jews and Greeks, hears the word of the Lord Jesus.
God does special miracles by the hands of Paul. Even handkerchiefs and aprons that have touched him are carried to the sick, and diseases leave them, and evil spirits go out of them.
Then some travelling Jewish exorcists try to use the name of Jesus as a magic word. Seven sons of a man named Sceva, a chief priest, do this. They say to a man with an evil spirit, we adjure you by Jesus whom Paul preaches. But the evil spirit answers, 'Jesus I know, and Paul I know; but who are ye?' The man with the evil spirit leaps on them, overpowers them, and they flee from the house naked and wounded.
This becomes known to everyone in Ephesus, and fear falls on them all, and the name of the Lord Jesus is magnified. Many who believe come and openly confess their evil deeds. And then comes the great bonfire of magic books.
After these things, Paul plans to go through Macedonia and Achaia to Jerusalem. 'After I have been there, I must also see Rome.' He sends Timothy and Erastus ahead, while he stays in Asia for a while longer.
About that time there arises no small stir about the Way. A silversmith named Demetrius makes silver shrines of the goddess Diana, which brings great profit to his craftsmen. He calls them together and says, you know that our wealth comes from this business. And you see and hear that not only in Ephesus, but almost throughout all Asia, this Paul has persuaded many people, saying that gods made with hands are not gods at all. Our trade is in danger, and so is the temple of the great goddess Diana.
When they hear this, they are full of wrath, and cry out, 'Great is Diana of the Ephesians.' The whole city is filled with confusion. They seize two of Paul’s travelling companions, Gaius and Aristarchus, and rush into the great theatre. Paul wants to go in to the people, but the disciples will not let him, and some city officials who are his friends beg him not to go into the theatre.
Inside, some cry one thing and some another. The assembly is confused, and most of them do not even know why they have come together. For about two hours they all shout with one voice, great is Diana of the Ephesians. At last the town clerk quietens the crowd. Everyone knows Ephesus is the guardian of the temple of Diana, he says. You have brought these men here, who are neither robbers of temples nor blasphemers of your goddess. If Demetrius has a complaint, the courts are open. We are in danger of being called to account for this uproar. And with that, he dismisses the assembly.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 2. 'He said unto them, Have ye received the Holy Ghost since ye believed? And they said unto him, We have not so much as heard whether there be any Holy Ghost.' These disciples had heard John the Baptist’s message, and had turned from sin. But they had not heard the whole story of Jesus, or of the Spirit he sends. Paul did not scold them. He gently filled in what was missing. The Holy Ghost is not an extra for a few believers. He is God’s gift to every follower of Jesus.
Verse 19. 'Many of them also which used curious arts brought their books together, and burned them before all men: and they counted the price of them, and found it fifty thousand pieces of silver.' Curious arts means magic. Fifty thousand pieces of silver was a huge fortune. Following Jesus cost these new believers something real. They did not sell their books to someone else, or hide them away. They destroyed them, so that no one else would be harmed. When Jesus comes into a life, the old things that held us must go.
Verse 20. 'So mightily grew the word of God and prevailed.' To prevail means to win, to overcome. In Ephesus the word of God came up against magic, money, idols, and a rioting city. And it prevailed. Not by force or violence, but by changed lives. This is another of Luke’s short reports, and it tells us again: nothing can stop the word of God.
Long before, God told Israel through Moses not to practise magic or enchantments, for 'all that do these things are an abomination unto the LORD'. And the prophet Isaiah mocked idols made by craftsmen: 'They have not known nor understood'. In Ephesus, people turned from both, to the living God.
Is there anything in your life that you need to let go of to follow Jesus fully, something that has a hold on you? Ask God for the courage to give it up, as the believers in Ephesus did. And take heart. The word of God still grows mightily and prevails.
Here is today’s question. The believers at Ephesus burned their books of magic to follow Jesus. What is one thing God might be asking you to let go of today?
Let us pray. Lord Jesus, your name is above every name, and your word prevails. Fill me with your Holy Spirit. Show me anything in my life that does not belong to you, and give me the courage to let it go. Let your word grow mightily in me. Amen.
