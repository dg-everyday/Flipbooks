A group of grown men are weeping on a beach. They are leaders of the church in Ephesus, and they have walked a long way to meet Paul here, by the sea. Paul has just told them that they will never see his face again. They kneel down together on the shore and pray. Then they throw their arms around his neck and kiss him, and walk with him to the ship.
In chapter nineteen, Paul spent a long time in Ephesus. The word of the Lord spread through the whole province, books of magic were burned, and the city rioted for its goddess Diana. Paul had decided by the Spirit to go to Jerusalem, and afterward, to see Rome.
Now, in chapter twenty, Paul begins that journey. Luke is travelling with him again, so the chapter says we. And at its heart is the only speech in Acts that Paul gives to believers, a farewell to the elders of the church he loved.
After the uproar has ended, Paul calls the disciples together, encourages them, says goodbye, and leaves for Macedonia. He travels through those regions, encouraging the believers with many words, and comes to Greece, where he stays three months. When some plot against him as he is about to sail for Syria, he decides to go back through Macedonia instead. Several men travel with him, from different churches: Sopater of Berea, Aristarchus and Secundus of Thessalonica, Gaius of Derbe, Timothy, and Tychicus and Trophimus of Asia. Luke and Paul sail from Philippi and join them at Troas.
The disciples at Troas come together to break bread, and Paul speaks to them. Because he is leaving the next day, he keeps on talking for a very long time. There are many lights in the upper room where they are gathered. A young man named Eutychus is sitting in a window. As Paul talks on and on, Eutychus sinks into a deep sleep, and falls from the third storey to the ground, and is taken up dead.
Paul goes down, falls on him, embraces him, and says, 'Trouble not yourselves; for his life is in him.' Then Paul goes back up, breaks bread, eats, and keeps talking until first light. And they bring the young man home alive, and are greatly comforted.
The group sails on, down the coast. Paul is in a hurry to reach Jerusalem, if possible, for the day of Pentecost. So he does not stop at Ephesus. Instead, from the port of Miletus, he sends for the elders of the church at Ephesus to come and meet him.
When they arrive, he speaks to them. You know how I lived among you the whole time, he says, serving the Lord with all humility, with many tears and trials. I held back nothing that was helpful to you, but taught you publicly and from house to house, testifying to Jews and Greeks about repentance toward God and faith toward our Lord Jesus Christ.
And now, Paul says, I am going to Jerusalem, bound in the spirit, not knowing what will happen to me there, except that the Holy Ghost warns me in every city that chains and afflictions are waiting for me.
I know that none of you, among whom I have gone preaching the kingdom of God, will see my face again. So I declare to you today that I am innocent of the blood of all men. For I have not shunned to declare unto you all the counsel of God. 'Take heed therefore unto yourselves, and to all the flock, over the which the Holy Ghost hath made you overseers, to feed the church of God, which he hath purchased with his own blood.' Watch, Paul says. After I leave, fierce wolves will come in among you, not sparing the flock. Even from among yourselves, men will rise up speaking twisted things, to draw disciples after them. So remember that for a long time I did not stop warning everyone, with tears.
Paul reminds them that he never wanted anyone’s silver, or gold, or clothing. With his own hands he worked to supply his own needs, and the needs of those with him.
When he has said these things, he kneels down and prays with them all. They all weep, and embrace Paul, and kiss him, sorrowing most of all for the words he spoke, that they would see his face no more. And they go with him to the ship.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 24. 'But none of these things move me, neither count I my life dear unto myself, so that I might finish my course with joy, and the ministry, which I have received of the Lord Jesus, to testify the gospel of the grace of God.' Paul knew chains were waiting for him. But none of these things move me, he said. A course is a race. Paul’s goal was not to live as long or as comfortably as possible, but to finish his race with joy, and to complete the work Jesus gave him. Every follower of Jesus has a course to run. The question is not how long, but how faithfully.
Verse 32. 'And now, brethren, I commend you to God, and to the word of his grace, which is able to build you up, and to give you an inheritance among all them which are sanctified.' To commend means to entrust. Paul was leaving, but he was not leaving them alone. He handed them over to God, and to God’s Word. Notice what the Word can do: build you up, and give you an inheritance. Teachers come and go. But God and his Word remain.
Verse 35. 'I have shewed you all things, how that so labouring ye ought to support the weak, and to remember the words of the Lord Jesus, how he said, It is more blessed to give than to receive.' These words of Jesus are not found in any of the four Gospels. Paul remembered them, and Luke wrote them down here. It is more blessed to give than to receive. Paul lived it, working with his own hands to help others. The world says happiness comes from getting. Jesus says the deeper joy is in giving.
Paul wrote near the end of his life, in a letter to Timothy, 'I have fought a good fight, I have finished my course, I have kept the faith'. What he hoped for on the beach at Miletus, he was able to say at the end. He ran his race to the finish.
Think about the course God has given you, your work, your family, your part in his kingdom. Ask him for joy in running it faithfully. And look for one way to give today, not to get, and see if Jesus’ words are true: it is more blessed to give than to receive.
Here is today’s question. Paul wanted to finish his course with joy. What would it look like for you to run your race faithfully today?
Let us pray. Lord Jesus, I want to finish my course with joy. Help me not to count my life dear, but to live for you. Build me up through your Word, and make me generous, remembering your words, it is more blessed to give than to receive. Amen.
