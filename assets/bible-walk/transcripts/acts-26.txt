A grand hall is filled with important people: a king and his sister in royal robes, a Roman governor, army commanders, and the leading men of the city. In the middle stands a prisoner in chains. He is the only person in the room with nothing to lose. And as he finishes speaking, the king says something surprising: almost thou persuadest me to be a Christian.
In chapter twenty-five, the new governor Festus wanted to send Paul back to Jerusalem for trial, and Paul appealed unto Caesar. Then King Agrippa came to visit, and Festus told him about the prisoner who said that Jesus, who was dead, is alive. Agrippa said, I would also hear the man myself.
Now, in chapter twenty-six, Paul speaks before King Agrippa. It is the third time Acts tells the story of how Jesus met Paul on the road. And this time Paul adds new details about the task Jesus gave him. Listen for Paul’s heart for the people in front of him.
Agrippa says to Paul, 'Thou art permitted to speak for thyself.' Paul stretches out his hand, and answers.
I think myself happy, King Agrippa, that I shall answer for myself today before you, especially because I know you are an expert in all the customs and questions of the Jews. So I beg you to hear me patiently.
All the Jews know how I lived from my youth, Paul says. I lived as a Pharisee, the strictest sect of our religion. And now I stand here on trial for the hope of the promise God made to our fathers, the hope our twelve tribes serve God continually to reach. 'Why should it be thought a thing incredible with you, that God should raise the dead?'
I myself once thought I ought to do many things against the name of Jesus of Nazareth. And I did them in Jerusalem. I shut many of the saints up in prison, with authority from the chief priests. When they were put to death, I gave my voice against them. I punished them often in every synagogue, and tried to force them to blaspheme. I was so furious against them that I hunted them even to foreign cities.
As I was going to Damascus, Paul says, at midday, O king, I saw on the road a light from heaven, brighter than the sun, shining around me and those travelling with me. We all fell to the earth, and I heard a voice speaking to me in the Hebrew tongue: Saul, Saul, why persecutest thou me? It is hard for thee to kick against the pricks. The pricks were sharp goads used to drive an ox. An ox that kicks against them only hurts itself. And I said, who art thou, Lord? And he said, I am Jesus whom thou persecutest.
But rise, Jesus said, and stand upon thy feet. I have appeared to you to make you a minister and a witness, both of what you have seen, and of what I will show you. I will deliver you from the people, and from the Gentiles, to whom I now send you. And then Jesus told him why.
And Paul tells the king what he did next. First in Damascus, then in Jerusalem, through all Judaea, and then to the Gentiles, I preached that they should repent and turn to God, and do works that show their repentance. That is why the Jews seized me in the temple and tried to kill me. But with God’s help I continue to this day, witnessing to small and great, saying nothing but what Moses and the prophets said would happen: that Christ should suffer, that he should be the first to rise from the dead, and that he should show light to the people, and to the Gentiles.
As Paul is saying this, Festus says with a loud voice, 'Paul, thou art beside thyself; much learning doth make thee mad.' But Paul says, I am not mad, most noble Festus; I speak the words of truth and soberness. The king knows about these things, and I speak freely to him, for none of these things are hidden from him; this thing was not done in a corner.
Then Paul turns to the king. 'King Agrippa, believest thou the prophets? I know that thou believest.' And Agrippa answers.
Paul replies, 'I would to God, that not only thou, but also all that hear me this day, were both almost, and altogether such as I am, except these bonds.' I wish that you and everyone here were just like me, except for these chains.
The king rises, with the governor, Bernice, and those who sat with them. As they leave, they talk among themselves, saying, this man does nothing worthy of death or of chains. And Agrippa says to Festus, this man might have been set free, if he had not appealed unto Caesar.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 18. 'To open their eyes, and to turn them from darkness to light, and from the power of Satan unto God, that they may receive forgiveness of sins, and inheritance among them which are sanctified by faith that is in me.' This is the task Jesus gave Paul, and it is a beautiful summary of the gospel. Opened eyes. Turned from darkness to light, from Satan’s power to God. Forgiveness of sins. An inheritance among God’s people. And all of it by faith in Jesus. Paul, who was struck blind on the road, was sent to open others’ eyes.
Verse 19. 'Whereupon, O king Agrippa, I was not disobedient unto the heavenly vision:' Paul had seen the risen Jesus, and he obeyed. It cost him his reputation, his comfort, his freedom, and in the end his life. But he never turned back. You may never see a light from heaven. But Jesus has spoken to you through his Word. The question for each of us is the same: will I obey what he has shown me?
Verse 28. 'Then Agrippa said unto Paul, Almost thou persuadest me to be a Christian.' Agrippa was close, so close. He knew the prophets. He had heard the gospel clearly from Paul himself. But almost is not the same as altogether. We do not know whether Agrippa ever believed. His words are a warning to every listener. It is possible to be almost persuaded, and still to walk away.
Jesus sent Paul to turn people from darkness to light. Long before, Isaiah wrote, 'The people that walked in darkness have seen a great light'. And Jesus said, 'I am the light of the world: he that followeth me shall not walk in darkness, but shall have the light of life.'
Is there anything God has clearly shown you that you have not yet obeyed? Do not stay almost persuaded. Say yes to him altogether. And think of someone who still walks in darkness. Pray that God will open their eyes and turn them to his light.
Here is today’s question. Agrippa said, almost thou persuadest me to be a Christian. Is there any area of your life where you are almost, but not altogether, following Jesus?
Let us pray. Lord Jesus, thank you for turning me from darkness to light, and for the forgiveness of my sins. I do not want to be almost yours, but altogether yours. Help me never to be disobedient to what you have shown me, and open the eyes of those I love. Amen.
