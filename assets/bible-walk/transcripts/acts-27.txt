For many days, the sky has been black. No sun, no stars, only wind and waves. The ship is breaking apart. The sailors have thrown the cargo overboard, and then even the ship’s tackle. Two hundred and seventy-six people are on board, and they have given up all hope of being saved. Then one man, a prisoner in chains, stands up in the middle of them and says, be of good cheer.
In chapter twenty-six, Paul told his story before King Agrippa, who said, almost thou persuadest me to be a Christian. Agrippa and Festus agreed that Paul had done nothing worthy of death. But because Paul had appealed unto Caesar, he had to be sent to Rome.
Now, in chapter twenty-seven, Paul sets sail. Luke is with him again, and this is one of the most vivid sea stories in any ancient book. Remember the promise the Lord gave Paul: you must bear witness also at Rome. The storm is about to put that promise to the test.
Paul and some other prisoners are handed over to a centurion named Julius, of the emperor’s band of soldiers. Aristarchus, a believer from Thessalonica, comes with them. At Sidon, Julius treats Paul kindly, and lets him visit his friends and be cared for. At Myra they change to a ship from Alexandria, sailing for Italy, full of grain.
The winds are against them. They sail slowly, with great difficulty, and come to a place called the Fair Havens, on the island of Crete. Much time has passed, and sailing is now dangerous, because the season of storms has come. So Paul warns them: sirs, I can see that this voyage will bring harm and much loss, not only to the cargo and the ship, but also to our lives. But the centurion believes the captain and the owner of the ship more than Paul. And because the harbour is not good for spending the winter, most of them decide to sail on, hoping to reach a better harbour further along the coast.
When a gentle south wind begins to blow, they think they have what they want, and they sail close along Crete. But not long after, a violent wind called Euroclydon rushes down on them. The ship is caught, and they let her drive before the wind. They pass ropes under the ship to hold her together, throw cargo overboard, and then the ship’s tackle with their own hands. 'And when neither sun nor stars in many days appeared, and no small tempest lay on us, all hope that we should be saved was then taken away.'
After they have gone a long time without food, Paul stands up in the middle of them. Sirs, you should have listened to me, and not sailed from Crete. But now I urge you, be of good cheer, for there will be no loss of any man’s life among you, only of the ship. Then he tells them why.
The angel said, fear not, Paul; you must be brought before Caesar. And God has given you all those who sail with you. Then Paul speaks the words of a man who trusts God completely. And he adds, however, we must run aground on a certain island.
On the fourteenth day of the storm, as they are driven across the sea, the sailors sense they are near land. Fearing the rocks, they drop four anchors, and long for the light. The sailors try to escape in the lifeboat. But Paul tells the centurion and the soldiers, unless these men stay in the ship, you cannot be saved. So the soldiers cut the ropes of the lifeboat, and let it fall away.
As the light begins to come, Paul urges them all to eat. Today is the fourteenth day you have gone without food, he says. Please eat, for your health. 'for there shall not an hair fall from the head of any of you.' Then he takes bread, gives thanks to God in front of them all, breaks it, and begins to eat. They all take heart, and eat too. Then they lighten the ship further by throwing the wheat into the sea.
When it is light, they do not recognise the land, but they see a bay with a beach, and decide to run the ship onto it if they can. They cut the anchors, loosen the rudders, raise the foresail, and head for the beach. But they strike a place where two seas meet, and the ship runs aground. The front sticks fast and will not move, and the back is broken to pieces by the violence of the waves.
The soldiers plan to kill the prisoners, so that none can swim away and escape. But the centurion, wanting to save Paul, stops them. He commands those who can swim to jump overboard first and get to land, and the rest to follow.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 23. 'For there stood by me this night the angel of God, whose I am, and whom I serve,' Look at how Paul describes God to a ship full of pagan sailors and soldiers: the God whose I am, and whom I serve. Not just the God I believe in, but the God I belong to. Paul was a prisoner of Rome. But he belonged to God. When you know whose you are, you can stand up in any storm.
Verse 25. 'Wherefore, sirs, be of good cheer: for I believe God, that it shall be even as it was told me.' This is faith in its simplest form. I believe God. Paul did not believe because the storm was calming. It was not. He believed because God had spoken. Faith is not pretending the storm is not real. Faith is trusting what God has said, even in the middle of the storm.
Verse 44. 'And the rest, some on boards, and some on broken pieces of the ship. And so it came to pass, that they escaped all safe to land.' Every single person was saved, just as God had promised. But notice how. Not in a grand rescue, but clinging to planks and broken pieces of wood. God kept his promise. He does not always rescue us in the way we expect. Sometimes we reach the shore on broken pieces. But we reach it.
Jonah was once on a ship in a great storm, running away from God, and the sailors threw him overboard to save themselves. Paul was on a ship in a great storm, obeying God, and his presence saved everyone on board. And a psalm says of sailors in a storm, 'He maketh the storm a calm, so that the waves thereof are still.'
Are you in a storm right now, with no sun or stars in sight? Hold on to what God has said in his Word, and say with Paul, I believe God. And remember whose you are. You belong to him. Even if you reach the shore on broken pieces, he will bring you safe to land.
Here is today’s question. Paul said, I believe God, that it shall be even as it was told me. What promise of God will you hold on to in your storm today?
Let us pray. Lord God, I belong to you, and I want to serve you. When the storms come, and there is no sun or stars, help me to say with Paul, I believe God. Thank you that you keep your promises, and that you will bring me safe to land. Amen.
