About a hundred and twenty people are gathered in one place in Jerusalem, praying and waiting, just as Jesus told them to. Suddenly a sound comes from heaven, like a rushing mighty wind, and fills the whole house. Something like flames of fire appears and rests on each one of them. And they begin to speak in languages they have never learned.
In Luke chapter twenty-four, Jesus rose from the dead, opened the Scriptures to his disciples, and told them to wait in Jerusalem until they were clothed with power from on high. Then he was carried up into heaven. The book of Acts was written by Luke too, as the second part of his story. It begins with Jesus telling them, you will receive power when the Holy Ghost comes on you, and you will be my witnesses in Jerusalem, and in all Judaea, and in Samaria, and to the ends of the earth.
So they waited, praying together. Then came the day of Pentecost, a harvest feast when Jews from many nations came to Jerusalem. In The Big Picture, this is the day the promise of the Spirit was kept, and the good news began to go out to the world.
When the day of Pentecost comes, they are all together in one place. The sound like a rushing mighty wind fills the house, and tongues like fire sit on each of them. They are all filled with the Holy Ghost, and begin to speak in other languages.
Jerusalem is full of devout Jews from every nation under heaven. When they hear the sound, a crowd gathers. And they are amazed, because each person hears the disciples speaking in his own language. Are not all these men from Galilee? How is it that we hear them in the language we were born with? People from Parthia and Mesopotamia, Egypt and Libya, Rome, Crete and Arabia: 'we do hear them speak in our tongues the wonderful works of God.' Some are amazed. Others mock: 'These men are full of new wine.'
Then Peter stands up. This is the same Peter who, only weeks before, swore three times that he did not know Jesus. Now he raises his voice before thousands. These men are not drunk, he says, 'seeing it is but the third hour of the day.' This is what the prophet Joel said would happen: God will pour out his Spirit on all flesh, on sons and daughters, young and old. 'And it shall come to pass, that whosoever shall call on the name of the Lord shall be saved.'
Then Peter preaches Jesus. Jesus of Nazareth, he says, a man approved by God through miracles and wonders, you crucified and killed. But God raised him up, because 'it was not possible that he should be holden of it.' Death could not hold him. Peter quotes King David’s psalms, and shows that David was speaking about the Christ, who would rise from the dead and sit at God’s right hand. We are all witnesses that God raised him up.
When the crowd hears this, they are cut to the heart. 'Men and brethren, what shall we do?' Peter answers, 'Repent, and be baptized every one of you in the name of Jesus Christ for the remission of sins, and ye shall receive the gift of the Holy Ghost.' The promise is for you, he says, and for your children, and for all who are far off, as many as the Lord our God shall call.
That day about three thousand people believe and are baptized. And then Luke describes the first church. They devote themselves to the apostles’ teaching, to fellowship, to breaking bread and to prayers. Wonders are done by the apostles. The believers share what they have, selling possessions to give to anyone in need. Every day they meet in the temple, and eat together in their homes with gladness and simple hearts, praising God. And the chapter ends, 'And the Lord added to the church daily such as should be saved.'
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 4. 'And they were all filled with the Holy Ghost, and began to speak with other tongues, as the Spirit gave them utterance.' Notice the word all. Not just the apostles, but all of them. The Holy Ghost is the Spirit of God, the third person of the one God, Father, Son and Spirit. In the Old Testament, the Spirit came on special people for special tasks. Now God pours out his Spirit on all his people, so that they can know him and tell others about Jesus.
Verse 36. 'Therefore let all the house of Israel know assuredly, that God hath made that same Jesus, whom ye have crucified, both Lord and Christ.' Peter’s sermon comes to its point. The one they rejected, God has raised and made Lord, the ruler of all, and Christ, the promised King. The throne promised to David is filled. And the question for everyone who hears it, then and now, is the same: what will you do with Jesus?
Verse 42. 'And they continued stedfastly in the apostles’ doctrine and fellowship, and in breaking of bread, and in prayers.' Doctrine means teaching. The first believers did four things, and they kept doing them steadfastly: they learned God’s Word, they shared their lives together, they ate together and remembered Jesus, and they prayed. The Christian life was never meant to be lived alone.
In Genesis chapter eleven, at the tower of Babel, God confused people’s languages and scattered them across the earth. At Pentecost, people of many languages heard one message and were gathered together. And through the prophet Ezekiel, God had promised, 'And I will put my spirit within you, and cause you to walk in my statutes'. The new covenant, with God’s law written on the heart by his Spirit, had begun.
The same Spirit who filled the disciples is given to everyone who turns to Jesus. Ask God to fill you and make you bold to speak of him. And look at the four things the first believers did. Which one is missing from your life right now?
Here is today’s question. The first believers continued in God’s Word, fellowship, breaking bread and prayer. Which of these will you give yourself to today?
Let us pray. Lord God, thank you for pouring out your Spirit, and for raising Jesus to be Lord and Christ. Fill me with your Spirit, make me bold to speak of Jesus, and keep me steadfast in your Word, in fellowship, and in prayer. Amen.
