An old man stands before a whole nation. He is a hundred and twenty years old, and he knows he will not go with them into the land they are about to enter. He has led them for forty years. Now he has one last chance to tell them what matters most. And he begins with two words that Jewish families have repeated ever since: 'Hear, O Israel'.
In Exodus chapter twenty, God gave his commandments at Mount Sinai. There, too, he told Moses to build the tabernacle, a tent of worship, so that God could live in the middle of his people as they travelled.
But when they reached the edge of the promised land, they sent spies ahead. The spies came back with fruit so large two men carried one cluster of grapes. They also brought fear. The cities are walled, they said, and the people are giants. And the whole nation refused to go in. So God told them that generation would wander in the wilderness for forty years, until their children grew up to take the land.
Now the forty years are over. The people are camped on the plains of Moab, just across the river Jordan from the land. And Moses preaches to the new generation. The book of Deuteronomy, whose name means the second law, is his sermon. In The Big Picture, chapter six is its heart.
Moses tells them that these are the commandments the LORD has given him to teach them, so that they will keep them in the land, and fear the LORD all their days, they and their children and their grandchildren.
Then he says the great words: 'Hear, O Israel: The LORD our God is one LORD:' And the great command follows.
These words, Moses says, must be in your heart. Teach them carefully to your children. Talk about them everywhere, all through the day. Bind them as a sign on your hand, and as frontlets between your eyes. Write them on the doorposts of your house and on your gates. God’s word is not only for the place of worship. It belongs at home, at the table, on the road, and at the door.
Then Moses gives a warning. When the LORD brings you into the land he promised to Abraham, Isaac and Jacob, you will find great cities you did not build, houses full of good things you did not fill, wells you did not dig, and vineyards and olive trees you did not plant. You will eat and be full. And then, Moses says, 'beware lest thou forget the LORD, which brought thee forth out of the land of Egypt, from the house of bondage.'
The danger is not only in hard times. Sometimes the greatest danger comes when life is good, and we no longer feel that we need God.
So fear the LORD, serve him, and do not go after the gods of the people around you. Do not test the LORD, as you tested him in the wilderness. Do what is right and good in his sight.
And Moses ends with a picture of family life. One day your son will ask you, what do all these commandments mean? And you are to tell him the story. 'We were Pharaoh’s bondmen in Egypt; and the LORD brought us out of Egypt with a mighty hand'. Tell him what God has done. The commandments only make sense inside the story of God’s rescue.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 4. 'Hear, O Israel: The LORD our God is one LORD:' Every nation around Israel worshipped many gods: a god of rain, a god of war, a god of the harvest. Israel was to know there is only one God, and he is the LORD who rescued them. There is no other to turn to, no one else to trust, and no rival for our hearts. Jewish families have spoken these words for thousands of years, and Jesus himself called them the beginning of the greatest commandment.
Verse 5. 'And thou shalt love the LORD thy God with all thine heart, and with all thy soul, and with all thy might.' God does not first ask for sacrifices, or rituals, or perfect behaviour. He asks for love. And not a part of our love, but all of it: heart, soul and strength. Love for God is not only a feeling. It is a whole life turned toward him. We can love him like this because he loved us first and brought us out of slavery.
Verse 7. 'And thou shalt teach them diligently unto thy children, and shalt talk of them when thou sittest in thine house, and when thou walkest by the way, and when thou liest down, and when thou risest up.' Faith is passed on not mainly in classrooms, but in ordinary life: at the table, on a walk, at bedtime, at the start of the day. Parents, grandparents, aunts and uncles: your children learn most about God by watching how you talk about him in everyday moments.
After his baptism, Jesus went into the wilderness for forty days, and the devil tempted him. Israel had failed in the wilderness for forty years. Jesus answered every temptation with words from Deuteronomy. When the devil offered him all the kingdoms of the world if he would worship him, Jesus said, 'Thou shalt worship the Lord thy God, and him only shalt thou serve.' Where Israel failed, Jesus stood firm, for us.
Is life comfortable right now? Beware lest you forget the LORD. Take a moment to remember what he brought you out of. And speak of him in an ordinary moment today, with your family, a friend, or a child.
Here is today’s question. Moses said, talk of them when thou sittest in thine house, and when thou walkest by the way. In what ordinary moment will you speak of God today?
Let us pray. Hear, O LORD, my prayer. You are the one true God. I want to love you with all my heart, all my soul, and all my strength. Keep me from forgetting you when life is good. Help me to pass on your word to the people I love. Amen.
