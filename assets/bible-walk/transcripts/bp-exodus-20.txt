A whole nation stands at the foot of a mountain. The mountain is wrapped in smoke, like a furnace. The ground shakes. Thunder rolls, lightning flashes, and a trumpet blast grows louder and louder, though no one can see who is blowing it. And then, out of the fire and the cloud, God speaks.
In Exodus chapter fourteen, God brought his people through the Red Sea and saved them from Pharaoh’s army. In the desert he fed them with bread from heaven, called manna, and gave them water from a rock. Three months after leaving Egypt, they reached Mount Sinai.
There God told Moses to say to the people, if you will obey my voice and keep my covenant, 'ye shall be a peculiar treasure unto me above all people'. Peculiar here means special, belonging to God. And the people answered together, 'All that the LORD hath spoken we will do.' God had rescued them. Now he would show them how a rescued people should live. In The Big Picture, this is the giving of the law.
God speaks these words. He begins not with a rule, but with a reminder of who he is and what he has done. Then come the commandments.
'Thou shalt have no other gods before me.' You shall not make any graven image to bow down to and serve. 'Thou shalt not take the name of the LORD thy God in vain', that is, use his name lightly or falsely. 'Remember the sabbath day, to keep it holy.' Six days you shall work, but the seventh is a day of rest to the LORD, for in six days the LORD made heaven and earth, and rested on the seventh. The day of rest was God’s gift, a time to stop working and remember that their lives were in his hands.
'Honour thy father and thy mother'. 'Thou shalt not kill.' 'Thou shalt not commit adultery.' 'Thou shalt not steal.' 'Thou shalt not bear false witness against thy neighbour.' And you shall not covet, or long to have, your neighbour’s house, or anything that is your neighbour’s.
Christians number and group these commandments in slightly different ways, but all agree on what they say. The first ones are about loving God. The rest are about loving the people around us.
When the people see the thunder and the lightning, hear the trumpet, and see the mountain smoking, they tremble and stand far off. They say to Moses, 'Speak thou with us, and we will hear: but let not God speak with us, lest we die.' They are terrified of the voice of God.
Moses answers them. And while the people stand far off, Moses goes near to 'the thick darkness where God was.'
The chapter ends with God telling Moses how they should worship him: not with idols of silver or gold, but at a simple altar of earth, where God promises, 'I will come unto thee, and I will bless thee.'
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 2. 'I am the LORD thy God, which have brought thee out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of bondage.' This is the most important verse of the chapter, and it comes before any of the commandments. God did not say, keep these rules and I will rescue you. He said, I have already rescued you; now live like it. Grace comes first, and obedience is the thankful answer to it. That is true for us too. We do not obey God to earn his love. We obey because he has already loved us.
Verse 3. 'Thou shalt have no other gods before me.' This is first because everything else flows from it. Whatever we love most, trust most, and live for most is our god. It could be money, success, a person, or our own comfort. God made us and saved us, and he will not share first place in our hearts with anything else. When God is first, every other part of life falls into its right place.
Verse 20. 'And Moses said unto the people, Fear not: for God is come to prove you, and that his fear may be before your faces, that ye sin not.' Fear not, and yet let his fear be before your faces. There are two kinds of fear here. One is terror that runs away from God. The other is a deep awe and respect for God that keeps us close to him and away from sin. God does not want us to be scared of him like a slave. He wants us to honour him like a child who loves his father.
Long after, Paul summed up the commandments about our neighbour in one sentence: 'Love worketh no ill to his neighbour: therefore love is the fulfilling of the law.' And Jesus said the whole law hangs on two commandments: love God with all your heart, and love your neighbour as yourself. The law shows us how God’s people should live. It also shows us how far short we fall, and why we need a Saviour.
Read through the commandments slowly in your mind. Which one is hardest for you right now? Do not try harder to earn God’s favour. Remember first what he has done for you, how he has carried you and forgiven you, and then ask him to help you love him and your neighbour today.
Here is today’s question. God said, I am the LORD thy God, which have brought thee out. Remembering what God has done for you, which commandment will you ask him to help you keep today?
Let us pray. LORD my God, you rescued me before you asked anything of me. Be first in my heart, above every other love. Teach me to honour you with awe and to love my neighbour as myself. Where I fall short, thank you for your mercy. Amen.
