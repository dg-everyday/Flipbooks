A man and a woman are hiding among the trees of a garden. They have never hidden from anyone before. Then they hear the sound of the LORD God 'walking in the garden in the cool of the day', and they press themselves deeper into the shadows. And God calls out a question that echoes through the whole rest of the Bible: 'Where art thou?'
In Genesis chapter one, God made a very good world, and made people in his own image. Chapter two tells it more closely. God formed the man, Adam, from the dust of the ground and breathed into him the breath of life. He planted a garden in Eden and put the man there to tend it. Every tree was his to enjoy except one, 'the tree of the knowledge of good and evil'. God said, 'in the day that thou eatest thereof thou shalt surely die.' Then God made a woman from the man’s side, and brought her to him.
Now, in chapter three, comes the turn that explains why our world is broken. In The Big Picture, this is the fall.
The serpent was 'more subtil than any beast of the field', which means more cunning. He comes to the woman with a question: 'Yea, hath God said, Ye shall not eat of every tree of the garden?' He makes God sound mean, as if God had forbidden everything. The woman answers that they may eat of all the trees except the one in the middle of the garden, or they will die.
Then the serpent says it plainly: 'Ye shall not surely die'. God knows that when you eat it, your eyes will be opened, 'and ye shall be as gods, knowing good and evil.' He is saying, God is holding out on you. You do not need him. You can be like him on your own.
The woman looks at the tree. It looks good to eat, beautiful to see, and it promises wisdom. She takes the fruit and eats, and gives some to her husband, who is with her, and he eats too.
Their eyes are opened, but not the way they hoped. For the first time they feel shame. They sew fig leaves together to cover themselves. And when they hear God walking in the garden, they hide.
God calls, and Adam answers from among the trees: 'I heard thy voice in the garden, and I was afraid, because I was naked; and I hid myself.' God asks if he has eaten from the tree. And Adam blames the woman, and even God: 'The woman whom thou gavest to be with me', she gave it to me. The woman blames the serpent: 'The serpent beguiled me, and I did eat.'
Then God speaks judgment. The serpent will crawl on its belly. The woman will bring forth children in pain. The ground will grow thorns and thistles, and the man will work it by the sweat of his face, until he returns to the ground: 'for dust thou art, and unto dust shalt thou return.' Death has come into the world.
Yet even here there is kindness. Adam names his wife Eve, because she would be the mother of all living. And God himself makes them coats of skins, and clothes them. Then he sends them out of the garden, and places cherubims and a flaming sword to guard the way to the tree of life.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 6. 'And when the woman saw that the tree was good for food, and that it was pleasant to the eyes, and a tree to be desired to make one wise, she took of the fruit thereof, and did eat, and gave also unto her husband with her; and he did eat.' Notice how temptation works. It looks good, it looks beautiful, it promises something we want. Sin almost never looks ugly at first. The real question was not about fruit. It was whether to trust God’s word, or to decide good and evil for themselves. That same choice comes to every one of us.
Verse 9. 'And the LORD God called unto Adam, and said unto him, Where art thou?' God knew where Adam was. This was not a question for God’s sake, but for Adam’s. It was an invitation to come out of hiding and be honest. Notice too who goes looking for whom. Adam ran away; God came seeking. That is the whole Bible in one verse. From this moment on, the story is about God seeking people who are hiding from him.
Verse 15. 'And I will put enmity between thee and the woman, and between thy seed and her seed; it shall bruise thy head, and thou shalt bruise his heel.' Enmity means war. In the middle of the judgment, God makes a promise. One day a child of the woman would come who would crush the serpent’s head, though he himself would be wounded. Christians have long seen this as the first hint of the good news, the first promise of a Saviour. The rest of the Bible is the story of how God kept it.
Long after, Paul wrote, 'For as in Adam all die, even so in Christ shall all be made alive.' Through one man, sin and death came into the world. Through another man, Jesus, life comes back. And at the very end of the Bible, in the city of God, the tree of life appears again, and the way to it is open.
When we do wrong, we still do what Adam and Eve did. We hide, and we blame others. But God still calls, where art thou? Not to shame you, but to bring you home. Come out of hiding and tell him the truth.
Here is today’s question. God called, where art thou? Is there something you have been hiding from God that you will bring to him today?
Let us pray. Lord God, I confess that I have trusted my own way instead of your word, and I have hidden from you and blamed others. Thank you that you come looking for me. Thank you for your promise of a Saviour who crushes the serpent’s head. I come out of hiding to you. Amen.
