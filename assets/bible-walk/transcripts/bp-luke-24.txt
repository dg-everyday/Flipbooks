Two friends are walking home from Jerusalem, heartbroken. The man they hoped would save Israel has been crucified. As they walk, a stranger joins them and asks what they are talking about. They stop, faces sad, and tell him. And then the stranger begins to explain the whole Bible to them, from Moses and all the prophets. They do not know it yet, but the stranger is Jesus.
In Mark chapter fifteen, Jesus was crucified, the veil of the temple was torn, and his body was laid in a tomb cut out of the rock, with a great stone across the door. His followers were scattered and afraid. On the sabbath, everything was still.
Now it is the first day of the week. In The Big Picture, this is the moment that changes everything. If Jesus stayed dead, the story would end in defeat. But it does not.
'very early in the morning', the women who had followed Jesus come to the tomb, bringing spices to anoint his body. They find the stone rolled away. They go in, and the body of the Lord Jesus is not there. While they stand there, perplexed, two men in shining clothes appear beside them, and the women bow their faces to the ground in fear. And the men say, 'Why seek ye the living among the dead?'
The women remember his words. They hurry back and tell the eleven disciples and the others. But 'their words seemed to them as idle tales, and they believed them not.' Peter runs to the tomb, stoops down, sees the linen cloths lying by themselves, and goes home wondering.
That same day, two of the disciples are walking to a village called Emmaus, about seven miles from Jerusalem. As they talk, Jesus himself draws near and walks with them, but their eyes are kept from recognising him. One of them, named Cleopas, tells the stranger about Jesus of Nazareth, a mighty prophet, crucified; we had hoped he was the one who would redeem Israel. And now some women say his tomb is empty and angels told them he is alive.
Jesus answers, 'O fools, and slow of heart to believe all that the prophets have spoken'. 'Ought not Christ to have suffered these things, and to enter into his glory?' And beginning at Moses and all the prophets, he explains to them in all the Scriptures the things concerning himself.
They reach the village, and he acts as if he would go further. But they urge him, 'Abide with us: for it is toward evening, and the day is far spent.' At the table he takes bread, blesses it, breaks it, and gives it to them. And their eyes are opened, and they know him. And he vanishes from their sight. They say to each other the words that begin our very first episode.
They rush back to Jerusalem and find the eleven saying, 'The Lord is risen indeed, and hath appeared to Simon.' While they are telling their story, Jesus himself stands among them and says, 'Peace be unto you.' They are terrified, thinking they see a spirit. He says, 'Behold my hands and my feet, that it is I myself: handle me, and see; for a spirit hath not flesh and bones, as ye see me have.' He even eats a piece of broiled fish in front of them.
Then he opens their understanding, so that they can understand the Scriptures: everything written about him in the law of Moses, the prophets and the psalms had to be fulfilled. Christ had to suffer and rise from the dead on the third day. And something else had to happen too. 'And ye are witnesses of these things.' He tells them to wait in Jerusalem until they are clothed with power from on high.
Then he leads them out as far as Bethany, lifts up his hands, and blesses them. And while he blesses them, he is parted from them, and carried up into heaven. And they return to Jerusalem with great joy, praising God.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 6. 'He is not here, but is risen: remember how he spake unto you when he was yet in Galilee,' These are the most important words in history. He is risen. Death could not hold him. That means everything he said is true. His promises are true. His sacrifice was accepted. And death is no longer the end for those who belong to him. And notice: remember how he spake. He had told them. God always keeps his word.
Verse 32. 'And they said one to another, Did not our heart burn within us, while he talked with us by the way, and while he opened to us the scriptures?' This is the verse our whole walk began with. When the risen Jesus opens the Scriptures, hearts burn. The whole Bible, from Genesis to the prophets, is about him. That is why we have walked through The Big Picture: to see Jesus on every page, and to let him open the Word to us.
Verse 47. 'And that repentance and remission of sins should be preached in his name among all nations, beginning at Jerusalem.' Remission means forgiveness, sins sent away. The news of the risen Jesus is not to stay in one room or one nation. It is for all nations, just as God promised Abraham in Genesis chapter twelve. And it starts where they are, in Jerusalem.
David had written in a psalm, 'neither wilt thou suffer thine Holy One to see corruption.' And Isaiah, after describing the servant’s death, said, 'he shall prolong his days'. The resurrection was not a surprise ending. It was the promise God had written into the story all along, from the serpent’s crushed head to the empty tomb.
Is there a place in your life where you are walking away sad, like the two on the road, thinking all hope is gone? The risen Jesus walks with you. Open the Scriptures, and ask him to open your eyes. And then, like those two disciples, go and tell someone.
Here is today’s question. The two disciples said, did not our heart burn within us? How has Jesus spoken to you through his Word on this walk?
Let us pray. Lord Jesus, you are not in the tomb. You are risen. Thank you that every promise is true because you live. Open the Scriptures to me, make my heart burn, and send me to tell others about you. Amen.
