A crowd has followed a young teacher out of the towns and up a hillside. Fishermen, farmers, the sick, the poor, the curious. He sits down, as teachers did in those days, and his disciples gather close. Everyone expects him to say that the rich, the strong and the religious are the ones God blesses. And then he opens his mouth, and turns everything upside down.
In Luke chapter two, Jesus was born in Bethlehem and grew in wisdom and stature. When he was about thirty, he was baptized by John in the river Jordan, and the voice from heaven said, this is my beloved Son. He was tempted in the wilderness, and he began to preach: 'Repent: for the kingdom of heaven is at hand.' He called fishermen to follow him, and he went through Galilee teaching and healing every kind of sickness, and great crowds followed him.
Now Jesus goes up a mountain to teach. In Exodus chapter twenty, God gave his law from a mountain in fire and smoke. Here Jesus sits on a mountain and explains what God’s law really means. In The Big Picture, this is the King describing life in his kingdom.
Jesus begins with blessings. 'Blessed are the poor in spirit: for theirs is the kingdom of heaven.' Blessed are those who mourn, for they will be comforted. 'Blessed are the meek: for they shall inherit the earth.' Blessed are those who hunger and thirst for righteousness, for they will be filled. Blessed are the merciful, for they will receive mercy. 'Blessed are the pure in heart: for they shall see God.' 'Blessed are the peacemakers: for they shall be called the children of God.' And blessed are those who are persecuted for doing right. Rejoice, Jesus says, because great is your reward in heaven.
Then he tells his followers what they are. 'Ye are the salt of the earth'. Salt keeps food from going bad and gives it flavour. And you are the light of the world. Nobody lights a candle and hides it under a basket. 'Let your light so shine before men, that they may see your good works, and glorify your Father which is in heaven.'
Some wondered if Jesus had come to get rid of God’s law. No, he says. 'I am not come to destroy, but to fulfil.' And then he shows that God’s law reaches much deeper than people thought.
You have heard that it was said, do not kill. But I say unto you, whoever is angry with his brother without a cause is in danger of judgment. Anger is murder in the heart. So if you are bringing a gift to God and remember that your brother has something against you, leave your gift and go and make peace first.
You have heard, do not commit adultery. But I say unto you, whoever looks at a woman to lust after her has already committed adultery in his heart. God’s law is about the heart, not just the hands.
Do not make a show of swearing oaths. Simply let your yes mean yes, and your no mean no.
You have heard, an eye for an eye. But I say unto you, if someone strikes you on one cheek, turn the other also. If someone forces you to go one mile, go with him two.
And then the hardest of all. You have heard, love your neighbour and hate your enemy. But Jesus says the opposite. For your Father in heaven sends sunshine and rain on the evil and the good alike. Even tax collectors love those who love them. Be different. And Jesus ends the chapter with these words: 'Be ye therefore perfect, even as your Father which is in heaven is perfect.'
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 3. 'Blessed are the poor in spirit: for theirs is the kingdom of heaven.' Jesus begins his whole sermon here, and it is the doorway to everything else. To be poor in spirit is to know that you have nothing to offer God, that you need him completely. The kingdom of heaven does not belong to people who think they are spiritually rich. It belongs to people who come with empty hands. That is good news for every one of us.
Verse 14. 'Ye are the light of the world. A city that is set on an hill cannot be hid.' Jesus did not say, try to become light. He said, you are light. Those who follow him carry his light into a dark world. Light does not need to shout. It simply shines, and helps others to see. Your kindness, honesty and good works point people to your Father in heaven.
Verse 44. 'But I say unto you, Love your enemies, bless them that curse you, do good to them that hate you, and pray for them which despitefully use you, and persecute you;' No one had ever taught like this. Love your enemies. Not just tolerate them, but bless them, do good to them, and pray for them. This is impossible for us on our own. But it is exactly what God did for us. While we were his enemies, he loved us and sent his Son. And on the cross, Jesus prayed for the people who crucified him.
Long before, the prophet Micah asked what God really wants from people, and answered, 'He hath shewed thee, O man, what is good; and what doth the LORD require of thee, but to do justly, and to love mercy, and to walk humbly with thy God?' Jesus shows us what that looks like from the inside out. And he is the only one who ever lived it perfectly.
Read the blessings again, and ask which one you most need to live today. Look for a way to let your light shine. And think of someone who has hurt you. Pray for them by name.
Here is today’s question. Jesus said, love your enemies and pray for them. Who is one person you find hard to love, that you will pray for today?
Let us pray. Lord Jesus, I come to you poor in spirit, with nothing to offer but my need. Make my heart pure, and make me a peacemaker. Let my light shine so that others glorify my Father. Give me your love for those who hurt me. Amen.
