A king who once kept sheep sits down to write a song. He has fought giants and led armies. He has been hunted, betrayed and crowned. He has known green fields and dark valleys. And when he looks back over his whole life and asks who has really been leading him, he does not write about himself as a king. He writes about himself as a sheep.
In Second Samuel chapter seven, God promised David that his throne would be established for ever. David was not only a king and a warrior. He was a musician and a poet. Many of the songs in the book of Psalms were written by him.
The Psalms are the songbook and prayer book of God’s people. There are a hundred and fifty of them, and they cover every mood of life: joy and sorrow, fear and praise, anger, guilt and hope. If you do not know how to pray, the Psalms will teach you.
Psalm twenty-three is only six verses long. But it may be the best loved chapter in the whole Bible. In The Big Picture, it shows us something about the King God promised. The king of Israel himself needed a King, and a Shepherd.
The psalm begins with five words: the LORD is my shepherd. In those days a shepherd lived with his sheep, out in the fields, and he never left them. He led them to food and water, protected them from wild animals, found them when they wandered, and carried the weak ones. Sheep cannot look after themselves. They wander off, they get lost, and they cannot fight. They need a shepherd. David knew that, because he had been one.
'He maketh me to lie down in green pastures: he leadeth me beside the still waters.' A sheep will only lie down when it is not afraid, not hungry, and not troubled. The shepherd brings it to that place of rest. And still waters, because sheep are frightened of rushing streams.
'He restoreth my soul'. He brings me back, refreshes me, when I am worn out or have wandered. 'he leadeth me in the paths of righteousness for his name’s sake.' He guides me in the right way, and he does it because of who he is.
Then the psalm walks into darkness. The path goes down into a valley, deep and shadowed. And something changes in the way David speaks. Until now, he has been talking about the LORD: he leads me, he restores me. Now, in the dark valley, David turns and speaks to him: thou art with me. In the darkest places, we stop talking about God and start talking to him.
'thy rod and thy staff they comfort me.' The rod was a club the shepherd used to drive off wolves and lions. The staff was a long crook used to guide the sheep and pull them back when they strayed. One protects. One guides. Both comfort.
Then the picture changes to a feast. 'Thou preparest a table before me in the presence of mine enemies'. The enemies are still there. But God has spread a meal for his guest right in front of them, and they cannot touch him. 'thou anointest my head with oil; my cup runneth over.' In those days a good host poured oil on the head of an honoured guest, and filled his cup to overflowing.
And the psalm ends with a promise for the whole journey and beyond.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 1. 'The LORD is my shepherd; I shall not want.' Notice the little word my. Not just a shepherd, or the shepherd of Israel, but my shepherd. David knew God personally. And because the LORD was his shepherd, he would not want, which means he would not lack anything he truly needed. Not everything he wished for, but everything he needed. A sheep with a good shepherd has all it needs.
Verse 4. 'Yea, though I walk through the valley of the shadow of death, I will fear no evil: for thou art with me; thy rod and thy staff they comfort me.' Notice two words: walk through. The valley is not where the sheep stops and stays. It is a place we pass through. And notice why David is not afraid. Not because the valley is not dark, and not because he is strong, but because thou art with me. The shepherd does not send his sheep through the valley alone. He goes with them.
Verse 6. 'Surely goodness and mercy shall follow me all the days of my life: and I will dwell in the house of the LORD for ever.' The word follow here means more than walk behind. It means pursue, chase after. God’s goodness and mercy run after David all his life, like two faithful dogs following the flock. And the journey ends not in the valley, but at home, in the house of the LORD, for ever.
Long afterward, Jesus stood up and said, 'I am the good shepherd: the good shepherd giveth his life for the sheep.' David’s Shepherd came in person. And this Shepherd did something no ordinary shepherd would do. He laid down his own life to save his sheep.
Which line of this psalm do you need most today? Green pastures, if you are worn out. The valley, if you are afraid or grieving. The table, if you are surrounded by trouble. Whatever it is, say it to him, as David did: thou art with me.
Here is today’s question. David said, the LORD is my shepherd. In what part of your life will you let him lead you today?
Let us pray. Lord, you are my shepherd; I shall not want. Lead me to rest, restore my soul, and guide me in the right paths. When I walk through dark valleys, help me to fear no evil, because you are with me. Thank you that your goodness and mercy follow me all my days. Amen.
