A man is walking through a busy market, staring down at the ground. He is looking for coins people have dropped. All day, he finds a few small coins. He is pleased. But he never once looks up. And so he never sees the beautiful sky, the mountains in the distance, or the friend waving to him from across the street. Paul says, do not spend your life looking down. Look up.
In chapter two, Paul warned the Colossians not to be carried off by clever philosophy or empty rules. In Christ dwells all the fulness of the Godhead bodily, and ye are complete in him. Jesus blotted out the record of our sins, and nailed it to his cross. And as you have received Christ, so walk in him, rooted and built up in him.
Now, in chapter three, Paul shows what a life in Christ looks like. Listen for what to take off, and what to put on. And listen for how Jesus changes ordinary things, like work and home.
Paul begins with a call to look up. 'If ye then be risen with Christ, seek those things which are above, where Christ sitteth on the right hand of God.' In chapter two, Paul said that believers have died and risen with Christ. Now he says, if that is true, live like it. Seek what is above, where Christ is.
And then.
'For ye are dead, and your life is hid with Christ in God.' Your old life is gone. Your new life is safely hidden with Christ, in God, like treasure kept in the safest place. 'When Christ, who is our life, shall appear, then shall ye also appear with him in glory.' One day, Jesus will come again. And then everyone will see who we really are, sharing in his glory.
So Paul says, put to death the old ways that belong to the earth. Sexual sin, impurity, wrong desires, and greed, which is really a kind of idol worship, putting things in God’s place. Those are the things that bring God’s anger. You used to live that way, Paul says. But not any more.
'But now ye also put off all these; anger, wrath, malice, blasphemy, filthy communication out of your mouth.' Put them off, like dirty clothes. Anger, rage, wanting to hurt others, cursing, dirty talk. 'Lie not one to another, seeing that ye have put off the old man with his deeds'.
Instead, you have put on the new self, which is being made new, to be like the God who made you. And in this new life, all the old divisions disappear. It does not matter whether someone is Greek or Jew, from a civilised nation or a wild one, a slave or free. 'but Christ is all, and in all.'
Then Paul tells them what to put on. As God’s chosen people, holy and dearly loved, put on tender mercy, kindness, humility, gentleness and patience. 'Forbearing one another, and forgiving one another, if any man have a quarrel against any: even as Christ forgave you, so also do ye.' We forgive, because we have been forgiven.
And then, over all these clothes, one more.
'And let the peace of God rule in your hearts'. Rule here means be the umpire, deciding what is right. Let God’s peace be the referee in your heart. 'and be ye thankful.'
'Let the word of Christ dwell in you richly in all wisdom; teaching and admonishing one another in psalms and hymns and spiritual songs, singing with grace in your hearts to the Lord.' Let God’s Word live in you, not as a visitor, but as a welcome guest who makes himself at home. Teach one another, and sing together.
'And whatsoever ye do in word or deed, do all in the name of the Lord Jesus, giving thanks to God and the Father by him.' Everything. Every word, every action. Do it all for Jesus, with thanks.
Then, as in Ephesians, Paul speaks to different people in a household. Wives and husbands, children and fathers, servants and masters. As we heard in Ephesians, Christians have understood some of these words in different ways. But listen to what Paul says to the husbands, fathers and masters, the people with power in those days. 'Husbands, love your wives, and be not bitter against them.' 'Fathers, provoke not your children to anger, lest they be discouraged.' Do not crush your children’s spirits with harshness. And to masters, give your servants what is right and fair, because you too have a Master in heaven.
And to servants, and to all workers, Paul gives a verse that changes the way we see every job.
For it is the Lord Christ you are serving, and he will reward you. And whoever does wrong will be repaid for the wrong they have done. God has no favourites.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 2. 'Set your affection on things above, not on things on the earth.' Affection means the mind and the heart. What do we think about most? What do we love most? Paul does not mean we should ignore the world, or our families, or our work. He means we should see everything in the light of heaven, and put Christ first. When our hearts are set on things above, everything on earth finds its right place.
Verse 14. 'And above all these things put on charity, which is the bond of perfectness.' Charity means love, the love that gives itself for others. Paul has listed kindness, humility, gentleness, patience and forgiveness, like clothes to put on. Over all of them, put on love, like a belt or a coat that holds everything together. Love is the bond of perfectness. It ties all the other good things together, and makes them complete.
Verse 23. 'And whatsoever ye do, do it heartily, as to the Lord, and not unto men'. Heartily means with all your heart. Whatever work you do, washing dishes, doing homework, serving customers, caring for children, do it with all your heart, as if you were doing it for Jesus himself. Because you are. No job is too small to be done for the Lord. And he sees and rewards it.
Paul wrote to the Philippians, 'For our conversation is in heaven'. And to the Corinthians, 'And now abideth faith, hope, charity, these three; but the greatest of these is charity.'
Pick one ordinary task today, and do it heartily, as for the Lord. And think about your clothes for the day: what do you need to put off, and what do you need to put on? Above all, put on love.
Here is today’s question. Paul says, whatsoever ye do, do it heartily, as to the Lord. How would it change your everyday work to do it for Jesus?
Let us pray. Lord Jesus, I am risen with you. Help me to seek the things above, and to set my heart on you. Help me to put off anger, lies and every old way, and to put on mercy, kindness, humility, patience and forgiveness. Above all, clothe me with love. Let your peace rule in my heart, and your word dwell in me richly. And whatever I do, let me do it heartily, for you. Amen.
