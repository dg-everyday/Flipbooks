A cook tastes a pot of soup. It is bland. Something is missing. She adds a pinch of salt, stirs it, and tastes again. Now it is delicious. Just a little salt brings out all the flavour. Paul says our words should be like that: seasoned with salt, so that they are good to hear, and bring out the best in others.
In chapter three, Paul said, if ye be risen with Christ, seek those things which are above. Put off anger and lies, and put on mercy, kindness and forgiveness, and above all, love, the bond of perfectness. Let the peace of God rule in your hearts, and the word of Christ dwell in you richly. And whatsoever ye do, do it heartily, as to the Lord.
Now we come to the last chapter of Colossians. Listen for what Paul says about prayer and words. And listen to the list of friends at the end. Each one has a story.
The chapter begins by finishing what Paul said to masters in chapter three. 'Masters, give unto your servants that which is just and equal; knowing that ye also have a Master in heaven.' Treat those who work for you fairly. You answer to God.
Then Paul turns to prayer.
And Paul asks them to pray for him. 'Withal praying also for us, that God would open unto us a door of utterance, to speak the mystery of Christ, for which I am also in bonds'. A door of utterance means an opportunity to speak. Paul is in chains, in prison. But he does not ask them to pray that the prison door would open. He asks them to pray that a door would open for the good news. Pray that I may make it clear, he says, as I should.
Then Paul talks about how to live among people who do not yet believe. 'Walk in wisdom toward them that are without, redeeming the time.' Them that are without means those outside the church. Live wisely among them. Make the most of every opportunity.
And then, the verse about salt.
Then Paul ends with greetings. And each name has a story.
First, Tychicus, 'a beloved brother, and a faithful minister and fellowservant in the Lord'. He is carrying this letter, and the letter to the Ephesians, and he will tell them all Paul’s news.
And with him, 'Onesimus, a faithful and beloved brother, who is one of you.' Onesimus came from Colosse. And there is more to his story than this verse tells. Onesimus had once been a servant who ran away from his master. We will hear what happened in the very next book on our walk.
Aristarchus, my fellow prisoner, sends greetings. Do you remember him from the book of Acts? He was with Paul in the riot at Ephesus, and sailed with him to Rome, through the storm and the shipwreck.
'and Marcus, sister’s son to Barnabas'. Marcus is Mark, the cousin of Barnabas. Do you remember? On Paul’s first journey, Mark left them and went home. Later, Paul refused to take him on another journey, and Paul and Barnabas argued so sharply that they went separate ways. But now, Mark is with Paul again, and Paul tells the church, if he comes to you, welcome him. Many believe this is the same Mark who wrote the Gospel of Mark, the very first book on our walk. A young man who failed, and was given a second chance.
Then Epaphras. The man who first brought the good news to Colosse.
'Luke, the beloved physician, and Demas, greet you.' Luke was a doctor. And he is the same Luke who wrote the Gospel of Luke, and the book of Acts, both of which we have walked through. He travelled with Paul, and stayed with him in prison. And Demas. Later, in another letter, Paul would sadly write that Demas had left him, because he loved this present world. Not everyone who starts well finishes well.
Paul greets Nymphas, and the church that meets in his house. In those days, believers met in each other’s homes.
'And when this epistle is read among you, cause that it be read also in the church of the Laodiceans; and that ye likewise read the epistle from Laodicea.' Paul’s letters were passed from church to church.
And then, Paul takes the pen from the scribe who has been writing for him, and writes the last line in his own hand. 'The salutation by the hand of me Paul. Remember my bonds. Grace be with you. Amen.' Remember my chains. Pray for me.
So ends the letter to the Colossians. It began with Jesus, the image of the invisible God, before all things, holding all things together. It showed that in him we are complete, that our sins are nailed to his cross, and that our lives are hid with Christ in God. And it ended with ordinary people, a doctor, a runaway, a young man given a second chance, all made one family in Christ.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 2. 'Continue in prayer, and watch in the same with thanksgiving'. Continue means keep on, do not give up. Watch means stay alert, pay attention. And with thanksgiving, once again. Prayer is not just something we do when we are in trouble. It is a way of life: keeping on, staying alert to what God is doing, and always thankful.
Verse 6. 'Let your speech be alway with grace, seasoned with salt, that ye may know how ye ought to answer every man.' With grace means kind, gracious, pleasant. Seasoned with salt means interesting, thoughtful, not dull or empty. Salt also kept food from going bad. Our words should be kind, wise and wholesome. And then we will know how to answer anyone who asks about our faith.
Verse 12. 'Epaphras, who is one of you, a servant of Christ, saluteth you, always labouring fervently for you in prayers, that ye may stand perfect and complete in all the will of God.' Labouring fervently means working hard, wrestling, in prayer. Epaphras could not be with his friends in Colosse, so he prayed for them, with all his strength. He wanted them to stand firm and mature in God’s will. Prayer for others is real work, and it is one of the greatest gifts we can give.
Peter wrote, 'be ready always to give an answer to every man that asketh you a reason of the hope that is in you'. And Paul later wrote of Mark, 'Take Mark, and bring him with thee: for he is profitable to me for the ministry.'
Pray today for someone the way Epaphras prayed, fervently, by name. And ask God to season your words with grace and salt in every conversation today. Is there someone who has failed, like Mark, whom you could encourage with a second chance?
Here is today’s question. Mark once let Paul down, but later Paul welcomed him back. What does Mark’s story teach you about failure, and about giving others a second chance?
Let us pray. Lord God, thank you for walking with me through the letter to the Colossians. Thank you that Jesus is above all, and that in him I am complete. Help me to continue in prayer, watchful and thankful. Let my speech always be gracious and seasoned with salt. Thank you for second chances, like Mark’s. And make me faithful in prayer for others, like Epaphras. Amen.
