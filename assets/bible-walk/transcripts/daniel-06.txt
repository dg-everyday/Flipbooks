An old man walks up the stairs to his room. He knows that a new law has just been signed. Anyone who prays to any god except the king will be thrown to the lions. He knows that his enemies are watching his window. He kneels down, facing the window, just as he has done every day for years. And he prays.
In chapter five, King Belshazzar drank from the holy vessels of God’s temple, and the fingers of a hand wrote on the wall. Daniel read the writing: thou art weighed in the balances, and art found wanting. Belshazzar lost his life, and his kingdom was given to the Medes and Persians. Darius the Mede took the throne.
Now we come to the last chapter of our walk through Daniel. Daniel is an old man, serving a new king in a new empire. Listen for how Daniel’s quiet, everyday faithfulness carries him through the greatest test of his life.
Darius decides to set a hundred and twenty princes over the whole kingdom, and over them, three presidents, of whom Daniel is the first. Daniel stands out above all the others, because an excellent spirit is in him. And the king plans to set him over the whole kingdom.
So the other presidents and princes look for something to accuse Daniel of, in how he runs the kingdom. But they cannot find any fault or wrongdoing, because he is faithful.
Then these men come up with a plan.
So they go together to the king, and say, King Darius, live for ever. All the leaders of the kingdom have agreed that the king should make a law, a firm decree, that for thirty days, anyone who prays to any god or man except you, O king, shall be thrown into the den of lions. Now, O king, sign the decree, so that it cannot be changed, according to the law of the Medes and Persians, which cannot be altered.
They flatter the king. And they lie: not all the leaders had agreed, because Daniel had not been asked. And King Darius signs the decree.
When Daniel knows that the law has been signed, he goes home to his upstairs room. Its windows are open toward Jerusalem. And three times a day, he kneels down, and prays, and gives thanks before his God, as he has always done.
Daniel does not hide, or close the windows. Nor does he make a protest. He simply keeps doing what he has always done.
Then these men gather together, and find Daniel praying and asking for help before his God.
They go to the king. Did you not sign the decree? The king answers, the thing is true.
Then they say, that Daniel, one of the captives of Judah, does not respect you, O king, or the decree you have signed. He prays three times a day.
When the king hears this, he is very upset with himself. He has been tricked. He sets his heart on rescuing Daniel, and works 'till the going down of the sun' to find a way to save him.
But the men remind him that no decree the king makes can be changed.
So the king gives the command, and Daniel is brought, and thrown into the den of lions.
And as Daniel goes in, the king calls out to him.
A stone is laid over the mouth of the den, and the king seals it with his own signet ring.
Then the king goes to his palace, and 'passed the night fasting'. He will not have any music played before him. And he cannot sleep.
'Then the king arose very early in the morning, and went in haste unto the den of lions.' When he comes near the den, he cries out to Daniel with an anguished voice, O Daniel, servant of the living God, is thy God, whom thou servest continually, able to deliver thee from the lions?
And from inside the den, a voice answers. O king, live for ever. 'My God hath sent his angel, and hath shut the lions mouths, that they have not hurt me'. For I was found innocent before him, and also before you, O king, I have done no wrong.
Then the king is overjoyed.
The men who accused Daniel are thrown into the den themselves.
Then King Darius writes to all peoples, nations and languages in all the earth. Peace be multiplied unto you. Everyone in my kingdom must tremble and fear before the God of Daniel. For he is the living God, and stedfast for ever. He delivereth and rescueth, and he hath delivered Daniel from the power of the lions.
So Daniel prospers in the reign of Darius, and in the reign of Cyrus the Persian.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 5. 'Then said these men, We shall not find any occasion against this Daniel, except we find it against him concerning the law of his God.' What a thing to say about someone. His enemies searched his whole life, and found nothing. The only thing they could attack was his faithfulness to God. Daniel was a man of integrity, the same in private as in public. May our lives be like that, so that the only thing anyone can say against us is that we love God.
Verse 16. 'Then the king commanded, and they brought Daniel, and cast him into the den of lions. Now the king spake and said unto Daniel, Thy God whom thou servest continually, he will deliver thee.' Thy God whom thou servest continually. Even the king had noticed. Daniel did not serve God only on special occasions. He served him continually, every day, in every season of his long life. That was what made him ready for the lions. Faith that holds in a crisis is built in ordinary days.
Verse 23. 'Then was the king exceeding glad for him, and commanded that they should take Daniel up out of the den. So Daniel was taken up out of the den, and no manner of hurt was found upon him, because he believed in his God.' No manner of hurt. Not a scratch. Because he believed in his God. Daniel’s trust in God was not just something he said. It was what he lived by. God does not always rescue his people from danger in this life. But he is always worthy of our trust. And for Daniel, the God he had served continually was faithful to the end.
A psalm says, 'Evening, and morning, and at noon, will I pray, and cry aloud: and he shall hear my voice.' And the letter to the Hebrews speaks of those who 'through faith subdued kingdoms, wrought righteousness, obtained promises, stopped the mouths of lions'.
Daniel prayed three times a day, every day, long before the lions. What is one small, steady habit of faith you could build in your life, so that you are ready when hard times come? Start it today.
Here is today’s question. We have walked through the stories of Daniel. Even King Darius said, thy God whom thou servest continually. What would it look like for you to serve God continually, in your everyday life?
