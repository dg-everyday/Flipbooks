Imagine walking in circles. Round and round the same mountain, year after year, until you know every rock and every path. And then one day, a voice says: that is enough. You have gone round this mountain long enough. Turn, and go north. In Deuteronomy chapter two, the long wandering finally comes to an end.
In chapter one, Moses began his farewell by looking back to Horeb, and to the day Israel stood at the edge of the land. God said, fear not, neither be discouraged. But the people would not go up, and grumbled that God hated them. Yet Moses reminded them that the LORD had carried them through the wilderness, as a man doth bear his son.
Now, in chapter two, Moses remembers the long years of wandering, and the journey north. Listen for how Israel was to treat their neighbours. And listen for Moses’ summary of the wilderness years.
Then we turned, Moses says, and set out into the wilderness, as the LORD told me. And we went around mount Seir for many days.
Mount Seir was the hill country of Edom, south of the Dead Sea. For years, Israel wandered in that region.
And then God spoke.
At last. The time of wandering was over. Now it was time to move toward the promised land.
But God gave careful instructions. You will pass through the land of your brothers, the descendants of Esau, who live in Seir. They will be afraid of you. So be very careful.
Esau was Jacob’s twin brother. His descendants, the Edomites, were Israel’s relatives. And God had given them their own land.
God says, buy food from them with money, and buy water from them to drink. Do not take anything by force.
And then Moses gives a wonderful summary of the whole journey.
Then God tells them not to fight against Moab, or against Ammon, either. These nations were descended from Lot, Abraham’s nephew. God had given them their land too. And Moses adds a few old notes about the peoples who lived there before: tall and powerful people, like the giants, whom God had driven out before Moab, and Ammon, and Edom.
Do you see what God was showing them? The giants that had terrified their parents at Kadesh were not too big for God. He had already driven out giants for other nations. He could certainly do it for Israel.
Then they crossed the brook Zered. And Moses tells us that by this time, the whole generation of fighting men who had refused to enter the land had passed away in the wilderness, just as the LORD had sworn.
Then God says, today you will cross the border of Moab. And now, for the first time, God tells them, begin to take possession.
Israel sends messengers to Sihon, the king of the Amorites, in Heshbon, with words of peace. Let us pass through your land. We will stay on the main road. We will not turn to the right or to the left. Sell us food and water for money, and let us simply walk through, as Edom and Moab did.
Notice that Israel offered peace first. They did not want a fight.
But Sihon would not let them pass. Moses says that the LORD had made his spirit stubborn, so that he would fall into Israel’s hands. Sihon came out with all his people to fight at Jahaz. And the LORD gave him into Israel’s hand, and Israel took his cities, from the river Arnon to Gilead. Not one city was too strong for them, because the LORD gave them all.
This is the beginning of the conquest. And Moses wants the new generation to see the pattern. When they followed God, nothing could stand in their way. The same giants and walled cities that had terrified their parents fell before them.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 3. 'Ye have compassed this mountain long enough: turn you northward.' Long enough. Sometimes we go round and round the same mountain in our lives, the same fears, the same habits, the same struggles. But there comes a time when God says, that is enough. Turn. Move forward. It is never too late to stop circling and start following.
Verse 5. 'Meddle not with them; for I will not give you of their land, no, not so much as a footbreadth; because I have given mount Seir unto Esau for a possession.' God had given land to Esau’s family too. Israel was God’s chosen people, but God cared for other nations as well. Israel was not to take what God had given to others. God’s people should respect what belongs to their neighbours.
Verse 7. 'For the LORD thy God hath blessed thee in all the works of thy hand: he knoweth thy walking through this great wilderness: these forty years the LORD thy God hath been with thee; thou hast lacked nothing.' He knoweth thy walking. God knew every step of their journey. And through all those years, they had lacked nothing. Manna every day. Water from the rock. Clothes that did not wear out. Even in the wilderness, God provided everything they needed.
A psalm says, 'The LORD is my shepherd; I shall not want.' And Paul wrote, 'But my God shall supply all your need according to his riches in glory by Christ Jesus.'
Is there a mountain you have been circling for a long time, a habit, a fear, a struggle? Ask God today to help you turn and move forward. And thank him for the ways he has provided for you, so that you have lacked nothing.
Here is today’s question. Moses said, 'these forty years the LORD thy God hath been with thee; thou hast lacked nothing.' Looking back over your own life, how has God provided for you, even in wilderness times?
Let us pray. Lord God, you know my walking, every step of my journey. Thank you that you have been with me, and that I have lacked nothing I truly needed. Where I have been going round in circles, help me to turn and follow you. And teach me to respect what you have given to others. Amen.
