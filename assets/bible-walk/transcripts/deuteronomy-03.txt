An old man kneels and prays the most heartfelt prayer of his life. Lord, you have shown me so much of your greatness. Please, let me go over the river and see the good land. Just let me set foot in it. And God answers. But the answer is no. And then God tells him to do something even harder: help someone else go where he cannot.
In chapter two, God said, ye have compassed this mountain long enough: turn you northward. Israel passed by Edom, Moab and Ammon in peace, because God had given those lands to Esau and Lot. Moses said, 'these forty years the LORD thy God hath been with thee; thou hast lacked nothing.' And Israel defeated Sihon.
Now, in chapter three, Moses remembers the defeat of Og, and his own disappointment. Listen for Moses’ prayer. And listen for how God answers.
Then we turned, Moses says, and went up the road to Bashan. And Og, the king of Bashan, came out against us with all his people, to fight at Edrei.
And the LORD said to me, do not be afraid of him. I have given him, and all his people, and his land, into your hand. Do to him as you did to Sihon.
So the LORD gave Og into our hands. Israel took all his cities, sixty of them, the whole region of Argob, the kingdom of Og in Bashan. All these cities were fortified, with high walls, gates and bars, besides many towns without walls.
Sixty walled cities. The very thing their parents had feared at Kadesh: the cities are great and walled up to heaven. And now, with God, they took every one.
Then Moses adds a remarkable detail. Og was the last of the giants. His bed was made of iron, nine cubits long and four cubits wide, about four metres by nearly two. It was still on display in a city of the Ammonites.
Imagine the people looking at that enormous iron bed. This was the kind of giant their parents had been afraid of. And God had given them the victory.
Then Moses describes how he gave the land east of the Jordan to Reuben, Gad and half the tribe of Manasseh. But only on condition that their fighting men would cross over and help their brothers take the land on the other side.
And Moses remembers what he told Joshua.
Moses was preparing Joshua. Look at what God did to Sihon and Og, he told him. God will do the same to every kingdom you face.
Then Moses tells the people about his own prayer.
And Moses prays, please let me go over, and see the good land beyond the Jordan, that goodly mountain country, and Lebanon.
It is such a human prayer. Moses had led these people for so long. He had given everything. And all he wanted was to see the promise come true, to stand in the land himself.
But the LORD was angry with me because of you, Moses says, and would not listen. And the LORD said to me, 'Let it suffice thee; speak no more unto me of this matter.' That is enough. Do not speak to me about this again.
Moses had struck the rock in anger at Meribah, and did not honour God as holy before the people. And the consequence stood.
But God did give Moses something. Go up to the top of Pisgah, God said, and look west and north and south and east. See the land with your own eyes. But you will not cross this Jordan.
And then God gave Moses one last task.
What an act of humility. Moses could not go in himself. And instead of being bitter, he was to pour his strength into Joshua, so that Joshua could lead the people where Moses could not go.
Sometimes God says no to our prayers. Even our most heartfelt prayers. And sometimes, instead of giving us what we ask, he gives us a different task: to help someone else succeed.
So we stayed in the valley, Moses says, opposite Beth-peor.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 22. 'Ye shall not fear them: for the LORD your God he shall fight for you.' Moses said this to Joshua, as he prepared him for leadership. The battle would not depend on Joshua’s strength, but on God’s. The best encouragement we can give someone is to remind them that God fights for them.
Verse 24. 'O Lord GOD, thou hast begun to shew thy servant thy greatness, and thy mighty hand: for what God is there in heaven or in earth, that can do according to thy works, and according to thy might?' Thou hast begun. After the plagues, the Red Sea, manna, water from the rock, Moses said God had only begun to show his greatness. The more we know God, the more we realise there is still more to see.
Verse 28. 'But charge Joshua, and encourage him, and strengthen him: for he shall go over before this people, and he shall cause them to inherit the land which thou shalt see.' Charge him, encourage him, strengthen him. Moses’ last great work was to build up the next leader. It is a beautiful thing to help others go further than we can go ourselves. God’s work is bigger than any one of us.
Paul prayed three times for his thorn in the flesh to be taken away, and heard God say, 'My grace is sufficient for thee: for my strength is made perfect in weakness.' And Paul wrote to a young leader, 'Thou therefore, my son, be strong in the grace that is in Christ Jesus.'
Is there a prayer God has answered with no? Bring your disappointment to him honestly today, as Moses did. And think of someone younger or newer in faith whom you could encourage and strengthen.
Here is today’s question. God told Moses, let it suffice thee, and then gave him the task of encouraging Joshua. How can we respond well when God says no to something we long for?
Let us pray. Lord God, you have only begun to show me your greatness. When you say no to my prayers, help me to trust you, and not to grow bitter. Show me who I can encourage and strengthen, so that they can go further than I can. Your grace is sufficient for me. Amen.
