Imagine standing at the foot of a mountain that is on fire. The flames reach up to the sky. Dark clouds swirl around it. And out of the fire comes a voice, speaking words you can understand. You see no shape, no face, no figure. Only fire, and a voice. Moses reminds the people: you were there. You heard God speak. Never forget it.
In chapter three, Israel defeated Og, king of Bashan, the last of the giants, and took sixty walled cities. Moses begged God to let him cross the Jordan, but God said, let it suffice thee. Instead, God told Moses to charge Joshua, and encourage him, and strengthen him.
Now, in chapter four, Moses turns from looking back to pleading with the people. Listen for what Moses warns them not to forget. And listen for the promise for anyone who seeks God.
Now therefore, listen, O Israel, Moses says, to the laws I am teaching you, and do them, so that you may live, and go in and possess the land.
And Moses gives a warning. Do not add to the word I command you, and do not take anything away from it.
Moses reminds them of what happened at Baal-peor, only recently. Everyone who followed Baal of Peor, the LORD destroyed. But you who held fast to the LORD your God are all alive today.
Then Moses tells them why God’s laws matter so much. Keep them and do them, for this will show your wisdom and understanding to the nations. They will hear about these laws and say, surely this great nation is a wise and understanding people.
And Moses asks two questions. What great nation has a god so near to them, as the LORD our God is near whenever we call on him? And what great nation has laws as righteous as this law I am setting before you today?
Israel’s greatest treasure was not their land, or their army. It was that God was near to them, and had given them his good law.
Then comes Moses’ great warning.
Teach them to your children, and your grandchildren. Remember the day you stood before the LORD your God at Horeb.
And Moses describes it. You came near, and stood at the foot of the mountain, while the mountain burned with fire to the heart of heaven, with darkness, cloud and thick darkness. And the LORD spoke to you out of the fire. You heard the sound of words, but you saw no form. There was only a voice. And he declared to you his covenant, the Ten Commandments, and wrote them on two tablets of stone.
So be very careful, Moses says. You saw no form of any kind at Horeb. So do not make yourselves an idol, in the shape of any figure, man or woman, animal or bird, creeping thing or fish. And when you look up at the sun, the moon and the stars, do not be drawn away to worship them.
God had shown himself to them, not as a statue they could carve, but as a living voice. He could not be captured in any image.
For the LORD thy God is a consuming fire, Moses says, even a jealous God.
Then Moses looks into the future, and gives a sobering warning. If, after you have lived long in the land, you turn to idols, you will not last long there. The LORD will scatter you among the nations, and you will serve gods of wood and stone, that cannot see or hear or eat or smell.
Sadly, much later in Israel’s history, that is exactly what happened. The people turned to idols, and were carried away into exile.
But then Moses gives a wonderful promise, even for that dark future.
When you are in trouble, and all these things have happened to you, if you turn to the LORD your God and obey his voice, then, Moses says, the LORD thy God is a merciful God. He will not abandon you, or destroy you, or forget the covenant he swore to your fathers.
Even if they were scattered to the ends of the earth, they could still seek God, and find him.
Then Moses asks them to look back over all of history. Ask from one end of heaven to the other. Has anything this great ever happened? Has any people ever heard the voice of God speaking out of the fire, as you have, and lived? Has any god ever come to take a nation for himself out of another nation, with signs and wonders, and a mighty hand, as the LORD did for you in Egypt?
You were shown these things, Moses says, so that you would know that the LORD is God, and there is no other besides him.
And the chapter ends with Moses setting apart three cities of refuge on the east side of the Jordan: Bezer, Ramoth and Golan.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 9. 'Only take heed to thyself, and keep thy soul diligently, lest thou forget the things which thine eyes have seen, and lest they depart from thy heart all the days of thy life: but teach them thy sons, and thy sons’ sons;' Take heed, keep thy soul, lest thou forget. Forgetting God is the great danger, and it happens slowly. The cure is to remember, and to teach. Telling the next generation what God has done keeps it alive in our own hearts too.
Verse 29. 'But if from thence thou shalt seek the LORD thy God, thou shalt find him, if thou seek him with all thy heart and with all thy soul.' From thence, from wherever you are, even far away, even after you have failed. Seek him with all your heart, and you will find him. This is one of the great promises of the Bible. No one is too far away to seek God, and find him.
Verse 39. 'Know therefore this day, and consider it in thine heart, that the LORD he is God in heaven above, and upon the earth beneath: there is none else.' There is none else. Not one god among many. The only God, in heaven and on earth. Everything Israel had seen was meant to teach them this. And knowing it was not just for the head. Consider it in thine heart.
Through Jeremiah, God said, 'And ye shall seek me, and find me, when ye shall search for me with all your heart.' And Jesus said, 'Seek, and ye shall find'.
Seek God today with all your heart. Set aside some time, with no distractions, and simply tell him you want to know him more. And share one thing God has done for you with someone younger.
Here is today’s question. Moses promised that if the people sought God with all their heart, even from far away, they would find him. What does it look like for you to seek God with all your heart?
Let us pray. Lord God, you are God in heaven above, and on the earth beneath, and there is none else. Thank you that you are near to all who call on you. Keep me from forgetting what you have done. I seek you now, with all my heart. Thank you for your promise that I will find you. Amen.
