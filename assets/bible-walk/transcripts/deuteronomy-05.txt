A new generation stands before Moses. Most of them were children, or not even born, when God spoke from the burning mountain at Sinai. They have heard the stories. But Moses tells them something surprising. God did not make that covenant only with your parents. He made it with you. You, standing here, alive today.
In chapter four, Moses pleaded with the people, take heed, lest thou forget. He reminded them how God spoke from the fire at Horeb, a voice with no form, so they must never make an idol. And he promised that even if they were scattered far away, if thou seek him with all thy heart, thou shalt find him. For the LORD he is God, and there is none else.
Now, in chapter five, Moses repeats the Ten Commandments. Listen for who the covenant was made with. And listen for the longing in God’s heart at the end of the chapter.
Moses calls all Israel together, and says, Hear, O Israel, the laws and judgments I speak in your hearing today, so that you may learn them, and keep them, and do them.
And then Moses says something important.
Many of the people who stood at Sinai were gone. But Moses says, this covenant is yours. God’s promises, and God’s call, are not only for people in the past. They are for every generation, for us, here, today.
Moses reminds them. The LORD spoke with you face to face on the mountain, out of the fire. I stood between the LORD and you at that time, to tell you the word of the LORD, because you were afraid of the fire, and did not go up the mountain.
And then Moses repeats the Ten Commandments, the words God spoke from the fire.
And then the commandments follow.
You shall have no other gods before me. You shall not make an idol, or bow down to one. For I am a jealous God, punishing sin, but showing mercy to thousands of those who love me and keep my commandments.
You shall not take the name of the LORD your God in vain.
Keep the sabbath day holy. Six days you shall work, but the seventh day is the sabbath of the LORD your God. On it, no one in your household is to work, not your children, nor your servants, nor even your animals, nor the foreigner living with you.
And then Moses adds a reason that was not in Exodus. Remember that you were a servant in the land of Egypt, and that the LORD brought you out with a mighty hand. In Egypt, they had worked without rest, as slaves. God’s day of rest for Israel was a sign of their freedom, and a gift even to their servants.
Honour your father and your mother, that your days may be long, and it may go well with you in the land.
Then the commandments come quickly, one after another. Do not take another person’s life. Be faithful in marriage. Do not steal. Do not lie about your neighbour. And do not covet, do not long for what belongs to your neighbour, his wife, his house, his field, or anything that is his.
These words the LORD spoke to all your assembly, Moses says, out of the fire, the cloud and the thick darkness, with a great voice. And he added no more. And he wrote them on two tablets of stone, and gave them to me.
Then Moses remembers what happened next. When you heard the voice out of the darkness, while the mountain burned with fire, your leaders came to me and said, the LORD has shown us his glory, and we have heard his voice. We have seen today that God can speak with a man, and he still lives. But why should we die? This great fire will consume us. You go near, and hear everything the LORD our God says. Then tell us, and we will listen, and do it.
And the LORD heard what you said, Moses says. And God said, they have spoken well.
And then God said something that shows the longing of his heart.
Then God told Moses, tell them to go back to their tents. But you, stand here with me, and I will tell you all the commandments, which you will teach them.
And Moses ends with a plea. Be careful to do as the LORD your God has commanded. Do not turn aside to the right or to the left. Walk in all the ways he has commanded, so that you may live, and it may be well with you.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 3. 'The LORD made not this covenant with our fathers, but with us, even us, who are all of us here alive this day.' Even us, here, alive this day. God’s word is not an old story about other people. It speaks to us, now. When we read the Bible, God is speaking to us, today, just as truly as he spoke to Israel at the mountain.
Verse 6. 'I am the LORD thy God, which brought thee out of the land of Egypt, from the house of bondage.' Before a single command, God reminds them who he is, and what he has done. I am the LORD, who set you free. The commandments were not a ladder to earn God’s love. They were given to people God had already rescued, showing them how to live as free people.
Verse 29. 'O that there were such an heart in them, that they would fear me, and keep all my commandments always, that it might be well with them, and with their children for ever!' Listen to God’s longing. O that there were such an heart in them. God did not give his commands to make life hard. He wanted it to go well with them, and their children for ever. And God knew their hearts needed changing. Long after, through the prophets, he promised to give his people a new heart.
Through Ezekiel, God promised, 'A new heart also will I give you, and a new spirit will I put within you'. And Jesus said, 'If ye love me, keep my commandments.'
Read the Ten Commandments slowly today, as words spoken to you. Thank God for setting you free. And ask him to give you a heart that loves him, and wants to do what he says.
Here is today’s question. God said, O that there were such an heart in them. Why do you think God cares so much about our hearts, and not just our actions?
Let us pray. Lord God, you are the LORD my God, who sets me free. Thank you that your word speaks to me, here, today. Give me a heart that fears you, and loves you, and keeps your commandments, not out of fear, but because I belong to you. Amen.
