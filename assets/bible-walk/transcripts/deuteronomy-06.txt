In homes all over the world, for thousands of years, families have spoken the same words, every day, when they wake and when they go to sleep. Children learn them before they can read. They are written on doorways. And when Jesus was asked, which is the greatest commandment of all, these are the words he gave.
In chapter five, Moses told the new generation that God’s covenant was made with us, even us, here alive this day. He repeated the Ten Commandments, beginning, I am the LORD thy God, which brought thee out of the land of Egypt. And God longed, O that there were such an heart in them, that it might be well with them for ever.
Now, in chapter six, Moses speaks the words at the very heart of the book. Listen for what Moses says about God. And listen for where God’s words are meant to live.
These are the commandments, Moses says, which the LORD your God told me to teach you, so that you would do them in the land.
So that you, and your children, and your grandchildren, would fear the LORD your God, and keep his commands all your life, and that your days may be long.
Hear therefore, O Israel, and be careful to do it, so that it may go well with you, and you may increase greatly in a land flowing with milk and honey.
And then Moses says it.
One LORD. Israel lived surrounded by nations who worshipped many gods: gods of rain, gods of harvest, gods of war, gods of the sun and moon. But Israel’s God was the only God. Not one god among many, but the one true LORD, over all.
And because there is only one God, Moses says, love him with everything you have.
Not a little love. Not a part of your heart, with other gods getting the rest. All your heart. All your soul. All your might. Your whole self, given in love to the one true God.
And then Moses says where these words are to live.
Not just on stone tablets. In your heart.
And then Moses tells them how to keep God’s words in their hearts, and pass them on.
When you sit at home. When you walk along the road. When you lie down, and when you get up. All day, every day, in ordinary life, God’s words were to be part of their conversation.
Moses says, tie them as a sign on your hand, and as a reminder between your eyes. Write them on the doorposts of your house, and on your gates.
God’s word was meant to fill every part of life: what you do with your hands, what you look at, your home, your going out and coming in.
Then Moses gives a warning. When the LORD brings you into the land, with great cities you did not build, houses full of good things you did not fill, wells you did not dig, vineyards and olive trees you did not plant, and when you have eaten and are full, then be careful not to forget the LORD, who brought you out of slavery.
Moses knew the danger. Hunger and hardship often drive people to God. But comfort and plenty can make them forget him.
Then Moses says, you shall fear the LORD your God, and serve him, and swear by his name. Do not follow other gods. And do not test the LORD your God, as you tested him at Massah, where you complained there was no water.
Many years later, Jesus was in the wilderness, hungry, and the devil tempted him. And Jesus answered from this very chapter. Thou shalt not tempt the Lord thy God. And, thou shalt worship the Lord thy God, and him only shalt thou serve.
And Moses ends the chapter with a picture of a family. When your son asks you in time to come, what do all these laws mean? Then tell him. We were slaves to Pharaoh in Egypt, and the LORD brought us out with a mighty hand. He did great signs and wonders before our eyes. He brought us out, so that he could bring us in, to the land he promised. And he commanded us to do all these things for our good, always.
When children ask, why do we do this? the answer is a story. The story of what God has done.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 4. 'Hear, O Israel: The LORD our God is one LORD:' These words are called the Shema, from the first word in Hebrew, which means hear. Jesus quoted them when he was asked for the greatest commandment. There is one God, and only one. Everything else in our faith grows from this.
Verse 5. 'And thou shalt love the LORD thy God with all thine heart, and with all thy soul, and with all thy might.' Jesus called this the first and great commandment. God does not just want obedience. He wants love. And not half of our love, but all of it. Our feelings, our life, our strength, all turned toward him.
Verse 7. 'And thou shalt teach them diligently unto thy children, and shalt talk of them when thou sittest in thine house, and when thou walkest by the way, and when thou liest down, and when thou risest up.' Faith is passed on not only in special services, but in everyday conversations. At home, on the road, at bedtime, at breakfast. Parents and grandparents are the first teachers of faith. God’s word belongs in ordinary life.
Jesus said, 'The first of all the commandments is, Hear, O Israel; The Lord our God is one Lord: And thou shalt love the Lord thy God with all thy heart, and with all thy soul, and with all thy mind, and with all thy strength'. And he said to the devil, 'Thou shalt worship the Lord thy God, and him only shalt thou serve.'
Say the words of the Shema today, when you wake and before you sleep. And find one natural moment to talk about God with someone in your home, or a friend, as you go about your day.
Here is today’s question. Moses said God’s words should fill everyday life, at home, on the road, when you lie down and when you rise up. What would it look like to bring God into the ordinary moments of your day?
Let us pray. Hear, O Israel: The LORD our God is one LORD. Lord God, you are the one true God. Teach me to love you with all my heart, and with all my soul, and with all my might. Write your words on my heart. And help me to pass them on to others, in the ordinary moments of life. Amen.
