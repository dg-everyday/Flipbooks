Imagine being hungry, really hungry, day after day, in the middle of a desert. And then, every day, food appears on the ground. Enough for that day. Never enough to store up. Every single day, you have to trust that there will be more the next day. Why would God feed his people like that? Moses tells them in chapter eight.
In chapter seven, Moses told the people that God chose them, not because they were more in number, for they were the fewest of all people, but because the LORD loved them. He is the faithful God, keeping covenant and mercy to a thousand generations.
Now, in chapter eight, Moses explains the hard lessons of the wilderness. Listen for why God let them be hungry. And listen for the danger that comes with plenty.
Be careful to do all the commands I give you today, Moses says, so that you may live, and grow, and go in and possess the land.
And then Moses asks them to remember.
The wilderness was not an accident. God led them there, on purpose, to humble them, and to test them. To show what was really in their hearts.
And Moses explains the manna.
God let them get hungry. And then he fed them with food from heaven. Why? To teach them that life does not come only from bread, from food and things we can see and hold. Life comes from God, and from every word he speaks. The manna came because God said it would. Every day, it was a lesson: trust the word of God.
Long after, Jesus was in the wilderness for forty days, and he was hungry. And the devil said, if you are the Son of God, turn these stones into bread. And Jesus answered with this very verse: it is written, man shall not live by bread alone, but by every word of God.
Then Moses reminds them of something else. Your clothes did not wear out, and your feet did not swell, all those years. God cared for them in every detail.
And Moses says, consider in your heart that, as a man disciplines his son, so the LORD your God disciplines you.
The hardships of the wilderness were not because God did not love them. They were because he did. A good father trains his children, even when it is hard, because he wants what is best for them.
Then Moses describes the land God is bringing them into. It is beautiful.
A good land, a land of brooks of water, of springs and deep waters flowing out in the valleys and hills. A land of wheat and barley, of vines and fig trees and pomegranates. A land of olive oil and honey. A land where you will eat bread without shortage, and lack nothing. A land whose stones are iron, and whose hills you can dig for copper.
After years of manna and desert, imagine hearing that. Rivers. Fruit. Bread whenever you want it.
And when you have eaten and are full, Moses says, bless the LORD your God for the good land he has given you.
But then Moses gives a warning. And it is a surprising one.
Be careful, Moses says, that you do not forget the LORD your God. When you have eaten and are full, and built fine houses to live in, and your herds and flocks grow large, and your silver and gold increase, then do not let your heart become proud, and forget the LORD your God, who brought you out of Egypt.
Moses was not worried about hunger any more. He was worried about plenty. In the wilderness, they had to depend on God every single day. In the land, with full barns and fine houses, they might start to think they did not need him.
Moses reminds them of the God who led them through the great and terrible wilderness, with its fiery serpents and scorpions and thirsty ground, who brought water out of the hard rock, who fed them with manna.
And then Moses warns against the most dangerous thought of all. Do not say in your heart, my power and the strength of my hand have made me this wealth.
And the chapter ends with a solemn warning. If you forget the LORD your God, and follow other gods, you will surely perish, like the nations the LORD is driving out before you.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 2. 'And thou shalt remember all the way which the LORD thy God led thee these forty years in the wilderness, to humble thee, and to prove thee, to know what was in thine heart, whether thou wouldest keep his commandments, or no.' To prove means to test. Hard seasons show what is really in our hearts. God already knew, but the people needed to learn. When we go through wilderness times, God may be humbling us and teaching us, so that we learn to depend on him.
Verse 3. 'And he humbled thee, and suffered thee to hunger, and fed thee with manna, which thou knewest not, neither did thy fathers know; that he might make thee know that man doth not live by bread only, but by every word that proceedeth out of the mouth of the LORD doth man live.' Man doth not live by bread only. We need food. But we need God’s word even more. His word gives life to our souls. Jesus lived by this verse, even when he was starving in the wilderness.
Verse 18. 'But thou shalt remember the LORD thy God: for it is he that giveth thee power to get wealth, that he may establish his covenant which he sware unto thy fathers, as it is this day.' It is he that giveth thee power. Every skill, every opportunity, every bit of strength to work, comes from God. When things go well, it is easy to take the credit. But a grateful heart remembers where every good thing comes from.
Jesus answered the tempter, 'It is written, Man shall not live by bread alone, but by every word that proceedeth out of the mouth of God.' And James wrote, 'Every good gift and every perfect gift is from above'.
Before you eat today, stop and thank God for your food, and for every good thing he has given you. And spend a few minutes feeding on his word, as well as on bread.
Here is today’s question. Moses warned that plenty could be more dangerous than hunger, because it can make us forget God. Why do you think comfort and success can draw people away from God?
Let us pray. Lord God, thank you for every good gift, which comes from you. Forgive me for the times I have taken the credit for what you have given. Teach me that I do not live by bread only, but by every word that comes from your mouth. In hard times and good times, help me to remember you. Amen.
