People have always asked the question: what does God really want from me? Rules? Sacrifices? Long prayers? Good deeds? Moses gives an answer so clear and so simple that it could be written on a single card. And yet it could take a whole lifetime to live.
In chapter nine, Moses told the people that God was giving them the land not for thy righteousness, for thou art a stiffnecked people. He reminded them of the golden calf, and the broken tablets, and how he lay on his face before God and prayed, yet they are thy people and thine inheritance.
Now, in chapter ten, Moses tells how God gave the commandments a second time. Listen for Moses’ great question. And listen for the three kinds of people God especially cares for.
At that time, Moses says, the LORD said to me, cut two tablets of stone like the first ones, and come up the mountain to me. And make a wooden ark. I will write on the tablets the words that were on the first tablets, which you broke, and you will put them in the ark.
After the golden calf, after the broken tablets, God gave them a second chance. He wrote his law again.
So Moses made an ark of acacia wood, and cut two tablets of stone, and went up the mountain. And God wrote on them the same Ten Commandments, which he had spoken out of the fire. And Moses came down, and put the tablets in the ark, and there they stayed, as the LORD commanded.
God did not give up on his people after they broke his covenant. He renewed it. He wrote his words again. That is the grace of God.
Moses mentions, in passing, that Aaron died and was buried, and Eleazar his son became priest in his place. And that the LORD set apart the tribe of Levi to carry the ark, and to serve him, and to bless in his name. That is why Levi has no land, Moses says. The LORD is his inheritance.
Then Moses says he stayed on the mountain as before, and the LORD listened to him that time too, and chose not to destroy them. And the LORD said, get up, and lead the people on their journey, so they can go in and take the land.
And then Moses asks his great question.
Fear the LORD. Walk in his ways. Love him. Serve him with all your heart and soul. And keep his commands, for your good.
Notice those last words. For thy good. God’s commands are not a burden he places on us to make life hard. They are for our good. He knows what makes life go well.
Then Moses reminds them how great God is. Look, the heavens, even the highest heavens, belong to the LORD your God, and the earth, and everything in it. And yet, the LORD set his heart in love on your fathers, and chose their children after them, you, above all peoples.
The God who owns the universe chose to love a small, stubborn people. That is astonishing.
So, Moses says, let your hearts be changed. Do not be stubborn any longer. The outward signs of belonging to God were not enough. God wanted their hearts to be soft and open toward him.
Then Moses describes God.
For the LORD your God is God of gods, and Lord of lords, the great God, mighty and awesome. He shows no favouritism, and takes no bribes.
And then Moses says what this great God cares about.
The fatherless, the widow, and the stranger. The orphan, with no father to protect them. The widow, with no husband to provide for her. The foreigner, far from home, with no family nearby. These were the most vulnerable people in that world. And the great God of gods takes their side.
And so Moses says.
You know what it is like, Moses says. You were foreigners once, in Egypt, and you were mistreated. So love the stranger.
Fear the LORD your God, Moses says. Serve him, hold fast to him, and make your promises in his name. He is your praise. He is your God, who has done for you these great and awesome things you have seen with your own eyes.
And Moses ends the chapter by looking back. Your fathers went down into Egypt, seventy people. And now the LORD your God has made you as many as the stars of heaven.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 12. 'And now, Israel, what doth the LORD thy God require of thee, but to fear the LORD thy God, to walk in all his ways, and to love him, and to serve the LORD thy God with all thy heart and with all thy soul,' What does God require? Not first of all rituals or achievements. Reverence, obedience, love, and wholehearted service. The prophet Micah would later ask the same question, and answer, to do justly, and to love mercy, and to walk humbly with thy God.
Verse 18. 'He doth execute the judgment of the fatherless and widow, and loveth the stranger, in giving him food and raiment.' Raiment means clothing. The greatest God of all stoops down to care for the weakest people. He gives food and clothing to the stranger. When we care for the vulnerable, we are doing what God himself does.
Verse 19. 'Love ye therefore the stranger: for ye were strangers in the land of Egypt.' Remember where you came from. Israel had been foreigners and slaves. Now they were to love foreigners. God’s people are never to forget what it felt like to be an outsider, and to treat outsiders with kindness.
The prophet Micah wrote, 'what doth the LORD require of thee, but to do justly, and to love mercy, and to walk humbly with thy God?' And James wrote, 'Pure religion and undefiled before God and the Father is this, To visit the fatherless and widows in their affliction'.
Think of someone who is an outsider, a newcomer, a widow, or a child without a parent. How could you show them God’s love today, with a kind word, a meal, or your time?
Here is today’s question. Moses asked, what doth the LORD thy God require of thee? How would you answer that question in your own words?
Let us pray. Lord God, you are God of gods and Lord of lords, and yet you care for the fatherless, the widow and the stranger. Teach me to fear you, to walk in your ways, to love you, and to serve you with all my heart. And help me to love the stranger, as you do. Amen.
