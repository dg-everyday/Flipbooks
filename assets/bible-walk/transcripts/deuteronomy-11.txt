In Egypt, farmers did not look up at the sky for rain. They watered their fields from the river Nile, by hand and by foot, digging channels, pushing water where they wanted it. But the land God was giving Israel was different. There was no great river to water the fields. Everything would depend on rain from heaven. On God.
In chapter ten, God wrote the commandments a second time, and Moses asked, what doth the LORD thy God require of thee, but to fear the LORD thy God, to walk in all his ways, and to love him? And he told them that the great God of gods cares for the fatherless, the widow, and the stranger: love ye therefore the stranger.
Now, in chapter eleven, Moses describes the land, and the choice before them. Listen for what makes this land different from Egypt. And listen for the two things Moses sets before the people.
Love the LORD your God, Moses says, and keep his commands always.
And then Moses speaks directly to the adults in front of him. I am not talking to your children, who have not seen these things. I am talking to you. You saw God’s greatness. His mighty hand. The signs he did in Egypt, to Pharaoh and all his land. What he did to the army of Egypt, their horses and chariots, how he made the Red Sea flow over them. What he did for you in the wilderness. And what he did to Dathan and Abiram, when the earth opened and swallowed them.
You saw it with your own eyes, Moses says. So keep his commands, so that you may be strong, and go in and take the land, and live long in it, a land flowing with milk and honey.
These people had been children when much of this happened. But they had seen it. And Moses wants them to remember, because seeing God’s work brings responsibility.
Then Moses describes the land.
The land you are going into, Moses says, is not like the land of Egypt, where you planted your seed and watered it with your foot, like a vegetable garden. The land you are going into is a land of hills and valleys, that drinks the rain from heaven.
And then Moses says something beautiful about it.
The eyes of the LORD are always on it. From the first day of the year to the last. God himself would watch over their land, and send the rain.
And Moses explains what that means. If you carefully obey God’s commands, to love the LORD your God and serve him with all your heart and soul, then he will send the rain on your land in its season, the early rain and the late rain, so you can gather in your grain, your wine and your oil. And he will give grass for your cattle, and you will eat and be full.
But be careful, Moses says, that your heart is not deceived, and you turn aside to serve other gods. Then the LORD’s anger would burn, and he would shut up the sky, so there would be no rain, and the land would not give its fruit.
In Egypt, you could control your water. In the promised land, you depended on God every single season. That dependence was meant to keep them close to him.
So Moses repeats the words of chapter six.
Teach them to your children, Moses says. Talk about them when you sit at home, and when you walk along the road, when you lie down, and when you get up. Write them on the doorposts of your house, and on your gates.
Then Moses promises that if they love God and walk in his ways, God will drive out the nations before them. Every place where the sole of your foot treads will be yours, from the wilderness to Lebanon, from the river Euphrates to the western sea.
Remember, God said almost the same words to Joshua, at the start of the book of Joshua.
And then Moses sets a choice before them.
A blessing, if you obey the commands of the LORD your God. And a curse, if you do not obey them, but turn aside from the way to follow other gods you have not known.
And when you come into the land, Moses says, you are to proclaim the blessing on mount Gerizim, and the curse on mount Ebal. Two mountains, side by side, facing each other, near Shechem.
Remember, in the book of Joshua, chapter eight, Joshua did exactly that. He gathered all the people between those two mountains, and read the whole law aloud, the blessings and the curses.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 12. 'A land which the LORD thy God careth for: the eyes of the LORD thy God are always upon it, from the beginning of the year even unto the end of the year.' The eyes of the LORD are always upon it. God did not give Israel a land and then walk away. He watched over it, every day of the year. God watches over his people the same way. A psalm says, he that keepeth Israel shall neither slumber nor sleep.
Verse 18. 'Therefore shall ye lay up these my words in your heart and in your soul, and bind them for a sign upon your hand, that they may be as frontlets between your eyes.' Lay up means store away, like treasure. God’s words were to be stored in their hearts, ready for every situation. When we memorise Scripture and think about it, we are laying up treasure that will help us when we need it most.
Verse 26. 'Behold, I set before you this day a blessing and a curse;' Behold, I set before you. Moses put a real choice in front of the people. God does not force anyone to love him. He sets the way of life before us, and invites us to choose it. And as we will hear later in this book, he pleads with us: choose life.
A psalm says, 'he that keepeth Israel shall neither slumber nor sleep.' And another psalm says, 'Thy word have I hid in mine heart, that I might not sin against thee.'
Choose one verse from today’s reading, or one you love, and lay it up in your heart. Write it down, say it several times, and think about it through the day. And thank God that his eyes are on you, from the beginning of the year to the end.
Here is today’s question. In Egypt, the farmers controlled the water, but in the promised land they depended on rain from God. Why do you think God wanted his people to depend on him like this?
Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that your eyes are always on your people, from the beginning of the year to the end. Help me to depend on you, not on my own control. Lay up your words in my heart. And when you set before me a blessing and a curse, help me to choose your way. Amen.
