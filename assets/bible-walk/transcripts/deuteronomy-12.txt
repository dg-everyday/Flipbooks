Imagine a family setting out on a journey together, children and parents and grandparents and servants, all walking to the same place, to bring gifts to God. When they arrive, they do not just hand over their gifts and go home. They sit down together, and eat, and rejoice, in the presence of God. That is the picture Moses paints in chapter twelve.
In chapter eleven, Moses described the land as a land which the LORD thy God careth for, whose eyes are always upon it. He told the people to lay up God’s words in their hearts. And he set before them a blessing and a curse, to be proclaimed on mount Gerizim and mount Ebal.
Now, in chapter twelve, Moses begins to explain the laws for life in the land. This is the start of a long section of the book, from chapter twelve to chapter twenty six. Listen for the place where they are to worship. And listen for how they are to feel when they get there.
These are the laws and judgments, Moses says, which you must be careful to do in the land the LORD God of your fathers is giving you, all the days you live on the earth.
First, Moses says, destroy all the places where the nations worshipped their gods, on the high mountains, on the hills, and under every green tree. Break down their altars, smash their sacred pillars, burn their idols, and cut down the images of their gods, and wipe out their names from those places.
And then Moses says something important.
The Canaanites worshipped their gods anywhere and everywhere, however they liked. God’s people were not to worship him that way. They were not to mix the worship of the LORD with the ways of other gods.
Instead, Moses says, you shall seek the place which the LORD your God will choose, out of all your tribes, to put his name there. There you shall go. And there you shall bring your burnt offerings and sacrifices, your tithes and gifts, your vows and your freewill offerings, and the firstborn of your herds and flocks.
The place God would choose. For many years, it was Shiloh, where the tabernacle stood in the days of Joshua and Samuel. Later, it would be Jerusalem, where Solomon built the temple.
And then Moses tells them how to worship there.
Rejoice. Worship was not meant to be gloomy or fearful. It was a feast, a celebration, a family meal in God’s presence, giving thanks for all he had done.
Then Moses says.
Remember that phrase? Every man did that which was right in his own eyes. We heard it at the end of the book of Judges, when everything fell apart. Moses was warning them about that very danger, long before it happened.
You have not yet come to the rest and the inheritance the LORD is giving you, Moses says. But when you cross the Jordan, and live in the land, and he gives you rest from all your enemies, so that you live in safety, then bring everything I command you to the place he chooses.
And there, Moses says again, you shall rejoice before the LORD your God, you and your sons and daughters, your servants, and the Levite who lives in your town, because he has no land of his own.
Notice who is included. Sons and daughters. Servants. The Levite. Everyone was invited to the feast. No one was left out of the rejoicing.
And Moses adds a gentle reminder. Be careful not to forget the Levite, as long as you live in the land.
Then Moses explains that they may eat meat at home, in their own towns, whenever they like, as God blesses them. Not every meal had to be a sacrifice at God’s chosen place. But there was one thing they must never eat: the blood. Pour it out on the ground like water. For the blood is the life.
God was teaching them that life is precious, and belongs to him.
Then Moses warns them again not to be drawn into the ways of the nations, after those nations are gone. Do not ask, how did these nations serve their gods? I will do the same. For they did terrible things in the worship of their gods, things the LORD hates.
And Moses ends the chapter with the same words as chapter four.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 7. 'And there ye shall eat before the LORD your God, and ye shall rejoice in all that ye put your hand unto, ye and your households, wherein the LORD thy God hath blessed thee.' Ye shall rejoice. God wants his people to be joyful in his presence. Our worship can be full of thanks and gladness, because God has blessed us. A psalm says, serve the LORD with gladness: come before his presence with singing.
Verse 8. 'Ye shall not do after all the things that we do here this day, every man whatsoever is right in his own eyes.' Every man whatsoever is right in his own eyes. That is the opposite of following God. Moses knew the danger of people making up their own religion, their own rules, their own way. God’s way is better than our own ideas.
Verse 32. 'What thing soever I command you, observe to do it: thou shalt not add thereto, nor diminish from it.' Do not add, and do not take away. It is tempting to add our own rules to God’s word, or to quietly leave out the parts we do not like. But God’s word is complete, and it is good. We are to receive it as it is.
A psalm says, 'Serve the LORD with gladness: come before his presence with singing.' And at the very end of the Bible, John warned not to add to, or take away from, the words of the book.
Worship God with gladness today. Thank him for the ways he has blessed you, and the people you share life with. And if you have been doing what is right in your own eyes in some area of life, ask God to show you his way.
Here is today’s question. Moses told the people to rejoice before the LORD, with their whole households. What helps you to worship God with joy, and not just out of duty?
Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that you invite me into your presence with joy. Thank you for every way you have blessed me. Keep me from doing what is right in my own eyes. Help me to receive your word as it is, without adding or taking away, and to serve you with gladness. Amen.
