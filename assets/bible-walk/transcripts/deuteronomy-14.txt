When someone you love dies, grief can make people do desperate things. In the ancient world, mourners would sometimes cut their own bodies, or shave their heads, to show their sorrow, the way the nations around Israel did for their gods. But God tells his people: you do not have to do that. You are my children.
In chapter thirteen, Moses warned that even a prophet with a sign or wonder must not be followed if he leads them to other gods. The LORD your God proveth you, to know whether ye love him with all your heart. Walk after him, and cleave unto him.
Now, in chapter fourteen, Moses tells the people who they are, and how that shapes everyday life, from what they eat, to how they share their harvest. Listen for what Moses calls them at the very start. And listen for who is invited to share in the tithe.
The chapter begins with a beautiful truth.
Ye are the children of the LORD your God, Moses says. So do not cut yourselves, or shave your heads, for the dead, as the nations do.
Because you are God’s children, your grief does not have to look like theirs. You do not need to harm yourselves to get the attention of a god. Your God is your Father. He sees your sorrow, and cares.
And Moses tells them why.
Peculiar here means a special treasure, belonging to God alone.
Then Moses lists the foods they may eat, and those they may not. They may eat animals that have a divided hoof and chew the cud, like the ox, the sheep and the goat, and the deer. But not the camel, or the rabbit, or the pig. From the water, they may eat anything with fins and scales. From the air, they may eat clean birds, but not the eagle, the vulture, the raven, the owl, and others like them.
Why these rules? The Bible does not explain every reason. Some may have protected their health. But the main reason is the one Moses gives: you are a holy people. Even what they ate reminded them, every day, that they belonged to God, and were set apart from the nations around them.
Christians have understood how these food laws apply today in different ways. The book of Acts tells how the apostle Peter saw a vision, and heard God say, what God hath cleansed, that call not thou common. And through that vision, God showed Peter that the good news was for people of every nation.
Then Moses speaks about the tithe, a tenth of every harvest.
Every year, Moses says, set aside a tenth of all your crops. And bring it to the place the LORD will choose. There you shall eat it before the LORD your God, your grain, your wine and your oil, and the firstborn of your herds and flocks, so that you may learn to fear the LORD your God always.
And if the place is too far away to carry it all, then sell it for money, and take the money to the place God chooses. And there, Moses says, spend it on whatever you like, oxen or sheep or wine, and eat it there before the LORD, and rejoice, you and your household.
Think about that. The tithe was not just a payment to God. Much of it became a great feast, a celebration in God’s presence, with the whole family. Giving to God and rejoicing before him went together.
And do not forget the Levite in your town, Moses says, because he has no land of his own.
Then Moses describes a special tithe, every third year. At the end of every three years, bring out the whole tithe of that year’s harvest, and store it in your towns.
The poorest people in the land, the ones with no land and no family to support them, were to eat and be satisfied from the tithe. God built care for the poor right into the rhythm of Israel’s life.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 1. 'Ye are the children of the LORD your God:' What a thing to be called. Not just servants, or subjects, but children of God. The New Testament says the same of all who believe in Jesus: as many as received him, to them gave he power to become the sons of God. Being God’s children shapes everything: how we grieve, how we live, how we treat others.
Verse 2. 'for thou art an holy people unto the LORD thy God, and the LORD hath chosen thee to be a peculiar people unto himself, above all the nations that are upon the earth.' Holy and chosen. Set apart, not to be proud, but to belong to God, and to show the world what he is like. God’s people are meant to be different, in small everyday ways as well as big ones.
Verse 29. 'And the Levite, (because he hath no part nor inheritance with thee,) and the stranger, and the fatherless, and the widow, which are within thy gates, shall come, and shall eat and be satisfied; that the LORD thy God may bless thee in all the work of thine hand which thou doest.' Shall come, and shall eat and be satisfied. Not just a few scraps. Enough to be satisfied. God wanted the poor to be well fed. And he promised to bless those who shared. Generosity to the needy opens the way for God’s blessing.
John wrote, 'But as many as received him, to them gave he power to become the sons of God'. And Peter heard a voice say, 'What God hath cleansed, that call not thou common.'
Remember today that you are a child of God. And think of someone in need, a newcomer, a widow, a child without a parent, or someone who serves God and his people. How could you share something with them, so that they eat and are satisfied?
Here is today’s question. God built care for the poor into Israel’s tithe, so that the stranger, the fatherless and the widow would eat and be satisfied. How could you build generosity into the rhythm of your own life?
Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that I am your child. Thank you that you care for the stranger, the fatherless and the widow. Make me generous, and help me to share what you have given me, with joy. Let my everyday life show that I belong to you. Amen.
