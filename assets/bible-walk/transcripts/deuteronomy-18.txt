People have always wanted to know the future. They read the stars, look for signs, visit fortune tellers, or try to contact the dead. Anything to find out what is coming, and what to do. The nations around Israel did all of these things. But God tells his people: you do not need any of that. I will speak to you myself, through a Prophet.
In chapter seventeen, Moses looked ahead to a day when Israel would have a king. The king must not multiply horses, wives, or gold. Instead, he must write out a copy of God’s law, and read therein all the days of his life, that his heart be not lifted up above his brethren.
Now, in chapter eighteen, Moses speaks about the Levites, and about how God will speak to his people. Listen for the ways of the nations God forbids. And listen for the great Prophet God promises.
The priests and Levites, Moses says, will have no share of land among the tribes. They will live from the offerings given to the LORD. The LORD is their inheritance, as he promised them.
The people were to give the priests a portion of their sacrifices, and the first of their grain, wine, oil and wool. For God had chosen them to stand and serve in his name.
And if a Levite from any town in Israel comes with all his heart to the place the LORD chooses, to serve there, he may serve like all the other Levites, and share equally in the food.
Then Moses turns to the ways of the nations.
When you come into the land, Moses says, do not learn to copy the detestable things those nations do. Let no one be found among you who offers their son or daughter in the fire, or who practises fortune telling, or reads omens, or casts spells, or consults mediums, or tries to speak with the dead.
These were the ways the nations tried to find out the future, or to control what would happen. Some of it was cruel beyond words, like offering children in the fire. All of it turned people away from the true God.
Everyone who does these things is detestable to the LORD, Moses says. It is because of these practices that the LORD your God is driving out those nations before you.
And then Moses says something short and simple.
Perfect here means wholehearted, blameless, undivided. Do not try to find guidance from God and from fortune tellers at the same time. Be wholly his.
For these nations listen to fortune tellers and diviners, Moses says. But as for you, the LORD your God has not allowed you to do so.
So how would Israel hear from God? Moses gives them a wonderful promise.
Moses reminds them of the day at Horeb. You asked the LORD your God, let us not hear the voice of the LORD our God again, or see this great fire any more, or we will die. And the LORD said, they have spoken well.
And then God made a promise.
And God says, whoever will not listen to my words, which the Prophet speaks in my name, I will hold him responsible.
Who was this Prophet? In one sense, God kept sending prophets to Israel, Samuel, Elijah, Isaiah, Jeremiah and many others, to speak his words. But Moses spoke of one Prophet, like Moses himself, who would speak face to face with God. And the people kept waiting. At the very end of Deuteronomy, we will hear that there never arose a prophet in Israel like Moses.
Until Jesus. When Jesus fed the five thousand, the people said, this is of a truth that prophet that should come into the world. And after Jesus rose from the dead, the apostle Peter stood in Jerusalem and quoted this very chapter, saying that Jesus was the Prophet Moses spoke of.
Then Moses warns about false prophets. If a prophet presumes to speak a word in my name that I have not commanded, or speaks in the name of other gods, that prophet will die.
And you may say in your heart, how can we know when a word is not from the LORD? Moses answers. When a prophet speaks in the name of the LORD, and the thing does not happen or come true, that is a word the LORD has not spoken. That prophet has spoken presumptuously. Do not be afraid of him.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 13. 'Thou shalt be perfect with the LORD thy God.' Seven words. Be wholehearted with God. Not divided, not looking for help from God with one hand and from other powers with the other. Jesus said, no man can serve two masters. God wants all of us.
Verse 15. 'The LORD thy God will raise up unto thee a Prophet from the midst of thee, of thy brethren, like unto me; unto him ye shall hearken;' A Prophet like Moses. One of their own people. And unto him ye shall hearken, listen. Christians believe that Jesus is this Prophet. And at the transfiguration, God spoke from the cloud about Jesus, saying, hear ye him.
Verse 18. 'I will raise them up a Prophet from among their brethren, like unto thee, and will put my words in his mouth; and he shall speak unto them all that I shall command him.' My words in his mouth. Jesus said, I have not spoken of myself; but the Father which sent me, he gave me a commandment, what I should say. When we listen to Jesus, we are hearing the very words of God.
Peter preached, 'For Moses truly said unto the fathers, A prophet shall the Lord your God raise up unto you of your brethren, like unto me; him shall ye hear in all things'. And a voice from the cloud said of Jesus, 'This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased; hear ye him.'
Is there anywhere you look for guidance other than God, horoscopes, superstitions, or anything that takes his place? Turn away from it today. And spend time listening to Jesus, the Prophet God promised, in the Gospels.
Here is today’s question. God told his people not to seek the future through fortune tellers, but promised to speak through a Prophet. Why do you think God wants us to listen to him, rather than seeking guidance anywhere else?
Let us pray. Lord God, I want to be wholehearted with you. Keep me from looking for guidance anywhere but you. Thank you for Jesus, the Prophet you promised, who speaks your very words. Help me to hear him, and to do what he says. Amen.
