You are walking home, and you see a neighbour’s sheep wandering loose on the road, far from its field. You could walk past. It is not your sheep. It is not your problem. And no one would ever know. But God says: do not hide yourself. Do not pretend you did not see. It is your problem, because it is your neighbour’s.
In chapter twenty one, Moses taught that every innocent death mattered, and the elders prayed, be merciful, O LORD. He protected the firstborn son of a less loved wife. And he said that a body hung on a tree must be buried the same day, for he that is hanged is accursed of God. Paul wrote that Christ was made a curse for us.
Now, in chapter twenty two, Moses gives a set of laws about everyday life. Listen for how they teach care for neighbours, animals and safety. Some of the laws in this chapter are about marriage, and we will tell those briefly.
The chapter begins with a neighbour’s lost animal.
And if your brother does not live nearby, or you do not know whose it is, Moses says, take it home with you, and keep it until he comes looking for it. Then give it back to him. Do the same with his donkey, his clothing, and anything he loses that you find. Do not hide yourself.
Do not hide yourself. That is the heart of it. It is easy to look away, to pretend we did not notice someone else’s trouble. God says, do not. Care about your neighbour’s things as if they were your own.
And if you see your brother’s animal fallen down on the road under a heavy load, do not look away. Help him lift it up again.
Long after, Jesus told a story about a man lying hurt by the road, and how two religious people saw him, and passed by on the other side. And a Samaritan stopped and helped. That is the kind of neighbour God wants us to be.
Then Moses gives a small and tender law.
If you find a bird’s nest in a tree or on the ground, with the mother bird sitting on the young or on the eggs, do not take the mother with the young.
Let the mother go free. God cared even about a mother bird. His people were not to be greedy or cruel, even with the smallest creatures.
Then Moses gives a law about building.
In those days, houses had flat roofs, where families would sit and work and sleep in hot weather. A low wall around the edge, a battlement, would stop anyone falling off.
It is a simple safety rule. But it shows that God cares about our neighbours’ safety. Being careless with other people’s lives is a sin. Loving your neighbour includes thinking ahead to keep them safe.
Then Moses gives some laws about keeping things separate. Do not plant two kinds of seed in your vineyard. Do not plough with an ox and a donkey together. Do not wear cloth made of wool and linen woven together. These laws may have been reminders that Israel was a people set apart, not to be mixed with the ways of the nations around them. And Moses repeats the law about tassels on the corners of their clothes, to remind them of God’s commands.
Then the chapter turns to laws about marriage and faithfulness. They are part of Israel’s law in that time, and Christians have understood them in different ways. Some protected a young wife from a husband who falsely accused her, to get rid of her. Such a husband was to be punished, fined, and never allowed to send her away. Others showed how seriously God took faithfulness in marriage.
And one law makes something very clear. If a woman was attacked in an isolated place, where she cried out, and no one was there to help her, she was innocent. She had done nothing wrong. The guilt belonged to the man alone. God’s law protected the victim.
God cares about the weak, the wronged, and those who cry out with no one to save them. He hears them, even when no one else does.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 1. 'Thou shalt not see the brother’s ox or his sheep go astray, and hide thyself from them: thou shalt in any case bring them again unto thy brother.' Bring them again unto thy brother. Love for our neighbour is practical. It means noticing, and stopping, and helping, even when it is inconvenient, and even when no one is watching.
Verse 7. 'But thou shalt in any wise let the dam go, and take the young to thee; that it may be well with thee, and that thou mayest prolong thy days.' The dam is the mother bird. God cared about even a small bird. Jesus said that not one sparrow falls to the ground without your Father. If God cares for a mother bird, how much more does he care for you.
Verse 8. 'When thou buildest a new house, then thou shalt make a battlement for thy roof, that thou bring not blood upon thine house, if any man fall from thence.' A wall on the roof, to keep people safe. God wants us to think ahead, and to take care that others are not harmed by our carelessness. Safety, too, is a way of loving our neighbour.
Jesus told of a Samaritan who 'went to him, and bound up his wounds' and 'took care of him.' And Jesus said, 'Are not two sparrows sold for a farthing? and one of them shall not fall on the ground without your Father.'
Look out today for someone who needs help, someone who has lost something, or is struggling under a heavy load. Do not hide yourself. Stop and help them, as the Samaritan did.
Here is today’s question. God said, thou shalt not hide thyself, when you see your neighbour’s lost sheep. Why do you think it is so easy to look away from other people’s troubles?
Let us pray. Lord God, forgive me for the times I have seen someone’s need, and hidden myself. Open my eyes to notice, and my hands to help. Thank you that you care for every sparrow, and every person who cries out. Help me to love my neighbour as myself. Amen.
