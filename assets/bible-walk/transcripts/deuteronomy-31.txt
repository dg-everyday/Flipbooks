An old leader stands before the people he has led for so long. He knows he will not be going with them any further. Beside him stands a younger man, who will take his place. The young man must be feeling the weight of it. How can anyone follow Moses? And the old leader turns to him, in front of everyone, and says the words he most needs to hear.
In chapter thirty, Moses promised that even after exile, if the people turned back to God, he would gather them home and change their hearts. He said, the word is very nigh unto thee, in thy mouth, and in thy heart. And he set before them life and death, and said, therefore choose life.
Now, in chapter thirty one, Moses prepares for the end of his leadership. Listen for what Moses says to the people, and then to Joshua. And listen for what they are to do with the law every seven years.
Moses goes and speaks these words to all Israel.
I am very old now, he says. I can no longer go out and come in as your leader. And the LORD has said to me, you shall not cross this Jordan.
Moses knew his time was nearly over. But he did not want the people to be afraid.
The LORD your God himself will cross over ahead of you, he says. He will destroy these nations before you, and you will take their land. And Joshua will cross over ahead of you, as the LORD has said.
The LORD will do to them what he did to Sihon and Og, the kings of the Amorites. When the LORD gives them into your hand, do everything I have commanded you.
And then Moses gives the people these words.
Then Moses calls Joshua, and speaks to him in front of all Israel. Be strong and of a good courage, he says, for you must go with this people into the land the LORD swore to give their fathers, and you shall lead them to inherit it.
And then he repeats the promise, this time for Joshua himself.
Notice the words. He goes before thee. Joshua would never walk into a battle, or a hard decision, alone. God would always be ahead of him, already there.
Then Moses writes down this law, and gives it to the priests, the sons of Levi, who carried the ark of the covenant, and to all the elders of Israel.
And he gives them a command. At the end of every seven years, at the feast of tabernacles, when all Israel comes to appear before the LORD at the place he chooses, you shall read this law aloud to all Israel.
And Moses says who should be there.
Everyone. Not only the leaders, or the priests, or the grown men. Women, children and foreigners too. And especially the children, who had never heard it, so that they could learn to fear the LORD.
Then the LORD speaks to Moses. Your time to die is coming near. Call Joshua, and come to the tent of meeting, so that I may give him his charge.
So Moses and Joshua go to the tent of meeting. And the LORD appears there, in a pillar of cloud, standing over the door of the tent.
And God tells Moses something heartbreaking. After you are gone, this people will turn away, and follow other gods, and break my covenant. And when troubles come upon them, they will say, have these troubles come upon us because our God is not among us?
God knew what was coming. But he did not give up on them. Instead, he gave them a gift.
Write down this song, God tells Moses, and teach it to the children of Israel. Put it in their mouths, so that this song may be a witness for me. When troubles come, this song will speak to them, for it will not be forgotten from the mouths of their children.
A song is easy to remember. Long after the people had forgotten God’s laws, they might still remember the words of a song. And that song would remind them of the truth, and call them home.
So Moses writes down the song that same day, and teaches it to the children of Israel.
And then the LORD himself gives Joshua his charge. Be strong and of a good courage, for you shall bring the children of Israel into the land I swore to give them. And I will be with you.
When Moses finishes writing the words of this law in a book, he gives it to the Levites who carry the ark. Put this book of the law beside the ark of the covenant of the LORD your God, he says, so that it may be there as a witness.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 6. 'Be strong and of a good courage, fear not, nor be afraid of them: for the LORD thy God, he it is that doth go with thee; he will not fail thee, nor forsake thee.' Courage does not come from being strong ourselves. It comes from knowing who goes with us. The letter to the Hebrews quotes this promise for every believer: I will never leave thee, nor forsake thee.
Verse 8. 'And the LORD, he it is that doth go before thee; he will be with thee, he will not fail thee, neither forsake thee: fear not, neither be dismayed.' God goes before us, and he goes with us. Whatever lies ahead, he is already there. That is why we do not need to be afraid or dismayed.
Verse 12. 'Gather the people together, men, and women, and children, and thy stranger that is within thy gates, that they may hear, and that they may learn, and fear the LORD your God, and observe to do all the words of this law:' Everyone was to hear God’s word, children and foreigners included. God wants every person to hear his word, and learn it, and live it. No one is too young, or too much of an outsider.
The letter to the Hebrews says, 'for he hath said, I will never leave thee, nor forsake thee.' And in the book of Joshua, God said, 'Be strong and of a good courage; be not afraid, neither be thou dismayed: for the LORD thy God is with thee whithersoever thou goest.'
Is there something ahead of you that makes you afraid? Remember: God goes before you, and with you. He will not fail you, nor forsake you. Take one brave step today, trusting in him.
Here is today’s question. Moses told Joshua that God would go before him, and be with him. How does it help to know that God is already ahead of you, in whatever you face?
Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that you go before me, and with me. You will never fail me, nor forsake me. When I am afraid, give me courage. Help me to listen to your word, and to pass it on to those who come after me. Be with me wherever I go. Amen.
