High on a cliff, a mother eagle stirs up her nest. Her young ones have grown, and it is time for them to fly. So she pushes them out. They tumble through the air, flapping wildly. And then, just before they fall too far, she swoops underneath them, catches them on her wings, and carries them back up. Moses says that is how God cared for his people.
In chapter thirty one, Moses told the people that he would not cross the Jordan. He said to Joshua, be strong and of a good courage. And he promised, the LORD will not fail thee, nor forsake thee. He told them to read the law to everyone every seven years. And God told Moses to write a song, to be a witness to the people.
Now, in chapter thirty two, Moses sings that song. Listen for the name Moses gives to God, again and again. And listen for the picture of the eagle.
Moses begins by calling on all creation to listen.
Give ear, O ye heavens, and I will speak; and hear, O earth, the words of my mouth.
And then he says how he hopes his words will fall on them. 'My doctrine shall drop as the rain, my speech shall distil as the dew'. Gentle, refreshing, soaking in, like rain on young plants.
For I will proclaim the name of the LORD, Moses sings. Ascribe greatness to our God.
And then he gives God a name that he will repeat all through the song.
The Rock. Solid, strong, unchanging. A place to stand, and a place to hide. Whatever else shifts, God stays the same.
But his people have not been like their Rock, Moses sings. They have acted corruptly. They are a crooked and twisted generation. Is this how you repay the LORD, you foolish and unwise people? Is he not your Father, who made you, and established you?
Then Moses calls them to remember.
'Remember the days of old, consider the years of many generations: ask thy father, and he will shew thee; thy elders, and they will tell thee.'
Then Moses tells how God chose Israel. For the LORD’s portion is his people, Jacob is the lot of his inheritance.
And Moses describes how God found them.
The apple of the eye is the pupil, the tender centre of the eye, which we protect more carefully than almost anything. That is how carefully God guarded his people.
And then comes the picture of the eagle.
So the LORD alone led him, Moses sings, and there was no strange god with him.
God made his people ride on the high places of the earth, and fed them with the best of the land, honey from the rock, and oil from the flinty rock, butter and milk, and the finest wheat.
But then the song turns sad.
Jeshurun is a tender name for Israel. It means the upright one. But Jeshurun grew fat and comfortable, and kicked, like a well fed animal. Then he forsook God which made him, and lightly esteemed the Rock of his salvation.
Being blessed made them forget the one who blessed them. They turned to new gods, gods their fathers had never known. 'Of the Rock that begat thee thou art unmindful, and hast forgotten God that formed thee.'
And the song speaks of God’s grief and anger, and of the hard times that would come to them, because they had turned away. But even here, God holds back. He says that he would not destroy them completely, so that their enemies could not boast that they had done it themselves.
And God says, 'To me belongeth vengeance, and recompence'. Paul later quoted these words, and told believers never to take revenge themselves, but to leave it to God.
And the song ends with hope. 'For the LORD shall judge his people, and repent himself for his servants'. God would have compassion on them, when he saw that their strength was gone.
When Moses has finished speaking all these words to all Israel, he says to them, 'Set your hearts unto all the words which I testify among you this day'. Teach them to your children. 'For it is not a vain thing for you; because it is your life'.
God’s word is not empty words. It is life itself.
And that same day, the LORD speaks to Moses. Go up into the mountains of Abarim, to mount Nebo, in the land of Moab, across from Jericho, and look at the land of Canaan, which I am giving to the children of Israel.
There, God says, you will be gathered to your people, just as your brother Aaron was gathered to his people on mount Hor. Because at the waters of Meribah, you did not honour me as holy among the children of Israel.
'Yet thou shalt see the land before thee; but thou shalt not go thither unto the land which I give the children of Israel.'
Moses would see it. But he would not enter it.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 4. 'He is the Rock, his work is perfect: for all his ways are judgment: a God of truth and without iniquity, just and right is he.' The Rock. God never changes, never fails, never does wrong. Everything he does is perfect and just. When life feels unsteady, we can stand on him. Jesus said the wise man built his house upon a rock.
Verse 10. 'He found him in a desert land, and in the waste howling wilderness; he led him about, he instructed him, he kept him as the apple of his eye.' God found his people when they were lost in the howling wilderness. He led them, taught them, and guarded them as carefully as the apple of his eye. That is how he cares for each of his children.
Verse 11. 'As an eagle stirreth up her nest, fluttereth over her young, spreadeth abroad her wings, taketh them, beareth them on her wings:' Sometimes God stirs up our nest. He lets life become unsettled, so that we learn to fly. But he never lets us fall. He is always there, underneath, ready to catch us, and carry us on his wings.
Isaiah wrote, 'they shall mount up with wings as eagles; they shall run, and not be weary; and they shall walk, and not faint.' And a psalm says, 'Keep me as the apple of the eye, hide me under the shadow of thy wings'.
Has God stirred up your nest lately? Things changing, unsettled, uncomfortable? Trust that he is teaching you to fly, and that his wings are underneath you. Thank him today that he is your Rock.
Here is today’s question. Moses said God is like an eagle, stirring up her nest, but catching her young on her wings. Has there been a time when God let things become unsettled in your life, but then caught you and carried you?
Let us pray. Lord God, you are my Rock. Your work is perfect, and all your ways are just. Thank you that you found me, and keep me as the apple of your eye. When you stir up my nest, help me to trust you. Carry me on your wings. And never let me forget you, the God who made me. Amen.
