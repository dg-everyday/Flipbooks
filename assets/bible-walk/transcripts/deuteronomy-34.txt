An old man climbs a mountain alone. He has walked with these people through the sea, through the desert, through rebellion and grumbling and battle. He has carried them in prayer. And now, at the top, God shows him the whole promised land, stretching out below, green and beautiful, all the way to the sea. He can see it. But he will not walk into it. This is the last chapter of Deuteronomy, and the last day of Moses.
In chapter thirty three, Moses, the man of God, blessed each of the tribes of Israel. He said that Benjamin would dwell between the shoulders of the LORD. He said, as thy days, so shall thy strength be. And he said, the eternal God is thy refuge, and underneath are the everlasting arms.
Now, in chapter thirty four, Moses climbs mount Nebo. Listen for what God shows him, and what God says. And listen for how the Bible remembers Moses at the very end.
Moses goes up from the plains of Moab to mount Nebo, to the top of Pisgah, across from Jericho.
And there, the LORD shows him all the land.
The land of Gilead, all the way to Dan in the far north. All of Naphtali, the land of Ephraim and Manasseh, and all the land of Judah, as far as the western sea. The south country, and the plain of the valley of Jericho, the city of palm trees, as far as Zoar.
From the top of the mountain, Moses could see the whole land, every part of it, laid out like a map. Everything God had promised, since the days of Abraham, was there before his eyes.
And then the LORD speaks to him.
Moses could not enter. But God gave him this gift. He let him see it. All the promises God had made to Abraham, Isaac and Jacob were about to come true. And Moses saw it with his own eyes.
Then the chapter says, simply, 'So Moses the servant of the LORD died there in the land of Moab, according to the word of the LORD.'
The servant of the LORD. Of all the titles Moses could have had, leader, prophet, lawgiver, the Bible chooses this one. He was God’s servant.
And then something remarkable happens. 'And he buried him in a valley in the land of Moab'. God himself buried Moses. 'But no man knoweth of his sepulchre unto this day.'
No one knew where Moses’ grave was. There was no tomb for people to visit, no shrine to worship at. God took care of his faithful servant himself, privately, tenderly.
Moses died in full strength. 'His eye was not dim, nor his natural force abated.' His body was still strong, and his eyes still clear. He did not fade away. God simply called him home.
And the children of Israel wept for Moses on the plains of Moab for thirty days, until the days of weeping and mourning for Moses were ended.
Then the story turns to the future.
Moses had laid his hands on Joshua, passing the leadership on. And God filled Joshua with wisdom for the task ahead. The people listened to him. The story would go on.
And then the book of Deuteronomy ends, with these words about Moses.
Face to face. God spoke with Moses as a friend speaks with a friend. No other prophet in Israel’s history was like him. No one else did such signs and wonders, in Egypt, before Pharaoh, and in the sight of all Israel.
But remember, in chapter eighteen, Moses said that God would one day raise up a prophet like him. 'The LORD thy God will raise up unto thee a Prophet from the midst of thee, of thy brethren, like unto me; unto him ye shall hearken'.
Christians believe that prophet is Jesus. Peter said so, in the book of Acts. And the letter to the Hebrews says that Moses was faithful as a servant in God’s house, but Christ as a Son over his own house.
And here is a beautiful thing. Moses never stood in the promised land in his lifetime. But in the gospels, on a high mountain, Jesus was transfigured, and his face shone like the sun. And who appeared, talking with him? Moses and Elijah. At last, Moses stood in the promised land, speaking with the very Prophet he had promised.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 4. 'And the LORD said unto him, This is the land which I sware unto Abraham, unto Isaac, and unto Jacob, saying, I will give it unto thy seed: I have caused thee to see it with thine eyes, but thou shalt not go over thither.' God keeps every promise. What he swore to Abraham, Isaac and Jacob, he was about to do. And he let Moses see it. God’s promises do not fail, even when we do not live to see all of them.
Verse 9. 'And Joshua the son of Nun was full of the spirit of wisdom; for Moses had laid his hands upon him: and the children of Israel hearkened unto him, and did as the LORD commanded Moses.' Leaders come and go, but God’s work goes on. When one servant finishes, God raises up another, and gives them what they need. No one is the end of God’s story.
Verse 10. 'And there arose not a prophet since in Israel like unto Moses, whom the LORD knew face to face,' Whom the LORD knew face to face. That is the greatest thing said about Moses. Not his miracles, not his laws, but his friendship with God. And through Jesus, every believer is invited into that same friendship.
The letter to the Hebrews says, 'And Moses verily was faithful in all his house, as a servant… But Christ as a son over his own house; whose house are we'. And Jesus said, 'Henceforth I call you not servants… but I have called you friends'.
Moses was remembered as God’s servant, and God’s friend. Spend some time with God today, simply talking with him, as a friend. And thank him that his promises never fail.
Here is today’s question. The last words about Moses say that the LORD knew him face to face. What do you think it means to know God as a friend?
Let us pray. Lord God, thank you for Moses, your servant, who led your people so faithfully. Thank you that you keep every promise. Thank you for Jesus, the Prophet greater than Moses, who calls me his friend. Help me to know you, and to walk with you, all my days, until I see your face. Amen.
