A great city roars with noise. Thousands of people pack into a huge open air theatre, shouting the same words over and over, for two hours. Great is Diana of the Ephesians! They are furious, because a small group of people have stopped buying silver statues of their goddess. These people say there is only one God, and his name is Jesus. That city was Ephesus. And some years later, Paul wrote a letter to the believers who lived there.
Welcome to the letter to the Ephesians, on The Living Voice. Ephesians has six chapters, so on this walk it is six steps, one chapter at a time.
We have just walked through the book of Proverbs, a book of practical wisdom for everyday life. Ephesians is very different. It soars up into heaven, and shows us God’s great plan. But it also comes right back down to earth, into our homes and workplaces.
We met Ephesus when we walked through the book of Acts. It was one of the greatest cities of the Roman world, on the coast of what is now Turkey. It had a famous temple to the goddess Diana, one of the wonders of the ancient world. Paul stayed there for a long time, teaching every day. Many people turned to Jesus. Those who had practised magic brought their books and burned them, a fortune’s worth. And the silversmiths, who made little statues of Diana, started a riot, because their business was suffering.
Later, Paul met the leaders of the church from Ephesus on a beach, said goodbye to them, and they all wept, because they thought they would never see him again.
Now Paul is in prison. He calls himself, the prisoner of the Lord. And he writes this letter, which his friend Tychicus carries to them.
The letter has two halves. In chapters one to three, Paul tells the believers who they are in Christ: chosen, loved, forgiven, saved by grace, brought near, made one family. In chapters four to six, he tells them how to live, because of who they are: in unity, in love, in the light, at home, at work, and in the battle against evil. First, what God has done. Then, how we respond.
Here are four things to watch for as we walk.
Watch for two little words: in Christ. Paul uses them, and words like them, again and again. Every blessing we have, we have in Christ.
Watch for grace. By grace are ye saved, Paul writes. Grace means God’s kindness that we do not deserve, and could never earn.
Watch for one body. Paul is amazed that God has brought together people who used to be enemies, and made them one family in Christ.
And watch for the word walk. Once Paul has told us who we are, he tells us how to walk: worthy of our calling, in love, as children of light.
A word before we begin. A few passages in Ephesians, about God’s choosing, and about marriage and work, are understood in different ways by faithful Christians. We will tell them as the Bible tells them, and point out where Christians have understood them differently.
Here is a question to carry with you. Ephesians first tells us who we are in Christ, and then how to live. How might knowing who you are in Christ change the way you live each day?
Let us pray. Lord God, as I walk through the letter to the Ephesians, open the eyes of my understanding. Show me the riches of your grace, and who I am in Christ. Help me to walk worthy of your calling, in love and in the light. Amen.
