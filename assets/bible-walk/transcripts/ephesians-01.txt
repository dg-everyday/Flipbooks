A child stands in an orphanage, holding a small bag with everything she owns. A couple walks in, kneels down in front of her, and smiles. We have chosen you, they say. You are ours now. Everything we have is yours. You will carry our name. And she realises that she will never be alone again. Paul says that God has done something like that for us, and much more.
In the introduction, we heard that Ephesus was a great city, famous for its temple to Diana, where Paul taught and many believed. Paul wrote this letter from prison. And we heard that the letter has two halves: who we are in Christ, and how we live. And we heard four things to watch for: in Christ, grace, one body, and walk.
Now, in chapter one, Paul begins. Listen for how many blessings he names. And listen for what he prays for the believers.
Paul begins with a short greeting. 'Paul, an apostle of Jesus Christ by the will of God, to the saints which are at Ephesus, and to the faithful in Christ Jesus'. Saints here means all God’s people, all believers. 'Grace be to you, and peace'.
And then Paul bursts into praise. In the Greek language he wrote in, verses three to fourteen are one enormous sentence, as if Paul is so excited he cannot stop to take a breath.
He begins with this.
And then he lists the blessings, one after another.
First, God chose us. 'According as he hath chosen us in him before the foundation of the world, that we should be holy and without blame before him in love'. Before God made the world, he had his people in mind. Christians have understood what it means for God to choose us in different ways. But all agree on this: our salvation starts with God’s love and God’s grace, not with us.
Second, God adopted us. He planned for us to become his own children, through Jesus Christ. 'To the praise of the glory of his grace, wherein he hath made us accepted in the beloved.' The beloved is Jesus, God’s beloved Son. Because we are in him, God accepts us. Welcomed. Wanted.
Third, God forgave us.
Fourth, God showed us his plan. He has made known to us the mystery of his will. A mystery here means a secret that God has now revealed. And what is it? That one day, God will bring everything together under Christ, all things in heaven and on earth.
Fifth, God gave us an inheritance, a share in everything that belongs to him.
And sixth, God sealed us with his Spirit. 'In whom ye also trusted, after that ye heard the word of truth, the gospel of your salvation: in whom also after that ye believed, ye were sealed with that holy Spirit of promise'. In those days, a seal on a letter or a package showed who it belonged to, and that it was safe. When we believe, God puts his seal on us, the Holy Spirit. We belong to him.
And Paul says the Spirit is 'the earnest of our inheritance'. An earnest is a down payment, the first part of a payment, which promises that the rest will come. The Spirit in our lives now is a taste of all that God will give us one day.
Chosen, adopted, accepted, forgiven, shown his plan, given an inheritance, and sealed. And Paul says three times that all this is to the praise of his glory.
Then Paul tells them how he prays for them. Ever since he heard of their faith in the Lord Jesus, and their love for all God’s people, he has not stopped thanking God for them. And he prays that God would give them the spirit of wisdom and revelation, so that they would know him better.
And then he prays something beautiful.
He also prays that they would know the exceeding greatness of God’s power towards those who believe. How great is that power? It is the same power that God used to raise Jesus from the dead, and seat him at his right hand in heaven.
Far above every ruler and authority, every power and every name, not only in this world, but in the world to come. God has put all things under Christ’s feet, and made him head over everything, for the sake of the church. 'Which is his body, the fulness of him that filleth all in all.'
What a picture. Jesus is the head, and the church, all believers together, is his body. The same power that raised Jesus from the dead is at work in us.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 3. 'Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, who hath blessed us with all spiritual blessings in heavenly places in Christ'. All spiritual blessings. Not some. All. In Christ, God has already given us every spiritual blessing we need. We do not have to earn them, or wait for them, or beg for them. They are ours, in Christ. The heavenly places means the spiritual world, where Christ now reigns.
Verse 7. 'In whom we have redemption through his blood, the forgiveness of sins, according to the riches of his grace'. Redemption means being bought back, set free by paying a price. Like a slave bought out of slavery. The price was Jesus’ blood, his life given on the cross. And through it, we have forgiveness. All our sins, forgiven. Not according to how good we are, but according to the riches of his grace. God is rich in grace, and he gives it freely.
Verse 18. 'The eyes of your understanding being enlightened; that ye may know what is the hope of his calling, and what the riches of the glory of his inheritance in the saints'. The eyes of your understanding means the eyes of your heart. Paul prays that our hearts would be filled with light, so we can see three things: the hope we have been called to, the riches God has for us, and the power at work in us. We need God to open our eyes. Otherwise we may never see how rich we really are.
Paul wrote to the Romans, 'ye have received the Spirit of adoption, whereby we cry, Abba, Father.' And John wrote, 'Behold, what manner of love the Father hath bestowed upon us, that we should be called the sons of God'.
Read through the list again: chosen, adopted, accepted, forgiven, sealed. Pick one of these blessings, and thank God for it today. And pray Paul’s prayer for someone you love: that the eyes of their heart would be opened.
Here is today’s question. Paul says we are accepted in the beloved. How does it make you feel to know that, in Christ, God accepts you and welcomes you as his child?
Let us pray. Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, who has blessed us with every spiritual blessing in Christ. Thank you that you chose me, adopted me, accepted me, forgave me, and sealed me with your Spirit. Open the eyes of my heart, so that I may see the hope, the riches and the power that are mine in Christ. Amen.
