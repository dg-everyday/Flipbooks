A young woman walks through the gates of the king’s palace for the first time. She has no mother and no father. She has left behind the only family she has, her cousin who raised her. She has been told to keep one thing secret: who her people are. She does not know what will happen to her. But every step she takes is part of a plan bigger than she can imagine.
In chapter one, King Ahasuerus held a great feast to show off his glory. When he called for Queen Vashti to come and show her beauty, she refused. On the advice of his princes, the king made a law that could not be changed: Vashti would come no more before the king, and her royal place would be given to another.
Now, in chapter two, we meet the two heroes of the story, Mordecai and Esther. Listen for how Esther wins the favour of everyone around her. And notice a small event at the end of the chapter, which seems forgotten, but which God will use later.
After these things, when the king’s anger has cooled, he remembers Vashti, and what she did, and what was decreed against her.
So his servants suggest a plan. Let officers be sent into every province, to gather beautiful young women to the palace in Shushan. And let the one who pleases the king become queen instead of Vashti. The king likes the idea, and does it.
Now in Shushan the palace there is a certain Jew, whose name is Mordecai, of the tribe of Benjamin. His family was among those carried away from Jerusalem to Babylon, long before.
Mordecai has a young cousin.
So when the king’s order is given, and many young women are gathered to the palace, Esther is taken too, into the care of Hegai, the king’s chamberlain who is in charge of the women.
Esther pleases Hegai, and wins his kindness. He quickly gives her the beauty treatments and the food she needs, and seven maids from the king’s house, and moves her and her maids to the best place in the house of the women.
But Esther has not told anyone who her people are, or her family. Mordecai has told her not to.
And every day, Mordecai walks back and forth in front of the courtyard of the women’s house, to find out how Esther is, and what is happening to her. He loves her like a father.
Each young woman must spend a long time in preparation, with oils and perfumes, before she is brought to the king. When it is her turn, she may take with her anything she wants.
When Esther’s turn comes, she asks for nothing except what Hegai advises. She trusts the one who knows the king best. And Esther wins the favour of everyone who sees her.
Esther is brought to King Ahasuerus in the seventh year of his reign. And the king loves Esther above all the women. She wins his grace and favour, and he sets the royal crown upon her head, and makes her queen instead of Vashti.
Then the king holds a great feast for all his princes and servants, Esther’s feast. He grants a holiday to the provinces, and gives gifts with royal generosity.
But even as queen, Esther keeps her secret.
In those days, while Mordecai is sitting in the king’s gate, he overhears something dangerous. Two of the king’s chamberlains, Bigthan and Teresh, who guard the door, are angry, and are plotting to lay hands on the king.
The investigation finds that it is true, and both men are hanged. And the whole thing is written down in the book of the chronicles, the official record, in the king’s presence.
And then, it seems, it is forgotten. Mordecai saved the king’s life, and receives no reward. No thank you. Nothing. But it is written in the book. And in a few chapters, at exactly the right moment, someone will read it.
Esther’s life has changed completely. She has gone from orphan to queen. But she did not plan any of this. She did not seek the crown. She simply did what was in front of her, with grace and humility, listening to wise advice. And step by step, she was being placed exactly where God would need her.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 7. 'And he brought up Hadassah, that is, Esther, his uncle’s daughter: for she had neither father nor mother, and the maid was fair and beautiful; whom Mordecai, when her father and mother were dead, took for his own daughter.' Esther had two names. Hadassah, her Hebrew name, means myrtle, a fragrant evergreen shrub. Esther, her Persian name, probably means star. She began life with great loss: no father, and no mother. But God placed her in the care of Mordecai, who took her as his own daughter. God sets the lonely in families. Esther’s story begins with an act of quiet love.
Verse 20. 'Esther had not yet shewed her kindred nor her people; as Mordecai had charged her: for Esther did the commandment of Mordecai, like as when she was brought up with him.' Esther was now the queen of Persia, the most powerful woman in the empire. And yet she still listened to the cousin who had raised her. The crown did not make her proud. Esther had won the favour of Hegai, of everyone who saw her, and now of the king. The book does not say that God gave her this favour. But we can see his hand. And the secret she was keeping would one day become the key to the whole story.
Verse 22. 'And the thing was known to Mordecai, who told it unto Esther the queen; and Esther certified the king thereof in Mordecai’s name.' Mordecai could have kept quiet. The king was a foreign ruler, and Mordecai owed him nothing. But Mordecai did what was right, and saved the king’s life. And he received no reward, not yet. Sometimes we do what is right, and no one notices, and nothing seems to come of it. But God notices. Nothing done in faithfulness is ever wasted.
A psalm says, 'A father of the fatherless, and a judge of the widows, is God in his holy habitation. God setteth the solitary in families'. And the book of Proverbs says, 'A man’s heart deviseth his way: but the LORD directeth his steps.'
Like Mordecai, have you done something good that no one noticed or thanked you for? Do not give up. God sees, and he does not forget. Today, do one good thing quietly, trusting God to see it.
Here is today’s question. Esther did not plan to become queen, but step by step, she was placed where God would need her. Where has God placed you right now, and why might he have put you there?
Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that you are a father to the fatherless, and that you set the lonely in families. Thank you that you see every good thing done in secret. Help me to live with grace and humility where you have placed me, trusting that you are directing my steps. Amen.
