A young queen stands at the edge of the throne room, dressed in her royal robes. She has fasted for three days, with no food and no water. Across the room, on a high throne, sits her husband, the king. He has not asked for her. If he is angry, she will die. If he lifts his golden sceptre, she will live. She takes a breath, and steps into his sight.
In chapter four, the Jews mourned in sackcloth because of Haman’s decree. Mordecai urged Esther to go to the king and plead for her people. She told him the danger: anyone who came uninvited could die. But Mordecai asked, who knoweth whether thou art come to the kingdom for such a time as this? And Esther said, fast for me, and I will go in unto the king, and if I perish, I perish.
Now, in chapter five, Esther goes in. Listen for her wisdom and patience, as well as her courage. And watch Haman, the proud man, whose joy is spoiled by one man who will not stand up for him.
On the third day, Esther puts on her royal robes, and stands in the inner court of the king’s house, facing the king’s hall. The king is sitting on his royal throne, facing the entrance.
Everything hangs on this moment.
Esther has done the most dangerous part. But she does not rush. Instead of asking for her people’s lives straight away, she makes a simple request. If it seem good unto the king, let the king and Haman come this day unto the banquet that I have prepared for him.
Esther had already prepared the banquet before she went in. She had planned carefully. She knew that this was not the moment to accuse the king’s most powerful man in front of the whole court.
The king says, hurry, bring Haman, so that we may do as Esther has asked. So the king and Haman come to the banquet that Esther has prepared.
At the banquet, as they are drinking wine, the king asks Esther again, what is thy petition? It shall be granted thee. And what is thy request? Even to the half of the kingdom it shall be performed.
Esther answers, my petition and my request is this. If I have found favour in the king’s sight, and if it please the king to grant my petition, let the king and Haman come to another banquet that I will prepare for them. And then I will do as the king has said, and tell him my request.
Why does Esther wait? The Bible does not say. Perhaps she sensed it was not yet the right moment. Perhaps she wanted the king’s curiosity to grow. But as we will see, this one day of waiting will change everything. Something is about to happen that Esther could never have planned.
Haman goes out that day joyful, and with a glad heart. He has been invited to a private banquet with the king and queen. Twice! Life could not be better.
But then, as he passes through the king’s gate, he sees Mordecai. And Mordecai does not stand up, or even move, for him. Haman is filled with fury against Mordecai.
But he controls himself, and goes home. He calls his friends and his wife Zeresh together. And he boasts to them. He tells them about the glory of his riches, and how many sons he has, and how the king has promoted him above all the princes and servants of the king.
He says, what is more, Queen Esther let no one come with the king to the banquet she prepared, except me. And I am invited to come with the king again.
But there is something he cannot get over.
Haman has wealth, power, a big family, and the favour of the king and queen. But one man who will not bow to him spoils it all.
His wife Zeresh and all his friends give him advice. Have a gallows built, fifty cubits high, as tall as a building of several floors. And speak to the king first thing, that Mordecai may be hanged on it. Then go happily with the king to the banquet.
The idea pleases Haman, and he has the gallows built.
So the chapter ends with a tall wooden gallows standing in Haman’s courtyard, waiting for Mordecai. Haman thinks he has solved his problem. He has no idea what the next few hours will bring.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 2. 'And it was so, when the king saw Esther the queen standing in the court, that she obtained favour in his sight: and the king held out to Esther the golden sceptre that was in his hand. So Esther drew near, and touched the top of the sceptre.' The moment Esther feared most turned out to be the moment of grace. The king held out his sceptre, and she was welcomed. The people had fasted, and Esther had risked everything, and the king’s heart was turned toward her. And think of this. Esther had to risk her life to come to an earthly king. But we may come to the King of heaven at any time, without fear, because Jesus has opened the way for us.
Verse 3. 'Then said the king unto her, What wilt thou, queen Esther? and what is thy request? it shall be even given thee to the half of the kingdom.' Up to half of the kingdom. This was a way of saying, ask for anything. The king must have known that Esther would never have risked her life for something small. He was ready to listen. Esther came trembling, and she found a king willing to give her more than she dared to ask.
Verse 13. 'Yet all this availeth me nothing, so long as I see Mordecai the Jew sitting at the king’s gate.' Availeth me nothing means it is worth nothing to me. Haman had almost everything a person could want. And he was miserable, because of one man who would not bow. That is what pride does. It is never satisfied. A proud and bitter heart can have everything, and still enjoy nothing. Envy and bitterness steal the joy from every blessing we have.
The letter to the Hebrews says, 'Let us therefore come boldly unto the throne of grace, that we may obtain mercy, and find grace to help in time of need.' And the book of Proverbs says, 'A sound heart is the life of the flesh: but envy the rottenness of the bones.'
Esther came to the king in fear. But you can come to God boldly, through Jesus, at any time. What do you need to bring to the throne of grace today? Bring it now. And if there is a Mordecai in your life, someone whose existence spoils your joy, ask God to set you free from that bitterness.
Here is today’s question. Haman had so much, yet he said, all this availeth me nothing. What helps you to be content and thankful, instead of letting one thing spoil all your blessings?
Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that I do not need to be afraid to come to you. Thank you that through Jesus, you hold out your welcome to me. Help me to come boldly to your throne of grace. Guard my heart from pride and envy, and teach me to be thankful for all that you have given me. Amen.
