The most powerful king in the world lies awake, unable to sleep. He tosses and turns. Finally he calls for a servant to read to him, something dull enough to help him drift off: the royal records. And the servant just happens to open the book at a page about a man who once saved the king’s life, and was never thanked.
In chapter five, Esther risked her life and went to the king uninvited. He held out the golden sceptre, and offered her up to half of his kingdom. She invited him and Haman to a banquet, and then to a second one. Haman went home full of joy, until he saw Mordecai, who would not stand for him. And Haman had a gallows built, to hang Mordecai on it.
Now, in chapter six, everything starts to turn around. This is one of the most surprising chapters in the Bible, and one of the funniest. Listen for all the things that happen at just the right moment.
The chapter opens in the king’s bedroom.
The servants read to him. And it is found written that Mordecai had told about Bigthana and Teresh, two of the king’s chamberlains who guarded the door, who had plotted to lay hands on King Ahasuerus.
The king asks, what honour and dignity has been given to Mordecai for this? His servants answer, nothing has been done for him.
Just then, the king asks, who is in the court?
And who should be there? Haman. He has come to the outer court of the king’s house, at that very moment, to ask the king to hang Mordecai on the gallows he has prepared for him.
The servants say, Haman is standing in the court. The king says, let him come in.
So Haman comes in. And before Haman can say a word about Mordecai, the king asks him a question.
So Haman answers, thinking he is describing his own reward. For the man whom the king delights to honour, let royal robes be brought, which the king himself has worn, and the horse that the king rides on, with a royal crown on its head. Let one of the king’s most noble princes dress the man in the robes, and lead him on horseback through the street of the city, and call out before him, thus shall it be done to the man whom the king delighteth to honour.
Haman has just described his dream. To wear the king’s robes, ride the king’s horse, and have everyone in the city see how great he is.
Then the king says to Haman, hurry! Take the robes and the horse, as you have said, and do exactly that to Mordecai the Jew, who sits at the king’s gate. Leave out nothing of all that you have spoken.
We can only imagine Haman’s face.
So Haman takes the robes and the horse, and dresses Mordecai, and leads him on horseback through the street of the city, calling out before him, 'Thus shall it be done unto the man whom the king delighteth to honour.'
Haman came that day to hang Mordecai. Instead, he has to lead him through the city as a hero, shouting his praises. The man Haman hated most is honoured, by Haman’s own mouth, in Haman’s own words.
Afterward, Mordecai goes back to the king’s gate. He does not seem to make a fuss. He just returns to his place. But Haman hurries home, mourning, with his head covered in shame.
Haman tells his wife Zeresh, and all his friends, everything that has happened to him.
And while they are still talking with him, the king’s chamberlains arrive, and hurry Haman away to the banquet that Esther has prepared.
Think about everything that had to happen at exactly the right moment for this chapter to take place. The king could not sleep, at that very time. The servant read from the records, and found the very page about Mordecai. The king realised Mordecai had never been rewarded. And Haman walked into the court at that very moment, to ask for Mordecai’s death. Esther’s one day of waiting gave time for all of it.
Was it all just luck? The writer of Esther never says that God did it. He just tells us what happened, and lets us see it for ourselves. God is not named, but his fingerprints are everywhere.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 1. 'On that night could not the king sleep, and he commanded to bring the book of records of the chronicles; and they were read before the king.' Of all the times for the king to be unable to sleep, it was this one. Haman’s gallows was ready. Mordecai’s life hung in the balance. Something small, a king who could not sleep, became the turning point of the whole story. God often works through small things. A sleepless hour, a chance meeting, a page in a book. Nothing is too small for him to use.
Verse 6. 'So Haman came in. And the king said unto him, What shall be done unto the man whom the king delighteth to honour? Now Haman thought in his heart, To whom would the king delight to do honour more than to myself?' To whom would the king delight to do honour more than to myself? Haman could not imagine anyone else being honoured. His pride blinded him. He was so full of himself that he walked straight into the trap of his own words. Pride makes us think the world revolves around us. And it often leads us into humiliation.
Verse 13. 'And Haman told Zeresh his wife and all his friends every thing that had befallen him. Then said his wise men and Zeresh his wife unto him, If Mordecai be of the seed of the Jews, before whom thou hast begun to fall, thou shalt not prevail against him, but shalt surely fall before him.' Even Haman’s wife and friends could see it. There was something different about the Jewish people. Thou shalt not prevail against him. They did not know the LORD, but they sensed that someone greater was protecting this people. Haman had begun to fall, and he would not get up again.
A psalm says, 'Behold, he that keepeth Israel shall neither slumber nor sleep.' And the book of Proverbs says, 'Pride goeth before destruction, and an haughty spirit before a fall.'
The king could not sleep, but God never sleeps. He is watching over you at every moment. Today, look for the small ways God may be at work in your life, a conversation, a coincidence, a door that opens. And thank him for them.
Here is today’s question. The king’s sleepless hours became the turning point of the whole story. What small thing has God used in your life to make a big difference?
Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that you never slumber nor sleep, and that you watch over me always. Thank you that you can use even the smallest things for your purposes. Guard me from pride. Help me to humble myself, and to trust you to lift me up in your own time. Amen.
