An eighty-year-old shepherd leads his flock to the far side of the desert, near a mountain. He has done this for forty years. Nothing ever changes. Then he sees something strange: a bush on fire, and yet the bush is not burning up. He turns aside to look. And a voice calls his name from the middle of the flames.
In chapter two, baby Moses was hidden in a basket among the reeds, and Pharaoh’s daughter drew him out of the water and raised him as her son. When he grew up, he killed an Egyptian, and fled to Midian. There he married Zipporah and kept sheep. And God heard the groaning of his people, and remembered his covenant.
Now, in chapter three, God speaks. This is one of the greatest moments in the whole Bible. God calls Moses, and reveals his name. Listen for how God describes himself, and how Moses tries to say no.
Moses is keeping the flock of Jethro, his father-in-law, the priest of Midian. He leads the flock to the far side of the desert, and comes to the mountain of God, Horeb, also called Sinai. And the angel of the LORD appears to him in a flame of fire out of the middle of a bush. Moses looks, and behold, the bush burns with fire, and the bush is not consumed.
Moses says, I will now turn aside, and see this great sight, why the bush is not burnt. When the LORD sees that he turns aside to see, God calls to him out of the middle of the bush, and says, Moses, Moses. And he says, here am I.
Then God gives a warning.
God says, 'I am the God of thy father, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob.' And Moses hides his face, for he is afraid to look upon God.
Then the LORD tells Moses why he has come.
I am come down, God says, to deliver them out of the hand of the Egyptians, and to bring them up out of that land unto a good land and a large, unto a land flowing with milk and honey. God has heard their cry. He has seen how the Egyptians oppress them. Come now therefore, God says, and I will send thee unto Pharaoh, that thou mayest bring forth my people the children of Israel out of Egypt.
Forty years before, Moses had tried to rescue his people in his own strength. Now that God is ready to send him, Moses does not want to go. He says to God, 'Who am I, that I should go unto Pharaoh, and that I should bring forth the children of Israel out of Egypt?'
God does not answer by telling Moses how great he is. He says, 'Certainly I will be with thee'. And God gives him a sign. When you have brought the people out of Egypt, you shall serve God upon this mountain.
Moses has another question. When I come to the children of Israel, and say, the God of your fathers has sent me to you, and they ask me, what is his name? what shall I say to them?
And God tells Moses his name.
God says also, tell the children of Israel, the LORD God of your fathers, the God of Abraham, of Isaac and of Jacob, has sent me. 'this is my name for ever, and this is my memorial unto all generations.' In English Bibles, when you see the word LORD in capital letters, it stands for this name of God, which is connected with I AM.
God tells Moses what to do. Gather the elders of Israel, and tell them that God has visited them. Then go with them to the king of Egypt, and ask him to let the people go three days’ journey into the wilderness, to sacrifice to the LORD.
God warns Moses that the king of Egypt will not let them go, no, not even by a mighty hand. So I will stretch out my hand, God says, and smite Egypt with all my wonders. And after that he will let you go. God also promises that his people will not go out empty. The Egyptians will give them silver and gold and clothing, and they will spoil the Egyptians. The people who were made poor by slavery will leave with the wages they were never paid.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 5. 'And he said, Draw not nigh hither: put off thy shoes from off thy feet, for the place whereon thou standest is holy ground.' The ground was holy, not because of the place, but because God was there. Moses took off his shoes as a sign of humility and respect. Before God sent Moses to do anything, he taught him who he was dealing with. God is holy, pure and set apart. When we come to God, we come with reverence, aware that he is great and we are small.
Verse 7. 'And the LORD said, I have surely seen the affliction of my people which are in Egypt, and have heard their cry by reason of their taskmasters; for I know their sorrows;' I have seen. I have heard. I know. God is not distant from suffering. He sees it, he hears it, and he knows it from the inside. And notice that God calls them my people. They were slaves, poor and powerless. But they belonged to God. If you belong to him, he sees your affliction too, and knows your sorrows.
Verse 14. 'And God said unto Moses, I AM THAT I AM: and he said, Thus shalt thou say unto the children of Israel, I AM hath sent me unto you.' God’s name means that he simply is. He was not made. He does not change. He depends on nothing and no one. He is the same for ever. Pharaoh’s power would pass away, but I AM would remain. Like the bush that burned without burning up, God never runs out. He is always enough.
Jesus once said to those who questioned him, 'Before Abraham was, I am.' And Jesus said, 'I am the bread of life', 'I am the light of the world', 'I am the good shepherd', and 'I am the resurrection, and the life'. In Jesus, the great I AM came down to deliver us.
Is there something God is asking you to do, and you feel like saying, who am I? Remember his answer to Moses: certainly I will be with thee. It is not about who you are, but about who he is. And take a moment today to come to God with reverence, remembering that he is holy.
Here is today’s question. Moses asked, who am I? God answered, certainly I will be with thee. Where do you need to stop looking at your own weakness, and look at who God is?
Let us pray. Lord God, you are the great I AM, holy and unchanging. Thank you that you see the affliction of your people, hear their cry, and know their sorrows. When I feel too small for what you ask, remind me that you are with me. Help me to come to you with reverence and trust. Amen.
