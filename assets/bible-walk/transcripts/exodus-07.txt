In Pharaoh’s throne room, Aaron throws down his staff, and it becomes a serpent. Pharaoh is not impressed. He calls his magicians, and they throw down their staffs, and they become serpents too. For a moment, it looks like a draw. And then Aaron’s serpent swallows every one of theirs.
In chapter six, God answered Moses’ discouraged prayer with a great promise. I am the LORD. I will bring you out. I will redeem you. I will take you to me for a people. But the people could not listen, because of their anguish. And Moses said again, how shall Pharaoh hearken unto me?
Now, in chapter seven, the contest between God and Pharaoh begins. Pharaoh asked, who is the LORD? Over the next few chapters, God will answer that question with ten mighty signs, called the plagues. Each one shows that the LORD is greater than Pharaoh, and greater than all the gods of Egypt.
The LORD says to Moses, see, I have made thee a god to Pharaoh, and Aaron thy brother shall be thy prophet. Moses will speak God’s words, and Aaron will speak them to Pharaoh. And I will harden Pharaoh’s heart, God says, and multiply my signs and my wonders in the land of Egypt. But Pharaoh shall not listen to you, so that I may lay my hand upon Egypt, and bring forth my people by great judgments.
Then God explains the purpose of it all.
Moses and Aaron do as the LORD commanded them. Moses is eighty years old, and Aaron eighty-three, when they speak to Pharaoh. God’s greatest work in Moses’ life began when he was eighty.
God tells them that Pharaoh will ask for a miracle. When he does, Aaron is to throw down his rod before Pharaoh, and it will become a serpent. So they go in to Pharaoh, and Aaron casts down his rod, and it becomes a serpent. Then Pharaoh calls his wise men. The magicians of Egypt also do the same with their secret arts.
But then something happens.
And yet Pharaoh’s heart is hardened, and he does not listen, as the LORD had said.
Then the LORD tells Moses to meet Pharaoh at the river, as Pharaoh goes out to the water. Take in your hand the rod that was turned into a serpent, God says. And say to Pharaoh, the LORD God of the Hebrews has sent me to you, saying, let my people go, that they may serve me in the wilderness. And behold, until now you would not hear. Thus says the LORD, 'In this thou shalt know that I am the LORD'. I will strike the waters in the river with the rod in my hand, and they shall be turned to blood. The fish shall die, and the river shall stink, and the Egyptians shall not be able to drink its water.
The Nile was the life of Egypt. Without it, there would be no crops, no food, no Egypt. The Egyptians even honoured the river as a god. Now the LORD will show that the river belongs to him.
Aaron stretches out his hand with the rod over the waters of Egypt, over their streams, their rivers, their ponds and their pools. In the sight of Pharaoh and his servants, Aaron lifts up the rod and strikes the waters of the river. And all the waters in the river are turned to blood.
The fish in the river die. The river stinks. The Egyptians cannot drink its water. And there is blood throughout all the land of Egypt.
Pharaoh’s magicians copy even this.
Pharaoh turns and goes into his house. He does not even take this to heart. All the Egyptians dig around the river for water to drink, because they cannot drink the water of the river. Seven days pass, after the LORD has struck the river.
Notice that the first plague turns the river to blood. At the start of Exodus, Pharaoh commanded that the Hebrew baby boys be thrown into that river. Now the river that Pharaoh used for death is turned to blood. God sees, and God judges.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 5. 'And the Egyptians shall know that I am the LORD, when I stretch forth mine hand upon Egypt, and bring out the children of Israel from among them.' This is the purpose of the plagues. Not just to punish Egypt, or even only to free Israel, but so that everyone would know who God is. Pharaoh asked, who is the LORD? Now God says, the Egyptians shall know. God wants all people, even his enemies, to know him. His acts of power are also invitations.
Verse 12. 'For they cast down every man his rod, and they became serpents: but Aaron’s rod swallowed up their rods.' The magicians could imitate God’s sign, at least for a moment. But Aaron’s rod swallowed theirs. The serpent was a symbol of power in Egypt, found on Pharaoh’s own crown. God was showing that his power swallows up every other power. There are many things that look powerful, and even imitate God’s works. But in the end, God’s power is greater than all of them.
Verse 22. 'And the magicians of Egypt did so with their enchantments: and Pharaoh’s heart was hardened, neither did he hearken unto them; as the LORD had said.' The magicians could make more blood, but they could not turn the blood back into water. They could copy the trouble, but they could not undo it. And Pharaoh used their tricks as an excuse not to listen. A hard heart will always find a reason not to believe. The problem was not a lack of evidence. It was Pharaoh’s heart.
In the book of Revelation, the waters are turned to blood again, and an angel says, 'Thou art righteous, O Lord… because thou hast judged thus.' And Paul wrote that 'at the name of Jesus every knee should bow', and every tongue confess that Jesus Christ is Lord.
Is there any area where your heart has become hard toward God, where you keep finding reasons not to listen? Ask him to soften it. And remember that there is no power in your life, or in the world, that God’s power cannot swallow up.
Here is today’s question. Pharaoh’s heart was hardened, and he would not listen. What helps keep your heart soft and open to God?
Let us pray. Lord God, you are the LORD, and your power is greater than every other power. Keep my heart soft toward you, ready to listen and obey. Thank you that you want all people to know you. Help me to make you known. Amen.
