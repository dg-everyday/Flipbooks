Frogs. Frogs in the houses. Frogs in the bedrooms, jumping on the beds. Frogs in the ovens, frogs in the kneading bowls where the bread is made. All over Egypt, everywhere you step, there are frogs. Pharaoh calls for Moses, and begs him to pray that God will take them away. And Moses asks a surprising question: when?
In chapter seven, Aaron’s rod swallowed the rods of Pharaoh’s magicians. Then Aaron struck the river Nile, and it turned to blood. The magicians copied the sign, and Pharaoh hardened his heart and would not listen. God had said, the Egyptians shall know that I am the LORD.
Now, in chapter eight, come the second, third and fourth plagues: frogs, lice and flies. Watch how the magicians begin to fail, how Pharaoh begins to bargain, and how God starts to set his own people apart.
The LORD tells Moses to go to Pharaoh once more. Thus saith the LORD, let my people go, that they may serve me. And if you refuse to let them go, I will smite all your borders with frogs.
Pharaoh refuses. So Aaron stretches out his hand over the waters of Egypt, and the frogs come up and cover the land. The magicians do the same with their enchantments, and bring up more frogs. But they cannot get rid of a single one.
Then Pharaoh calls for Moses and Aaron. Intreat the LORD, he says, that he may take away the frogs from me and from my people, and I will let the people go, that they may sacrifice to the LORD.
Moses says, you may have the honour of choosing. When shall I pray for you, and for your servants, and for your people, to destroy the frogs from you and your houses, so that they remain only in the river? Pharaoh answers, the next day. So Moses says, be it according to thy word.
Moses and Aaron go out from Pharaoh, and Moses cries to the LORD about the frogs. And the LORD does according to the word of Moses. The frogs die out of the houses, out of the villages, and out of the fields. The people gather them together in heaps, and the land stinks.
But when Pharaoh sees that there is respite, relief, he hardens his heart, and does not listen to them, as the LORD had said.
So the LORD tells Moses, say to Aaron, stretch out thy rod, and smite the dust of the land, that it may become lice throughout all the land of Egypt. Aaron strikes the dust of the earth, and it becomes lice on man and on beast. All the dust of the land becomes lice throughout all the land of Egypt.
The magicians try to do the same with their enchantments, to bring forth lice. But they cannot.
Then comes the fourth plague. The LORD tells Moses to rise up early and stand before Pharaoh as he comes out to the water. Thus saith the LORD, let my people go, that they may serve me. Else, if you will not let my people go, I will send swarms of flies upon you, and upon your servants, and upon your people, and into your houses.
But this time there will be a difference.
And the LORD does so. There come grievous swarms of flies into the house of Pharaoh, and into his servants’ houses, and into all the land of Egypt. The land is corrupted by reason of the swarm of flies. But in the land of Goshen, where Israel lives, there are no flies at all.
Now Pharaoh begins to bargain. He calls for Moses and Aaron and says, go ye, sacrifice to your God in the land. Stay here in Egypt, and do it here. But Moses says, it is not right to do so. Our sacrifices would offend the Egyptians, and they might stone us. We will go three days’ journey into the wilderness, and sacrifice to the LORD our God, as he shall command us.
Pharaoh says, I will let you go, that you may sacrifice to the LORD your God in the wilderness. Only you shall not go very far away. Intreat for me. Moses says, I will go out and intreat the LORD, that the swarms of flies may depart. 'but let not Pharaoh deal deceitfully any more in not letting the people go'.
Moses prays to the LORD, and the LORD does according to the word of Moses. He removes the swarms of flies from Pharaoh, from his servants and from his people. There remains not one.
And Pharaoh hardens his heart at this time also, neither will he let the people go.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 10. 'Be it according to thy word: that thou mayest know that there is none like unto the LORD our God.' Moses let Pharaoh choose the time, so that when the frogs left exactly then, no one could say it was chance. The point of every plague was the same: that thou mayest know that there is none like unto the LORD our God. The Egyptians had many gods. But none of them could stop the frogs. Only the LORD could. There is no one like him.
Verse 19. 'Then the magicians said unto Pharaoh, This is the finger of God: and Pharaoh’s heart was hardened, and he hearkened not unto them; as the LORD had said.' The magicians reached the end of their power. They could not make lice from dust. And they admitted the truth. This is the finger of God. It did not even take God’s whole hand, only his finger. Even Pharaoh’s own advisers could see it. But Pharaoh still would not listen. Knowing the truth is not enough. We must also bow to it.
Verse 23. 'And I will put a division between my people and thy people'. For the first time, God made a clear difference between Egypt and Israel. The flies swarmed in Egypt, but not in Goshen. God was showing that he knows his own, and protects them. This does not mean God’s people never suffer. But it means that God sees the difference between those who belong to him and those who do not, and he cares for his own.
Jesus once said, 'But if I with the finger of God cast out devils, no doubt the kingdom of God is come upon you.' The same finger of God that defeated the magicians of Egypt was at work in Jesus. And Paul wrote, 'The Lord knoweth them that are his.'
Is there something in your life you have been bargaining with God about, offering half-obedience instead of the whole? Let it go, and give him everything. And thank God that he knows you as his own, and cares for you.
Here is today’s question. Pharaoh kept bargaining, offering to obey only part way. Is there an area where you are offering God half-obedience instead of the whole?
Let us pray. Lord God, there is none like you. Thank you that you know those who are yours, and care for them. Forgive me when I bargain with you, and keep back part of my obedience. Help me to see your hand at work, and to bow to the truth, with my whole heart. Amen.
