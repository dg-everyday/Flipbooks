Across Egypt, a darkness falls so thick that it can be felt. No one can see their own hand. No one can see the person next to them. For three days, no one moves from where they are. But a few miles away, in the land of Goshen, the lamps are shining in every house.
In chapter nine, a disease struck the cattle of Egypt, then painful boils, then a terrible hailstorm. God told Pharaoh, I have raised thee up, for to shew in thee my power. Some of Pharaoh’s servants feared the word of the LORD and were saved. Pharaoh said, I have sinned, but when the hail stopped, he hardened his heart again.
Now, in chapter ten, come the eighth and ninth plagues: locusts and darkness. Pharaoh’s own servants begin to beg him to give in. And Pharaoh tries one bargain after another. But God will not accept half an exodus.
The LORD says to Moses, go in unto Pharaoh. For I have hardened his heart, and the heart of his servants, that I might shew these my signs before him. And God gives another reason. That thou mayest tell in the ears of thy son, and of thy son’s son, what things I have wrought in Egypt, and my signs which I have done among them, 'that ye may know how that I am the LORD.' These stories are meant to be handed down, from parents to children.
So Moses and Aaron come in to Pharaoh with a question from God.
If you refuse, God says, I will bring the locusts into your land. They will cover the ground, eat what is left after the hail, and fill your houses. And Moses turns, and goes out from Pharaoh.
This time Pharaoh’s servants speak up. How long shall this man be a snare to us? Let the men go, that they may serve the LORD their God. 'knowest thou not yet that Egypt is destroyed?'
So Moses and Aaron are brought back to Pharaoh. He says, go, serve the LORD your God. But who are they that shall go? Moses says, we will go with our young and with our old, with our sons and with our daughters, with our flocks and with our herds. For we must hold a feast unto the LORD.
Pharaoh says, let the LORD be so with you, as I will let you go, and your little ones. Look, you have evil in mind. Not so. Go now, ye that are men, and serve the LORD. And they are driven out from Pharaoh’s presence. Pharaoh will let the men go, but he wants to keep the children, to be sure they come back.
So Moses stretches out his rod over the land of Egypt, and the LORD brings an east wind upon the land all that day. When it is light, the east wind brings the locusts. They cover the face of the whole earth, so that the land is darkened. They eat every plant of the land, and all the fruit of the trees that the hail had left. There remains not any green thing, in the trees or in the plants of the field, through all the land of Egypt.
Then Pharaoh calls for Moses and Aaron in haste.
Now therefore forgive, I pray thee, my sin only this once, Pharaoh says, and intreat the LORD your God, that he may take away from me this death only. So Moses prays, and the LORD turns a mighty strong west wind, which takes away the locusts, and casts them into the Red Sea. Not one locust remains in all the borders of Egypt. But the LORD hardens Pharaoh’s heart, and he does not let the children of Israel go.
Then, without warning this time, the ninth plague comes. The LORD tells Moses, stretch out thine hand toward heaven, that there may be darkness over the land of Egypt, 'even darkness which may be felt.' Moses stretches out his hand, and there is a thick darkness in all the land of Egypt for three days.
The Egyptians worshipped the sun as one of their greatest gods. Now the sun itself is hidden.
Pharaoh calls Moses again, and makes his final offer. Go, serve the LORD. Let your little ones go with you too. Only let your flocks and your herds stay behind. But Moses says, our cattle also shall go with us. There shall not an hoof be left behind. We must take of them to serve the LORD our God, and we do not know what we must offer until we come there.
But the LORD hardens Pharaoh’s heart, and he will not let them go. Pharaoh says to Moses, get thee from me. Take heed to thyself. See my face no more, for in that day you see my face, you shall die. And Moses says, thou hast spoken well. I will see thy face again no more.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 3. 'And Moses and Aaron came in unto Pharaoh, and said unto him, Thus saith the LORD God of the Hebrews, How long wilt thou refuse to humble thyself before me? let my people go, that they may serve me.' How long? God had been patient with Pharaoh through seven plagues. Each time, Pharaoh had a chance to humble himself. The real issue was not the Israelites, or the economy of Egypt. It was pride. Pharaoh would not bow. God asks each of us the same question: how long will you refuse to humble yourself before me?
Verse 16. 'Then Pharaoh called for Moses and Aaron in haste; and he said, I have sinned against the LORD your God, and against you.' These sound like the words of repentance. But notice that Pharaoh only wanted the locusts gone. As soon as they were, he went back to his old ways. Real repentance is not just being sorry about the consequences of sin. It is turning away from the sin itself. Pharaoh’s sorrow was about the locusts, not about his heart.
Verse 23. 'They saw not one another, neither rose any from his place for three days: but all the children of Israel had light in their dwellings.' In the middle of the darkness, God’s people had light. The darkness was real, and close by. But it did not reach their homes. It is a beautiful picture of what God does for his people. The world around us may be dark, but those who belong to God can walk in his light.
Jesus said, 'I am the light of the world: he that followeth me shall not walk in darkness, but shall have the light of life.' And Paul wrote that God 'hath delivered us from the power of darkness, and hath translated us into the kingdom of his dear Son'.
Is there pride in your heart that keeps you from bowing fully to God? Humble yourself before him today. And if the world around you seems dark, remember that Jesus is the light of the world. Ask him to shine in your home and your heart.
Here is today’s question. Pharaoh kept trying to obey only part way. God said, there shall not an hoof be left behind. Is there anything you are holding back from God?
Let us pray. Lord God, forgive me for the pride that refuses to bow to you. Help me to turn from sin, not just to be sorry for its consequences. Thank you for Jesus, the light of the world. Let your light shine in my home and in my heart, even when the world around is dark. Amen.
