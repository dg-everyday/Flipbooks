Moses stands before Pharaoh for the last time. His face is burning with anger. He has just delivered the most terrible message he has ever had to speak. Pharaoh has told him, see my face no more. Moses answers, thou hast spoken well. And he turns, and walks out of the palace, and never comes back.
In chapter ten, locusts devoured everything that the hail had left in Egypt. Then a darkness fell that could be felt, for three days, but all the children of Israel had light in their dwellings. Pharaoh kept bargaining, and finally told Moses, see my face no more, for in that day you see my face, you shall die.
Now, in chapter eleven, the shortest chapter of our walk so far, God announces the last plague. It is a heavy and solemn chapter. Pharaoh has refused nine times. He has been warned again and again. And now judgment is about to fall.
Before Moses leaves Pharaoh’s presence, the LORD has already spoken to him.
God tells Moses to speak to the people. Let every man ask of his neighbour, and every woman of her neighbour, jewels of silver and jewels of gold. God had promised Moses at the burning bush that his people would not leave empty handed. After hundreds of years of unpaid labour, they would leave with their wages.
And something has changed in the hearts of the Egyptians.
Then Moses speaks to Pharaoh. He gives the message of the last plague.
Thus saith the LORD, 'About midnight will I go out into the midst of Egypt'. And all the firstborn in the land of Egypt shall die, from the firstborn of Pharaoh who sits upon his throne, to the firstborn of the maidservant at the mill, and all the firstborn of the animals. 'And there shall be a great cry throughout all the land of Egypt, such as there was none like it, nor shall be like it any more.'
This is a hard part of the Bible to hear. But remember what we have seen. At the very beginning of Exodus, Pharaoh ordered that the baby boys of Israel be thrown into the river. God called Israel my son, even my firstborn, and warned Pharaoh in chapter four that if he would not let God’s firstborn go, his own firstborn would die. Pharaoh had been warned before any plague began. He had nine chances to turn. God is patient, but his patience is not endless. Judgment, when it comes, is just.
And then God makes a promise to Israel.
Moses tells Pharaoh what will happen next. All these thy servants shall come down unto me, and bow down themselves unto me, saying, get thee out, and all the people that follow thee. And after that I will go out. And Moses goes out from Pharaoh in a great anger.
Moses’ anger is not the anger of a man who has been insulted. It is anger at Pharaoh’s stubbornness, which is about to bring sorrow on the whole land. Moses had prayed for Pharaoh again and again. Now there is nothing more to say.
The LORD had said to Moses, Pharaoh shall not hearken unto you, that my wonders may be multiplied in the land of Egypt. And Moses and Aaron did all these wonders before Pharaoh. 'and the LORD hardened Pharaoh’s heart, so that he would not let the children of Israel go out of his land.'
Throughout these chapters, we read that Pharaoh hardened his heart, and also that the LORD hardened Pharaoh’s heart. Both are true. Pharaoh chose, again and again, to refuse God. And in the end, God gave him over to the choice he had made. It is a sober warning. The more we say no to God, the harder our hearts become.
The chapter ends there, with everything ready. Israel is waiting. Pharaoh is refusing. And the next chapter will tell of the hour that changed everything.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 1. 'And the LORD said unto Moses, Yet will I bring one plague more upon Pharaoh, and upon Egypt; afterwards he will let you go hence: when he shall let you go, he shall surely thrust you out hence altogether.' One more. After all the waiting, all the broken promises, all the hardening, God said, one more, and it will be over. God had a plan from the beginning, and he knew exactly when it would end. When we are in a long struggle, it can feel as though it will never end. But God knows the end from the beginning.
Verse 3. 'And the LORD gave the people favour in the sight of the Egyptians. Moreover the man Moses was very great in the land of Egypt, in the sight of Pharaoh’s servants, and in the sight of the people.' Pharaoh hated Moses. But the ordinary people of Egypt had come to respect him, and to show kindness to the Israelites. God can change the hearts of people toward us, even in hostile places. The slaves who had been despised were now given gifts by their neighbours. God turned contempt into favour.
Verse 7. 'But against any of the children of Israel shall not a dog move his tongue, against man or beast: that ye may know how that the LORD doth put a difference between the Egyptians and Israel.' When the great cry went up in Egypt, there would be such peace among Israel that not even a dog would bark at them. God puts a difference between those who belong to him and those who do not. In the next chapter, we will see what made the difference. It was not that Israel was better than Egypt. It was the blood of a lamb.
Peter wrote that the Lord 'is longsuffering to us-ward', wanting that 'all should come to repentance.' And the letter to the Hebrews warns, 'To day if ye will hear his voice, harden not your hearts'.
Is there a way in which you keep saying no to God? Do not let your heart grow hard. Today, if you hear his voice, answer him. And thank God for his patience with you, and for the difference he makes in the lives of those who belong to him.
Here is today’s question. Pharaoh refused God again and again, until his heart was hard. What helps you to say yes to God quickly, instead of putting him off?
Let us pray. Lord God, thank you for your patience, and that you want everyone to come back to you. Keep my heart soft toward you. When I hear your voice, help me to answer straight away. Thank you that you put a difference between those who are yours and the world, through the blood of the Lamb. Amen.
