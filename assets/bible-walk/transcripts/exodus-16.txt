When the dew lifts, the desert floor is covered with something no one has ever seen before: small, round, white flakes, like frost on the ground. People bend down and pick it up. They look at each other and ask, what is it? In Hebrew, the words for what is it sound like manna. And that becomes its name.
In chapter fifteen, Israel sang to the LORD on the shore of the sea: the LORD is my strength and song. Three days later, at Marah, the water was bitter, and the people murmured. God made the waters sweet, and said, I am the LORD that healeth thee. Then he brought them to Elim, with twelve wells and seventy palm trees.
Now, in chapter sixteen, about a month after leaving Egypt, the food runs out. The people grumble again. And God answers them, not with anger, but with bread from heaven. It is a chapter about daily trust, and about God’s faithful provision.
The whole congregation of Israel comes into the wilderness of Sin, between Elim and Sinai, on the fifteenth day of the second month after they left Egypt. And the whole congregation murmurs against Moses and Aaron. They say, would to God we had died by the hand of the LORD in the land of Egypt, when we sat by the flesh pots, and when we did eat bread to the full. For you have brought us out into this wilderness, to kill this whole assembly with hunger.
Already they remember Egypt as a place of good food, and forget that it was a place of slavery.
Then the LORD makes a promise.
Moses and Aaron tell the people that they will see the glory of the LORD, for he has heard their murmurings. And Moses adds, what are we? 'your murmurings are not against us, but against the LORD.' When we grumble about our circumstances, we are really grumbling against God.
As Aaron speaks to the whole congregation, they look toward the wilderness, and behold, the glory of the LORD appears in the cloud. The LORD says to Moses, I have heard the murmurings of the children of Israel. Speak to them, and say, you shall eat flesh, and you shall be filled with bread. 'and ye shall know that I am the LORD your God.'
As the day ends, quails come up and cover the camp. And when the next day begins, the dew lies round about the camp. When the dew is gone up, behold, on the face of the wilderness lies a small round thing, as small as the hoar frost on the ground.
The people do not know what it is.
God’s instructions are clear. Gather of it every man according to his eating, an omer for every person, about two litres. Some gather more, some less. But when they measure it, something remarkable happens.
Moses says, let no man leave of it till the next day. But some of them do not listen. They keep part of it, and it breeds worms and stinks. And Moses is angry with them. So they gather it every day, every man according to his eating. And when the sun grows hot, it melts.
On the sixth day, they gather twice as much bread, two omers for each person. Moses explains that the LORD has given them the seventh day as a rest, holy to the LORD. Bake what you will bake today, he says, and keep the rest for the seventh day. They keep it, and it does not stink, and there are no worms in it. On the seventh day, there will be none in the field.
Some of the people go out on the seventh day to gather, and find nothing. The LORD says to Moses, how long refuse ye to keep my commandments and my laws? See, the LORD has given you the rest, and so on the sixth day he gives you bread for two days. So the people rest on the seventh day.
The house of Israel calls it manna. It is like coriander seed, white, and its taste is like wafers made with honey.
Then Moses tells Aaron to fill a pot with an omer of manna, and lay it up before the LORD, to be kept for their generations. That way, people in years to come could see the bread with which God fed them in the wilderness. The children of Israel eat manna for forty years, until they come to the borders of the land of Canaan.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 4. 'Then said the LORD unto Moses, Behold, I will rain bread from heaven for you; and the people shall go out and gather a certain rate every day, that I may prove them, whether they will walk in my law, or no.' God could have given them a whole year’s food at once. Instead, he gave them enough for each day. Every day they had to trust him again. That was the test. Would they believe that God would provide the next day, as he had that day? God often provides for us one day at a time, so that we learn to depend on him every day.
Verse 15. 'And when the children of Israel saw it, they said one to another, It is manna: for they wist not what it was. And Moses said unto them, This is the bread which the LORD hath given you to eat.' They had never seen anything like it. Moses told them plainly: this is the bread which the LORD hath given you. God’s provision does not always look the way we expect. But it is always enough. And notice that the bread came down from heaven. It was a gift, not something they earned or grew.
Verse 18. 'And when they did mete it with an omer, he that gathered much had nothing over, and he that gathered little had no lack; they gathered every man according to his eating.' Some gathered more, some less, but everyone had exactly enough. God’s provision was fair, and it was sufficient. There was no point in grabbing more than you needed. God was teaching his people contentment, and fairness, and trust. In God’s economy, no one goes without, and no one has too much.
Jesus taught us to pray, 'Give us this day our daily bread.' And he said, 'I am the bread of life: he that cometh to me shall never hunger'. And Paul quoted this very verse, 'He that had gathered much had nothing over; and he that had gathered little had no lack', to teach believers to share with each other.
Instead of worrying about next week or next year, trust God for today. Thank him for the daily bread he has given you. And if you have more than enough, is there someone you could share with?
Here is today’s question. God gave manna one day at a time, so his people had to trust him every day. What would it look like for you to trust God one day at a time?
Let us pray. Lord God, thank you for giving me my daily bread. Forgive me for grumbling, and for worrying about what I cannot see. Teach me to trust you one day at a time, to be content with what you give, and to share with others. Thank you for Jesus, the bread of life. Amen.
