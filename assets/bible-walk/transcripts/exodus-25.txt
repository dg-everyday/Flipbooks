Imagine the King of the whole universe saying, I want to come and live in your neighbourhood. Not in a palace far away. Right here, in the middle of your town, among the ordinary people. Would you believe it? On Mount Sinai, that is exactly what God tells Moses. I want to live among my people. Build me a tent, and I will dwell in the middle of the camp.
In chapter twenty four, the people promised, all the words which the LORD hath said will we do. Moses sprinkled the blood on the altar and on the people, and said, behold the blood of the covenant. The elders saw the God of Israel, and ate and drank in his presence. And Moses went up into the cloud on the mountain, where the glory of the LORD was like devouring fire.
Now, in chapter twenty five, God begins to tell Moses about the tabernacle. Listen for why God wants it built. And listen for the special place where God promises to meet with Moses.
For the next seven chapters, God gives Moses detailed instructions for building the tabernacle. A tabernacle is a tent. But this would be no ordinary tent. It would be God’s house, his dwelling place, right in the middle of the camp of Israel.
It begins with an offering.
And what were they to bring? 'gold, and silver, and brass, And blue, and purple, and scarlet, and fine linen, and goats’ hair, And rams’ skins dyed red, and badgers’ skins, and shittim wood'. Brass here means bronze. The coloured threads were very costly. Shittim wood is acacia wood, a hard, tough wood that grows in the desert.
Where would a group of former slaves, wandering in the desert, get gold and silver and precious stones? Do you remember? When they left Egypt, the Egyptians gave them silver and gold and clothing. God had provided, long before, for the house he was going to ask them to build.
Then God tells Moses why he wants the tabernacle.
'According to all that I shew thee, after the pattern of the tabernacle, and the pattern of all the instruments thereof, even so shall ye make it.' God showed Moses a pattern, a design. Everything had to be made exactly as God showed him. The letter to the Hebrews says that the tabernacle was a copy and shadow of heavenly things. It was a picture of something greater.
The first thing God describes is the most important. The ark. An ark is a box, a chest. It was to be made of acacia wood, two and a half cubits long, and a cubit and a half wide and high. A cubit was the length from a man’s elbow to the tip of his fingers. So the ark was about the size of a large chest. And it was covered with pure gold, inside and out.
It had four golden rings on its sides, and two long poles, also covered with gold, to carry it. 'The staves shall be in the rings of the ark: they shall not be taken from it.' The poles were never to be removed. The ark was always ready to travel, wherever God led his people.
And inside the ark? 'And thou shalt put into the ark the testimony which I shall give thee.' The testimony was the two tables of stone, with the Ten Commandments written on them by God himself.
On top of the ark was a lid of pure gold, called the mercy seat. And on each end of the mercy seat was a cherub made of beaten gold. Cherubim are mighty angels who guard God’s holiness. Their wings stretched up and over the mercy seat, and they faced each other, looking down towards it.
And then God says something wonderful about the mercy seat.
Then God describes a table, also of acacia wood covered with gold, with golden dishes and bowls. 'And thou shalt set upon the table shewbread before me alway.' Shewbread means bread of the presence. Twelve loaves of bread, one for each tribe, were always on the table before God. It was a sign that God provided for his people, and that his people lived in his presence.
And then a beautiful lampstand, called the candlestick. It was made from one solid piece of pure gold, hammered into shape. It had a central stem, and six branches, three on each side. And each branch was decorated with cups shaped like almond blossoms, with buds and flowers. In Israel, the almond tree is the first tree to blossom, a sign of new life. So the lampstand looked like a golden tree in flower, with seven lamps burning on top.
Why all this detail? Because God is holy, and he wanted his people to learn that we cannot come to him any way we like. We must come his way. And every piece of the tabernacle was a picture, pointing forward to something God would one day do.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 2. 'Speak unto the children of Israel, that they bring me an offering: of every man that giveth it willingly with his heart ye shall take my offering.' God did not force anyone to give. He did not set a price. He wanted gifts that came willingly, from the heart. God loves a cheerful giver. When we give to God, he looks at our hearts.
Verse 8. 'And let them make me a sanctuary; that I may dwell among them.' A sanctuary is a holy place. This is the heart of the whole tabernacle. God did not just want to rescue his people from Egypt. He wanted to live with them. The holy God, who made heaven and earth, wanted to dwell in a tent in the middle of their camp. And later, John wrote that the Word, Jesus, was made flesh, and dwelt among us. God came to live with us in person.
Verse 22. 'And there I will meet with thee, and I will commune with thee from above the mercy seat, from between the two cherubims which are upon the ark of the testimony'. Commune means talk with, have fellowship with. Above the mercy seat, between the cherubim, was where God would speak to Moses. Notice that the place where God met his people was called the mercy seat. God meets with sinful people on the basis of his mercy. And the New Testament tells us that Jesus is our mercy seat, the place where God meets us in mercy.
Paul wrote, 'Every man according as he purposeth in his heart, so let him give; not grudgingly, or of necessity: for God loveth a cheerful giver.' And John wrote, 'And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us'.
Give something to God today, willingly, from your heart. It might be your time, helping someone. It might be a gift. And thank God that he wants to dwell with you, so much that he sent Jesus to live among us.
Here is today’s question. God said, let them make me a sanctuary; that I may dwell among them. Why do you think God wants to live so close to his people?
Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that you want to dwell among your people. Thank you that you meet with us at the mercy seat, because of your mercy. Help me to give to you willingly, from my heart. And thank you for Jesus, who came and lived among us, so that we could know you. Amen.
