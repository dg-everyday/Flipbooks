Imagine being offered a wonderful land, full of good things, everything you have ever dreamed of. But there is one catch. Your best friend, the person you love most, will not be coming with you. Would you still go? In Exodus chapter thirty three, God offers Israel the promised land, but without his presence. And Moses says, if you do not come with us, we do not want to go.
In chapter thirty two, while Moses was on the mountain, the people made a golden calf, and said, these be thy gods, O Israel. Moses came down and broke the tables of stone. Aaron made excuses. Moses stood in the gate of the camp and called, who is on the LORD’S side? And he prayed for the people, even offering to be blotted out of God’s book in their place.
Now, in chapter thirty three, the people face the result of their sin. Listen for how God speaks with Moses. And listen for Moses’ boldest request of all.
God tells Moses, go on to the land I promised to Abraham, Isaac and Jacob, a land flowing with milk and honey. I will send an angel before you, and drive out your enemies. 'for I will not go up in the midst of thee; for thou art a stiffnecked people: lest I consume thee in the way.'
God would keep his promise about the land. But he would not go with them himself. Because they were so stubborn and sinful, his holy presence among them would destroy them.
When the people heard this, they mourned. They took off their jewellery and ornaments, as a sign of sorrow. Without God, the land would be empty.
Then the chapter tells us about a special tent. The great tabernacle had not been built yet. But Moses had a tent, and he pitched it outside the camp, a long way off, and called it the tabernacle of the congregation, the tent of meeting. Anyone who wanted to seek the LORD went out to that tent.
And whenever Moses went out to it, all the people stood up, each at the door of his own tent, and watched him go. As Moses went inside, the pillar of cloud came down and stood at the door. And all the people saw it, and worshipped, each at his tent door.
And then.
When Moses went back to the camp, his young helper Joshua stayed behind in the tent. He did not want to leave the place where God met with Moses.
Then Moses spoke to God, inside the tent. You have told me to lead this people. But you have not told me who will go with me. You have said, I know you by name, and you have found grace in my sight. Now, if that is true, 'shew me now thy way, that I may know thee'. And remember, this nation is your people.
And God answered.
But Moses wanted to be sure. 'If thy presence go not with me, carry us not up hence.' Do not send us from here without you. 'For wherein shall it be known here that I and thy people have found grace in thy sight? is it not in that thou goest with us?' What makes us different from every other nation? Not our land. Not our laws. Your presence with us.
And God said yes. I will do what you ask, 'for thou hast found grace in my sight, and I know thee by name.'
Then Moses made his boldest request of all. 'I beseech thee, shew me thy glory.' I beseech means I beg you. Moses had seen God’s power in the plagues. He had heard God’s voice. He had talked with God as a friend. But he wanted more. He wanted to see God himself.
God said, 'I will make all my goodness pass before thee, and I will proclaim the name of the LORD before thee'. God would show Moses his goodness, and tell him his name. But, God said, 'Thou canst not see my face: for there shall no man see me, and live.' God’s glory is so great and so holy that no sinful human being could look at it and survive.
So God made a plan. 'Behold, there is a place by me, and thou shalt stand upon a rock'.
'And I will take away mine hand, and thou shalt see my back parts: but my face shall not be seen.' Moses would see only the glory God left behind as he passed by, like seeing the glow in the sky after the sun has gone down.
John wrote in his Gospel, no man hath seen God at any time; the only begotten Son, which is in the bosom of the Father, he hath declared him. Moses could not see God’s face. But we can see God’s glory in the face of Jesus Christ. Jesus said, he that hath seen me hath seen the Father.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 11. 'And the LORD spake unto Moses face to face, as a man speaketh unto his friend. And he turned again into the camp: but his servant Joshua, the son of Nun, a young man, departed not out of the tabernacle.' Face to face, as a friend. Not like a king giving orders to a servant. Like two friends talking together. That is the kind of relationship God wants with his people. And Jesus said to his disciples, I have called you friends. Joshua, the young man, loved being near God’s presence. Do we?
Verse 14. 'And he said, My presence shall go with thee, and I will give thee rest.' God’s presence is the greatest gift he can give. More than land, more than riches, more than success. And with his presence comes rest. Not necessarily an easy life. But a deep peace inside, knowing that God is with us. Jesus promised his followers, lo, I am with you alway, even unto the end of the world.
Verse 22. 'And it shall come to pass, while my glory passeth by, that I will put thee in a clift of the rock, and will cover thee with my hand while I pass by'. A clift is a crack or opening in the rock. God hid Moses there, and covered him with his hand, to protect him from the full blaze of his glory. Many have seen this as a picture of how God protects us. In Jesus, our rock, we are safe, even before a holy God.
John wrote, 'No man hath seen God at any time; the only begotten Son, which is in the bosom of the Father, he hath declared him.' And Jesus said, 'Henceforth I call you not servants; for the servant knoweth not what his lord doeth: but I have called you friends'.
Spend some time with God today, like Moses in the tent of meeting. Talk to him as a friend. Tell him what is on your heart. And thank him for his promise, my presence shall go with thee.
Here is today’s question. Moses said, if thy presence go not with me, carry us not up hence. Why do you think God’s presence mattered more to Moses than the promised land?
Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that you want to speak with me as a friend. More than anything else, I want your presence with me. Do not send me anywhere without you. Thank you that you have shown your glory in the face of Jesus. Hide me in the rock, and cover me with your hand. And give me your rest. Amen.
