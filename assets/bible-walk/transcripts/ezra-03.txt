A huge crowd stands among the ruins of an old building. Workers have just laid the last stone of a new foundation. The trumpets blast. The cymbals crash. The singers begin to sing. And the people shout for joy, so loudly it can be heard far away. But mixed in with the shouting is another sound. Old men, standing at the edge of the crowd, are weeping aloud.
In chapter two, we heard the long list of those who came home from Babylon. Families named after their ancestors and their towns, priests, singers, gatekeepers and servants, every one counted. When they arrived, some of the leaders gave freely for the house of God, and everyone settled in their own towns.
Now, in chapter three, the rebuilding begins. Listen for what they build first. And listen for the two very different sounds at the end.
Some months after they arrive, the people gather together in Jerusalem, as one man, united.
And the first thing they build is not a wall, or a house, or even the temple. It is an altar. Jeshua the priest and Zerubbabel, with their families, build the altar of the God of Israel, so they can offer burnt offerings, just as it is written in the law of Moses.
And notice why they did it straight away.
They also keep the feast of tabernacles, as it is written in God’s law. It was a feast when the people lived in shelters made of branches, to remember how God had looked after them in the wilderness. How fitting, for a people who had just made a long journey through the desert, and were living in a broken city.
And they begin to offer all the regular sacrifices again. But the writer adds, 'But the foundation of the temple of the LORD was not yet laid.' The altar was there, but the house of God was still in ruins.
So they get ready. They pay the stonemasons and carpenters. They send food, drink and oil to the people of Tyre and Sidon, to bring cedar trees from Lebanon by sea to Joppa, just as Solomon had done long before. And Cyrus had given them permission for it all.
In the second year after they arrive, the work begins. Zerubbabel and Jeshua appoint the Levites to oversee the building work. And the builders lay the foundation of the temple of the LORD.
Then comes the celebration. The priests stand in their robes with trumpets. The Levites, the sons of Asaph, stand with cymbals, to praise the LORD, the way king David had arranged long before.
And they sing.
He is good, for his mercy endureth for ever. Those words were sung when David brought the ark to Jerusalem, and when Solomon dedicated the first temple. Now, after the exile, after the ruins, after everything, the people sing the same words. God is still good. His mercy still endures.
And all the people shout with a great shout, because the foundation of the house of the LORD is laid.
But not everyone is shouting.
The old men could remember Solomon’s temple, with its gold and its cedar and its great bronze pillars. They had seen it with their own eyes as children, before it was burned. Now they looked at the new foundation, and it seemed so small, so poor. And they wept.
And the young people, who had never seen the old temple, shouted for joy. To them, this was wonderful. God’s house was rising again.
'So that the people could not discern the noise of the shout of joy from the noise of the weeping of the people: for the people shouted with a loud shout, and the noise was heard afar off.' Joy and sorrow, mixed together so you could not tell them apart. And the sound carried far away.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 3. 'And they set the altar upon his bases; for fear was upon them because of the people of those countries: and they offered burnt offerings thereon unto the LORD, even burnt offerings morning and evening.' They were afraid of the people around them. But instead of building walls first, they built an altar. They turned to God first. When we are afraid, the best thing we can do is to worship. Bring your fear to God, and put him first, before anything else.
Verse 11. 'And they sang together by course in praising and giving thanks unto the LORD; because he is good, for his mercy endureth for ever toward Israel. And all the people shouted with a great shout, when they praised the LORD, because the foundation of the house of the LORD was laid.' By course means taking turns, one group answering another. Only a foundation was laid. The building was far from finished. But they celebrated the beginning. It is good to thank God for small beginnings, and not wait until everything is complete.
Verse 12. 'But many of the priests and Levites and chief of the fathers, who were ancient men, that had seen the first house, when the foundation of this house was laid before their eyes, wept with a loud voice; and many shouted aloud for joy'. Ancient means old. It is natural to look back and grieve what has been lost. The old men were not wrong to weep. But the prophet Haggai would later tell them that God’s glory in this new house would be even greater. God was not finished. The best was still to come.
The psalmist wrote, 'They that sow in tears shall reap in joy.' And Haggai heard God say, 'The glory of this latter house shall be greater than of the former'.
Is there something in your life that is just a small beginning? Thank God for it today, even if it is far from finished. And if you are grieving something you have lost, bring it to God. He can bring joy out of tears.
Here is today’s question. Some people wept for what was lost, and some shouted for joy at what was beginning. Which are you more likely to do, and why?
Let us pray. Lord God, you are good, and your mercy endures for ever. When I am afraid, help me to turn to you first. Teach me to thank you for small beginnings. And when I grieve for what is lost, remind me that you are not finished, and that the best is still to come. Amen.
