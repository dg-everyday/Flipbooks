A man hears some terrible news. He sits down on the ground in shock, and does not move. He tears his clothes. Hours go by, and he still sits there, too upset to speak. Other people quietly gather around him, people who also love God, and they wait with him. Then at last, he gets up on his knees, spreads out his hands towards heaven, and prays.
In chapter eight, Ezra gathered his travellers by the river Ahava. He was ashamed to ask the king for soldiers, because he had said, the hand of our God is upon all them for good that seek him. So they fasted and prayed, and God heard them, and brought them safely to Jerusalem with all their treasure.
Now, in chapter nine, Ezra discovers a serious problem. This chapter and the next are among the hardest in the book. Listen for what the problem was. And listen to Ezra’s prayer.
After Ezra has delivered the offerings, some of the leaders come to him with bad news. The people of Israel, even the priests and the Levites, have not kept themselves separate from the peoples of the land, and their abominations. And they name the nations, the very same nations that had led Israel astray long ago: the Canaanites, the Hittites, the Moabites, the Egyptians and others.
What had they done? 'For they have taken of their daughters for themselves, and for their sons'. Many of the men had married women from those nations, women who worshipped other gods. And the leaders and officials had been the first to do it.
We need to understand why this mattered so much. It was not about which nation someone came from. Remember Ruth, the Moabite, who was welcomed into Israel and became the great-grandmother of king David. Remember Rahab of Jericho. Remember, in chapter six, how those who turned from idols to seek the LORD were welcomed to the passover. God’s people were always open to anyone who truly turned to the LORD.
The problem was idolatry. These marriages were bringing the worship of other gods into the homes of God’s people. And that was exactly what had led to the exile. Do you remember king Solomon? His foreign wives turned his heart away to other gods. It had happened before, and it had destroyed the nation. Now, only a short time after coming home, it was happening again.
When Ezra hears it, he is devastated.
Then everyone who trembled at the words of the God of Israel gathers around him. And Ezra sits there, stunned, for hours.
Then he gets up from his grief, kneels down, spreads out his hands to the LORD, and prays.
Notice how Ezra prays. He did not commit this sin himself. But he does not say, they have sinned. He says, our iniquities, our trespass. He stands with his people, and confesses their sin as his own.
He goes on. Ever since the days of our fathers, we have been deeply guilty. That is why we and our kings and our priests were handed over to foreign kings, to the sword, to captivity, and to shame.
And then, in the middle of his confession, Ezra remembers God’s kindness.
For we were slaves, Ezra prays. Yet our God did not forsake us in our slavery. He has shown us kindness before the kings of Persia, to give us new life, to rebuild the house of our God, and to give us a wall of protection in Judah and Jerusalem.
And now, after all that, what can we say? We have forsaken your commands. Your prophets told us not to marry into the peoples who fill the land with their idolatry, so that we could be strong, and leave the land to our children for ever.
And then Ezra says something that shows how deeply he understood God’s mercy.
Should we break your commands again, Ezra asks, and join with the people who do these things? Would you not be angry, until there was no one left?
'O LORD God of Israel, thou art righteous: for we remain yet escaped, as it is this day: behold, we are before thee in our trespasses: for we cannot stand before thee because of this.'
Ezra does not make any excuses. He does not ask God to pretend it does not matter. He simply brings the sin before God, honestly, and throws himself on God’s mercy.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 6. 'And said, O my God, I am ashamed and blush to lift up my face to thee, my God: for our iniquities are increased over our head, and our trespass is grown up unto the heavens.' Ashamed and blush. Ezra felt the weight of sin, like a flood rising over his head. Sometimes we treat sin lightly. Ezra did not. But notice, even in his shame, he still came to God. He called him, my God, twice. Shame should drive us towards God, not away from him.
Verse 8. 'And now for a little space grace hath been shewed from the LORD our God, to leave us a remnant to escape, and to give us a nail in his holy place, that our God may lighten our eyes, and give us a little reviving in our bondage.' A nail in his holy place is like a tent peg, firmly fixed. God had given them a secure place again. A little reviving means a little new life. Ezra treasured these small mercies. Even in hard times, God gives us grace, and new life, and a place to stand.
Verse 13. 'And after all that is come upon us for our evil deeds, and for our great trespass, seeing that thou our God hast punished us less than our iniquities deserve, and hast given us such deliverance as this'. Less than our iniquities deserve. That is mercy. The exile had been terrible. But Ezra knew it was far less than they deserved. And for us, God has done even more. Through Jesus, he does not give us what our sins deserve at all. He gives us forgiveness.
The psalmist wrote, 'He hath not dealt with us after our sins; nor rewarded us according to our iniquities.' And John wrote, 'If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins'.
Is there a sin you have been treating lightly? Bring it to God honestly today, as Ezra did. And thank God for his mercy, that he has not treated you as your sins deserve.
Here is today’s question. Ezra confessed the people’s sin as if it were his own, saying our iniquities. Why do you think he did that, and what can we learn from it?
Let us pray. O my God, I come to you honestly with my sin. I will not make excuses. Thank you that you have not dealt with me as my sins deserve, but have shown me mercy, and given me a place to stand. Thank you for Jesus, who took my sin upon himself. Keep my heart from anything that would turn it away from you. Amen.
