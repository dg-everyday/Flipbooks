Rain is pouring down on a huge crowd of people, sitting in the open square in front of the house of God. They are soaked through, and shivering. But they are not shivering only because of the cold. They are trembling, because they know they have done wrong, and they have come to make it right. And it is not going to be easy.
In chapter nine, Ezra heard that many of the people, even priests and leaders, had married into the peoples of the land who worshipped other gods. The problem was not where those women came from, but the idols that came with them, just as Solomon’s wives had turned his heart. Ezra tore his clothes, and prayed, our iniquities are increased over our head. And he praised God, who had punished them less than their iniquities deserved.
Now we come to the last chapter of Ezra. It is a hard one. Listen for the hope in the middle of sorrow. And listen for what the people decide to do.
While Ezra is praying and confessing, weeping and throwing himself down before the house of God, a crowd begins to gather.
Then a man named Shechaniah speaks up. His own father was among those who had married wives who worshipped other gods.
Shechaniah suggests that they make a covenant with God to send away the foreign wives and their children, as Ezra and those who tremble at God’s command advise, according to God’s law. Arise, he says to Ezra, this is your task. We are with you. Be of good courage, and do it.
So Ezra gets up, and makes the leaders of the priests, the Levites and all Israel promise to do it. Then Ezra goes into a room by the house of God, and spends the time without food or water, mourning over the people’s unfaithfulness.
A message is sent out to everyone who had come back from exile, to gather in Jerusalem within three days. And they come. All the men of Judah and Benjamin sit in the open square before the house of God, trembling because of this matter, and because of the heavy rain.
Ezra stands up, and speaks to them.
And the whole assembly answers with a loud voice, 'As thou hast said, so must we do.' But they add, there are many of us, and it is the rainy season. We cannot stand out here. And this is not something that can be done in a day or two. Let our leaders deal with it, and let each case be looked at carefully, with the elders and judges of each town.
So that is what happens. Over the next few months, Ezra and the heads of families sit down and examine each case, one by one. The writer then lists the names of the men who had married wives who worshipped other gods. Even some of the priests, from the family of the high priest. They promise to send their wives away, and offer a ram as a guilt offering.
This is a very hard ending. Families were broken up. Wives and children were sent away. We should not pretend that this was easy, or that no one was hurt. Many people wept. Christians have understood this passage in different ways. In Ezra’s time, the survival of God’s people and their faithfulness to God were at stake, and the leaders believed this drastic step was needed to keep idolatry out.
But it is important to know that this is not God’s instruction for families today. In the New Testament, Paul told believers who were married to someone who did not share their faith, if they are willing to stay, do not send them away. Stay together, and let your life show them God’s love. In Jesus, the way to keep our hearts from idols is not by sending people away, but by the power of his love, changing us from the inside.
So the book of Ezra ends, not with a celebration, but with a people taking their faith seriously, and turning away from the sin that had once destroyed them.
The book began with God stirring a king’s heart to send his people home. It told of an altar and a great shout, of discouragement and delay, of prophets who got the people building again, and of a temple finished with joy. It told of Ezra, who prepared his heart to seek God’s Word, to do it and to teach it. And through it all, the good hand of God was upon his people.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 1. 'Now when Ezra had prayed, and when he had confessed, weeping and casting himself down before the house of God, there assembled unto him out of Israel a very great congregation of men and women and children: for the people wept very sore.' Ezra did not shout at the people, or force them. He wept, and prayed, and confessed. And when the people saw how much it grieved him, they began to weep too. A leader’s humble, honest sorrow over sin can move others to turn back to God.
Verse 2. 'And Shechaniah the son of Jehiel, one of the sons of Elam, answered and said unto Ezra, We have trespassed against our God, and have taken strange wives of the people of the land: yet now there is hope in Israel concerning this thing.' Yet now there is hope. Even after a serious failure, there was hope. That is always true with God. However badly we have failed, if we turn to him, there is hope. Sin is never the end of the story, when we bring it to God.
Verse 11. 'Now therefore make confession unto the LORD God of your fathers, and do his pleasure: and separate yourselves from the people of the land, and from the strange wives.' Make confession, and do his pleasure. Confession means admitting our sin to God. Doing his pleasure means doing what pleases him. True repentance is not just saying sorry. It is turning around, and changing direction. And it can be costly.
Paul wrote, 'if any brother hath a wife that believeth not, and she be pleased to dwell with him, let him not put her away.' And David wrote, 'The sacrifices of God are a broken spirit: a broken and a contrite heart, O God, thou wilt not despise.'
Is there something in your life that is pulling your heart away from God? Bring it to him honestly. Remember that there is always hope. And pray for families who are going through hard times, that God would comfort them.
Here is today’s question. Shechaniah said, yet now there is hope in Israel. When you have failed, what helps you to remember that there is still hope with God?
Let us pray. Lord God, thank you for walking with me through the book of Ezra. Thank you that your good hand is upon those who seek you. Thank you that even after failure, there is hope. Give me a heart that takes your Word seriously, and turns from anything that would lead me away from you. And thank you that in Jesus, your love changes us from the inside. Amen.
