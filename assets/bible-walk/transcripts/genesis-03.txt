The garden is perfect. Every tree is lovely, every need is met, and God himself walks there. Only one tree is forbidden. Then a voice asks the woman a quiet question, a question that sounds innocent, but is not: 'Yea, hath God said, Ye shall not eat of every tree of the garden?'
In chapter two, the LORD God formed the man from the dust, and breathed into him the breath of life. He planted a garden in Eden, gave the man work to do, and one command: do not eat of the tree of the knowledge of good and evil. Then he made the woman, and brought her to the man, and they lived together in the garden with nothing to hide.
Now, in chapter three, comes the saddest chapter in Genesis. Everything that is wrong in the world begins here. But listen carefully, because in the middle of the sorrow, God makes the first promise of a Saviour.
The serpent is more subtle, more cunning, than any beast of the field. Later, the Bible tells us that behind the serpent was Satan, the devil. He begins with a question that twists God’s words. Did God really say you may not eat of every tree?
The woman answers that they may eat the fruit of the trees, but of the tree in the middle of the garden God has said, you shall not eat of it, neither shall you touch it, lest you die. Then the serpent openly contradicts God. 'Ye shall not surely die: For God doth know that in the day ye eat thereof, then your eyes shall be opened, and ye shall be as gods, knowing good and evil.'
The lie is clear. God is holding something back from you. He does not want what is best for you. You can be like God without him.
The woman looks at the tree. She sees that it is good for food, pleasant to the eyes, and a tree to be desired to make one wise. She takes the fruit, and eats, and gives some to her husband, who is with her. And he eats.
Their eyes are opened, just as the serpent said. But what they see is not glory. They feel shame. They sew fig leaves together to cover themselves.
Then they hear the voice of the LORD God walking in the garden in the cool of the day. Before, they would have run to meet him. Now they hide among the trees. And God calls out to the man.
Adam answers that he heard God’s voice, and was afraid, and hid himself. God asks, who told you that you needed to cover yourself? Have you eaten of the tree I commanded you not to eat? And the blaming begins. The man says, the woman whom thou gavest to be with me, she gave me of the tree, and I did eat. The woman says, the serpent beguiled me, and I did eat.
Then God speaks to each of them. He curses the serpent, to crawl on its belly and eat dust. To the woman, he says that she will bring forth children in sorrow, and that there will be strain between her and her husband. To the man, he says the ground is cursed because of him. It will bring forth thorns and thistles. He will eat bread by the sweat of his face, until he returns to the ground. 'for dust thou art, and unto dust shalt thou return.'
Adam names his wife Eve, because she is the mother of all living. Even under the shadow of death, he names her for life.
God makes them clothing from the skins of animals. Then he sends them out of the garden, so that they will not eat of the tree of life and live for ever in their fallen state. At the east of the garden he places cherubims, and a flaming sword which turns every way, to keep the way of the tree of life.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 9. 'And the LORD God called unto Adam, and said unto him, Where art thou?' God knew exactly where Adam was. This was not the question of someone who was lost. It was the call of a Father looking for his child. The man had sinned and hidden himself, but God came looking for him. That is the story of the whole Bible. We run and hide, and God comes calling. He is still asking each of us, where are you?
Verse 15. 'And I will put enmity between thee and the woman, and between thy seed and her seed; it shall bruise thy head, and thou shalt bruise his heel.' This is the first promise of the gospel. God speaks to the serpent, and promises that one day a son of the woman will crush its head, though he himself will be wounded. A bruised heel, but a crushed head. Christians have seen here, from the earliest days, a promise of Jesus, who was wounded on the cross, and who defeated sin, death and the devil.
Verse 21. 'Unto Adam also and to his wife did the LORD God make coats of skins, and clothed them.' The fig leaves could not cover their shame. So God himself clothed them. And to make coats of skins, a life had to be given. It is a quiet picture of grace. God judged their sin, but he also covered them. He does not leave us in our shame. He provides a covering we could never make for ourselves.
The apostle Paul wrote, 'For as in Adam all die, even so in Christ shall all be made alive.' And at the end of the Bible, in the city of God, the curse of this chapter is lifted: 'And there shall be no more curse'. What was lost in the garden is restored through Jesus.
Is there something you have been hiding from God? He already knows, and he is not calling you to punish you, but to bring you home. Answer his call today. Tell him honestly where you are, and let him cover you with his grace.
Here is today’s question. God called out to Adam, where art thou? If God asked you that question today, how would you answer him?
Let us pray. Lord God, like Adam and Eve, I have listened to the wrong voice and gone my own way. Thank you that you came looking for me. Thank you for the promise of a Saviour, and for covering my sin through Jesus. Help me not to hide from you, but to come to you. Amen.
