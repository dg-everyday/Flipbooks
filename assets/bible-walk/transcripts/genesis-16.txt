A young woman sits alone by a spring of water in the desert. She is far from home, and expecting a child. She has run away from a household where she was treated harshly, and she has nowhere to go. No one knows where she is. No one cares. Or so she thinks. Then a voice calls her by name.
In chapter fifteen, God told Abram to look up and count the stars. So shall thy seed be, God said. Abram believed the LORD, and God counted it to him for righteousness. Then God made a solemn covenant with Abram, passing alone between the pieces as a smoking furnace and a burning lamp.
Now, in chapter sixteen, ten years have passed since Abram came into Canaan, and still there is no child. Sarai decides it is time to take matters into her own hands. It is a chapter about what happens when we stop waiting for God. But it is also a chapter about how God cares for the people everyone else overlooks.
Sarai, Abram’s wife, has no children. But she has a maidservant, an Egyptian, whose name is Hagar. Perhaps Hagar joined the household when they were in Egypt.
Sarai says to Abram, 'Behold now, the LORD hath restrained me from bearing'. She suggests that Abram take Hagar as a second wife, so that Sarai might have children through her. In that time and place, this was a common custom. But it was not God’s way. 'And Abram hearkened to the voice of Sarai.' These words echo what God said to Adam in the garden, because thou hast hearkened unto the voice of thy wife. Once again, someone listens to a human plan instead of waiting for God.
So Sarai gives Hagar to Abram as his wife, and Hagar is soon expecting a child. And at once there is trouble. When Hagar sees that she will have a child, she begins to look down on Sarai. Sarai is hurt and angry, and she blames Abram. My wrong be upon thee, she says. The LORD judge between me and thee.
Abram does not want to deal with it. Your maid is in your hand, he says. Do to her as it pleases you. And when Sarai deals harshly with Hagar, Hagar runs away.
Then something wonderful happens.
This is the first time in the Bible that the angel of the LORD appears. And he comes, not to a king, or a priest, or to Abram, but to a runaway servant girl from Egypt.
The angel says, 'Hagar, Sarai’s maid, whence camest thou? and whither wilt thou go?' He calls her by name. He knows who she is and where she has come from. Hagar answers honestly. I flee from the face of my mistress Sarai.
The angel tells her to return to her mistress, and submit herself under her hands. It is not an easy thing to hear. But then he gives her a promise, much like the promise given to Abram. 'I will multiply thy seed exceedingly, that it shall not be numbered for multitude.'
Then the angel tells her about the son she will bear.
The angel says that her son will be a wild man, strong and independent. His hand will be against every man, and every man’s hand against him. He will live near all his brothers, but apart from them.
Hagar is amazed. She gives God a new name.
The well is named Beer-lahai-roi, the well of him that liveth and seeth me. It lies between Kadesh and Bered.
So Hagar returns. She bears Abram a son, and Abram calls his son’s name Ishmael, as the angel said. Abram is eighty-six years old when Hagar bears Ishmael.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 7. 'And the angel of the LORD found her by a fountain of water in the wilderness, by the fountain in the way to Shur.' Notice the word found. Hagar was not looking for God. God was looking for her. She was a slave, a foreigner, and a runaway. She had no power and no place. But the God of heaven came looking for her in the desert. No one is too small, too far away, or too forgotten for God to find.
Verse 11. 'And the angel of the LORD said unto her, Behold, thou art with child, and shalt bear a son, and shalt call his name Ishmael; because the LORD hath heard thy affliction.' The name Ishmael means God hears. Every time Hagar called her son by name, she would remember that God had heard her. God hears the cries of those who are mistreated. He heard Hagar, and he still hears every cry of pain, even the cries we cannot put into words.
Verse 13. 'And she called the name of the LORD that spake unto her, Thou God seest me: for she said, Have I also here looked after him that seeth me?' Hagar is the first person in the Bible to give God a name. And the name she chose was, the God who sees me. She had felt invisible, used and thrown away. But God saw her. Whatever you are going through, you are not invisible to God. He sees you, and he cares.
A psalm says, 'The eyes of the LORD are upon the righteous, and his ears are open unto their cry.' And Jesus said that not one sparrow falls to the ground without the Father, and 'the very hairs of your head are all numbered. Fear ye not therefore, ye are of more value than many sparrows.'
Is there someone around you who is overlooked, mistreated, or forgotten? Be the one who sees them, as God saw Hagar. And if you feel invisible yourself, remember that he is the God who sees you, and the God who hears.
Here is today’s question. Hagar called God, thou God seest me. What difference does it make to you today that God sees you and hears you?
Let us pray. Lord God, you are the God who sees me, and the God who hears. Thank you that no one is too small or too far away for you to find. Forgive me for running ahead of you instead of waiting for your promises. Help me to see the people others overlook. Amen.
