Two sisters live in the same household, married to the same man. One is loved, but has no children. The other has many children, but is not loved. Each one wants what the other has. Year after year, the rivalry grows, and the family grows with it. And in the middle of it all, God is quietly building a nation.
In chapter twenty-nine, Jacob arrived in Haran and fell in love with Rachel. He served her father Laban seven years for her, but Laban tricked him, and gave him Leah instead. Jacob served seven more years for Rachel. The LORD saw that Leah was unloved, and gave her four sons. With the fourth, Judah, she said, now will I praise the LORD.
Now, in chapter thirty, we hear how the rest of Jacob’s children were born. It is not a peaceful story. It is full of jealousy and competition. These children will become the fathers of the twelve tribes of Israel. And yet they are born into a family full of hurt. God’s plan goes forward, not because the family is perfect, but because God is faithful.
When Rachel sees that she bears Jacob no children, she envies her sister. She says to Jacob, 'Give me children, or else I die.' Jacob’s anger is kindled against Rachel. He says, am I in God’s stead, who has kept children from you?
So Rachel does what Sarah once did. She gives her maid Bilhah to Jacob as a wife, so that she may have children through her. Bilhah has a son, and Rachel says, God hath judged me, and hath also heard my voice. She calls him Dan, which means judge. Bilhah has a second son, and Rachel says, with great wrestlings have I wrestled with my sister, and I have prevailed. She calls him Naphtali, which means my wrestling.
Leah sees that she has stopped having children for now. So she gives her own maid Zilpah to Jacob. Zilpah has two sons, and Leah names them Gad, meaning good fortune, and Asher, meaning happy.
The rivalry goes on. Leah’s son Reuben finds some mandrakes in the field, plants that people believed would help a woman to have children. Rachel asks for them, and the two sisters strike a bargain over them. But it is not the mandrakes that bring children. It is God. And God hearkens unto Leah. She has two more sons, Issachar and Zebulun. With Zebulun she says, God hath endued me with a good dowry. Now will my husband dwell with me. Afterwards she has a daughter, and calls her Dinah.
And then, at last, the turning point comes.
Rachel has a son. She says, God hath taken away my reproach. She calls him Joseph, which means may he add, and she says, The LORD shall add to me another son. Joseph will become one of the most important people in the whole book.
After Joseph is born, Jacob says to Laban, send me away, that I may go to my own place, and to my country. Give me my wives and my children, for whom I have served you, and let me go.
But Laban does not want to lose him.
Jacob answers him plainly.
Laban asks, what shall I give thee? Jacob says, you shall not give me anything. He suggests a plan. He will keep caring for Laban’s flocks. But every speckled and spotted goat, and every brown sheep, will be his wages. In those lands, most sheep were white and most goats were dark, so Jacob is asking for the rarer animals. Laban agrees. But that same day, Laban removes all the speckled and spotted animals from the flock, and gives them to his sons, and sets three days’ journey between himself and Jacob. Laban is still trying to cheat him.
Jacob tries a plan of his own. He takes rods of green poplar, hazel and chestnut, and peels white stripes in them, and sets them in the watering troughs where the strong animals come to drink. And the flocks bring forth young that are streaked, speckled and spotted. Later, Jacob himself will say that it was God, not his rods, who made his flocks grow.
So Jacob increases exceedingly. He has many flocks, and maidservants, and menservants, and camels, and donkeys.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 22. 'And God remembered Rachel, and God hearkened to her'. Rachel had tried everything: blaming her husband, using her maid, bargaining for mandrakes. None of it worked. Then God remembered her. Just as God remembered Noah in the ark, he remembered Rachel. Our schemes and efforts cannot produce what only God can give. But he does not forget us. He hears our prayers, and answers in his time.
Verse 27. 'And Laban said unto him, I pray thee, if I have found favour in thine eyes, tarry: for I have learned by experience that the LORD hath blessed me for thy sake.' Even Laban, a selfish and scheming man, could see that God was blessing him because of Jacob. God had promised Abraham, I will bless them that bless thee. Here it was happening. God’s blessing on his people often spills over to the people around them. Others notice when God is at work in someone’s life.
Verse 30. 'For it was little which thou hadst before I came, and it is now increased unto a multitude; and the LORD hath blessed thee since my coming: and now when shall I provide for mine own house also?' Jacob had worked faithfully for fourteen years. Laban had grown rich. But Jacob had nothing of his own. He was right to ask for fair wages, to provide for his family. Hard and honest work is good. And Jacob now gave the credit to God: the LORD hath blessed thee. Slowly, Jacob was learning where blessing comes from.
Rachel cried, give me children, or else I die. Long after, a psalm would say, 'Lo, children are an heritage of the LORD'. And Paul wrote that whatever we do, we should 'do it heartily, as to the Lord, and not unto men'.
Is there jealousy in your heart toward someone who has what you want? Bring it to God honestly. Stop comparing, and trust him with your story. And in your work today, do it faithfully, as though you were working for God himself.
Here is today’s question. Rachel and Leah were caught up in jealousy and competition. Who are you tempted to compare yourself with, and how could you bring that to God?
Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that you remember those who wait, as you remembered Rachel. Forgive me for jealousy and comparison. Help me to trust you with my story, to work faithfully, and to give you the credit for every blessing in my life. Amen.
