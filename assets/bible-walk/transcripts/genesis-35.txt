A family stands in a circle under a great oak tree. One by one, they drop small carved figures into a hole in the ground: the idols they have been secretly keeping. Earrings follow, and the hole is filled in. Then they turn, and set out on a journey back to the place where, long ago, a runaway saw a ladder reaching to heaven.
In chapter thirty-four, Jacob’s daughter Dinah was wronged by Shechem, the son of a local prince. Jacob stayed silent, and his sons Simeon and Levi took a cruel revenge, killing the men of the city. Jacob rebuked them only because he feared what his neighbours would do.
Now, in chapter thirty-five, God speaks again. After the darkness of the last chapter, God calls Jacob back to where his journey with God began. It is a chapter of fresh starts, and of goodbyes. Three people die in this chapter. And God speaks a promise that looks far ahead.
God says to Jacob, 'Arise, go up to Bethel, and dwell there: and make there an altar unto God, that appeared unto thee when thou fleddest from the face of Esau thy brother.' Long ago, at Bethel, Jacob had vowed that if God brought him home safely, he would worship him there. Twenty years had passed, and then more years at Shechem, and he had not gone back. Now God calls him to keep his promise.
Jacob speaks to his household, and to everyone who is with him.
They give Jacob all the strange gods in their hands, and the earrings in their ears, and Jacob hides them under the oak near Shechem. Then they set out. 'and the terror of God was upon the cities that were round about them, and they did not pursue after the sons of Jacob.' After what happened at Shechem, Jacob feared attack. But God protected them on the road.
Jacob comes to Bethel, with all the people that are with him. He builds an altar there, and calls the place El-beth-el, which means the God of the house of God. This is where God appeared to him when he fled from his brother.
There, Deborah, Rebekah’s old nurse, dies, and is buried under an oak below Bethel. Jacob’s family names it the oak of weeping. She had travelled with Rebekah from Haran long ago, and served the family for many years.
God appears to Jacob again, and blesses him.
And God says to him, 'I am God Almighty: be fruitful and multiply; a nation and a company of nations shall be of thee'. Kings will come from his family. And God promises to give him the land he gave to Abraham and Isaac. Then God goes up from him. And Jacob sets up a pillar of stone in the place where God talked with him, and pours a drink offering on it, and oil. He calls the place Bethel, the house of God.
Then they journey on from Bethel. And when there is still a little way to go to Ephrath, Rachel goes into labour, and has a very hard time. The midwife says to her, fear not, for you will have this son also. Many years before, Rachel had named Joseph, saying, the LORD shall add to me another son. Now her prayer is answered. But as her life is departing, she names the baby Benoni, which means son of my sorrow. His father calls him Benjamin, which means son of my right hand.
Rachel dies, and is buried on the way to Ephrath, which is Bethlehem. Jacob sets a pillar on her grave, and it is still there when Genesis is written.
The chapter mentions, briefly, a grievous sin by Reuben, Jacob’s eldest son, that dishonours his father. And Israel heard it. Like the silence after Dinah, this will come back later.
Then Genesis lists the twelve sons of Jacob. The sons of Leah: Reuben, Simeon, Levi, Judah, Issachar and Zebulun. The sons of Rachel: Joseph and Benjamin. The sons of Bilhah: Dan and Naphtali. The sons of Zilpah: Gad and Asher.
At last Jacob comes home to Isaac his father, at Mamre, near Hebron. Isaac lives to be a hundred and eighty years old. Then he gives up the ghost, and dies, and is gathered to his people, being old and full of days. And his sons Esau and Jacob bury him. Once again, two brothers who were long divided stand together at their father’s grave.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 2. 'Then Jacob said unto his household, and to all that were with him, Put away the strange gods that are among you, and be clean, and change your garments:' Jacob’s family had been carrying idols. Rachel had stolen her father’s household gods, and the captives from Shechem may have brought others. Before they could meet God at Bethel, they had to get rid of everything that competed with him. Put away. Be clean. Change. Those three steps still describe what it means to turn back to God.
Verse 3. 'And let us arise, and go up to Bethel; and I will make there an altar unto God, who answered me in the day of my distress, and was with me in the way which I went.' Jacob looked back over his life, and saw God’s faithfulness. God answered me in the day of my distress. God was with me in the way which I went. Every promise God made at Bethel had come true. Remembering what God has done is one of the best ways to renew our faith, especially after a time when we have drifted.
Verse 10. 'And God said unto him, Thy name is Jacob: thy name shall not be called any more Jacob, but Israel shall be thy name: and he called his name Israel.' God had given Jacob the name Israel at the river Jabbok. Now, at Bethel, he confirms it. After the failures at Shechem, Jacob may have felt like the old Jacob again. But God reminded him of who he really was. When we fail, God does not take back the new identity he has given us. He calls us back to it.
Many years later, the prophet Jeremiah wrote of Rachel weeping for her children. And Matthew tells us that when King Herod killed the children of Bethlehem, these words came true: 'Rachel weeping for her children, and would not be comforted'. But Bethlehem, where Rachel died in sorrow, became the birthplace of Jesus.
Is there anything in your life that competes with God, something you need to put away? Ask him to show you. And take some time to remember how God has answered you in the day of your distress, and been with you on the way.
Here is today’s question. Jacob told his family to put away their strange gods before going to Bethel. What might God be asking you to put away, so that you can draw closer to him?
Let us pray. Lord God, you answered Jacob in the day of his distress, and you have been with me in the way I have gone. Show me anything I need to put away. Make me clean, and change me. Thank you that when I fail, you call me back to who I am in you. Amen.
