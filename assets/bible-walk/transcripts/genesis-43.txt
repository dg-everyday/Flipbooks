The ten brothers have returned to Egypt, this time with Benjamin. They are led, not to the governor’s office, but to his private house. They are terrified. Is this a trap? Then the governor comes home, sees Benjamin, and asks, is this your youngest brother? He speaks a blessing over him. And suddenly, he hurries from the room, looking for somewhere to weep.
In chapter forty-two, the famine drove Joseph’s brothers to Egypt. They bowed before him, not knowing who he was. Joseph accused them of being spies, kept Simeon in prison, and told them not to come back without their youngest brother. And the brothers said, we are verily guilty concerning our brother. Jacob refused to let Benjamin go, saying, all these things are against me.
Now, in chapter forty-three, the food runs out again. Jacob must make a painful decision. And we see one of the brothers beginning to change.
The famine is severe in the land. When they have eaten up the corn they brought from Egypt, Jacob says, go again, buy us a little food.
Judah answers him. The man solemnly warned us, saying, you shall not see my face unless your brother is with you. If you send our brother with us, we will go down and buy food. But if you will not send him, we will not go.
Jacob says, why did you deal so badly with me, as to tell the man you had another brother? They say, the man asked us closely about our family. Is your father yet alive? Have you another brother? Could we know he would say, bring your brother down?
Then Judah speaks to his father, and makes a promise.
Judah adds, if we had not delayed, by now we could have gone and returned twice.
This is the same Judah who once said, let us sell Joseph to the Ishmeelites. He had broken his father’s heart over one son of Rachel. Now he is offering his own life for the other.
And at last, Jacob gives way. If it must be so now, do this. Take some of the best fruits of the land in your vessels, and carry down the man a present: a little balm, a little honey, spices, myrrh, nuts and almonds. Take double money, and the money that was returned in your sacks. Perhaps it was an oversight. Take also your brother, and arise, go again unto the man.
And then Jacob prays.
The men take the present, and double money, and Benjamin, and go down to Egypt, and stand before Joseph.
When Joseph sees Benjamin with them, he tells the ruler of his house, bring these men home, and prepare a meal, for these men will dine with me. The brothers are afraid when they are brought into Joseph’s house. They think, it is because of the money that was returned in our sacks. He wants to seize us, and make us slaves, and take our donkeys. So they speak to the steward at the door, and explain about the money. But the steward says, peace be to you, fear not. Your God, and the God of your father, has given you treasure in your sacks. I had your money. And he brings Simeon out to them.
They get their present ready for Joseph. When Joseph comes home, they bring him the present, and bow down to the earth before him. Again the dream comes true.
Joseph asks about their welfare, and says, is your father well, the old man of whom you spoke? Is he yet alive? They answer, thy servant our father is in good health, he is yet alive. And they bow their heads again.
Then Joseph sees Benjamin.
Joseph hurries out, for his heart yearns for his brother, and he looks for a place to weep. He goes into his room, and weeps there. Then he washes his face, comes out, controls himself, and says, set on bread.
They serve Joseph by himself, the brothers by themselves, and the Egyptians by themselves, because the Egyptians would not eat with Hebrews. The brothers are seated before him in order of their ages, from the firstborn to the youngest, and they look at one another in amazement. How could this Egyptian know their order of birth?
Joseph sends portions from his own table to them. And Benjamin’s portion is five times as much as any of theirs. Joseph is testing them. Will they be jealous of this favoured son of Rachel, as they were of him? But this time, there is no envy. 'And they drank, and were merry with him.'
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 9. 'I will be surety for him; of my hand shalt thou require him: if I bring him not unto thee, and set him before thee, then let me bear the blame for ever:' To be surety means to take full responsibility, to put yourself in someone’s place. Judah offered himself as the guarantee for Benjamin’s safety. If anything happened to the boy, Judah would carry the blame for ever. This is a changed man. And it is a picture of someone greater from the tribe of Judah, who would one day take our blame upon himself.
Verse 14. 'And God Almighty give you mercy before the man, that he may send away your other brother, and Benjamin. If I be bereaved of my children, I am bereaved.' Jacob had done everything he could. He sent gifts, double money and his sons. Then he put the outcome in God’s hands. If I be bereaved, I am bereaved. It is not giving up. It is giving over. There comes a time when we have done all we can, and must trust God with what we cannot control.
Verse 29. 'And he lifted up his eyes, and saw his brother Benjamin, his mother’s son, and said, Is this your younger brother, of whom ye spake unto me? And he said, God be gracious unto thee, my son.' Joseph had not seen his little brother for twenty years. Benjamin was just a small boy when Joseph was sold. Now Joseph looked at him, and the first thing he did was bless him in the name of God. Even while still hiding who he was, Joseph’s heart was full of love, and his first words were a prayer.
The letter to the Hebrews says that Jesus was 'made a surety of a better testament.' And Jesus said, 'Greater love hath no man than this, that a man lay down his life for his friends.' Judah, offering himself for his brother, points forward to the Lion of the tribe of Judah.
Is there a situation where you have done everything you can? Like Jacob, put it into God’s hands, and trust him with the outcome. And think of someone you could stand up for this week, as Judah stood up for Benjamin.
Here is today’s question. Judah said, I will be surety for him. Who is someone you could take responsibility for, protect, or stand beside this week?
Let us pray. Lord God Almighty, when I have done all I can, help me to trust you with the rest. Thank you for Jesus, who took my blame upon himself. Make me willing, like Judah, to stand up for others, and let my words, like Joseph’s, be full of blessing. Amen.
