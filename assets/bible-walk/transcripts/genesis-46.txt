An old man stands at the edge of the land God promised his family. Behind him is Canaan, where his grandfather Abraham and his father Isaac lived and died. Ahead is Egypt, a foreign land. His son is waiting there. But should he go? The last time someone in his family went to Egypt without God’s word, it went badly. So the old man stops, and offers sacrifices, and waits.
In chapter forty-five, Joseph made himself known to his brothers. He said, I am Joseph your brother, whom ye sold into Egypt. Be not grieved, for God did send me before you to preserve life. He sent wagons to bring his father. And when Jacob saw them, his spirit revived, and he said, Joseph my son is yet alive: I will go and see him before I die.
Now, in chapter forty-six, Jacob begins the journey to Egypt. It is a big step for the whole family. The promised land is being left behind, for a time. And before Jacob crosses the border, God speaks to him one last time in Genesis.
Israel takes his journey with all that he has, and comes to Beersheba. This is the place where Abraham called on the name of the everlasting God, and where Isaac built an altar. And there Jacob offers sacrifices to the God of his father Isaac.
And God speaks to Israel in visions. He says, Jacob, Jacob. And Jacob says, here am I. It is the same answer Abraham gave when God called him on the mountain in Moriah.
Then God makes Jacob a promise.
God goes on, with a promise about the journey and its end.
So Jacob rises up from Beersheba. His sons carry Jacob their father, their little ones and their wives, in the wagons Pharaoh sent. They take their cattle and their goods, and they come into Egypt: Jacob, and all his seed with him. His sons and his sons’ sons, his daughters and his sons’ daughters, all his seed he brings with him into Egypt.
Then Genesis lists the names of the children of Israel who came into Egypt. The sons of Leah, and their sons. The sons of Zilpah. The sons of Rachel, Joseph and Benjamin, and their sons. The sons of Bilhah. Among them are some names we have met before, and some we will meet again. Judah’s son Pharez is there, with his sons Hezron and Hamul, the line that leads to David.
All the souls of the house of Jacob who come into Egypt are seventy. It is a small number for a family that has been promised to become as many as the stars of heaven. But Egypt will be the place where God makes them a great nation, just as he said.
Jacob sends Judah ahead to Joseph, to show the way to Goshen. Once, Judah led the way in selling Joseph. Now he leads the family to him.
And they come into the land of Goshen.
Joseph makes ready his chariot, and goes up to meet Israel his father, to Goshen. He presents himself to him. And he falls on his neck, and weeps on his neck a good while. For twenty-two years Jacob had mourned his son as dead. Now he holds him in his arms.
Then Jacob speaks.
Then Joseph prepares his family to meet Pharaoh. I will go up and tell Pharaoh, he says, my brethren and my father’s house, which were in the land of Canaan, are come unto me. And the men are shepherds. When Pharaoh asks what your occupation is, tell him, thy servants’ trade hath been about cattle from our youth even until now. Then you may live in the land of Goshen. Joseph explains that every shepherd is an abomination to the Egyptians. The Egyptians look down on shepherds.
This is part of God’s care for them. In Goshen, apart from the Egyptians, Israel’s family will be able to live together, keep their flocks, and remain a separate people. In Canaan, they were starting to mix with the people of the land. In Egypt, they will grow into a nation, without losing who they are.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 3. 'And he said, I am God, the God of thy father: fear not to go down into Egypt; for I will there make of thee a great nation:' Jacob was afraid. He was leaving the land of promise, and he was old. But God spoke into his fear. Fear not. And God told him that this move was part of the plan. Egypt would not be the end of the promise. It would be the place where the promise grew. Sometimes God leads us into places that look like a step backward, but they are part of his plan.
Verse 4. 'I will go down with thee into Egypt; and I will also surely bring thee up again: and Joseph shall put his hand upon thine eyes.' I will go down with thee. That is the greatest comfort of all. Jacob would not go to Egypt alone. God would go with him. And God promised that the family would come back to Canaan one day. And there was a tender personal promise too. Joseph would be with Jacob at his death, and close his eyes. God cares about the great plan, and about an old man’s heart.
Verse 30. 'And Israel said unto Joseph, Now let me die, since I have seen thy face, because thou art yet alive.' Jacob had said, I will go down into the grave unto my son mourning. Instead, he held his son again. His deepest sorrow had been turned to joy. Jacob would live seventeen more years, but in that moment, he felt that his life was complete. God had given him back what he thought was lost for ever.
When Moses was afraid to lead God’s people, God said to him, 'Certainly I will be with thee'. Jesus’ last words in Matthew’s Gospel are a promise to all his followers: 'and, lo, I am with you alway, even unto the end of the world.'
Is God leading you into something new and unfamiliar? Bring your fears to him, as Jacob did. Listen for his words: fear not, I will go with thee. And thank him for the times he has turned your sorrow into joy.
Here is today’s question. God told Jacob, I will go down with thee into Egypt. Where do you need the promise that God will go with you?
Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that you go with me into every new place. When I am afraid, speak to me as you spoke to Jacob: fear not. Thank you for turning sorrow into joy, and for giving back what seemed lost. Help me to trust your plan, even when it leads me somewhere unexpected. Amen.
