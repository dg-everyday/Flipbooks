An old man sits up in bed, too weak to stand, his eyes almost blind. Two young men are brought to him, his grandsons. Their father places the elder at the old man’s right hand, and the younger at his left. But as the old man lifts his hands to bless them, he crosses his arms, and places his right hand on the younger boy’s head.
In chapter forty-seven, Jacob stood before Pharaoh and blessed him. He called his life a pilgrimage. Joseph nourished his family, and wisely saved the people of Egypt through the famine. And Jacob made Joseph swear to bury him in Canaan, not in Egypt.
Now, in chapter forty-eight, Jacob is dying. Before he goes, he does something important. He blesses Joseph’s two sons, and makes them part of his own family. And in his blessing, he looks back over his whole life, and sees God’s hand in every part of it.
After these things, someone tells Joseph, behold, thy father is sick. So Joseph takes his two sons, Manasseh and Ephraim, and goes to him. When Jacob is told that Joseph is coming, Israel strengthens himself, and sits up on the bed.
Jacob tells Joseph about the day God appeared to him at Luz, which is Bethel, in the land of Canaan. God Almighty blessed me there, he says, and promised, I will make thee fruitful, and multiply thee, and give this land to thy seed after thee for an everlasting possession.
Then Jacob makes an announcement. Your two sons, Ephraim and Manasseh, who were born to you in Egypt before I came, are mine. As Reuben and Simeon, they shall be mine. In other words, Jacob adopts Joseph’s sons as his own. Each of them will become the father of a whole tribe of Israel. Joseph, the son who was thrown away, receives a double portion.
Jacob remembers Rachel, Joseph’s mother. When I came from Padan, Rachel died by me in the land of Canaan on the way, when there was still a little way to come to Ephrath. And I buried her there, on the way to Ephrath, which is Bethlehem. Even at the end of his life, Jacob has not forgotten his beloved wife.
Then Israel sees Joseph’s sons, and asks, who are these? Joseph says, they are my sons, whom God hath given me in this place. Jacob says, bring them to me, I pray thee, and I will bless them.
Jacob’s eyes are dim with age, so that he cannot see. Joseph brings the boys near, and Jacob kisses them and embraces them.
Joseph brings them out from between his father’s knees, and bows himself with his face to the earth. Then Joseph takes his two sons. He puts Ephraim, the younger, toward Israel’s left hand, and Manasseh, the elder, toward Israel’s right hand. The right hand was the hand of the greater blessing.
But Israel stretches out his right hand, and lays it on Ephraim’s head, the younger. And he lays his left hand on Manasseh’s head, guiding his hands wittingly, on purpose. For Manasseh is the firstborn.
Then Jacob blesses Joseph.
When Joseph sees that his father has laid his right hand on Ephraim’s head, it displeases him. He takes hold of his father’s hand, to move it from Ephraim’s head to Manasseh’s head. Not so, my father, he says, for this is the firstborn. Put thy right hand upon his head.
But his father refuses. I know it, my son, I know it, he says. He also shall become a people, and he also shall be great. But truly his younger brother shall be greater than he, and his seed shall become a multitude of nations. And he sets Ephraim before Manasseh.
Once again in Genesis, the younger is put before the elder. Isaac before Ishmael. Jacob before Esau. Joseph before his older brothers. Pharez before Zarah. Now Ephraim before Manasseh. God does not follow the world’s order. He chooses by grace.
Israel says to Joseph, behold, I die. But God shall be with you, and bring you again unto the land of your fathers. And he gives Joseph a portion of land above his brothers.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 11. 'And Israel said unto Joseph, I had not thought to see thy face: and, lo, God hath shewed me also thy seed.' For years Jacob believed his son was dead. He never expected to see Joseph again. But God gave him not only his son, but his grandsons too. God gave Jacob more than he had dared to hope for. Paul wrote that God is able to do exceeding abundantly above all that we ask or think. Jacob would have agreed.
Verse 15. 'And he blessed Joseph, and said, God, before whom my fathers Abraham and Isaac did walk, the God which fed me all my life long unto this day,' The God which fed me all my life long. Jacob was a shepherd, and he describes God as his shepherd. This is the first time in the Bible that God is spoken of in this way. Looking back on a long and troubled life, Jacob saw that God had been feeding him, leading him and caring for him at every step, even when he did not know it.
Verse 16. 'The Angel which redeemed me from all evil, bless the lads; and let my name be named on them, and the name of my fathers Abraham and Isaac; and let them grow into a multitude in the midst of the earth.' To redeem means to rescue, to buy back, to set free. Jacob remembered the Angel who met him at Bethel, who wrestled with him at the river, who protected him from Laban and from Esau. This Angel had redeemed him from all evil. Jacob had made many mistakes, but God had rescued him again and again. That is the story of every believer: redeemed by God.
The letter to the Hebrews says, 'By faith Jacob, when he was a dying, blessed both the sons of Joseph; and worshipped, leaning upon the top of his staff.' And the psalm says, 'The LORD is my shepherd; I shall not want.'
Look back over your own life. Where can you see that God has been your shepherd, feeding you and leading you all your life long? Thank him for it. And think of the younger people around you. How could you speak a blessing over them today?
Here is today’s question. Jacob called God the God which fed me all my life long. Looking back over your own life, where can you see God’s care?
Let us pray. Lord God, you have been my shepherd all my life long, feeding me and leading me, even when I did not know it. Thank you for redeeming me from evil through Jesus. Help me to bless those who come after me, and to trust you to do more than I can ask or think. Amen.
