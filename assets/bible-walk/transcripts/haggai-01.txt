Imagine working hard all week, and being paid on the last day. You put the money in your pocket and walk home. But when you get there, your pocket is empty. There was a hole in it, and the coins have fallen out, one by one, all along the road. That is the picture God gives his people in this chapter. And then he tells them why it is happening.
In the introduction, we heard that Haggai is one of the shortest books in the Bible, and the first book of the prophets on our walk. A prophet carried God’s message to his people. We heard that the people of Judah had come home from Babylon and laid the foundation of the temple, but the work stopped, and for years the house of God lay in ruins.
Now, in chapter one, God speaks. Listen for the sharp question he asks. Listen for what he tells the people to do. And listen for how they answer.
The book begins with a date. 'In the second year of Darius the king, in the sixth month, in the first day of the month, came the word of the LORD by Haggai the prophet'. Haggai wants us to know that this really happened, on a real day, to real people. The message is for Zerubbabel the governor, and Joshua the high priest, and through them, for all the people.
God begins by telling Haggai what the people are saying. 'This people say, The time is not come, the time that the LORD’S house should be built.'
It sounded sensible. Times were hard. Money was short. Their enemies had stopped the work before. Maybe one day, when things were better, they would build. But not yet.
So God asks them a question. 'Is it time for you, O ye, to dwell in your cieled houses, and this house lie waste?' Cieled means panelled, with fine wooden walls and ceilings. Wood was costly in that land. The people had found the time and the money to make their own homes beautiful. But God’s house was still a ruin. It was not really a question of time. It was a question of what came first.
Then God says words that he will repeat in this chapter.
Next, God shows them what has been happening in their lives. 'Ye have sown much, and bring in little; ye eat, but ye have not enough; ye drink, but ye are not filled with drink; ye clothe you, but there is none warm; and he that earneth wages earneth wages to put it into a bag with holes.'
They planted big fields, and got small harvests. They ate, but were still hungry. They had clothes, but were still cold. And their money seemed to slip away, like coins through a hole in a bag. They had been working so hard for themselves. And it was never enough.
Again God says, 'Consider your ways.' And then he tells them what to do.
God explains what has gone wrong. You looked for much, and it came to little. And when you brought it home, I did blow upon it, like chaff blown away by the wind. Why? 'Because of mine house that is waste, and ye run every man unto his own house.' Every man ran to his own house. They were not lazy. They were busy. But they were busy with the wrong things first.
So God had held back the rain and the dew. He called for a drought on the land and the mountains, on the corn and the new wine and the oil, on people and cattle, and on all the work of their hands. God was not being cruel. He was getting their attention, the way a loving father might, so they would turn back to him.
And this time, the people listen. 'Then Zerubbabel the son of Shealtiel, and Joshua the son of Josedech, the high priest, with all the remnant of the people, obeyed the voice of the LORD their God, and the words of Haggai the prophet'. The remnant means those who were left, the small group who had come home. And the people did fear before the LORD. That means they took God seriously, with deep respect.
Then Haggai brings them a second message, very short.
And God does something inside them. 'And the LORD stirred up the spirit of Zerubbabel the son of Shealtiel, governor of Judah, and the spirit of Joshua the son of Josedech, the high priest, and the spirit of all the remnant of the people; and they came and did work in the house of the LORD of hosts, their God'.
The last verse gives another date. It tells us the work began just twenty three days after Haggai first spoke. For years, nothing had happened. Then God spoke, the people obeyed, and within a few weeks, they were building.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 5. 'Now therefore thus saith the LORD of hosts; Consider your ways.' To consider your ways means to stop, and think carefully about how you are living, and where it is leading. God says it twice in this chapter, and later he will say consider again. Sometimes we are so busy that we never stop to ask, why is my life like this? Is God first? God’s question was not meant to shame them. It was an invitation to look honestly, and to change.
Verse 8. 'Go up to the mountain, and bring wood, and build the house; and I will take pleasure in it, and I will be glorified, saith the LORD.' Notice how simple God’s command is. Go up. Bring wood. Build. He does not ask them to wait for more money, or better times. He asks them to start, with what they have. And he gives them a promise. I will take pleasure in it. God delights when his people put him first. And when they do, he is glorified, which means people see how great and good he is.
Verse 13. 'Then spake Haggai the LORD’S messenger in the LORD’S message unto the people, saying, I am with you, saith the LORD.' This is the shortest message in the book, just four words. I am with you. The people had just obeyed, and they were afraid. They knew they had let God down. But God did not scold them again. As soon as they turned back, he drew near. And those four words gave them the courage to begin.
A psalm says, 'Except the LORD build the house, they labour in vain that build it'. And Jesus said, 'But seek ye first the kingdom of God, and his righteousness; and all these things shall be added unto you.'
Is there something God has been asking you to do, and you keep saying, the time is not come? Today, consider your ways. Take one small step of obedience, with what you have. And remember God’s promise to those who turn back to him: I am with you.
Here is today’s question. The people said, the time is not come, while they made their own houses beautiful. Is there something you keep putting off, that you know God wants you to do?
Let us pray. Lord God, forgive me for the times I have been busy with my own things and left you until later. Help me to consider my ways. Stir up my spirit, as you stirred up your people, to put you first. Thank you that when I turn back to you, you say, I am with you. Amen.
