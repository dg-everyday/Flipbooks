A camper drops one small match onto dry grass. It is only a tiny flame. But the wind picks it up. The grass catches, then the bushes, then the trees. Soon a whole hillside is burning, and it takes many firefighters many days to put it out. All from one small match. James says our words can be like that.
In chapter two, James warned us not to treat the rich better than the poor, because God has chosen the poor of this world, rich in faith. And he asked his great question: what good is faith, if it does nothing? Faith without works is dead, as a body without breath is dead.
Now, in chapter three, James turns to something we use every day, many times a day. Our words. Listen for the pictures he uses. And listen for two kinds of wisdom.
James begins with a gentle warning. 'My brethren, be not many masters, knowing that we shall receive the greater condemnation.' Masters here means teachers. Not many of you should become teachers, James says, because those who teach will be judged more strictly. Their words carry weight.
And James includes himself. 'For in many things we offend all.' We all stumble, in many ways. 'If any man offend not in word, the same is a perfect man, and able also to bridle the whole body.' If anyone could control what they say perfectly, they could control everything else too.
Then come the pictures. 'Behold, we put bits in the horses’ mouths, that they may obey us; and we turn about their whole body.' A bit is a small piece of metal in a horse’s mouth. Yet with it, a rider can turn a big, strong horse wherever he wants to go.
'Behold also the ships, which though they be so great, and are driven of fierce winds, yet are they turned about with a very small helm, whithersoever the governor listeth.' A helm is a rudder. The governor is the pilot, steering the ship. A huge ship, pushed by strong winds, is steered by one small rudder.
And then James draws the lesson.
James goes on. 'And the tongue is a fire, a world of iniquity'. Like a fire, it can spread and destroy. A word of gossip, a lie, a cruel joke, can travel further than we ever imagined, and hurt people we have never met. It can damage a friendship, a family, or a whole church.
Then James says something humbling. People have tamed all kinds of animals: beasts and birds, snakes and sea creatures. 'But the tongue can no man tame; it is an unruly evil, full of deadly poison.' On our own, none of us can fully tame our tongue. We need God’s help.
And here is the strange thing. With the same tongue, we praise God, our Father. And with the same tongue, we curse people, who are made in the image of God.
So James asks some questions from nature. Does a spring of water pour out fresh water and bitter water from the same opening? Can a fig tree bear olives? Or a grapevine bear figs? Of course not. A spring gives the water that is inside it. A tree bears its own kind of fruit. And our words show what is really in our hearts.
Then James turns to wisdom. 'Who is a wise man and endued with knowledge among you? let him shew out of a good conversation his works with meekness of wisdom.' In the King James Bible, conversation means the way you live, not just the way you talk. If you are truly wise, James says, show it by a good life, and by gentle, humble actions.
But there is another kind of wisdom. If your heart is full of bitter jealousy and selfish ambition, do not boast about it, and do not lie against the truth. That kind of wisdom does not come from God. 'For where envying and strife is, there is confusion and every evil work.'
And then James describes the other kind, the true wisdom.
And he ends the chapter with a promise. 'And the fruit of righteousness is sown in peace of them that make peace.' Peacemakers are like farmers. They plant seeds of peace, and in time they see a harvest of goodness.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 5. 'Even so the tongue is a little member, and boasteth great things. Behold, how great a matter a little fire kindleth!' A member means a part of the body. The tongue is small, but it is powerful. Like the bit in a horse’s mouth, and the rudder on a ship, it can steer the direction of a whole life. Like a small spark, it can start a great fire. Never think that your words do not matter. They matter a great deal.
Verse 10. 'Out of the same mouth proceedeth blessing and cursing. My brethren, these things ought not so to be.' We can sing praise to God, and a few minutes later speak harshly to someone at home. James says simply, this should not be. It is not that we must try harder to sound nice. It is that God wants to change our hearts, so that the spring is fresh, and good words flow out.
Verse 17. 'But the wisdom that is from above is first pure, then peaceable, gentle, and easy to be intreated, full of mercy and good fruits, without partiality, and without hypocrisy.' Easy to be intreated means willing to listen, and open to reason. This is God’s wisdom. It is not proud, or harsh, or clever at hurting others. It is pure and peaceful, gentle and kind, fair to everyone, and honest. It is a picture of Jesus himself. And it is wisdom that God gives to all who ask.
Solomon wrote, 'Death and life are in the power of the tongue'. Paul wrote, 'Let no corrupt communication proceed out of your mouth, but that which is good to the use of edifying'. And Jesus said, 'Blessed are the peacemakers: for they shall be called the children of God.'
Think about the words you will speak today. Before you speak, ask yourself: is it true, is it kind, and will it help? Is there someone you have hurt with your words? Ask God to forgive you, and to give you the courage to put it right.
Here is today’s question. James says our words are like a rudder, a spark, and a spring of water. What would change in your home, your work or your friendships if your words were full of the wisdom from above?
Let us pray. Lord God, I confess that I cannot tame my tongue on my own. Forgive me for words that have hurt others. Make my heart like a fresh spring, so that good words flow from it. Fill me with your wisdom from above, pure, peaceable, gentle and full of mercy. Make me a peacemaker. Amen.
