Steam rises from a hot cup of tea. For a moment you can see it, curling up into the air. Then it is gone. You cannot catch it, or hold it, or keep it. James says our life is a little like that steam. Here for a short time, and then gone. So what will we do with the time we have?
In chapter three, James showed us the power of the tongue. A small bit turns a horse. A small rudder turns a ship. A little fire sets a forest burning. And he showed us two kinds of wisdom: the earthly kind, full of jealousy and strife, and the wisdom from above, pure, peaceable and gentle.
Now, in chapter four, James asks where our quarrels come from. Listen for his answer. And listen for how God responds when we come to him humbly.
James begins with a question. 'From whence come wars and fightings among you?' Where do all the quarrels and fights come from? And he answers it himself. They come from the selfish desires that battle inside us. We want something, and we do not get it. So we become jealous, and angry, and we fight.
Then James says something very simple. 'yet ye have not, because ye ask not.' How often do we worry, and grumble, and scheme, when we have never once asked God?
And sometimes, James says, we do ask, but with the wrong heart. 'Ye ask, and receive not, because ye ask amiss'. To ask amiss means to ask wrongly, for selfish reasons, only to please ourselves.
Then James speaks strongly to those who are trying to love God and love the world’s ways at the same time. 'know ye not that the friendship of the world is enmity with God?' The world here does not mean God’s good creation, or the people in it. It means a way of life that leaves God out, and lives for pride, and possessions, and pleasure. You cannot hold on to both.
That sounds hard. But then comes good news.
So James gives a list of things to do, one after another, like a coach calling to his team.
'Submit yourselves therefore to God. Resist the devil, and he will flee from you.' Submit means to come under God, to let him lead. And when we stand firm against temptation, in God’s strength, the devil runs.
Then the most beautiful promise of the chapter.
James continues. 'Cleanse your hands, ye sinners; and purify your hearts, ye double minded.' Be truly sorry for your sin. Do not laugh it off. And then, 'Humble yourselves in the sight of the Lord, and he shall lift you up.' The way up, in God’s kingdom, is down. When we bow low before God, he lifts us up.
Next James turns to our words again. 'Speak not evil one of another, brethren.' When we run others down, and judge them, we are putting ourselves in God’s place. 'There is one lawgiver, who is able to save and to destroy: who art thou that judgest another?'
And then James speaks to people making big plans. 'Go to now, ye that say, To day or to morrow we will go into such a city, and continue there a year, and buy and sell, and get gain'. They have it all worked out. Where they will go, how long they will stay, and how much money they will make. But they have left God out.
Instead, James says, you ought to say, 'If the Lord will, we shall live, and do this, or that.' It is not wrong to make plans. But we hold our plans in an open hand, and trust God with what lies ahead. Boasting about our plans as if we are in control is pride.
James ends the chapter with a short, searching sentence. 'Therefore to him that knoweth to do good, and doeth it not, to him it is sin.' Sin is not only doing what is wrong. It is also failing to do the good we know we should do.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 6. 'But he giveth more grace. Wherefore he saith, God resisteth the proud, but giveth grace unto the humble.' He giveth more grace. However great our need, God’s grace is greater. However many times we fail, he gives more. But notice who receives it. God resists the proud. He stands against those who think they do not need him. But he pours out grace on the humble, those who know they need him.
Verse 8. 'Draw nigh to God, and he will draw nigh to you. Cleanse your hands, ye sinners; and purify your hearts, ye double minded.' Draw nigh means come near. What a promise. Take one step towards God, and he comes to meet you. You do not have to clean yourself up first and then come. You come, and as you draw near, he cleanses and changes you. God is not far away, waiting for you to earn your way to him. He is near, waiting for you to come.
Verse 14. 'Whereas ye know not what shall be on the morrow. For what is your life? It is even a vapour, that appeareth for a little time, and then vanisheth away.' A vapour is a mist, like steam rising from a cup. None of us knows what lies ahead. Our lives are short. That is not meant to frighten us. It is meant to wake us up, so that we do not waste the time God gives, and so that we hold every plan humbly, saying, if the Lord will.
Solomon wrote, 'Boast not thyself of to morrow; for thou knowest not what a day may bring forth.' And Jesus told of a son who came home, and 'when he was yet a great way off, his father saw him, and had compassion, and ran'.
Is there something you have been worrying about, but have not yet asked God about? Ask him today. And is there a good thing you know you should do, but have been putting off? Take one step towards God, and towards that good thing, today.
Here is today’s question. James promises, draw nigh to God, and he will draw nigh to you. What is one way you can come closer to God this week?
Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that you give more grace, and that you draw near to all who come to you. Forgive my pride, and my selfish desires. I humble myself before you. Help me to resist the devil, to hold my plans in an open hand, and to say in everything, if the Lord will. Amen.
