An old man sits down to write. Long ago, as a young fisherman, he left his nets to follow a teacher from Nazareth. He walked with him for three years. He leaned on his shoulder at supper. He stood at the foot of his cross, and ran to his empty tomb. Now he writes it all down, so that people he will never meet might believe.
Welcome to the Gospel of John, on The Living Voice. It has twenty-one chapters, so on this walk it is twenty-one steps, one chapter at a time.
Who wrote it? The book calls its writer the disciple whom Jesus loved. From the earliest days, Christians have understood him to be John, the son of Zebedee, one of the twelve apostles, and the brother of James. He was one of the three closest to Jesus, with Peter and James.
John is different from the other three Gospels. Matthew, Mark and Luke tell many of the same stories, in a similar order. John chose different ones. Most of his book takes place in and around Jerusalem. He gives us long conversations, with Nicodemus, with a Samaritan woman at a well, and with the disciples at the last supper before the cross. Nearly half of the book is about the last week of Jesus’ life.
And John tells us exactly why he wrote it. Near the end, he says, 'these are written, that ye might believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God; and that believing ye might have life through his name.' The whole book has one goal: that you might believe, and have life.
Here are four things to watch for as we walk.
Watch for the signs. John calls the miracles of Jesus signs, because each one points to who he is. Water turned to wine. A royal official’s son healed. A man who could not walk for thirty-eight years made whole. Five thousand fed. Jesus walking on the sea. A man born blind given sight. And Lazarus raised from the dead.
Watch for the I AM sayings. When God spoke to Moses from the burning bush, he gave his name as I AM. In John, Jesus says again and again, I am. I am the bread of life. I am the light of the world. I am the door. I am the good shepherd. I am the resurrection, and the life. I am the way, the truth, and the life. I am the true vine.
Watch for the word believe. John uses it nearly a hundred times. Some people believe, some refuse, and some are still deciding.
And watch for light and darkness, life and death, love and truth. John begins his Gospel with the words, in the beginning, just as Genesis does. The same God who spoke light into the darkness has come into the world in Jesus, and 'the light shineth in darkness; and the darkness comprehended it not.'
Some people call John the simplest book in the Bible and the deepest. A child can understand it, and a lifetime is not long enough to explore it.
Here is a question to carry with you. John wrote his Gospel so that people might believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God. As we walk through this book, what question about Jesus would you most like to see answered?
Let us pray. Lord Jesus, as I walk through the Gospel of John, show me who you are. Open my eyes to see your glory, full of grace and truth. Help me to believe in you more deeply, and to find life in your name. Amen.
