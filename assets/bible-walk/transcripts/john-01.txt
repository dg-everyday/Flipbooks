Before there was a world, before there was time, there was someone. John does not begin his Gospel with a baby in a manger, or a voice in the wilderness. He begins before the beginning. And then he says something that has amazed people ever since: the One who was there before everything came and lived among us.
In the introduction, we heard that John wrote his Gospel so that we might believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God, and have life through his name. We heard that John was the disciple whom Jesus loved, and that he tells us of signs and of I AM sayings.
Now, in chapter one, John opens with words that echo the first page of the Bible. Then he brings us down to earth, to the river Jordan, where a prophet named John the Baptist points to Jesus. And we meet the first men who follow him.
John begins with the Word.
The Word was in the beginning with God. All things were made by him, and without him was not any thing made that was made. 'In him was life; and the life was the light of men.' And the light shines in darkness, and the darkness comprehended it not. The darkness could not understand it, or overcome it.
There was a man sent from God, whose name was John. This is John the Baptist, not the writer of this Gospel. He came as a witness, to bear witness of the Light, that all men through him might believe. He was not that Light, but was sent to bear witness of that Light. That was the true Light, which lighteth every man that cometh into the world.
He was in the world, and the world was made by him, and the world knew him not. 'He came unto his own, and his own received him not.' But not everyone turned him away.
Then John writes the greatest sentence of all.
John the Baptist bore witness of him, and cried, this was he of whom I spake, he that cometh after me is preferred before me, for he was before me. And of his fulness have all we received, and grace for grace. For the law was given by Moses, 'but grace and truth came by Jesus Christ.' No man hath seen God at any time. The only begotten Son, which is in the bosom of the Father, he hath declared him. Jesus has made God known.
Then the scene changes to the river Jordan. The leaders in Jerusalem send priests and Levites to ask John the Baptist, who art thou? He says plainly, I am not the Christ. Are you Elijah? I am not. Are you that prophet? No. Then who are you? He says, 'I am the voice of one crying in the wilderness, Make straight the way of the Lord'. John says that he is not worthy even to untie the shoes of the one coming after him.
The next day, John sees Jesus coming to him, and says, 'Behold the Lamb of God, which taketh away the sin of the world.' John bears witness that he saw the Spirit descending from heaven like a dove, and it abode upon him. And I saw, and bare record that this is the Son of God.
The next day, John stands with two of his disciples. He looks at Jesus as he walks, and says, behold the Lamb of God. The two disciples hear him, and they follow Jesus. Jesus turns, sees them following, and asks, what seek ye? They say, Rabbi, where dwellest thou? He says, come and see. They come, and see where he lives, and stay with him that day.
One of the two is Andrew, Simon Peter’s brother. The first thing Andrew does is find his own brother Simon, and tell him, we have found the Messias, the Christ. And he brings him to Jesus. Jesus looks at him and says, thou art Simon the son of Jona: thou shalt be called Cephas, which means a stone. In Greek, that name is Peter.
The next day Jesus goes to Galilee, and finds Philip, and says to him, follow me. Philip finds Nathanael, and says, we have found him, of whom Moses in the law, and the prophets, did write, Jesus of Nazareth. Nathanael says, can there any good thing come out of Nazareth? Philip says, come and see.
Jesus says that he saw Nathanael under the fig tree before Philip called him. Nathanael answers, 'Rabbi, thou art the Son of God; thou art the King of Israel.' And Jesus tells him, thou shalt see greater things than these. You shall see heaven open, and the angels of God ascending and descending upon the Son of man. Jesus is Jacob’s ladder, the way between heaven and earth.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 1. 'In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God.' Jesus is called the Word, because a word is how we make ourselves known. God spoke, and the world was made. Now God has spoken his final and clearest Word, in a person. Notice three things. The Word already was, in the beginning, so he was never created. The Word was with God, so he is distinct from the Father. And the Word was God. Jesus is not just a good teacher. He is God himself.
Verse 12. 'But as many as received him, to them gave he power to become the sons of God, even to them that believe on his name:' Many did not receive him. But anyone who does receive him, by believing in his name, is given the right to become a child of God. It is not about our family background, or our efforts. The next verse says that such people are born, not of blood, nor of the will of man, but of God. To receive Jesus is to be welcomed into God’s own family.
Verse 14. 'And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us, (and we beheld his glory, the glory as of the only begotten of the Father,) full of grace and truth.' The eternal Word became a real human being, with a body that could be tired, hungry and hurt. The word dwelt means pitched his tent. Just as God once lived among Israel in the tabernacle in the wilderness, now he lived among us in Jesus. And John says, we saw his glory. Full of grace, and full of truth.
Genesis begins, 'In the beginning God created the heaven and the earth.' John begins, 'In the beginning was the Word'. Paul wrote of Jesus, 'For by him were all things created'. And the letter to the Hebrews says that God, who spoke in times past by the prophets, 'Hath in these last days spoken unto us by his Son'.
Jesus asked the first disciples, what seek ye? And he said, come and see. What are you seeking? Come to him, and see. And like Andrew and Philip, think of one person you could invite to come and see Jesus.
Here is today’s question. John says that to as many as received him, Jesus gave power to become the sons of God. What does it mean for you to receive Jesus?
Let us pray. Lord Jesus, you are the Word who was with God and was God, and you became flesh and dwelt among us. Thank you for coming to make God known. I receive you, and believe on your name. Help me to see your glory, full of grace and truth, and to bring others to come and see. Amen.
