In the heat of the day, a woman walks alone to the town well, carrying her water jar. The other women come when it is cool, together. She comes when no one else is there. But today someone is sitting by the well: a tired Jewish traveller. And he asks her for a drink.
In chapter three, Nicodemus came to Jesus in secret, and Jesus told him, ye must be born again. Then Jesus spoke the most famous words in the Bible: for God so loved the world, that he gave his only begotten Son. And John the Baptist said of Jesus, he must increase, but I must decrease.
Now, in chapter four, Jesus has a very different conversation. Nicodemus was a respected Jewish man, a teacher. This is a Samaritan woman, an outsider with a troubled past. Jews and Samaritans did not mix. Men did not speak with women in public like this. But Jesus speaks to her, and her whole town hears about him.
Jesus leaves Judea, and goes again into Galilee. And he must needs go through Samaria. Many Jews went the long way around, to avoid Samaria. But Jesus goes straight through.
He comes to a city of Samaria called Sychar, near the field that Jacob gave to his son Joseph. Jacob’s well is there. Jesus, being wearied with his journey, sits on the well. It is about the sixth hour, around the middle of the day.
A woman of Samaria comes to draw water. Jesus says to her, give me to drink. His disciples have gone into the city to buy food. The woman says, how is it that you, being a Jew, ask a drink of me, a woman of Samaria? For the Jews have no dealings with the Samaritans.
Jesus answers, if thou knewest the gift of God, and who it is that saith to thee, give me to drink, thou wouldest have asked of him, and he would have given thee living water.
She says, sir, you have nothing to draw with, and the well is deep. Where do you get this living water? Are you greater than our father Jacob, who gave us this well? Jesus answers, whosoever drinketh of this water shall thirst again.
The woman says, sir, give me this water. She is still thinking of ordinary water.
Then Jesus says, go, call thy husband, and come hither. She says, I have no husband. Jesus says, you have well said, I have no husband. For you have had five husbands, and the man you now have is not your husband. Jesus knows her whole story. But he does not condemn her. He gently brings the truth into the light.
She says, sir, I perceive that thou art a prophet. And she changes the subject to a religious argument. Our fathers worshipped in this mountain, and you Jews say that Jerusalem is the place where men ought to worship.
Jesus says, woman, believe me, the hour cometh, when you shall neither in this mountain, nor yet at Jerusalem, worship the Father. The hour cometh, and now is, when the true worshippers shall worship the Father in spirit and in truth, for the Father seeketh such to worship him.
The woman says, I know that Messias cometh, which is called Christ. When he is come, he will tell us all things. And Jesus says to her, 'I that speak unto thee am he.' This is the first time in John’s Gospel that Jesus tells anyone plainly that he is the Christ. And he tells it to a Samaritan woman at a well.
The disciples return, and marvel that he talks with the woman. The woman leaves her waterpot, goes into the city, and says to the men, 'Come, see a man, which told me all things that ever I did: is not this the Christ?'
The disciples urge Jesus to eat. He says, my meat is to do the will of him that sent me. Lift up your eyes, and look on the fields, for they are white already to harvest. Across the fields, the people of Sychar are coming out to meet him.
Many of the Samaritans of that city believe on him because of the woman’s words. They ask him to stay with them, and he stays two days. And many more believe because of his own word.
Then Jesus goes into Galilee, to Cana, where he made the water wine. A nobleman, a royal official, whose son is sick at Capernaum, comes and begs Jesus to come down and heal his son, for he is at the point of death. Jesus says, go thy way; thy son liveth. The man believes the word Jesus spoke, and goes his way. On the road, his servants meet him and say, thy son liveth. He asks the hour when the boy began to recover. And it was at the same hour that Jesus said, thy son liveth. He believes, and his whole house. This is the second sign Jesus does in Galilee.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 14. 'But whosoever drinketh of the water that I shall give him shall never thirst; but the water that I shall give him shall be in him a well of water springing up into everlasting life.' The woman had come to the well every day of her life, and every day she was thirsty again. Perhaps she had looked for satisfaction in relationships too, and was still thirsty. Jesus offers something different: not a bucket of water, but a spring inside, that never runs dry. Only Jesus can satisfy the deepest thirst of the human heart.
Verse 24. 'God is a Spirit: and they that worship him must worship him in spirit and in truth.' The woman wanted to argue about the right place to worship. Jesus said the place is not what matters most. What matters is the heart. In spirit means from the heart, not just with outward forms. In truth means according to who God really is. God is not looking for the right building. He is looking for true worshippers, anywhere in the world.
Verse 42. 'And said unto the woman, Now we believe, not because of thy saying: for we have heard him ourselves, and know that this is indeed the Christ, the Saviour of the world.' The woman’s testimony brought her neighbours to Jesus. But then they heard him for themselves. That is how faith grows. Someone tells us about Jesus, and then we meet him ourselves. And the Samaritans understood something many others missed. Jesus is not only the Saviour of one nation. He is the Saviour of the world.
The prophet Isaiah called out, 'Ho, every one that thirsteth, come ye to the waters'. And the last chapter of the Bible ends with an invitation: 'And whosoever will, let him take the water of life freely.'
Is there a thirst in your heart that nothing seems to satisfy? Bring it to Jesus. And remember the woman at the well. She had a messy past, and she became the first evangelist to her town. Who could you tell, come, see?
Here is today’s question. Jesus offered the woman living water, so that she would never thirst again. What are you thirsty for, and how could Jesus satisfy that thirst?
Let us pray. Lord Jesus, you know everything I have ever done, and still you offer me living water. Satisfy the deepest thirst of my heart. Teach me to worship the Father in spirit and in truth. And give me courage, like the woman at the well, to tell others, come, see. Amen.
