Around a pool in Jerusalem lie crowds of sick people: the blind, the lame, the withered. They wait for the water to move, hoping for healing. One man has been lying there for thirty-eight years. Longer than many people live. Then a stranger walks up to him, looks at him, and asks a strange question. Do you want to be made well?
In chapter four, Jesus met a Samaritan woman at a well, and offered her living water. She told her town, come, see a man, which told me all things that ever I did. And many believed, saying, this is indeed the Christ, the Saviour of the world. Then Jesus healed a nobleman’s son with a word.
Now, in chapter five, Jesus does his third sign, a healing at a pool in Jerusalem. But the healing leads to a fierce argument with the religious leaders. And Jesus makes some of the boldest claims about himself in the whole Gospel.
There is a feast of the Jews, and Jesus goes up to Jerusalem. By the sheep gate there is a pool called Bethesda, with five porches, or covered walkways. In these lie a great crowd of sick people, blind, lame and withered, waiting for the moving of the water. People believed that when the water was stirred, the first to step in would be healed.
A certain man is there, who has had an infirmity thirty and eight years.
The man answers, sir, I have no man, when the water is troubled, to put me into the pool. But while I am coming, another steps down before me. He does not answer the question. He gives an excuse, and a sad one. He has no one to help him.
Jesus says to him, 'Rise, take up thy bed, and walk.' And immediately the man is made whole, and takes up his bed, and walks.
But that day is the sabbath. The religious leaders say to the man who was healed, it is the sabbath day. It is not lawful for you to carry your bed. A man unable to walk for thirty-eight years is walking, and all they can see is that he is carrying his mat on the sabbath.
He answers, he that made me whole, the same said unto me, take up thy bed, and walk. They ask, who is the man? But the healed man does not know, for Jesus has slipped away into the crowd.
Afterward, Jesus finds him in the temple, and says to him, behold, thou art made whole. Sin no more, lest a worse thing come unto thee. The man goes and tells the leaders that it was Jesus who had made him whole.
And the leaders begin to persecute Jesus, because he has done these things on the sabbath. Jesus answers them, 'My Father worketh hitherto, and I work.' God does not stop doing good on the sabbath, and neither does his Son. And this makes them even more determined to put him to death, because he not only broke the sabbath, as they saw it, but also said that God was his Father, making himself equal with God.
Then Jesus speaks at length. The Son can do nothing of himself, but what he sees the Father do. Whatever the Father does, the Son does likewise. For the Father loves the Son, and shows him all things. As the Father raises up the dead, and gives them life, even so the Son gives life to whom he will. And the Father has committed all judgment unto the Son, so that all men should honour the Son, even as they honour the Father.
Then Jesus makes a wonderful promise.
The hour is coming, Jesus says, and now is, when the dead shall hear the voice of the Son of God, and they that hear shall live. And the hour is coming, when all that are in the graves shall hear his voice, and come forth.
Jesus then points to the witnesses who speak for him. John the Baptist was a burning and a shining light, and he bore witness to the truth. But the works Jesus does are a greater witness, for they show that the Father has sent him. The Father himself has borne witness of him.
Then Jesus points them to the scriptures they study so carefully.
And ye will not come to me, Jesus says, that ye might have life. They knew the Bible, but they did not know the One it was about. If you had believed Moses, Jesus says, you would have believed me, for he wrote of me.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 6. 'When Jesus saw him lie, and knew that he had been now a long time in that case, he saith unto him, Wilt thou be made whole?' It seems an odd question. Of course he wanted to be well. But after thirty-eight years, perhaps he had given up hope. Perhaps he had grown used to his condition. Jesus’ question was an invitation to hope again. Jesus still asks us, do you want to be made whole? Sometimes we cling to our brokenness, or to our excuses. Jesus offers more.
Verse 24. 'Verily, verily, I say unto you, He that heareth my word, and believeth on him that sent me, hath everlasting life, and shall not come into condemnation; but is passed from death unto life.' Notice the tense. Not will have, but hath everlasting life. Not will pass, but is passed from death unto life. Eternal life does not begin when we die. It begins the moment we hear Jesus’ word and believe. If you believe in him, you are already safe. There is no condemnation waiting for you.
Verse 39. 'Search the scriptures; for in them ye think ye have eternal life: and they are they which testify of me.' These leaders read the scriptures carefully every day. But they missed the point. The whole Bible points to Jesus. It is possible to know a great deal about the Bible, and still not come to the One it is about. As we walk through Scripture, the goal is not just knowledge. It is to come to Jesus, and have life.
On the road to Emmaus, the risen Jesus began 'at Moses and all the prophets' and 'expounded unto them in all the scriptures the things concerning himself.' And Paul wrote, 'There is therefore now no condemnation to them which are in Christ Jesus'.
Is there an area of your life where you have grown used to being stuck, and have stopped hoping? Hear Jesus’ question: wilt thou be made whole? And when you read the Bible, ask, what does this show me about Jesus?
Here is today’s question. Jesus asked the man, wilt thou be made whole? If Jesus asked you that question today, what would you say?
Let us pray. Lord Jesus, you saw the man who had waited thirty-eight years, and you made him whole. Thank you that whoever hears your word and believes has everlasting life, and is passed from death unto life. Help me to hope in you again, and to find you on every page of the scriptures. Amen.
