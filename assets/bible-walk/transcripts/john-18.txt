Torches flicker between the olive trees. Soldiers and temple guards are coming into the garden, with lanterns and weapons, led by one of Jesus’ own disciples. Jesus steps forward to meet them. Whom do you seek? Jesus of Nazareth, they answer. And Jesus says, I am he. At those words, the whole armed crowd goes backward, and falls to the ground.
In chapter seventeen, Jesus lifted up his eyes to heaven and prayed. He prayed for himself, for his disciples, and for all who would believe on him through their word, that they all may be one. He said, this is life eternal, that they might know thee the only true God.
Now, in chapter eighteen, the hour has come. Jesus is arrested, questioned and put on trial. But as you listen, notice who is really in control. Jesus is the one who steps forward, who speaks the truth, and who chooses to drink the cup the Father has given him.
When Jesus has spoken these words, he goes out with his disciples over the brook Cedron, where there is a garden, which he and his disciples enter. Judas, who betrayed him, also knows the place, for Jesus often went there with his disciples.
Judas then, having received a band of men and officers from the chief priests and Pharisees, comes there with lanterns and torches and weapons. Jesus, knowing all things that should come upon him, goes forth, and says to them, whom seek ye? They answer him, Jesus of Nazareth. Jesus says, I am he.
As soon as he says, I am he, they go backward, and fall to the ground. In the Greek, Jesus simply says, I am. It is the name of God.
He asks them again, whom seek ye? And they say, Jesus of Nazareth. Jesus answers, I have told you that I am he. If therefore ye seek me, let these go their way. Even as he is arrested, Jesus protects his friends.
Then Simon Peter, having a sword, draws it, and strikes the high priest’s servant, and cuts off his right ear. The servant’s name is Malchus.
Then the band of soldiers and the officers take Jesus, and bind him, and lead him away to Annas first, the father-in-law of Caiaphas, the high priest that year. It was Caiaphas who had said that it was expedient that one man should die for the people.
Simon Peter follows Jesus, and so does another disciple, who is known to the high priest. That disciple speaks to the girl who keeps the door, and brings Peter in. The girl says to Peter, art not thou also one of this man’s disciples? Peter says, I am not. The servants and officers have made a fire of coals, for it is cold, and they warm themselves. And Peter stands with them, and warms himself.
The high priest asks Jesus about his disciples and his teaching. Jesus answers, I spake openly to the world. I ever taught in the synagogue, and in the temple. In secret have I said nothing. Why askest thou me? Ask them which heard me. One of the officers strikes Jesus with the palm of his hand, saying, answerest thou the high priest so? Jesus answers, if I have spoken evil, bear witness of the evil. But if well, why smitest thou me?
Annas sends him bound to Caiaphas. Meanwhile, Simon Peter stands and warms himself. They say to him, art not thou also one of his disciples? He denies it, and says, I am not. One of the servants of the high priest, a relative of the man whose ear Peter cut off, says, did not I see thee in the garden with him? Peter denies again. And immediately the cock crows.
Then they lead Jesus from Caiaphas to the hall of judgment, the Roman governor’s palace. It is early. They themselves do not go in, lest they become unclean, and so unable to eat the passover. They are careful about ritual cleanness while plotting to put an innocent man to death.
Pilate goes out to them, and asks, what accusation bring ye against this man? They answer, if he were not a malefactor, an evildoer, we would not have delivered him up unto thee.
Pilate goes back into the hall, calls Jesus, and asks him, art thou the King of the Jews?
Pilate says, art thou a king then?
Pilate says to him, 'What is truth?' And with that question, he goes out again to the people, and says, I find in him no fault at all. But you have a custom, that I should release unto you one prisoner at the passover. Will ye therefore that I release unto you the King of the Jews? Then they all cry again, not this man, but Barabbas. Now Barabbas was a robber.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 11. 'Then said Jesus unto Peter, Put up thy sword into the sheath: the cup which my Father hath given me, shall I not drink it?' Peter wanted to fight. But Jesus did not need Peter’s sword. He could have called on far greater power. He chose instead to drink the cup, to accept the suffering the Father had given him, for our sake. Jesus was not captured. He gave himself up.
Verse 36. 'Jesus answered, My kingdom is not of this world: if my kingdom were of this world, then would my servants fight, that I should not be delivered to the Jews: but now is my kingdom not from hence.' Jesus is a King. But his kingdom does not come by armies and swords. It does not depend on earthly power. It comes from heaven, and it grows in human hearts. That is why Peter’s sword was the wrong weapon. Jesus’ kingdom advances through truth, love and sacrifice.
Verse 37. 'Pilate therefore said unto him, Art thou a king then? Jesus answered, Thou sayest that I am a king. To this end was I born, and for this cause came I into the world, that I should bear witness unto the truth. Every one that is of the truth heareth my voice.' Pilate asked, what is truth? But the Truth was standing right in front of him. Jesus came into the world to bear witness to the truth. And everyone who loves the truth listens to his voice. Pilate heard Jesus, but did not listen. The question for each of us is the same: will we hear his voice?
The prophet Isaiah wrote, 'He was oppressed, and he was afflicted, yet he opened not his mouth: he is brought as a lamb'. And Peter, who denied Jesus, later wrote that Jesus, 'when he suffered, he threatened not; but committed himself to him that judgeth righteously'.
Are there times when, like Peter, you have been afraid to say that you belong to Jesus? He knows, and he still loves you. Ask him for courage. And remember that his kingdom is not of this world. Trust him, not in the power of this world.
Here is today’s question. Pilate asked, what is truth? Jesus said, every one that is of the truth heareth my voice. How do you listen for the voice of Jesus?
Let us pray. Lord Jesus, you gave yourself up willingly, and drank the cup the Father gave you, for me. Thank you. Forgive me for the times I have been afraid to say I belong to you. You are the King, and your kingdom is not of this world. Help me to hear your voice and to follow the truth. Amen.
