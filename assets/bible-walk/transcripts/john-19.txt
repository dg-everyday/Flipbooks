A man stands before a crowd, beaten and bleeding. Soldiers have pressed a crown of thorns onto his head, and thrown a purple robe over his shoulders to mock him as a king. The Roman governor brings him out, and points to him. Behold the man, he says. Perhaps he hopes the crowd will feel pity. Instead, they shout, crucify him.
In chapter eighteen, Jesus was arrested in the garden. He said, I am he, and the soldiers fell back. Peter denied him three times. Before Pilate, Jesus said, my kingdom is not of this world, and to this end was I born, that I should bear witness unto the truth. Pilate asked, what is truth? and found no fault in him. But the crowd chose Barabbas.
Now, in chapter nineteen, we come to the cross. This is the centre of John’s Gospel, and of the whole Bible. It is a hard chapter to hear. But listen for how John shows that Jesus is in control, that every detail fulfils the scriptures, and that his last word is not a cry of defeat, but of victory.
Pilate takes Jesus, and has him scourged, beaten with a whip. The soldiers plait a crown of thorns, put it on his head, put on him a purple robe, and say, hail, King of the Jews. And they strike him with their hands.
Pilate goes out again, and says to the crowd, behold, I bring him forth to you, that ye may know that I find no fault in him.
When the chief priests and officers see him, they cry out, crucify him, crucify him. Pilate says, take ye him, and crucify him, for I find no fault in him. The leaders answer, we have a law, and by our law he ought to die, because he made himself the Son of God.
Pilate is afraid. He says to Jesus, knowest thou not that I have power to crucify thee? Jesus answers, thou couldest have no power at all against me, except it were given thee from above.
Pilate seeks to release him. But the crowd cries, if thou let this man go, thou art not Caesar’s friend. Pilate says, behold your King! They cry, away with him, crucify him. The chief priests say, we have no king but Caesar. Then Pilate delivers him to be crucified.
Jesus goes forth, bearing his cross, to a place called the place of a skull, which in Hebrew is called Golgotha. There they crucify him, and two others with him, on either side one, and Jesus in the middle.
Pilate puts a title on the cross, in Hebrew, Greek and Latin: Jesus of Nazareth the King of the Jews. The chief priests object, but Pilate answers, what I have written I have written.
The soldiers take his garments, and divide them into four parts, one for each soldier. His coat is without seam, woven from the top throughout. So they say, let us not tear it, but cast lots for it. This fulfils the scripture, 'They parted my raiment among them, and for my vesture they did cast lots.'
Standing by the cross of Jesus are his mother, his mother’s sister, Mary the wife of Cleophas, and Mary Magdalene. When Jesus sees his mother, and the disciple whom he loves standing by, he says to his mother, woman, behold thy son! Even in his agony, Jesus cares for his mother.
After this, Jesus, knowing that all things are now accomplished, that the scripture might be fulfilled, says, I thirst. A vessel full of vinegar is there, and they fill a sponge with it, put it on hyssop, and put it to his mouth.
The leaders do not want the bodies left on the cross for the sabbath, so the soldiers come to hasten the deaths of the men crucified. But when they come to Jesus, and see that he is dead already, they do not break his legs. One of the soldiers pierces his side with a spear, 'and forthwith came there out blood and water.' John says, he that saw it bare record, and his record is true, that ye might believe. These things were done, that the scripture should be fulfilled, a bone of him shall not be broken. And they shall look on him whom they pierced.
After this, Joseph of Arimathea, a disciple of Jesus, but secretly for fear, asks Pilate if he may take away the body of Jesus. Pilate gives him leave. And Nicodemus comes too, the man who at the first came to Jesus in secret, bringing a large amount of myrrh and aloes. They take the body of Jesus, and wind it in linen clothes with the spices, as the Jewish manner is to bury. In the place where he was crucified there is a garden, and in the garden a new sepulchre, where no man was ever yet laid. There they lay Jesus.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 5. 'Then came Jesus forth, wearing the crown of thorns, and the purple robe. And Pilate saith unto them, Behold the man!' Pilate meant, look at this poor, beaten man. How could he be a threat to anyone? But his words say more than he knew. Behold the man. Here is what a truly human life looks like, perfectly obedient to God, giving itself in love for others. And here is the Son of God, wearing a crown of thorns, the curse of Genesis chapter three, on his head.
Verse 27. 'Then saith he to the disciple, Behold thy mother! And from that hour that disciple took her unto his own home.' While he was carrying the sins of the world, Jesus still cared about his mother’s needs. He gave her into the care of the disciple he loved. The cross is about the salvation of the whole world. But it is also about a Saviour who cares about each person. Even in his greatest suffering, Jesus was thinking of others.
Verse 30. 'When Jesus therefore had received the vinegar, he said, It is finished: and he bowed his head, and gave up the ghost.' It is finished. In the original language, it is one word, and it was sometimes written on receipts, meaning paid in full. Jesus did not say, I am finished. He said, it is finished. The work he came to do, to take away the sin of the world, was complete. Nothing more needs to be added. And notice, he bowed his head, and gave up his spirit. No one took his life. He laid it down.
Isaiah wrote, 'But he was wounded for our transgressions, he was bruised for our iniquities: the chastisement of our peace was upon him; and with his stripes we are healed.' And the letter to the Hebrews says that Jesus, 'after he had offered one sacrifice for sins for ever, sat down on the right hand of God'.
Take a quiet moment to thank Jesus for the cross. Whatever guilt or shame you carry, hear his words: it is finished. The price has been paid in full. You do not need to add anything. Just receive it.
Here is today’s question. Jesus said, it is finished. What does it mean to you that the work of your salvation has been paid in full?
Let us pray. Lord Jesus, thank you for wearing the crown of thorns, and for dying on the cross for me. Thank you that it is finished, that my sin is paid for in full. Help me to rest in what you have done, and to love others as you loved, even in your suffering. Amen.
