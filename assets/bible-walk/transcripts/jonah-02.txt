Imagine the darkest place you can. No light at all. Cold water. The sound of the sea. No way out, and no one who could ever find you. That is where Jonah is. And there, at the very bottom, he does the one thing he refused to do on the ship. He prays.
In chapter one, God told Jonah to go to Nineveh, and Jonah ran the other way, on a ship to Tarshish. The LORD sent a great storm. Jonah told the sailors to throw him into the sea, and when they did, the sea was calm, and they feared the LORD. And the LORD had prepared a great fish to swallow Jonah. He was in the fish 'three days and three nights.'
Now, in chapter two, we hear Jonah’s prayer. It is a short chapter, and it is almost all poetry. Jonah prays like the psalms, using words that God’s people had prayed for generations. Listen for how his prayer turns from going down to being brought up.
Then Jonah prays to the LORD his God out of the fish’s belly.
Notice that. The LORD his God. Jonah had been running from God. But now he calls him my God again.
He says, I cried because of my trouble to the LORD, and he heard me. From the depths of the grave I cried, and thou heardest my voice.
Jonah prays as if the answer has already come. He is still inside the fish. But he knows that God has heard him, and that the fish was God’s way of saving him from drowning.
Thou hadst cast me into the deep, he prays, into the heart of the seas. 'and the floods compassed me about: all thy billows and thy waves passed over me.'
Jonah does not blame the sailors. He knows that it was God who sent the storm. Thy billows. Thy waves. Even in the sea, Jonah knows he is in God’s hands.
Then Jonah tells God what he thought, as he sank.
He goes on describing what it was like. 'The waters compassed me about, even to the soul: the depth closed me round about, the weeds were wrapped about my head.' Seaweed tangled around his head. He was going down to the roots of the mountains, under the sea. It was as if the earth had locked its gates behind him for ever.
And then the prayer turns. 'yet hast thou brought up my life from corruption, O LORD my God.' Down, down, down, Jonah went, all through chapter one. But now, for the first time, the direction changes. God brought him up.
Then Jonah tells how it happened.
They that observe lying vanities forsake their own mercy, Jonah says. Lying vanities means empty idols, false gods that cannot help. The sailors had cried to their gods, and nothing happened. Those who cling to worthless things, Jonah says, turn away from the only one who could show them mercy.
But Jonah will not do that anymore.
And then the LORD speaks to the fish, and it brings Jonah up onto the dry land.
Think about how this chapter began and how it ends. Jonah ran from the presence of the LORD. He went down to Joppa, down into the ship, down into the sea, down to the roots of the mountains. And from the lowest place of all, he prayed, and God brought him up. There is no place so deep that God cannot reach it. No one has run so far that God cannot bring them back.
Jonah is not a perfect man yet. We will see that in the chapters ahead. But he has learned something that he will never forget. Salvation, rescue, deliverance, comes from God alone. Jonah could not save himself. He could only cry out, and trust.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 4. 'Then I said, I am cast out of thy sight; yet I will look again toward thy holy temple.' As Jonah sank, he thought, this is the end. I am cast out of God’s sight. And then he says, yet. Yet I will look again toward thy holy temple. The temple in Jerusalem was the place where God promised to hear the prayers of his people, even from far away. Jonah could not go there. But he could turn his heart toward God. That little word, yet, is where hope begins.
Verse 7. 'When my soul fainted within me I remembered the LORD: and my prayer came in unto thee, into thine holy temple.' When Jonah reached the end of his own strength, he remembered the LORD. Sometimes we have to come to the very end of ourselves before we turn to God. But notice, his prayer came in unto thee. From the belly of a fish at the bottom of the sea, Jonah’s prayer reached the throne of heaven. No prayer is too far away for God to hear.
Verse 9. 'But I will sacrifice unto thee with the voice of thanksgiving; I will pay that that I have vowed. Salvation is of the LORD.' Jonah is still inside the fish when he says this. He has not been rescued yet. But he gives thanks anyway. And then he sums up the whole book in four words. Salvation is of the LORD. Not of Jonah, not of the sailors, not of any idol. Rescue belongs to God, and he gives it to whom he will. Jonah is about to discover just how far God’s salvation reaches.
A psalm says, 'Out of the depths have I cried unto thee, O LORD. Lord, hear my voice'. And Paul wrote, 'For whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved.'
Do you feel as if you have sunk to a low place, maybe because of your own choices? Remember Jonah’s word: yet. Turn your heart toward God, wherever you are. He hears prayers from the deepest places. Say it today: salvation is of the LORD.
Here is today’s question. Jonah remembered the LORD when his soul fainted within him. What has helped you turn back to God when you were at your lowest?
Let us pray. O LORD my God, thank you that no place is too deep for you to reach me. When I feel cast out of your sight, help me to look to you again. Thank you for hearing my prayer. I cannot save myself. Salvation is of the LORD. Amen.
