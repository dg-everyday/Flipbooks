Imagine stepping into the shoes of the greatest leader your people have ever known. He spoke with God face to face. He parted the sea. He brought down the law from the mountain. And now he is gone, and everyone is looking at you. That is where Joshua is standing at the start of this book. And the first words he hears are from God himself.
In the introduction, we heard that Joshua comes straight after the books of Moses, and that it tells how Israel crossed the Jordan and settled in the land. We heard that Joshua’s name means the LORD is salvation, the same name as Jesus.
Now, in chapter one, God speaks to Joshua. Listen for how many times he says, be strong. And listen for the promise that stands behind every command.
The chapter opens with a death. 'Now after the death of Moses the servant of the LORD it came to pass, that the LORD spake unto Joshua the son of Nun, Moses’ minister, saying,'
Minister here means helper, or servant. For many years, Joshua had served Moses. Now Moses is dead, and God speaks directly to Joshua.
God does not soften the news. 'Moses my servant is dead; now therefore arise, go over this Jordan, thou, and all this people, unto the land which I do give to them, even to the children of Israel.'
Moses is dead. Now therefore arise. The death of a great leader does not stop God’s plan. The work goes on.
Then God makes a promise. 'Every place that the sole of your foot shall tread upon, that have I given unto you, as I said unto Moses.' God describes the land, from the wilderness in the south to Lebanon in the north, and from the great river Euphrates to the great sea in the west.
Notice how God puts it. The land is already given. But Israel still has to walk into it. God’s gifts are real, but we have to step out in faith to receive them.
And then God speaks to Joshua about Joshua himself.
Then God says it a second time, and adds something. 'Only be thou strong and very courageous, that thou mayest observe to do according to all the law, which Moses my servant commanded thee: turn not from it to the right hand or to the left'.
Here is a surprise. The courage God asks for is not mainly about battles. It is about obedience. It takes courage to keep God’s word when others turn aside. It takes courage not to drift a little to the right, or a little to the left.
And God tells Joshua how to stay on that path.
Then God says it a third time.
Three times in one short speech, God tells Joshua to be strong and of a good courage. Perhaps Joshua needed to hear it three times. Perhaps we do too.
Then Joshua gets to work. He gives orders to the officers of the people. Pass through the camp, he says, and tell the people to prepare food for the journey. 'for within three days ye shall pass over this Jordan, to go in to possess the land, which the LORD your God giveth you to possess it.'
Next, Joshua speaks to two and a half of the tribes, Reuben, Gad and half the tribe of Manasseh. These tribes had already been given land on the east side of the Jordan, in the time of Moses. They could have settled down and stayed at home. But Moses had made them promise to help their brothers. So Joshua reminds them. Your wives, your little ones and your cattle can stay here. But your fighting men must cross over with your brothers, and help them, until the LORD has given them rest as well.
How will they answer? These tribes have everything they need. Will they cross a dangerous river to help others?
Their answer is wonderful. 'All that thou commandest us we will do, and whithersoever thou sendest us, we will go.' And they add, as we listened to Moses, so we will listen to you. 'only the LORD thy God be with thee, as he was with Moses.'
And they end with the very words God spoke to Joshua. 'only be strong and of a good courage.' God has told Joshua to be strong. Now the people say it too. God often sends his encouragement to us through the people around us.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 5. 'There shall not any man be able to stand before thee all the days of thy life: as I was with Moses, so I will be with thee: I will not fail thee, nor forsake thee.' Joshua had watched God be with Moses, at the sea, at the mountain, in the wilderness. Now God says, the same God, the same presence, the same faithfulness, will be with you. I will not fail thee, nor forsake thee. Those words are repeated in the New Testament, for every believer. God does not leave his people on their own.
Verse 8. 'This book of the law shall not depart out of thy mouth; but thou shalt meditate therein day and night, that thou mayest observe to do according to all that is written therein: for then thou shalt make thy way prosperous, and then thou shalt have good success.' Joshua was about to lead an army. And God tells him that his most important weapon is a book. To meditate means to think over God’s word, again and again, to speak it to yourself. Success in God’s eyes does not come from cleverness or strength. It comes from knowing his word and doing it.
Verse 9. 'Have not I commanded thee? Be strong and of a good courage; be not afraid, neither be thou dismayed: for the LORD thy God is with thee whithersoever thou goest.' Notice the reason for the courage. Not, you are strong. Not, you are clever. But, the LORD thy God is with thee. Joshua’s courage did not depend on Joshua. It depended on God. And God says, whithersoever thou goest. Wherever you go. There is no place Joshua could go where God would not be with him.
The letter to the Hebrews takes God’s words to Joshua and gives them to every believer: 'for he hath said, I will never leave thee, nor forsake thee.' And Jesus said to his followers, 'lo, I am with you alway, even unto the end of the world.'
Is there a step ahead of you that feels too big? Take God’s words to Joshua with you today. Say them to yourself, and say them to someone else who needs them. Be strong and of a good courage, for the LORD thy God is with thee.
Here is today’s question. God told Joshua that his courage would come from God’s presence, and from God’s word. Which of those two do you most need to hold on to today, and why?
Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that you were with Moses, and with Joshua, and that you are with me. You will not fail me, nor forsake me. Help me to keep your word in my mouth and in my heart. Make me strong and of a good courage, wherever I go. Amen.
