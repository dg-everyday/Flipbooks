Inside a tent, under the floor, something is buried. A beautiful robe from Babylon. A pile of silver. A heavy bar of gold. No one knows it is there except the man who hid it, and perhaps his family. He thinks his secret is safe. But that hidden treasure is about to bring defeat on a whole nation.
In chapter six, Israel marched round Jericho for seven days, and on the seventh day the people shouted, and the wall fell down flat. Rahab and her family were saved, because of the scarlet line. Everything in the city was devoted to the LORD, and no one was to take anything for themselves. And the LORD was with Joshua, and his fame spread.
Now, in chapter seven, after a great victory comes a terrible defeat. Listen for how one hidden sin affects everyone. And listen for Joshua’s gentle words to the man who did it.
The chapter begins by telling us what Joshua does not yet know. The children of Israel have broken faith over the things devoted to God. A man named Achan, from the tribe of Judah, has taken some of them for himself. And the anger of the LORD burns against Israel.
Meanwhile, Joshua sends men from Jericho to spy out a town called Ai. They come back confident. Do not send the whole army, they say. Two or three thousand men will be enough. The people there are few.
So about three thousand men go up against Ai. And they run away from the men of Ai. They are chased from the gate and down the slope, and about thirty six of Israel’s men lose their lives. And the hearts of the people melt, and become like water.
Notice that no one asked God before they went. After Jericho, they were confident in themselves.
Joshua tears his clothes, and falls on his face to the ground before the ark of the LORD, and stays there for hours, he and the elders of Israel, with dust on their heads. Joshua cries out to God. Alas, O Lord GOD, why did you bring us over the Jordan, only to let our enemies destroy us? If only we had stayed on the other side. 'O Lord, what shall I say, when Israel turneth their backs before their enemies!' Now all the people of the land will hear, and surround us. 'and what wilt thou do unto thy great name?'
It is an honest prayer. Joshua does not understand what has happened. But he brings his confusion straight to God.
And the LORD answers. 'Get thee up; wherefore liest thou thus upon thy face?' Get up, Joshua. This is not the time for lying on the ground. There is something you need to know.
God tells Joshua that the people cannot stand before their enemies until the stolen things are removed. Get the people ready, God says. Then they will come before me tribe by tribe, and I will show who has done this.
So Joshua rises early, and brings Israel forward tribe by tribe. The tribe of Judah is taken. Then he brings the families of Judah, and the family of the Zarhites is taken. Then household by household, and the household of Zabdi is taken. Then man by man. And Achan, the son of Carmi, is taken.
Imagine Achan, watching as the circle grows smaller and smaller. Tribe, family, household. He had every chance to come forward and confess. But he waits until there is nowhere left to hide.
Then Joshua speaks to him, not with anger, but with gentleness.
And Achan finally tells the truth. 'Indeed I have sinned against the LORD God of Israel, and thus and thus have I done:'
Joshua sends messengers, who run to Achan’s tent, and there it is, hidden in the ground, with the silver underneath. They bring it all out, and lay it before the LORD.
Then the judgment comes, as God had said. Achan, and all that he had, are taken to a valley, and there Achan is put to death. A great heap of stones is raised over the place, which stands there for many years. And the LORD turns from his fierce anger. The place is called the valley of Achor, which means trouble.
This is a hard ending to hear. It shows us how serious sin is in God’s eyes, especially among his own people. Achan’s sin was not just a private matter. It hurt his whole nation, and men lost their lives at Ai because of it.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 11. 'Israel hath sinned, and they have also transgressed my covenant which I commanded them: for they have even taken of the accursed thing, and have also stolen, and dissembled also, and they have put it even among their own stuff.' Only one man took the things. But God says, Israel hath sinned. God’s people belong together, and the sin of one affects them all. Notice the steps of the sin: taken, stolen, dissembled, which means lied and pretended, and then hidden. Sin rarely stays small. It grows, one step at a time.
Verse 19. 'And Joshua said unto Achan, My son, give, I pray thee, glory to the LORD God of Israel, and make confession unto him; and tell me now what thou hast done; hide it not from me.' My son. Even now, Joshua speaks to Achan with tenderness. And he tells him that confessing his sin will give glory to God. When we admit what we have done, we are agreeing with God, and saying that he is right. Hide it not. That is the way back, always. Not hiding, but telling the truth.
Verse 21. 'When I saw among the spoils a goodly Babylonish garment, and two hundred shekels of silver, and a wedge of gold of fifty shekels weight, then I coveted them, and took them; and, behold, they are hid in the earth in the midst of my tent, and the silver under it.' I saw, I coveted, I took, they are hid. Those four steps are the story of so much sin. It is the same pattern as Eve in the garden, who saw the fruit, desired it, and took it. Sin begins with what we look at, and what we let ourselves want. The best place to stop it is at the very beginning.
Long after, God promised through the prophet Hosea that the place of trouble would become a place of hope: 'and the valley of Achor for a door of hope'. And John wrote, 'If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.'
Is there something you are hiding, from God or from others? Do not wait until there is nowhere left to hide. Bring it into the light today. Confess it to God, and if you need to, tell someone you trust. God is faithful to forgive.
Here is today’s question. Achan saw, coveted, took, and hid. At which of those steps do you think it is easiest to stop sin, and how can you do that?
Let us pray. Lord God, you see everything, even what is hidden. Forgive me for the things I have wanted that were not mine, and for the times I have hidden my sin. I confess it to you now. Thank you that you are faithful to forgive, and that you can turn the valley of trouble into a door of hope. Amen.
