Two mountains stand side by side, with a valley between them. On the slopes of one mountain stands half of a nation. On the slopes of the other stands the other half. In the valley, priests stand around the ark of God. And one man begins to read aloud, every word of God’s law, while men, women, children and foreigners listen, and answer.
In chapter seven, Israel was defeated at Ai, because Achan had taken some of the things devoted to God and hidden them in his tent. Joshua fell on his face, and God said, get thee up. Achan was found, man by man, and he confessed, I saw, I coveted, I took, and they are hid. The judgment fell, and the place was called the valley of Achor.
Now, in chapter eight, the sin has been dealt with, and God turns again to Joshua with encouragement. Listen for God’s plan for Ai. And then listen for what Joshua does once the battle is over.
The chapter begins with grace.
After the shame of defeat, Joshua might have been afraid to try again. But God speaks first, and his words are full of hope. This time, God gives Joshua a plan, and this time, Israel may keep the cattle and the goods from the city for themselves.
The plan is an ambush. Joshua sends a large group of fighting men off in secret, to hide behind the city, on the west side, between Ai and Bethel. Be ready, he tells them. Do not go far from the city.
Then Joshua and the rest of the army camp on the north side of Ai, in plain sight, with a valley between them and the city.
Joshua explains the plan. When the men of Ai come out against us, as they did the first time, we will run away from them. They will say, they are running from us, just like before. And they will chase us away from the city. Then the men in hiding will rise up and take the city.
It all happens just as Joshua said. The king of Ai sees Israel’s army, and he hurries out with all his men to fight, not knowing that men are waiting behind the city. Joshua and Israel pretend to be beaten, and flee toward the wilderness. And every man in Ai, and in Bethel nearby, goes out to chase them. They leave the city wide open behind them.
Then the LORD says to Joshua, stretch out the spear in your hand toward Ai, for I will give it into your hand. And Joshua stretches out his spear toward the city.
At that signal, the men in hiding rise up, and run into the empty city, and set it on fire. The men of Ai look back, and see the smoke of their city rising up to heaven. And they have nowhere to go. Israel’s army turns back to face them, and the men from the city come out behind them. The men of Ai are caught in the middle, and the battle is lost for them. Joshua does not lower his spear until the victory is complete.
The town that defeated Israel when they trusted in themselves now falls easily, when they follow God’s plan.
But the most important part of this chapter comes after the battle.
Joshua leads the people north to two mountains, Ebal and Gerizim, near the town of Shechem. Moses had commanded them to do this, long before, when they came into the land.
This was a holy moment, in the middle of a war. But Joshua knew that obeying God’s word was more important than winning the next battle.
On the stones there, Joshua writes a copy of the law of Moses, in front of all the people.
Then all Israel gathers, with their elders, officers and judges. Half of them stand in front of mount Gerizim, and half in front of mount Ebal. And the priests stand in the middle, with the ark of the covenant. The writer adds, 'as well the stranger, as he that was born among them'. Not only Israelites, but foreigners living among them too, like Rahab and her family.
And Joshua reads all the words of the law, the blessings and the curses, everything that is written in the book.
Think of the blessings, ringing out from one mountain. And the warnings, from the other. God’s word was not only for priests and leaders. It was read aloud for everyone to hear, and everyone was expected to answer.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 1. 'And the LORD said unto Joshua, Fear not, neither be thou dismayed: take all the people of war with thee, and arise, go up to Ai: see, I have given into thy hand the king of Ai, and his people, and his city, and his land:' After failure, God does not say, you are finished. He says, fear not. The sin had been confessed and dealt with, and now God speaks words of encouragement again. Failure is not the end of the story for God’s people. When we turn back to him, he lifts us up and sends us out again.
Verse 30. 'Then Joshua built an altar unto the LORD God of Israel in mount Ebal,' Before Israel builds houses, or cities, or walls, Joshua builds an altar. An altar was a place of sacrifice and worship. In the middle of a war, Joshua stops to worship. It shows us what matters most. God comes first, before every plan, and every victory.
Verse 35. 'There was not a word of all that Moses commanded, which Joshua read not before all the congregation of Israel, with the women, and the little ones, and the strangers that were conversant among them.' Not a word was left out. Joshua did not skip the hard parts, or the warnings. And notice who heard it: the women, the little ones, and the strangers. God’s word was for everyone, young and old, born in Israel or from far away. It still is.
God had told Joshua, 'This book of the law shall not depart out of thy mouth'. And Paul wrote to a young church leader, 'Give attendance to reading, to exhortation, to doctrine.'
Joshua read God’s word aloud to everyone, even the little ones. Read a passage of the Bible aloud today, perhaps with your family or a friend. Let God’s word be heard, not only read silently.
Here is today’s question. Joshua read every word of the law, the blessings and the warnings. Why do you think it matters to hear the whole of God’s word, and not just the parts we like?
Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that after failure, you say to your people, fear not. Thank you for your word, which is for everyone, the young and the old, and those who come from far away. Help me to put you first, before every plan, and to listen to every word you speak. Amen.
