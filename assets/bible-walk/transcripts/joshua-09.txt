A group of travellers stagger into the camp. Their clothes are worn thin. Their sandals are patched and falling apart. Their wine skins are cracked and split. And when they open their food bags, the bread inside is dry and mouldy. We have come from a very far country, they say. And everything about them seems to say that it is true.
In chapter eight, God said to Joshua, fear not. Israel took Ai by an ambush, and then Joshua built an altar on mount Ebal, and read every word of the law before all the people, the women, the little ones, and the strangers among them.
Now, in chapter nine, Israel faces a different kind of danger. Not an army, but a trick. Listen for the one thing Israel forgets to do. And listen for how they keep a promise, even when it was made by mistake.
When the kings of the land hear what has happened, they gather together to fight against Joshua and Israel. The Hittites, the Amorites, the Canaanites and the others, all with one purpose.
But the people of one city, Gibeon, have a different idea. They have heard what Israel did to Jericho and Ai. And they decide that they cannot win a battle. So they work wilily, which means cleverly, with a trick.
They dress up as ambassadors who have travelled a long way. They take old sacks on their donkeys, and old wine skins, torn and tied up. They put old patched shoes on their feet, and old clothes on their backs. And all the bread they carry is dry and mouldy.
They come to Joshua at the camp at Gilgal, and say, we have come from a far country. Now make a league with us. A league is a treaty, a promise of peace.
The men of Israel are cautious at first. Perhaps you live near us, they say. How can we make a treaty with you?
We are your servants, the Gibeonites answer.
Joshua asks them directly, who are you, and where do you come from?
From a very far country, they say, because of the name of the LORD your God. We have heard all that he did in Egypt, and to the two kings of the Amorites beyond the Jordan. So our elders sent us to meet you, and to say, we are your servants, make a league with us.
Then they show their evidence. This bread was hot from the oven when we left home. Now look at it. Dry and mouldy. These wine skins were new when we filled them. Now they are torn. And our clothes and shoes are worn out from the very long journey.
It is a good story. And it is very convincing.
And there is the mistake.
Joshua makes peace with them, and promises to let them live. And the leaders of Israel swear an oath to them, in the name of the LORD.
Three days later, the truth comes out. These people are not from a far country at all. They are neighbours, living just a few days’ walk away. Israel goes to their cities, and finds Gibeon, and three other towns nearby.
The people of Israel are angry with their leaders. They murmur and complain. But the leaders stand firm.
So the Gibeonites are allowed to live. But they will serve Israel, cutting wood and carrying water for the people, and for the altar of the LORD.
Joshua calls the Gibeonites and asks them, why did you deceive us, saying you live far away, when you live among us? Now you will serve, as woodcutters and water carriers for the house of my God.
And the Gibeonites answer honestly at last. We were told that the LORD your God commanded Moses to give you all this land. And we were very afraid for our lives. So we did this.
Then they say something remarkable.
So Joshua protects them, and does not let Israel harm them.
There is something surprising in this story. The Gibeonites lied, and that was wrong. But underneath their lie was a kind of faith. They believed that the God of Israel was real, and that his word would come true. And like Rahab, they put themselves in Israel’s hands, and were spared.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 14. 'And the men took of their victuals, and asked not counsel at the mouth of the LORD.' Victuals means food. The leaders looked at the mouldy bread, and the torn wine skins, and believed what they could see. But they did not ask God. They had the priests, and they had the LORD himself, ready to guide them. And they never asked. When something looks obvious, that is when we are most tempted to decide on our own. We should bring every decision to God, even the ones that seem easy.
Verse 19. 'But all the princes said unto all the congregation, We have sworn unto them by the LORD God of Israel: now therefore we may not touch them.' The promise was made by mistake, and it was made to people who lied. And still Israel kept it. They had sworn by the name of the LORD, and God’s name mattered more than their own advantage. Keeping our word, even when it costs us, honours God.
Verse 25. 'And now, behold, we are in thine hand: as it seemeth good and right unto thee to do unto us, do.' The Gibeonites stop pretending, and simply put themselves in Joshua’s hands. Do what is good and right. And Joshua treats them well. There is a picture here of how we come to God. We stop making excuses, and we put ourselves in his hands. And we find that his hands are good.
A proverb says, 'In all thy ways acknowledge him, and he shall direct thy paths.' And a psalm describes the one who may dwell with God as one who 'sweareth to his own hurt, and changeth not.'
Is there a decision in front of you that seems simple, so you have not prayed about it? Stop and ask God about it today. And think of a promise you have made. Ask God to help you keep it, even if it costs you.
Here is today’s question. Israel’s leaders looked at the evidence, but asked not counsel at the mouth of the LORD. What are some ways you can ask God for guidance before you make decisions?
Let us pray. Lord God, forgive me for the times I have made decisions without asking you. Teach me to seek your guidance in all my ways, even when the answer seems obvious. Help me to keep my word, even when it is hard. And thank you that when I put myself in your hands, I find that they are good. Amen.
