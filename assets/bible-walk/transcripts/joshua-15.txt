A young bride is riding to her new home, on a donkey. Her father has given her husband a piece of land in the dry south country. It is good land, but there is no water. Before she arrives, she has an idea. She slips down from her donkey, goes to her father, and asks him for a gift. And her father, who loves her, gives her more than she asked for.
In chapter fourteen, the land began to be shared out by lot. Old Caleb came to Joshua and said, I wholly followed the LORD my God. I am as strong this day as I was then. Now therefore give me this mountain. And Joshua blessed him, and gave him Hebron.
Now, in chapter fifteen, the first and largest share of land goes to the tribe of Judah. Listen for a familiar name hidden in the borders. And listen for Caleb’s daughter, and what she asks for.
The first lot falls to the tribe of Judah. And the writer describes its borders carefully, like a surveyor walking round a field.
The south border runs from the bottom of the Salt Sea, across the wilderness of Zin, past Kadesh-barnea, and out to the river of Egypt, ending at the great sea. The east border is the Salt Sea itself, all the way up to where the Jordan flows in.
The north border climbs up from the Jordan, toward a place we have heard of before. The border went up toward Debir from the valley of Achor. That is the valley of trouble, where Achan’s sin was judged. Now it is simply part of the border of Judah’s land. Trouble does not have the last word.
The border goes on past the valley of the son of Hinnom, along the south side of the Jebusite city, the city that is Jerusalem. It climbs to the top of the mountain, and down to Kirjath-jearim, and Beth-shemesh, and out to the great sea in the west. And the west border is the great sea and its coast.
Judah was given a large and important share. Long before, as Jacob was dying, he blessed his son Judah with a special promise. The sceptre shall not depart from Judah, he said. From the tribe of Judah would come Israel’s great king, David. And from the line of David, many years later, would come Jesus.
Then the writer turns to Caleb again.
And Caleb drives out the three sons of Anak from Hebron, the very giants whose families had frightened the spies long ago. The old man’s faith is proved true.
Then Caleb goes up against the city of Debir, which used to be called Kirjath-sepher. And he makes an offer. Whoever takes this city, he says, I will give him my daughter Achsah to be his wife.
A man named Othniel, a close relative of Caleb, takes the city. And Caleb gives him Achsah to be his wife. Keep the name Othniel in mind. He will appear again in the book of Judges, as the very first of the judges God raises up to rescue Israel.
When Achsah comes to her new husband, she encourages him to ask her father for a field. Then she gets down from her donkey, and Caleb says to her, what do you want?
And Achsah answers.
After this, the writer lists the cities of Judah, group by group. Cities in the far south toward Edom. Cities in the low country, like Lachish and Azekah. Cities in the hill country, like Hebron and Debir. And cities in the wilderness, by the Salt Sea, like En-gedi. More than a hundred cities in all, with their villages.
And then, at the end of the chapter, a sad note.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 13. 'And unto Caleb the son of Jephunneh he gave a part among the children of Judah, according to the commandment of the LORD to Joshua, even the city of Arba the father of Anak, which city is Hebron.' Notice the words, according to the commandment of the LORD. Caleb did not grab Hebron for himself. He received it as a gift from God, at the right time. Many years earlier, God had promised Caleb this land. Now the promise is kept. God never forgets what he has promised, even when it seems a very long time.
Verse 19. 'Who answered, Give me a blessing; for thou hast given me a south land; give me also springs of water. And he gave her the upper springs, and the nether springs.' Achsah was bold. She knew the land without water would struggle to grow anything. So she asked for springs. And her father gave her both the upper springs and the lower springs, more than she asked for. Jesus said that if earthly fathers know how to give good gifts to their children, how much more will our Father in heaven. We can come to God boldly, and ask him for what we need.
Verse 63. 'As for the Jebusites the inhabitants of Jerusalem, the children of Judah could not drive them out: but the Jebusites dwell with the children of Judah at Jerusalem unto this day.' Here is the first of several sad notes in these chapters. Could not drive them out. Jerusalem, the city that would one day become the city of God’s temple, remained in other hands. It would stay that way until the time of King David. Some battles take longer than we expect. And some things that we leave undone come back to trouble us later.
Jesus said, 'If ye then, being evil, know how to give good gifts unto your children, how much more shall your Father which is in heaven give good things to them that ask him?' And Jesus said, 'If any man thirst, let him come unto me, and drink.'
What do you need from God today? Do not be afraid to ask him, as Achsah asked her father. He is a good Father, and he loves to give good gifts to his children. Ask him today, and trust him to answer.
Here is today’s question. Achsah asked her father for springs of water, and he gave her more than she asked. What is something you need to ask your heavenly Father for boldly?
Let us pray. Father God, thank you that you are a good Father, who loves to give good gifts. Thank you that you keep every promise, and that trouble does not have the last word. Give me springs of water, Lord, so that my life can be fruitful for you. And help me not to leave undone the things you have asked me to do. Amen.
