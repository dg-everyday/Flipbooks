An old man lies in bed, nearly blind. Two boys are brought to him, his grandsons. He reaches out his hands to bless them. But instead of putting his right hand on the older boy’s head, as everyone expects, he crosses his arms, and puts it on the younger. His son tries to correct him. But the old man says, I know, my son, I know. Long after, in Joshua chapter sixteen, the families of those two boys receive their land.
In chapter fifteen, the first lot fell to Judah, and its borders were marked out, from the Salt Sea to the great sea, even through the valley of Achor. Caleb drove out the giants from Hebron. His daughter Achsah asked, give me also springs of water, and he gave her the upper springs, and the nether springs. But the Jebusites in Jerusalem could not be driven out.
Now, in chapter sixteen, the lot falls to the children of Joseph. To understand this chapter, we need to remember a story from long before.
The children of Joseph. Do you remember Joseph? He was the favourite son of Jacob, the boy with the coat of many colours. His brothers hated him, and sold him as a slave into Egypt. But God was with Joseph. He rose from slave to prisoner to ruler of all Egypt, second only to Pharaoh. And when a great famine came, Joseph saved his whole family from starving, and brought them to live in Egypt.
Joseph told his brothers, ye thought evil against me; but God meant it unto good.
In Egypt, Joseph had two sons, Manasseh and Ephraim. When Jacob was old and dying, he did something special. He said to Joseph, your two sons are mine. Ephraim and Manasseh shall be mine, as Reuben and Simeon are. Jacob adopted his grandsons as his own sons. So each of them would become the head of a whole tribe. That is why Joseph’s family receives a double share of the land.
And when Jacob blessed the two boys, he crossed his hands, and put his right hand, the hand of the greater blessing, on the younger boy, Ephraim. Joseph thought his father had made a mistake. But Jacob knew what he was doing. The younger one shall be greater, he said.
There was one more thing Joseph asked before he died. He made his family promise, God will surely visit you, and ye shall carry up my bones from hence. Joseph believed that God would bring his people back to the promised land. And so when Israel left Egypt, Moses took the bones of Joseph with them. Through all the years in the wilderness, they carried Joseph’s bones. And at the very end of this book, we will hear where they were finally laid to rest.
Now, at last, Joseph’s descendants receive their land.
The lot of the children of Joseph runs from the Jordan near Jericho, up into the hill country around Bethel, and west down to Beth-horon and Gezer, and out to the sea. It is the rich, green hill country in the middle of the land. Good land, with rain and trees and fields.
Then the writer describes the land of Ephraim in more detail. Its border runs from Ataroth-addar to upper Beth-horon, and on to the sea. It turns east, past Taanath-shiloh, down to Jericho, and out at the Jordan. Some of Ephraim’s cities are inside the land of Manasseh, their brother tribe.
Ephraim, the younger brother, receives its land first. Just as Jacob had said, with his crossed hands, so long before. God’s word through the old man was coming true.
And Ephraim would become one of the greatest tribes in Israel. Joshua himself was from the tribe of Ephraim. And the tabernacle of God would soon be set up at Shiloh, in Ephraim’s land.
But the chapter ends with a sad note, like the end of chapter fifteen.
So the lot of Joseph’s sons is a story of God’s faithfulness through the generations. A boy sold as a slave. A dream that came true. Two grandsons blessed by an old man. And now, long after, a whole tribe settling in the hills of the promised land. God had not forgotten Joseph, and he had not forgotten his promise.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 1. 'And the lot of the children of Joseph fell from Jordan by Jericho, unto the water of Jericho on the east, to the wilderness that goeth up from Jericho throughout mount Bethel,' The children of Joseph. Joseph had been dead for a very long time. But his name lived on, and God’s promise to him lived on. Joseph trusted God to keep his word, even after his own death. And God did. Our faithfulness can bless people long after we are gone.
Verse 4. 'So the children of Joseph, Manasseh and Ephraim, took their inheritance.' Took their inheritance. Joseph had once lost everything. His home, his family, his freedom. Now his children receive a home in the land God promised. The story that began with a pit and a prison ends with an inheritance. God can turn even the darkest beginnings into a place of blessing.
Verse 10. 'And they drave not out the Canaanites that dwelt in Gezer: but the Canaanites dwell among the Ephraimites unto this day, and serve under tribute.' Under tribute means that the Canaanites were made to work for Ephraim, and to pay them. Ephraim had the power to make them work, but they did not drive them out, as God had commanded. It was half obedience. It looked like success, but it was not what God had asked. And the book of Judges will show the trouble that came from it.
Joseph told his brothers, 'But as for you, ye thought evil against me; but God meant it unto good'. And the letter to the Hebrews says, 'By faith Joseph, when he died, made mention of the departing of the children of Israel; and gave commandment concerning his bones.'
Is there something in your life that is half done, where you have obeyed God partly, but not fully? Talk to God honestly about it today. And thank him that he is faithful through the generations, and can bring good even from hard beginnings.
Here is today’s question. God kept his promise to Joseph, long after Joseph had died. How does it encourage you to know that God’s faithfulness reaches beyond one lifetime?
Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that you are faithful from generation to generation. Thank you for Joseph, who trusted you even in the pit and the prison. Thank you that you can turn evil into good. Forgive me for the times I have obeyed you only halfway. Help me to follow you fully. Amen.
