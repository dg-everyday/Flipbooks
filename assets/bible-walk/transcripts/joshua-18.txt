Imagine being given the keys to a beautiful new home. The deeds are signed. The house is yours. But instead of moving in, you stay in a tent outside the gate, year after year. You never go in to look around. You never unpack. That is how seven of Israel’s tribes were living. And Joshua finally asks them, how long?
In chapter seventeen, the tribe of Manasseh received its land. The five daughters of Zelophehad reminded the leaders of God’s promise, and they were given their share. The children of Joseph complained that their land was too small, and Joshua said, get thee up to the wood country, and cut it down. Thou shalt drive out the Canaanites, though they have iron chariots.
Now, in chapter eighteen, the whole people gather at a new place, Shiloh. Listen for what they set up there. And listen for Joshua’s challenge to the seven tribes who have not yet received their land.
The whole congregation of Israel gathers at Shiloh, in the hill country of Ephraim. And there they set up the tabernacle of the congregation.
The tabernacle was the holy tent, the place where God met with his people. It had travelled with them all through the wilderness. Since they crossed the Jordan, the camp had been at Gilgal. Now, for the first time, the tabernacle has a settled home in the middle of the land. And the writer adds, 'And the land was subdued before them.'
But there is a problem. Seven tribes still have not received their inheritance.
Judah has its land. Joseph’s tribes, Ephraim and Manasseh, have theirs. Reuben, Gad and half Manasseh have land beyond the Jordan. But seven tribes are still waiting. Benjamin, Simeon, Zebulun, Issachar, Asher, Naphtali and Dan.
Why were they waiting? The chapter does not say exactly. Perhaps they were tired after years of war. Perhaps they were comfortable where they were. Perhaps they were afraid of the people still living in the land. Whatever the reason, they had stopped moving forward.
And Joshua speaks to them plainly.
Then he gives them a practical plan. Choose three men from each tribe, he says. I will send them out, and they will walk through the land, and describe it, and come back to me. Divide it into seven parts. Judah will stay in the south, and the house of Joseph in the north. Write down the description of the land, and bring it here, and I will cast lots for you here, before the LORD our God.
The Levites will not have a share, Joshua reminds them, because the priesthood of the LORD is their inheritance. And Gad, Reuben and half Manasseh already have their land.
So the men set out. Joshua tells them, 'Go and walk through the land, and describe it, and come again to me, that I may here cast lots for you before the LORD in Shiloh.'
They travel through the land, and they write a description of it, city by city, in seven parts, in a book. And they come back to Joshua at Shiloh.
Think of those men, walking the hills and valleys, writing down every town and every stream. For the first time, the seven tribes could see what God had given them. Sometimes we need to stop and look carefully at what God has given us, before we can take hold of it.
Then comes the casting of the lots.
The first lot comes up for the tribe of Benjamin. Benjamin was the youngest son of Jacob, and the only full brother of Joseph. Both were sons of Rachel, the wife Jacob loved.
Benjamin’s land lies between Judah in the south and Joseph in the north. It is a small piece of land, but it holds some of the most important places in the story so far. Jericho, where the walls fell down. Bethel, where Jacob dreamed of the ladder to heaven. Gibeon, the city Joshua saved. And the city of the Jebusites, which is Jerusalem.
The tribe of Benjamin was small, but it would play a great part in Israel’s story. Israel’s first king, Saul, would come from Benjamin. And many centuries later, so would the apostle Paul, who wrote that he was of the tribe of Benjamin.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 1. 'And the whole congregation of the children of Israel assembled together at Shiloh, and set up the tabernacle of the congregation there. And the land was subdued before them.' Before they finished dividing the land, they put the tabernacle in the centre. God’s presence came first, in the middle of the land and in the middle of their life. Shiloh would be the place of worship for Israel for many years. Everything else was arranged around the place where God met his people.
Verse 3. 'And Joshua said unto the children of Israel, How long are ye slack to go to possess the land, which the LORD God of your fathers hath given you?' Slack means slow, or lazy. God had already given them the land. All they had to do was go and take hold of it. But they were waiting. It is possible to have God’s promises, and still not enjoy them, because we never step out to receive them. Joshua’s question is for us too. How long will we wait?
Verse 10. 'And Joshua cast lots for them in Shiloh before the LORD: and there Joshua divided the land unto the children of Israel according to their divisions.' Notice the words, before the LORD. The lots were not a game of chance. They were cast in God’s presence, at the tabernacle, trusting God to decide. Each tribe received its land from God’s hand. Each one could say, the LORD chose this place for us.
Paul wrote, 'Not slothful in business; fervent in spirit; serving the Lord'. And a psalm says, 'The lines are fallen unto me in pleasant places; yea, I have a goodly heritage.'
Is there something God has given you that you have been slow to take hold of? A promise in his word, a gift, a calling? Write it down today, and take one step toward it.
Here is today’s question. Joshua asked, how long are ye slack to go to possess the land? Why do you think people sometimes hold back from what God has given them, and what helps us move forward?
Let us pray. Lord God, thank you for all that you have given me. Forgive me for the times I have been slack, and slow to take hold of your gifts. Put your presence at the centre of my life. And give me courage to step out and receive all that you have promised. Amen.
