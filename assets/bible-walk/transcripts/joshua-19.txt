A leader has spent years making sure everyone else is looked after. Every family has a place. Every tribe has a home. He has fought their battles and settled their quarrels. And only when the very last person has been given their share does anyone think to ask, what about you? Where will you live?
In chapter eighteen, the tabernacle was set up at Shiloh, in the centre of the land. Joshua asked the seven tribes still waiting, how long are ye slack to go to possess the land? Men walked through the land and described it in a book, and Joshua cast lots before the LORD. The first of those lots fell to Benjamin, with Jericho, Bethel, Gibeon and Jerusalem.
Now, in chapter nineteen, the last six lots are cast. Listen for a region in the north that will one day be very important in the story of Jesus. And listen for who receives the very last inheritance.
The second lot comes out for Simeon.
Simeon’s cities lie in the far south, among the cities of Judah. Places like Beer-sheba, where Abraham and Isaac had lived long before, and Ziklag, a town that will appear again in the story of David.
There is something behind this. Long before, when Jacob was dying, he spoke about Simeon and his brother Levi. They had done a violent thing in anger. And Jacob said, I will divide them in Jacob, and scatter them in Israel. Now we see it. Levi has no land of its own, only cities scattered among the tribes. And Simeon lives inside the land of Judah. God’s word through Jacob was coming true.
The third lot comes up for Zebulun. Its land is in the north, in the hills of Galilee, with twelve cities and their villages. One of them is called Bethlehem. But this is a different Bethlehem, in the north, not the Bethlehem of Judah where David and Jesus would be born.
The fourth lot comes out for Issachar, in the valley of Jezreel, with cities like Shunem and Jezreel, reaching to mount Tabor and the Jordan.
The fifth lot comes out for Asher, along the coast in the north-west, by mount Carmel, reaching up toward great Zidon and the strong city of Tyre.
The sixth lot comes out for Naphtali, in the far north, by the Sea of Chinnereth. That is the lake later called the Sea of Galilee. Naphtali’s cities include Kedesh and Hazor.
Zebulun and Naphtali were far from Jerusalem, on the edge of the land, near other nations. Many people looked down on that region. But the prophet Isaiah said that a great light would shine there. And the Gospel of Matthew tells us that this is where Jesus lived and began to preach. The land of Zebulun and Naphtali, Galilee, where Jesus called his first disciples by the lake.
The seventh lot comes out for Dan. Dan’s land lies in the west, between Judah and Ephraim, toward the coast and the city of Joppa. But the writer tells us that Dan’s land was too small for them. So the men of Dan go far to the north, and take a city called Leshem, and live there, and rename it Dan, after their father.
And now, every tribe has its land. Every family has a home.
But there is one person left.
Joshua had been there from the beginning. He was a young man when Israel left Egypt. He fought Amalek in the wilderness. He was a spy who believed God. He led the people across the Jordan. He fought for years. He divided the whole land. And through it all, he never took anything for himself first.
Notice where Joshua chose to live. Timnath-serah was in the hill country of Ephraim, his own tribe. It was not one of the great cities. And it was not finished. Joshua had to build it himself. Even in his old age, he was still building.
The chapter ends with these words.
The country is divided. The promise has been shared out. And it was done before the LORD, at the door of the tabernacle.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 9. 'Out of the portion of the children of Judah was the inheritance of the children of Simeon: for the part of the children of Judah was too much for them: therefore the children of Simeon had their inheritance within the inheritance of them.' Judah had more land than they needed. So they shared it with Simeon. Instead of keeping it all, they made room for their brothers. When God gives us more than we need, it is often so that we can share with others. God’s people make room for one another.
Verse 49. 'When they had made an end of dividing the land for inheritance by their coasts, the children of Israel gave an inheritance to Joshua the son of Nun among them:' Joshua was the last to receive his share. He made sure everyone else was settled first. That is the mark of a true servant leader. Jesus said that whoever wants to be great must be the servant of all. Joshua lived that out. And in the end, it was the people themselves who gave him his inheritance.
Verse 50. 'According to the word of the LORD they gave him the city which he asked, even Timnath-serah in mount Ephraim: and he built the city, and dwelt therein.' According to the word of the LORD. Even Joshua’s own home came from God’s word, not from his own grabbing. He asked for one small city in the hills. And he built it, and lived there. After a life of battles, Joshua received a place of rest. God takes care of those who serve him faithfully.
Matthew wrote of Galilee, 'The land of Zabulon, and the land of Nephthalim, by the way of the sea, beyond Jordan, Galilee of the Gentiles; The people which sat in darkness saw great light'. And Jesus said, 'whosoever will be great among you, let him be your minister'.
Who could you put first today? Think of one way you could serve someone else before yourself, or share something you have more than enough of. Do it quietly, as Joshua did, and trust God to take care of you.
Here is today’s question. Joshua made sure everyone else had their land before he took his own. What would it look like for you to put others first, in your home, your work or your church?
Let us pray. Lord God, thank you for Joshua, who served your people faithfully and put himself last. Thank you for Jesus, who came not to be served, but to serve. Teach me to make room for others, to share what you have given me, and to trust you to take care of me. Amen.
