An old man knows his life is almost over. He gathers the people he loves most, and looks at their faces. He has so much he could say. Stories of battles, lessons learned, mistakes and victories. But time is short. So he chooses his words carefully. What would you say, if you had one last chance to speak to the people you love?
In chapter twenty two, the eastern tribes went home with Joshua’s blessing: love the LORD, and cleave unto him. They built a great altar by the Jordan, and the rest of Israel nearly went to war. But Phinehas went to ask first, and found it was an altar of witness, called Ed, for it shall be a witness between us that the LORD is God.
Now, in chapter twenty three, Joshua is old. He calls the leaders of Israel together for his farewell. Listen for what he wants them to remember. And listen for his warning.
A long time has passed since the LORD gave Israel rest from all their enemies. Joshua is now old, and stricken in age.
He calls together all Israel, their elders, their heads, their judges and their officers. And he says to them, I am old.
Joshua does not start by talking about himself, or his own achievements. He points them to God. You have seen all that the LORD your God has done to these nations because of you. 'for the LORD your God is he that hath fought for you.'
Joshua reminds them that he has divided the land among them by lot, even the parts that are not yet fully taken. And he promises that the LORD will drive out the rest of the nations, and they will possess the land, as God has said.
Then he gives them his first charge. 'Be ye therefore very courageous to keep and to do all that is written in the book of the law of Moses, that ye turn not aside therefrom to the right hand or to the left'.
Do you hear the echo? These are almost the same words God spoke to Joshua in chapter one. Be strong and very courageous. Turn not to the right hand or to the left. Now, at the end of his life, Joshua passes them on.
Joshua tells them not to mix with the nations who remain in the land. Do not even speak the names of their gods. Do not swear by them, or serve them, or bow down to them. 'But cleave unto the LORD your God, as ye have done unto this day.'
Then Joshua reminds them where their strength comes from.
And from that, he draws the most important lesson of all.
But then Joshua gives a solemn warning. If you turn back, he says, and hold on to the nations that remain, and marry among them, then know for certain that the LORD will no longer drive them out. They will become snares and traps for you, whips on your sides and thorns in your eyes, until you are gone from this good land.
These are strong words. Joshua is not trying to frighten them for no reason. He loves them. He has seen what happens when God’s people turn away. And he knows that the greatest danger to Israel is not the armies around them. It is the slow drift of their hearts away from God.
Then Joshua speaks of his own death.
And he adds a final warning. Just as God has kept all his good promises, he will also keep his warnings. If you break his covenant and serve other gods, then the anger of the LORD will burn against you, and you will quickly be gone from the good land he has given you.
It is a sober ending. But it comes from a man who has watched God keep every word he ever spoke. Joshua knew that God’s promises are sure. And that means his warnings are sure too.
Sadly, the book of Judges will show that Israel did not listen. But for now, the people hear Joshua’s words, and the choice is set before them. And in the next chapter, Joshua will ask them to make it.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 10. 'One man of you shall chase a thousand: for the LORD your God, he it is that fighteth for you, as he hath promised you.' One against a thousand. Those are impossible odds, unless God is fighting for you. Joshua had seen it again and again. The walls of Jericho, the hailstones from heaven, the sun standing still. Israel’s victories were never about their own strength. The LORD fought for them. And the same God fights for his people today.
Verse 11. 'Take good heed therefore unto yourselves, that ye love the LORD your God.' This is the heart of Joshua’s farewell. Not just obey God, not just fear God, but love him. Take good heed means watch carefully, guard this above everything else. Love for God does not happen by accident. We have to tend it, and protect it, like a fire that must be kept burning.
Verse 14. 'And, behold, this day I am going the way of all the earth: and ye know in all your hearts and in all your souls, that not one thing hath failed of all the good things which the LORD your God spake concerning you; all are come to pass unto you, and not one thing hath failed thereof.' Going the way of all the earth means that Joshua knows he will soon die. And at the end of his life, what does he want them to know? Not one thing hath failed. He says it twice. After a whole life of following God, Joshua’s testimony is this: God kept every promise. You know it, he says, in all your hearts and all your souls.
Jesus said that the first and great commandment is, 'Thou shalt love the Lord thy God with all thy heart, and with all thy soul, and with all thy mind.' And Paul wrote, 'If God be for us, who can be against us?'
Take good heed today to love the LORD your God. Spend some time simply telling him that you love him, and why. And think about what might be drawing your heart away from him. Ask him to keep your love for him burning.
Here is today’s question. At the end of his life, Joshua could say, not one thing hath failed. What would you want to be able to say about God at the end of your life?
Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that not one of your good promises has failed. Thank you that you fight for your people. Keep my heart from drifting away from you. Help me to take good heed, to cleave to you, and above all, to love you with all my heart and soul. Amen.
