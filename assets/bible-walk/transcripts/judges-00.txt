A left-handed man with a hidden dagger. A woman who leads a nation from under a palm tree. A frightened farmer hiding his wheat in a winepress, who is called a mighty man of valour. The strongest man who ever lived, undone by his own weakness. The book of Judges is full of surprising heroes. And behind every one of them is a God who will not give up on his people.
Welcome to the book of Judges, on The Living Voice. Judges has twenty one chapters, so on this walk it is twenty one steps, one chapter at a time.
At the end of the book of Joshua, the people stood at Shechem and promised, we will serve the LORD. And Israel served the LORD all the days of Joshua, and of the elders who had seen what God had done. The book of Judges tells what happened next. And it is not a happy story.
A new generation grew up who did not know the LORD, or the great things he had done. They turned to the gods of the nations around them. And so begins a pattern that repeats again and again through the book.
Here it is. First, Israel does evil in the sight of the LORD. Second, God lets their enemies oppress them. Third, the people cry out to the LORD. And fourth, God raises up a deliverer to rescue them. Then there is peace for a while. But when the deliverer dies, the people turn away again, and the circle starts over. Each time round, things get a little worse.
The deliverers are called judges. They were not judges in a courtroom, like today. They were leaders, raised up by God to rescue his people from their enemies, and to lead them for a time. There are twelve of them in the book. Some, like Gideon and Samson, have long stories. Others are named in just a verse or two.
Here are three things to watch for as we walk.
Watch for the cycle. Listen for the words, the children of Israel did evil in the sight of the LORD, and the words, they cried unto the LORD. You will hear them again and again. And ask yourself honestly whether you ever go round the same circle.
Watch for unlikely heroes. God chooses a left-handed man, a woman, a farmer who thinks he is the least in his family, and a man with a terrible temper. Most of them are deeply flawed. God does not wait for perfect people. He uses weak and ordinary people, so that everyone can see the victory is his.
And watch for a sentence near the end of the book. In those days there was no king in Israel, but every man did that which was right in his own eyes. It sums up the whole book. When people decide for themselves what is right, without God, things fall apart. The book leaves us longing for a good king, a true deliverer, who will not fail. The rest of the Bible tells us who he is.
Before we begin, a gentle word. Judges is an honest book. It does not hide how bad things became when God’s people forgot him. Some chapters tell of violence and cruelty, and the last chapters are among the darkest in the whole Bible. We will tell them gently, and we will not dwell on the details. But if you are listening with children, you may want to listen to the later chapters yourself first, especially chapter nineteen.
Remember too that the book of Ruth, which we have already walked, happened in the days when the judges ruled. While the nation was falling apart, faithful love was still growing in a field near Bethlehem. God is never without his people, even in the darkest times.
Here is a question to carry with you. The people of Israel forgot God within one generation. What helps you to remember what God has done, so that you do not drift away from him?
Let us pray. Lord God, as I walk through the book of Judges, show me my own heart. Thank you that every time your people cried out to you, you heard them. Thank you that you use weak and ordinary people. Keep me from going round in circles, and help me to follow you faithfully. Amen.
