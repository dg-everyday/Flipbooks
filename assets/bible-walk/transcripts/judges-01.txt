Imagine a garden where someone has pulled up most of the weeds, but not all. A few here and there are left, because they were hard to reach, or because it was easier to leave them. At first, it hardly seems to matter. But give it time. The weeds spread. And one day the garden is overgrown. Judges chapter one is the story of a few weeds being left in the garden.
In the introduction, we heard that Judges tells what happened after the death of Joshua. A new generation forgot the LORD, and the book goes round and round a sad circle: sin, trouble, crying out to God, and rescue. We heard that God raised up judges, unlikely heroes, to deliver his people.
Now, in chapter one, the tribes set out to finish taking the land. Listen for how it starts well. And listen for one phrase that comes back again and again.
The book begins with a good question.
And the LORD answers. Judah shall go up. I have delivered the land into his hand.
So Judah asks his brother tribe Simeon to come and fight with him, and promises to help Simeon in return. Together they go up, and the LORD gives them victory at a place called Bezek.
There they meet a king named Adoni-bezek. He runs, but they catch him, and cut off his thumbs and his big toes. And Adoni-bezek says something surprising. Seventy kings, with their thumbs and toes cut off, used to gather scraps under my table. 'as I have done, so God hath requited me.' Requited means paid back. This cruel king had done the same thing to seventy others. Now he recognises that God has repaid him for what he did.
Then the men of Judah go on through the land. They fight against Jerusalem and set it on fire. Yet, as we will hear, the Jebusites go on living there. They go to Hebron, and drive out the giants there. And then we hear an old story again.
Caleb says, whoever takes the city of Debir will marry my daughter Achsah. Othniel, Caleb’s younger brother, takes the city. And Achsah asks her father for springs of water, and he gives her the upper springs and the lower springs. We heard this story in Joshua. Now it is repeated, as a bright memory of faith at the start of a darker book. Keep an ear out for Othniel. In chapter three, he will become the first judge.
The family of Moses’ father in law, the Kenites, also come and live among the people of Judah, in the wilderness in the south.
Judah and Simeon take more cities, including Gaza, Ashkelon and Ekron on the coast.
But then comes the first sign of trouble.
And then the house of Joseph goes up against Bethel, and the LORD is with them. Their spies see a man coming out of the city, and say, show us the way into the city, and we will show you mercy. He shows them, and they take the city, but they let the man and his family go. He goes off to the land of the Hittites, and builds a new city, and calls it Luz, the old name of Bethel.
And now the chapter turns into a long list. Listen to how it sounds.
'Neither did Manasseh drive out the inhabitants of Beth-shean and her towns'. 'Neither did Ephraim drive out the Canaanites that dwelt in Gezer'. 'Neither did Zebulun drive out the inhabitants of Kitron'. Neither did Asher. Neither did Naphtali. And the tribe of Dan is pushed back into the hills by the Amorites, who will not let them come down into the valley.
Neither, neither, neither. Over and over.
The children of Benjamin did not drive out the Jebusites from Jerusalem either. So the Jebusites go on living there.
Why does this matter so much? Because God had warned his people. He had told them, through Moses and through Joshua, that if they let the nations stay, those nations would become snares and traps to them. The people of the land worshipped other gods. And living side by side with them, Israel would be tempted to worship those gods too.
It was not that God was weak. Remember Jericho. Remember the sun standing still. The problem was not that God could not help them. It was that they stopped trusting him, and settled for less than he had promised.
And notice something else. Some of the tribes, when they became strong, put the Canaanites to work instead. They had the power to obey God, but they chose to profit from the people instead. It looked like success. But it was disobedience.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 1. 'Now after the death of Joshua it came to pass, that the children of Israel asked the LORD, saying, Who shall go up for us against the Canaanites first, to fight against them?' The book of Joshua began with the death of Moses. The book of Judges begins with the death of Joshua. And it begins well. The people ask the LORD what to do. That is always the right place to start. The trouble in this book does not begin because God stops answering. It begins when the people stop asking.
Verse 19. 'And the LORD was with Judah; and he drave out the inhabitants of the mountain; but could not drive out the inhabitants of the valley, because they had chariots of iron.' The LORD was with Judah. And yet they could not drive out the people with iron chariots. Do you remember what Joshua told the children of Joseph? Thou shalt drive out the Canaanites, though they have iron chariots. The same God who scattered the chariots at Merom was with them. But they looked at the chariots, and not at God.
Verse 28. 'And it came to pass, when Israel was strong, that they put the Canaanites to tribute, and did not utterly drive them out.' When Israel was strong. This was not weakness. It was a choice. They found it easier, and more profitable, to make the Canaanites work for them than to obey God fully. Half obedience often looks sensible. But it leaves weeds in the garden. And weeds grow.
Paul wrote, 'Be not deceived: evil communications corrupt good manners.' And the letter to the Hebrews says, 'let us lay aside every weight, and the sin which doth so easily beset us'.
Is there something in your life you have tolerated, instead of dealing with it? A habit, an attitude, a sin that seems small? Talk to God about it today. Ask him for the courage to pull up the weed while it is still small.
Here is today’s question. Israel settled for less than God had promised, because it seemed easier. Where are you tempted to settle for half obedience, instead of following God fully?
Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that you are always with your people, and that nothing is too hard for you. Forgive me for the times I have settled for less than you promised, or tolerated sin because it seemed easier. Help me to trust you fully, and to obey you completely. Amen.
