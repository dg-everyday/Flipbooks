A man walks into a king’s palace carrying a gift. The guards check him, but they do not find what he is hiding. He is left-handed, so he wears his dagger on the side no one expects. He asks to speak to the king alone. And he says, I have a message from God for you.
In chapter two, an angel spoke to the people at Bochim, and they wept. A new generation arose which knew not the LORD, and they served the Baals. The writer showed us the sad circle: sin, trouble, crying out, rescue, and sin again. But nevertheless, the LORD raised up judges to deliver them.
Now, in chapter three, we meet the first of those judges. Listen for the circle turning, once, twice, and a third time. And listen for the surprising ways God rescues his people.
The chapter begins by naming the nations God left in the land, to test Israel. The five lords of the Philistines, the Canaanites, the Sidonians, and the Hivites in the mountains of Lebanon. A whole new generation of Israel had never fought in the wars of Canaan. These nations would test whether they would obey the LORD.
And Israel fails the test. They live among the Canaanites and the other peoples. They marry their sons and daughters. And they serve their gods. They forget the LORD their God, and serve the Baals and the idols.
So the anger of the LORD is hot against Israel, and he lets a king from Mesopotamia oppress them. Mesopotamia was far to the north and east. 'and the children of Israel served Chushan-rishathaim eight years.'
And then the circle turns.
Othniel. We have met him twice already. He took the city of Debir, and married Caleb’s daughter Achsah. He came from a family of faith. Now he becomes the first judge.
So Othniel goes out to war, and the LORD gives the king of Mesopotamia into his hand. And the land has rest, until Othniel dies.
But the circle turns again. The children of Israel do evil again in the sight of the LORD. And God lets Eglon, king of Moab, grow strong against them. Eglon joins with the Ammonites and the Amalekites, and they defeat Israel and take the city of palm trees, near Jericho. And Israel serves Eglon for many years.
And again, the people cry out to the LORD.
Ehud makes himself a short dagger, with two edges, and straps it under his clothes on his right thigh. A right-handed man would carry a weapon on his left side, ready to draw. No one would think of checking the right side.
Ehud brings the tribute to Eglon. Then he sends the men who carried it away. But he turns back, and says to the king, I have a secret message for you, O king. And the king sends everyone out of the room.
Ehud comes close to Eglon, who is sitting alone in his cool upper room. And Ehud says, 'I have a message from God unto thee.' The king rises from his seat. And Ehud reaches with his left hand, takes the dagger, and strikes the king, and Eglon dies.
Ehud goes out, locks the doors behind him, and escapes. The king’s servants find the doors locked, and think the king wants to be left alone. They wait and wait, until they are embarrassed. At last they take a key and open the doors, and find their lord on the floor, dead.
By then, Ehud is long gone. He blows a trumpet in the hill country of Ephraim, and the men of Israel come down with him. 'Follow after me: for the LORD hath delivered your enemies the Moabites into your hand.' They take the fords of the Jordan, the river crossings, so that the Moabites cannot escape home. And Moab is defeated that day. 'And the land had rest fourscore years.'
This is a hard story, and the Bible tells it plainly. Eglon was a foreign king, holding God’s people in cruel slavery, and God raised up Ehud to set them free.
The chapter ends with one more judge, in just one verse. Shamgar, the son of Anath, fought against the Philistines with an ox goad. An ox goad was a long wooden stick with a sharp point, used to drive oxen when ploughing. It was a farmer’s tool, not a soldier’s weapon. And Shamgar too delivered Israel.
Othniel, from a family of faith. Ehud, left-handed. Shamgar, with a farmer’s stick. God uses all kinds of people, and all kinds of tools.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 9. 'And when the children of Israel cried unto the LORD, the LORD raised up a deliverer to the children of Israel, who delivered them, even Othniel the son of Kenaz, Caleb’s younger brother.' When they cried, the LORD raised up a deliverer. It is the pattern of the whole book. God’s people had brought their trouble on themselves. And still, when they cried out to him, he answered. God does not say, you made this mess, so sort it out yourselves. He hears, and he sends help.
Verse 10. 'And the Spirit of the LORD came upon him, and he judged Israel, and went out to war:' And the LORD gave the king of Mesopotamia into his hand. The Spirit of the LORD came upon him. This is the secret of every true judge. Othniel did not win by his own strength or cleverness. God’s Spirit gave him what he needed. We will hear these words again, about Gideon, Jephthah and Samson. God’s work is done by God’s Spirit.
Verse 15. 'But when the children of Israel cried unto the LORD, the LORD raised them up a deliverer, Ehud the son of Gera, a Benjamite, a man lefthanded: and by him the children of Israel sent a present unto Eglon the king of Moab.' A man lefthanded. In those days, being left-handed might have seemed unusual, even a weakness. But God used the very thing that made Ehud different. What others might overlook, God can use. The thing that makes you different may be the very thing God wants to use.
The prophet Zechariah heard God say, 'Not by might, nor by power, but by my spirit, saith the LORD of hosts.' And Paul wrote, 'But God hath chosen the foolish things of the world to confound the wise'.
What do you think of as your weakness, or the thing that makes you different? Offer it to God today. Ask him to use you, just as you are, by his Spirit.
Here is today’s question. God used Othniel from a family of faith, Ehud who was left-handed, and Shamgar with a farmer’s stick. What ordinary thing in your life might God want to use for his purposes?
Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that when your people cry out to you, you hear and you send help. Thank you that you use all kinds of people, even those the world overlooks. Fill me with your Spirit, and use me, just as I am, for your glory. Amen.
