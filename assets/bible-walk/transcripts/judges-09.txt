A young man climbs to the top of a mountain, and looks down on a city that has just done something terrible. Then he shouts down to them a story. A story about trees. The olive tree, the fig tree, the vine, and a thorny bramble. And when he has finished, he runs for his life.
In chapter eight, Gideon’s men were faint, yet pursuing. The people asked Gideon to rule over them, and he said, I will not rule over you: the LORD shall rule over you. But he made a golden ephod that became a snare. When Gideon died, the children of Israel remembered not the LORD their God. And we heard of a son named Abimelech, born to Gideon by a woman in Shechem.
Now, in chapter nine, Abimelech decides to make himself king. This is a dark chapter. Listen for Jotham’s parable. And listen for how the evil that Abimelech sowed comes back on him.
Abimelech goes to Shechem, to his mother’s family. And he says, ask the men of Shechem which is better: to have all seventy sons of Gideon ruling over you, or just one? And remember, I am your own flesh and blood.
His mother’s family speak to the men of Shechem for him. And their hearts lean toward Abimelech, because, they say, he is our brother.
They give him seventy pieces of silver from the temple of their god Baal-berith. With it, Abimelech hires worthless and reckless men to follow him.
Then Abimelech goes to his father’s house at Ophrah, and his brothers, the sons of Gideon, lose their lives at his hand. All except the youngest, Jotham, who hides himself and escapes.
The men of Shechem gather by a great tree and a pillar, and make Abimelech king.
Gideon had said, I will not rule over you, and neither shall my son. Now one of his sons has seized the throne through cruelty.
When Jotham hears it, he climbs to the top of mount Gerizim, above Shechem. And he cries out. 'Hearken unto me, ye men of Shechem, that God may hearken unto you.'
And he tells them a story.
The trees go to the olive tree, and say, reign over us. But the olive tree says, should I leave my oil, by which God and man are honoured, to go and wave over the trees?
So the trees go to the fig tree, and say, come and reign over us. But the fig tree says, should I leave my sweetness and my good fruit, to go and wave over the trees?
Then the trees say to the vine, come and reign over us. But the vine says, should I leave my wine, that cheers God and man, to go and wave over the trees?
The good trees are all busy being useful. None of them wants to rule.
Then all the trees say to the bramble, come and reign over us.
A bramble is a thorny, scrubby bush. It gives no fruit, and almost no shade. You cannot rest in a bramble’s shade. And dry brambles catch fire easily, and spread fire to everything around them.
Jotham explains. If you have done right by my father, who risked his life to save you from Midian, then rejoice in Abimelech, and let him rejoice in you. But if not, let fire come out from Abimelech and burn up the men of Shechem. And let fire come out from the men of Shechem and burn up Abimelech.
Then Jotham runs away, and lives far off, for fear of his brother.
And it all comes true.
After Abimelech has ruled for a short time, God sends a spirit of trouble between Abimelech and the men of Shechem. The men of Shechem begin to turn against him. They set men in the hills to rob everyone who passes.
A man named Gaal comes to Shechem, and the people trust him. At a harvest feast in the temple of their god, they mock Abimelech. Who is Abimelech, says Gaal, that we should serve him?
The ruler of the city sends a secret message to Abimelech. Abimelech comes with his army, and drives Gaal out. Then he turns on the city of Shechem itself. He fights against it, and destroys it, and scatters salt on it. The people of the tower of Shechem take shelter in the stronghold of their god’s temple. And Abimelech sets it on fire. Fire came out from Abimelech, just as Jotham had said.
Then Abimelech goes to another town, Thebez, and captures it. But there is a strong tower inside, and all the people flee to the top of it. Abimelech comes close to the door to burn it. And a woman on the roof drops a piece of a millstone on his head.
Abimelech is badly wounded. He calls to his young armour bearer and asks him to finish it, so that no one will say a woman defeated him. And so Abimelech dies.
When the men of Israel see that Abimelech is dead, they all go home.
The writer ends with the meaning of it all.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 8. 'The trees went forth on a time to anoint a king over them; and they said unto the olive tree, Reign thou over us.' This is the first parable in the Bible, a story with a hidden meaning. The olive, the fig and the vine were all fruitful. They were content to do the good work they were made for. They did not need power to feel important. The best people are often busy serving, not grasping for control.
Verse 15. 'And the bramble said unto the trees, If in truth ye anoint me king over you, then come and put your trust in my shadow: and if not, let fire come out of the bramble, and devour the cedars of Lebanon.' Put your trust in my shadow. A bramble has no shadow worth sitting in. It promises protection it cannot give, and threatens destruction. That was Abimelech. A leader who grabs power by cruelty promises much but brings ruin. Be careful whose shadow you trust.
Verse 57. 'And all the evil of the men of Shechem did God render upon their heads: and upon them came the curse of Jotham the son of Jerubbaal.' To render means to pay back. The men of Shechem had helped Abimelech do evil. And in the end, the evil came back on them. It may take time, but God sees. Wickedness does not go unnoticed by him. What we sow, we reap.
Paul wrote, 'Be not deceived; God is not mocked: for whatsoever a man soweth, that shall he also reap.' And Jesus said, 'whosoever will be great among you, let him be your minister'.
Are you more like the olive tree, content to be fruitful where God has placed you? Or are you sometimes tempted to grasp for importance? Ask God today to make you fruitful, humble and content.
Here is today’s question. The olive tree, the fig tree and the vine all said no to ruling, because they were busy being fruitful. What fruitful work has God given you to do right where you are?
Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that you see everything, and that wickedness does not go unnoticed. Keep me from grasping for power or importance. Make me like the olive, the fig and the vine, content to be fruitful where you have planted me. And help me to trust only in your shadow. Amen.
