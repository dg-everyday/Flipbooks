A child keeps running away, and every time, the parent goes out to find them and bring them home. Again and again. And then one day, the child runs off once more, and calls for help. And the parent says, why are you calling me? Call on the friends you ran away to. And there is a long, painful silence.
In chapter nine, Abimelech, son of Gideon, made himself king by cruelty. His youngest brother Jotham told the parable of the trees, and the bramble that promised shade but brought fire. And the evil that Abimelech and the men of Shechem had done came back on their own heads.
Now, in chapter ten, we meet two quiet judges. Then the circle turns again, and this time God’s answer is different. Listen for what God says when Israel cries out. And listen for what the people do next.
After Abimelech, a man named Tola rises up to defend Israel. He is from the tribe of Issachar, and lives in the hill country of Ephraim. He judges Israel for many years, and then he dies, and is buried.
After him comes Jair, from Gilead, on the east side of the Jordan. He has thirty sons, who ride on thirty young donkeys, and have thirty towns. Then Jair dies, and is buried.
That is all we are told about them. No great battles. No dramatic stories. Just two men who served God’s people faithfully for many years, and then died.
Not every faithful life makes a big story. Some of God’s servants are known for quiet, steady years of service. And God sees and values them all.
But then, the circle turns once more. And this time, it is worse than ever.
The children of Israel do evil again in the sight of the LORD. They serve the Baals and the Ashtaroth. And the gods of Syria, and of Zidon, and of Moab. The gods of the Ammonites, and of the Philistines. They forsake the LORD, and do not serve him at all.
Listen to that list. Not one false god, but the gods of seven nations. They will worship anything, except the LORD.
So the anger of the LORD burns against Israel. He lets the Philistines in the west, and the Ammonites in the east, oppress them. For many years, the Ammonites crush the Israelites who live in Gilead, east of the Jordan. Then they cross the river, and fight against Judah, Benjamin and Ephraim. And Israel is in great distress.
Then the people cry out to the LORD.
But this time, God’s answer is not what they expect.
God reminds them of everything he has done. Did I not rescue you from the Egyptians, and from the Amorites, the Ammonites and the Philistines? When the Zidonians and the Amalekites and the Maonites oppressed you, and you cried to me, did I not save you? 'Yet ye have forsaken me, and served other gods: wherefore I will deliver you no more.'
Then comes the hardest word of all.
Imagine hearing that from God. It is not cruelty. It is the pain of a God whose love has been thrown back at him again and again. He wants them to see what they have done. Their idols cannot help them. Only the God they have rejected can.
And this time, the people do not just cry. They say, 'We have sinned: do thou unto us whatsoever seemeth good unto thee; deliver us only, we pray thee, this day.' They do not make demands. They put themselves in God’s hands.
And then they do something they have not done before in this book. They act.
Then the Ammonites gather and camp in Gilead. And the Israelites gather and camp at Mizpeh. But they have no leader. The people and the princes of Gilead say to one another, who will begin the fight against the Ammonites? Whoever does will be head over all who live in Gilead.
The stage is set for the next judge. And he is a very unlikely choice.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 10. 'And the children of Israel cried unto the LORD, saying, We have sinned against thee, both because we have forsaken our God, and also served Baalim.' This time, the people do more than cry for help. They confess. We have sinned. They name what they have done. Real repentance is not just being sorry that we are in trouble. It is being honest about what we have done wrong.
Verse 14. 'Go and cry unto the gods which ye have chosen; let them deliver you in the time of your tribulation.' Tribulation means trouble. These are some of the hardest words God speaks in the whole book. But they are true. The things we put in God’s place cannot save us when trouble comes. Money, success, people, pleasures. When life falls apart, they have nothing to give. Only the living God can save.
Verse 16. 'And they put away the strange gods from among them, and served the LORD: and his soul was grieved for the misery of Israel.' They put away the strange gods. Turning back to God means turning away from what took his place. And look at God’s heart. His soul was grieved for the misery of Israel. Even after he said, I will deliver you no more, he could not bear their suffering. God’s love is deeper than his anger. When we truly turn back to him, he is moved with compassion.
Through the prophet Hosea, God said, 'How shall I give thee up, Ephraim? how shall I deliver thee, Israel?' And, 'mine heart is turned within me, my repentings are kindled together.' And Isaiah wrote, 'In all their affliction he was afflicted'.
Is there a strange god in your life, something you have turned to instead of God? Do not only ask God for help. Put it away today. Then turn to him, and trust his heart of compassion.
Here is today’s question. When the people put away their strange gods, the Bible says God’s soul was grieved for their misery. What does this tell you about God’s heart toward people who turn back to him?
Let us pray. Lord God, I have sinned against you, and I have turned to other things instead of you. Forgive me. I put them away now, and I turn to you. Thank you that your heart is moved by our suffering, and that your love is deeper than our sin. Deliver me, I pray. Amen.
