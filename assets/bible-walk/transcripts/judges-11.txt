A father comes home from battle, victorious. And out of his front door comes his only child, his daughter, dancing and playing a tambourine, overjoyed to see him safe. And instead of running to hug her, the father tears his clothes and cries out in grief. Because of a promise he made before the battle. A promise he never should have made.
In chapter ten, two quiet judges, Tola and Jair, served Israel faithfully. Then Israel served the gods of seven nations, and when they cried out, God said, go and cry unto the gods which ye have chosen. But the people put away their strange gods, and God’s soul was grieved for their misery. The Ammonites gathered for war, and Gilead had no leader.
Now, in chapter eleven, we meet Jephthah. This is one of the saddest stories in the Bible. Listen for how Jephthah speaks of God. And listen for the vow that brings him so much sorrow.
Jephthah is a mighty man of valour, from Gilead. But his mother was not his father’s wife. When his father’s other sons grow up, they drive Jephthah out. You will not inherit anything in our father’s house, they say, for you are the son of another woman.
So Jephthah runs away, and lives in the land of Tob. And a band of rough, restless men gather around him.
Then the Ammonites make war against Israel. And the elders of Gilead go to fetch Jephthah. Come and be our captain, they say, so that we can fight the Ammonites.
Jephthah answers, did you not hate me, and drive me out of my father’s house? Why have you come to me now that you are in trouble?
The elders say, that is why we have come back to you. Come with us, and be our head over everyone in Gilead.
So Jephthah agrees. If I fight the Ammonites, he says, and the LORD gives them to me, will I really be your head? And they promise, the LORD be witness between us. So Jephthah goes with them, and speaks all his words before the LORD at Mizpeh.
The man rejected by his own family becomes the leader of his people. God often lifts up those whom others have pushed aside.
Before he fights, Jephthah tries to make peace. He sends messengers to the king of Ammon. Why have you come to fight against me in my land?
The king says, because Israel took my land when they came up out of Egypt. Give it back peacefully.
Jephthah answers with a careful history lesson. Israel did not take the land of Moab or Ammon, he says. When Israel came out of Egypt, they asked permission to pass through Edom and Moab, and were refused, so they went around. Then Sihon, king of the Amorites, attacked Israel. And the LORD God of Israel gave Sihon into Israel’s hand. The land was the Amorites’ land, not yours. And Israel has lived there for a very long time. Why did you not take it back before?
And he ends with these words.
But the king of Ammon will not listen.
Then the Spirit of the LORD comes upon Jephthah, and he goes out against the Ammonites.
And then Jephthah makes a vow.
The LORD gives the Ammonites into Jephthah’s hand, and Israel wins a great victory.
Then Jephthah comes home to Mizpeh. And out of his door comes his daughter, to meet him, with tambourines and dancing. She is his only child. He has no other son or daughter.
And when he sees her, he tears his clothes.
His daughter answers with astonishing courage. My father, if you have opened your mouth to the LORD, do to me what you have said, since the LORD has given you victory over your enemies.
She asks for one thing. Let me go for two months, up and down the mountains with my friends, and mourn that I will never marry. He says, go. And she goes with her friends, and mourns on the mountains.
At the end of two months she comes back to her father, and he does with her as he had vowed. The writer adds that she never married. And it became a custom in Israel that the daughters of Israel went every year to remember the daughter of Jephthah.
What exactly happened to her? Christians have understood this in different ways. Some believe that Jephthah offered her as he had said, a terrible act that God had forbidden in his law. Others believe that she was given to the LORD in a different way, to serve him all her life, and never to marry or have children, which is why she mourned that she would never marry.
Either way, it is a deep sorrow. A brave young woman, and a father whose careless words cost him what he loved most. God had never asked Jephthah for a vow. God had already given his Spirit. Jephthah did not need to bargain.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 27. 'Wherefore I have not sinned against thee, but thou doest me wrong to war against me: the LORD the Judge be judge this day between the children of Israel and the children of Ammon.' The LORD the Judge. Jephthah knew who the real judge was. Not Jephthah, nor the king of Ammon, but the LORD. In this book full of judges, this is the only time the word is used of God himself. All human judges fail. Only the LORD judges with perfect justice.
Verse 30. 'And Jephthah vowed a vow unto the LORD, and said, If thou shalt without fail deliver the children of Ammon into mine hands,' The Spirit of the LORD had already come upon him. God had already promised to be with his people. But Jephthah tried to bargain, as people did with the gods of the nations. God does not need to be bribed or bargained with. He gives freely, out of his love.
Verse 35. 'And it came to pass, when he saw her, that he rent his clothes, and said, Alas, my daughter! thou hast brought me very low, and thou art one of them that trouble me: for I have opened my mouth unto the LORD, and I cannot go back.' I have opened my mouth unto the LORD. Words matter. A rash promise, made without thinking, can bring terrible sorrow. Jesus taught his followers to let their yes be yes, and their no be no, without dramatic oaths. Think before you speak, especially when you speak to God.
The Preacher wrote, 'Be not rash with thy mouth, and let not thine heart be hasty to utter any thing before God'. And Jesus said, 'But let your communication be, Yea, yea; Nay, nay'.
Have you made promises to God, or to others, without thinking them through? Ask God for wisdom with your words today. And if you know someone who has been pushed aside, like Jephthah, show them kindness.
Here is today’s question. Jephthah tried to bargain with God, when God had already given him his Spirit. Why do you think people try to make deals with God, and what does it show about how we see him?
Let us pray. Lord God, you are the Judge of all the earth, and you judge with perfect justice. Forgive me for careless words, and for the times I have tried to bargain with you. Thank you that you give freely, out of love. Set a guard over my mouth, and help me to trust you. Amen.
