A young man stands up in the synagogue of the town where he grew up. Everyone knows him. He is the carpenter’s son. They hand him the scroll of the prophet Isaiah, and he finds a particular place and reads it aloud. Then he rolls up the scroll, gives it back, and sits down. Every eye in the room is fixed on him. And he says, 'This day is this scripture fulfilled in your ears.'
In chapter three, John the Baptist called the crowds to turn back to God, and Jesus was baptized. The Holy Ghost came down on him like a dove, and the Father said, thou art my beloved Son. Luke then traced his family line all the way back to Adam.
Now, in chapter four, Jesus begins his work. But first, the Spirit leads him into the wilderness to be tested. Then he returns to Galilee in the power of the Spirit, and tells his own town what he has come to do.
Full of the Holy Ghost, Jesus is led into the wilderness, and for forty days he is tempted by the devil. He eats nothing, and afterward he is hungry.
The devil says, if you are the Son of God, tell this stone to become bread. Jesus answers with Scripture. Then the devil shows him all the kingdoms of the world in a moment of time. All this power and glory I will give you, he says, if you will worship me. Jesus answers, 'Get thee behind me, Satan: for it is written, Thou shalt worship the Lord thy God, and him only shalt thou serve.' Then the devil takes him to Jerusalem, to the highest point of the temple. Throw yourself down, he says, for it is written that God will send his angels to catch you. Jesus answers, 'It is said, Thou shalt not tempt the Lord thy God.' Every time, Jesus answers with the word of God. And the devil leaves him 'for a season'.
Jesus returns to Galilee in the power of the Spirit, and news of him spreads everywhere. He comes to Nazareth, where he grew up, and on the sabbath he goes to the synagogue, as he always did. He stands up to read, and they hand him the book of the prophet Isaiah.
He reads about one anointed by the Spirit of the Lord, to bring good news to the poor, and freedom, and sight, and healing. Then he sits down and says that this Scripture has been fulfilled that very day.
At first everyone speaks well of him, amazed at his gracious words. But then they begin to say, 'Is not this Joseph’s son?' Jesus knows they want to see miracles like the ones he did in Capernaum. 'No prophet is accepted in his own country.' He reminds them that in Elijah’s day, God sent the prophet to a widow in a foreign town, and in Elisha’s day, the only leper healed was Naaman the Syrian. God’s grace was going to outsiders.
When they hear this, they are filled with rage. They drive him out of the town, to the edge of the hill it was built on, to throw him off. 'But he passing through the midst of them went his way'.
Jesus goes down to Capernaum, and teaches there on the sabbath, and the people are astonished, 'for his word was with power.' A man with an unclean spirit cries out, 'I know thee who thou art; the Holy One of God.' Jesus commands the spirit to come out, and it does. Then Jesus goes to Simon’s house, where Simon’s mother-in-law is sick with a great fever. He stands over her and rebukes the fever, and it leaves her, and she gets up at once and serves them. As the day ends, people bring all their sick to him, and he lays his hands on every one of them and heals them.
The next day, he goes to a quiet place, and the crowds come looking for him and try to keep him from leaving. But Jesus tells them why he must go on.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 4. 'And Jesus answered him, saying, It is written, That man shall not live by bread alone, but by every word of God.' Jesus was truly hungry. The devil’s offer seemed small and reasonable. But Jesus would not use his power for himself outside his Father’s will. And he shows us how to fight temptation: not with clever arguments, but with the word of God. We need bread to live. We need God’s word even more.
Verse 18. 'The Spirit of the Lord is upon me, because he hath anointed me to preach the gospel to the poor; he hath sent me to heal the brokenhearted, to preach deliverance to the captives, and recovering of sight to the blind, to set at liberty them that are bruised,' This is Jesus’ own description of his work. Notice who it is for: the poor, the brokenhearted, the captives, the blind, the bruised. Jesus did not come for people who have it all together. He came for people who know they need him. Read the rest of Luke with this verse in mind. You will see him do every part of it.
Verse 43. 'And he said unto them, I must preach the kingdom of God to other cities also: for therefore am I sent.' The people of Capernaum wanted to keep Jesus for themselves. But he knew he was sent for more people than one town. The good news of God’s kingdom was meant to keep moving: to other cities, and one day to the whole world. That is still true.
The words Jesus read in Nazareth come from Isaiah chapter sixty-one, written long before, about one the Spirit of the LORD would anoint 'to proclaim liberty to the captives'. In the time of Moses, every fiftieth year was to be a year of jubilee, when debts were cancelled and slaves set free. Jesus announced that God’s great year of freedom had arrived in him.
Which words from Isaiah describe you today: poor, brokenhearted, captive, blind, or bruised? Jesus came for you. And when temptation comes, do what Jesus did. Answer it with what God has said.
Here is today’s question. Jesus said the Spirit of the Lord was upon him to heal the brokenhearted and set the captives free. Where do you need his freedom or healing today?
Let us pray. Lord Jesus, anointed by the Spirit, you came for the poor, the brokenhearted and the captive. Come to me where I need you most. Teach me to live by every word of God, and to answer temptation with your word. Amen.
