A fisherman is washing his nets on the shore. He and his partners have worked hard and caught nothing, not a single fish. He is tired, and he wants to go home. Then a teacher steps into his boat and asks him to push out a little from the land. And when the teaching is done, the teacher tells him to do something no fisherman would ever suggest: go back out, into the deep water, and let the nets down again.
In chapter four, Jesus was tempted in the wilderness, read from Isaiah in Nazareth and was driven out of his home town, then healed many people in Capernaum, including the mother-in-law of a man named Simon. Now, in chapter five, Jesus begins to call people to follow him, and his authority begins to raise questions.
Watch for two kinds of people in this chapter: those who know they are sinners and come close to Jesus, and those who think they are not, and stand back and complain.
Jesus is standing by the lake of Gennesaret, another name for the sea of Galilee, and the crowd is pressing in to hear the word of God. He sees two boats by the shore, gets into the one that belongs to Simon, and asks him to push out a little. Then he sits down and teaches the people from the boat.
When he has finished speaking, he says to Simon, 'Launch out into the deep, and let down your nets for a draught.' A draught means a catch. Simon answers.
They let down the nets, and catch so many fish that the nets begin to break. They signal to their partners in the other boat to come and help, and they fill both boats so full that they begin to sink.
When Simon Peter sees it, he falls down at Jesus’ knees. He and everyone with him are astonished, including his partners James and John, the sons of Zebedee. And Jesus says to Simon, 'Fear not; from henceforth thou shalt catch men.' When they bring their boats to land, they forsake everything, and follow him.
In one of the towns, a man 'full of leprosy' falls on his face before Jesus. 'Lord, if thou wilt, thou canst make me clean.' Jesus touches him: 'I will: be thou clean.' And the leprosy leaves him. News of Jesus spreads further, and great crowds come to hear him and be healed. But Luke adds, 'And he withdrew himself into the wilderness, and prayed.' The busier Jesus became, the more he made time to be alone with his Father.
One day, as Jesus is teaching, Pharisees and teachers of the law have come from every town. Some men bring a paralysed man on a bed, but they cannot get through the crowd. So they go up on the housetop and let him down through the tiling, right in front of Jesus. When Jesus sees their faith, he says, 'Man, thy sins are forgiven thee.' The scribes and Pharisees think, who can forgive sins but God alone? Jesus answers their thoughts, and to show that he has power on earth to forgive sins, he tells the man to rise, take up his bed, and go home. The man gets up at once, glorifying God. Everyone is amazed, and they say, 'We have seen strange things to day.'
Then Jesus sees a tax collector named Levi sitting at his tax booth, and says, 'Follow me.' 'And he left all, rose up, and followed him.' Levi throws a great feast for Jesus in his house, with many tax collectors and others. The Pharisees and scribes complain: why do you eat and drink with tax collectors and sinners? And Jesus answers them.
They also ask why his disciples do not fast. Jesus speaks of wedding guests, and of new wine that needs new wineskins. And Luke alone adds a gentle, true saying about people who prefer the familiar: no one who has drunk old wine wants new, because he says, 'The old is better.'
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 5. 'And Simon answering said unto him, Master, we have toiled all the night, and have taken nothing: nevertheless at thy word I will let down the net.' Simon was the expert fisherman, and Jesus was a carpenter’s son. Everything Simon knew said this was pointless. But he said, nevertheless at thy word. That word nevertheless is where faith begins: I do not see how, and I am tired, but because you say so, I will.
Verse 8. 'When Simon Peter saw it, he fell down at Jesus’ knees, saying, Depart from me; for I am a sinful man, O Lord.' Strange. Peter has just received the biggest catch of his life, and he asks Jesus to leave. When he saw the power and holiness of Jesus up close, he suddenly saw himself clearly too. But Jesus did not depart. He said, fear not, and called Peter to follow him. Jesus does not choose people who are good enough. He chooses people who know they are not.
Verse 32. 'I came not to call the righteous, but sinners to repentance.' The Pharisees thought Jesus was keeping bad company. Jesus said that sinners are exactly the people he came for, like a doctor who goes to the sick. To repent means to turn around, to come back to God. The only people Jesus cannot help are those who think they do not need him.
Long before, the prophet Isaiah saw the LORD high and lifted up in the temple, and cried, 'Woe is me! for I am undone; because I am a man of unclean lips'. Peter’s words in the boat are the same cry. And in both stories, God did not send the trembling man away. He cleansed him, and then he sent him out to serve.
Is there something you have toiled at and given up on? Bring it to Jesus. And if he tells you to try again, or to go deeper, answer like Peter: nevertheless at thy word. And remember Jesus’ habit: no matter how busy, make time to be alone with God.
Here is today’s question. Peter said, nevertheless at thy word I will let down the net. What has Jesus asked of you that you will do today, even if you do not see how it will work?
Let us pray. Lord Jesus, I am a sinful person, and yet you call me to follow you. Thank you that you came for sinners like me. Give me faith to obey your word even when I am tired and cannot see the way. Nevertheless, at your word, I will. Amen.
