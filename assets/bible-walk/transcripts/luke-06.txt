Jesus goes up into a mountain alone, and he stays there, praying to God, until the sky grows light again. He does not sleep. He talks with his Father. And when he comes down, he calls his followers together and chooses twelve of them. Before every big decision in Luke’s Gospel, Jesus prays.
In chapter five, Jesus called Peter, James and John from their fishing boats, healed a leper and a paralysed man, and called Levi the tax collector to follow him. The religious leaders began to complain that he ate with sinners.
Now, in chapter six, the complaints grow into anger. Then Jesus chooses his twelve apostles and teaches the crowds in one of the greatest sermons ever preached. Luke tells it as a sermon on a level place, a plain, with crowds from all around.
On a sabbath, Jesus and his disciples walk through fields of grain, and the disciples pick some heads, rub them in their hands, and eat. Some Pharisees ask why they are doing what is not lawful on the sabbath. Jesus reminds them how David ate the holy bread when he was hungry, and says, 'the Son of man is Lord also of the sabbath.'
On another sabbath, in the synagogue, there is a man whose right hand is withered. The scribes and Pharisees watch to see if Jesus will heal him, so they can accuse him. Jesus knows their thoughts. He asks them whether it is lawful on the sabbath to do good, or to do evil, to save life or to destroy it. Then he tells the man to stretch out his hand, and it is made whole. And the leaders are 'filled with madness', and discuss what they might do to Jesus.
Then Jesus goes up into the mountain to pray. When the sky grows light, he calls his disciples and chooses twelve, whom he names apostles: Simon whom he names Peter, and Andrew his brother, James and John, Philip and Bartholomew, Matthew and Thomas, James the son of Alphaeus, Simon called Zelotes, Judas the brother of James, and Judas Iscariot, 'which also was the traitor.'
He comes down and stands on a level place with a great crowd, from Judaea and Jerusalem and the coast of Tyre and Sidon. They come to hear him and to be healed, and all try to touch him, 'for there went virtue out of him, and healed them all.'
Then he looks at his disciples, and begins. 'Blessed be ye poor: for yours is the kingdom of God.' Blessed are you who hunger now, for you will be filled. Blessed are you who weep now, for you will laugh. Blessed are you when people hate you for the Son of man’s sake. But woe to you who are rich, for you have already received your comfort. Woe to you who are full now, and who laugh now.
'Love your enemies, do good to them which hate you'. Bless those who curse you, and pray for those who mistreat you. If someone strikes you on one cheek, offer the other. 'And as ye would that men should do to you, do ye also to them likewise.' Anyone can love those who love them; even sinners do that. But love your enemies, do good, and lend, expecting nothing back, and you will be the children of the Highest, for he is kind to the unthankful and to the evil.
Judge not, and you will not be judged. Forgive, and you will be forgiven. Give, and it will be given to you, 'good measure, pressed down, and shaken together, and running over'.
Jesus asks why we look at the speck of dust in our brother’s eye but do not notice the beam in our own. A good tree brings forth good fruit, he says, and 'of the abundance of the heart his mouth speaketh.'
And he ends with a picture. Whoever hears my words and does them is like a man who dug deep and laid his foundation on a rock. When the flood came, the house stood firm. But whoever hears and does not do them is like a man who built on the earth with no foundation, and when the flood came, it fell, and the ruin was great.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 12. 'And it came to pass in those days, that he went out into a mountain to pray, and continued all night in prayer to God.' The Son of God spent those long hours in prayer before choosing the twelve. If Jesus needed to pray like that before a big decision, how much more do we? Prayer is not what we do when everything else fails. For Jesus it came first. Before your next big decision, take time to pray, and listen.
Verse 36. 'Be ye therefore merciful, as your Father also is merciful.' This is the heart of the sermon. Why love enemies, give without expecting anything back, and refuse to judge? Because that is what our Father is like. He is kind even to the unthankful and the evil. God’s children are meant to look like their Father. We can show mercy because we have been shown so much mercy ourselves.
Verse 46. 'And why call ye me, Lord, Lord, and do not the things which I say?' Lord means master. It is easy to call Jesus Lord with our lips. Jesus asks us to call him Lord with our lives. That is the difference between the two builders. Both heard the same words. Only one dug deep and built on them, and only that house stood when the flood came.
Long before, God showed his glory to Moses on the mountain and proclaimed his own name: 'The LORD, The LORD God, merciful and gracious, longsuffering, and abundant in goodness and truth'. When Jesus says, be merciful as your Father is merciful, he is calling us to reflect the very character God revealed to Moses.
Think of one person who has wronged you, or who is hard to love. Do one good thing for them today, or pray for them by name. And look at your foundations. Are you only hearing Jesus’ words, or building your life on them?
Here is today’s question. Jesus said, be ye therefore merciful, as your Father also is merciful. Who will you show mercy to today?
Let us pray. Father, you are merciful and kind, even to the unthankful. Make me merciful like you. Help me to love my enemies, to give without counting, and to stop judging others. Let me not only call Jesus Lord, but do what he says, and build my life on his words. Amen.
