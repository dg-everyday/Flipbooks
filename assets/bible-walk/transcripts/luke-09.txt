Jesus is walking north in Galilee with his friends. Crowds are following him everywhere. He has fed thousands, healed the sick and raised the dead. He could stay in Galilee and be loved. But Luke tells us that, when the time came, he turned his face toward Jerusalem, where he knew he would be rejected and killed. And he did not turn back.
In chapter eight, Jesus told the parable of the sower, calmed a storm, set a man free from a legion of devils, and raised Jairus’ daughter. Chapter nine is a turning point in Luke’s Gospel. In the first half, people keep asking, who is this? In this chapter, Peter answers. And at the end of the chapter, Jesus begins the long journey to Jerusalem that fills the next ten chapters of Luke.
Jesus gives the twelve power and authority over devils and diseases, and sends them out to preach the kingdom of God and to heal. Take nothing for the journey, he says. They go from town to town, preaching and healing everywhere. King Herod hears about it and is perplexed. Some say John the Baptist has risen from the dead. Herod says, I beheaded John; 'but who is this, of whom I hear such things?'
When the apostles return, Jesus takes them to a quiet place near Bethsaida. But the crowds follow, and Jesus welcomes them, speaks to them of the kingdom of God, and heals those who need healing. As the day wears on, the twelve say, send the crowd away to find food and lodging. Jesus says, 'Give ye them to eat.' They have only five loaves and two fishes, and there are about five thousand men. Jesus has them sit down in groups of fifty, looks up to heaven, blesses the food, breaks it, and gives it to the disciples to set before the crowd. Everyone eats and is filled, and twelve baskets of pieces are left over.
Then, as Jesus is praying alone with his disciples, he asks, 'Whom say the people that I am?' Some say John the Baptist, others Elias, others one of the old prophets risen again. 'But whom say ye that I am?' And Peter answers. Jesus tells them to tell no one, and says that the Son of man must suffer many things, be rejected, be killed, and be raised the third day. And he tells them what it means to follow him.
About eight days later, Jesus takes Peter, John and James up a mountain to pray. And as he prays, his face changes, and his clothes become white and glistening. Moses and Elias appear in glory and speak with him 'of his decease which he should accomplish at Jerusalem.' Decease means his departure, his death. Peter, hardly knowing what he is saying, offers to build three shelters. Then a cloud overshadows them, and a voice comes out of the cloud: 'This is my beloved Son: hear him.'
The next day, a man in the crowd begs Jesus to help his only son, who is seized by a spirit. The disciples could not cast it out. Jesus rebukes the spirit, heals the child, and gives him back to his father, and everyone is amazed at the mighty power of God.
Again Jesus tells his disciples he will be delivered into the hands of men. But they do not understand, and they are afraid to ask. Instead they argue about which of them will be the greatest. Jesus takes a child, sets him beside him, and says, 'he that is least among you all, the same shall be great.'
Then comes the turning point of the book.
On the way, a Samaritan village refuses to receive Jesus, because he is heading for Jerusalem. James and John ask, Lord, shall we call down fire from heaven on them? Jesus turns and rebukes them: 'For the Son of man is not come to destroy men’s lives, but to save them.'
And as they walk, some people say they will follow him. Jesus is honest about the cost. Foxes have holes, and birds have nests, but the Son of man has nowhere to lay his head. To another who wants to go home first, he says, 'No man, having put his hand to the plough, and looking back, is fit for the kingdom of God.'
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 20. 'He said unto them, But whom say ye that I am? Peter answering said, The Christ of God.' Notice that Jesus asks this as they are praying together. The crowds had many opinions about Jesus. But Jesus wanted to know what his own friends believed. The Christ of God means God’s anointed King, the one all the prophets promised. Every person who hears about Jesus must one day answer this same question for themselves.
Verse 23. 'And he said to them all, If any man will come after me, let him deny himself, and take up his cross daily, and follow me.' Only Luke includes the word daily. Following Jesus is not one big decision made once and then forgotten. It is a choice made every day: to say no to ourselves, and yes to him. Each day, take up your cross. Each day, follow.
Verse 51. 'And it came to pass, when the time was come that he should be received up, he stedfastly set his face to go to Jerusalem,' To be received up means to return to heaven, through his death and resurrection. Jesus knew exactly what was waiting in Jerusalem. And he set his face like someone who will not be turned aside. He walked toward the cross on purpose, for us.
Long before, Isaiah wrote of the servant of the LORD who would suffer: 'therefore have I set my face like a flint, and I know that I shall not be ashamed.' Flint is the hardest stone. When Luke says Jesus steadfastly set his face toward Jerusalem, he shows us that servant, determined to finish the work his Father gave him.
Jesus’ call is daily. Ask yourself right now what it would mean to deny yourself and take up your cross today. Is there something you need to stop looking back at?
Here is today’s question. Jesus said, take up his cross daily, and follow me. What will it mean for you to follow Jesus today, in one specific way?
Let us pray. Lord Jesus, you are the Christ of God. Thank you that you set your face toward Jerusalem for me, and did not turn back. Help me to deny myself, take up my cross today, and follow you without looking back. Amen.
