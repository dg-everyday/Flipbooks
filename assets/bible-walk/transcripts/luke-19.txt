A crowd is lining the street in Jericho, everyone pushing to see Jesus pass by. At the back is a short man who cannot see over anyone’s head. He is rich, and everyone hates him, because he is the chief tax collector. So he does something no respectable man would do. He runs ahead and climbs a tree. And when Jesus reaches that tree, he stops, looks up, and calls the man by name.
In chapter eighteen, Jesus told of the tax collector who prayed, God be merciful to me a sinner, and he gave sight to a blind beggar near Jericho. Now, in chapter nineteen, the long journey that began in chapter nine comes to its end. Jesus passes through Jericho, tells one last parable on the road, and then rides into Jerusalem. This chapter contains the verse that sums up the whole book of Luke.
In Jericho there is a man named Zacchaeus, the chief among the tax collectors, and he is rich. He wants to see who Jesus is, but he cannot, because of the crowd, and because he is little of stature, which means short. So he runs ahead and climbs a sycomore tree.
When Jesus comes to the place, he looks up and speaks to him. And Zacchaeus hurries down and welcomes him joyfully.
But everyone who sees it begins to grumble. He has gone to be the guest of a man who is a sinner! And Zacchaeus stands up and says to the Lord, 'Behold, Lord, the half of my goods I give to the poor; and if I have taken any thing from any man by false accusation, I restore him fourfold.' Fourfold means four times as much. Jesus says, 'This day is salvation come to this house, forsomuch as he also is a son of Abraham.' And then Jesus says why he came.
Because they are near Jerusalem, and the people think the kingdom of God is about to appear at once, Jesus tells them a parable. A nobleman goes into a far country to receive a kingdom, and then to return. Before he leaves, he gives ten servants a pound each, a sum of money, and tells them, 'Occupy till I come.' Occupy means do business, put it to work. But his citizens hate him and say, we will not have this man reign over us.
When he returns as king, he calls his servants. The first says, Lord, your pound has gained ten pounds. 'Well, thou good servant: because thou hast been faithful in a very little, have thou authority over ten cities.' The second has gained five, and is given five cities. But another brings back the pound wrapped in a napkin. I was afraid of you, he says, because you are a hard man. The king takes the pound from him and gives it to the one who has ten. God’s servants are given gifts to use while they wait for their King to return.
Then Jesus goes on ahead, up to Jerusalem. Near the Mount of Olives, he sends two disciples to bring a colt that no one has ever ridden. If anyone asks, say, the Lord hath need of him. They throw their clothes on the colt and set Jesus on it, and as he rides, people spread their clothes on the road. As he comes down the Mount of Olives, the whole crowd of disciples begins to praise God with loud voices for all the mighty works they have seen: 'Blessed be the King that cometh in the name of the Lord: peace in heaven, and glory in the highest.'
Some of the Pharisees in the crowd say, Master, rebuke your disciples. And Jesus answers them.
Then, as he comes near and sees the city, he weeps over it. If you had only known, even now, the things that would bring you peace! But now they are hidden from your eyes. He says that days will come when enemies will surround the city and tear it down, 'because thou knewest not the time of thy visitation.' God had come to visit them, and they did not recognise him.
Jesus goes into the temple and begins to drive out those who buy and sell there. 'My house is the house of prayer: but ye have made it a den of thieves.' He teaches every day in the temple. The chief priests and scribes look for a way to destroy him, but they cannot find one, 'for all the people were very attentive to hear him.'
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 5. 'And when Jesus came to the place, he looked up, and saw him, and said unto him, Zacchaeus, make haste, and come down; for to day I must abide at thy house.' Jesus knew his name. Of all the people in that crowd, Jesus chose to stay with the most hated man in town. And he said, I must, as if it were part of his mission. Zacchaeus did not have to clean up his life before Jesus would come to his house. Jesus came first, and Zacchaeus’ life changed after.
Verse 10. 'For the Son of man is come to seek and to save that which was lost.' This is the key verse of the whole book of Luke. It explains everything we have seen: the shepherd going after one sheep, the father running to his son, Jesus eating with sinners, touching lepers, and welcoming outsiders. Jesus did not come for people who had it all together. He came looking for the lost. And he is still looking.
Verse 40. 'And he answered and said unto them, I tell you that, if these should hold their peace, the stones would immediately cry out.' To hold their peace means to be quiet. The Pharisees wanted the praise to stop. Jesus said it could not be stopped. The King had come. If people would not praise him, the very stones of the road would. All creation knows who Jesus is.
The crowd’s words come from a psalm sung by pilgrims going up to Jerusalem: 'Blessed be he that cometh in the name of the LORD'. They had sung it many times. On this day, the one it described was riding down the road in front of them.
Like Zacchaeus, is there anything keeping you from seeing Jesus clearly? Do whatever it takes to get close to him. And remember he knows your name. Then ask yourself what Zacchaeus asked: what needs to change in my life now that Jesus has come?
Here is today’s question. Jesus said, the Son of man is come to seek and to save that which was lost. How has Jesus found you, and who do you know who still needs to be found?
Let us pray. Lord Jesus, thank you that you came to seek and to save the lost, and that you know my name. Come into my house, and change whatever needs to change. Help me use the gifts you have given me faithfully until you come. Blessed be the King that cometh in the name of the Lord. Amen.
