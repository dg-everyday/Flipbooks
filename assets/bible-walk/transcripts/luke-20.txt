Spies are sent to listen to Jesus, pretending to be honest men. They come with a question so carefully built that any answer will trap him. Should we pay taxes to Caesar or not? If he says yes, the people will turn against him. If he says no, the Romans will arrest him. Jesus looks at them, and asks for a coin.
In chapter nineteen, Jesus brought salvation to the house of Zacchaeus, rode into Jerusalem as the crowds praised the King, wept over the city, and cleared the temple. Now he is teaching every day in the temple courts, and the leaders are looking for a way to destroy him. In chapter twenty, one group after another comes with a question meant to trap him: the chief priests and elders, then spies, then the Sadducees. And each time, Jesus answers with wisdom that leaves them silent.
As Jesus teaches the people and preaches the gospel in the temple, the chief priests, the scribes and the elders confront him. 'Tell us, by what authority doest thou these things?' Jesus answers with a question: the baptism of John, was it from heaven, or of men? They discuss it among themselves. If we say from heaven, he will ask why we did not believe John. If we say of men, all the people will stone us, for they are sure John was a prophet. So they answer, we cannot tell. And Jesus says, neither will I tell you by what authority I do these things.
Then he tells the people a parable. A man plants a vineyard, rents it to tenant farmers, and goes away for a long time. At harvest he sends a servant to collect some of the fruit, but the tenants beat him and send him away empty. He sends another, and a third, and they wound them all. Then the owner says, 'What shall I do? I will send my beloved son: it may be they will reverence him when they see him.' But when the tenants see the son, they reason among themselves, 'This is the heir: come, let us kill him, that the inheritance may be ours.' They throw him out of the vineyard and kill him.
What will the owner do? He will come and destroy those tenants, and give the vineyard to others. When the people hear it, they say, 'God forbid.' Then Jesus looks at them and asks about a psalm. The chief priests and scribes know he has spoken this parable against them, and they want to seize him at once, but they are afraid of the people.
So they send spies, who pretend to be righteous, to catch him in his words. Master, they say, we know you teach the way of God truly. 'Is it lawful for us to give tribute unto Caesar, or no?' Jesus sees their cunning. 'Shew me a penny. Whose image and superscription hath it?' They answer, 'Caesar’s.' And Jesus answers them. They cannot catch him in front of the people. They marvel at his answer, and fall silent.
Then some Sadducees come, who say there is no resurrection. They tell a story about a woman who married seven brothers, one after another, as each died without children. In the resurrection, whose wife will she be? Jesus says that those who are counted worthy of the world to come and the resurrection do not marry. They cannot die any more; they are like the angels, and are the children of God, 'being the children of the resurrection.' And even Moses showed that the dead are raised, when at the bush he called the Lord the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob.
Some of the scribes say, 'Master, thou hast well said.' And after that, no one dares to ask him any more questions.
Then Jesus asks them a question. How can they say that the Christ is David’s son, when David himself, in the book of Psalms, calls him Lord? 'The LORD said unto my Lord, Sit thou on my right hand'. How then is he his son?
And in the hearing of all the people, Jesus warns his disciples: beware of the scribes, who love to walk in long robes, love greetings in the market and the best seats, and who devour widows’ houses, and for a show make long prayers. 'the same shall receive greater damnation.'
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 17. 'And he beheld them, and said, What is this then that is written, The stone which the builders rejected, the same is become the head of the corner?' The builders were the leaders of Israel. The rejected stone was Jesus. They were about to throw him away, as the tenants threw out the beloved son. But God would make that rejected stone the most important stone of all, the cornerstone that holds the whole building together.
Verse 25. 'And he said unto them, Render therefore unto Caesar the things which be Caesar’s, and unto God the things which be God’s.' To render means to give back. The coin had Caesar’s image on it, so give it back to him. Pay what you owe to those who govern. But there is a deeper truth here. What bears God’s image? We do. Genesis says God made people in his own image. So give God what bears his image: give him yourself.
Verse 38. 'For he is not a God of the dead, but of the living: for all live unto him.' God said to Moses, I am the God of Abraham, long after Abraham had died. Not, I was. Jesus says this shows that Abraham, Isaac and Jacob are alive to God. Those who belong to God do not stop existing when they die. All live unto him. This is a great comfort for everyone who has lost someone who trusted in God.
The words Jesus pointed to come from the burning bush, when God said to Moses, 'I am the God of thy father, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob.' The Sadducees accepted the books of Moses, so Jesus answered them from those very books. The God who spoke from the bush is the God of the living.
Ask yourself Jesus’ question about the coin: whose image do I bear? Give back to God what is his today: your time, your heart, yourself. And if you are grieving someone who trusted God, take comfort: he is not the God of the dead, but of the living.
Here is today’s question. Jesus said, render unto God the things which be God’s. What part of yourself will you give back to God today?
Let us pray. Lord God, you are not the God of the dead, but of the living. Thank you that all live unto you. I bear your image; I give myself back to you. Thank you for Jesus, the stone the builders rejected, who has become the head of the corner. Amen.
