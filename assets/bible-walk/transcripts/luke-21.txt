Jesus is sitting in the temple, watching people put their gifts into the offering box. Rich people drop in large gifts. Then a poor widow comes, and drops in two mites, the smallest coins there were, worth almost nothing. And Jesus says that she has given more than all of them.
In chapter twenty, Jesus answered every trap the leaders set for him in the temple, about his authority, about Caesar’s coin, and about the resurrection, and warned the people about scribes who devour widows’ houses. Chapter twenty-one begins with a widow, and then Jesus looks ahead. He tells his disciples that the great temple will be destroyed, and teaches them how to live faithfully through hard times until he returns.
Christians have understood parts of what Jesus says here in different ways. Some of it came true when Jerusalem and its temple were destroyed, a generation later. Some of it still waits for the day of his return. But what Jesus asks of his followers is clear: do not be deceived, do not be afraid, endure, watch and pray.
Jesus looks up and sees the rich putting their gifts into the treasury. He also sees a poor widow putting in two mites. And he speaks.
Some people are admiring the temple, how it is adorned with beautiful stones and gifts. Jesus says the days will come when not one stone will be left on another. They ask, 'Master, but when shall these things be?' and what will be the sign?
Jesus says, 'Take heed that ye be not deceived'. Many will come in my name, saying, I am Christ, and the time draws near. Do not follow them. When you hear of wars and commotions, do not be terrified; these things must happen first, but the end is not yet. Nation will rise against nation. There will be great earthquakes, famines, plagues, and fearful sights and great signs from heaven.
But before all this, they will lay hands on you and persecute you. You will be brought before kings and rulers for my name’s sake. It will be your chance to testify. 'For I will give you a mouth and wisdom, which all your adversaries shall not be able to gainsay nor resist.' Even parents and brothers, relatives and friends will betray you, and some of you will be put to death. You will be hated by all for my name’s sake. 'But there shall not an hair of your head perish.' 'In your patience possess ye your souls.' By standing firm, you will win your lives.
When you see Jerusalem surrounded by armies, Jesus says, know that its destruction is near. Then let those in Judaea flee to the mountains. Jerusalem will be trodden down by the Gentiles, the nations, until the times of the Gentiles are fulfilled.
There will be signs in the sun, the moon and the stars, and on the earth distress of nations, with the sea and the waves roaring. People’s hearts will fail them for fear of what is coming. 'And then shall they see the Son of man coming in a cloud with power and great glory.'
Then Jesus gives them a parable. Look at the fig tree, and all the trees. When they put out their leaves, you know that summer is near. In the same way, when you see these things happen, know that the kingdom of God is near.
Take heed, he says, that your hearts are not weighed down with careless living, drunkenness and the cares of this life, so that that day comes upon you unexpectedly, like a trap. 'Watch ye therefore, and pray always'.
Every day Jesus teaches in the temple, and the people come early to hear him there.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 3. 'And he said, Of a truth I say unto you, that this poor widow hath cast in more than they all:' The rich gave out of their plenty. She gave out of her poverty, everything she had to live on. God does not measure a gift by its size, but by what it costs us and the love behind it. Notice too that Jesus saw her. No one else noticed. Your small, hidden faithfulness is seen by him.
Verse 28. 'And when these things begin to come to pass, then look up, and lift up your heads; for your redemption draweth nigh.' When the world is shaking and other people’s hearts are failing for fear, Jesus tells his followers to do the opposite: look up. Redemption means rescue, being set free. For those who belong to Jesus, the troubles of the world are not the end of the story. They are signs that their rescue is coming closer.
Verse 33. 'Heaven and earth shall pass away: but my words shall not pass away.' The disciples had been admiring the stones of the temple, which looked as if they would last for ever. Jesus says even heaven and earth will pass away. Buildings fall. Nations change. But every word Jesus has spoken will still stand. That is a firm foundation for our lives, in good times and in fearful ones.
Long before, Isaiah wrote, 'The grass withereth, the flower fadeth: but the word of our God shall stand for ever.' Jesus says the same about his own words. Only God can say that his words will outlast heaven and earth. Jesus said it of his.
Is your heart weighed down with the cares of this life? Bring them to God. When the news is frightening, remember Jesus’ words: look up, lift up your heads. And like the widow, give God something today that costs you, out of love.
Here is today’s question. Jesus said, look up, and lift up your heads; for your redemption draweth nigh. What fear will you bring to him today, and trade for hope?
Let us pray. Lord Jesus, your words will never pass away. When the world shakes and my heart is afraid, help me to look up and lift up my head. Keep me watchful and prayerful, and make me generous like the widow, who gave all she had. Amen.
