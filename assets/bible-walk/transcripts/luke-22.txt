Peter sits by a fire in a courtyard, warming his hands. Three times, someone looks at him and says, you were with Jesus. Three times he says, I do not know him. As he speaks the third time, a rooster crows. And Jesus, standing nearby under guard, turns and looks at Peter.
In chapter twenty-one, Jesus praised the poor widow, and taught his disciples to watch and pray, and to lift up their heads when trouble comes. Now the feast of the Passover draws near. It is the feast when Israel remembered how God set them free from Egypt. The chief priests want to kill Jesus, but they fear the people. Chapter twenty-two moves from a supper table, to a garden, to a courtyard. Watch the faithfulness of Jesus, and the failure of his friends.
Satan enters into Judas Iscariot, one of the twelve. Judas goes to the chief priests and offers to betray Jesus. They are glad, and promise him money. From then on, he looks for a chance to hand Jesus over when no crowd is near.
Jesus sends Peter and John to prepare the Passover meal. They find a large upper room, just as he told them.
When the hour comes, Jesus sits down with the apostles. He says, 'With desire I have desired to eat this passover with you before I suffer'. He takes bread, gives thanks, breaks it, and gives it to them. 'This is my body which is given for you: this do in remembrance of me.' After supper he takes the cup. 'This cup is the new testament in my blood, which is shed for you.' Testament here means covenant, the new covenant God promised through the prophet Jeremiah. Then Jesus says that the one who will betray him is at the table.
Even then, the disciples argue about which of them is the greatest. Jesus tells them, the kings of the nations rule over people. But you must not be like that. 'I am among you as he that serveth.'
Then Jesus turns to Peter. 'Simon, Simon, behold, Satan hath desired to have you, that he may sift you as wheat'. Peter says, Lord, I am ready to go with you to prison, and to death. Jesus answers that before the rooster crows that day, Peter will deny three times that he knows him.
They go out to the Mount of Olives, as Jesus often did. He tells the disciples, 'Pray that ye enter not into temptation.' He goes a short distance away, kneels down, and prays. An angel from heaven appears and strengthens him. Being in agony, he prays more earnestly, and his sweat falls like great drops of blood to the ground. Luke the doctor is the only Gospel writer who tells us this. When Jesus rises from prayer, he finds his disciples asleep, worn out with sorrow.
While he is still speaking, a crowd arrives, led by Judas. Judas comes near to kiss him. Jesus says, 'Judas, betrayest thou the Son of man with a kiss?' One of the disciples strikes the servant of the high priest, and cuts off his right ear. Jesus says, 'Suffer ye thus far', and he touches the man’s ear, and heals him. Even as he is arrested, Jesus heals one of the men who came to take him.
They lead him to the high priest’s house. Peter follows at a distance, and sits down by a fire in the courtyard. A servant girl looks closely at him and says, this man was with him. Peter says, 'Woman, I know him not.' A little later, another person says, you are one of them. Peter says, 'Man, I am not.' After about an hour, someone else insists, surely this man was with him, for he is from Galilee. Peter says, I do not know what you are saying. And while he is still speaking, the rooster crows.
Then Peter remembers the word of the Lord. He goes out, and weeps bitterly.
The men guarding Jesus mock him and beat him. They cover his eyes and say, prophesy, who struck you? Then the council of the elders meets, and asks him, 'Art thou then the Son of God?' He answers, 'Ye say that I am.' They say, we need no more witnesses. We have heard it from his own mouth.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 32. 'But I have prayed for thee, that thy faith fail not: and when thou art converted, strengthen thy brethren.' Jesus knew Peter would fall. And before it happened, Jesus prayed for him. Notice the word when, not if. When thou art converted means when you turn back. Jesus had already planned for Peter to be restored, and he gave him work to do: strengthen your brothers. Jesus prays for you too, even when you fall.
Verse 42. 'Saying, Father, if thou be willing, remove this cup from me: nevertheless not my will, but thine, be done.' The cup was the suffering ahead of him. Jesus was honest about his anguish. He asked if there was another way. And then he chose his Father’s will. This is the prayer that led him to the cross for us. It is also a prayer we can pray in our own hardest moments: not my will, but thine, be done.
Verse 61. 'And the Lord turned, and looked upon Peter. And Peter remembered the word of the Lord, how he had said unto him, Before the cock crow, thou shalt deny me thrice.' Only Luke tells us about this look. What was in the eyes of Jesus? Not anger, but sorrow and love. That look broke Peter’s heart, and he went out and wept. When we fail Jesus, he does not turn away from us. He looks at us with love, and that love calls us back.
At the table Jesus said, 'this that is written must yet be accomplished in me, And he was reckoned among the transgressors'. He was quoting Isaiah, who wrote of the suffering servant: 'he was numbered with the transgressors; and he bare the sin of many'. Jesus knew what was written about him, and he walked into it willingly.
Is there a cup in front of you, something hard you would rather avoid? Tell God honestly how you feel, and then pray the prayer of Jesus. And if you have failed him, like Peter, do not run away. Jesus has prayed for you, and he looks at you with love.
Here is today’s question. Jesus prayed, not my will, but thine, be done. Where in your life will you pray that prayer today?
Let us pray. Father, not my will, but thine, be done. Thank you, Lord Jesus, that you drank the cup for me, that you pray for me when I am weak, and that you look on me with love when I fail. Strengthen my faith, and help me to strengthen others. Amen.
