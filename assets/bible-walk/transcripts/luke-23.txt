Two criminals hang on crosses on either side of Jesus. One of them joins the crowd in mocking him: if you are the Christ, save yourself and us. But the other one tells him to be quiet. We deserve this, he says, but this man has done nothing wrong. Then he turns his head toward Jesus, and with almost his last breath he asks to be remembered.
In chapter twenty-two, Jesus ate the Passover with his disciples, prayed in agony on the Mount of Olives, not my will, but thine, be done, was betrayed with a kiss, and was denied three times by Peter. The council of elders condemned him for saying he was the Son of God.
Now, in chapter twenty-three, they bring him to the Roman governor, because only Rome could put a man to death. This is the day of the cross. Luke tells us things the other Gospels do not: Jesus before Herod, the women of Jerusalem, the criminal who believed, and three of the words Jesus spoke from the cross.
The whole council brings Jesus to Pilate. They accuse him of stirring up the nation, forbidding taxes to Caesar, and claiming to be a king. Pilate asks him, 'Art thou the King of the Jews?' Jesus answers, 'Thou sayest it.' And Pilate tells the chief priests and the people, 'I find no fault in this man.'
When Pilate hears that Jesus is from Galilee, he sends him to Herod, who rules Galilee and happens to be in Jerusalem. Herod is glad, because he has long wanted to see Jesus perform a miracle. He questions him at length, but Jesus answers him nothing. Herod and his soldiers mock him, dress him in a gorgeous robe, and send him back to Pilate. That same day, Pilate and Herod, who had been enemies, become friends.
Pilate calls the leaders together again. Neither I nor Herod have found anything worthy of death in this man, he says. I will punish him and let him go. But the whole crowd cries out, 'Away with this man, and release unto us Barabbas', a man in prison for rebellion and murder. Pilate speaks to them again, wanting to release Jesus. But they shout, 'Crucify him, crucify him.' A third time Pilate asks, 'Why, what evil hath he done?' But their voices prevail. Pilate releases Barabbas, and delivers Jesus to their will.
As they lead him away, they seize a man named Simon, from Cyrene, coming in from the country, and lay the cross on him to carry behind Jesus. A great crowd follows, including women who mourn and weep for him. Jesus turns to them: daughters of Jerusalem, weep not for me, but weep for yourselves and for your children.
Two criminals are led out with him to be put to death. When they come to the place called Calvary, they crucify him there, with one criminal on his right and the other on his left. And Jesus prays. They divide his clothes by casting lots.
The people stand watching. The rulers sneer, 'He saved others; let him save himself, if he be Christ, the chosen of God.' The soldiers mock him too. Above him is written in Greek, Latin and Hebrew, THIS IS THE KING OF THE JEWS.
One of the criminals rails at him. But the other rebukes him: do you not fear God? 'And we indeed justly; for we receive the due reward of our deeds: but this man hath done nothing amiss.' And he says to Jesus, 'Lord, remember me when thou comest into thy kingdom.' And Jesus answers him.
At the sixth hour, darkness covers all the earth until the ninth hour. The sun is darkened, and the veil of the temple is torn down the middle. And Jesus cries with a loud voice, and gives up the ghost.
When the centurion sees what has happened, he glorifies God, saying, 'Certainly this was a righteous man.' The crowds who came to watch go home beating their breasts. And all who knew Jesus, and the women who had followed him from Galilee, stand far off, watching.
A good and righteous man named Joseph, from Arimathaea, a member of the council who had not agreed with their decision, goes to Pilate and asks for the body of Jesus. He takes it down, wraps it in linen, and lays it in a tomb cut in stone, where no one had ever been laid. The women follow, see the tomb and how his body is laid, and go home to prepare spices. And on the sabbath they rest, according to the commandment.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 34. 'Then said Jesus, Father, forgive them; for they know not what they do. And they parted his raiment, and cast lots.' While the nails were going in, Jesus prayed for the people driving them. He had taught, love your enemies and pray for those who mistreat you. Here he does it, at the worst moment imaginable. If Jesus could forgive those who crucified him, there is no sin of ours too great for him to forgive.
Verse 43. 'And Jesus said unto him, Verily I say unto thee, To day shalt thou be with me in paradise.' The criminal had no time to do good works, no chance to make up for his life, nothing to offer but a request. Lord, remember me. And Jesus promised him far more than he asked: not only to remember him, but to have him in paradise, with him, that very day. Salvation is a gift, received by trusting Jesus, even at the very end.
Verse 46. 'And when Jesus had cried with a loud voice, he said, Father, into thy hands I commend my spirit: and having said thus, he gave up the ghost.' To commend means to entrust. Jesus’ last words were a prayer, and the first word was Father. He did not die in despair, but in trust. No one took his life from him. He gave it, placing himself in his Father’s hands.
Jesus’ last words came from a psalm of David: 'Into thine hand I commit my spirit: thou hast redeemed me, O LORD God of truth.' Many Jewish children were taught to pray those words at the end of the day. Jesus died praying the prayer of a trusting child.
Is there someone you find hard to forgive? Remember Jesus on the cross: Father, forgive them. Ask him to help you pray that prayer too. And like the criminal beside him, bring Jesus your simple request: Lord, remember me.
Here is today’s question. The criminal said, Lord, remember me, and Jesus promised him paradise. What will you say to Jesus today, as you stand at his cross?
Let us pray. Lord Jesus, you prayed for those who crucified you, and you welcomed a dying criminal into paradise. Forgive me, and remember me. Teach me to forgive others as you have forgiven me. Into your hands I commit my spirit. Amen.
