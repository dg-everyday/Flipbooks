Two friends are walking home from Jerusalem, heartbroken. The man they hoped would save Israel has been crucified. As they walk, a stranger joins them and asks what they are talking about. They stop, faces sad, and tell him. And then the stranger begins to explain the whole Bible to them, from Moses and all the prophets. They do not know it yet, but the stranger is Jesus.
In chapter twenty-three, Jesus was crucified between two criminals. He prayed, Father, forgive them, promised paradise to the man beside him, and committed his spirit into his Father’s hands. Joseph of Arimathaea laid his body in a new tomb, and the women saw where he was laid. Then came the sabbath, and everything was still.
Now it is the first day of the week. This is the last chapter of Luke, and the best news of all. And it ends where Luke’s Gospel began, in the temple at Jerusalem, with people praising God.
'very early in the morning', the women who had followed Jesus come to the tomb, bringing spices to anoint his body. They find the stone rolled away. They go in, and the body of the Lord Jesus is not there. While they stand there, perplexed, two men in shining clothes appear beside them, and the women bow their faces to the ground in fear. And the men speak. 'He is not here, but is risen: remember how he spake unto you when he was yet in Galilee'.
The women remember his words. They are Mary Magdalene, Joanna, Mary the mother of James, and others. They hurry back and tell the eleven disciples. But 'their words seemed to them as idle tales, and they believed them not.' Peter runs to the tomb, stoops down, sees the linen cloths lying by themselves, and goes home wondering.
That same day, two of the disciples are walking to a village called Emmaus, about seven miles from Jerusalem. As they talk, Jesus himself draws near and walks with them, but their eyes are kept from recognising him. One of them, named Cleopas, tells the stranger about Jesus of Nazareth, a mighty prophet, crucified; we had hoped he was the one who would redeem Israel. And now some women say his tomb is empty and angels told them he is alive.
Jesus answers, 'O fools, and slow of heart to believe all that the prophets have spoken'. 'Ought not Christ to have suffered these things, and to enter into his glory?' And he begins to explain.
They reach the village, and he acts as if he would go further. But they urge him, 'Abide with us: for it is toward evening, and the day is far spent.' At the table he takes bread, blesses it, breaks it, and gives it to them. And their eyes are opened, and they know him. And he vanishes from their sight. They say to each other, 'Did not our heart burn within us, while he talked with us by the way, and while he opened to us the scriptures?'
They rush back to Jerusalem and find the eleven saying, 'The Lord is risen indeed, and hath appeared to Simon.' While they are telling their story, Jesus himself stands among them and says, 'Peace be unto you.' They are terrified, thinking they see a spirit. He says, 'Behold my hands and my feet, that it is I myself: handle me, and see; for a spirit hath not flesh and bones, as ye see me have.' He even eats a piece of broiled fish in front of them.
Then he opens their understanding, so that they can understand the Scriptures: everything written about him in the law of Moses, the prophets and the psalms had to be fulfilled. Christ had to suffer and rise from the dead on the third day, and repentance and forgiveness of sins must be preached in his name among all nations, beginning at Jerusalem. 'And ye are witnesses of these things.' He tells them to wait in Jerusalem until they are clothed with power from on high.
Then he leads them out as far as Bethany, lifts up his hands, and blesses them. And while he blesses them, he is parted from them, and carried up into heaven.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 5. 'And as they were afraid, and bowed down their faces to the earth, they said unto them, Why seek ye the living among the dead?' The women came with spices for a dead body. They were looking in the right place for a dead man, but Jesus was not dead. Sometimes we look for Jesus as if he were only a great teacher from the past. But he is alive. We do not visit his memory. We meet him, risen and living.
Verse 27. 'And beginning at Moses and all the prophets, he expounded unto them in all the scriptures the things concerning himself.' To expound means to explain. On the road to Emmaus, Jesus himself took the two friends through the whole Old Testament and showed them that it was all about him: the promise in the garden, the lamb on the mountain, the Passover, the King of David’s line, the suffering servant. That is what The Living Voice has been trying to do on this walk. The whole Bible points to Jesus.
Verse 52. 'And they worshipped him, and returned to Jerusalem with great joy:' Their teacher had just left them, and yet they were full of great joy. Luke’s Gospel began with angels bringing good tidings of great joy, and it ends with the disciples filled with great joy, praising God continually in the temple. Jesus is risen, he reigns, and he has promised his Spirit. That is reason for joy that nothing can take away.
Luke wrote to Theophilus so that he might know the certainty of what he had been taught. And the risen Jesus himself said that everything written in the law of Moses, the prophets and the psalms had to be fulfilled. David had written, 'neither wilt thou suffer thine Holy One to see corruption.' The empty tomb was the promise of the whole Old Testament, kept.
Ask Jesus to open the Scriptures to you as he did on the road to Emmaus, and to make your heart burn. And like those two friends, who ran back to Jerusalem that very hour to tell the others, tell someone today that Jesus is alive.
Here is today’s question. Now that you have walked through the whole book of Luke, what has Jesus shown you about himself, and who will you tell?
Let us pray. Lord Jesus, you are not among the dead. You are risen indeed. Thank you for opening the Scriptures and showing me yourself on every page. Make my heart burn, fill me with your great joy, and make me your witness. Amen.
That is the end of our walk through the book of Luke. Well walked. Luke’s story continues in his second book, the Acts of the Apostles, where the disciples wait in Jerusalem, as Jesus told them, for power from on high.
