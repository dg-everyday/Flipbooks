In the middle of the book of Mark, Jesus stops his friends on a road and asks them a question: 'But whom say ye that I am?' Everyone in Mark has to answer that question. The crowds, the leaders, the soldiers, his own friends. By the time you reach the last chapter, you will have to answer it too.
Mark is the shortest of the four Gospels, the four books that tell the life of Jesus. It has sixteen chapters, and you could read the whole book in about an hour and a half. On Bible Walk it is sixteen steps, one chapter at a time.
Who wrote it? The book does not give its writer’s name, but the early Christians said it was John Mark. We meet him in the book of Acts. His mother had a house in Jerusalem where the believers met to pray. Acts calls it 'the house of Mary the mother of John, whose surname was Mark; where many were gathered together praying.' Mark travelled with Paul and Barnabas, and later he was close to Peter. Peter even calls him 'Marcus my son.' Early Christian writers said that Mark wrote down what Peter preached about Jesus. So as you read Mark, you may be hearing Peter’s memories.
Who was it written for? Mark explains things that Jewish readers would already know. He explains their customs, and when Jesus speaks in his own language, Mark translates it. When Jesus raises a little girl, Mark gives his very words, 'Talitha cumi', and then tells us what they mean: 'Damsel, I say unto thee, arise.' So Mark was writing for people far from the land of Israel, people meeting Jesus for the first time. That makes it a perfect place for us to start.
Mark’s book has four parts. In the first, John the Baptist prepares the way, and Jesus is baptized. In the second, Jesus works in Galilee, in the north. He heals the sick, calms a storm, and feeds thousands of people. In the third, Jesus and his friends travel to Jerusalem, and he begins to tell them that he must suffer and die. And in the fourth part, almost a third of the whole book, Mark slows right down and tells us one single week: Jesus coming into Jerusalem, the Passover meal with his friends, the cross, and the empty tomb.
Here are three things to watch for as we walk.
First, watch for the word straightway. It means at once, and Mark uses it again and again. Jesus is always moving. The book feels like running.
Second, watch for the question, who is this? When Jesus calms the storm, his friends are afraid and say, 'What manner of man is this, that even the wind and the sea obey him?' Keep asking it. Mark tells us the answer in his very first line, that Jesus is 'the Son of God', and at the cross a Roman soldier says it too: 'Truly this man was the Son of God.'
Third, watch how Jesus serves. Mark shows Jesus as a servant, always giving himself for others. If you remember one verse from the whole book, make it this one: 'For even the Son of man came not to be ministered unto, but to minister, and to give his life a ransom for many.'
Here is a question to carry with you. If someone asked you right now who Jesus is, what would you say? Hold on to your answer, and see how it grows over these sixteen chapters.
Let us pray. Lord Jesus, as I walk through the book of Mark, show me who you are. Let me see you heal, teach and serve, and help me to follow you. Amen.
