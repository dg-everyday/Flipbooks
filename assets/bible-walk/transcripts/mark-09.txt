A father has brought his son to be healed. Since the boy was a child, a spirit has thrown him to the ground, into fire and into water. The disciples tried to help, and they could not. Now the father stands in front of Jesus, worn out and almost hopeless, and says, 'if thou canst do any thing, have compassion on us, and help us.' Jesus answers, if thou canst believe. And the father cries out, with tears, the most honest prayer in the Bible.
In chapter eight, Peter said to Jesus, thou art the Christ. And then Jesus began to tell his friends that he must suffer, be killed, and rise again, and that anyone who follows him must take up a cross.
Chapter nine begins six days later. First, three of the disciples are given a glimpse of who Jesus really is, on a mountain. Then they come down to a crowd, an argument, and a boy in great trouble. And on the road, the disciples argue about which of them is the greatest. Watch how far they still have to go, and how patient Jesus is with them.
Jesus takes Peter, James and John up a high mountain, alone. And there, in front of them, he is transfigured. His clothes become shining, whiter than snow, whiter than anyone on earth could make them. And Moses and Elias, the prophet Elijah, appear and talk with Jesus. Peter, so frightened he does not know what to say, offers to build three shelters, one for each of them. Then a cloud covers them, and a voice comes out of the cloud. Suddenly, when they look around, they see no one any more, 'save Jesus only with themselves.'
Coming down the mountain, they find a great crowd around the other disciples, and the scribes arguing with them. A father explains: his son has a spirit that makes him unable to speak, throws him down, and makes him foam at the mouth. The disciples could not cast it out. Jesus says, 'O faithless generation, how long shall I be with you?' and, 'bring him unto me.'
When the boy is brought, the spirit throws him down at once. Jesus asks the father how long this has been happening. 'Of a child,' he says, since he was a little boy. And then he asks for help, if Jesus can. Jesus answers, 'If thou canst believe, all things are possible to him that believeth.' The father cries out. Then Jesus commands the spirit to come out and never come back. The boy lies still, so still that many say, 'He is dead.' But Jesus takes him by the hand and lifts him up, and he stands.
Afterward, in the house, the disciples ask, 'Why could not we cast him out?' Jesus answers, 'This kind can come forth by nothing, but by prayer and fasting.'
As they walk through Galilee, Jesus tells them again that he will be killed and will rise the third day. But they do not understand, and they are afraid to ask. Instead, on the road, they argue about something else. In Capernaum Jesus asks what they were discussing. Nobody answers. They had been arguing about who was the greatest.
Jesus sits down and calls the twelve. Then he takes a little child, sets him in the middle of them, and holds him in his arms. Whoever welcomes a little child like this in my name, he says, welcomes me.
John tells Jesus they stopped a man who was casting out devils in his name, because he was not one of their group. Jesus says, 'Forbid him not', and adds, 'For he that is not against us is on our part.' Even a cup of water given in his name will be rewarded.
Then Jesus uses strong and frightening pictures to show how serious sin is. It would be better to lose a hand, a foot or an eye than to let it lead you into sin and be lost. Jesus is not telling us to harm ourselves. He is telling us to deal with sin firmly, and not to play with it. And he ends with salt: 'Have salt in yourselves, and have peace one with another.'
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 7. 'And there was a cloud that overshadowed them: and a voice came out of the cloud, saying, This is my beloved Son: hear him.' At the baptism in chapter one, the voice from heaven spoke to Jesus: thou art my beloved Son. Now it speaks to the disciples, and to us. And it adds two words: hear him. Moses, who gave the law, and Elijah, the great prophet, were there. But when the cloud lifted, only Jesus was left. God is saying, listen to my Son above every other voice. That is what we are doing on this walk.
Verse 24. 'And straightway the father of the child cried out, and said with tears, Lord, I believe; help thou mine unbelief.' Here is a man with faith and doubt in the same breath. He does not pretend. He says, I do believe, and I am also struggling to believe. Help me. And Jesus does not send him away for having too little faith. He heals his son. You do not need perfect faith to come to Jesus. You can bring him your faith and your doubts together, and pray this prayer.
Verse 35. 'And he sat down, and called the twelve, and saith unto them, If any man desire to be first, the same shall be last of all, and servant of all.' The disciples wanted to know who was greatest. Jesus did not tell them it was wrong to want to be great. He told them the way to get there. In his kingdom, the greatest is the one who serves the most, who puts others first, who makes room for a little child. Jesus himself is the greatest servant of all.
Long before, Moses told the people of Israel that God would one day raise up a prophet like him. And Moses said, 'unto him ye shall hearken'. On the mountain, with Moses standing there, the voice of God says about Jesus, hear him. The prophet Moses promised had come.
Maybe you are like the father: you believe, and you also doubt. Pray his prayer today, honestly. And look for one way to serve someone else, especially someone small, or someone others overlook.
Here is today’s question. The father prayed, Lord, I believe; help thou mine unbelief. What is one thing you find hard to trust God with, that you will bring to him in that same prayer today?
Let us pray. Lord, I believe; help thou mine unbelief. Thank you that you do not turn away people with weak faith. Help me to hear your Son above every other voice, and to be the servant of all, as he was. Amen.
