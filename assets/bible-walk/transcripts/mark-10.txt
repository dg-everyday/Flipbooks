A blind beggar sits by the road outside Jericho. He hears a crowd going by, and someone says it is Jesus of Nazareth. So he starts to shout. People tell him to be quiet. He shouts louder. 'Thou Son of David, have mercy on me.' And Jesus, walking toward Jerusalem and toward the cross, stands still.
In chapter nine, three disciples saw Jesus shining on the mountain, a father prayed, Lord, I believe; help thou mine unbelief, and the disciples argued about who was the greatest.
Now, in chapter ten, Jesus leaves Galilee for the last time. He is walking south, toward Jerusalem, and the disciples are afraid. Along the way, Jesus meets all kinds of people: Pharisees with a trick question, mothers with children, a rich young man, two disciples who want the best seats, and a blind beggar. In each meeting, watch what it really means to enter the kingdom of God.
The Pharisees ask Jesus a question to trap him: may a man divorce his wife? Jesus takes them back to the beginning, when God made them male and female, and the two became one flesh. 'What therefore God hath joined together, let not man put asunder.'
Then people bring little children for Jesus to touch and bless, and the disciples tell them off. Jesus is much displeased with his disciples. He welcomes the children, takes them up in his arms, puts his hands on them, and blesses them.
As Jesus sets out on the road, a young man comes running and kneels before him. 'Good Master, what shall I do that I may inherit eternal life?' Jesus reminds him of the commandments. The young man says he has kept them all since he was young. Then Mark says, 'Then Jesus beholding him loved him'. Jesus tells him there is one thing he lacks: sell what you have, give to the poor, and come, follow me. The young man’s face falls. He goes away sad, 'for he had great possessions.'
Jesus turns to his disciples. How hard it is for those who trust in riches to enter the kingdom of God. 'It is easier for a camel to go through the eye of a needle, than for a rich man to enter into the kingdom of God.' The disciples are astonished. 'Who then can be saved?'
On the road up to Jerusalem, Jesus walks ahead of them, and they follow, amazed and afraid. For the third time he tells them plainly what will happen: he will be handed over, mocked, beaten and killed, and the third day he will rise again.
Right after this, James and John come and ask for the two best places, one on his right hand and one on his left, when he comes in glory. 'Ye know not what ye ask', Jesus says. Can you drink the cup I drink? When the other ten hear about it, they are angry with James and John. So Jesus calls them all together. The rulers of the nations love to lord it over people, he says. 'But so shall it not be among you'. Whoever wants to be great must be your servant.
Then they come to Jericho, and blind Bartimaeus is sitting by the road, shouting. Jesus stops and calls for him. The people now say to him, 'Be of good comfort, rise; he calleth thee.' Bartimaeus throws off his coat, jumps up, and comes to Jesus. Jesus asks, 'What wilt thou that I should do unto thee?' 'Lord, that I might receive my sight.' 'Go thy way; thy faith hath made thee whole.' And immediately he can see, and he follows Jesus on the road.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 14. 'But when Jesus saw it, he was much displeased, and said unto them, Suffer the little children to come unto me, and forbid them not: for of such is the kingdom of God.' Suffer here is an old word that means let, or allow. Let the little children come. The disciples thought children were not important enough for Jesus. Jesus was angry about that. Children matter to him. And then he says the kingdom belongs to such as these: people who come like children, with empty hands, trusting, not trying to earn their place.
Verse 27. 'And Jesus looking upon them saith, With men it is impossible, but not with God: for with God all things are possible.' The disciples asked, who then can be saved? Jesus answered honestly: no one, by their own effort. You cannot buy your way into God’s kingdom, or work your way in, or be good enough to get in. It is impossible with men. But not with God. Salvation is God’s work, not ours. What we could never do, he can do, and he does.
Verse 45. 'For even the Son of man came not to be ministered unto, but to minister, and to give his life a ransom for many.' This is the key verse of the whole book of Mark. To be ministered unto means to be served. Jesus did not come to be served, but to serve. And a ransom is the price paid to set a prisoner free. Jesus is telling us why he is walking to Jerusalem: to pay with his own life the price that sets many free. James and John wanted thrones. Jesus was going to a cross, for them, and for us.
Isaiah wrote about God’s suffering servant, and said, 'he bare the sin of many'. Long afterward, Jesus says he came to give his life a ransom for many. Jesus is the servant Isaiah saw, carrying the sins of many people, so that they could go free.
The rich young man walked away from Jesus sad, holding on to his things. Bartimaeus threw off his coat and ran to Jesus. Is there something you are holding on to that is keeping you from following him? And like Bartimaeus, do not be quiet. Call out to Jesus. He stands still for those who call.
Here is today’s question. Jesus asked Bartimaeus, what wilt thou that I should do unto thee? If Jesus asked you that today, what would you say?
Let us pray. Lord Jesus, thank you that you came not to be served, but to serve, and to give your life a ransom for me. Help me to come to you like a child, and to let go of anything I love more than you. Jesus, thou Son of David, have mercy on me. Amen.
