The road into Jerusalem is covered with coats. People have taken off their own cloaks and thrown them on the ground, and others are cutting leafy branches from the fields and spreading them in the way. And down the middle of the road comes Jesus, not on a war horse, but on a young donkey that no one has ever ridden. The crowd is shouting: 'Hosanna; Blessed is he that cometh in the name of the Lord'.
In chapter ten, Jesus walked toward Jerusalem, telling his disciples that he came to give his life a ransom for many, and a blind man named Bartimaeus received his sight and followed him on the road.
Now Jesus arrives. From here to the end of the book, Mark slows down and tells us one single week, the last week of Jesus’ life before the cross. In this chapter Jesus enters Jerusalem as a king, goes into the temple, and does something that makes the leaders want to destroy him.
Near the Mount of Olives, Jesus sends two disciples into a village. You will find a colt tied there, he says, that no one has ever sat on. Untie it and bring it. If anyone asks, say 'the Lord hath need of him'. It happens just as he said. They throw their coats on the colt, and Jesus sits on it.
As he rides toward the city, the crowd goes wild. Some spread their coats on the road, and others spread branches. Those in front and those behind cry out, 'Hosanna; Blessed is he that cometh in the name of the Lord: Blessed be the kingdom of our father David'. Jesus enters Jerusalem and goes into the temple. He looks around at everything, and then, because it is late, he goes back to the village of Bethany with the twelve.
The next day, on the way into the city, Jesus is hungry. He sees a fig tree in the distance, covered in leaves. But when he comes to it, there is no fruit, only leaves. Jesus says to the tree, 'No man eat fruit of thee hereafter for ever.' And his disciples hear it.
In the temple, Jesus finds people buying and selling, and money changers at their tables. He drives them out. He turns over the tables of the money changers and the seats of those selling doves, and he will not let anyone carry goods through the temple courts. Then he teaches them: 'Is it not written, My house shall be called of all nations the house of prayer? but ye have made it a den of thieves.' The chief priests and scribes hear this, and they begin looking for a way to destroy him. They are afraid of him, because all the people are amazed at his teaching.
The next time they pass that way, the disciples see the fig tree, dried up from the roots. Peter remembers. 'Master, behold, the fig tree which thou cursedst is withered away.' The tree had plenty of leaves and no fruit, like the temple that was busy with religion but had no true prayer. Then Jesus teaches them about faith and prayer.
Back in the temple, the chief priests, scribes and elders come to Jesus and demand, 'By what authority doest thou these things?' Jesus answers with a question of his own. John’s baptism: was it from heaven, or of men? They argue among themselves. If we say from heaven, he will ask why we did not believe John. If we say of men, the people will turn on us, for everyone believes John was a prophet. So they say, 'We cannot tell.' And Jesus says, then neither will I tell you by what authority I do these things.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 9. 'And they that went before, and they that followed, cried, saying, Hosanna; Blessed is he that cometh in the name of the Lord:' Hosanna is a Hebrew word that means save, we pray. It is both a shout of praise and a cry for help. The people welcomed Jesus as their king. Many of them hoped he would throw out the Romans. But Jesus came to save them from something deeper, and he would do it in a way they did not expect. Hosanna is still a good prayer: Lord, save us.
Verse 22. 'And Jesus answering saith unto them, Have faith in God.' The disciples were amazed that a tree could wither at a word. Jesus points them away from the tree, and away from themselves, to God. Notice what he says: not, have faith in your faith, or have faith in your feelings, but have faith in God. Great faith is not a feeling that we work up. It is trust in a great God. Even mountains move at his word.
Verse 25. 'And when ye stand praying, forgive, if ye have ought against any: that your Father also which is in heaven may forgive you your trespasses.' Right after teaching about faith that moves mountains, Jesus teaches about forgiveness. Sometimes the mountain in the way of our prayers is a grudge we are holding on to. If you have ought against any, that is, anything against anyone, forgive. We who have been forgiven so much by our Father are to forgive others. Unforgiveness blocks the way; forgiveness clears it.
Long before, the prophet Zechariah had written, 'behold, thy King cometh unto thee: he is just, and having salvation; lowly, and riding upon an ass, and upon a colt the foal of an ass.' When Jesus chose a young donkey to ride into Jerusalem, he was saying without words: I am that king. Not a king on a war horse, but a lowly king, bringing salvation.
Jesus looked for fruit and found only leaves. Is your faith all leaves, looking good outside, with little fruit inside? Ask God to make your life a house of prayer. And if there is someone you need to forgive, do it before you pray.
Here is today’s question. Jesus said, when ye stand praying, forgive. Is there someone you will choose to forgive today?
Let us pray. Hosanna, Lord Jesus. You are the King who comes in the name of the Lord. Save me. Make my heart a house of prayer, and my life a tree with fruit. Help me to have faith in God, and to forgive as I have been forgiven. Amen.
