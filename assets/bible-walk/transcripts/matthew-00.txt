A man sits at a table by the road, counting coins. He collects taxes for the Romans, and people cross the street to avoid him. Then a stranger stops in front of his table, looks at him, and says two words. Follow me. The man stands up, leaves the money on the table, and goes. Years later, that same man’s name would be on the very first book of the New Testament.
Welcome to the Gospel of Matthew, on The Living Voice. Matthew has twenty eight chapters, so on this walk it is twenty eight steps, one chapter at a time, after this introduction.
You have already walked through three Gospels: Mark, Luke and John. So why another? Because each Gospel writer was like a different person standing around the same event, telling what they saw in their own way. Matthew tells the story of Jesus for people who knew the Old Testament well. And now, so do you. You have walked from Genesis through the kings, the exile, and the return home with Ezra, Nehemiah and Haggai. That is exactly the story Matthew expects his readers to know.
The book has been linked from early times with Matthew, one of the twelve apostles. In chapter nine, Jesus calls a tax collector named Matthew. Mark and Luke call him Levi. A tax collector worked for the Romans, and many of them cheated people, so they were hated. But Jesus called Matthew anyway, and he followed.
Here are three things to watch for as we walk.
Watch for the words, that it might be fulfilled. Matthew uses them again and again. When something happens to Jesus, Matthew points back to a prophet who said it would happen. Bethlehem, Egypt, Nazareth, a donkey, thirty pieces of silver. Matthew wants us to see that Jesus did not come out of nowhere. God had been promising him all along.
Watch for the kingdom of heaven. Matthew uses those words more than thirty times. Jesus came as a King, but not the kind of king anyone expected. His kingdom starts small, like a seed, and grows. And it belongs to the humble, the merciful and the poor in spirit.
And watch for the great teachings. Matthew gathers the words of Jesus into five long sections, like five great talks. The first and most famous is the Sermon on the Mount, in chapters five to seven. Then come teachings for the twelve, the parables of the kingdom, how to live together as God’s family, and the last teachings about the end of the age.
Matthew begins with a family line: Abraham, David, the kings, and then a name you met just recently in Haggai. Zorobabel, who is Zerubbabel the governor, God’s signet. Matthew wants you to know that Jesus is the son of David, the King God promised.
And notice how the book begins and ends. Near the start, Jesus is given the name Emmanuel, which means God with us. And in the very last verse, Jesus says, lo, I am with you alway. Haggai’s people heard God say, I am with you. In Matthew, that promise walks among us.
Here is a question to carry with you. Jesus called Matthew, a man many people despised, to follow him. Who might God be calling that others would overlook, and could that person be you?
Let us pray. Lord Jesus, as I walk through the Gospel of Matthew, show me who you are. Thank you that you are Emmanuel, God with us. Teach me the ways of your kingdom, and help me to follow you, as Matthew did, when you call. Amen.
