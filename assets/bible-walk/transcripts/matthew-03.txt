Out in the desert, far from the cities, a strange man is preaching. He wears rough clothes made of camel’s hair, and he eats locusts and wild honey. But crowds walk for miles to hear him. Tax collectors, soldiers, farmers, even the religious leaders. And one day, a quiet man from Galilee steps into the river and asks to be baptized. The preacher stops, and says, it should be the other way round.
In chapter two, wise men came from the east to worship the child Jesus. King Herod tried to destroy him, but God warned Joseph in a dream, and the family fled to Egypt. After Herod died, they came back and settled in Nazareth.
Now, in chapter three, many years have passed. Jesus is grown up. And before he begins his work, God sends a messenger to prepare the way. Listen for what John the Baptist calls people to do. And listen for the voice from heaven.
'In those days came John the Baptist, preaching in the wilderness of Judaea'. John was a cousin of Jesus, though Matthew does not tell us that. Luke does. John had one message.
Matthew says John was the one Isaiah spoke of. 'The voice of one crying in the wilderness, Prepare ye the way of the Lord, make his paths straight.' Before a king visited a town, workers would go ahead to fill in holes and straighten the road. John’s job was to straighten hearts, so they would be ready for the King.
John looked like the prophets of old. Like Elijah, he wore a rough garment with a leather belt. He lived simply in the desert.
And people came from everywhere. From Jerusalem, all Judaea, and all the country around the Jordan river. They 'were baptized of him in Jordan, confessing their sins.' Baptize means to wash or dip in water. Being dipped in the river was a picture of being washed clean, and starting again.
But when John saw many of the Pharisees and Sadducees coming, he spoke to them very sharply. 'O generation of vipers, who hath warned you to flee from the wrath to come?' Vipers are poisonous snakes. These were the religious leaders, proud of how good they were. John could see that they were not really sorry for their sins.
So John tells them what real repentance looks like.
Then he says, do not think you are safe just because Abraham is your father. 'God is able of these stones to raise up children unto Abraham.' Being born into a good family does not make anyone right with God. Every person must turn to him for themselves.
John says the axe is already at the root of the trees. Every tree that does not bear good fruit will be cut down. And then John points away from himself. I baptize you with water, he says. But someone is coming after me who is mightier than I. I am not even worthy to carry his shoes. He will baptize you with the Holy Ghost, and with fire.
Then Jesus comes from Galilee to the Jordan, to be baptized by John.
John tries to stop him. 'I have need to be baptized of thee, and comest thou to me?' John knew that Jesus had no sins to confess.
But Jesus answers, 'Suffer it to be so now: for thus it becometh us to fulfil all righteousness.' Suffer means allow. Jesus chose to stand with sinners in the river, though he had never sinned himself. He was doing everything that was right, and taking his place beside us.
So John baptizes him. And as Jesus comes up out of the water, the heavens are opened. He sees the Spirit of God coming down like a dove and resting on him.
And then a voice speaks from heaven.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 2. 'And saying, Repent ye: for the kingdom of heaven is at hand.' To repent means to turn around. To stop going your own way, and turn back to God. At hand means very close, right here. The King was about to arrive. John did not say, try harder to be good. He said, turn around, because God is coming near. Jesus would begin his own preaching with the very same words.
Verse 8. 'Bring forth therefore fruits meet for repentance:' Meet means fitting, or matching. A fruit tree shows what kind of tree it is by its fruit. In the same way, if someone has really turned to God, it will show in how they live. Luke tells us that John told people to share their coats and food, and to stop cheating and bullying. Real repentance is not only words. It changes what we do.
Verse 17. 'And lo a voice from heaven, saying, This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased.' Here, at the very start of Jesus’ work, the Father speaks, the Son stands in the water, and the Spirit comes down like a dove. And notice what the Father says. Beloved. Well pleased. Jesus had not yet preached a sermon or done a single miracle. But the Father loved him, and was pleased with him. That love came first.
Isaiah wrote of God’s chosen servant, 'Behold my servant, whom I uphold; mine elect, in whom my soul delighteth; I have put my spirit upon him'. And the psalm said of the King, 'Thou art my Son; this day have I begotten thee.'
Is there something in your life you need to turn away from? Repentance is not a punishment. It is turning around to face God, who is near. And if you belong to Jesus, remember that God’s love for you does not depend on what you achieve today.
Here is today’s question. John said that real repentance shows in our lives, like fruit on a tree. What fruit would you like to see growing in your life?
Let us pray. Father in heaven, thank you for Jesus, your beloved Son. Thank you that he stood beside sinners, to bring us back to you. Show me what I need to turn from, and help me to bear good fruit that shows I belong to you. Amen.
