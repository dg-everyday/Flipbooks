After weeks without food, a man is so hungry his body aches. Then a voice speaks. Why go hungry? You have the power. Just say the word, and these stones will turn into bread. It sounds so reasonable. Who would know? Who would it hurt? But the hungry man answers with words he learned from the Scriptures. And the battle in the desert begins.
In chapter three, John the Baptist preached in the wilderness, repent ye, for the kingdom of heaven is at hand. Jesus came to be baptized, the Spirit came down like a dove, and the Father said, this is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased.
Now, in chapter four, the Spirit leads Jesus into the wilderness, where he is tested. Then he begins to preach, and calls his first followers. Listen for the weapon Jesus uses against the devil. And listen for what Jesus says to the fishermen.
'Then was Jesus led up of the Spirit into the wilderness to be tempted of the devil.' Straight after the Father called him beloved, Jesus faced the enemy. Jesus went without food for forty days. Afterward, Matthew says simply, he 'was afterward an hungred.' He was very, very hungry.
Then the tempter came. If thou be the Son of God, command that these stones be made bread. The devil was trying to make Jesus use his power for himself, and doubt his Father’s care.
Jesus answers with words from Deuteronomy.
Then the devil takes him to the holy city, Jerusalem, and sets him on the highest point of the temple. If thou be the Son of God, throw yourself down. And this time, the devil quotes Scripture too. It is written, he shall give his angels charge concerning thee, and they will catch you. But the devil was twisting God’s promise, to make Jesus put God to the test.
Jesus answers, 'It is written again, Thou shalt not tempt the Lord thy God.' Notice the word again. The devil took one verse out of its place. Jesus answered with the rest of God’s Word. We do not test God to make him prove himself. We trust him.
Then the devil takes Jesus up to a very high mountain, and shows him all the kingdoms of the world, and all their glory. All these things will I give thee, if thou wilt fall down and worship me. Here was a short cut. Jesus could have all the kingdoms of the world without the cross. All he had to do was bow down once.
And Jesus answers with a command.
'Then the devil leaveth him, and, behold, angels came and ministered unto him.' To minister means to serve and care for. Jesus did not throw himself down so angels would catch him. But after he obeyed, God sent angels to care for him anyway.
When Jesus hears that John has been put in prison, he goes to Galilee. He leaves Nazareth and makes his home in Capernaum, a town by the sea of Galilee. Matthew says this fulfilled Isaiah’s words. 'The people which sat in darkness saw great light'. Galilee was a mixed land, far from Jerusalem, where many outsiders lived. And that is where the light of the world began to shine.
'From that time Jesus began to preach, and to say, Repent: for the kingdom of heaven is at hand.' The very same words John had preached.
One day, Jesus is walking by the sea of Galilee. He sees two brothers, Simon, called Peter, and Andrew, throwing a net into the sea. They were fishermen.
And Jesus calls to them.
'And they straightway left their nets, and followed him.' A little further on, he sees two more brothers, James and John, the sons of Zebedee, in a boat with their father, mending their nets. Jesus calls them. And they immediately leave the boat and their father, and follow him.
Then Jesus goes all over Galilee, teaching in the synagogues, preaching the good news of the kingdom, and healing every kind of sickness and disease. News about him spreads far and wide. People bring him the sick, the suffering, those troubled by devils, those who had seizures, those who could not move. And he heals them. Great crowds follow him, from Galilee, from the cities beyond, from Jerusalem and Judaea, and from across the Jordan.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 4. 'But he answered and said, It is written, Man shall not live by bread alone, but by every word that proceedeth out of the mouth of God.' Moses said these words to Israel after forty years in the wilderness, where God fed them with manna. Jesus, after forty days in the wilderness, holds on to the same truth. Bread keeps our bodies alive. But we need God’s Word to truly live. Jesus fought the devil, not with a miracle, but with Scripture he had learned by heart.
Verse 10. 'Then saith Jesus unto him, Get thee hence, Satan: for it is written, Thou shalt worship the Lord thy God, and him only shalt thou serve.' Get thee hence means go away. The devil offered Jesus the whole world, if he would only bow down. But Jesus would not worship anyone but God. Many things in our lives can ask for our worship: money, popularity, getting our own way. Jesus shows us how to answer. God alone.
Verse 19. 'And he saith unto them, Follow me, and I will make you fishers of men.' Jesus spoke to these men in words they understood. They caught fish. Now he would teach them to bring people to God. Notice that Jesus said, I will make you. They did not have to know how yet. They only had to follow. Jesus would do the making.
Moses told the people, 'And thou shalt remember all the way which the LORD thy God led thee these forty years in the wilderness'. And the psalmist prayed, 'Thy word have I hid in mine heart, that I might not sin against thee.'
When temptation comes, what will you say? Jesus answered with God’s Word. Choose one verse to learn by heart this week, so it is ready when you need it. And remember that following Jesus starts with a step. You do not have to know how. He will make you.
Here is today’s question. The fishermen left their nets to follow Jesus. What might Jesus be asking you to leave behind, so you can follow him more closely?
Let us pray. Lord Jesus, thank you that you faced temptation and did not give in. When I am tempted, help me to remember your Word, and to worship God alone. Thank you for calling me to follow you. Make me what you want me to be. Amen.
