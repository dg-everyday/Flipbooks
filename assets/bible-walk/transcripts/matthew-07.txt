Two men build houses side by side. One is in a hurry. He builds on the soft, flat sand, and finishes quickly. The other digs and digs, until he reaches solid rock, and builds there. For a while, the two houses look exactly the same. Then the storm comes. And only then can you see the difference.
In chapter six, Jesus taught about giving, praying and fasting in secret, for our Father sees in secret. He gave us the Lord’s Prayer, and told us not to worry, but to seek first the kingdom of God.
Now, in chapter seven, Jesus finishes the Sermon on the Mount. Listen for what he says about judging others. Listen for his invitation to ask, seek and knock. And listen for the difference between the two builders.
Jesus begins with a warning about judging others. 'Judge not, that ye be not judged.' The way you judge others is the way you will be judged.
Then he paints a funny picture. Why do you look at the tiny speck of dust in your brother’s eye, when there is a whole wooden beam sticking out of your own? How can you say, let me get that speck out for you, with a beam in your eye? You hypocrite. First take the beam out of your own eye, and then you will see clearly to help your brother.
Jesus is not saying we must never tell right from wrong. He is saying, do not be quick to criticise others while ignoring your own faults. Deal with yourself first, and then help others gently.
Then Jesus gives a wonderful invitation.
He says, everyone who asks receives. Everyone who seeks finds. Everyone who knocks will find the door opened.
And then he asks the fathers in the crowd a question. If your son asks for bread, would you give him a stone? If he asks for a fish, would you give him a snake? Of course not. 'If ye then, being evil, know how to give good gifts unto your children, how much more shall your Father which is in heaven give good things to them that ask him?' Even imperfect parents want to give their children good things. God is a perfect Father.
Then Jesus sums up the whole law in one sentence.
Next, Jesus speaks about two ways. 'Enter ye in at the strait gate: for wide is the gate, and broad is the way, that leadeth to destruction, and many there be which go in thereat'. Strait means narrow. The easy road, where everyone does what they like, leads to ruin. 'Because strait is the gate, and narrow is the way, which leadeth unto life, and few there be that find it.'
Then Jesus warns about false prophets, who come looking like gentle sheep, but inside are like hungry wolves. How can you tell? 'Ye shall know them by their fruits.' You do not pick grapes from thorn bushes, or figs from thistles. A good tree produces good fruit. A bad tree produces bad fruit. Look at how people live, not only what they say.
Then Jesus says something very serious. 'Not every one that saith unto me, Lord, Lord, shall enter into the kingdom of heaven; but he that doeth the will of my Father which is in heaven.' Some will say, Lord, did we not preach in your name, and do great works? And he will say, 'I never knew you'. Saying the right words is not enough. What matters is knowing Jesus, and doing what he says.
And so Jesus ends his sermon with a story.
The rain fell, the floods came, the winds blew and beat on the house built on the rock. 'and it fell not: for it was founded upon a rock.' But everyone who hears these words and does not do them is like a foolish man who built his house on the sand. The rain fell, the floods came, the winds blew, and beat on that house. 'and it fell: and great was the fall of it.'
When Jesus finished, the people were astonished at his teaching. 'For he taught them as one having authority, and not as the scribes.' The scribes always quoted other teachers. Jesus said, I say unto you. He spoke as the one who had the right to say what God means.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 7. 'Ask, and it shall be given you; seek, and ye shall find; knock, and it shall be opened unto you:' These are three ways of coming to God, each one a little stronger. Ask, like a child asking a parent. Seek, like someone searching for something precious. Knock, like someone at a door who will not go away. Jesus wants us to keep coming to God in prayer. God is not annoyed by our asking. He invites it. And he gives what is good, as a loving Father does.
Verse 12. 'Therefore all things whatsoever ye would that men should do to you, do ye even so to them: for this is the law and the prophets.' This is often called the golden rule. Think about how you would like to be treated: listened to, helped, forgiven, spoken to kindly. Then treat others that way, first, without waiting for them to go first. Jesus says this sums up the law and the prophets. It is simple to say, and it takes a lifetime to live.
Verse 24. 'Therefore whosoever heareth these sayings of mine, and doeth them, I will liken him unto a wise man, which built his house upon a rock:' Both builders heard the words of Jesus. The difference was that one did them. Storms come to everyone, wise and foolish alike: sickness, loss, hard days. A life built on hearing and doing what Jesus says will stand. Listening to this walk is a good start. The wise builder goes one step further, and does what he hears.
James wrote, 'But be ye doers of the word, and not hearers only, deceiving your own selves.' And a psalm says, 'The LORD is my rock, and my fortress, and my deliverer'.
Is there something you have been asking God for, and you have stopped asking? Jesus says, keep asking, keep seeking, keep knocking. And think of one person today you could treat the way you would like to be treated, and do it.
Here is today’s question. Jesus said the wise builder hears his words and does them. What is one thing Jesus said in the Sermon on the Mount that you want to start doing?
Let us pray. Lord Jesus, thank you for inviting me to ask, seek and knock. Thank you that our Father gives good things to his children. Help me not to judge others, but to treat them as I would want to be treated. Make me a wise builder, who hears your words and does them. Amen.
