Imagine being sent on a long journey with no money, no suitcase, no spare clothes, not even a walking stick. You would have to trust that people along the way would welcome you and feed you. And some would not. That is how Jesus sent out his twelve disciples. And before they left, he told them everything they would need to know.
In chapter nine, Jesus forgave and healed a paralysed man, called Matthew the tax collector, raised a girl to life, and opened blind eyes. When he saw the crowds, he had compassion on them, because they were like sheep without a shepherd. He told his disciples, pray the Lord of the harvest to send out workers.
Now, in chapter ten, the disciples become the workers. This is the second great teaching in Matthew. Listen for what Jesus tells them to give. And listen for why they do not need to be afraid.
Jesus calls his twelve disciples, and gives them power to cast out evil spirits and to heal every sickness. Matthew lists their names. Simon, called Peter, and his brother Andrew. James and John, the sons of Zebedee. Philip and Bartholomew. Thomas, and 'Matthew the publican'. Matthew calls himself the tax collector, not hiding where Jesus found him. James the son of Alphaeus, and Thaddaeus. Simon the Canaanite, and Judas Iscariot, who later betrayed him. Ordinary men. Fishermen, a tax collector, and others, chosen to be sent.
Apostle means one who is sent. And Jesus sends them first to 'the lost sheep of the house of Israel.' Later, after the resurrection, he will send his followers to all nations.
As you go, Jesus says, preach, 'The kingdom of heaven is at hand.' And then he tells them what to do.
Take no gold or silver or copper in your belts, no bag, no spare coat, no extra shoes, no staff. The workman deserves his food. When you enter a town, find someone worthy and stay with them. When you enter a house, greet it with peace. If the house welcomes you, let your peace rest on it. If it does not, let your peace come back to you. And if a town will not listen, shake the dust off your feet as you leave.
Then Jesus warns them honestly. 'Behold, I send you forth as sheep in the midst of wolves: be ye therefore wise as serpents, and harmless as doves.' Be wise and careful, but never cruel or cunning.
People will hand you over to councils, and beat you, and bring you before governors and kings because of me. But do not worry about what to say. 'For it is not ye that speak, but the Spirit of your Father which speaketh in you.' Even families may turn against one another because of me. 'but he that endureth to the end shall be saved.'
The disciple is not above his master. If they called me evil names, they will do the same to you.
But then, three times, Jesus says, do not be afraid. Nothing hidden will stay hidden. What I tell you quietly, shout from the housetops.
'Are not two sparrows sold for a farthing? and one of them shall not fall on the ground without your Father.' A farthing was the smallest coin. Sparrows were so cheap that two were sold for that tiny coin. Yet God sees every one. 'But the very hairs of your head are all numbered.'
And Jesus draws the lesson.
Whoever confesses me before people, I will confess before my Father in heaven.
Then Jesus says something surprising. 'Think not that I am come to send peace on earth: I came not to send peace, but a sword.' Jesus is the Prince of Peace, so what does he mean? He means that following him will divide people. Some in a family will follow him, and some will not. Jesus was not telling anyone to fight. He was warning that following him could cost even close friendships.
Whoever loves father or mother, son or daughter, more than me, is not worthy of me. 'And he that taketh not his cross, and followeth after me, is not worthy of me. He that findeth his life shall lose it: and he that loseth his life for my sake shall find it.'
And then Jesus ends with a word of great kindness.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 8. 'Heal the sick, cleanse the lepers, raise the dead, cast out devils: freely ye have received, freely give.' Freely means as a free gift, without charging. The disciples had not paid for the power Jesus gave them, and they had not earned his love. So they must not sell it. They must pass it on for free. Everything we have received from God is a gift: forgiveness, love, our lives themselves. So we give freely too.
Verse 31. 'Fear ye not therefore, ye are of more value than many sparrows.' If God watches over a tiny sparrow, worth almost nothing, how much more does he care for you? He even knows how many hairs are on your head. Jesus did not promise that nothing hard would ever happen. He promised that nothing would happen without our Father knowing. We are precious to him.
Verse 42. 'And whosoever shall give to drink unto one of these little ones a cup of cold water only in the name of a disciple, verily I say unto you, he shall in no wise lose his reward.' After all the talk of courage and danger, Jesus ends with a cup of water. Not everyone will be sent to faraway places. But anyone can give a cup of water to someone who belongs to Jesus. God notices the smallest kindness done for his sake. Nothing done in love for him is ever wasted.
Jesus said, 'It is more blessed to give than to receive.' And a psalm says, 'The LORD shall preserve thee from all evil: he shall preserve thy soul.'
What has God freely given you? Think of one thing, and thank him. Then look for one way to give freely today, your time, your help, or even a cup of water. And if you are afraid, remember the sparrows.
Here is today’s question. Jesus said, freely ye have received, freely give. What is something God has given you that you could share with someone else?
Let us pray. Lord Jesus, thank you that everything I have from you is a free gift. Help me to give freely, as I have received. When I am afraid, remind me that you see every sparrow, and that I am precious to you. Give me courage to follow you, and to speak of you. Amen.
