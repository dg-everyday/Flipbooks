The wind is howling, the waves are crashing, and the disciples have been rowing for hours. Then, through the spray, they see a figure walking towards them on top of the water. They scream. It is a ghost! But a voice calls out, it is I, be not afraid. And one of the fishermen says something no one expected. Lord, if it is really you, tell me to come to you on the water.
In chapter thirteen, Jesus told parables of the kingdom of heaven: the sower, the mustard seed, the treasure hid in a field and the pearl of great price. Then he went home to Nazareth, but the people there were offended at him, and did not believe.
Now, in chapter fourteen, we hear sad news about John the Baptist. Then Jesus feeds a huge crowd, and walks on the sea. Listen for what Jesus tells his disciples to do with the hungry crowd. And listen for what happens when Peter takes his eyes off Jesus.
Herod the tetrarch, the ruler of Galilee, hears about Jesus. He says to his servants, this must be John the Baptist, risen from the dead. Then Matthew tells us what had happened to John.
Herod had arrested John and put him in prison. John had told Herod plainly that it was wrong for him to take his brother Philip’s wife, Herodias. Herod wanted to put John to death, but he was afraid of the people, because they believed John was a prophet.
Then, at Herod’s birthday feast, the daughter of Herodias danced, and pleased Herod. He made a rash promise with an oath to give her whatever she asked. Her mother told her what to ask for: the death of John the Baptist. Herod was sorry. But because of his oath, and because his guests were watching, he gave the order. And John was put to death in the prison. John’s disciples came, took his body and buried it, and went and told Jesus.
When Jesus hears it, he goes away by boat to a quiet place by himself. He was grieving. But the crowds hear where he is going, and follow on foot. When Jesus sees the great crowd, he is moved with compassion, and heals their sick.
As the day draws to an end, the disciples come to him. This is a lonely place, and it is late. Send the crowds away, so they can go to the villages and buy food.
But Jesus gives them a surprising answer.
They say, we have only five loaves and two fish. Jesus says, 'Bring them hither to me.' He tells the people to sit down on the grass. He takes the five loaves and two fish, looks up to heaven, blesses, and breaks the loaves. He gives them to the disciples, and the disciples give them to the crowd. 'And they did all eat, and were filled'. And they pick up twelve baskets full of leftovers. About five thousand men ate, besides women and children.
Straight away, Jesus sends the disciples ahead in the boat, while he sends the crowd home. Then he goes up a mountain by himself to pray.
Meanwhile, the boat is far out on the sea, tossed by the waves, because the wind is against them. In the last hours before daybreak, Jesus comes to them, walking on the sea. When the disciples see him, they are terrified. It is a spirit, they cry out. But at once Jesus speaks to them. 'Be of good cheer; it is I; be not afraid.'
Peter answers, 'Lord, if it be thou, bid me come unto thee on the water.' Jesus says, 'Come.' So Peter climbs out of the boat, and walks on the water towards Jesus.
But then Peter notices the wind.
Jesus reaches out his hand at once, and catches him.
When they climb into the boat, the wind stops. And the disciples worship him, saying, 'Of a truth thou art the Son of God.' In chapter eight, they asked, what manner of man is this? Now they know.
When they cross over to the land of Gennesaret, the people recognise Jesus. They send word all around, and bring him all their sick. They beg to touch just the hem of his garment. And all who touch it are made perfectly whole.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 16. 'But Jesus said unto them, They need not depart; give ye them to eat.' The disciples saw the problem and wanted to send it away. Jesus said, you feed them. They only had five loaves and two fish, nowhere near enough. But when they brought what little they had to Jesus, he blessed it, and it became more than enough. Jesus still works this way. He asks us to bring him what we have, however small, and he does the multiplying.
Verse 30. 'But when he saw the wind boisterous, he was afraid; and beginning to sink, he cried, saying, Lord, save me.' Boisterous means wild and strong. While Peter looked at Jesus, he walked on water. When he looked at the wind and the waves, he began to sink. That happens to us too. When we fix our eyes on our problems, we start to go under. But notice that Peter knew what to do. He cried out, Lord, save me. It is one of the shortest and best prayers in the Bible.
Verse 31. 'And immediately Jesus stretched forth his hand, and caught him, and said unto him, O thou of little faith, wherefore didst thou doubt?' Immediately. Jesus did not wait to see if Peter could swim. He did not let him sink first to teach him a lesson. He reached out at once. Then he gently asked, why did you doubt? Peter’s faith was small, but he was the only one who stepped out of the boat. And even when his faith failed, Jesus held on to him.
The psalmist wrote, 'He sent from above, he took me; he drew me out of many waters.' And Isaiah heard God say, 'When thou passest through the waters, I will be with thee'.
Is there a need around you that seems too big? Bring Jesus what you have, even if it is small. And if you feel like you are sinking today, do what Peter did. Cry out, Lord, save me. He will reach out his hand.
Here is today’s question. Peter began to sink when he looked at the wind instead of Jesus. What are the winds and waves that make you afraid, and how can you keep your eyes on Jesus?
Let us pray. Lord Jesus, thank you that you take the little I have and make it enough. Thank you that when I am sinking, you reach out your hand at once. Help me to keep my eyes on you, and not on the wind. Lord, save me, and hold me fast. Amen.
