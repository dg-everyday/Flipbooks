A mother is desperate. Her daughter is suffering terribly, and nothing has helped. She hears that Jesus has come near her town, so she runs after him, crying out for mercy. But Jesus does not answer her a single word. His disciples say, send her away, she keeps shouting after us. Most people would give up. But this mother will not give up.
In chapter fourteen, John the Baptist was put to death by Herod. Then Jesus fed five thousand people with five loaves and two fish, and walked on the stormy sea. Peter stepped out of the boat, began to sink, and cried, Lord, save me. And the disciples worshipped Jesus as the Son of God.
Now, in chapter fifteen, religious leaders come from Jerusalem to complain about the disciples. Then Jesus travels outside Israel, where he meets a woman of great faith. Listen for what Jesus says about the heart. And listen for how the woman answers him.
Some Pharisees and scribes come all the way from Jerusalem to Jesus. They ask, why do your disciples break the tradition of the elders? They do not wash their hands before they eat. This was not about keeping clean from germs. It was a religious washing ceremony the elders had added to God’s law.
Jesus answers with a question. Why do you break the commandment of God for the sake of your tradition? God said, honour your father and mother. But you say, if a man tells his parents, the money I could have used to help you is a gift for God, then he does not have to help them. So you cancel God’s Word with your own rules.
Then Jesus quotes Isaiah.
He goes on, 'But in vain they do worship me, teaching for doctrines the commandments of men.'
Then Jesus calls the crowd and says, listen and understand. It is not what goes into the mouth that makes a person unclean before God. It is what comes out of the mouth.
The disciples say, do you know the Pharisees were offended? Jesus says, every plant my Father did not plant will be pulled up. Leave them. 'they be blind leaders of the blind. And if the blind lead the blind, both shall fall into the ditch.'
Peter asks Jesus to explain. Jesus says, food goes into the stomach and passes away. But what comes out of the mouth comes from the heart. Out of the heart come evil thoughts, cruelty, unfaithfulness, stealing, lies and words against God. These are what make a person unclean before God, not eating with unwashed hands.
Then Jesus goes north, to the region of Tyre and Sidon, outside the land of Israel. A woman of Canaan comes out, crying, 'Have mercy on me, O Lord, thou Son of David; my daughter is grievously vexed with a devil.' She was not Jewish. Canaanites had been enemies of Israel long ago. Yet she called Jesus Son of David, the name of the promised King.
But he answered her not a word. The disciples say, send her away, she keeps crying after us. Jesus says, I am sent only to the lost sheep of the house of Israel. But she comes and kneels before him, and says, 'Lord, help me.'
Jesus says, it is not right to take the children’s bread and throw it to the dogs. It sounds harsh to us. Jesus was speaking of the little dogs kept in a family home, and of the order of his work: first to Israel, and then to the nations. He seems to be testing her, drawing out her faith.
And she gives an answer full of humble faith. 'Truth, Lord: yet the dogs eat of the crumbs which fall from their masters’ table.' Even the crumbs from your table would be enough for me.
And Jesus answers her.
Jesus goes back to the sea of Galilee, and sits on a mountain. Great crowds come, bringing the lame, the blind, those who could not speak, those with crippled limbs, and many others. They lay them at his feet, and he heals them. The crowds are amazed, 'and they glorified the God of Israel.' Many in this crowd were probably not Jewish either. And they praised Israel’s God.
Then Jesus calls his disciples, and tells them how he feels about the crowd.
The disciples ask, where could we get enough bread in this lonely place for such a crowd? Jesus asks, how many loaves do you have? Seven, and a few small fish. Jesus has the crowd sit down. He takes the seven loaves and the fish, gives thanks, breaks them, and gives them to the disciples, who give them to the people. Everyone eats and is satisfied, and they pick up seven baskets full of leftovers. Four thousand men ate, besides women and children.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 8. 'This people draweth nigh unto me with their mouth, and honoureth me with their lips; but their heart is far from me.' It is possible to say all the right words in worship, and keep all the right customs, while our hearts are far from God. God is not fooled by outward show. He wants our hearts. When we sing or pray, we can ask ourselves, do I mean this? Is my heart near to God, or only my lips?
Verse 28. 'Then Jesus answered and said unto her, O woman, great is thy faith: be it unto thee even as thou wilt. And her daughter was made whole from that very hour.' The religious leaders from Jerusalem had the Scriptures, but missed the point. This foreign woman had nothing, but she had great faith. She did not argue or demand. She trusted that even a crumb of Jesus’ power would be enough. And Jesus praised her faith, and healed her daughter. Keep coming to Jesus, even when the answer seems slow.
Verse 32. 'Then Jesus called his disciples unto him, and said, I have compassion on the multitude, because they continue with me now three days, and have nothing to eat: and I will not send them away fasting, lest they faint in the way.' Jesus cared about people’s hunger, not just their souls. He noticed they had been with him a long time, and were tired and hungry. He would not send them away empty. Jesus cares about every part of our lives, even our ordinary daily needs.
God said through Samuel, 'for man looketh on the outward appearance, but the LORD looketh on the heart.' And the psalmist wrote, 'The LORD is nigh unto all them that call upon him, to all that call upon him in truth.'
When you pray or worship today, ask God to bring your heart close, not just your lips. And if you have been praying for something for a long time, do not give up. Keep coming to Jesus, like the woman of Canaan.
Here is today’s question. The woman of Canaan kept asking, even when Jesus was silent at first. What helps you keep trusting God when an answer seems slow?
Let us pray. Lord Jesus, keep my heart close to you, and not only my lips. Clean my heart from the things that make it unclean. Give me faith like the woman of Canaan, humble and persistent. Thank you that you care about all my needs. Amen.
