A young man runs up to Jesus. He is rich, he is good, and he has kept the rules all his life. He asks Jesus the most important question there is: what must I do to have eternal life? Jesus gives him an answer. And the young man walks away, very sad. Because Jesus asked him for the one thing he could not let go.
In chapter eighteen, Jesus set a little child in the middle of his disciples and said, unless you become as little children, you will not enter the kingdom of heaven. He promised to be with two or three gathered in his name, and told Peter to forgive until seventy times seven.
Now, in chapter nineteen, Jesus leaves Galilee and heads south towards Jerusalem. He is asked about marriage, he welcomes children, and he meets a rich young man. Listen for who Jesus makes time for. And listen for what is impossible with men, but possible with God.
Jesus leaves Galilee and comes into Judaea, beyond the river Jordan. Great crowds follow him, and he heals them there.
Some Pharisees come to test him with a question about divorce. Is it lawful for a man to put away his wife for any reason at all? In those days, some teachers said a man could divorce his wife for almost anything.
Jesus takes them back to the very beginning. Have you not read that the one who made them at the beginning made them male and female? And he said, for this reason a man shall leave his father and mother, and cleave to his wife, and the two shall be one flesh.
And then Jesus draws the conclusion.
The Pharisees ask, then why did Moses allow divorce? Jesus answers, Moses allowed it because your hearts were hard, 'but from the beginning it was not so.' God’s plan for marriage is a lifelong promise of love. Jesus was protecting wives, who in those days could be sent away with nothing. The disciples are surprised at how seriously Jesus takes marriage. Jesus says that God gives some people the gift to marry, and some the gift to stay single for the kingdom of heaven.
Then people bring little children to Jesus, so he can lay his hands on them and pray. The disciples tell them off. Jesus is too busy for children, they thought.
But Jesus says something different.
'And he laid his hands on them, and departed thence.'
Then a young man comes to Jesus. 'Good Master, what good thing shall I do, that I may have eternal life?' Jesus says, why do you call me good? Only God is good. If you want to enter into life, keep the commandments. Which ones? the man asks. Jesus lists them. Do not murder. Be faithful in marriage. Do not steal. Do not lie. Honour your father and mother. And love your neighbour as yourself.
The young man says, 'All these things have I kept from my youth up: what lack I yet?' He sensed that something was still missing.
Jesus says, 'If thou wilt be perfect, go and sell that thou hast, and give to the poor, and thou shalt have treasure in heaven: and come and follow me.' Jesus does not ask everyone to sell everything. But he saw that this young man’s money was the thing that came first in his heart. 'But when the young man heard that saying, he went away sorrowful: for he had great possessions.'
Jesus says to his disciples, it is hard for a rich man to enter the kingdom of heaven. 'It is easier for a camel to go through the eye of a needle, than for a rich man to enter into the kingdom of God.' The camel was the biggest animal in the land. The eye of a needle is the tiniest hole. It is a picture of something impossible.
The disciples are amazed. 'Who then can be saved?'
And Jesus looks at them, and answers.
Peter says, look, we have left everything to follow you. What will we have? Jesus promises that those who have left houses, family or land for his sake will receive a hundred times as much, and eternal life. 'But many that are first shall be last; and the last shall be first.'
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 6. 'Wherefore they are no more twain, but one flesh. What therefore God hath joined together, let not man put asunder.' Twain means two. Put asunder means pull apart. Jesus points to God’s design from the very beginning. Marriage is not just a contract between two people. It is God who joins them together. That is why it is precious, and why it should be guarded with love and faithfulness. Many families have known the pain of a broken marriage. God sees that pain, and his grace is there too.
Verse 14. 'But Jesus said, Suffer little children, and forbid them not, to come unto me: for of such is the kingdom of heaven.' Suffer means allow, let. The disciples thought children were not important enough for Jesus. Jesus said, let them come. Do not stop them. Jesus always has time for children. And if you are young and listening to this, Jesus wants you to come to him. You do not have to wait until you are grown up.
Verse 26. 'But Jesus beheld them, and said unto them, With men this is impossible; but with God all things are possible.' No one can save themselves, whether rich or poor. We cannot earn eternal life by being good enough, or by giving enough away. It is impossible. But God can do what we cannot. He can change any heart, even the heart of someone who loves money more than anything. Salvation is God’s work, and nothing is impossible for him.
In the beginning, 'Therefore shall a man leave his father and his mother, and shall cleave unto his wife: and they shall be one flesh.' And the angel told Mary, 'For with God nothing shall be impossible.'
Is there something in your life that is hard to let go of, that might be coming before Jesus? Bring it to him honestly. And if you know children, remember that Jesus welcomes them. Help a child come closer to Jesus this week.
Here is today’s question. The rich young man went away sad because he had great possessions. What do you think Jesus would ask you to let go of, to follow him more fully?
Let us pray. Lord Jesus, thank you that you welcome little children, and that you welcome me. Thank you for your good plan for families. Show me anything I love more than you, and help me to let it go. Thank you that with God all things are possible, even saving me. Amen.
