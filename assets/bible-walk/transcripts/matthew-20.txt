Imagine working hard all day long in the hot sun. At the end of the day, you line up for your pay. Ahead of you are some workers who only started an hour ago. They get a full day’s wages. You think, if they got that much, I will surely get more! But when it is your turn, you get exactly the same. Is that fair? Jesus told this story. And it is one of the most surprising stories he ever told.
In chapter nineteen, Jesus said, what God hath joined together, let not man put asunder. He welcomed little children, and a rich young man went away sad because he had great possessions. Jesus said, with God all things are possible, and the last shall be first.
Now, in chapter twenty, Jesus tells a story to explain what the last being first means. Then, on the road to Jerusalem, he tells his disciples what greatness really looks like. Listen for what the vineyard owner says to the grumbling workers. And listen for why Jesus came.
Jesus says, the kingdom of heaven is like a landowner who went out at first light to hire workers for his vineyard. He agreed to pay them a penny, a full day’s wage, and sent them to work.
Later in the day, he went to the marketplace and saw others standing around with nothing to do. You go into the vineyard too, he said, and I will pay you whatever is right. And they went. He did the same thing again in the middle of the day, and later still.
Then, with only one hour of work left, he went out and found still more people standing around. Why are you standing here idle all day? Because no one has hired us, they said. He said, you go into the vineyard too.
When the work was done, the owner told his manager, call the workers and pay them, starting with the last ones hired. Those who had worked only one hour each received a penny. A full day’s wage!
When the first workers came, they expected more. But they each received a penny too. And they grumbled. These last ones worked only one hour, and you made them equal to us, who worked all day in the burning heat.
The owner answered one of them. 'Friend, I do thee no wrong: didst not thou agree with me for a penny?' Take your pay and go. I want to give this last one the same as you. 'Is it not lawful for me to do what I will with mine own? Is thine eye evil, because I am good?'
And Jesus ends the story.
Then, as Jesus goes up to Jerusalem, he takes the twelve aside on the road, and tells them for the third time what will happen. The Son of man will be betrayed to the chief priests and scribes. They will condemn him to death, and hand him over to the Romans to be mocked, beaten and crucified. 'and the third day he shall rise again.'
Straight after this, the mother of James and John comes to Jesus with her sons, and kneels before him. Grant that my two sons may sit, one on your right hand and one on your left, in your kingdom. They wanted the best seats.
Jesus says, you do not know what you are asking. Are you able to drink the cup I am about to drink? The cup meant the suffering ahead of him. They say, we are able. Jesus says, you will indeed drink from my cup. But the places at my right and left are for those my Father has prepared them for.
When the other ten disciples hear about it, they are angry with the two brothers. Perhaps they wanted those seats too.
So Jesus calls them all together. You know that the rulers of the nations lord it over their people, and great men use their power over others. But it will not be so among you.
He tells them how greatness works in his kingdom.
And whoever wants to be first must be your servant. And then Jesus points to himself.
As they leave Jericho, a great crowd follows. Two blind men are sitting by the road. When they hear Jesus is passing, they cry out, 'Have mercy on us, O Lord, thou Son of David.' The crowd tells them to be quiet. But they shout even louder. Jesus stops, and calls them. What do you want me to do for you? 'Lord, that our eyes may be opened.' Jesus has compassion on them and touches their eyes. Immediately they can see, and they follow him.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 16. 'So the last shall be first, and the first last: for many be called, but few chosen.' The workers who came first thought they deserved more. But the owner was not unfair to them. He gave them what he promised. He was simply generous to the others. God’s kingdom is not about earning. It is about grace. Someone who comes to God late in life receives the same forgiveness and the same eternal life as someone who has followed him for many years. Instead of grumbling, we can rejoice that God is so good.
Verse 26. 'But it shall not be so among you: but whosoever will be great among you, let him be your minister:' A minister here means a servant, someone who helps others. In the world, great people are served by others. In Jesus’ kingdom, great people serve others. The way up is down. The disciples were arguing about the best seats. Jesus said, look for the lowest place, and serve.
Verse 28. 'Even as the Son of man came not to be ministered unto, but to minister, and to give his life a ransom for many.' This is why Jesus came. Not to be served, but to serve. And to give his life as a ransom. A ransom is a price paid to set someone free. We were held captive by sin, and could not free ourselves. Jesus paid the price with his own life. He was about to do it in Jerusalem. The greatest one of all became the servant of all.
Isaiah wrote of God’s servant, 'he bare the sin of many, and made intercession for the transgressors.' And Paul wrote that Jesus 'made himself of no reputation, and took upon him the form of a servant'.
Is there someone you have been comparing yourself with, thinking you deserve more? Thank God for his generosity instead. And look for one way to serve someone today, especially in a way no one will notice or thank you for.
Here is today’s question. Jesus said, whoever wants to be great must be a servant. What is one way you could serve someone at home, at school or at work this week?
Let us pray. Lord Jesus, thank you that you came not to be served, but to serve, and to give your life a ransom for many. Thank you for your grace, which I could never earn. Keep me from grumbling and comparing, and make me a servant, like you. Amen.
