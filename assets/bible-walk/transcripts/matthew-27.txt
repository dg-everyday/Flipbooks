Two prisoners stand before the crowd. One is Barabbas, a well known criminal. The other is Jesus, who has healed the sick and taught about the love of God. The governor can set one of them free. He asks the crowd, which one do you want? And the answer comes back, loud and clear. Barabbas. A guilty man walks free, and the innocent one takes his place.
In chapter twenty six, a woman anointed Jesus with costly ointment, Judas agreed to betray him for thirty pieces of silver, and Jesus shared the last supper with his disciples. In Gethsemane he prayed, not as I will, but as thou wilt. He was arrested and tried before the high priest, and Peter denied him three times, and wept bitterly.
Now, in chapter twenty seven, Jesus is taken to the Roman governor, Pontius Pilate, and crucified. This is a hard chapter to hear, but it is the heart of the good news. Listen for what the crowd and the mockers say. And listen for what happens in the temple the moment Jesus dies.
The chief priests and elders tie Jesus up, and hand him over to Pontius Pilate, the Roman governor. Only the Romans could put someone to death.
When Judas sees that Jesus has been condemned, he is filled with regret. He brings the thirty pieces of silver back to the chief priests. I have sinned, he says, I have betrayed an innocent man. But they say, what is that to us? That is your problem. Judas throws the silver down in the temple, and goes away, and takes his own life. It is one of the saddest moments in the Bible. Judas was sorry, but he did not come back to Jesus, who would have forgiven him. If you ever feel that hopeless, please tell someone you trust, and tell God. There is always a way back.
Jesus stands before Pilate. 'Art thou the King of the Jews?' Pilate asks. Jesus answers, 'Thou sayest.' When the chief priests accuse him, he says nothing at all, and Pilate is amazed.
At the feast, the governor used to release one prisoner chosen by the crowd. Pilate asks, shall I release Barabbas, or Jesus who is called Christ? He knew the leaders had handed Jesus over out of envy. Even Pilate’s wife sends word, have nothing to do with that just man. But the leaders persuade the crowd, and they shout, Barabbas.
Then Pilate asks another question.
Why? What evil has he done? But they shout all the louder. When Pilate sees that a riot is starting, he washes his hands in front of the crowd. I am innocent of the blood of this just person. But washing his hands could not make him innocent. He had the power to set Jesus free, and he did not use it. Pilate releases Barabbas, has Jesus whipped, and hands him over to be crucified.
The soldiers put a scarlet robe on Jesus and a crown of thorns on his head. They kneel and mock him, Hail, King of the Jews! Then they lead him away to crucify him.
On the way, they force Simon, a man from Cyrene, to carry the cross. They come to a place called Golgotha, the place of a skull. They crucify Jesus, and divide his clothes by lots, as the psalm said. Above his head they put the charge against him. 'THIS IS JESUS THE KING OF THE JEWS.' They meant it as a joke. But it was true. Two thieves are crucified with him, one on each side.
People passing by mock him. If you are the Son of God, come down from the cross. The chief priests and elders mock him too.
And they say, 'He saved others; himself he cannot save.' They meant it as an insult. But it was true. If Jesus had saved himself, he could not have saved us.
From the middle of the day, darkness covers all the land for three hours. Then Jesus cries out with a loud voice.
Some think he is calling Elijah. Then Jesus cries out again with a loud voice, 'yielded up the ghost.' He gave up his spirit. He died.
And at that very moment, something happens in the temple.
When the centurion and the soldiers guarding Jesus see the earthquake and all that has happened, they are terrified. 'Truly this was the Son of God.' A Roman soldier, a foreigner, sees who Jesus is.
Women who had followed Jesus from Galilee watch from a distance, Mary Magdalene among them. Later, a rich disciple named Joseph of Arimathaea asks Pilate for the body, wraps it in clean linen, and lays it in his own new tomb, cut in the rock. He rolls a great stone against the door. The chief priests remember that Jesus said he would rise after three days, so they have the stone sealed, and set a guard.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 22. 'Pilate saith unto them, What shall I do then with Jesus which is called Christ? They all say unto him, Let him be crucified.' Pilate’s question is the most important question anyone can ask. What shall I do with Jesus? Pilate tried to avoid answering it. He tried to pass the decision to the crowd, and then to wash his hands of it. But no one can stay neutral about Jesus. Every person must answer Pilate’s question for themselves.
Verse 46. 'And about the ninth hour Jesus cried with a loud voice, saying, Eli, Eli, lama sabachthani? that is to say, My God, my God, why hast thou forsaken me?' These are the first words of Psalm twenty two, written by David long before, a psalm that describes suffering, mocking, and clothes divided by lots. Christians have understood this cry in different ways, but all agree it shows the depth of what Jesus suffered. He took the weight of our sin, and felt the loneliness that sin brings. He was forsaken, so that we never need to be.
Verse 51. 'And, behold, the veil of the temple was rent in twain from the top to the bottom; and the earth did quake, and the rocks rent;' The veil was a thick curtain in the temple that separated the most holy place, where God’s presence dwelt, from everyone else. Only the high priest could go behind it, once a year. When Jesus died, it was torn in two, from the top to the bottom. Not by human hands, but by God. The way into God’s presence was now open, for everyone, through Jesus.
Isaiah wrote, 'But he was wounded for our transgressions, he was bruised for our iniquities: the chastisement of our peace was upon him; and with his stripes we are healed.' And Peter wrote, 'Who his own self bare our sins in his own body on the tree'.
Barabbas walked free because Jesus took his place. In a way, we are all Barabbas. Jesus took the punishment we deserved. Take a quiet moment today simply to thank him. And answer Pilate’s question for yourself: what will I do with Jesus?
Here is today’s question. The mockers said, he saved others; himself he cannot save. Why do you think Jesus did not come down from the cross, though he could have?
Let us pray. Lord Jesus, thank you that you did not save yourself, so that you could save me. Thank you for taking my place, as you took Barabbas’s place. Thank you that the veil was torn, and the way to God is open. I will not wash my hands of you. You are my Saviour and my King. Amen.
