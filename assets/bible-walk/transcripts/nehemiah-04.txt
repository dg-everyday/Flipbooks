A builder kneels on top of a wall, lifting a heavy stone into place. Strapped to his side is a sword. Next to him, a man carries a basket of rubble on his shoulder with one hand, and grips a spear in the other. Out on the hills, enemies are watching, waiting for a chance to attack. Somewhere along the wall, a man stands with a trumpet, ready to sound the alarm. And still, they keep building.
In chapter three, priests and goldsmiths, perfume makers and rulers, men and daughters, all worked on the wall, each family repairing the part in front of their own home. The nobles of Tekoa would not help, but Baruch worked earnestly. And piece by piece, the whole circle came together.
Now, in chapter four, the enemies attack. First with words, then with threats. Listen for how Nehemiah responds. And listen for how the people keep working.
When Sanballat hears that the Jews are building the wall, he is furious. And he mocks them, in front of his friends and the army of Samaria. 'What do these feeble Jews? will they fortify themselves? will they sacrifice? will they make an end in a day? will they revive the stones out of the heaps of the rubbish which are burned?' What do these weak Jews think they are doing? Do they think they can finish in a day? Can they bring burnt stones back to life?
And Tobiah, standing beside him, adds a joke. Even if a fox climbed up on that wall they are building, it would fall down!
How does Nehemiah respond? He does not shout back. He prays. Hear, O our God, for we are despised. He pours out his hurt and frustration to God, and asks God to deal with their enemies. He leaves the matter with God, instead of taking revenge himself.
And then they get on with the work.
But when Sanballat, Tobiah, the Arabs, the Ammonites and the people of Ashdod hear that the walls are going up, and the gaps are being closed, they are very angry. They all plot together to come and fight against Jerusalem, and cause confusion.
And what does Nehemiah do? 'Nevertheless we made our prayer unto our God, and set a watch against them day and night, because of them.' They prayed, and they set guards. Both. Trusting God, and being sensible.
But then discouragement sets in, from inside. The people of Judah say, the strength of the workers is giving out, and there is so much rubble. We cannot build the wall. They were tired, and the task seemed too big.
Then the enemies spread a rumour. They will not know or see anything, until we are right among them, and we will stop the work. And the Jews who lived near the enemies kept coming, ten times over, warning, they will attack you from every side!
So Nehemiah takes action. He stations people behind the lowest parts of the wall, in the open places, by families, with their swords, spears and bows. And then he stands up and speaks.
And when their enemies hear that the plot is known, and that God has frustrated their plans, the people all go back to the wall, every one to his work.
From then on, half of Nehemiah’s men work, and the other half stand guard, with spears, shields, bows and armour. And the officers stand behind the people.
Nehemiah says to them, the work is great, and spread out, and we are far apart from one another on the wall. Wherever you hear the trumpet sound, come to us there. 'our God shall fight for us.'
So they work hard, from first light until the stars come out, half of them holding spears. And Nehemiah tells everyone to stay inside Jerusalem at the end of the day, so that they can guard the city, and work again the next day. Nehemiah and his men do not even take off their clothes to sleep, except to wash. They are ready, all the time.
Mockery, threats, rumours, tiredness and fear. But the people kept building.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 6. 'So built we the wall; and all the wall was joined together unto the half thereof: for the people had a mind to work.' A mind to work means their hearts were in it. They were determined. The enemies laughed. But the people did not stop to argue. They kept building, and the whole wall reached half its height. When people have a mind to work, great things can be done.
Verse 14. 'And I looked, and rose up, and said unto the nobles, and to the rulers, and to the rest of the people, Be not ye afraid of them: remember the Lord, which is great and terrible, and fight for your brethren, your sons, and your daughters, your wives, and your houses.' Remember the Lord. That was the cure for their fear. When we look at our problems, they seem huge. When we remember how great God is, they shrink. And Nehemiah reminded them what they were fighting for: their families and their homes.
Verse 17. 'They which builded on the wall, and they that bare burdens, with those that laded, every one with one of his hands wrought in the work, and with the other hand held a weapon.' A tool in one hand, a weapon in the other. They built, and they guarded. Following God is like that. We build what is good, and we also guard against what would destroy it. Paul tells us that our weapon is the sword of the Spirit, which is the Word of God.
Paul wrote, 'the sword of the Spirit, which is the word of God'. And Jesus said, 'Watch and pray, that ye enter not into temptation'.
Is someone mocking you, or something discouraging you, in what you are doing for God? Do not stop to argue. Pray, and keep building. And remember the Lord, who is great and awesome. He fights for his people.
Here is today’s question. Nehemiah’s people prayed, and set a guard. How can you both trust God, and act wisely, when you face trouble?
Let us pray. Lord God, when others mock or threaten, help me to bring it to you, and keep on with the work you have given me. When I am tired and discouraged, remind me how great you are. Give me a mind to work, and help me both to build and to guard what is good. Thank you that you fight for your people. Amen.
