A man stands on top of a high wall, hard at work. Down below, a messenger calls up to him. Come down! Some important people want to meet you, in a village out in the plain. It will only take a little while. The man looks down at the messenger. He knows it is a trap. And he calls back, I am doing a great work. I cannot come down.
In chapter five, the poor cried out because rich Jews were charging them interest, taking their land, and even their children. Nehemiah was very angry, and made the nobles give everything back. He himself never took the governor’s allowance, because of the fear of God, and he prayed, think upon me, my God, for good.
Now, in chapter six, the wall is nearly finished. Listen for all the tricks the enemies try. And listen for how Nehemiah sees through every one.
By now, the wall is complete. There are no gaps left, though the doors have not yet been hung in the gates. And Sanballat, Tobiah and Geshem hear about it. Mocking has not worked. Threats have not worked. So they try trickery.
Sanballat and Geshem send a message. Come, let us meet together in one of the villages in the plain of Ono. The plain of Ono was far from Jerusalem, near their own territory. 'But they thought to do me mischief.' Nehemiah knew it was a trap.
And he sends back his famous answer.
They send the same message four times. And four times, Nehemiah gives the same answer.
So they try something else. The fifth time, Sanballat sends his servant with an open letter, one that anyone could read along the way. It says, it is being reported among the nations, and Geshem says it is true, that you and the Jews are planning to rebel. That is why you are building the wall. They say you want to make yourself king. They say you have even appointed prophets to announce, there is a king in Judah! The king of Persia is going to hear about this. So come, let us talk.
It was a clever lie. If the king of Persia heard such a rumour, he would stop the work at once, and Nehemiah would be in great danger. And by sending an open letter, Sanballat made sure everyone in Judah would hear the rumour too.
But Nehemiah does not panic. He sends back a short reply. Nothing like this has happened. You are making it up out of your own head.
And then he tells us what was really going on, and prays.
Then comes the most cunning trick of all. Nehemiah goes to visit a man named Shemaiah, who claims to be a prophet. And Shemaiah says, let us meet in the house of God, inside the temple, and shut the doors. Your enemies are coming for you. They are coming to take your life.
It sounded like friendly advice, even a word from God. But Nehemiah was not a priest. God’s law said only priests could go inside the temple building. If he hid there, he would be breaking God’s law, and showing that he was afraid.
And Nehemiah says, 'Should such a man as I flee? and who is there, that, being as I am, would go into the temple to save his life? I will not go in.'
And then he realises the truth. 'And, lo, I perceived that God had not sent him'. Tobiah and Sanballat had hired Shemaiah. They wanted to frighten Nehemiah into sinning, so they could give him a bad name and discredit him. There were other false prophets too, including a prophetess named Noadiah, who tried to frighten him. And Nehemiah asks God to remember what they had done.
But none of it worked. And here is the result.
Fifty two days. Less than two months. Walls that had lain in ruins for generations, rebuilt in under two months.
And what did the enemies think?
But even then, the opposition did not completely stop. Some of the nobles of Judah were friendly with Tobiah, because he had married into important Jewish families. They kept sending letters back and forth with him. They kept telling Nehemiah what a good man Tobiah was, and passing on to Tobiah everything Nehemiah said. And Tobiah kept sending letters to frighten Nehemiah.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 3. 'And I sent messengers unto them, saying, I am doing a great work, so that I cannot come down: why should the work cease, whilst I leave it, and come down to you?' Nehemiah knew what mattered most, and he would not be distracted. Not every invitation is worth accepting. Not every argument is worth having. Sometimes the wisest thing is to say, I am doing a great work, and stay focused on what God has given us to do.
Verse 9. 'For they all made us afraid, saying, Their hands shall be weakened from the work, that it be not done. Now therefore, O God, strengthen my hands.' The enemies wanted to weaken their hands. Nehemiah’s answer was a quick, simple prayer: O God, strengthen my hands. When fear tries to weaken us, we can ask God for strength. It is one of the best prayers we can pray.
Verse 16. 'And it came to pass, that when all our enemies heard thereof, and all the heathen that were about us saw these things, they were much cast down in their own eyes: for they perceived that this work was wrought of our God.' Wrought means done, accomplished. Even the enemies could see it: God did this. When God’s people work faithfully, through every difficulty, the result points to him. The best answer to those who mock our faith is the work God does through us.
Jesus said, 'No man, having put his hand to the plough, and looking back, is fit for the kingdom of God.' And Isaiah heard God say, 'Fear thou not; for I am with thee'.
What is the great work God has given you to do? Your family, your studies, your job, your service? Is anything trying to pull you away from it? Say to it, I am doing a great work, I cannot come down. And pray, O God, strengthen my hands.
Here is today’s question. The enemies tried mockery, threats, lies and fear to stop Nehemiah. Which of those do you find hardest to stand against, and why?
Let us pray. O God, strengthen my hands. When I am distracted, help me to remember the great work you have given me. When people try to frighten me, give me courage. Give me wisdom to see through lies and traps. And let my life show others that your work is wrought by you. Amen.
