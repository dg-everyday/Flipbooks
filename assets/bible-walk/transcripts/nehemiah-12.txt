Two great processions climb up onto the top of the city wall, one at each end. Singers, priests with trumpets, musicians with harps and cymbals, and leaders of the people. Then they begin to walk, in opposite directions, singing as they go, all the way around the walls. Below them, the whole city looks up. And the music is so loud and joyful that people hear it in the hills, far away.
In chapter eleven, the people cast lots for one family in ten to move into Jerusalem, and some willingly offered themselves, and were blessed for it. Mattaniah was given the job of leading the thanksgiving in prayer. And the people of Judah settled again in their towns, from Beer-sheba to the valley of Hinnom.
Now, in chapter twelve, the wall is dedicated to God. Listen for how much singing there is. And listen for who joins in the joy.
The chapter begins with more lists. The priests and Levites who first came back with Zerubbabel and Jeshua, many years before. Then the line of high priests that followed. Then the leaders of the Levites in later years, who stood in two groups, facing each other, to praise and give thanks, taking turns, just as king David had arranged long ago.
These lists remind us that worship in Jerusalem was not something new. It went back through the generations, to David, and beyond. Each generation took its turn.
And then comes the great day.
They gather the singers from all the villages around Jerusalem, where they had built homes. The priests and Levites purify themselves, and then purify the people, the gates and the wall. Everything is made ready for God.
Then Nehemiah takes charge. He brings the leaders of Judah up onto the top of the wall, and divides them into two great choirs, two companies of people giving thanks.
The first choir goes to the right, along the top of the wall. Half the leaders of Judah follow behind it, with priests blowing trumpets, and Levites playing the instruments of David. And Ezra the scribe walks in front of them. They go past the fountain gate, up the stairs of the city of David, all the way round to the water gate in the east.
The second choir goes the other way, to the left, along the top of the wall. And Nehemiah follows behind it, with the other half of the people. They pass the tower of the furnaces, the broad wall, the gate of Ephraim, the old gate, the fish gate, and the towers, to the sheep gate.
Think about where they were walking. On top of the very wall they had built with their own hands, while enemies mocked them, and said even a fox would knock it down. Now hundreds of people were marching on top of it, singing. Tobiah’s fox would have had quite a surprise.
And the two choirs meet, coming from opposite directions, at the house of God. The priests blow their trumpets. And the singers sing loudly.
That day, they offer great sacrifices, and rejoice.
Then, the writer tells us, people were appointed to look after the storerooms, for the offerings, the firstfruits and the tithes, to collect the portions for the priests and Levites from the fields around the towns. Judah was glad for the priests and Levites who served. The singers and the gatekeepers did their work faithfully, as David and Solomon had commanded. And in the days of Zerubbabel and of Nehemiah, all Israel gave the daily portions for the singers and the gatekeepers.
The covenant of chapter ten was being kept. For now.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 27. 'And at the dedication of the wall of Jerusalem they sought the Levites out of all their places, to bring them to Jerusalem, to keep the dedication with gladness, both with thanksgivings, and with singing, with cymbals, psalteries, and with harps.' Psalteries are a kind of stringed instrument. To dedicate something means to give it to God. They did not just finish the wall and walk away. They gave it to God, with music and thanksgiving. When God helps us finish something, it is good to stop, and thank him, and give it back to him.
Verse 30. 'And the priests and the Levites purified themselves, and purified the people, and the gates, and the wall.' Before they celebrated, they made themselves ready. The priests started with themselves, then the people, then the gates and the wall. Before we come to worship God, it is good to prepare our hearts, starting with ourselves.
Verse 43. 'Also that day they offered great sacrifices, and rejoiced: for God had made them rejoice with great joy: the wives also and the children rejoiced: so that the joy of Jerusalem was heard even afar off.' God had made them rejoice. Their joy came from him. And everyone joined in: the leaders, the priests, the singers, the wives and the children. And the joy was so great it was heard far away. When God’s people truly rejoice in him, others notice. Joy is a witness to the world.
The psalmist wrote, 'Enter into his gates with thanksgiving, and into his courts with praise'. And John wrote, 'And I John saw the holy city, new Jerusalem, coming down from God out of heaven'.
Is there something God has helped you finish, that you have not thanked him for? Stop and dedicate it to him today. And let your joy in God be something others can hear.
Here is today’s question. The joy of Jerusalem was heard even afar off. How might your joy in God be noticed by the people around you?
Let us pray. Lord God, thank you for every good work you help us finish. I give it back to you. Prepare my heart to worship you. Make me rejoice with great joy, as you made your people rejoice. Let my family and I rejoice together, and let our joy in you be heard by others. Amen.
