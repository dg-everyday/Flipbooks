When God brought Israel out of Egypt, every firstborn son in the land was in danger. But in the homes of Israel, where the blood of the lamb was on the doorposts, the firstborn were spared. They were saved. And from then on, God said, every firstborn of Israel belongs to me. Now, in the wilderness, God shows what that means.
In chapter two, God arranged the camp. Every man pitched by his own standard, with Judah on the east, Reuben on the south, Ephraim on the west, and Dan on the north. In the middle, the Levites camped around the tabernacle. Everything faced the place where God dwelt. And the people did all that the LORD commanded.
Now, in chapter three, God sets apart the tribe of Levi. Listen for why the Levites belong to God in a special way. And listen for how God keeps careful count, even of the smallest detail.
The chapter begins with the family of Aaron and Moses.
Aaron had four sons, Nadab, Abihu, Eleazar and Ithamar. They were the priests, anointed to serve God at the altar.
The writer reminds us of something sad.
Then the LORD says to Moses, bring the tribe of Levi near, and present them to Aaron the priest, so that they may serve him. The Levites are to help the priests, and to look after the tabernacle and everything in it, serving on behalf of the whole people.
Aaron and his sons are the priests. The rest of the tribe of Levi are their helpers. And anyone else who tries to take on the priests’ work, God says, will be put to death. The holy things of God are not to be treated lightly.
Then God explains why the Levites belong to him.
God says, I have taken the Levites from among the children of Israel, in place of every firstborn son. So the Levites shall be mine.
The firstborn of Israel belonged to God, because he had spared them in Egypt. Now, instead of taking the firstborn son from every family, God takes the whole tribe of Levi to serve him. The Levites would stand in the place of all the firstborn.
Then God tells Moses to count the Levites, every male from a month old and upward. And Moses counts them, according to the word of the LORD.
The tribe of Levi has three families, named after Levi’s three sons: Gershon, Kohath and Merari.
The family of Gershon camps on the west side of the tabernacle. Their job is to look after the curtains and coverings of the tent, and the hangings of the court.
The family of Kohath camps on the south side. They have the most holy work of all. They look after the ark, the table, the lampstand, the altars, and the holy vessels.
The family of Merari camps on the north side. They look after the heavy wooden frame of the tabernacle, the boards and bars and pillars and sockets.
And on the east side, right in front of the entrance, camp Moses and Aaron and Aaron’s sons, guarding the way into the sanctuary.
Every family had its own job. Some carried curtains. Some carried boards. Some carried the holiest things. Every job was needed, and every job was service to God.
Then the LORD tells Moses to count all the firstborn sons of Israel, from a month old and upward. And to take the Levites in their place, and the Levites’ cattle in place of the firstborn of Israel’s cattle.
When Moses counts, there are about twenty two thousand Levites. But there are two hundred and seventy three more firstborn sons than there are Levites. So what happens to those extra firstborn? They must be redeemed, bought back, with five shekels of silver each. And Moses gives this redemption money to Aaron and his sons.
God did not round up, or forget the extra two hundred and seventy three. Every single firstborn was accounted for. Every one was either represented by a Levite, or redeemed with a price.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 4. 'And Nadab and Abihu died before the LORD, when they offered strange fire before the LORD, in the wilderness of Sinai, and they had no children: and Eleazar and Ithamar ministered in the priest’s office in the sight of Aaron their father.' The book of Leviticus tells how Nadab and Abihu offered fire that God had not commanded, and died. It is a sobering reminder, at the start of the journey, that God is holy. Serving God is a privilege, but it must be done his way, not our own.
Verse 13. 'Because all the firstborn are mine; for on the day that I smote all the firstborn in the land of Egypt I hallowed unto me all the firstborn in Israel, both man and beast: mine shall they be: I am the LORD.' Hallowed means made holy, set apart. Israel’s firstborn were saved by the blood of the lamb. And because they were saved, they belonged to God. The New Testament says the same of us. We were bought with a price, not with silver, but with the precious blood of Christ. So we belong to God.
Verse 45. 'Take the Levites instead of all the firstborn among the children of Israel, and the cattle of the Levites instead of their cattle; and the Levites shall be mine: I am the LORD.' Instead of. The Levites stood in the place of others. This idea of one standing in the place of another runs all through the Bible. And it points to Jesus, who stood in our place, and gave his life as a ransom for many.
Peter wrote, 'Forasmuch as ye know that ye were not redeemed with corruptible things, as silver and gold, from your vain conversation received by tradition from your fathers; But with the precious blood of Christ, as of a lamb without blemish and without spot'. And Paul wrote, 'For ye are bought with a price'.
You belong to God, bought with the precious blood of Christ. How could you live today as someone who belongs to him? Offer him your work, whatever it is, as service to him.
Here is today’s question. Every Levite family had a different job, curtains, boards, or holy things, but all of it was service to God. What job has God given you, and how can you do it as service to him?
Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that I belong to you, bought with the precious blood of Jesus. Thank you that you count every one of your people, and forget no one. Help me to serve you in whatever work you give me, big or small, and to treat holy things with reverence. Amen.
