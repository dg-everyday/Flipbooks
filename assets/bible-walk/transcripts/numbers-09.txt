Imagine living in a tent, and never knowing how long you will stay in one place. You wake up, and look out at the great tent in the middle of the camp. If the cloud is still resting on it, you stay. If it has lifted, you pack up everything and go. Sometimes you stay a year. Sometimes you leave the next day. All you know is: follow the cloud.
In chapter eight, Aaron lit the seven lamps of the golden lampstand, so that they gave light. The Levites were cleansed, and the whole people laid hands on them, and gave them to God. The Levites shall be mine, God said. And the older Levites stepped back from heavy work, to help their younger brothers.
Now, in chapter nine, Israel keeps the Passover in the wilderness, and learns to follow the cloud. Listen for how God makes room for people who missed out. And listen for how often the people moved at God’s command.
The LORD speaks to Moses in the wilderness of Sinai, a year after they came out of Egypt. Let the children of Israel keep the Passover at its appointed time.
The Passover remembered how God rescued Israel from Egypt, when the blood of the lamb on the doorposts kept their firstborn safe. Now, a year later, God wanted them to remember.
So the people keep the Passover in the wilderness, at the time God appointed, exactly as the LORD commanded Moses.
But there is a problem. Some men had touched a dead body, and so they were ceremonially unclean, and could not take part in the Passover that day. They come to Moses and Aaron, upset. Why should we be kept out? Why can we not bring the LORD’s offering at the right time, with everyone else?
These men wanted to worship. They did not want to be left out.
And Moses gives a wise answer. 'Stand still, and I will hear what the LORD will command concerning you.' Moses did not make up a rule. He took the question to God.
And God answers with grace.
They could keep the Passover a month later, in exactly the same way, with unleavened bread and bitter herbs. God made a second chance for those who could not come the first time.
But God also says that anyone who was clean, and not on a journey, and simply chose not to keep the Passover, would be cut off from the people. There was grace for those who could not come. But there was no excuse for those who would not come.
And God says one more thing. If a foreigner is living among you, and wants to keep the Passover to the LORD, let him keep it in the same way. There is one law, for the stranger and for the one born in the land. God’s welcome was open to outsiders too.
Then the chapter turns to the cloud.
On the day the tabernacle was set up, the cloud covered it. And in the dark hours, there was something like fire over the tabernacle until it was light. So it was always. A cloud by day, and the look of fire in the dark.
When the cloud lifted from the tabernacle, the people set out. And wherever the cloud settled, there they pitched their tents.
Sometimes the cloud stayed over the tabernacle for many days, and the people waited. Sometimes it stayed only a few days. Sometimes it stayed only a single day, and then they moved on. And sometimes it stayed two days, or a month, or a whole year.
Imagine how that tested their patience. You have just put up your tent, and the cloud moves. Or you are tired of a place, and the cloud stays for months. But the people learned to wait, and to move, at God’s word.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 10. 'Speak unto the children of Israel, saying, If any man of you or of your posterity shall be unclean by reason of a dead body, or be in a journey afar off, yet he shall keep the passover unto the LORD.' Yet he shall keep the passover. God did not shut out the people who could not come at the first time. He made another way for them. God is a God of second chances. He wants his people to come to him, and makes a way even for those who were left out.
Verse 18. 'At the commandment of the LORD the children of Israel journeyed, and at the commandment of the LORD they pitched: as long as the cloud abode upon the tabernacle they rested in their tents.' Journeyed, and pitched, and rested, all at the commandment of the LORD. Moving and waiting were both obedience. Sometimes following God means going. Sometimes it means staying put. Both can be hard. Both are ways of trusting him.
Verse 23. 'At the commandment of the LORD they rested in the tents, and at the commandment of the LORD they journeyed: they kept the charge of the LORD, at the commandment of the LORD by the hand of Moses.' Five times in the last few verses, the writer says, at the commandment of the LORD. It is like a drumbeat. The whole life of the camp was ordered by God’s word. That is a picture of a life that follows God: not rushing ahead, not lagging behind, but moving when he moves.
A psalm says, 'Thy word is a lamp unto my feet, and a light unto my path.' And Paul wrote, 'For as many as are led by the Spirit of God, they are the sons of God.'
Is God asking you to wait, or to move? Ask him today, and listen. If you are in a waiting season, rest in your tent and trust him. If he is moving you on, follow. And if you have missed out on something with God, remember he still makes a way.
Here is today’s question. Sometimes the cloud stayed a year, and sometimes it moved the next day. Which is harder for you: waiting for God, or moving when he says go?
Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that you lead your people, by day and in the dark. Teach me to wait when you say wait, and to move when you say move. Thank you that you make a way even for those who have missed out. Help me to live at the commandment of the LORD. Amen.
