A brother and sister are talking quietly about their younger brother. He has become very important. Everyone listens to him. And they are starting to feel left out. After all, they say, God has spoken through us too. Why should he be the only one? They think no one is listening. But someone is.
In chapter eleven, the people complained, and grew tired of the manna. Moses cried, I am not able to bear all this people alone, and God gave seventy elders his Spirit to share the load. When Joshua wanted to stop Eldad and Medad prophesying, Moses said, would God that all the LORD’s people were prophets. God sent quails, and asked Moses, is the LORD’s hand waxed short?
Now, in chapter twelve, the trouble comes from inside Moses’ own family. Listen for how Moses responds when he is criticised. And listen for his prayer at the end.
Miriam and Aaron, Moses’ older sister and brother, begin to speak against him.
The first complaint is about Moses’ wife, a woman from Cush, a land to the south of Egypt. She was a foreigner, and perhaps her skin was darker than theirs. Miriam and Aaron criticise Moses for marrying her.
But it seems that was not their real complaint. The real issue comes next. They say, has the LORD spoken only through Moses? Has he not spoken through us as well?
Miriam was a prophetess. She had led the women in song at the Red Sea. Aaron was the high priest. They were important too. And they were jealous.
And the LORD heard it.
Then the writer adds a note about Moses.
Moses did not defend himself. He did not argue, or demand his rights. He left it to God.
And God acts at once. The LORD suddenly says to Moses, Aaron and Miriam, come out, all three of you, to the tabernacle. And the three of them go out.
The LORD comes down in the pillar of cloud, and stands at the door of the tabernacle, and calls Aaron and Miriam. And they both step forward.
And God says, listen to my words. If there is a prophet among you, I make myself known to him in a vision, and speak to him in a dream. But not so with my servant Moses. He is faithful in all my house.
Then God goes on.
Then the anger of the LORD burns against them, and he leaves. And when the cloud lifts from the tabernacle, Miriam has become ill with a skin disease, white as snow.
Aaron looks at Miriam, and sees what has happened. And he turns to Moses, the brother they had just criticised, and begs him. Please, my lord, do not hold this sin against us, because we have acted foolishly, and we have sinned.
And Moses, the man they had spoken against, cries out to God for her.
There is no anger in Moses’ prayer. No, well, she deserved it. Just a desperate plea for his sister to be healed.
And God answers. Miriam must stay outside the camp for seven days, as anyone with this illness would, and then she can be brought back in.
So Miriam is shut out of the camp for seven days. And the people do not move on until Miriam is brought back.
That is a beautiful detail. The whole nation waited for her. They did not leave her behind. Even in her discipline, Miriam was not forgotten. And when the seven days were over, she was welcomed back.
After that, the people set out from Hazeroth, and camp in the wilderness of Paran.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 3. 'Now the man Moses was very meek, above all the men which were upon the face of the earth.' Meek does not mean weak. Moses stood up to Pharaoh, and led a nation. Meek means humble, gentle, not needing to defend himself or fight for his own position. Jesus said, blessed are the meek, for they shall inherit the earth. Moses did not need to win the argument. He trusted God to defend him.
Verse 8. 'With him will I speak mouth to mouth, even apparently, and not in dark speeches; and the similitude of the LORD shall he behold: wherefore then were ye not afraid to speak against my servant Moses?' Apparently here means openly, clearly. God spoke with Moses face to face, as a man speaks with his friend. That was a special closeness. And God was not pleased when people spoke against the servant he had chosen. It is a serious thing to speak against those God has called.
Verse 13. 'And Moses cried unto the LORD, saying, Heal her now, O God, I beseech thee.' Heal her now. Moses prayed for the very person who had spoken against him. That is what meekness and love look like. Jesus said, pray for them which despitefully use you. Moses did that, long before Jesus said it. And it is one of the shortest, most heartfelt prayers in the Bible.
Jesus said, 'Blessed are the meek: for they shall inherit the earth.' And he said, 'Love your enemies, bless them that curse you, do good to them that hate you, and pray for them which despitefully use you'.
Has someone spoken against you, or treated you unfairly? Instead of defending yourself, pray for them today, as Moses prayed for Miriam. And if you have been jealous of someone, confess it to God.
Here is today’s question. Moses did not defend himself when Miriam and Aaron spoke against him, and then prayed for Miriam. What helps you to respond to criticism with meekness instead of anger?
Let us pray. Lord God, make me meek, like Moses, humble and trusting you to defend me. Forgive me for jealousy, and for speaking against others. Help me to pray for those who hurt me. Heal those who are suffering today, I beseech you. And thank you that no one is left behind in your family. Amen.
