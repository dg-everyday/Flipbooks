Two men stagger into camp, carrying a pole between them on their shoulders. Hanging from the pole is a single bunch of grapes, so big and heavy that it takes two men to carry it. The whole camp crowds round to see. This is what the promised land is like! But then the men who carried it start to talk. And what they say turns joy into terror.
In chapter twelve, Miriam and Aaron spoke against Moses, and God called Moses very meek, faithful in all his house, a man he spoke with mouth to mouth. Miriam was struck with a skin disease, and Moses prayed, heal her now, O God. After seven days outside the camp, she was brought back, and the people waited for her.
Now, in chapter thirteen, Israel reaches the edge of the promised land. Listen for what the spies see. And listen for the difference between what ten of them say, and what Caleb says.
The LORD tells Moses, send men to explore the land of Canaan, which I am giving to the children of Israel. Send one man from each tribe, a leader among them.
So Moses sends twelve men, from the wilderness of Paran. The writer names them. Among them are Caleb, the son of Jephunneh, from the tribe of Judah, and Oshea, the son of Nun, from the tribe of Ephraim. And Moses gives Oshea a new name: Jehoshua, which is Joshua. It means the LORD is salvation.
Moses gives them their instructions. Go up through the south, and into the hill country. See what the land is like. Are the people strong or weak, few or many? Is the land good or bad? Do they live in camps, or in strong cities? Is the soil rich or poor? Are there trees? 'And be ye of good courage, and bring of the fruit of the land.'
It is the season of the first ripe grapes.
So the twelve go up, and explore the land from the wilderness of Zin in the south, all the way north to Rehob. They come to Hebron, where the descendants of Anak live, tall and powerful people.
Hebron. Remember that name. It was where Abraham, Isaac and Jacob were buried. It was part of the land God had promised Abraham, long before.
They come to a valley, and cut down a branch with a single cluster of grapes. It is so big that two men carry it between them on a pole. They also bring pomegranates and figs. And they call the place the valley of Eshcol, which means cluster, because of the grapes.
After forty days, they come back to Moses and Aaron and the whole congregation at Kadesh, in the wilderness of Paran. And they show them the fruit of the land.
And they give their report.
So far, so good. The land is wonderful, just as God had said. But then comes one word.
Nevertheless, they say. The people who live there are strong. The cities are walled, and very great. And we saw the descendants of Anak there. The Amalekites live in the south. The Hittites, the Jebusites and the Amorites live in the hills. And the Canaanites live by the sea and along the Jordan.
Nevertheless. One word, and the mood changes. The fruit is forgotten. All the people can think about is giants and walls.
Then Caleb stands up, and quiets the people before Moses.
But the other men who had gone with him say, we cannot attack those people. They are stronger than we are.
And they spread a bad report about the land among the Israelites. The land we explored devours those who live in it. All the people we saw there are huge.
And then they say the words that would echo down the years.
Think about it. Twelve men saw the same land, the same fruit, the same giants, the same walls. Ten of them saw only the problems. Two of them, Caleb and Joshua, saw the promise. The difference was not in what they saw. It was in whether they remembered God.
The ten spies forgot the plagues of Egypt, the Red Sea, the manna, the water from the rock. They measured the giants against themselves, and felt like grasshoppers. Caleb measured the giants against God. And the giants did not look so big after all.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 27. 'And they told him, and said, We came unto the land whither thou sentest us, and surely it floweth with milk and honey; and this is the fruit of it.' Surely it floweth with milk and honey. Everything God had promised was true. The land was as good as he said. The spies held the proof in their hands. God always keeps his word. The question is never whether God is faithful. It is whether we will trust him.
Verse 30. 'And Caleb stilled the people before Moses, and said, Let us go up at once, and possess it; for we are well able to overcome it.' Let us go up at once. Caleb was not ignoring the giants. He had seen them too. But he was sure that with God, they could do it. Faith does not deny the problems. It sees God as bigger than the problems.
Verse 33. 'And there we saw the giants, the sons of Anak, which come of the giants: and we were in our own sight as grasshoppers, and so we were in their sight.' In our own sight as grasshoppers. Notice where it started: in their own sight. They saw themselves as small and helpless, and assumed everyone else did too. When we forget God, our problems grow, and we shrink. When we remember God, it is the other way round.
Paul wrote, 'If God be for us, who can be against us?' And John wrote, 'greater is he that is in you, than he that is in the world.'
What giants are you facing? Look at them today, not against your own strength, but against the greatness of God. And remember one thing God has already done for you.
Here is today’s question. Ten spies saw giants, and Caleb saw the promise. When you face something hard, what helps you to see God as bigger than the problem?
Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that every promise you make is true. When I face giants, help me not to see myself as a grasshopper, but to see you as you are, great and faithful. Give me the courage of Caleb, to say, with you, we are well able. Amen.
