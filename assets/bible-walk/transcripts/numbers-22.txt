A man is riding his donkey along a narrow path between vineyard walls. Suddenly the donkey stops dead, and refuses to go on. The man beats her. She presses against the wall, and crushes his foot. He beats her again. Then she lies down in the road. And as he raises his staff a third time, the donkey turns her head and speaks to him.
In chapter twenty one, the people complained about the light bread, and fiery serpents came among them. They said, we have sinned, and Moses prayed. God told Moses to put a bronze serpent on a pole, and whoever looked at it lived. And Israel defeated Sihon and Og.
Now, in chapter twenty two, Israel camps on the plains of Moab, and the king of Moab is terrified. Listen for what he wants Balaam to do. And listen for who sees what Balaam cannot see.
The children of Israel set out, and camp in the plains of Moab, across the Jordan from Jericho.
Balak, the son of Zippor, the king of Moab, sees everything Israel has done to the Amorites. And Moab is terrified, because the people are so many. They say to the leaders of Midian, this crowd will lick up everything around us, like an ox licking up the grass of the field.
So Balak sends messengers to a man named Balaam, the son of Beor, who lives far away, near the river Euphrates. Balaam was a famous prophet, paid to bless or curse. Balak’s message says, a people has come out of Egypt, and they cover the face of the earth. Come and curse them for me, because they are too strong for me. I know that whoever you bless is blessed, and whoever you curse is cursed.
And Balaam says, stay here, and I will bring you the word the LORD speaks to me.
And God comes to Balaam, and asks, who are these men with you? Balaam tells him. And God answers.
So Balaam sends the messengers home. The LORD refuses to let me go with you.
But Balak does not give up. He sends more princes, more important than the first. And they say, let nothing stop you from coming. I will honour you greatly, and do whatever you say. Just come and curse this people for me.
And Balaam gives a fine answer. Even if Balak gave me his house full of silver and gold, I cannot go beyond the word of the LORD my God, to do less or more.
But then Balaam says, stay here, and I will see what more the LORD will say to me.
God had already given his answer. Balaam knew it. But he asked again. It seems his heart was drawn to the silver and gold, even while his mouth said the right words.
God comes to Balaam, and says, if the men come to call you, go with them. But only do what I tell you.
So Balaam gets up, saddles his donkey, and goes with the princes of Moab. But God is angry because he goes, and the angel of the LORD stands in the road to oppose him.
Balaam is riding his donkey, with two servants. And the donkey sees the angel of the LORD standing in the road, with a drawn sword in his hand. So the donkey turns off the road into a field. And Balaam beats her, to turn her back onto the road.
Then the angel stands in a narrow path between vineyards, with a wall on each side. When the donkey sees the angel, she presses against the wall, and crushes Balaam’s foot. And he beats her again.
Then the angel goes further on, and stands in a narrow place where there is no room to turn either way. When the donkey sees the angel, she lies down under Balaam. And Balaam, furious, beats her with his staff.
And then something astonishing happens. The LORD opens the donkey’s mouth, and she speaks. What have I done to you, she asks, that you have beaten me these three times?
Balaam is too angry even to be surprised. He shouts back at her, you have made a fool of me!
And the donkey says, am I not your own donkey, which you have ridden all your life? Have I ever done anything like this before? And Balaam says, no.
Then the LORD opens Balaam’s eyes.
The angel says, why have you beaten your donkey three times? I came out to stand against you, because your way is reckless before me. The donkey saw me, and turned away from me three times. If she had not turned away, I would have struck you, and spared her.
And Balaam says, I have sinned. I did not know you were standing in the road against me. If it displeases you, I will go back.
But the angel answers.
So Balaam goes on with the princes of Balak. And Balak comes out to meet him, saying, why did you not come when I first called? Am I not able to honour you? And Balaam says, I have come now. But can I say whatever I like? I can only speak the word that God puts in my mouth.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 12. 'And God said unto Balaam, Thou shalt not go with them; thou shalt not curse the people: for they are blessed.' For they are blessed. Balak wanted Israel cursed. But God had already blessed them, from the time of Abraham. No one can curse those whom God has blessed. Whatever enemies may say or plan against God’s people, God’s blessing stands.
Verse 35. 'And the angel of the LORD said unto Balaam, Go with the men: but only the word that I shall speak unto thee, that thou shalt speak. So Balaam went with the princes of Balak.' Only the word that I shall speak. Balaam could go, but he could not say whatever he liked, or whatever Balak paid for. God would control every word. Whatever plans people make, God has the last word. And those who speak for God must speak only what he gives them.
Verse 31. 'Then the LORD opened the eyes of Balaam, and he saw the angel of the LORD standing in the way, and his sword drawn in his hand: and he bowed down his head, and fell flat on his face.' The LORD opened his eyes. Balaam could not see until God showed him. We too can be blind to danger, and to God’s warnings, when we are set on our own way. We need God to open our eyes.
Peter wrote that Balaam 'loved the wages of unrighteousness', and was rebuked by his own donkey, speaking with a man’s voice. And a psalm says, 'Open thou mine eyes, that I may behold wondrous things out of thy law.'
Is there something you keep asking God about, hoping he will change his answer, because you really want it? Be honest with him today. And ask him to open your eyes to anything he is trying to show you.
Here is today’s question. Balaam said all the right words, but his heart was drawn to Balak’s reward. How can we tell when our hearts are not really following what our mouths say?
Let us pray. Lord God, open my eyes, so that I can see what you are showing me. Keep me from greed, and from asking again and again for what you have already said no to. Thank you that no one can curse those you have blessed. Help me to speak only what is true. Amen.
