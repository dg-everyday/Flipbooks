Five sisters walk up to the door of the tabernacle. In front of them stand Moses, the high priest, all the leaders, and the whole congregation of Israel. In their world, women did not usually speak up in a gathering like this. But these sisters have a question. And their question is so good that Moses has to take it to God.
In chapter twenty six, after the plague, Moses and Eleazar counted the people again, on the plains of Moab. The total was almost the same as at Sinai. But not one man was left from the first count, save Caleb the son of Jephunneh, and Joshua the son of Nun. We heard the names of five sisters, the daughters of Zelophehad.
Now, in chapter twenty seven, those five sisters come forward. Listen for what they ask, and what God says. And then listen for Moses’ prayer for a new leader.
The daughters of Zelophehad come forward. Their names are Mahlah, Noah, Hoglah, Milcah and Tirzah. They are from the tribe of Manasseh.
They stand before Moses, and Eleazar the priest, and the leaders, and the whole congregation, at the door of the tabernacle. And they say, our father died in the wilderness. He was not one of Korah’s company, who rebelled against the LORD. He died for his own sin, like everyone of that generation. And he had no sons.
Then they ask their question. Why should our father’s name disappear from his family, just because he had no son? Give us a share of land among our father’s brothers.
The land was about to be divided, family by family, through the sons. A family with no sons would have no land, and the father’s name would be lost. The sisters were asking for something new.
Moses does not know the answer. So he does something wise. Moses brings their case before the LORD.
And the LORD answers.
And God makes it a law for all Israel. If a man dies and has no son, his inheritance will go to his daughter. If he has no daughter, to his brothers. If he has no brothers, to his father’s brothers. And if there are none of those, to his nearest relative. So no family’s land would be lost.
Five ordinary women, by asking a brave question, changed the law of Israel. And God said they were right. God cares about justice, and about those who might be left out.
Then the LORD says to Moses, go up this mountain, and look at the land I have given to the children of Israel. And when you have seen it, you too will be gathered to your people, as Aaron your brother was.
Moses would see the land, but not enter it, because of what happened at the water of Meribah. It is a painful moment. But notice what Moses does next. He does not argue, or complain, or plead for himself. He thinks about the people.
Moses says, may the LORD, the God of the spirits of all flesh, appoint a man over the congregation.
Even at the end of his life, Moses thought like a shepherd. He did not want the people to be left without someone to lead them, guide them, and care for them.
And the LORD answers.
And God says, stand him before Eleazar the priest, and before all the congregation, and give him a charge in their sight. Put some of your honour on him, so that all the people will obey him.
So Moses does as the LORD commanded. He takes Joshua, and stands him before Eleazar the priest and all the congregation. And he lays his hands on him, and gives him his charge, as the LORD commanded.
Joshua. The young man who fought the Amalekites in the wilderness. Who went up the mountain with Moses. Who was one of only two spies who trusted God. Now he would lead God’s people into the promised land. And his name, Joshua, means the LORD is salvation, the same name as Jesus.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 7. 'The daughters of Zelophehad speak right: thou shalt surely give them a possession of an inheritance among their father’s brethren; and thou shalt cause the inheritance of their father to pass unto them.' Speak right. God himself said the sisters were right. Their request was fair, and God made sure they were included. God does not overlook those whom others might forget. He hears the cry of those who seek justice.
Verse 17. 'Which may go out before them, and which may go in before them, and which may lead them out, and which may bring them in; that the congregation of the LORD be not as sheep which have no shepherd.' As sheep which have no shepherd. Moses knew how lost the people would be without a leader. Long after, the Gospels tell us that Jesus looked at the crowds, and was moved with compassion, because they were as sheep having no shepherd. And Jesus is the good shepherd, who goes out and in before his flock.
Verse 18. 'And the LORD said unto Moses, Take thee Joshua the son of Nun, a man in whom is the spirit, and lay thine hand upon him;' A man in whom is the spirit. Joshua was not chosen for his strength or cleverness, but because God’s Spirit was in him. That is what God looks for in a leader. And Moses laid his hands on him, passing on the leadership, so that everyone could see God had chosen him.
Matthew wrote of Jesus, 'But when he saw the multitudes, he was moved with compassion on them, because they fainted, and were scattered abroad, as sheep having no shepherd.' And Jesus said, 'I am the good shepherd, and know my sheep, and am known of mine.'
Pray today for the leaders in your church, your community and your country, that God would give them his Spirit, and make them good shepherds. And if you see someone being left out or treated unfairly, speak up for them, as the daughters of Zelophehad did.
Here is today’s question. At the end of his life, Moses did not pray for himself, but for a shepherd for the people. What does this tell you about the kind of leader Moses was?
Let us pray. Lord God, the God of the spirits of all flesh, thank you that you care about justice, and about those who might be overlooked. Thank you for Jesus, the good shepherd. Give your people leaders full of your Spirit. And make me someone who cares for others, as Moses did. Amen.
