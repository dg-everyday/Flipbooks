Imagine a fire that never goes out. Every single day, at the start of the day and again at its close, a priest offers a lamb to God on the altar. Day after day, year after year, whatever else is happening, whatever the weather, whether the people are happy or sad. The offering never stops. It is called the continual offering. And it was the heartbeat of Israel’s worship.
In chapter twenty seven, five sisters, the daughters of Zelophehad, asked for their father’s inheritance, and God said, they speak right. Moses prayed that the congregation would not be as sheep which have no shepherd. And God chose Joshua, a man in whom is the spirit, and Moses laid his hands on him.
Now, in chapters twenty eight and twenty nine, God sets out the offerings Israel was to bring at the appointed times, once they were in the land. Listen for the offering that was brought every day. And listen for how these offerings point beyond themselves.
The LORD speaks to Moses.
The new generation was about to enter the land. And God reminds them that worship is at the centre of their life. Before the battles, before the dividing of the land, God tells them how they are to worship him.
The first offering is the one that happens every day.
One lamb at the start of each day, and one at its close. With each lamb, a portion of fine flour mixed with oil, and a drink offering of wine poured out to the LORD.
This was not new. God had first given this command at Mount Sinai. Now he reminds the new generation.
Every single day began and ended with an offering to God. It was a way of saying, every day belongs to the LORD. Our whole life is lived before him.
Then God lists the offerings for Israel’s special days. On the sabbath, Israel’s weekly day of rest, the sign of God’s covenant with them, two more lambs were offered, besides the daily offering.
At the beginning of each month, there were larger offerings: two young bulls, a ram, seven lambs, with their grain and drink offerings, and a goat for a sin offering.
Then came the Passover, Israel’s great feast remembering their rescue from Egypt. And for the seven days after it, the feast of unleavened bread, with offerings every day.
Then the day of firstfruits, at the beginning of the harvest, when the people brought the first of their new grain to God.
And notice something. Every one of these special offerings was given beside the continual burnt offering. Whatever feast or festival came, the daily lamb was never forgotten. The special days were added on top of the everyday faithfulness, never instead of it.
These offerings were part of God’s covenant with Israel, given for their life in the land. They show us how seriously God took worship, and how every part of Israel’s time, every day, every week, every month, every year, was to be given to him.
Christians read these chapters in the light of Jesus. The letter to the Hebrews explains that all these offerings, repeated day after day, could never fully take away sin. They were a shadow, pointing forward. And then Jesus came, and offered himself once for all. His one sacrifice did what all the lambs on all the altars could never do.
The continual offering was a picture of a God who wanted to be with his people every day. And in Jesus, God is with us every day. Not through a lamb on an altar, but through the Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world.
And so our worship, too, is meant to be continual. Paul wrote that we should offer ourselves as a living sacrifice. Not just on special days, but every day.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 2. 'Command the children of Israel, and say unto them, My offering, and my bread for my sacrifices made by fire, for a sweet savour unto me, shall ye observe to offer unto me in their due season.' A sweet savour means a pleasing aroma. God found joy in his people’s worship, when it was offered with a willing heart. In their due season means at the right time. God wanted his people’s worship to be regular, faithful, not forgotten in the busyness of life.
Verse 3. 'And thou shalt say unto them, This is the offering made by fire which ye shall offer unto the LORD; two lambs of the first year without spot day by day, for a continual burnt offering.' Day by day. Not only on special days, but every day. The lambs had to be without spot, the best. Long after, John the Baptist saw Jesus and said, behold the Lamb of God, which taketh away the sin of the world. Jesus is the Lamb without spot.
Verse 6. 'It is a continual burnt offering, which was ordained in mount Sinai for a sweet savour, a sacrifice made by fire unto the LORD.' Continual. It never stopped. That is a picture of what God wants from our lives: not worship that comes and goes, but a continual turning of our hearts to him. A life lived before God, day by day.
John the Baptist said, 'Behold the Lamb of God, which taketh away the sin of the world.' And the letter to the Hebrews says, 'By him therefore let us offer the sacrifice of praise to God continually'.
Begin and end your day with God, as Israel’s day began and ended with an offering. A short prayer when you wake, and another before you sleep. Offer him each day, as a living sacrifice.
Here is today’s question. The daily offering was never forgotten, even on the great feast days. Why do you think everyday faithfulness matters so much to God?
Let us pray. Lord God, thank you for Jesus, the Lamb of God without spot, who offered himself once for all. Help me to offer my life to you, not only on special days, but every day. Let my praise rise to you continually, a sweet savour to you. Amen.
