A group of older boys hang around at the corner of the street. They see a younger boy walking past, and call him over. Come with us. We have a plan. We are going to make some easy money. You can be part of our gang. Everything we get, we will share. The younger boy hesitates. It sounds exciting. And then he remembers something his father said.
In the introduction, we heard that Proverbs is a book of wise sayings, many from king Solomon, others collected later. It has a father’s talks in chapters one to nine, hundreds of short sayings, and the words of Agur, Lemuel and the virtuous woman at the end. And we heard that a proverb tells how life usually works, not a promise for every case.
Now, in chapter one, the book explains why it was written. Listen for the key to all wisdom. And listen for two voices calling: the voice of bad company, and the voice of Wisdom.
The book begins. 'The proverbs of Solomon the son of David, king of Israel'. And straight away, it tells us its purpose. 'To know wisdom and instruction; to perceive the words of understanding'. To help us understand justice and fairness. 'To give subtilty to the simple, to the young man knowledge and discretion.' Subtilty here means good sense, the ability to see what is really going on. The simple are people who are easily led, who believe anything. Discretion means knowing what to do.
So the book is for young people. But not only for them. 'A wise man will hear, and will increase learning'. Even wise people can keep learning. The day we think we have nothing more to learn is the day we stop growing.
And then comes the key that opens the whole book.
Then the father begins to speak to his son. 'My son, hear the instruction of thy father, and forsake not the law of thy mother'. Their teaching, he says, will be like a beautiful garland on your head, and a chain of honour around your neck. Wisdom makes a person attractive, in the best sense.
And the very first warning the father gives is about the company his son keeps.
The father describes what the gang will say. Come with us. Let us hide and wait for someone innocent, and ambush him. We will get rich. We will fill our houses with stolen goods. Join us, and we will share everything.
My son, the father says, do not walk with them. Keep your feet far away from their path. They are rushing towards evil.
And then he says something clever. It is pointless to spread a net while the bird is watching. Any bird with sense will fly away. But these men are not as wise as birds. They set a trap, and fall into it themselves. 'So are the ways of every one that is greedy of gain; which taketh away the life of the owners thereof.' Greed always ends up hurting the greedy.
Then, suddenly, a new voice speaks. 'Wisdom crieth without; she uttereth her voice in the streets'. Proverbs pictures wisdom as a woman, standing in the busiest places, in the market, at the city gates, calling out to everyone who passes by.
And she asks, 'How long, ye simple ones, will ye love simplicity? and the scorners delight in their scorning, and fools hate knowledge?' Scorners are people who mock anything good or serious. How long will you go on like this?
Then she makes an offer. 'Turn you at my reproof: behold, I will pour out my spirit unto you, I will make known my words unto you.' Reproof means correction. If you will turn around when I correct you, I will teach you everything.
But many will not listen. Wisdom says, I called, and you refused. I held out my hand, and no one paid attention. You ignored my advice, and would not accept my correction. So when trouble comes like a storm, and you call on me then, it will be too late. 'Therefore shall they eat of the fruit of their own way, and be filled with their own devices.' Those who reject wisdom will reap what they have sown.
And the chapter ends with a beautiful promise for those who do listen.
So chapter one sets out the choice that runs through the whole book. Two voices are calling. The voice of bad company, offering easy gain. And the voice of Wisdom, offering life. Which one will we listen to?
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 7. 'The fear of the LORD is the beginning of knowledge: but fools despise wisdom and instruction.' This is the motto of the whole book. The fear of the LORD does not mean being terrified of God. It means deep respect and awe, taking God seriously, honouring him as God. That is where true knowledge begins. You can be very clever and still be a fool, if you leave God out. And you can be very simple and still be wise, if you honour him.
Verse 10. 'My son, if sinners entice thee, consent thou not.' Entice means tempt, lure, try to draw you in. Consent thou not means do not agree, do not go along. It is a short sentence, but it can save a life. Temptation often comes through friends, people we want to impress. The father’s advice is simple: just say no. You do not have to argue or explain. Just do not go.
Verse 33. 'But whoso hearkeneth unto me shall dwell safely, and shall be quiet from fear of evil.' Hearkeneth means listens and obeys. Whoever listens to Wisdom will live safely, and be at peace, free from the fear of disaster. Not because nothing bad can ever happen, but because they are walking the right path, with God. There is a deep peace that comes from choosing wisdom.
The psalmist wrote, 'The fear of the LORD is the beginning of wisdom'. And Paul wrote to young Timothy, 'from a child thou hast known the holy scriptures, which are able to make thee wise unto salvation'.
Think about the voices you listen to most: friends, screens, songs, advertisements. Are they leading you towards wisdom, or away from it? And if someone tries to draw you into something wrong today, remember: consent thou not.
Here is today’s question. Proverbs says the fear of the LORD is the beginning of knowledge. What do you think it means to fear the LORD, and how could it make you wiser?
Let us pray. Lord God, teach me to fear you, with deep respect and awe, for that is where wisdom begins. Help me to listen to wisdom, and to turn when you correct me. When others try to draw me into wrong, give me courage to say no. And let me dwell safely, at peace, because I walk with you. Amen.
