A man is digging in his field. Suddenly his spade hits something hard. He brushes away the soil, and sees the glint of gold. A buried treasure! He looks around, and keeps digging, faster now, with his heart pounding. He forgets he is tired. He forgets everything else. Nothing will stop him until he has dug it all out. Proverbs says that is how we should search for wisdom.
In chapter one, we heard that Proverbs was written to give wisdom to the simple and knowledge to the young. Its motto is, the fear of the LORD is the beginning of knowledge. A father warned his son, if sinners entice thee, consent thou not. And Wisdom cried out in the streets, inviting everyone to listen.
Now, in chapter two, the father tells his son how to find wisdom. Listen for the little word, if. And listen for what wisdom will protect us from.
The chapter begins with a long sentence, built on the little word if.
'My son, if thou wilt receive my words, and hide my commandments with thee; So that thou incline thine ear unto wisdom, and apply thine heart to understanding'. If you take my words to heart. If you turn your ear towards wisdom. If you give your whole heart to understanding.
'Yea, if thou criest after knowledge, and liftest up thy voice for understanding'. If you call out for it, like someone shouting for help.
And then the picture of treasure.
If you do all this, then something wonderful will happen. 'Then shalt thou understand the fear of the LORD, and find the knowledge of God.' Do you see? We search for wisdom, and what we find is God himself.
And then the father tells us where wisdom really comes from.
'He layeth up sound wisdom for the righteous: he is a buckler to them that walk uprightly.' A buckler is a small shield. God stores up wisdom for those who live rightly, and protects them like a shield. 'He keepeth the paths of judgment, and preserveth the way of his saints.' He guards the path of those who belong to him.
'Then shalt thou understand righteousness, and judgment, and equity; yea, every good path.' Righteousness is doing what is right. Judgment is good decision making. Equity is fairness. When God gives you wisdom, you will understand every good path, how to live well.
'When wisdom entereth into thine heart, and knowledge is pleasant unto thy soul'. Notice, wisdom does not just stay in our heads. It enters our hearts. And it becomes pleasant to us. We begin to enjoy it.
And then the father tells us what wisdom will do.
Wisdom will protect us from two dangers.
The first danger is evil men. 'To deliver thee from the way of the evil man, from the man that speaketh froward things'. Froward means twisted, perverse. These are people who leave the straight path to walk in darkness. They enjoy doing wrong. Their ways are crooked, and they twist everything. Wisdom will keep you from following them.
The second danger the father calls the strange woman. Strange here means a stranger, an outsider to the right way. The father is talking about someone who tries to tempt a person into being unfaithful in marriage. She flatters with smooth words. She has left the husband of her youth, and forgotten the promise she made before God. Her house leads down to death, the father says. Those who go to her do not come back to the paths of life. The father is warning his son, long before he faces this temptation, so that he will be ready. We will hear more about this in the chapters to come.
So wisdom protects us from bad people, and from bad choices that look sweet but lead to ruin.
'That thou mayest walk in the way of good men, and keep the paths of the righteous.' Instead of following the wicked, walk with good people. 'For the upright shall dwell in the land, and the perfect shall remain in it.' The perfect here means those who are whole and true. But the wicked will be removed from the land.
The father is painting a picture of two roads again. One leads to life, and the other to death. And wisdom is what keeps us on the right road.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 4. 'If thou seekest her as silver, and searchest for her as for hid treasures'. People will work very hard for money. They dig mines, search for gold, and spend their lives trying to get rich. Proverbs says, seek wisdom like that. With that much effort. With that much longing. Wisdom is worth more than any treasure. But we will not find it if we are lazy about it. We have to want it, and look for it.
Verse 6. 'For the LORD giveth wisdom: out of his mouth cometh knowledge and understanding.' We search, but God is the one who gives. Wisdom is not something we invent. It is a gift from God, and it comes out of his mouth, that is, from his Word. That is why reading and listening to the Bible is the best way to grow wise. When we listen to God’s Word, we are listening to the source of all wisdom.
Verse 11. 'Discretion shall preserve thee, understanding shall keep thee'. Discretion is good judgment, knowing what to do and what not to do. Preserve and keep both mean protect. Wisdom is like a bodyguard. It warns us of danger before we walk into it. It helps us see through lies and flattery. Those who have wisdom avoid many troubles that others fall into.
Jesus said, 'the kingdom of heaven is like unto treasure hid in a field'. And Paul wrote of Christ, 'In whom are hid all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge.'
How much effort do you put into seeking wisdom, compared with other things you want? Spend a few minutes today really searching God’s Word, as if you were digging for treasure. Ask God to give you wisdom.
Here is today’s question. Proverbs says to search for wisdom as for hidden treasure. What would it look like for you to seek God’s wisdom with that kind of eagerness?
Let us pray. Lord God, you are the giver of wisdom, and out of your mouth come knowledge and understanding. Give me a heart that longs for wisdom more than silver or gold. Help me to search your Word like hidden treasure. Let your wisdom protect me from evil people and from tempting choices that lead to harm. Amen.
