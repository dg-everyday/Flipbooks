A woman stands at her front door. A neighbour asks to borrow something she needs urgently, and the woman has it right there in her house. But she is busy. Come back another time, she says, and closes the door. It seems such a small thing. But Proverbs says something about moments like that. When you have it, and you can help, help now.
In chapter two, the father told his son to seek wisdom like silver, and search for it like hidden treasure. For the LORD giveth wisdom, and out of his mouth comes knowledge. Wisdom would protect him from evil men, and from the flattering words of one who would lead him into unfaithfulness.
Now, in chapter three, the father gives some of his best known advice. Listen for what it means to trust God. And listen for how wisdom shows itself in the way we treat our neighbours.
The father begins again. 'My son, forget not my law; but let thine heart keep my commandments'. They will give you long life, and peace.
Then he gives his first piece of advice.
Keep mercy and truth so close that they are part of you. 'So shalt thou find favour and good understanding in the sight of God and man.'
And then come two of the most loved verses in the whole Bible. 'Trust in the LORD with all thine heart; and lean not unto thine own understanding. In all thy ways acknowledge him, and he shall direct thy paths.' Trust God with all your heart, not just part of it. Do not rely only on your own cleverness. In everything you do, remember him, and put him first. And he will make your paths straight.
'Be not wise in thine own eyes: fear the LORD, and depart from evil.' One of the most dangerous things is to think we are already wise enough, and do not need anyone else, not even God.
Then the father talks about money.
'My son, despise not the chastening of the LORD; neither be weary of his correction: For whom the LORD loveth he correcteth; even as a father the son in whom he delighteth.' When God corrects us, it is not because he is angry with us, but because he loves us, like a good father.
Then the father sings the praises of wisdom. 'Happy is the man that findeth wisdom, and the man that getteth understanding.' It is worth more than silver, and better than gold. 'She is more precious than rubies: and all the things thou canst desire are not to be compared unto her.' In one hand she holds long life, and in the other, riches and honour.
'Her ways are ways of pleasantness, and all her paths are peace. She is a tree of life to them that lay hold upon her'. Do you remember the tree of life in the garden of Eden? Proverbs says that wisdom is like that tree. Those who hold on to her find life.
And wisdom is not just something for people. God himself used it. 'The LORD by wisdom hath founded the earth; by understanding hath he established the heavens.' The same wisdom God used to make the universe, he offers to us.
Keep hold of wisdom, the father says, and you will walk safely, and not stumble. 'When thou liest down, thou shalt not be afraid: yea, thou shalt lie down, and thy sleep shall be sweet.' 'For the LORD shall be thy confidence'. Peaceful sleep is a gift to those who trust God.
Then the father turns to how we treat our neighbours.
'Say not unto thy neighbour, Go, and come again, and to morrow I will give; when thou hast it by thee.' Do not put people off, when you could help them now.
Do not plot harm against your neighbour, who lives trustingly beside you. 'Strive not with a man without cause, if he have done thee no harm.' Do not pick quarrels for no reason. Do not envy violent people, or copy their ways, even if they seem to get ahead.
And the chapter ends with a contrast. The LORD’s curse is on the house of the wicked, but he blesses the home of the righteous. 'Surely he scorneth the scorners: but he giveth grace unto the lowly.' God resists the proud, but gives grace to the humble. James quoted these very words, in the letter we walked through earlier.
So in this one chapter, wisdom touches everything: our trust in God, our money, our sleep, and how we treat the people next door.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 3. 'Let not mercy and truth forsake thee: bind them about thy neck; write them upon the table of thine heart'. Mercy means kindness and loyal love. Truth means faithfulness and honesty. Bind them about thy neck means wear them like a necklace, where everyone can see. Write them on your heart means let them become part of who you are, inside. Kindness and faithfulness should show on the outside and live on the inside.
Verse 9. 'Honour the LORD with thy substance, and with the firstfruits of all thine increase'. Substance means what you own. Firstfruits means the first and best of what you receive. Honour God with your money and possessions, not with leftovers, but with the first and best. When we give to God first, we show that we trust him to provide, and that everything we have really belongs to him.
Verse 27. 'Withhold not good from them to whom it is due, when it is in the power of thine hand to do it.' Withhold means hold back. If someone needs your help, and you are able to give it, do not hold it back. This is wisdom in action. It is not enough to know what is right. Wisdom means doing good when we have the chance, not putting it off until later.
Paul wrote, 'As we have therefore opportunity, let us do good unto all men'. And Jesus said, 'But seek ye first the kingdom of God, and his righteousness; and all these things shall be added unto you.'
Is there someone you could help today, while you have the chance? Do not say, come back later. And think about how you could honour God with the first and best of what you have this week.
Here is today’s question. Proverbs says, withhold not good from them to whom it is due, when it is in thy power to do it. Who could you help this week, that you might have put off until later?
Let us pray. Lord God, I want to trust you with all my heart, and to acknowledge you in all my ways. Let mercy and truth be written on my heart. Help me to honour you with the first and best of what I have. And when I have the power to do good, help me to do it now, not later. Amen.
