A man walks through the desert, very thirsty. In his own garden, back home, there is a well of cool, clean water. But on the way, he sees a stranger’s pond, glittering in the sun, and it looks so inviting. He does not know that the water is poisoned. A wise father would say, son, do not drink from that pond. Go home. Drink from your own well.
In chapter four, a father remembered what his own father had taught him. Get wisdom, for wisdom is the principal thing. The path of the just is as the shining light. And above all, keep thy heart with all diligence, for out of it are the issues of life.
Now, in chapter five, the father speaks honestly to his son about faithfulness in marriage. We will tell it gently. Listen for how something can be sweet at first, and bitter at the end. And listen for the joy of a faithful marriage.
The father begins, as he often does. 'My son, attend unto my wisdom, and bow thine ear to my understanding'. Listen carefully. So that you will have good sense, and your lips will speak what is right.
Then he warns his son about the strange woman we heard of in chapter two. Remember, strange here means outside of what is right. The father is talking about the temptation to be unfaithful in marriage, to be drawn away from your husband or wife by someone else.
And he describes how that temptation works. 'For the lips of a strange woman drop as an honeycomb, and her mouth is smoother than oil: But her end is bitter as wormwood, sharp as a twoedged sword.' At first, the words are sweet, like honey dripping from a honeycomb. Smooth, like oil. Flattering. Exciting. But at the end, it is bitter as wormwood, a very bitter plant, and painful, like a sword that cuts both ways.
Temptation always looks sweet at the start. It never shows you the end.
Her path leads to death, the father says. And she does not even think about the path of life. Her ways wander about, and she does not know where she is going.
So the father gives clear advice. 'Remove thy way far from her, and come not nigh the door of her house'. Do not just avoid the sin. Stay far away from the place of temptation. Do not even walk past the door.
Then the father describes what unfaithfulness costs. You would give away your honour to others. You would waste your years. Strangers would take your wealth, and your hard work would benefit someone else’s house. And at the end of your life, you would groan with regret, and say, 'How have I hated instruction, and my heart despised reproof; And have not obeyed the voice of my teachers, nor inclined mine ear to them that instructed me!' Why did I not listen?
Unfaithfulness hurts everyone. The husband or wife who is betrayed. The children. The person who did wrong. And it grieves God, who made marriage.
Then the father changes his tone, and speaks about something beautiful.
He uses a picture of water. In a dry land, having your own well was precious. You did not drink from other people’s wells. The father means, find your joy and love in your own husband or wife, the one God has given you. Do not go looking for it elsewhere.
Keep your love for your own family, the father says, not for strangers.
And then a beautiful verse.
The father wants his son to delight in his wife, and to keep on delighting in her, all his life. Faithfulness is not just about saying no to temptation. It is about saying yes, again and again, to the one you have promised to love. Why would you go after someone else, when you have such a gift at home?
And then the father reminds his son that nothing is hidden.
'His own iniquities shall take the wicked himself, and he shall be holden with the cords of his sins.' A person who chooses sin becomes trapped by it, tied up by his own choices, like an animal caught in ropes. 'He shall die without instruction; and in the greatness of his folly he shall go astray.'
Even if you are young, and marriage seems a long way off, this is wise advice. Faithfulness begins long before marriage, in what we let into our hearts, and in the choices we make about friendships and temptations.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 15. 'Drink waters out of thine own cistern, and running waters out of thine own well.' A cistern is a tank for storing water. In a dry land, your own well was one of your most valuable things. The father uses this picture to mean, find all your love and delight in your own husband or wife. God’s gift of marriage is like a fresh, clean well. Do not go looking for water in someone else’s well. Be content, and be faithful.
Verse 18. 'Let thy fountain be blessed: and rejoice with the wife of thy youth.' The wife of thy youth is the one you married when you were young. Rejoice with her. God wants marriage to be full of joy, not just duty. A faithful marriage, where a husband and wife keep delighting in each other through the years, is a blessing from God, like a fountain that never runs dry.
Verse 21. 'For the ways of man are before the eyes of the LORD, and he pondereth all his goings.' Pondereth means he considers, he weighs. People might think their secret sins are hidden. But God sees everything. Nothing we do is hidden from him. That is a warning. But it is also a comfort. God sees our faithfulness too, even when no one else does.
The letter to the Hebrews says, 'Marriage is honourable in all'. And David wrote to God, 'Thou compassest my path and my lying down, and art acquainted with all my ways.'
If you are married, thank God for your husband or wife today, and find a way to show them you delight in them. If you are not, pray for faithfulness in your friendships, and in your future. And guard your heart from temptations that look sweet, but end bitter.
Here is today’s question. Proverbs says temptation is sweet at the start, but bitter at the end. Why do you think temptation never shows us the end, and how can we remember it?
Let us pray. Lord God, thank you for the gift of marriage and family. Help me to be faithful, in my thoughts, my words and my actions. Keep me far from the door of temptation. Help me to find my joy in the people and the gifts you have given me. And thank you that you see all my ways, and that you are with me. Amen.
