Think of a set of scales, the old kind, with two pans hanging from a bar. Put something heavy in one pan, and it goes down, while the other goes up. Proverbs chapter ten is like that, line after line. On one side, the wise. On the other, the foolish. On one side, the hardworking. On the other, the lazy. Each saying weighs one way of life against another.
In chapter nine, the first part of Proverbs ended with two houses and two invitations. Wisdom built her house and prepared a feast, and Folly offered stolen waters. And we heard again, the fear of the LORD is the beginning of wisdom.
Now, in chapter ten, the book changes. Listen for the short, two-line sayings that will fill most of the rest of the book. And listen for how often they talk about our words.
The chapter begins with a new heading. 'The proverbs of Solomon.' From now on, instead of long speeches, we hear hundreds of short sayings. Most of them have two lines. Often the second line says the opposite of the first, like the two pans of a scale. Many of them are not in any special order. So as we walk, we will pick some out, and group a few together.
The very first saying is about families. 'A wise son maketh a glad father: but a foolish son is the heaviness of his mother.' Heaviness means sorrow. Our choices do not only affect us. They bring joy or sorrow to the people who love us.
Then come sayings about money and work. 'Treasures of wickedness profit nothing: but righteousness delivereth from death.' Money gained by doing wrong does not really help in the end. 'He becometh poor that dealeth with a slack hand: but the hand of the diligent maketh rich.' A slack hand is a lazy hand. Diligent means hardworking and careful. 'He that gathereth in summer is a wise son: but he that sleepeth in harvest is a son that causeth shame.' Do the work when the time is right.
Then sayings about how we will be remembered. 'The memory of the just is blessed: but the name of the wicked shall rot.' Good people are remembered with gladness. And, 'He that walketh uprightly walketh surely'. Someone who lives honestly can walk without fear, with nothing to hide.
And then, many sayings about our words. 'The mouth of a righteous man is a well of life'. Like a well in the desert, good words give life to others.
And then a beautiful saying about love.
There are more sayings about words. Wise people store up knowledge, and think before they speak. But the mouth of a fool brings trouble close.
'He that hideth hatred with lying lips, and he that uttereth a slander, is a fool.' Slander means spreading lies about someone, to damage them. Pretending to be friendly while secretly hating someone is foolish, and so is spreading untrue stories.
And then one of the wisest sayings about words.
'The tongue of the just is as choice silver'. Good words are precious, like fine silver. 'The lips of the righteous feed many'. Wise words nourish people, like good food.
Then a promise about God’s blessing.
Then a funny picture. 'As vinegar to the teeth, and as smoke to the eyes, so is the sluggard to them that send him.' Have you ever bitten into something sour, or had smoke blow in your eyes? That is what it is like to send a lazy person to do a job. Irritating and painful.
And near the end of the chapter, a picture of storms. 'As the whirlwind passeth, so is the wicked no more: but the righteous is an everlasting foundation.' When the storm comes, those who have built on wickedness are swept away. But those who live rightly with God stand firm, like a house built on solid rock.
'The fear of the LORD prolongeth days'. 'The hope of the righteous shall be gladness'. 'The way of the LORD is strength to the upright'. God is the source of strength, hope and life for those who walk with him.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 12. 'Hatred stirreth up strifes: but love covereth all sins.' Strifes are quarrels and fights. Hatred keeps old wrongs alive, digging them up, and stirring up trouble again and again. Love does the opposite. It covers sins. That does not mean pretending wrong does not matter. It means love chooses to forgive, and not to keep bringing up the past. Peter quoted this very idea in his letter.
Verse 19. 'In the multitude of words there wanteth not sin: but he that refraineth his lips is wise.' In the multitude of words means when someone talks too much. There wanteth not sin means sin is never far away. The more we talk, the more likely we are to say something wrong, unkind, or untrue. Refraineth his lips means holds back his words. Sometimes the wisest thing to say is nothing at all.
Verse 22. 'The blessing of the LORD, it maketh rich, and he addeth no sorrow with it.' Some riches come with sorrow attached: stress, worry, guilt, broken relationships. But when God blesses us, his blessing makes us truly rich, and it does not come with sorrow. God’s blessing is not always money. It can be peace, love, family, contentment, and the joy of knowing him.
Peter wrote, 'for charity shall cover the multitude of sins.' And James wrote, 'let every man be swift to hear, slow to speak, slow to wrath'.
Try to listen more than you speak today. Before you speak, ask yourself, is this needed, and is it kind? And if someone has wronged you, ask God to help your love cover it, instead of stirring it up again.
Here is today’s question. Proverbs says, in the multitude of words there wanteth not sin. When have you said something you later wished you had not said, and what did you learn from it?
Let us pray. Lord God, thank you for your blessing, which makes us truly rich. Help me to work diligently, to speak words that give life, and to hold back words that would hurt. Let my love cover the wrongs of others, as your love covers mine. Help me to be a child who brings joy to those who love me. Amen.
Next on the walk, Proverbs chapter eleven. God delights in honest scales. When pride comes, shame follows. And the generous soul will be refreshed.
