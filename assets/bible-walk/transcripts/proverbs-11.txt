A gardener spends the whole day watering other people’s plants, carrying bucket after bucket from the well. By the end of the day, she is hot and tired. But as she pours the last bucket, she notices her own garden. It is greener than ever. The rain has fallen on it while she was busy helping others. Proverbs says generosity works like that.
In chapter ten, the proverbs of Solomon began, short sayings that weigh one way of life against another. A wise son maketh a glad father. Hatred stirs up quarrels, but love covers all sins. And in the multitude of words, sin is never far away. The blessing of the LORD makes rich, and adds no sorrow.
Now, in chapter eleven, we hear more of Solomon’s sayings. Listen for what God thinks of honesty in business. And listen for the surprising way generosity works.
The chapter begins in the marketplace. 'A false balance is abomination to the LORD: but a just weight is his delight.'
In those days, people bought and sold using scales, with stones as weights. A dishonest trader might use a stone that was a little too light when selling, or a little too heavy when buying, to cheat his customers. God hates that. He loves honest dealing. God cares about how we do business, not only how we pray.
Then comes a famous saying about pride.
'The integrity of the upright shall guide them'. Integrity means being whole, honest, the same on the inside as on the outside. When we live with integrity, it guides us, like a compass. We do not have to wonder what to do. We simply do what is right.
'Riches profit not in the day of wrath: but righteousness delivereth from death.' When we stand before God, money will not help us.
Then come sayings about our words, and how they affect our neighbours. 'An hypocrite with his mouth destroyeth his neighbour'. A hypocrite is someone who pretends to be good. Their words can do great harm.
'When it goeth well with the righteous, the city rejoiceth'. Good people make a whole town a better place. 'By the blessing of the upright the city is exalted: but it is overthrown by the mouth of the wicked.'
'He that is void of wisdom despiseth his neighbour: but a man of understanding holdeth his peace.' A wise person does not mock others. They know when to keep quiet.
'A talebearer revealeth secrets: but he that is of a faithful spirit concealeth the matter.' A talebearer is a gossip, someone who passes on things that should be kept private. A faithful friend keeps a secret.
Then comes a saying about advice.
'He that is surety for a stranger shall smart for it'. Remember, being surety means promising to pay someone else’s debt. Proverbs warns again: if you do it for a stranger, you will get hurt.
Then sayings about kindness. 'The merciful man doeth good to his own soul: but he that is cruel troubleth his own flesh.' Kindness blesses the one who gives it. Cruelty hurts the cruel person too.
'To him that soweth righteousness shall be a sure reward.' Doing right is like planting seeds. The harvest will come.
And then, the surprising way generosity works. 'There is that scattereth, and yet increaseth; and there is that withholdeth more than is meet, but it tendeth to poverty.' Some people give freely, and somehow end up with more. Others hold on tightly to more than they need, and end up poorer.
And then the famous saying.
'He that withholdeth corn, the people shall curse him: but blessing shall be upon the head of him that selleth it.' In a famine, someone who hoards grain to sell later at a high price is hated. But someone who sells it fairly when people need it is blessed.
'He that trusteth in his riches shall fall: but the righteous shall flourish as a branch.' Money is not a safe thing to lean on. But those who live rightly with God grow like healthy green branches.
'He that troubleth his own house shall inherit the wind'. Someone who brings trouble on his own family ends up with nothing, like trying to hold the wind.
And then a beautiful saying. 'The fruit of the righteous is a tree of life; and he that winneth souls is wise.' Winning souls means helping people turn to God and live wisely. The life of a good person is like a tree that gives fruit to others.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 2. 'When pride cometh, then cometh shame: but with the lowly is wisdom.' Pride and shame often travel together. When we think too highly of ourselves, we are setting ourselves up for a fall. The lowly are the humble, people who do not think they are better than others. And Proverbs says that is where wisdom is found. Humble people are teachable, and so they grow wise.
Verse 14. 'Where no counsel is, the people fall: but in the multitude of counsellors there is safety.' Counsel means advice. A nation, a family, or a person who never listens to advice will stumble. But when we ask for advice from many wise people, we are much safer. None of us sees everything. Wise people know they need others to help them see what they might miss.
Verse 25. 'The liberal soul shall be made fat: and he that watereth shall be watered also himself.' Liberal here means generous. Made fat means made rich, well fed, blessed. Those who generously refresh others will themselves be refreshed. This is not a trick for getting rich. It is simply how God has made the world. A generous heart is a happy heart, and God loves to bless those who bless others.
Jesus said, 'Give, and it shall be given unto you'. And Paul wrote, 'He which soweth sparingly shall reap also sparingly'.
Is there someone you could refresh today, with your time, your help, or your gifts? Be generous. And if you are facing a big decision, ask for advice from several wise people before you make it.
Here is today’s question. Proverbs says, he that watereth shall be watered also himself. When have you been blessed by being generous to someone else?
Let us pray. Lord God, you delight in honesty, so help me to be honest in everything, even in small things. Keep me humble, for with the lowly is wisdom. Give me a generous heart, that loves to refresh others. And help me to listen to wise counsel, and not to trust in my own understanding alone. Amen.
