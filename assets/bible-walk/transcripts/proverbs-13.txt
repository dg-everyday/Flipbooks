Put a cold stone next to a warm fire, and before long the stone grows warm. Put it out in the snow, and it grows cold. Without even trying, it takes on the temperature of whatever is around it. People are a little like that. Spend time with kind people, and you grow kinder. Spend time with foolish people, and foolishness rubs off.
In chapter twelve, we heard that whoso loveth instruction loveth knowledge. The way of a fool is right in his own eyes, but he that listens to counsel is wise. Some words pierce like a sword, but the tongue of the wise is health. And a good word makes a heavy heart glad.
Now, in chapter thirteen, Solomon’s sayings go on. Listen for what Proverbs says about the friends we choose. And listen for a saying about hope.
The chapter begins with children again. 'A wise son heareth his father’s instruction: but a scorner heareth not rebuke.' A wise child listens. A mocker will not.
Then a saying about guarding our words.
'The soul of the sluggard desireth, and hath nothing: but the soul of the diligent shall be made fat.' The lazy person wants things, but never gets them, because he will not work for them. Wishing is not enough.
Then a saying about pretending. 'There is that maketh himself rich, yet hath nothing: there is that maketh himself poor, yet hath great riches.' Some people show off, pretending to be rich, when they have nothing. Others live simply, and are truly rich. Do not judge people by how they look.
'Only by pride cometh contention'. Contention means arguments and fights. Where do most quarrels come from? Pride. Someone refuses to back down, or admit they were wrong. 'but with the well advised is wisdom.'
'Wealth gotten by vanity shall be diminished: but he that gathereth by labour shall increase.' Money gained quickly, by dishonest or foolish means, soon disappears. But money gathered little by little, by honest work, grows.
Then a saying about hope.
'The law of the wise is a fountain of life, to depart from the snares of death.' Wise teaching is like a spring of fresh water, helping us avoid deadly traps. 'Good understanding giveth favour: but the way of transgressors is hard.' Many people think sin is the easy way. Proverbs says the opposite. The way of those who break God’s law is hard, full of trouble and regret.
'A wicked messenger falleth into mischief: but a faithful ambassador is health.' Someone who can be trusted to carry a message faithfully is a blessing.
Then comes a very important saying about friends.
'Evil pursueth sinners: but to the righteous good shall be repaid.' Our choices have consequences that follow us.
'A good man leaveth an inheritance to his children’s children'. A good person thinks about the future, not just about today. They leave something for their grandchildren: perhaps money or land, but more importantly, a good name, wise teaching, and an example of faith.
'Much food is in the tillage of the poor: but there is that is destroyed for want of judgment.' Even a poor person’s small field can produce plenty of food, when it is worked wisely. But injustice can sweep it all away.
Then comes a well known saying about children. 'He that spareth his rod hateth his son: but he that loveth him chasteneth him betimes.' Chasteneth means corrects, disciplines. Betimes means early, promptly. Christians have understood the rod in this verse in different ways. But the heart of it is clear. A parent who never corrects their child is not showing love. Loving parents guide and correct their children, gently and firmly, while they are still young, because they want them to grow up wise. God corrects us in the same way, because he loves us.
And the chapter ends with a contrast. 'The righteous eateth to the satisfying of his soul: but the belly of the wicked shall want.' Those who live God’s way find deep satisfaction. Those who live without him are always left wanting more.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 3. 'He that keepeth his mouth keepeth his life: but he that openeth wide his lips shall have destruction.' Keepeth his mouth means guards what he says. Someone who thinks before speaking protects themselves from much trouble. But someone who opens their mouth wide, saying whatever comes into their head, will bring disaster on themselves. Careless words can ruin friendships, reputations and lives. Careful words protect them.
Verse 12. 'Hope deferred maketh the heart sick: but when the desire cometh, it is a tree of life.' Deferred means put off, delayed. When we wait and wait for something we long for, and it does not come, our hearts grow weary and sick. Proverbs understands that. It is honest about how hard waiting can be. But when the thing we hoped for finally comes, it is like a tree of life, full of joy. If you are waiting for something, God understands. Keep bringing it to him.
Verse 20. 'He that walketh with wise men shall be wise: but a companion of fools shall be destroyed.' We become like the people we spend the most time with. If we walk with wise people, their wisdom rubs off on us. If we choose foolish friends, we will be pulled into their foolishness, and it will harm us. Choose your friends carefully. They are shaping who you are becoming.
Paul wrote, 'Be not deceived: evil communications corrupt good manners.' And the letter to the Hebrews says, 'Now no chastening for the present seemeth to be joyous, but grievous: nevertheless afterward it yieldeth the peaceable fruit of righteousness'.
Think about the people you spend the most time with. Are they helping you grow wiser? Seek out time with someone wise this week. And if you are waiting for something you long for, tell God about it honestly.
Here is today’s question. Proverbs says, he that walketh with wise men shall be wise. Who are the wise people in your life, and how could you spend more time learning from them?
Let us pray. Lord God, help me to choose my friends wisely, and to walk with wise people. Help me to guard my mouth, and to work diligently. When hope is delayed and my heart grows weary, comfort me, and help me to keep trusting you. And help me to leave a good inheritance of faith for those who come after me. Amen.
