A young man sits down with a big sheet of paper and plans out his whole life. Where he will live. What job he will do. Who he will marry. How much money he will make. It is a very good plan. But years later, looking back, he laughs. Almost nothing went the way he planned. And yet, somehow, God led him somewhere better than anything he could have imagined.
In chapter fifteen, we heard that a soft answer turneth away wrath. The eyes of the LORD are in every place, beholding the evil and the good. Better a dinner of herbs where love is, than a feast with hatred. And before honour is humility.
Now, in chapter sixteen, Solomon’s sayings turn to God and our plans. Listen for who really directs our lives. And listen for one of the most famous sayings in the Bible, about pride.
The chapter begins with God and our hearts. 'The preparations of the heart in man, and the answer of the tongue, is from the LORD.' We may plan in our hearts what we will say. But God has the final word.
'All the ways of a man are clean in his own eyes; but the LORD weigheth the spirits.' We usually think we are right. We can always find a good reason for what we do. But God weighs our hearts. He sees our real motives.
So what should we do with our plans?
'Every one that is proud in heart is an abomination to the LORD'. God hates pride.
'By mercy and truth iniquity is purged: and by the fear of the LORD men depart from evil.' Purged means cleansed, taken away. God deals with sin through his mercy and faithfulness. And honouring God helps us turn away from evil.
Then a wonderful promise. 'When a man’s ways please the LORD, he maketh even his enemies to be at peace with him.'
'Better is a little with righteousness than great revenues without right.' It is better to have a little, honestly, than to be rich by cheating.
Then comes another verse about plans.
Then several sayings about kings. A good king speaks fairly, and judges justly. Kings should hate wickedness, because a throne is made strong by righteousness. They should love those who speak the truth. A wise person knows how to calm a king’s anger. And the favour of a good king is like the spring rain that brings the harvest.
'How much better is it to get wisdom than gold! and to get understanding rather to be chosen than silver!' We have heard this before. Proverbs keeps saying it, because we keep forgetting.
'The highway of the upright is to depart from evil'. The main road for good people is the road away from evil. 'he that keepeth his way preserveth his soul.' Stay on that road, and you protect your life.
And then the famous saying.
'Better it is to be of an humble spirit with the lowly, than to divide the spoil with the proud.' It is better to be humble, among humble people, than to share in riches with the proud.
'He that handleth a matter wisely shall find good: and whoso trusteth in the LORD, happy is he.'
Then sayings about words. 'The heart of the wise teacheth his mouth'. A wise heart guides what we say. 'Pleasant words are as an honeycomb, sweet to the soul, and health to the bones.' Kind words are sweet, like honey, and healthy, like medicine.
And then, the same saying we heard in chapter fourteen. 'There is a way that seemeth right unto a man, but the end thereof are the ways of death.' When something is repeated in the Bible, it is important.
Then a warning about troublemakers. 'A froward man soweth strife: and a whisperer separateth chief friends.' A whisperer is a gossip. Gossip can break up even the closest friendships.
Then a lovely saying about old age. 'The hoary head is a crown of glory, if it be found in the way of righteousness.' Hoary means grey or white haired. An old person who has walked with God all their life wears their grey hair like a crown. Honour the old.
And then a saying about self control.
And the chapter ends with this. 'The lot is cast into the lap; but the whole disposing thereof is of the LORD.' In those days, people sometimes cast lots, a bit like rolling dice, to make decisions. It looks like chance. But God is in control of everything, even what looks like chance.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 3. 'Commit thy works unto the LORD, and thy thoughts shall be established.' Commit means hand over, roll onto. The picture is of rolling a heavy load off your own back onto someone stronger. Hand your work, your plans and your worries over to God. And he will make your thoughts steady and your plans firm. We do not have to carry everything ourselves.
Verse 9. 'A man’s heart deviseth his way: but the LORD directeth his steps.' Deviseth means plans. It is good and wise to make plans. But God directs our steps. He guides us, often in ways we did not expect. Sometimes our plans fail, and later we see that God was leading us somewhere better. We plan, but we hold our plans in an open hand, trusting him.
Verse 18. 'Pride goeth before destruction, and an haughty spirit before a fall.' Haughty means proud, looking down on others. This is one of the most famous sayings in the Bible, and people use it every day without knowing where it comes from. Pride comes first, and then the fall. Think of Saul, or Uzziah, or the proud king of Assyria. Pride always ends in a fall. Humility keeps us safe.
James wrote, 'For that ye ought to say, If the Lord will, we shall live, and do this, or that.' And Peter wrote, 'God resisteth the proud, and giveth grace to the humble.'
Take your plans for this week, and commit them to the LORD in prayer. Hand them over to him. And watch out for pride in your heart today. Ask God to keep you humble.
Here is today’s question. Proverbs says, a man’s heart deviseth his way: but the LORD directeth his steps. When has God led you somewhere different from what you planned, and how did it turn out?
Let us pray. Lord God, I commit my works and my plans to you. Direct my steps, even when I do not understand the way. Keep me from pride, which goes before a fall, and give me a humble spirit. Help me to speak pleasant words, and to rule my own spirit. Thank you that everything, even what looks like chance, is in your hands. Amen.
