A man spends his whole life chasing money. He works late, misses his children growing up, and never has time for friends, or for God. At last, he has a great deal of money. And then, in one bad year, almost all of it is gone. It flew away, like a bird. And he looks around, and realises what he gave up to get it.
In chapter twenty two, we heard that a good name is rather to be chosen than great riches. The rich and poor meet together, and the LORD is the maker of them all. Train up a child in the way he should go. And the words of the wise began.
Now, in chapter twenty three, the words of the wise go on. Listen for what Proverbs says about chasing money. And listen for what the father most wants from his son.
The chapter begins with some advice about table manners, at a very important dinner. 'When thou sittest to eat with a ruler, consider diligently what is before thee'. If you are invited to eat with a powerful person, be careful. 'And put a knife to thy throat, if thou be a man given to appetite.' That is an old way of saying, control yourself! Do not stuff yourself greedily. 'Be not desirous of his dainties: for they are deceitful meat.' His fancy food might come with strings attached. He may want something from you.
Then a saying about riches.
Then a warning about a stingy host. Do not eat the food of someone who is mean and grudging, even if they say, eat and drink. 'For as he thinketh in his heart, so is he'. What a person really thinks inside is who they really are, whatever they say out loud. His heart is not with you. You will regret eating his food, and all your polite words will be wasted.
'Speak not in the ears of a fool: for he will despise the wisdom of thy words.' Do not waste your wisdom on someone who will only mock it.
'Remove not the old landmark; and enter not into the fields of the fatherless'. Do not cheat orphans out of their land. 'For their redeemer is mighty; he shall plead their cause with thee.' A redeemer was a family member who protected the helpless, like Boaz in the book of Ruth. Orphans may have no earthly protector. But God himself is their mighty redeemer.
'Apply thine heart unto instruction, and thine ears to the words of knowledge.' Then a reminder that loving parents correct their children, to save them from far worse harm later.
Then the father speaks tenderly. 'My son, if thine heart be wise, my heart shall rejoice, even mine.' When you speak what is right, my innermost being will rejoice. Nothing makes a parent happier than a child who chooses wisdom.
'Let not thine heart envy sinners: but be thou in the fear of the LORD all the day long.' Do not be jealous of people who seem to get ahead by doing wrong. Keep honouring God, all day long. 'For surely there is an end; and thine expectation shall not be cut off.' There is a future. Your hope will not be disappointed.
Then a warning about overindulging. Do not spend time with people who drink too much wine, or who stuff themselves with food. Living for drink and food leads to poverty, and laziness leaves people dressed in rags.
'Hearken unto thy father that begat thee, and despise not thy mother when she is old.' Listen to your parents, and do not look down on them when they grow old. Honour them all their lives.
Then comes a beautiful saying about truth.
'The father of the righteous shall greatly rejoice'. Let your father and mother be glad, and let your mother rejoice.
And then the heart of the chapter, and perhaps of the whole book.
The father warns once more about the temptation to be unfaithful, which is like a deep pit, and traps many.
And the chapter ends with a vivid poem about too much wine. 'Who hath woe? who hath sorrow? who hath contentions? who hath babbling? who hath wounds without cause? who hath redness of eyes?' Who is miserable, always arguing, talking nonsense, getting hurt for no reason, with red eyes? 'They that tarry long at the wine'. Those who sit drinking for hours.
'Look not thou upon the wine when it is red, when it giveth his colour in the cup, when it moveth itself aright. At the last it biteth like a serpent, and stingeth like an adder.' It looks lovely, sparkling in the cup. It goes down smoothly. But in the end, it bites like a snake. Too much strong drink confuses the mind, and makes people say twisted things. They stagger like someone trying to sleep on a ship in a storm. And the saddest part is the end of the poem. They get hurt, and do not even feel it. And when they wake up, the first thing they want is more. That is how something can take control of a person’s life.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 5. 'Wilt thou set thine eyes upon that which is not? for riches certainly make themselves wings; they fly away as an eagle toward heaven.' The verse before says, labour not to be rich. Do not wear yourself out trying to get wealthy. Riches are like a bird that suddenly grows wings and flies away. They can be gone in a moment. Why set your heart on something so uncertain? Work hard, yes. But do not make money the goal of your life.
Verse 23. 'Buy the truth, and sell it not; also wisdom, and instruction, and understanding.' Buy the truth means, whatever it costs, get it. It is worth any price. And sell it not means, once you have it, never give it up, whatever anyone offers you. Some people trade the truth for popularity, or money, or an easy life. Proverbs says, hold on to the truth, and never let it go.
Verse 26. 'My son, give me thine heart, and let thine eyes observe my ways.' This is what the father wants most. Not just obedience on the outside, but his son’s heart. And this is what God wants from each of us too. He does not just want our rules kept, or our money given, or our time spent. He wants our hearts. When we give God our hearts, everything else follows.
Jesus said, 'Lay not up for yourselves treasures upon earth, where moth and rust doth corrupt'. And Paul wrote, 'For the love of money is the root of all evil'.
Ask yourself honestly, what am I chasing? Is it something that might fly away? And give God your heart today, not just your time or your words. Say to him, here is my heart, Lord.
Here is today’s question. The father says, my son, give me thine heart. What do you think it means to give God your heart, and what might be holding you back?
Let us pray. Lord God, keep me from setting my heart on riches, which fly away like an eagle. Help me to buy the truth, and never sell it. Keep me from anything that would take control of my life. Help me to honour my parents all their lives. And Lord, here is my heart. I give it to you. Amen.
