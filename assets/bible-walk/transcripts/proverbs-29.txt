A group of travellers is crossing a wide desert, with no roads and no signs. Without a map or a guide, they wander in circles, getting more and more lost and thirsty. Then someone finds an old map, with a clear route marked on it. Suddenly everyone knows which way to go. They have direction, and hope. Proverbs says God’s Word is like that map.
In chapter twenty eight, we heard that the wicked flee when no man pursueth, but the righteous are bold as a lion. He that covereth his sins shall not prosper, but whoso confesseth and forsaketh them shall have mercy. And he that giveth to the poor shall not lack.
Now, in chapter twenty nine, the last chapter of Solomon’s proverbs. Listen for a famous saying about vision. And listen for what to do when we are afraid of what people think.
The chapter begins with a serious warning. 'He, that being often reproved hardeneth his neck, shall suddenly be destroyed, and that without remedy.' Reproved means corrected. Hardeneth his neck means becomes stubborn, like an ox that refuses to turn. Someone who is warned again and again, and keeps refusing to listen, will one day face disaster with no way out. Do you remember Israel, in the books of Kings, warned by prophet after prophet?
'When the righteous are in authority, the people rejoice: but when the wicked beareth rule, the people mourn.' Good leaders bring joy. Wicked leaders bring sorrow.
'Whoso loveth wisdom rejoiceth his father'. A wise child makes a parent glad.
'The king by judgment establisheth the land: but he that receiveth gifts overthroweth it.' A ruler who judges fairly makes the land strong. A ruler who takes bribes destroys it.
'A man that flattereth his neighbour spreadeth a net for his feet.' Flattery is a trap. Be careful of people who praise you too much.
'In the transgression of an evil man there is a snare: but the righteous doth sing and rejoice.' Wicked people are caught in their own sins. Good people sing for joy.
'The righteous considereth the cause of the poor: but the wicked regardeth not to know it.' Good people care about what is fair for the poor. Wicked people do not even want to know.
'Scornful men bring a city into a snare: but wise men turn away wrath.' Mockers stir up trouble in a city. Wise people calm things down.
Then a saying about self control.
'If a ruler hearken to lies, all his servants are wicked.' When a leader listens to lies, everyone around him learns to lie.
'The poor and the deceitful man meet together: the LORD lighteneth both their eyes.' God gives light and life to everyone, rich or poor, honest or not. We all depend on him.
'The king that faithfully judgeth the poor, his throne shall be established for ever.'
Then two sayings about raising children. Correction and guidance give wisdom, but a child left completely to themselves will bring shame to their mother. 'Correct thy son, and he shall give thee rest; yea, he shall give delight unto thy soul.' Parents who lovingly guide and correct their children will find joy in them later.
Then comes the famous saying about vision.
'Seest thou a man that is hasty in his words? there is more hope of a fool than of him.' Someone who speaks without thinking is in more trouble than a fool.
'An angry man stirreth up strife, and a furious man aboundeth in transgression.' Anger leads to fights, and to many other sins.
'A man’s pride shall bring him low: but honour shall uphold the humble in spirit.' This is one of the great themes of Proverbs. Pride brings us down. Humility lifts us up.
'Whoso is partner with a thief hateth his own soul'. Going along with someone who steals harms your own soul.
Then a saying about fear.
'Many seek the ruler’s favour; but every man’s judgment cometh from the LORD.' People rush to get the attention of powerful people, hoping for help. But in the end, justice comes from God. It is his opinion that really matters.
And the chapter ends with a contrast. 'An unjust man is an abomination to the just: and he that is upright in the way is abomination to the wicked.' Good and evil cannot stand each other. The righteous are disgusted by injustice. And the wicked are disgusted by goodness.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 11. 'A fool uttereth all his mind: but a wise man keepeth it in till afterwards.' Uttereth all his mind means says everything he thinks, the moment he thinks it. A fool lets everything out: every angry word, every opinion, every complaint. A wise person holds some things back, and waits for the right time, or decides not to say them at all. Not every thought needs to be spoken.
Verse 18. 'Where there is no vision, the people perish: but he that keepeth the law, happy is he.' People often use this verse to mean, you need goals and plans for the future. But in the Bible, a vision means God revealing his word, through prophets. Perish here means run wild, without restraint. Where God’s Word is not heard, people lose their way, and do whatever they like. But whoever keeps God’s law is happy. We need God’s Word to guide us, like a map in the desert.
Verse 25. 'The fear of man bringeth a snare: but whoso putteth his trust in the LORD shall be safe.' The fear of man means being afraid of what people think, or what they might do to us. It is a trap. It makes us do things we know are wrong, just to be liked, or stops us doing what is right, because we are afraid. But whoever trusts in the LORD is safe. When we care more about what God thinks than what people think, we are free.
The prophet Amos warned of a famine, 'not a famine of bread, nor a thirst for water, but of hearing the words of the LORD'. And the psalmist wrote, 'The LORD is on my side; I will not fear: what can man do unto me?'
Is there something you are afraid to do, or say, because of what people might think? Ask God to free you from the fear of man. And take time today to let God’s Word guide you, like a map.
Here is today’s question. Proverbs says, the fear of man bringeth a snare. When has worrying about what people think trapped you, and how can trusting God set you free?
Let us pray. Lord God, keep me from hardening my neck when you correct me. Help me to hold back words that should not be said. Thank you for your Word, which gives vision and direction. Free me from the fear of man, which is a snare. I put my trust in you, and in you I am safe. Amen.
