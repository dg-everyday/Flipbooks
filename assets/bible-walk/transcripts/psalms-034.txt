A young man stands before a foreign king. He is in terrible danger. The king’s servants have recognised him. This is David, they whisper, the great warrior who killed thousands of our people. David is terrified. So he does something strange. He pretends to be mad. He scratches at the doors of the gate, and lets spit run down his beard. The king is disgusted. Get this madman out of here! he says. And David escapes with his life.
In Psalm ninety, Moses prayed, LORD, thou hast been our dwelling place in all generations. From everlasting to everlasting, thou art God. Our lives are short, like grass. So he prayed, teach us to number our days, that we may apply our hearts unto wisdom. And establish thou the work of our hands.
Now we move into psalms of praise and thanksgiving. Psalm thirty four is David’s song of thanks after a narrow escape. Listen for the invitation David gives us. And listen for who God is close to.
Psalm thirty four has a title that tells its story. It is a psalm of David, when he changed his behaviour before Abimelech, who drove him away.
We heard the story in the first book of Samuel. David was running from King Saul, who wanted to kill him. In desperation, he fled to the land of the Philistines, Israel’s enemies, to the city of Gath, the hometown of Goliath. The king of Gath was called Achish. When the servants recognised David, he was afraid, and pretended to be mad. And the king sent him away.
David got out alive. And afterwards, he wrote this song of praise. Like Psalm thirty seven, it is an alphabet psalm, each verse beginning with the next Hebrew letter.
It begins, 'I will bless the LORD at all times: his praise shall continually be in my mouth.' At all times. Not just when life is good. Even after a frightening, humiliating experience, David praises God.
'My soul shall make her boast in the LORD: the humble shall hear thereof, and be glad.' He does not boast about his clever escape, but about God.
'O magnify the LORD with me, and let us exalt his name together.' To magnify means to make bigger. We cannot make God bigger, but we can make him bigger in our eyes, and in the eyes of others. David invites everyone to join in. Let us do it together.
Then David tells what happened.
'They looked unto him, and were lightened: and their faces were not ashamed.' Lightened means their faces shone, they became radiant. People who look to God have bright faces, not ashamed ones.
'This poor man cried, and the LORD heard him, and saved him out of all his troubles.' This poor man. David is talking about himself. He was not a king then. He was a poor, frightened man, pretending to be mad. And God heard him.
'The angel of the LORD encampeth round about them that fear him, and delivereth them.' Encampeth means sets up camp. Like an army camping all around a city to protect it, God’s angel surrounds those who honour him.
And then David gives an invitation.
'The young lions do lack, and suffer hunger: but they that seek the LORD shall not want any good thing.' Young lions are strong, fierce hunters. Yet even they sometimes go hungry. But those who seek the LORD will not lack any good thing.
Then David speaks like a teacher to his students. 'Come, ye children, hearken unto me: I will teach you the fear of the LORD.' Hearken means listen. And what does he teach? 'What man is he that desireth life, and loveth many days, that he may see good? Keep thy tongue from evil, and thy lips from speaking guile. Depart from evil, and do good; seek peace, and pursue it.' Guile means deceit, lies. Do you want a good life? Watch your words. Turn away from evil. Do good. And do not just wait for peace. Chase after it! The apostle Peter quoted these very words in his first letter.
Then one of the most comforting verses in the Bible.
'Many are the afflictions of the righteous: but the LORD delivereth him out of them all.' Notice, it does not say the righteous have no troubles. It says they have many. But God delivers them out of them all.
'He keepeth all his bones: not one of them is broken.' When Jesus died on the cross, the soldiers broke the legs of the two thieves crucified beside him. But when they came to Jesus, he was already dead, so they did not break his legs. John tells us this happened, that the scripture should be fulfilled, a bone of him shall not be broken.
And the psalm ends, 'The LORD redeemeth the soul of his servants: and none of them that trust in him shall be desolate.' Desolate means abandoned, ruined. No one who trusts in God will be left alone.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 4. 'I sought the LORD, and he heard me, and delivered me from all my fears.' David was full of fears in Gath. Fear of the king. Fear of death. Fear of being caught. And he did the one thing that could help. He sought the LORD. He prayed. And God heard him, and delivered him, not just from danger, but from all his fears. When you are afraid, seek the LORD. Tell him your fears. He hears.
Verse 8. 'O taste and see that the LORD is good: blessed is the man that trusteth in him.' You cannot really know how good a food is until you taste it. Someone can describe a delicious mango to you. But you will only truly know when you taste it for yourself. David says, God is like that. Do not just hear about him. Try him. Trust him. Taste for yourself, and see how good he is.
Verse 18. 'The LORD is nigh unto them that are of a broken heart; and saveth such as be of a contrite spirit.' Nigh means near, close. When our hearts are broken, by sadness, loss, or disappointment, God does not stay far away. He comes close. Contrite means crushed, humbled. When we feel crushed, God saves us. If your heart is broken today, God is near you. Closer than you think.
Peter wrote, 'let him eschew evil, and do good; let him seek peace, and ensue it.' And John wrote, 'For these things were done, that the scripture should be fulfilled, A bone of him shall not be broken.'
Taste and see that the LORD is good today. Think of one way God has been good to you, and tell someone else about it, so that you can magnify the LORD together. And if you know someone whose heart is broken, remember that God is near them, and perhaps he wants to use you to show it.
Here is today’s question. David invites us to taste and see that the LORD is good. What do you think it means to taste God’s goodness for yourself, rather than just hearing about it?
Let us pray. Lord, I will bless you at all times, and your praise shall continually be in my mouth. Thank you that when I seek you, you hear me, and deliver me from all my fears. Help me to taste and see that you are good. Thank you that you are near to the broken hearted. Help me to keep my tongue from evil, to do good, and to seek peace. Amen.
