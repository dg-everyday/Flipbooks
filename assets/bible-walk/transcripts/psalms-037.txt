Have you ever seen someone cheat in a game, and win? Or someone tell lies and get away with it, while you told the truth and got into trouble? It does not seem fair. It can make you angry, or even make you wonder whether doing right is worth it. Psalm thirty seven was written for exactly that feeling.
Before this, in Psalm one hundred and thirty, the writer cried out of the depths to the LORD. If thou shouldest mark iniquities, who shall stand? But there is forgiveness with thee.
Now we move to psalms of wisdom for life. Our thirty favourite psalms are grouped by theme, not in number order, so we go back to Psalm thirty seven, the twentieth of the thirty. Here an old man gives wise advice to anyone who is upset because wicked people seem to be winning. Listen for the words fret not. And listen for what he tells us to do instead.
Psalm thirty seven is a psalm of David. And it seems he wrote it when he was old. Near the middle, he says, 'I have been young, and now am old'. So this is like a grandfather sitting down with his grandchildren, and sharing what he has learned over a long life.
Like Psalm one hundred and nineteen, this psalm is an alphabet poem. In the Hebrew, every second verse begins with the next letter of the alphabet. It is not so much a song, as a collection of wise sayings, like the book of Proverbs.
It begins, 'Fret not thyself because of evildoers, neither be thou envious against the workers of iniquity.' To fret means to worry, to get hot and bothered, to stew about something. Envious means wishing you had what they have. Do not get upset about wicked people, David says. And do not be jealous of them.
Why not? 'For they shall soon be cut down like the grass, and wither as the green herb.' In the hot sun of Israel, grass that looked green and fresh one day could be brown and dead not long after. Wicked people may look successful now. But their success will not last.
Then David gives a series of instructions. And each one is about turning away from worry, and towards God.
First, 'Trust in the LORD, and do good; so shalt thou dwell in the land, and verily thou shalt be fed.' Instead of worrying about others, trust God, and keep doing what is right.
Second, delight in God himself, more than in anything he gives.
Third, roll your worries and your plans onto him.
Fourth, 'Rest in the LORD, and wait patiently for him'. Rest means be still, be quiet. Stop churning inside. 'fret not thyself because of him who prospereth in his way'. Prospereth means doing well, getting rich.
'Cease from anger, and forsake wrath: fret not thyself in any wise to do evil.' Cease means stop. Forsake means let go of. Anger at the unfairness of life can lead us to do wrong ourselves. So let it go.
'But the meek shall inherit the earth; and shall delight themselves in the abundance of peace.' The meek are the gentle, humble people, who do not push themselves forward or fight for their own way. They seem to lose out. But in the end, they will inherit the whole earth. Jesus said exactly the same thing in his Sermon on the Mount. Blessed are the meek: for they shall inherit the earth.
David goes on with more wise sayings. 'A little that a righteous man hath is better than the riches of many wicked.' It is better to have a little with God, than a lot without him.
Then a lovely saying about God’s care.
And then David speaks from his own long experience. 'I have been young, and now am old; yet have I not seen the righteous forsaken, nor his seed begging bread.' Seed here means children. In all his long life, David had seen God take care of those who followed him.
Then he describes a wicked man he once knew. 'I have seen the wicked in great power, and spreading himself like a green bay tree.' A bay tree is a large, leafy evergreen tree. The wicked man looked so strong and successful, spreading out in every direction. 'Yet he passed away, and, lo, he was not: yea, I sought him, but he could not be found.' He was gone. All his power and success came to nothing.
'Mark the perfect man, and behold the upright: for the end of that man is peace.' Mark means look carefully at. Watch the person who follows God. Their life may not always be easy. But in the end, there is peace.
And the psalm ends, 'But the salvation of the righteous is of the LORD: he is their strength in the time of trouble. And the LORD shall help them and deliver them'.
So what is the old king’s advice? When you see the wicked doing well, do not fret. Trust God. Delight in him. Commit your way to him. Rest in him, and wait patiently. In the end, God will put everything right.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 4. 'Delight thyself also in the LORD; and he shall give thee the desires of thine heart.' To delight in God means to enjoy him, to find your greatest happiness in him. This verse does not mean that God will give us anything we want, like a magic wish. It means that when God is our greatest delight, our desires begin to change. We start wanting what he wants. And those are the desires he loves to give.
Verse 5. 'Commit thy way unto the LORD; trust also in him; and he shall bring it to pass.' Commit means roll it onto him, as you might roll a heavy stone off your shoulders onto someone stronger. Thy way means your whole life, your plans, your future, your worries. Roll it all onto God. Trust him. And he will act. He will bring it to pass, in his own time and his own way.
Verse 23. 'The steps of a good man are ordered by the LORD: and he delighteth in his way.' Ordered means made firm, directed. God guides every step of the person who follows him. And the next verse adds, 'Though he fall, he shall not be utterly cast down: for the LORD upholdeth him with his hand.' We may stumble. We may even fall. But God holds our hand, and he will not let us be thrown down completely.
Jesus said, 'Blessed are the meek: for they shall inherit the earth.' And Paul wrote, 'And let us not be weary in well doing: for in due season we shall reap, if we faint not.'
Is there something unfair that is making you angry or upset? Instead of fretting, roll it onto God. Tell him about it, and leave it with him. Then keep doing good, and find your delight in him.
Here is today’s question. David says, fret not thyself because of evildoers. Why do you think it is so easy to worry about unfair things, and what does David say we should do instead?
Let us pray. Lord God, when I see wrong things happening, and wicked people seeming to win, help me not to fret. Help me to trust in you, and do good. Help me to delight in you above everything else. I commit my way to you. Order my steps, and when I fall, hold me up with your hand. I will rest in you, and wait patiently for you. Amen.
