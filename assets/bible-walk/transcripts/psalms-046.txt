The ground begins to shake. Dishes rattle in the cupboard. Pictures fall from the walls. Outside, people run into the streets, frightened. An earthquake is one of the scariest things that can happen, because the one thing we always count on, the solid ground beneath our feet, is moving. Psalm forty six imagines something even bigger: the whole earth shaking, and the mountains falling into the sea. And yet it says, we will not fear.
In Psalm ninety one, we heard about the one who dwells in the secret place of the most High, and abides under the shadow of the Almighty. He shall cover thee with his feathers, and under his wings shalt thou trust. And God promised, he shall call upon me, and I will answer him: I will be with him in trouble.
Now, in Psalm forty six, we hear about God as our strength when the whole world seems to fall apart. Listen for a line that is repeated, like the chorus of a song. And listen for God’s voice at the end.
Psalm forty six is a song for the sons of Korah. They were a family of singers and musicians who served in the temple. The psalm is like a song with three verses. And each verse ends with the strange word, Selah, perhaps a pause, a moment to stop and think.
The first verse begins with a great statement.
And then, a brave response. 'Therefore will not we fear, though the earth be removed, and though the mountains be carried into the midst of the sea; Though the waters thereof roar and be troubled, though the mountains shake with the swelling thereof.' Imagine the worst disaster you can think of. Earthquakes. Mountains crashing into the ocean. Huge waves roaring. Even then, the psalm says, we will not be afraid. Why? Because God is bigger than any earthquake, any flood, any disaster. When everything else is shaken, he is not.
Then the second verse of the song moves to a quieter picture. 'There is a river, the streams whereof shall make glad the city of God, the holy place of the tabernacles of the most High.' After the roaring sea comes a gentle river.
Now here is something interesting. Jerusalem, the city of God, did not have a great river flowing through it, like many famous cities. It had a spring, and some water channels, but no great river. So what is this river? Many think the psalm means God himself. God is the river that makes his city glad, the source of life and joy for his people. And at the very end of the Bible, in the book of Revelation, John sees a pure river of water of life, flowing from the throne of God, through the heavenly city.
'God is in the midst of her; she shall not be moved: God shall help her, and that right early.' In the midst means right in the middle. God lives among his people. So the city will not be moved, even when the earth moves.
'The heathen raged, the kingdoms were moved: he uttered his voice, the earth melted.' The nations rage, as we heard in Psalm two. Kingdoms rise and fall. But God only has to speak, and the earth melts.
And then the chorus.
Then the third verse of the song invites us to look. 'Come, behold the works of the LORD, what desolations he hath made in the earth.' Desolations means ruins. Come and see what God has done to the armies of those who fought against his people.
'He maketh wars to cease unto the end of the earth; he breaketh the bow, and cutteth the spear in sunder; he burneth the chariot in the fire.' In sunder means in two. Bows, spears and chariots were the weapons of war. God breaks them all. One day, he will bring all wars to an end, everywhere on earth. Isaiah spoke of a day when they shall beat their swords into plowshares.
And then, suddenly, God himself speaks.
And the song ends with the chorus once more. 'The LORD of hosts is with us; the God of Jacob is our refuge. Selah.'
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 1. 'God is our refuge and strength, a very present help in trouble.' A refuge is a shelter. Strength is the power to keep going. And a very present help means a help who is always right here, close at hand, not far away. When trouble comes, we do not have to wait for God to arrive, or search for him. He is already present. He is a very present help.
Verse 7. 'The LORD of hosts is with us; the God of Jacob is our refuge. Selah.' This is the chorus of the psalm, sung twice. The LORD of hosts means the LORD of armies, the commander of all the armies of heaven. That is how powerful he is. But he is also the God of Jacob. Jacob was no hero. He was a trickster, who made many mistakes. Yet God was faithful to him. The mighty LORD of hosts is the God of ordinary, weak, imperfect people like Jacob. And like us.
Verse 10. 'Be still, and know that I am God: I will be exalted among the heathen, I will be exalted in the earth.' Be still means stop. Stop fighting. Stop rushing. Stop worrying. In the middle of all the noise, the raging nations, the roaring seas, God says, be still. Stop, and remember who I am. I am God. And one day every nation will honour me. Sometimes the best thing we can do in trouble is to be still, and trust him.
Isaiah wrote, 'they shall beat their swords into plowshares, and their spears into pruninghooks'. And John saw 'a pure river of water of life, clear as crystal, proceeding out of the throne of God and of the Lamb.'
Find a few minutes today to be still. No phone, no noise. Just sit quietly, and say to yourself, be still, and know that I am God. Let God’s greatness calm your worries.
Here is today’s question. The psalm says that even if the mountains fall into the sea, we will not fear. What is something that shakes you, and how could remembering that God is with you help?
Let us pray. Lord God, you are our refuge and strength, a very present help in trouble. When my world is shaken, help me not to fear, because you are not shaken. Thank you that the LORD of hosts is with us, and the God of Jacob is our refuge. Help me to be still, and know that you are God. Amen.
