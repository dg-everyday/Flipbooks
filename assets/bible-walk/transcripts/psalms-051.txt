A beautiful white shirt has a big, dark stain right on the front. You scrub it, and scrub it. You try soap, and hot water, and everything you can think of. But the stain will not come out. In fact, the more you scrub, the worse it seems to get. Some stains we cannot remove by ourselves. Psalm fifty one is a prayer about a stain on the heart that only God can wash away.
In Psalm thirty two, David sang, blessed is he whose transgression is forgiven, whose sin is covered. When he kept silence about his sin, he felt dried up and heavy. But when he said, I will confess my transgressions unto the LORD, God forgave him. And God promised, I will instruct thee and teach thee in the way which thou shalt go.
Now we come to Psalm fifty one, the most famous prayer of repentance in the Bible. Listen for the pictures David uses for being made clean. And listen for what God really wants from us.
Psalm fifty one has a title that tells us exactly when it was written. It was written by David, after the prophet Nathan came to him.
Do you remember the story from the second book of Samuel? David was king. He had everything. But one day, he saw a woman named Bathsheba, the wife of one of his soldiers, Uriah. And David took her for himself, though she was another man’s wife. Then, to hide what he had done, he arranged for Uriah to be killed in battle. It was a terrible sin, and for a long time David said nothing.
Then God sent the prophet Nathan to David. Nathan told him a story about a rich man who stole a poor man’s only little lamb. David was furious. That man deserves to die! he said. And Nathan said, thou art the man. Suddenly, David saw what he had done. And he said, I have sinned against the LORD.
Psalm fifty one is the prayer David prayed from his heart.
It begins, not with excuses, but with a cry for mercy. 'Have mercy upon me, O God, according to thy lovingkindness: according unto the multitude of thy tender mercies blot out my transgressions.' David does not claim to deserve forgiveness. He asks for mercy because of who God is, full of lovingkindness, full of tender mercies. Blot out means wipe away, like wiping writing off a slate.
'Wash me throughly from mine iniquity, and cleanse me from my sin.' Throughly means thoroughly, completely. Wash me all the way through.
'For I acknowledge my transgressions: and my sin is ever before me.' David cannot stop thinking about what he has done. It is always in front of his eyes.
'Against thee, thee only, have I sinned, and done this evil in thy sight'. Of course, David had sinned terribly against Bathsheba, and against Uriah. But he realises that all sin is, first of all, against God. God made us, and his law is what we break. And God has every right to judge.
David knows the problem goes deep. It is not just what he did. It is what he is, deep inside. 'Behold, thou desirest truth in the inward parts: and in the hidden part thou shalt make me to know wisdom.' God wants us to be true and honest, deep down inside, not just on the outside.
Then David asks God to clean him.
'Make me to hear joy and gladness; that the bones which thou hast broken may rejoice.' David feels as if his bones are broken, he is so crushed by his guilt.
And then David asks for something even bigger than forgiveness.
'Cast me not away from thy presence; and take not thy holy spirit from me.' David remembered King Saul. When Saul disobeyed God, the Spirit of the LORD departed from him. David pleads, please do not let that happen to me. 'Restore unto me the joy of thy salvation; and uphold me with thy free spirit.' David had lost his joy. He asks God to give it back.
And then David looks forward. 'Then will I teach transgressors thy ways; and sinners shall be converted unto thee.' Once forgiven, David wants to help other sinners come back to God. Who better to teach about God’s mercy than someone who has received it?
'O Lord, open thou my lips; and my mouth shall shew forth thy praise.' His guilt has kept his mouth shut. He asks God to open it, so he can praise again.
And then David says something surprising. 'For thou desirest not sacrifice; else would I give it: thou delightest not in burnt offering.' David could have offered a thousand animals as sacrifices. But he knew that was not what God wanted most. God does not want religious actions on the outside, without a changed heart inside.
So what does God want?
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 7. 'Purge me with hyssop, and I shall be clean: wash me, and I shall be whiter than snow.' Hyssop was a small, bushy plant. At the first Passover in Egypt, the Israelites used a bunch of hyssop to put the lamb’s blood on their doorposts. And the priests used it to sprinkle water or blood when someone was made clean. Purge means make clean. David asks God to clean him in a way that only God can. And the result? Whiter than snow. No stain left at all.
Verse 10. 'Create in me a clean heart, O God; and renew a right spirit within me.' Create is the same word used in the very first verse of the Bible. In the beginning God created the heaven and the earth. Only God can create. David knows that he cannot fix his own heart. He needs God to make a brand new one. And a right spirit means a steady, faithful spirit, one that will not wobble. This is a prayer we can all pray, every day.
Verse 17. 'The sacrifices of God are a broken spirit: a broken and a contrite heart, O God, thou wilt not despise.' Contrite means truly sorry, crushed by sorrow for sin. God does not want us to pretend we are fine, or try to pay for our sins with good deeds. He wants us to come humbly, truly sorry. And when we do, he will never turn us away. Despise means look down on, reject. God will never reject a heart that is truly sorry.
Isaiah heard God say, 'though your sins be as scarlet, they shall be as white as snow'. And Paul wrote, 'Therefore if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature: old things are passed away; behold, all things are become new.'
If you have done something wrong, do not make excuses. Come to God as David did, with an honest, sorry heart. Then pray, create in me a clean heart, O God. And thank him that he is able to wash you whiter than snow.
Here is today’s question. David asks God not just to forgive him, but to create in him a clean heart. Why do you think we need a new heart, and not just forgiveness for what we have done?
Let us pray. Have mercy upon me, O God, according to your lovingkindness. Wash me thoroughly from my sin, and I shall be whiter than snow. Create in me a clean heart, O God, and renew a right spirit within me. Restore to me the joy of your salvation. Thank you that you will not despise a broken and a contrite heart. Open my lips, and my mouth shall show forth your praise. Amen.
