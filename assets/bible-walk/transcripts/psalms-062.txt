Imagine a big set of scales, the old kind, with a pan on each side. On one side, someone piles up all the most important people in the world: kings and presidents, the richest and most famous. On the other side, a single breath of air. And the scales do not move. Psalm sixty two says that, compared with God, even the greatest people weigh almost nothing. So why trust in them, when we can trust in him?
Before this, in Psalm twenty seven, David said, the LORD is my light and my salvation; whom shall I fear? His one great desire was to dwell in the house of the LORD, and behold his beauty. Even if his father and mother forsook him, the LORD would take him up. And he said, wait on the LORD: be of good courage.
Now we jump ahead to Psalm sixty two, the twelfth of our thirty favourite psalms, which are grouped by theme, not in number order. It is the last of our psalms about trusting God. David is under attack again. Listen for one small word that he keeps using, the word only. And listen to David talking to his own soul.
Psalm sixty two is a psalm of David. The title says it was given to Jeduthun, one of the chief musicians David appointed to lead worship.
The psalm begins quietly.
The words truly and only in this psalm come from the same little Hebrew word, and David uses it again and again. God only. God alone. He only is my rock. Not God and my money. Not God and my clever plans. God alone.
'He only is my rock and my salvation; he is my defence; I shall not be greatly moved.' A rock is solid, unshakeable. A defence is a high, strong tower where you can be safe. Because God is his rock, David says, I shall not be greatly moved. I may be shaken a little, but not knocked over.
Then David turns to his enemies, and asks them, how long will you keep attacking one man? He says they are like a leaning wall, and a tottering fence, about to fall over. They are pushing at David, trying to knock him down from his high position. 'they delight in lies: they bless with their mouth, but they curse inwardly. Selah.' They say nice things to his face, but inside they are full of hatred. Two faced.
Then David does something interesting. He speaks to himself. 'My soul, wait thou only upon God; for my expectation is from him.' In the first verse, David said, my soul waiteth upon God. Now he tells his soul, wait only upon God. Perhaps his soul was starting to worry again. Sometimes we need to preach to ourselves, and remind our own hearts to trust God. Expectation means hope, what we are looking forward to. My hope comes from him.
Then David repeats the words of verse two, but with one small change.
'In God is my salvation and my glory: the rock of my strength, and my refuge, is in God.'
Then David turns from himself to everyone listening, and gives them an invitation.
Then David brings out the scales. 'Surely men of low degree are vanity, and men of high degree are a lie: to be laid in the balance, they are altogether lighter than vanity.' Men of low degree means ordinary people. Men of high degree means important, powerful people. Vanity means a breath, a puff of air. Put them all on the scales, and they weigh less than a breath. Ordinary or important, no human being is strong enough to be our rock. Only God is.
'Trust not in oppression, and become not vain in robbery: if riches increase, set not your heart upon them.' Do not trust in power gained by hurting others. Do not put your hope in stealing. And even if you become rich in honest ways, do not set your heart on your riches. Money can disappear. It cannot be our rock.
And the psalm ends with something David has heard from God. 'God hath spoken once; twice have I heard this; that power belongeth unto God.' David heard it so clearly, it was as if God said it twice. Power belongs to God.
'Also unto thee, O Lord, belongeth mercy: for thou renderest to every man according to his work.' And mercy belongs to God too. God is all powerful, and he is full of mercy. That is a wonderful combination. If God were powerful but not merciful, he would be frightening. If he were merciful but not powerful, he could not help us. But he is both.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 1. 'Truly my soul waiteth upon God: from him cometh my salvation.' The word for waiteth here carries the idea of silence, of quietness. David’s soul is quiet before God, not panicking, not complaining, but resting in trust. Like a child who has stopped crying because their parent is holding them. When we are worried, we can still our souls before God, and wait for him to help.
Verse 6. 'He only is my rock and my salvation: he is my defence; I shall not be moved.' Did you notice the change? In verse two, David said, I shall not be greatly moved. Now he says, I shall not be moved. Not even a little. As David waited on God and talked to his soul, his trust grew stronger. That is what happens when we keep looking to God. Our faith grows.
Verse 8. 'Trust in him at all times; ye people, pour out your heart before him: God is a refuge for us. Selah.' At all times, in good times and bad times. And pour out your heart before him. Just as you would pour water out of a jug, until it is empty, tell God everything that is in your heart. Your worries, your fears, your sadness, your hopes. He will not be shocked or angry. He is a refuge for us. A safe place.
Paul wrote, 'Be careful for nothing; but in every thing by prayer and supplication with thanksgiving let your requests be made known unto God.' And Jesus said, 'Lay not up for yourselves treasures upon earth, where moth and rust doth corrupt'.
Pour out your heart to God today. Find a quiet place, and tell him everything, holding nothing back. And if you feel worried, speak to your own soul, as David did: my soul, wait thou only upon God.
Here is today’s question. David says that, compared with God, even the most powerful people weigh less than a breath. What are some things people trust in instead of God, and why are they not strong enough to be our rock?
Let us pray. Lord God, my soul waits upon you alone. You only are my rock and my salvation, my defence. I shall not be moved. Help me to trust you at all times, and to pour out my heart before you. Keep me from setting my heart on riches or on people. Thank you that power belongs to you, and mercy belongs to you too. Amen.
