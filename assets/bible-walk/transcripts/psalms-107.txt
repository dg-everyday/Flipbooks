A small fishing boat is caught in a terrible storm. The waves are as big as houses. One moment, the boat is lifted high into the air. The next, it plunges down into a deep trough between the waves. The sailors are soaked and terrified. They have tried everything. Nothing works. And then, at their wits’ end, they cry out to God. And the wind dies down. The waves grow still. And the boat glides safely into the harbour.
In Psalm one hundred and three, David told his soul, bless the LORD, O my soul, and forget not all his benefits. God forgives all our iniquities, and crowns us with lovingkindness. As far as the east is from the west, so far hath he removed our transgressions from us. And like as a father pitieth his children, so the LORD pitieth them that fear him.
Now, in Psalm one hundred and seven, we hear four stories of people in trouble. Listen for the pattern that repeats in each story. And listen for the chorus.
Psalm one hundred and seven begins with a call to give thanks. 'O give thanks unto the LORD, for he is good: for his mercy endureth for ever.'
Then comes a challenge.
The redeemed are people God has rescued. And he has gathered them 'out of the lands, from the east, and from the west, from the north, and from the south.' From every direction, God brings his people home.
Then the psalm tells four short stories, four pictures of people in trouble. And each story follows the same pattern. First, there is trouble. Second, they cry out to the LORD. Third, God rescues them. And fourth, a chorus of thanks.
The first story is about wanderers. 'They wandered in the wilderness in a solitary way; they found no city to dwell in. Hungry and thirsty, their soul fainted in them.' Lost in the desert, alone, with no food or water.
'Then they cried unto the LORD in their trouble, and he delivered them out of their distresses. And he led them forth by the right way, that they might go to a city of habitation.' God showed them the right way, and led them to a city where they could live.
And then the chorus. 'Oh that men would praise the LORD for his goodness, and for his wonderful works to the children of men! For he satisfieth the longing soul, and filleth the hungry soul with goodness.'
The second story is about prisoners. 'Such as sit in darkness and in the shadow of death, being bound in affliction and iron'. Chained in a dark dungeon. And why? 'Because they rebelled against the words of God'. Their own sin had brought them there.
'Then they cried unto the LORD in their trouble, and he saved them out of their distresses. He brought them out of darkness and the shadow of death, and brake their bands in sunder.' God broke their chains. Even though it was their own fault, God rescued them. And again, the chorus. 'For he hath broken the gates of brass, and cut the bars of iron in sunder.'
The third story is about people who are sick. 'Fools because of their transgression, and because of their iniquities, are afflicted.' Their foolish, sinful choices made them ill. 'Their soul abhorreth all manner of meat; and they draw near unto the gates of death.' They cannot even eat. They are close to death.
'Then they cry unto the LORD in their trouble, and he saveth them out of their distresses.'
And again, the chorus.
The fourth story is about sailors. 'They that go down to the sea in ships, that do business in great waters; These see the works of the LORD, and his wonders in the deep.' Then a storm blows up. 'They mount up to the heaven, they go down again to the depths: their soul is melted because of trouble.' The sailors stagger about on the tossing deck, and are at their wit’s end. They do not know what to do.
'Then they cry unto the LORD in their trouble, and he bringeth them out of their distresses.'
And then.
'Then are they glad because they be quiet; so he bringeth them unto their desired haven.' A haven is a safe harbour.
Do you remember the story in the Gospels, when Jesus and his disciples were in a boat on the Sea of Galilee, and a great storm arose? The disciples were terrified, and woke Jesus, who was asleep. And Jesus rebuked the wind, and said to the sea, Peace, be still. And there was a great calm. Only the LORD can still the storm. And Jesus did.
The last part of the psalm shows how God can change things completely. He can turn rivers into desert. But he can also turn the desert into pools of water, and dry ground into springs, where hungry people can build homes and plant fields. He lifts up the poor, and brings down the proud.
And the psalm ends, 'Whoso is wise, and will observe these things, even they shall understand the lovingkindness of the LORD.' If you are wise, you will look at these stories, and understand how kind and faithful God is.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 2. 'Let the redeemed of the LORD say so, whom he hath redeemed from the hand of the enemy'. Say so! If God has rescued you, do not keep quiet about it. Tell others. Tell your family, tell your friends. Each of us has our own story of how God has helped us. And when we tell it, it brings glory to God, and gives hope to others.
Verse 20. 'He sent his word, and healed them, and delivered them from their destructions.' God healed the sick by sending his word. His word has power. When Jesus walked on earth, he healed people with a word. A Roman centurion once said to him, speak the word only, and my servant shall be healed. And he was. John’s Gospel calls Jesus himself the Word. God sent his Word, Jesus, to heal us and save us.
Verse 29. 'He maketh the storm a calm, so that the waves thereof are still.' Some storms are real storms, at sea. Others are storms in our lives: fear, trouble, sickness, worry. God has power over every kind of storm. Sometimes he calms the storm around us. Sometimes he calms the storm inside us. Either way, when we cry to him, he can bring peace.
Jesus 'arose, and rebuked the wind, and said unto the sea, Peace, be still. And the wind ceased, and there was a great calm.' And the centurion said, 'but speak the word only, and my servant shall be healed.'
Let the redeemed of the LORD say so. Think of one time when God helped you or answered your prayer, and tell someone about it today. And if you are in a storm right now, cry out to the LORD in your trouble.
Here is today’s question. In each of the four stories, the people cry out to the LORD in their trouble, and he rescues them. Why do you think we often wait until we are at our wits’ end before we cry out to God?
Let us pray. O give thanks unto the LORD, for he is good: for his mercy endureth for ever. Thank you, Lord, that when your people cry to you in their trouble, you deliver them. Thank you that you break chains, heal the sick, and make the storm a calm. Help me to cry to you in every trouble. And help me to tell others what you have done for me. Amen.
