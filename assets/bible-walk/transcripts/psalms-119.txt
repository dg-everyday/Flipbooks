Imagine walking along a narrow mountain path in pitch darkness. You cannot see the rocks under your feet. You cannot see where the path turns, or where it drops away. Then someone hands you a small lamp. It does not light up the whole mountain. But it shows you the next step, and the next, and the next. And that is all you need to get home safely. Psalm one hundred and nineteen says God’s Word is like that lamp.
In Psalm nineteen, we heard that the heavens declare the glory of God. Day after day, the sky pours out its message, in every language. And God’s Word is perfect, converting the soul, more to be desired than gold, and sweeter than honey. And David prayed, let the words of my mouth, and the meditation of my heart, be acceptable in thy sight.
Now we come to Psalm one hundred and nineteen, the longest chapter in the whole Bible. It has one hundred and seventy six verses, and almost every one is about God’s Word. Listen for the many different names it uses for God’s Word. And listen for how honest the writer is about his troubles.
Psalm one hundred and nineteen is built in a very special way. The Hebrew alphabet has twenty two letters. And the psalm has twenty two sections, one for each letter, in order. Each section has eight verses. And in the Hebrew, all eight verses in a section begin with the same letter. The first eight verses all begin with the first letter, Aleph. The next eight with the second letter, Beth. And so on, right through to the last letter, Tau. In your Bible, you may see the names of the letters printed above each section.
It is like an alphabet poem about God’s Word. As if the writer is saying, I want to praise God’s Word from A to Z.
And he uses many different names for it. The law. The testimonies. The precepts. The statutes. The commandments. The judgments. The word. Each one shows a different side of what God has said. His teaching, his witness, his instructions, his decisions, his promises.
We cannot walk through all one hundred and seventy six verses on this walk. So let us visit some of the sections, and listen to what the writer says.
It begins, like Psalm one, with blessing. 'Blessed are the undefiled in the way, who walk in the law of the LORD. Blessed are they that keep his testimonies, and that seek him with the whole heart.' Undefiled means clean, without blame.
In the second section, the writer asks a question that every young person faces. 'Wherewithal shall a young man cleanse his way? by taking heed thereto according to thy word.' How can a young person keep their life clean and pure? By paying attention to God’s Word, and living by it.
In the third section, he prays, 'Open thou mine eyes, that I may behold wondrous things out of thy law.' That is a wonderful prayer to pray before we read the Bible. Lord, open my eyes, so I can see the treasures hidden in your Word.
In the fourth section, the writer is feeling very low. 'My soul cleaveth unto the dust: quicken thou me according to thy word.' Cleaveth unto the dust means he feels flat on the ground, worn out. Quicken means give life. Lord, bring me back to life, through your Word.
And in the fifth section he says, 'I will run the way of thy commandments, when thou shalt enlarge my heart.' When God gives us a bigger heart, a heart that loves him, then obeying him is not a heavy chore. We run.
The writer is honest about his troubles. Proud people mock him, lie about him, and try to trap him. Princes speak against him. And he says something surprising. 'It is good for me that I have been afflicted; that I might learn thy statutes.' Afflicted means going through suffering. Hard times taught him to depend on God’s Word in a way he never had before.
In another section, he looks up, and says, 'For ever, O LORD, thy word is settled in heaven.' Settled means fixed, firm, standing fast. Everything on earth changes. But God’s Word never changes.
'O how love I thy law! it is my meditation all the day.' And, 'How sweet are thy words unto my taste! yea, sweeter than honey to my mouth!' Just as David said in Psalm nineteen.
Then comes the most famous verse in the whole psalm.
A little later, 'Thou art my hiding place and my shield: I hope in thy word.' And near the end, 'Great peace have they which love thy law: and nothing shall offend them.' Offend here means make them stumble.
And the very last verse of this enormous psalm is a surprise.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 11. 'Thy word have I hid in mine heart, that I might not sin against thee.' To hide something in your heart means to treasure it, store it up, learn it by heart. When we memorise God’s Word, it stays with us wherever we go. And when we are tempted to do wrong, God’s Word comes back to our mind, to warn us and help us. When Jesus was tempted in the wilderness, he answered every temptation with words from Scripture. They were hidden in his heart.
Verse 105. 'Thy word is a lamp unto my feet, and a light unto my path.' A lamp in those days was a small clay dish of oil, with a flickering flame. It did not light up the whole countryside. It lit up just enough of the path to see where to put your feet. God’s Word may not show us everything about our future. But it shows us the next step, how to live and what to do. And as we take each step, the light goes with us.
Verse 176. 'I have gone astray like a lost sheep; seek thy servant; for I do not forget thy commandments.' After one hundred and seventy five verses about loving God’s Word, the writer does not end by saying how good he is. He ends by admitting that he has wandered, like a lost sheep. And he asks God to come and find him. Even people who love God’s Word still need God to seek them. And Jesus said he came to seek and to save that which was lost.
Jesus answered the tempter, 'It is written, Man shall not live by bread alone, but by every word that proceedeth out of the mouth of God.' And he said, 'For the Son of man is come to seek and to save that which was lost.'
Choose one short verse today, perhaps one from this psalm, and hide it in your heart. Say it over and over until you know it by heart. And before you next read your Bible, pray, open thou mine eyes, that I may behold wondrous things out of thy law.
Here is today’s question. The writer says God’s Word is a lamp unto his feet. Can you think of a time when something from the Bible helped you to know what to do next?
Let us pray. Lord God, thank you for your Word. Open my eyes, that I may see wondrous things in it. Help me to hide it in my heart, so that I might not sin against you. Let it be a lamp unto my feet, and a light unto my path. And when I go astray like a lost sheep, come and seek me, and bring me home. Amen.
