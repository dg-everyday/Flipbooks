Two men decide to build a house. The first man works hard, but he forgets to lay a proper foundation. He builds his walls on the sand, because it is quicker and easier. The second man digs deep, all the way down to the rock, and builds there. Then the storm comes. The rain pours down, the floods rise, and the wind beats against both houses. One falls with a great crash. The other stands firm. Jesus told that story. And long before him, Psalm one hundred and twenty seven had something to say about building.
Before this, in Psalm eighty four, the sons of Korah sang, how amiable are thy tabernacles, O LORD of hosts! Even the sparrow had found a nest near God’s altar. A day in thy courts is better than a thousand.
Now, still with God’s house, we jump ahead to Psalm one hundred and twenty seven, the twenty sixth of our thirty favourite psalms, which are grouped by theme, not in number order. It is a short song about building, guarding, working and family. Listen for the little word vain. And listen for the gift God gives to those he loves.
Psalm one hundred and twenty seven is another song of degrees, sung by pilgrims on their way up to Jerusalem. And its title says it is for Solomon, or of Solomon. Many think Solomon wrote it.
Solomon was David’s son, and the wisest king Israel ever had. He was also a great builder. He built the temple in Jerusalem, the magnificent house of God. It took seven years. He built palaces, and city walls, and whole cities. If anyone knew about building, it was Solomon.
And yet this psalm says that all our building is useless without God.
The psalm uses the word vain three times in its first two verses. Vain means empty, pointless, for nothing. Without God, all our hard work is in vain.
What kind of house does the psalm mean? It could mean a real house, made of stone and wood. It could mean God’s house, the temple. But in the Bible, the word house can also mean a family. When God promised David that he would build him a house, he meant a family, a line of kings that would go on for ever. So the psalm is saying something about every kind of building: buildings, families, and whole lives. Unless God is the builder, it will not last.
And the same is true of guarding a city. In those days, cities had high walls, and watchmen who stood on them, looking out for enemies. Do you remember Nehemiah? When he rebuilt the walls of Jerusalem, he set watchmen to guard the city. But Nehemiah also prayed. He knew that the watchmen could only do so much. It was God who truly kept the city safe.
Then the psalm talks about work.
It is vain to rise up early, to sit up late, to eat the bread of sorrows. The bread of sorrows means food eaten with worry and stress. The psalm is not saying that hard work is wrong. The Bible praises hard work. It is saying that it is pointless to work and worry all the time, as if everything depends on us. Some people get up before everyone else, and work until they drop, anxious and stressed, as if they could control everything by working harder. But God gives rest to those he loves.
Here is something interesting about Solomon. When he was born, God sent a message through the prophet Nathan, and gave him a special name, Jedidiah. It means beloved of the LORD. So when the psalm says, he giveth his beloved sleep, it may be a little play on Solomon’s own name.
Then the psalm turns to families. 'Lo, children are an heritage of the LORD'. A heritage is an inheritance, a gift passed down. Children are not just ours. They are a gift from God. And in a world where many people thought that strong walls, big armies and lots of money were the most important things, the psalm says, children are the real treasure.
And then a picture of arrows.
'Happy is the man that hath his quiver full of them: they shall not be ashamed, but they shall speak with the enemies in the gate.' A quiver is the bag an archer carries his arrows in. In those days, the gate of the city was where the elders sat to judge disputes, like a courtroom. A man with grown up children standing beside him would not be pushed around by his enemies there. His family would stand with him.
So this little psalm covers almost all of life. Our homes, our safety, our work, our rest, our families. And in every one, the message is the same. Do not try to do it all without God. Let him be the builder.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 1. 'Except the LORD build the house, they labour in vain that build it: except the LORD keep the city, the watchman waketh but in vain.' Except means unless. Unless God is involved, all our hard work is for nothing. We can make plans, and work hard, and do our best. And we should. But only what God builds will last. Whatever you are building, a friendship, a family, your schoolwork, your future, invite God to be the builder.
Verse 2. 'It is vain for you to rise up early, to sit up late, to eat the bread of sorrows: for so he giveth his beloved sleep.' God does not want us to be anxious and exhausted, trying to hold the whole world together by ourselves. He wants us to work, and then to trust him, and rest. Sleep is a gift. Every time we lie down to sleep, we are saying, Lord, I cannot keep everything going. But you can. And you never sleep.
Verse 4. 'As arrows are in the hand of a mighty man; so are children of the youth.' An arrow is not made to stay in the quiver. It is made to be aimed carefully, and sent out to hit the target. Parents shape and guide their children, like an archer straightening and aiming an arrow. And then, one day, the children are sent out into the world, to make a difference. If you are young, you are like an arrow in God’s hand. He has a target for your life.
Jesus said, 'Whosoever heareth these sayings of mine, and doeth them, I will liken him unto a wise man, which built his house upon a rock'. And Paul wrote, 'For other foundation can no man lay than that is laid, which is Jesus Christ.'
Think about something you are working hard on right now. Have you invited God into it? Pray about it today, and ask him to build it. And when you lie down to sleep, thank God that you can rest, because he never sleeps.
Here is today’s question. The psalm says, except the LORD build the house, they labour in vain that build it. What do you think it looks like to let God be the builder of your life?
Let us pray. Lord God, except you build the house, we labour in vain. Be the builder of my life, my family and my future. Keep me from working and worrying as if everything depends on me. Thank you that you give your beloved sleep. Thank you for children, a gift from you. Aim my life like an arrow, and send me where you want me to go. Amen.
