Two brothers have not spoken to each other for years. They had a terrible argument, and neither would say sorry. But one day, at a family gathering, one brother walks across the room, holds out his hand, and says, I have missed you. The other brother hesitates. Then he grabs his hand, and pulls him into a hug. Around them, the whole family smiles. Some of them are crying. Something broken has been mended. Psalm one hundred and thirty three is about that kind of joy.
In Psalm one hundred and twenty seven, a song for Solomon, we heard, except the LORD build the house, they labour in vain that build it. It is vain to work and worry as if everything depends on us, for he giveth his beloved sleep. And children are an heritage of the LORD, like arrows in the hand of a mighty man.
Now we come to Psalm one hundred and thirty three, one of the shortest psalms in the Bible, only three verses long. Listen for the two pictures it uses. And listen for where God commands his blessing.
Psalm one hundred and thirty three is another song of degrees, sung by pilgrims going up to Jerusalem. And it is a psalm of David.
Imagine the scene. For the great feasts, God’s people came to Jerusalem from every part of the land. From the north and the south, from the hills and the valleys, from every one of the twelve tribes of Israel. People who lived far apart, who had different ways and different accents, and who sometimes did not get on, all walked up to Jerusalem together, and camped side by side. And as they gathered, they sang this song.
David knew how hard it was for God’s people to be united. When he first became king, only his own tribe of Judah followed him. For years, there was war between his followers and the followers of Saul’s family. Only after a long struggle did all the tribes come together, and make David king over all Israel. David knew how precious unity was.
And the Bible is full of brothers who did not get on. Cain and Abel. Jacob and Esau. Joseph and his brothers, who sold him as a slave. Families, and God’s people, have often quarrelled. So when they live together in peace, it is something to celebrate.
The psalm begins with a joyful exclamation.
Then come two pictures, to show how good unity is.
The first picture is of oil running down Aaron’s head. Do you remember Aaron, the brother of Moses? God chose him to be the first high priest of Israel. In the book of Leviticus, when Aaron was made high priest, Moses poured the holy anointing oil on his head. It was a special oil, made with sweet smelling spices, and it was so precious that no one was allowed to make it for their own use. And the psalm pictures it running down over his beard, all the way down to the collar of his robes.
The second picture is of dew. Mount Hermon is the highest mountain in the land, far in the north, often covered with snow. Its slopes are soaked with heavy dew, which keeps everything green and fresh, even in the hot, dry summer. Mount Zion, where Jerusalem stood, was much drier. The psalm pictures the rich dew of Hermon falling on the dry mountains of Zion.
And the psalm ends with God’s blessing.
Three verses. That is all. But they say something very big. When God’s people live together in love and peace, it is beautiful, like holy oil. It is refreshing, like dew. And it is the place where God gives his blessing.
Many years later, just before he died, Jesus prayed for everyone who would believe in him. And he prayed that they all may be one. He wanted his followers to be united, so that the world would believe that God had sent him. And after Jesus rose again and went back to heaven, the first believers in Jerusalem were of one heart and of one soul. People from different backgrounds, rich and poor, shared what they had, and cared for one another.
The apostle Paul says that all believers are like one body, with many parts. Each part is different, but all are needed, and all belong together. Unity does not mean everyone being the same. It means different people, loving one another, and living together in peace, because they all belong to the same Lord.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 1. 'Behold, how good and how pleasant it is for brethren to dwell together in unity!' Behold means look! Notice this! Good means right, the way things should be. Pleasant means lovely, delightful. Brethren means brothers and sisters. It can mean a family, or all of God’s people. Unity means being one, living in harmony. When brothers and sisters get along, at home or in God’s family, it is not just nice. It is good, and it is delightful to God.
Verse 2. 'It is like the precious ointment upon the head, that ran down upon the beard, even Aaron’s beard: that went down to the skirts of his garments'. The oil was poured on Aaron’s head, but it did not stay there. It ran down, over his whole body, to the very edge of his robes. Unity is like that. It starts with one person, then flows out and touches everyone around. And just as the oil set Aaron apart as holy for God, unity among God’s people is holy. It is something that belongs to God.
Verse 3. 'As the dew of Hermon, and as the dew that descended upon the mountains of Zion: for there the LORD commanded the blessing, even life for evermore.' Dew comes quietly, without anyone noticing, and brings life to the dry ground. Unity does the same. It refreshes people, and makes them grow. And notice the word there. Where God’s people live in unity, there God commands his blessing. Not just a little blessing. Life for evermore.
Jesus prayed, 'That they all may be one; as thou, Father, art in me, and I in thee'. And Luke wrote, 'And the multitude of them that believed were of one heart and of one soul'.
Is there someone you have fallen out with, at home, at school, or among your friends? Ask God to help you make peace. Perhaps you need to say sorry, or to forgive. Take the first step today. And thank God for the people he has given you to live alongside.
Here is today’s question. The psalm says that where God’s people live in unity, there the LORD commands the blessing. Why do you think unity matters so much to God?
Let us pray. Lord God, how good and how pleasant it is when your people live together in unity! Help me to be a peacemaker, in my family, among my friends, and in your family. Where there is quarrelling, help me to bring peace. Thank you that where your people are united, you command your blessing, even life for evermore. Make us one, as Jesus prayed. Amen.
