A woman stands at the edge of a road with her two daughters in law. Ten years ago, she left Bethlehem with a husband and two sons. Now all three are buried in a foreign land. She is going home alone, she thinks. Go back, she tells the young women. I have nothing left to give you. One turns back. But the other says words that people still say at weddings, thousands of years later.
In the introduction, we heard that Ruth is a short book of four chapters, set in the days when the judges ruled. We heard that it is a story of faithful, loyal love, and of a redeemer, and that it moves from empty to full.
Now, in chapter one, we begin with the empty part. It is a chapter of famine, grief and a hard choice. But listen for the small signs that God has not forgotten this family. And listen for the moment when a young woman from Moab chooses the God of Israel.
In the days when the judges ruled, there is a famine in the land. Bethlehem means house of bread, but there is no bread in Bethlehem. So a man named Elimelech takes his wife Naomi and their two sons, Mahlon and Chilion, and goes to live in the country of Moab.
There, Elimelech dies, and Naomi is left with her two sons. The sons marry women of Moab. One is named Orpah, and the other Ruth. They live there about ten years. And then both sons die too. 'and the woman was left of her two sons and her husband.'
Naomi is now a widow in a foreign land, with no husband and no sons. In that world, a woman alone like this had no one to provide for her, and no one to protect her.
Then Naomi hears news from home. The LORD has visited his people in giving them bread. The famine is over. So Naomi sets out to return to the land of Judah, and her two daughters in law go with her.
On the way, Naomi stops. She knows what lies ahead for these young women if they come with her: a strange land, poverty, and probably no new husbands.
Then she kisses them, and they all weep aloud. The young women say, surely we will return with thee unto thy people.
But Naomi says, turn again, my daughters. Why will you go with me? I am too old to have a husband. And even if I had sons again, would you wait for them to grow up? No, my daughters. It grieves me very much for your sakes, 'that the hand of the LORD is gone out against me.'
They weep again. Then Orpah kisses her mother in law goodbye, and goes back to her people. 'but Ruth clave unto her.' She holds on, and will not let go.
Naomi says, look, your sister in law has gone back to her people, and to her gods. Go back with her.
And Ruth answers with a promise.
She goes on. 'Where thou diest, will I die, and there will I be buried: the LORD do so to me, and more also, if ought but death part thee and me.' Ruth uses the name of the LORD, the God of Israel. She is not just choosing Naomi. She is choosing Naomi’s God.
When Naomi sees that Ruth is 'stedfastly minded to go with her', she says no more. So the two of them walk on together until they come to Bethlehem.
When they arrive, the whole town is stirred. The women say, is this Naomi? Can this really be her? Naomi’s name means pleasant.
Then Naomi answers them.
So Naomi comes back, and Ruth the Moabitess with her. 'and they came to Bethlehem in the beginning of barley harvest.' They left because there was no bread. They come back just as the harvest is beginning.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 8. 'And Naomi said unto her two daughters in law, Go, return each to her mother’s house: the LORD deal kindly with you, as ye have dealt with the dead, and with me.' Here is the first mention of kindness in the book. Naomi is thanking these young women for their faithful love to her sons, and to her. Even in her deepest grief, Naomi prays a blessing on them. And she still believes that the LORD is kind. Listen for that word, kindly, all through the book. It is the thread that ties the story together.
Verse 16. 'And Ruth said, Intreat me not to leave thee, or to return from following after thee: for whither thou goest, I will go; and where thou lodgest, I will lodge: thy people shall be my people, and thy God my God:' Intreat means beg. Ruth had every reason to go home, where she might marry again and be safe. Instead, she chose a grieving widow, a foreign land and a hard future. And she chose Naomi’s God. This is more than loyalty to family. It is faith. Ruth gave up everything she knew, because she had come to trust the LORD.
Verse 21. 'I went out full, and the LORD hath brought me home again empty: why then call ye me Naomi, seeing the LORD hath testified against me, and the Almighty hath afflicted me?' Naomi tells them, call me not Naomi, call me Mara, which means bitter. She is completely honest about her pain. And the Bible does not hide her words, or scold her for them. God can hear our honest grief. But notice what Naomi cannot see yet. She says she has come home empty, and Ruth is standing right beside her. And the barley harvest is just beginning.
Moses told the people, 'And the LORD, he it is that doth go before thee; he will be with thee, he will not fail thee, neither forsake thee'. And a psalm says, 'He healeth the broken in heart, and bindeth up their wounds.'
Is there someone around you who is grieving, or who feels empty? You may not be able to fix their pain. But like Ruth, you can stay. You can walk beside them. Ask God today to show you one way to be faithful to someone who is hurting.
Here is today’s question. Ruth said to Naomi, thy people shall be my people, and thy God my God. What has it cost you, or what might it cost you, to choose to follow God?
Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that you hear my honest grief, even when I feel empty. Thank you for Ruth’s faithful love. Help me to be faithful to the people you have given me, and to choose you, whatever it costs. Let your people be my people, and you be my God. Amen.
