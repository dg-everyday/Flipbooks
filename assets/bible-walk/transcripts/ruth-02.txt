A young foreign woman walks out into the fields at harvest. She has no money and no land. She is going to walk behind the workers and pick up the stalks of grain they leave behind, so that she and her mother in law will not go hungry. She does not know whose field she is walking into. But God does.
In chapter one, Naomi lost her husband and both her sons in Moab, and set out for home. Ruth refused to leave her, saying, whither thou goest, I will go, and thy God my God. They came to Bethlehem with nothing, and Naomi said, call me Mara, for I came home empty. But they arrived at the beginning of barley harvest.
Now, in chapter two, Ruth goes to work. In this chapter we meet Boaz, one of the kindest men in the Bible. Listen for how often the word happened seems to fit, and ask yourself whether it really was chance.
Naomi has a relative on her husband’s side, a mighty man of wealth, of the family of Elimelech. His name is Boaz.
Ruth says to Naomi, let me now go to the field, and glean ears of corn after him in whose sight I shall find grace. To glean means to gather the stalks of grain that the harvesters drop or leave behind. The law of Moses told farmers in Israel not to gather every last stalk, but to leave some for the poor, the widow and the stranger. Ruth is all three. Naomi says, go, my daughter.
So Ruth goes out, and gleans in the field behind the reapers.
Just then, Boaz comes from Bethlehem, and greets his workers. 'The LORD be with you.' And they answer, 'The LORD bless thee.'
Boaz asks the servant in charge of the reapers, whose young woman is this? The servant says, it is the Moabite girl who came back with Naomi out of the country of Moab. She asked if she could glean among the sheaves. She has worked hard, and hardly stopped to rest.
Then Boaz speaks to Ruth. Listen, my daughter. Do not go to glean in another field. Stay here, close by my young women. Keep your eyes on the field they are reaping, and follow them. I have told the young men not to touch you. And when you are thirsty, go and drink from the water jars the young men have filled.
Ruth falls on her face, and bows to the ground. 'Why have I found grace in thine eyes, that thou shouldest take knowledge of me, seeing I am a stranger?'
Boaz answers, I have been told all that you have done for your mother in law since your husband died. How you left your father and your mother, and the land where you were born, and came to a people you did not know before.
And then he blesses her.
Ruth says, let me find favour in thy sight, my lord. You have comforted me, and spoken kindly to me, though I am not even one of your servants.
At mealtime, Boaz says to her, come here, and eat of the bread, and dip your piece in the sauce. She sits beside the reapers, and he passes her roasted grain. She eats until she is satisfied, and has some left over.
When she gets up to glean again, Boaz quietly gives orders to his young men. Let her glean even among the sheaves, and do not shame her. 'And let fall also some of the handfuls of purpose for her'. Leave them for her to pick up. Do not scold her. Boaz is generous, but he protects Ruth’s dignity.
So Ruth gleans in the field until the end of the day, and beats out the grain. It comes to about an ephah of barley, enough for many days.
She carries it into town, and shows Naomi what she has gathered. She also gives her the food left over from her meal. Naomi is astonished. Where did you glean today? Where did you work? Blessed be the man who took notice of you!
Ruth tells her, the man’s name with whom I worked today is Boaz.
And Naomi, who said she came home empty, suddenly sees a glimmer of hope.
Ruth says, he told me to stay close to his young men until they have finished all his harvest. Naomi says, it is good, my daughter. Stay with his young women, so that no one troubles you in another field. So Ruth keeps close to the young women of Boaz, gleaning to the end of the barley harvest and the wheat harvest. And she lives with her mother in law.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 3. 'And she went, and came, and gleaned in the field after the reapers: and her hap was to light on a part of the field belonging unto Boaz, who was of the kindred of Elimelech.' Her hap was to light on. In other words, it just so happened. Out of all the fields around Bethlehem, Ruth just happened to walk into the field of a relative of Naomi’s family. The writer is smiling as he says it. It looks like chance. But the whole book is showing us that God is at work in the ordinary choices of ordinary days, even when we cannot see him.
Verse 12. 'The LORD recompense thy work, and a full reward be given thee of the LORD God of Israel, under whose wings thou art come to trust.' Boaz paints a picture: a bird spreading her wings over her little ones, to shelter them. Ruth, the stranger from Moab, has come to hide under the wings of the God of Israel. And notice, Boaz prays that God will reward her. Soon, he himself will become the answer to his own prayer.
Verse 20. 'And Naomi said unto her daughter in law, Blessed be he of the LORD, who hath not left off his kindness to the living and to the dead. And Naomi said unto her, The man is near of kin unto us, one of our next kinsmen.' Naomi’s bitterness begins to lift. She praises the LORD, who has not stopped showing his kindness. And she tells Ruth that Boaz is one of their next kinsmen. That means he is one of the close relatives who has the right to redeem the family, to buy back their land and keep their name alive. Hope has come into the story.
The law of Moses said, 'When thou cuttest down thine harvest in thy field, and hast forgot a sheaf in the field, thou shalt not go again to fetch it: it shall be for the stranger, for the fatherless, and for the widow'. And a psalm says, 'He shall cover thee with his feathers, and under his wings shalt thou trust'.
Boaz noticed someone others might have overlooked, and he was kind in a way that protected her dignity. Who might God be asking you to notice today? Look for one person you could help quietly, without making them feel small.
Here is today’s question. It just so happened that Ruth came to the field of Boaz. Can you look back and see a time when something that seemed like chance was really God at work in your life?
Let us pray. Lord God of Israel, thank you that I can come to you and take shelter under your wings. Thank you that you are at work in my ordinary days, even when I cannot see it. Make me kind and generous, like Boaz, to those who are in need. Amen.
