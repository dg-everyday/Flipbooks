The harvest is almost over. Soon the fields will be bare, and there will be nothing left to glean. Naomi has been thinking. She has watched how Boaz treats Ruth. And she has a bold idea, one that could change everything for them both. But it means Ruth will have to be brave, and Boaz will have to choose.
In chapter two, Ruth went to glean in the fields, and it just so happened that she came to the field of Boaz, a relative of Naomi’s husband. Boaz was kind and generous to her, and blessed her as one who had come to trust under the wings of the God of Israel. And Naomi told Ruth that Boaz was one of their next kinsmen, one who could redeem the family.
Now, in chapter three, Ruth makes a request. Some of the customs in this chapter are strange to us. But listen for how everyone in this scene acts with honour and courage, and how Boaz answers the prayer he himself prayed.
Naomi says to Ruth, my daughter, shall I not seek rest for thee, that it may be well with thee? Rest here means a settled home, a husband and safety. All through the harvest, Ruth has been looking after Naomi. Now Naomi wants to look after Ruth.
Naomi says, is not Boaz our relative, the one whose young women you worked with? Look, he is winnowing barley at the threshing floor. Winnowing was the work of tossing the grain into the air, so that the wind would carry away the light husks, and the good grain would fall to the ground. At harvest, the owner would stay by the grain pile to guard it.
So Naomi gives Ruth careful instructions. Wash, and put on perfume, and your best clothes, and go down to the threshing floor. But do not let him know you are there until he has finished his meal. When he lies down to sleep, note the place. Then go in, and uncover his feet, and lie down there. And he will tell you what to do.
To us, this sounds strange. But in that time and place, it was a quiet, private way for a woman to ask a man to take her under his protection, as her husband. Ruth was asking Boaz to be her redeemer. Ruth says, 'All that thou sayest unto me I will do.'
So she goes down to the threshing floor, and does all that her mother in law told her. When Boaz has eaten, and is in good spirits, he goes to lie down at the end of the heap of grain. Ruth comes softly, uncovers his feet, and lies down.
'And it came to pass at midnight, that the man was afraid, and turned himself: and, behold, a woman lay at his feet.'
He says, who art thou?
And Ruth asks him plainly.
Boaz is not angry, or embarrassed. He blesses her.
And he goes on, speaking with care.
But there is a problem, and Boaz is honest about it. It is true that I am your near kinsman. But there is a kinsman nearer than I. By law, that man has the first right to redeem the family. Boaz will not go behind his back.
So Boaz tells her, stay here, and in the light of day, if he will do the part of a kinsman for you, good, let him. But if he will not, then I will do it, as the LORD liveth. He seals his promise with the name of the LORD.
So Ruth stays at his feet, and gets up before it is light enough for anyone to recognise another. Boaz is careful to protect her good name. He says, 'Let it not be known that a woman came into the floor.'
Then he says, bring the shawl you are wearing, and hold it out. And he pours into it six measures of barley, a heavy load, and helps her lift it. Then she goes back to the town.
When Ruth comes to her mother in law, Naomi asks, how did it go, my daughter? And Ruth tells her all that the man has done for her. She says, he gave me these six measures of barley. For he said to me, 'Go not empty unto thy mother in law.'
Go not empty. Remember what Naomi said when she came home to Bethlehem: the LORD hath brought me home again empty. Now Boaz sends a gift to her, so that she will not be empty. Naomi’s emptiness is being filled, one sack of barley at a time.
And Naomi knows Boaz well enough to know what he will do next.
Now, three verses to hold on to.
Verse 9. 'And he said, Who art thou? And she answered, I am Ruth thine handmaid: spread therefore thy skirt over thine handmaid; for thou art a near kinsman.' Skirt here means the corner of his cloak. To spread it over her was a picture of protection and marriage. And listen to the words. The same picture Boaz used in chapter two, when he blessed Ruth for coming to trust under the wings of the LORD. In Hebrew, the word for the corner of a cloak and the word for wings are the same. Boaz had prayed that God would shelter Ruth under his wings. Now Ruth asks Boaz to be the answer to his own prayer.
Verse 11. 'And now, my daughter, fear not; I will do to thee all that thou requirest: for all the city of my people doth know that thou art a virtuous woman.' Virtuous means a woman of noble character, strong and good. Ruth came to Bethlehem as a poor stranger from Moab. But by her loyalty and hard work and faith, she had won the respect of the whole town. Boaz does not see her as an outsider, or as a burden. He sees her worth. And he says to her, fear not. He will take care of everything.
Verse 18. 'Then said she, Sit still, my daughter, until thou know how the matter will fall: for the man will not be in rest, until he have finished the thing this day.' Ruth has done all she can do. Now it is time to wait. Sit still, says Naomi. Trust the one who has promised. Boaz will not rest until it is done. Sometimes faith means acting bravely. And sometimes it means sitting still, and trusting God, and the people he has sent, to do what they have promised.
The book of Proverbs asks, 'Who can find a virtuous woman? for her price is far above rubies.' And a psalm says, 'Rest in the LORD, and wait patiently for him'.
Is there something you have done all you can do about, and now you must wait? Hear Naomi’s words: sit still. Bring it to God in prayer, and trust him to finish what he has started.
Here is today’s question. Ruth asked Boaz to spread his protection over her, and he answered, fear not. What do you need to ask God for, trusting that he will answer you with the same care?
Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that I can come to you and ask you to spread your protection over me. Give me courage like Ruth’s, honour like Boaz’s, and patience to sit still and trust you when I have done all that I can do. Fill the empty places in my life. Amen.
