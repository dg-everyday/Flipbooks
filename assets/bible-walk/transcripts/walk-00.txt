Two friends are walking home along a dusty road, about seven miles from Jerusalem. They are sad and confused, and they talk the whole way about everything that has happened. Then, the Bible says, 'Jesus himself drew near, and went with them.' He walks beside them, and as they go he opens the Scriptures to them, from Moses and all the prophets. Later they say to each other, 'Did not our heart burn within us, while he talked with us by the way, and while he opened to us the scriptures?'
That is what this walk is for. Not to rush through a book, but to walk through the Bible one chapter at a time, and let the Word open up as we go.
Welcome to Bible Walk, in Daily Grace. Each step of the walk is one chapter of the Bible, in about eight minutes. You do not need to begin on the first page of the Bible and push through to the end. Many people try that, and many stop somewhere in the middle, tired and lost. So we will start where the story is easiest to follow: with Jesus himself, in the book of Mark. After that we will step back and see the big picture, from the first chapter of Genesis to the last chapters of Revelation, and then walk on through the other books one at a time.
Every chapter is walked the same way. First, where we are: what came before, and where the story stands. Then the chapter itself, told as a story, so you can see it happen. Every time someone speaks, you will hear the very words of the King James Bible. Then we slow down and hold on to three verses, and explain them in plain words. We see how the chapter connects to the rest of the Bible. And we finish with one thing to carry into your day, a question, and a short prayer.
The King James Bible has some old words. Thee and thou simply mean you. Straightway means at once. When an old word comes along, we will explain it as we walk, so you never get lost.
Here is a simple way to walk through any chapter, with Bible Walk or on your own.
First, pray before you read. A short prayer is enough. The psalm says, 'Open thou mine eyes, that I may behold wondrous things out of thy law.'
Second, read the chapter, or listen to it, and then read it again. You can open the chapter in Daily Grace and read along while you listen. In the book of Acts, the people of Berea 'received the word with all readiness of mind, and searched the scriptures daily, whether those things were so.' Do the same. Check what you hear against the Book itself.
Third, notice. Who is in the chapter? What happens? Who speaks? Which words come back again and again?
Fourth, ask two questions. What does this show me about God? And what does this show me about people, and about me?
Fifth, do one thing. James wrote, 'But be ye doers of the word, and not hearers only, deceiving your own selves.' Each episode ends with a question. Write your answer in My Faith Journal, and look back at it later.
You do not have to walk fast. A chapter a few times a week, done well, is better than a long reading you forget. And if you miss a day, nothing is lost. The walk waits for you. Simply press play on the next chapter.
Here is a question to carry with you. As you begin this walk through the Bible, what do you hope to find?
Let us pray. Lord, as I walk through your Word one chapter at a time, open my eyes. Draw near and walk with me, as you walked with those two friends on the road. Help me not only to hear, but to do what you show me. Amen.
