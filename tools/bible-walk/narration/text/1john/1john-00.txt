The Living Voice. Meet the First Letter of John.

An old man sits at a table, with a pen in his hand. His hair is white. He has lived a long life. But when he closes his eyes, he can still remember. A voice by the Sea of Galilee saying, follow me. Leaning close to his teacher at a supper table. An empty tomb, and grave clothes lying folded. He has seen it all with his own eyes. And now he writes to people he loves as his own children.

Welcome to the first letter of John, on The Living Voice. First John has five chapters, so on this walk it is five steps, one chapter at a time. After all the kings and battles of first Kings, it is a short and gentle letter. And it may be the warmest letter in the whole Bible.

The letter does not give its writer’s name. But from very early times, Christians have linked it with John, one of the twelve disciples, the brother of James, and the one the Gospel of John calls the disciple whom Jesus loved. It sounds very much like the Gospel of John. Listen to how the Gospel begins: 'In the beginning was the Word'. And now listen to how this letter begins. 'That which was from the beginning, which we have heard, which we have seen with our eyes, which we have looked upon, and our hands have handled, of the Word of life'.

The writer is an eyewitness. He is not passing on rumours. He heard Jesus. He saw him. He touched him.

And he writes like a loving grandfather. Again and again, he calls his readers, my little children, or beloved. He is writing to Christians he cares about deeply, who are going through a confusing time.

What was the trouble? Some people had been part of the church, and then left. And they were teaching ideas that sounded clever and spiritual, but were not true. Some were saying that Jesus did not really come in a human body. Some were saying that what you do does not matter, as long as you know the right things. And some were saying that they had no sin at all. The believers who stayed were unsettled. Are we really God’s children? Do we really have eternal life?

So John writes to reassure them. Near the end of the letter, he tells us exactly why he wrote it. 'These things have I written unto you that believe on the name of the Son of God; that ye may know that ye have eternal life'.

Here are three things to watch for as we walk.

Watch for light. John says, God is light, and in him is no darkness at all. Listen for what it means to walk in the light, and what happens when we bring our sins out of the darkness.

Watch for love. This is the letter where we read, God is love. John says it is impossible to love God and hate your brother. And he shows us where love began: not that we loved God, but that he loved us, and sent his Son.

And watch for two little words: we know. John uses them again and again. We know that we know him. We know that we have passed from death to life. We know that he hears us. John wants us to be sure. Not proud, but confident, resting on what God has done.

This letter is simple enough for a child to understand, and deep enough to think about for a lifetime. Many people who are new to the Bible find that John’s short sentences speak straight to their hearts.

Here is a question to carry with you. John wrote so that his readers could know they have eternal life. Is there anything that makes you unsure about your relationship with God?

Let us pray. Lord God, as I walk through the first letter of John, help me to walk in your light, and to know your love. Thank you that you want me to be sure that I belong to you. Teach me to love others, as you have loved me. Amen.
