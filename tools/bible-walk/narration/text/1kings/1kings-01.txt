The Living Voice. 1 Kings, chapter 1. God Save King Solomon.

Down by a spring outside Jerusalem, a priest lifts up a horn of oil, and pours it over a young man’s head. A trumpet blasts. And a great crowd shouts, God save king Solomon. They play their pipes, and cheer so loudly that the ground seems to shake. Not far away, at another feast, another son of David hears the noise, and his face goes pale.

In the introduction, we heard that first Kings tells of Solomon’s wisdom and the temple, of a kingdom torn in two, and of the prophet Elijah. We heard three things to watch for: the heart, the prophets, and how every king is measured against David.

Now, in chapter one, David is near the end of his life, and the question everyone is asking is, who will be the next king? Listen for a son who decides for himself. And listen for a promise kept.

The chapter begins, 'Now king David was old and stricken in years; and they covered him with clothes, but he gat no heat.' The great warrior king is now frail, and always cold. His servants find a young woman named Abishag to look after him and care for him.

And while David lies weak, one of his sons makes his move. 'Then Adonijah the son of Haggith exalted himself, saying, I will be king'. Adonijah was the oldest of David’s sons who was still living. He was very handsome, like his brother Absalom. He gets chariots and horsemen, and fifty men to run before him, just as Absalom had done.

And there is something about Adonijah we are told.

Some powerful men join him. Joab, the commander of the army, and Abiathar the priest. But others do not. Zadok the priest, Benaiah the captain, and Nathan the prophet stay loyal to David. Adonijah holds a great feast, and invites all the king’s sons, except one. He does not invite Solomon.

Nathan the prophet hears what is happening, and goes straight to Bathsheba, Solomon’s mother. Have you not heard, he says, that Adonijah has made himself king, and David our lord does not know? Your life, and your son’s life, are in danger. Go in to the king, and remind him. Did you not promise me, my lord, that Solomon my son would be king after you? Why then is Adonijah king? And while you are still speaking, I will come in and back you up.

So Bathsheba goes in to the old king, and bows before him. My lord, she says, you swore by the LORD your God that Solomon would reign after you. But now Adonijah is king, and you do not even know it. All Israel is waiting for you to tell them who will sit on your throne.

While she is still speaking, Nathan comes in. He bows to the ground and asks, my lord, have you said that Adonijah shall reign after you? He is feasting right now, and they are shouting, God save king Adonijah.

And David, old as he is, rises to the moment. He calls Bathsheba back in, and makes a solemn promise.

Then David acts. He calls Zadok the priest, Nathan the prophet, and Benaiah. Put my son Solomon on my own mule, he says, and take him down to Gihon. Anoint him king over Israel there. Blow the trumpet, and say, God save king Solomon. Then bring him back to sit on my throne.

Benaiah answers, 'Amen: the LORD God of my lord the king say so too.'

'And Zadok the priest took an horn of oil out of the tabernacle, and anointed Solomon. And they blew the trumpet; and all the people said, God save king Solomon.' The people follow him back up into the city, playing pipes and rejoicing, so loudly that the earth seems to split with the sound.

Meanwhile, Adonijah and his guests are just finishing their feast. Joab hears the trumpet, and asks, why is the city in an uproar? Just then, a priest’s son named Jonathan runs in, with the news. Our lord king David has made Solomon king. He is sitting on the throne. And even the old king has bowed on his bed and worshipped.

And David says something lovely, which we will come back to.

Adonijah’s guests jump up in fear, and slip away, every man to his own home. And Adonijah, terrified, runs to the altar, and takes hold of its horns, the corners of the altar, hoping for mercy. Solomon sends word. 'If he will shew himself a worthy man, there shall not an hair of him fall to the earth'. So they bring Adonijah down from the altar. He bows to king Solomon. And Solomon says, 'Go to thine house.'

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 6. 'And his father had not displeased him at any time in saying, Why hast thou done so? and he also was a very goodly man; and his mother bare him after Absalom.' David never once said to Adonijah, why have you done that? He never corrected him. David had been a great king, but not always a wise father. Amnon, Absalom, and now Adonijah all went their own way. Love sometimes says no. Children need to hear, why hast thou done so, because a life with no limits easily becomes a life of pride.

Verse 30. 'Even as I sware unto thee by the LORD God of Israel, saying, Assuredly Solomon thy son shall reign after me, and he shall sit upon my throne in my stead; even so will I certainly do this day.' In my stead means in my place. David had made a promise, and now he keeps it, that very day. Behind David’s promise was a greater one. God had told David that his son would build a house for God’s name. Adonijah made a plan. But God had made a promise. And God’s promises always stand.

Verse 48. 'And also thus said the king, Blessed be the LORD God of Israel, which hath given one to sit on my throne this day, mine eyes even seeing it.' David has lived a long and troubled life. He has run from Saul, fought many wars, sinned, and grieved. And now, at the end, he blesses the LORD. God has let him see his promise come true with his own eyes. What a way to finish, with praise on his lips.

A psalm for Solomon prays, 'Give the king thy judgments, O God, and thy righteousness unto the king’s son.' And Solomon himself later wrote, 'Train up a child in the way he should go: and when he is old, he will not depart from it.'

Is there a promise you have made, to God or to someone else, that you need to keep? Keep it today, as David did. And if you have children, or help to look after them, ask God for the courage to say no in love, when it is needed.

Here is today’s question. Adonijah decided for himself, I will be king. Where are you tempted to push your own plans, instead of waiting for God’s?

Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that your promises always stand, even when people try to overturn them. Keep me from pride that says, I will be king. Help me to keep my promises, and to love others enough to correct them when they need it. And like David, let me bless your name all my days. Amen.
