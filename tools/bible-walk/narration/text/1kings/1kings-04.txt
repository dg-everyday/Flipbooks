The Living Voice. 1 Kings, chapter 4. Wisdom and Understanding Exceeding Much.

A farmer sits in the shade of his own fig tree, at the end of a long, warm day. Above him, the grapes are ripening on his vine. His children play nearby. There is no war. No enemy is coming over the hill. His barns are full. For a family in ancient Israel, it was the most beautiful picture of peace there was. And in the days of Solomon, for a while, it came true.

In chapter three, God appeared to Solomon in a dream and said, ask what I shall give thee. Solomon asked for an understanding heart, and God gave him wisdom, and riches and honour as well. Then two women came, each claiming the same baby, and Solomon’s wisdom revealed the true mother. All Israel saw that the wisdom of God was in him.

Now, in chapter four, we see what Solomon’s kingdom looked like. Listen for peace and plenty. And listen for how far Solomon’s wisdom reached.

'So king Solomon was king over all Israel.' And a king needs helpers. So the chapter begins with a list of Solomon’s officials. There is Azariah, the son of Zadok the priest. There are scribes, who wrote the royal letters and records. There is a recorder, who kept track of events. Benaiah is over the army. Zadok and Abiathar are named as priests. There is one man over the household, and another over the workers. And one man has a special title. Zabud is called 'the king’s friend.' Even the wisest king needed a friend.

Then Solomon divides the land into twelve districts, and puts an officer over each one. Each district has to provide food for the king and his household for one month of the year. And the king’s household was large. Every single day, Solomon’s court needed many measures of fine flour and meal, oxen from the stalls and the pastures, and a hundred sheep, as well as deer and gazelles and fattened birds.

How did ordinary people live? 'Judah and Israel were many, as the sand which is by the sea in multitude, eating and drinking, and making merry.' As the sand by the sea. Do you remember where we heard that before? God promised Abraham that his family would be as many as the stars in the sky, and the sand on the sea shore. Now, here it is. God’s promise to Abraham, coming true in the days of Solomon.

Solomon ruled over many kingdoms, from the river Euphrates to the land of the Philistines, and down to the border of Egypt. They brought him gifts, and served him. 'and he had peace on all sides round about him.'

And then comes the picture of peace.

We also hear that Solomon had a great many horses for his chariots, and many horsemen. That might sound impressive. But long before, God had told Moses that Israel’s kings must not gather many horses for themselves, or trust in their own strength. Keep that in mind too. Solomon’s riches were a blessing from God. But they could also become a danger.

Then the chapter turns to Solomon’s wisdom.

His wisdom was greater than all the wise men of the east, and all the wisdom of Egypt, which was famous throughout the ancient world. He was wiser than the most famous wise men of his day, and they are named: Ethan, and Heman, and others. 'and his fame was in all nations round about.'

And Solomon’s wisdom was not only about ruling. It was about everything God had made. 'And he spake of trees, from the cedar tree that is in Lebanon even unto the hyssop that springeth out of the wall: he spake also of beasts, and of fowl, and of creeping things, and of fishes.' From the tallest cedar on the mountains, to the tiny hyssop plant growing in a crack in a wall. From animals and birds, to insects and fish. Solomon studied God’s world, and saw God’s wisdom in it.

'And there came of all people to hear the wisdom of Solomon, from all kings of the earth, which had heard of his wisdom.' People travelled from far away, just to listen to him.

This was the high point of Israel’s history. A wise king, a peaceful land, full barns, and a people as many as the sand. It was a glimpse of what God’s kingdom could look like.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 25. 'And Judah and Israel dwelt safely, every man under his vine and under his fig tree, from Dan even to Beer-sheba, all the days of Solomon.' From Dan in the far north, to Beer-sheba in the far south, people lived in safety. Under his vine and under his fig tree means each family enjoying its own land and its own harvest, in peace. Later, the prophets used these same words to describe the peace God will one day bring to the whole world, when no one will make anyone afraid.

Verse 29. 'And God gave Solomon wisdom and understanding exceeding much, and largeness of heart, even as the sand that is on the sea shore.' Exceeding much means more than enough. Largeness of heart means a mind that could take in a great deal, and a heart that cared widely. And notice where it came from. God gave it. Solomon’s wisdom was a gift, the answer to his prayer at Gibeon. Like the sand on the shore, it was too much to count.

Verse 32. 'And he spake three thousand proverbs: and his songs were a thousand and five.' A proverb is a short, wise saying, that is easy to remember. Some of Solomon’s proverbs are in the book of Proverbs, which we will walk through later. And one of his songs is the Song of Solomon. Solomon did not keep his wisdom to himself. He put it into words, so that others could learn and remember it, and so that we can still learn from it today.

The prophet Micah wrote of a coming day, 'But they shall sit every man under his vine and under his fig tree; and none shall make them afraid'. And Jesus said, 'behold, a greater than Solomon is here.'

Take a moment to look closely at something God has made. A tree, a bird, a flower growing through a crack. Like Solomon, see God’s wisdom in it, and thank him. And ask God for wisdom for one thing you have to do today.

Here is today’s question. Solomon’s wisdom showed itself in peace, in fairness, and in wonder at God’s world. Which of these would you most like God to grow in you, and why?

Let us pray. Lord God, thank you for the peace and plenty you gave your people in the days of Solomon, and for keeping your promise to Abraham. Thank you for the wonder of your world, from the cedar to the hyssop. Give me wisdom, and a large heart. And thank you for Jesus, who is greater than Solomon, and who will one day bring perfect peace. Amen.
