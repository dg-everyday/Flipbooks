The Living Voice. 1 Kings, chapter 10. The Half Was Not Told Me.

A long line of camels winds across the desert, loaded with spices, gold and precious stones. At the front rides a queen. She has travelled a very long way, from the land of Sheba, far to the south. She has heard amazing stories about a king in Jerusalem, and she does not believe them. So she has come to see for herself. And she has brought her hardest questions with her.

In chapter nine, God appeared to Solomon a second time. He said, I have heard thy prayer, and mine eyes and mine heart shall be there perpetually. But he also set two roads before Solomon. Walk with me as David did, and your throne will stand. Turn to other gods, and even this house will become a ruin. Then Solomon’s ships sailed to Ophir, and brought back gold.

Now, in chapter ten, Solomon’s fame reaches its highest point. Listen for what the queen of Sheba says. And listen carefully to the list of Solomon’s riches. There is a warning hidden in it.

The chapter begins with a visitor.

She arrives in Jerusalem with a great company, camels carrying spices, and a great deal of gold and precious stones. And when she meets Solomon, she talks with him about everything that is on her heart. 'And Solomon told her all her questions: there was not any thing hid from the king, which he told her not.' No question was too hard for him.

Then she looks around. She sees Solomon’s wisdom. She sees the house he has built. She sees the food on his table, his officials sitting in their places, his servants in their fine clothes, his cupbearers, and the way he goes up to the house of the LORD. 'there was no more spirit in her.' It took her breath away.

And she says to the king, it was a true report I heard in my own land, about your deeds and your wisdom.

She goes on. 'Happy are thy men, happy are these thy servants, which stand continually before thee, and that hear thy wisdom.' And then she says something even more important.

The queen gives Solomon a huge gift of gold, and more spices than had ever been seen in Israel, and precious stones. And Solomon gives her everything she asks for, and much more besides, from his royal generosity. Then she returns to her own country.

Next, the writer describes Solomon’s riches, and they are almost beyond belief. Every year, a huge weight of gold came to Solomon, as well as everything brought by traders and merchants, and the kings of Arabia. He made large shields and smaller shields of beaten gold, and hung them in the house of the forest of Lebanon.

He made a great throne of ivory, covered with the finest gold. It had six steps up to it, with a lion standing at each end of every step, and two more lions beside the arms of the throne. 'there was not the like made in any kingdom.'

All of Solomon’s drinking cups were gold. Silver was not worth much in his days, there was so much of it. His ships sailed with Hiram’s ships, and came back every few years with gold, silver, ivory, apes and peacocks.

'So king Solomon exceeded all the kings of the earth for riches and for wisdom. And all the earth sought to Solomon, to hear his wisdom, which God had put in his heart.' Every year, visitors came with gifts. Silver and gold, robes, weapons, spices, horses and mules.

And here is where we need to listen carefully. Solomon gathered many chariots and horsemen. 'And the king made silver to be in Jerusalem as stones'. And Solomon had horses brought out of Egypt.

Long before, in the book of Deuteronomy, God gave Moses three rules for any future king of Israel. He must not gather many horses, or send his people back to Egypt to get them. He must not take many wives, so that his heart would not turn away. And he must not pile up great amounts of silver and gold for himself.

Look at Solomon. Many horses, brought from Egypt. Silver as common as stones. And in the very next chapter, we will hear about his wives. Solomon was wise enough to answer every question the queen of Sheba asked. But he was not wise enough to obey God’s simple commands about his own heart. The blessings God had given him were slowly becoming a danger.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 1. 'And when the queen of Sheba heard of the fame of Solomon concerning the name of the LORD, she came to prove him with hard questions.' Notice the words, concerning the name of the LORD. Solomon was famous not just for being clever or rich, but because of his God. To prove means to test. The queen came with her hardest questions about life. And through Solomon’s wisdom, she came face to face with the God who gave it. Our lives, too, can point people to God.

Verse 7. 'Howbeit I believed not the words, until I came, and mine eyes had seen it: and, behold, the half was not told me: thy wisdom and prosperity exceedeth the fame which I heard.' The queen had heard the stories, but she did not believe them, until she came and saw for herself. Then she found the truth was even greater than she had heard. It is like that with God. People may tell us about him. But when we come to him ourselves, we find that the half was not told us. He is greater and better than we ever imagined.

Verse 9. 'Blessed be the LORD thy God, which delighted in thee, to set thee on the throne of Israel: because the LORD loved Israel for ever, therefore made he thee king, to do judgment and justice.' A foreign queen blesses the LORD, the God of Israel. And she sees why God gave Israel a king: because he loved them, and wanted them to have justice. That is what kings are for. Not to grow rich, but to do what is right and fair for the people.

Jesus said, 'The queen of the south shall rise up in the judgment with this generation, and shall condemn it: for she came from the uttermost parts of the earth to hear the wisdom of Solomon; and, behold, a greater than Solomon is here.' And the psalmist wrote, 'O taste and see that the LORD is good'.

The queen travelled a very long way to hear Solomon. Jesus is greater than Solomon, and we can come to him today, right where we are. Bring him your hardest question, and listen. And ask him to show you if any of his blessings are starting to take his place in your heart.

Here is today’s question. The queen of Sheba said, the half was not told me. In what ways have you found that God is greater and better than you first thought?

Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that you give wisdom, and that your fame goes out to the ends of the earth. Thank you that when we come to you, we find the half was not told us. Keep my heart from loving your gifts more than you. And thank you for Jesus, who is greater than Solomon, and who welcomes all who come to him. Amen.
