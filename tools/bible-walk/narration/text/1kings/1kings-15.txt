The Living Voice. 1 Kings, chapter 15. Asa Did That Which Was Right.

A young king walks through his kingdom, and everywhere he looks, he sees idols. On the hills, under the trees, even in the royal family. His father and his grandfather had allowed them. Everyone is used to them. It would be easy to leave things as they are. But this young king has decided. The idols must go, every one, even if it costs him dearly.

In chapter fourteen, Jeroboam’s wife came in disguise to the blind prophet Ahijah, but he knew her before she spoke. Jeroboam had cast God behind his back, and his son died, the only one in his family in whom some good thing was found. In Judah, Rehoboam’s people built idols on every hill, and Shishak of Egypt carried away Solomon’s golden shields.

Now, in chapter fifteen, the story moves quickly through several kings. Listen for one king in Judah who does what is right. And listen for how God keeps his promise to David.

When Rehoboam dies, his son Abijam becomes king of Judah. He reigns only a short time. And sadly, he walks in all the sins of his father. 'and his heart was not perfect with the LORD his God, as the heart of David his father.'

But even so, God does not give up on David’s family.

The writer adds why. 'Because David did that which was right in the eyes of the LORD, and turned not aside from any thing that he commanded him all the days of his life, save only in the matter of Uriah the Hittite.' The Bible does not hide David’s great sin. But it shows that his life, as a whole, was turned towards God.

Then Abijam dies, and his son Asa becomes king of Judah. And at last, here is good news.

Asa gets to work. He removes the idols his fathers had made, and drives out the wicked practices from the land. And then he does something very hard. His grandmother, Maachah, the queen mother, had made a horrible idol, an image for the goddess Asherah. Asa removes her from her position as queen mother. He cuts down her idol, and burns it by the brook Kidron. Asa loved his family. But he loved God more.

Asa was not perfect. He did not remove all the high places, the hilltop shrines around the land. But the writer says something wonderful about his heart.

Asa also brings into the temple the silver and gold that he and his father had dedicated to God, to replace some of what had been lost.

But there was war between Asa and Baasha, the king of Israel. Baasha builds up the town of Ramah, just a few miles north of Jerusalem, as a fortress, to stop anyone from going in or out of Judah.

And here Asa makes a mistake. Instead of trusting God, he takes the silver and gold that are left in the temple and the palace, and sends them to Ben-hadad, the king of Syria, in Damascus. There is a treaty between you and me, he says. Here is a gift. Break your treaty with Baasha, so he will leave me alone. And Ben-hadad does. He attacks the cities in the north of Israel, and Baasha has to stop building Ramah.

Asa then calls out all the people of Judah, and they carry away the stones and timber of Ramah, and use them to build two towns of their own. It worked. But another book of the Bible tells us that a prophet came and told Asa he should have relied on the LORD, not on the king of Syria.

At the end of his long reign, in his old age, Asa has a disease in his feet. Then he dies, and his son Jehoshaphat becomes king.

Now the story turns to the northern kingdom of Israel. Nadab, the son of Jeroboam, becomes king. He reigns only a short time, and he does evil, walking in the way of his father. Then a man named Baasha plots against him, and takes his life, and becomes king in his place. And as soon as Baasha is king, he destroys the whole family of Jeroboam, just as the prophet Ahijah had said, because of the sins of Jeroboam, who made Israel to sin.

But Baasha does no better. He reigns for many years, and he too does evil in the sight of the LORD, and walks in the way of Jeroboam.

Do you notice the difference between the two kingdoms? In Judah, David’s family continues, from father to son, because of God’s promise. Some kings are good, and some are bad, but the lamp keeps burning. In Israel, one family after another is cut off, and the throne passes from one man to the next, often by violence.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 4. 'Nevertheless for David’s sake did the LORD his God give him a lamp in Jerusalem, to set up his son after him, and to establish Jerusalem'. A lamp in the darkness. Abijam did not deserve it. But God kept the lamp burning, not because of Abijam, but because of his promise to David. God is faithful, even when people are not. And that is good news for us too. God loves us and keeps us, not because we deserve it, but because of his promise in Jesus, the Son of David.

Verse 11. 'And Asa did that which was right in the eyes of the LORD, as did David his father.' David was his great-great-grandfather, but the writer calls him his father, because David was the father of the whole royal family. Asa was like David, not in every detail, but in his heart. He did what was right in the eyes of the LORD. Not in the eyes of the people, or the court, or his family. In God’s eyes.

Verse 14. 'But the high places were not removed: nevertheless Asa’s heart was perfect with the LORD all his days.' Asa did not get everything right. Some high places remained. He made mistakes, like sending the temple gold to Syria. And yet God says his heart was perfect, whole, devoted to him, all his days. God looks at the heart. A heart that truly belongs to God, though imperfect, is precious to him.

A prophet told king Asa, 'For the eyes of the LORD run to and fro throughout the whole earth, to shew himself strong in the behalf of them whose heart is perfect toward him.' And Jesus said, 'He that loveth father or mother more than me is not worthy of me'.

Is there an idol in your life that everyone around you accepts, something that has taken God’s place? Ask God for the courage of Asa to remove it. And when trouble comes, turn to God first, before you turn to anyone else for help.

Here is today’s question. Asa removed the idols, even when it meant standing against his own family. What would it look like for you to put God first, even when it is hard?

Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that you kept a lamp burning for David’s sake, and that you keep your promises even when people fail. Give me a heart that is wholly yours, all my days. Give me courage to remove anything that takes your place. And when I am in trouble, help me to rely on you first. Amen.
