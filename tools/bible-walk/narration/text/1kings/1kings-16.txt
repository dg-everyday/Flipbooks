The Living Voice. 1 Kings, chapter 16. Ahab Did Evil Above All.

Think of a slide in a playground. Once you start going down, you go faster and faster. That is what this chapter feels like. In Israel, king follows king, each one worse than the one before. Plots, betrayals, a palace on fire. Faster and faster, down and down. Until, at the bottom, we meet the worst king of all.

In chapter fifteen, Asa, king of Judah, did that which was right in the eyes of the LORD. He removed the idols, even his grandmother’s, and his heart was perfect with the LORD all his days. God kept a lamp burning in Jerusalem for David’s sake. But in Israel, Baasha took the throne, and walked in the way of Jeroboam.

Now, in chapter sixteen, things get worse in Israel. Listen for how quickly the kings come and go. And listen for the names of Ahab and Jezebel. They will fill the rest of this book.

God sends a prophet named Jehu, the son of Hanani, to king Baasha. And the message is serious.

God tells Baasha that because he has walked in the way of Jeroboam, his family will be swept away, just like Jeroboam’s. Baasha had destroyed Jeroboam’s family, as God had said. But he did not learn from it. He did exactly the same evil things.

So Baasha dies, and his son Elah becomes king. Elah reigns for only a short time. One day, while Elah is feasting and drinking at the house of his steward, one of his own commanders, a man named Zimri, plots against him, and takes his life. Zimri makes himself king, and destroys the whole family of Baasha, just as the prophet Jehu had said.

But Zimri does not last long. 'did Zimri reign seven days in Tirzah.' Seven days. When the army hears what he has done, they make their commander, Omri, king instead. Omri and the army march on the city of Tirzah. And when Zimri sees that the city has fallen, he goes into the palace and sets it on fire around himself, and dies in the flames. All because he walked in the way of Jeroboam.

Then the people of Israel are divided. Half of them follow a man named Tibni, and half follow Omri. Omri’s side wins, Tibni dies, and Omri becomes king.

Omri does something that will matter for a very long time.

Samaria became the capital of the northern kingdom. And many years later, the people who lived in that region would be called Samaritans. Do you remember the good Samaritan in the parable Jesus told, and the Samaritan woman Jesus met at the well? Their story began here, on Omri’s hill.

Omri was a strong king, and other nations knew his name. But in God’s eyes, he was a failure. 'But Omri wrought evil in the eyes of the LORD, and did worse than all that were before him.'

Then Omri dies, and his son Ahab becomes king. 'And Ahab the son of Omri did evil in the sight of the LORD above all that were before him.' Worse than Jeroboam. Worse than Baasha. Worse than his own father.

What did he do? The writer says it was as if the sins of Jeroboam were not enough for him.

Jezebel was a princess from Sidon, on the coast. She worshipped Baal, the god of storms and rain, who her people believed made the crops grow. And Ahab brought her gods with him. He built a temple for Baal in Samaria, and set up an altar for Baal inside it. He also made an image of the goddess Asherah. 'and Ahab did more to provoke the LORD God of Israel to anger than all the kings of Israel that were before him.'

The chapter ends with one more sad detail. In Ahab’s days, a man named Hiel rebuilt the city of Jericho. Do you remember Jericho, from the book of Joshua? After the walls fell down, Joshua warned that whoever rebuilt Jericho would lose his eldest son when he laid the foundations, and his youngest son when he set up the gates. And that is exactly what happened to Hiel. God’s word, spoken long before, had come true. In Ahab’s days, no one cared about God’s word any more. But God’s word had not changed.

So we have reached the bottom of the slide. The worst king Israel had ever known, and a queen who worshipped Baal. It seems as though God’s people have completely forgotten him. And then, suddenly, without any warning, God sends a man. His name is Elijah.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 2. 'Forasmuch as I exalted thee out of the dust, and made thee prince over my people Israel; and thou hast walked in the way of Jeroboam, and hast made my people Israel to sin, to provoke me to anger with their sins'. Out of the dust. Baasha had been a nobody, and God lifted him up. Every good thing he had was a gift. And notice what God calls Israel: my people. Even when they were wandering, they were still his people, and he cared deeply about what their leaders did to them.

Verse 24. 'And he bought the hill Samaria of Shemer for two talents of silver, and built on the hill, and called the name of the city which he built, after the name of Shemer, owner of the hill, Samaria.' A talent was a large weight of silver. Omri built a strong capital on a hill, easy to defend. In the world’s eyes, that was a great achievement. But the Bible gives it only a few verses, and then says Omri did worse than all before him. What matters to God is not what we build, but how we walk with him.

Verse 31. 'And it came to pass, as if it had been a light thing for him to walk in the sins of Jeroboam the son of Nebat, that he took to wife Jezebel the daughter of Ethbaal king of the Zidonians, and went and served Baal, and worshipped him.' As if it had been a light thing. Ahab treated sin as small. Jeroboam’s golden calves were bad enough. But Ahab went further, and openly worshipped another god. When we treat small sins lightly, it becomes easier to fall into bigger ones.

Hannah prayed, 'He raiseth up the poor out of the dust'. And Solomon wrote, 'Righteousness exalteth a nation: but sin is a reproach to any people.'

Think of what God has lifted you out of, and thank him. And is there a sin you have been treating as a light thing? Bring it to God today, before it grows.

Here is today’s question. Omri built a great city, but the Bible says he did evil in God’s eyes. When you think about your own life, what do you think matters most to God?

Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that you lift people up out of the dust. Everything good I have is a gift from you. Keep me from treating sin as a light thing. And thank you that even in the darkest times, when it seems everyone has forgotten you, you never forget your people. You always send help. Amen.
