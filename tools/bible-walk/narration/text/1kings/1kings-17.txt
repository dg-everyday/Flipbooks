The Living Voice. 1 Kings, chapter 17. The Barrel of Meal Shall Not Waste.

A poor widow bends down at the gate of her town, gathering a few sticks. Her flour jar is almost empty. There is a little oil left in the bottom of a jug. She is planning to make one last small loaf for herself and her son, and then they will wait to die. And at that moment, a stranger walks up to her, and asks her for a drink of water, and a piece of bread.

In chapter sixteen, Israel went downhill fast. Kings rose and fell, one after another. Omri built Samaria, and did worse than all before him. Then his son Ahab did evil above them all. He married Jezebel, a princess of Sidon, and built a temple for Baal in Samaria. It seemed that God’s people had forgotten him completely.

Now, in chapter seventeen, God sends his answer. Listen for a man who appears out of nowhere. And listen for three ways God takes care of him.

The chapter opens without any introduction.

That is all we are told about where Elijah comes from. He was a Tishbite, from Gilead, the rough hill country east of the Jordan. His name means, the LORD is my God. And that is exactly what he came to show. Baal was supposed to be the god of storms and rain. Now the LORD would show who really controls the rain.

Then God tells Elijah to hide. Go east, and hide yourself by the brook Cherith, near the Jordan. 'And it shall be, that thou shalt drink of the brook; and I have commanded the ravens to feed thee there.'

Ravens! Big black birds, which usually take food for themselves. And Elijah obeys. He goes and lives by the brook. And twice a day, the ravens bring him bread and meat, and he drinks from the brook.

But there is no rain. And after a while, the brook dries up.

So God speaks again. 'Arise, get thee to Zarephath, which belongeth to Zidon, and dwell there: behold, I have commanded a widow woman there to sustain thee.' Zarephath was a small town on the coast, in the land of Sidon. Jezebel’s own country, the home of Baal worship. And God was sending his prophet there, to be looked after by a poor widow, a foreigner.

When Elijah reaches the town gate, there is a widow gathering sticks. He calls to her, please bring me a little water to drink. As she goes, he calls again, and bring me a piece of bread, please.

And she turns, and tells him the truth. 'As the LORD thy God liveth, I have not a cake, but an handful of meal in a barrel, and a little oil in a cruse: and, behold, I am gathering two sticks, that I may go in and dress it for me and my son, that we may eat it, and die.' Meal means flour. A cruse is a small jar. She has almost nothing left.

And Elijah says, 'Fear not; go and do as thou hast said: but make me thereof a little cake first, and bring it unto me, and after make for thee and for thy son.'

That must have sounded like a lot to ask. Give your last food to a stranger, first? But then Elijah gives her a promise from God.

'And she went and did according to the saying of Elijah'. She trusted God’s word, and gave first. And every day, when she went to the jar, there was flour. When she tipped the jug, there was oil. Day after day, for many days, the widow, her son and Elijah had enough to eat, just as the LORD had said.

But then, something terrible happens. The widow’s son becomes very sick, and he stops breathing. And in her grief, she turns on Elijah. What have you got against me, man of God? Did you come here to remind me of my sins, and take my son from me?

Elijah does not argue. He says, 'Give me thy son.' He takes the boy from her arms, carries him upstairs to his own room, and lays him on his bed. And he cries out to God. O LORD my God, have you brought this trouble even on the widow who has taken me in?

Then Elijah stretches himself out over the boy three times, and prays. 'O LORD my God, I pray thee, let this child’s soul come into him again.' 'And the LORD heard the voice of Elijah; and the soul of the child came into him again, and he revived.'

Elijah carries the boy downstairs, and gives him back to his mother. 'See, thy son liveth.'

It is the first time in the whole Bible that someone is raised from the dead.

And the mother’s answer is the high point of the chapter.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 1. 'And Elijah the Tishbite, who was of the inhabitants of Gilead, said unto Ahab, As the LORD God of Israel liveth, before whom I stand, there shall not be dew nor rain these years, but according to my word.' Before whom I stand. Elijah stood before a wicked king without fear, because he knew he stood before a greater King. Ahab had his throne and his soldiers. But Elijah lived in the presence of the living God. When we know who we stand before, we do not need to fear anyone else.

Verse 14. 'For thus saith the LORD God of Israel, The barrel of meal shall not waste, neither shall the cruse of oil fail, until the day that the LORD sendeth rain upon the earth.' God did not fill the widow’s kitchen with sacks of flour. He gave her just enough, every day. Like the manna in the wilderness, it had to be trusted for, day by day. God often provides for us like that. Not everything at once, but enough for today, and then enough again the next day.

Verse 24. 'And the woman said to Elijah, Now by this I know that thou art a man of God, and that the word of the LORD in thy mouth is truth.' This woman was not an Israelite. She lived in the land of Baal. But through the flour, the oil, and her son brought back to life, she came to know that the LORD’s word is truth. While Israel was worshipping Baal, a foreign widow in Baal’s own country was learning to trust the living God.

Jesus said, 'But unto none of them was Elias sent, save unto Sarepta, a city of Sidon, unto a woman that was a widow.' And Paul wrote, 'But my God shall supply all your need according to his riches in glory by Christ Jesus.'

Are you worried about having enough, of money, or strength, or time? Trust God for today’s portion. And is there something God is asking you to give, even when you have little? Like the widow, give first, and watch how he provides.

Here is today’s question. God fed Elijah through ravens, a brook, and a poor widow. How has God provided for you in ways you did not expect?

Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that you are the living God, who sends the rain and feeds the hungry. Thank you that you provide for me day by day. Help me to trust you when the jar looks almost empty. Give me the faith of the widow, to give first, and to know that your word is truth. Amen.
