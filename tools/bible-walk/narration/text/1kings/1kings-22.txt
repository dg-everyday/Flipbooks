The Living Voice. 1 Kings, chapter 22. What the LORD Saith unto Me.

Two kings sit on their thrones, wearing their royal robes, at the gate of a city. In front of them, four hundred prophets are shouting, all at once. Go up! You will win! One prophet has even made himself iron horns, and is charging about like a bull. Everyone agrees. Everyone, except one man, who is not there. He has been sent for. And the king of Israel hates him.

In chapter twenty one, Ahab wanted Naboth’s vineyard, and Naboth said, the LORD forbid it me, that I should give the inheritance of my fathers. Jezebel had Naboth falsely accused and put to death. But Elijah met Ahab in the vineyard and spoke God’s judgment. And when Ahab humbled himself, God showed him mercy, and delayed the disaster.

Now we come to the last chapter of the first book of Kings. Listen for the one prophet who will only speak God’s truth. And listen for how Ahab’s story ends.

Jehoshaphat, the good king of Judah, comes to visit Ahab. And Ahab says, the Syrians still hold our city of Ramoth-gilead. Will you go to battle with me? And Jehoshaphat agrees.

But then Jehoshaphat adds, first, please, ask what the word of the LORD is.

So Ahab gathers his prophets, about four hundred men. Shall I go to war against Ramoth-gilead, or not? Go up, they say, for the Lord will give it to the king.

But these were Ahab’s court prophets, paid to please him. Is there not a prophet of the LORD here, he asks, that we could ask?

And Ahab says, there is one man, Micaiah, the son of Imlah. 'but I hate him; for he doth not prophesy good concerning me, but evil.' Jehoshaphat says, the king should not say that. So they send for Micaiah.

Meanwhile Zedekiah, with his iron horns, says, with these you will push the Syrians until they are destroyed. And the messenger who fetches Micaiah tells him, please, make your words agree with the others.

But Micaiah answers.

When Micaiah arrives, Ahab asks him, shall we go to war against Ramoth-gilead? And at first, Micaiah says exactly what the others said. Go, and win. But he must have said it in a way that made it obvious he did not mean it. Because Ahab snaps back, how many times must I make you swear to tell me nothing but the truth in the name of the LORD?

So Micaiah tells him the truth.

Ahab turns to Jehoshaphat. Did I not tell you he would prophesy nothing good about me?

Then Micaiah describes a vision. I saw the LORD sitting on his throne, with all the host of heaven standing around him. And the LORD asked, who will persuade Ahab to go up and fall at Ramoth-gilead? And a spirit said, I will be a lying spirit in the mouth of all his prophets. And the LORD said, go and do so. So now, Micaiah says, the LORD has allowed a lying spirit to speak through all these prophets of yours, and the LORD has spoken disaster for you.

This is a hard vision, and Christians have understood it in different ways. But its message to Ahab was clear. You have chosen to listen to lies. Now the lies will lead you to your end. And Micaiah told him plainly, so that he could still choose to turn back.

Zedekiah walks up and slaps Micaiah on the cheek. And Ahab says, put this man in prison, and give him only bread and water, until I come back safely. And Micaiah answers, 'If thou return at all in peace, the LORD hath not spoken by me.'

So the two kings go up to Ramoth-gilead. And Ahab has a plan. I will disguise myself, he tells Jehoshaphat, but you wear your royal robes. As if he could trick God’s word.

The Syrian chariots are told to fight only the king of Israel. They chase Jehoshaphat in his robes, until he cries out, and they see it is not Ahab.

And then it happens.

Ahab is propped up in his chariot through the battle, and by the end of the day he dies. They bring his body back to Samaria and bury him there. Everything happened just as the LORD had said through Elijah and through Micaiah.

The chapter ends with Jehoshaphat in Judah doing what is right in the eyes of the LORD, and Ahab’s son Ahaziah in Israel serving Baal, like his father and mother.

And so the first book of Kings ends. It began with the wisest king in the world, and a golden temple. It ends with a wicked king, dying after refusing God’s word. Through it all, God kept speaking through his prophets, calling his people back.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 14. 'And Micaiah said, As the LORD liveth, what the LORD saith unto me, that will I speak.' Four hundred against one. Everyone was telling him to go along with the crowd. But Micaiah would only say what God said, even if it meant being slapped and thrown into prison. It takes courage to speak the truth when everyone else is saying something easier. Faithfulness to God matters more than being popular.

Verse 17. 'And he said, I saw all Israel scattered upon the hills, as sheep that have not a shepherd: and the LORD said, These have no master: let them return every man to his house in peace.' Sheep without a shepherd. Micaiah was telling Ahab that he would die in the battle, and his army would scatter. Israel’s king was supposed to be their shepherd, like David. Ahab had failed them. Many years later, Jesus looked at the crowds, and had compassion on them, because they were like sheep without a shepherd. And he said, I am the good shepherd.

Verse 34. 'And a certain man drew a bow at a venture, and smote the king of Israel between the joints of the harness: wherefore he said unto the driver of his chariot, Turn thine hand, and carry me out of the host; for I am wounded.' At a venture means at random. The soldier was not aiming at Ahab. He did not even know who he was. But that one random arrow found the tiny gap between the pieces of Ahab’s armour. Ahab’s disguise could not hide him from God. What looks like chance to us is never outside God’s hand.

Solomon wrote, 'Faithful are the wounds of a friend; but the kisses of an enemy are deceitful.' And Paul wrote, 'Be not deceived; God is not mocked: for whatsoever a man soweth, that shall he also reap.'

Do you mostly listen to people who tell you what you want to hear? Ask God to give you friends like Micaiah, who will tell you the truth in love. And ask for the courage to speak the truth yourself, even when it is not popular.

Here is today’s question. Ahab hated Micaiah because he told the truth. How do you respond when someone tells you something true that you do not want to hear?

Let us pray. Lord God, thank you for walking with me through the first book of Kings. Thank you that you kept speaking to your people through your prophets, and that your word always comes true. Give me the courage of Micaiah, to speak only what you say. Keep me from loving comfortable lies. And thank you for Jesus, the good shepherd, and the true King who will never fail. Amen.
