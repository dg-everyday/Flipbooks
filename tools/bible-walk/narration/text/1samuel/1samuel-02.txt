The Living Voice. 1 Samuel, chapter 2. Them That Honour Me I Will Honour.

A mother has just handed over her little boy to live at the house of God. You might expect her to cry. Instead, she sings. And her song is so full of joy and power that, many years later, another young mother named Mary will sing a song very much like it.

In chapter one, Hannah longed for a child, and was taunted year after year. She poured out her soul before the LORD at Shiloh, and promised that if God gave her a son, she would give him back to the LORD. God remembered her, and she named her son Samuel. When he was old enough, she brought him to Eli and said, 'For this child I prayed;' 'I have lent him to the LORD'.

Now, in chapter two, we hear Hannah’s song. Then we see two very different kinds of young men at the house of God. Listen for the contrast between Samuel and the sons of Eli.

Hannah prays, and her prayer is a song.

'My heart rejoiceth in the LORD,' she sings. 'because I rejoice in thy salvation.'

Then she says something about God himself.

Then Hannah sings about how God turns things upside down. Do not talk proudly any more, she says. The LORD is a God of knowledge, and he weighs every action. The bows of the mighty are broken, and those who stumbled are given strength. Those who were full now work for bread, and those who were hungry are hungry no more. 'The LORD maketh poor, and maketh rich: he bringeth low, and lifteth up.' He raises the poor from the dust, and lifts the needy from the ash heap, to sit with princes.

Hannah knows this from her own life. She was the one who was looked down on. And God lifted her up.

And she ends with something surprising. The LORD 'shall give strength unto his king, and exalt the horn of his anointed.' Israel has no king yet. But Hannah looks ahead to a king God will raise up. Her son Samuel will one day anoint that king.

Then Elkanah goes home to Ramah. And the boy Samuel serves the LORD, before Eli the priest.

But now the story turns to Eli’s own sons.

'Now the sons of Eli were sons of Belial; they knew not the LORD.' Sons of Belial means worthless men. They were priests, serving at the house of God. And they did not know God.

When people came to offer sacrifices, the priests were given a share of the meat. But Eli’s sons were not content with that. Their servant would come while the meat was boiling, with a three pronged fork, and stab it into the pot, and whatever it brought up, the priest kept. Even worse, before the fat was burned for the LORD, the servant would demand raw meat for the priests. And if anyone said, let the fat be burned first, then take what you want, the servant would say, no, give it to me now, or I will take it by force.

The writer tells us, the sin of the young men was very great before the LORD, because they treated the LORD’s offering with contempt.

But in the middle of this, there is a little boy.

'But Samuel ministered before the LORD, being a child, girded with a linen ephod.' And every year, his mother makes him a little coat, and brings it to him when she comes with her husband to offer the yearly sacrifice.

Can you picture Hannah, sewing a little coat every year, a little bigger each time, thinking of her son as she works?

Eli blesses Elkanah and Hannah, and prays that God will give them more children, in place of the one they gave to the LORD. And the LORD visits Hannah, and she has three more sons and two daughters. Hannah gave one child to God, and God gave her five more.

Meanwhile Eli is very old. He hears of all the evil his sons are doing, including shameful sins with the women who served at the door of the tabernacle. He says to them, why do you do such things? It is not a good report that I hear. But his sons do not listen. And Eli does nothing more.

And again, in contrast, the writer tells us about Samuel.

Then a man of God comes to Eli with a hard message. God chose your father’s family to be priests, he says. Why do you despise my sacrifices? Why do you honour your sons more than me, and make yourselves fat with the best of the offerings?

God had promised that Eli’s family would serve him for ever. But now God says, that promise depended on faithfulness.

And the man of God gives a sign. Both your sons, Hophni and Phinehas, will die on the same day. And God says, I will raise up for myself a faithful priest, who will do what is in my heart and mind.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 2. 'There is none holy as the LORD: for there is none beside thee: neither is there any rock like our God.' Hannah’s song is not mainly about Samuel. It is about God. There is no one holy like him. No one else is God. And he is a rock, strong and unchanging, someone you can stand on. When everything else shakes, the LORD is a rock.

Verse 26. 'And the child Samuel grew on, and was in favour both with the LORD, and also with men.' Samuel grew up surrounded by bad examples. The priests around him were wicked. And yet he grew in favour with God and with people. Our surroundings matter, but they do not decide everything. Many years later, the Gospel of Luke would say something very similar about the boy Jesus.

Verse 30. 'Wherefore the LORD God of Israel saith, I said indeed that thy house, and the house of thy father, should walk before me for ever: but now the LORD saith, Be it far from me; for them that honour me I will honour, and they that despise me shall be lightly esteemed.' Them that honour me I will honour. This is one of the great principles of the Bible. Eli honoured his sons above God. He let them do evil, because he did not want to stand up to them. But God is to be honoured first. Those who honour God, God will honour.

Mary sang, 'He hath put down the mighty from their seats, and exalted them of low degree.' And Luke wrote of the boy Jesus, 'And Jesus increased in wisdom and stature, and in favour with God and man.'

How can you honour God today, in a way others may not see? It might be in how you work, how you speak, or how you give. Put God first in one choice today, even if it costs you something.

Here is today’s question. Samuel grew in favour with God, even though the priests around him were wicked. What helps a young person stay faithful when the people around them are not?

Let us pray. Lord God, there is none holy like you, and there is no rock like you. Thank you that you lift up the lowly. Forgive me for the times I have honoured others, or myself, above you. Help me to honour you first, in everything I do. Amen.
