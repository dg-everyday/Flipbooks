The Living Voice. 1 Samuel, chapter 9. Behold the Man Whom I Spake to Thee Of.

A young man and his servant have been walking for days, searching the hills for some lost donkeys. They have found nothing. Their food is gone. The young man is ready to give up and go home. He has no idea that, at that very moment, an old prophet in the next town is waiting for him, with the best seat at the table saved just for him.

In chapter eight, Samuel was old, and his sons took bribes. The elders said, make us a king to judge us like all the nations. Samuel was displeased, but the LORD said, they have not rejected thee, but they have rejected me. Samuel warned them that a king would take and take. But the people said, we will have a king, that we may be like all the nations. And the LORD said, make them a king.

Now, in chapter nine, we meet the man who will become Israel’s first king. Listen for how ordinary the story is. And listen for how God is guiding every step.

There is a man of the tribe of Benjamin named Kish, a man of wealth and standing. He has a son named Saul.

Saul is a choice young man, and handsome. There is no one in Israel more impressive. He stands head and shoulders above everyone else.

Remember that the people wanted a king like the nations. Saul looks exactly like the king they imagined.

One day, Kish’s donkeys go missing. And Kish says to Saul, take one of the servants with you, and go and look for the donkeys.

So Saul and the servant search through the hill country of Ephraim, and the land around. But they do not find them. They search the land of Benjamin. Still nothing.

When they reach the land of Zuph, Saul says, come, let us go home, or my father will stop worrying about the donkeys and start worrying about us.

But the servant has an idea. There is a man of God in this town, he says. He is highly respected, and everything he says comes true. Let us go to him. Perhaps he can tell us which way to go.

Saul says, but what can we give him? Our bread is gone. We have no gift for the man of God.

And the servant says, I have a quarter of a shekel of silver. I will give it to the man of God, and he will tell us the way.

Notice who has the faith in this story. It is the servant, not Saul. Saul does not even seem to know who Samuel is.

So they go up the hill toward the town. And they meet some young women coming out to draw water. Is the seer here, they ask. A seer was the old word for a prophet. Yes, the girls say. He has just come to town, because there is a sacrifice for the people today, at the high place. Hurry, and you will find him before he goes up to eat. The people will not eat until he comes, because he blesses the sacrifice.

So they go into the town. And there is Samuel, coming out toward them, on his way up to the high place.

Now the writer lets us in on a secret. The day before, the LORD had spoken to Samuel.

When Samuel sees Saul, the LORD speaks again.

Saul comes up to Samuel in the gateway, and asks, please tell me, where is the seer’s house?

Samuel says, I am the seer. Go up ahead of me to the high place, for you will eat with me today. 'and to morrow I will let thee go,' and tell you everything that is on your mind. And as for the donkeys you lost three days ago, do not worry about them. They have been found. And then Samuel says something mysterious. And who is all the desire of Israel for? Is it not for you, and for all your father’s family?

Saul is astonished.

Samuel takes Saul and his servant into the hall, and seats them at the head of the table, among about thirty guests. And he says to the cook, bring the portion I gave you, and told you to set aside. The cook brings the best piece of meat, and sets it before Saul. Samuel says, this was kept for you, for this very moment.

Imagine Saul’s surprise. A stranger in town, looking for donkeys, and the great prophet has saved him the best seat and the best food.

Saul eats with Samuel that day. Then they go down into the town, and Samuel talks with Saul on the flat roof of the house.

Early the next day, Samuel calls Saul on the roof, and says, get up, and I will send you on your way. And as they walk to the edge of the town, Samuel says, tell your servant to go on ahead. But you stand here a while, so that I may tell you the word of God.

Saul set out to find his father’s donkeys. He never found them. But God found him.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 16. 'To morrow about this time I will send thee a man out of the land of Benjamin, and thou shalt anoint him to be captain over my people Israel, that he may save my people out of the hand of the Philistines: for I have looked upon my people, because their cry is come unto me.' Notice God’s heart. The people had sinned in asking for a king. And yet God says, I have looked upon my people, because their cry is come unto me. God heard their suffering under the Philistines. Even when we choose wrongly, God still cares for us.

Verse 17. 'And when Samuel saw Saul, the LORD said unto him, Behold the man whom I spake to thee of! this same shall reign over my people.' Lost donkeys, a servant’s idea, girls at the well, perfect timing at the gate. None of it was chance. God was guiding every step to bring Saul to Samuel. God is at work in the ordinary details of our lives, even when we cannot see it.

Verse 21. 'And Saul answered and said, Am not I a Benjamite, of the smallest of the tribes of Israel? and my family the least of all the families of the tribe of Benjamin? wherefore then speakest thou so to me?' At the beginning, Saul is humble. He sees himself as small. Remember that Benjamin was almost wiped out at the end of Judges. Gideon said something very similar. This humility was a good start. The tragedy of Saul’s story is that he would not stay humble.

A proverb says, 'A man’s heart deviseth his way: but the LORD directeth his steps.' And Jesus said, 'For the Son of man is come to seek and to save that which was lost.'

Think back over the past week. Can you see any small, ordinary moments where God might have been guiding you? Thank him for his care in the details of your life.

Here is today’s question. Saul went looking for donkeys and found his calling instead. Has God ever led you somewhere unexpected through ordinary events?

Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that you direct my steps, even in ordinary things. Thank you that you hear the cry of your people. Keep me humble, like Saul at the beginning. And help me to trust that you are guiding me, even when I cannot see where the road leads. Amen.
