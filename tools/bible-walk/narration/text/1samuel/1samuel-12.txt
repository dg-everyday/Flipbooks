The Living Voice. 1 Samuel, chapter 12. God Forbid That I Should Cease to Pray for You.

An old man stands before a whole nation, and asks them a question that few leaders would dare to ask. Have I ever cheated any of you? Have I ever taken anything that was not mine? Have I ever taken a bribe? If I have, tell me now, and I will pay it back. And the whole nation answers: no. Not once.

In chapter eleven, Nahash the Ammonite threatened Jabesh-gilead with a cruel demand. The Spirit of God came upon Saul, and he gathered Israel as one man and rescued the town. When the people wanted to punish those who had mocked him, Saul said, to day the LORD hath wrought salvation in Israel. And at Gilgal they made Saul king before the LORD, and rejoiced greatly.

Now, in chapter twelve, Samuel gives his farewell speech as Israel’s leader. Listen for how Samuel has lived. And listen for the promise he makes to the people at the end.

Samuel speaks to all Israel. I have listened to you, he says, and given you a king. Now the king walks before you. And I am old and grey. I have walked before you from my childhood until today.

Then Samuel asks his question. Here I am. Bear witness against me, before the LORD and before his anointed king. Whose ox have I taken? Whose donkey? Whom have I cheated? Whom have I oppressed? From whom have I taken a bribe? Tell me, and I will make it right.

And the people answer, you have not cheated us or oppressed us, and you have not taken anything from anyone.

Samuel says, the LORD is witness, and his anointed is witness, that you have found nothing in my hand. And they say, he is witness.

What a testimony. Samuel’s own sons took bribes. But Samuel himself had served the people his whole life with complete honesty. He could look a whole nation in the eye.

Then Samuel reminds the people of their story. Stand still, he says, and let me remind you of all the righteous acts of the LORD.

When Jacob went down into Egypt, and your fathers cried to the LORD, the LORD sent Moses and Aaron, who brought them out and settled them in this land. But they forgot the LORD their God, and he let their enemies rule over them: Sisera, the Philistines, the king of Moab. Then they cried to the LORD, we have sinned, deliver us and we will serve you. And the LORD sent deliverers, Gideon, Jephthah, and others, and you lived in safety.

Samuel is reminding them of the book of Judges. The same old circle.

Then he says, but when Nahash the Ammonite came against you, you said to me, no, we want a king to reign over us, 'when the LORD your God was your king.'

Now here is the king you have chosen, and asked for. And the LORD has set a king over you. If you will fear the LORD, and serve him, and obey his voice, then both you and your king will do well. But if you rebel, the hand of the LORD will be against you, as it was against your fathers.

Then Samuel does something dramatic. Is it not wheat harvest today, he asks. I will call on the LORD, and he will send thunder and rain.

In Israel, it almost never rained at wheat harvest. It was the dry season. A thunderstorm then would be like snow in summer.

So Samuel calls to the LORD, and the LORD sends thunder and rain that day. And all the people are terrified of the LORD and of Samuel.

The people say, pray for us to the LORD your God, so that we do not die. We have added to all our sins this evil, asking for a king.

And Samuel answers them tenderly. Fear not. You have done all this wrong. But do not turn aside from following the LORD. Serve the LORD with all your heart. Do not turn aside to empty things that cannot help or save you, because they are empty.

And then he speaks of God’s faithfulness, and of his own.

But Samuel ends with a warning. If you keep on doing evil, you and your king will be swept away.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 22. 'For the LORD will not forsake his people for his great name’s sake: because it hath pleased the LORD to make you his people.' Here is the reason for hope. Not because Israel was good. They had just admitted their sin. But because it pleased the LORD to make them his people. God’s love for his people does not depend on how good they are. It depends on him, and on his great name. He will not forsake his own.

Verse 23. 'Moreover as for me, God forbid that I should sin against the LORD in ceasing to pray for you: but I will teach you the good and the right way:' Samuel had every reason to be hurt. The people had rejected his leadership. But he says, God forbid that I should stop praying for you. He calls it a sin not to pray for them. Praying for others is not just a nice extra. It is part of loving them. And Samuel will keep teaching them the right way.

Verse 24. 'Only fear the LORD, and serve him in truth with all your heart: for consider how great things he hath done for you.' Consider how great things he hath done for you. That is the reason to serve God with all our heart. Not out of fear of punishment, but out of gratitude for all he has done. When we remember God’s goodness, serving him becomes a joy.

Paul wrote to the Colossians, 'For this cause we also, since the day we heard it, do not cease to pray for you'. And a psalm says, 'The LORD hath done great things for us; whereof we are glad.'

Is there someone you have stopped praying for, perhaps because they hurt you or disappointed you? Pray for them by name today. And take a moment to consider the great things God has done for you.

Here is today’s question. Samuel said it would be a sin against the LORD to stop praying for the people. Who has God placed in your life to pray for faithfully?

Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that you will not forsake your people, for your great name’s sake. Thank you for the great things you have done for me. Help me to serve you in truth, with all my heart. And God forbid that I should cease to pray for those you have placed in my life. Amen.
