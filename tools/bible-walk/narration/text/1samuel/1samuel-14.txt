The Living Voice. 1 Samuel, chapter 14. No Restraint to the LORD to Save by Many or by Few.

Two young men are climbing a cliff, on their hands and feet, up a narrow pass between two sharp rocks. At the top is an enemy outpost, full of soldiers. Behind them, Israel’s army is sitting under a tree, doing nothing. No one knows the two young men have gone. One of them is the king’s son. And he believes that God can win the battle, even with just two.

In chapter thirteen, the Philistines gathered an army like the sand on the seashore. Saul waited at Gilgal for Samuel, but on the seventh day he offered the sacrifice himself. Samuel said, thou hast done foolishly. The LORD hath sought him a man after his own heart. Saul was left with six hundred men, and only he and Jonathan had swords.

Now, in chapter fourteen, Jonathan steps out in faith. Listen for what Jonathan believes about God. And listen for the rash oath his father makes.

One day Jonathan, the son of Saul, says to the young man who carries his armour, come, let us go over to the Philistine outpost on the other side. But he does not tell his father.

Saul is sitting under a pomegranate tree at the edge of Gibeah, with his six hundred men. With him is a priest, wearing the ephod, a great grandson of Eli.

Between Jonathan and the Philistine outpost is a steep pass, with a sharp rock on each side.

And Jonathan says something wonderful to his armour bearer.

And the armour bearer answers.

Jonathan says, we will show ourselves to the men there. If they say, wait there until we come to you, then we will stay where we are. But if they say, come up to us, then we will go up, because that will be the sign that the LORD has given them into our hands.

So the two of them show themselves to the Philistines. And the Philistines laugh. Look, the Hebrews are crawling out of the holes where they have been hiding. And they call out, come up to us, and we will teach you a lesson!

That is the sign. Jonathan says to his armour bearer, come up after me, for the LORD has given them into the hand of Israel.

Jonathan climbs up on his hands and feet, with his armour bearer behind him. And the Philistines fall before Jonathan.

Then the ground shakes. Panic spreads through the whole Philistine army, in the camp and in the field. The earth trembles. It is a panic sent from God.

Saul’s lookouts see the huge Philistine army melting away, running in every direction. Saul counts his men, and finds Jonathan and his armour bearer missing. And the Philistines are so confused that they turn their swords against each other.

Then Saul and all his men join the battle. Hebrews who had gone over to the Philistines come back to Israel. Men who had been hiding in the hills of Ephraim come out and join the chase.

'So the LORD saved Israel that day'.

But then Saul does something foolish. He makes the people swear an oath. 'Cursed be the man that eateth any food until evening, that I may be avenged on mine enemies.' So the soldiers fight all day with nothing to eat, and they are exhausted.

Notice Saul’s words. 'that I may be avenged on mine enemies.' Not the LORD’s enemies. His own.

The army comes into a wood, and there is honey dripping on the ground. But no one dares to eat it, because of the oath. Jonathan, however, had not heard his father’s oath. He dips the end of his staff into a honeycomb, and eats, and his eyes brighten.

Someone tells him about the oath. And Jonathan says, my father has troubled the land. See how my eyes have brightened because I tasted a little honey. How much greater would the victory have been if the people had eaten.

Later, the people are so faint with hunger that they seize the animals they have captured and eat them in a way God’s law forbade. Saul’s rash oath has led the people into sin.

Then Saul wants to chase the Philistines through the dark. But God does not answer when Saul asks him. So Saul decides that someone has sinned. And he swears, even if it is my son Jonathan, he shall surely die.

Lots are cast, and Jonathan is chosen. Saul asks him what he has done, and Jonathan says, I tasted a little honey, and now I must die.

And Saul says, you shall surely die, Jonathan.

But the people stand up for Jonathan.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 6. 'And Jonathan said to the young man that bare his armour, Come, and let us go over unto the garrison' of the Philistines, 'it may be that the LORD will work for us: for there is no restraint to the LORD to save by many or by few.' No restraint means nothing holding him back. God does not need a big army. He can save with many or with few, even with two. Jonathan’s faith was not certain of the outcome. It may be, he said. But he was certain of God’s power. That is real faith. Trusting God enough to step out, even when we do not know exactly what he will do.

Verse 7. 'And his armourbearer said unto him, Do all that is in thine heart: turn thee; behold, I am with thee according to thy heart.' We do not even know this young man’s name. But what a friend. I am with thee, heart and soul. Jonathan’s faith inspired his friend to follow. And his friend’s loyalty gave Jonathan courage. Faith is easier when we have someone beside us.

Verse 45. 'And the people said unto Saul, Shall Jonathan die, who hath wrought this great salvation in Israel? God forbid: as the LORD liveth, there shall not one hair of his head fall to the ground; for he hath wrought with God this day. So the people rescued Jonathan, that he died not.' He hath wrought with God this day. The people saw clearly what Saul could not. Jonathan had worked with God, and God had given the victory. Saul’s rash oath would have cost the life of the very one God had used. Sometimes our pride and anger blind us to what God is doing.

Zechariah heard God say, 'Not by might, nor by power, but by my spirit, saith the LORD of hosts.' And a proverb says, 'Two are better than one'.

Is there a step of faith you have been holding back from, because you feel too few or too small? Remember Jonathan’s words: there is no restraint to the LORD to save by many or by few. And think of a friend you could stand beside in faith.

Here is today’s question. Jonathan said, it may be that the LORD will work for us. What is the difference between being certain of the outcome, and being certain of God?

Let us pray. Lord God, there is no restraint to you to save by many or by few. Give me faith like Jonathan’s, to step out and trust you. Thank you for friends who stand beside me. Keep me from rash words and promises, and help me to see clearly what you are doing. Amen.
