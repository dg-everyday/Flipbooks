The Living Voice. 1 Samuel, chapter 22. With Me Thou Shalt Be in Safeguard.

A cave in the hills is filling up with people. Not soldiers or nobles, but people in trouble. People who owe money they cannot pay. People who are bitter and fed up with life. People with nowhere else to go. They have all come for the same reason: to follow a young man who is himself on the run. And he becomes their leader.

In chapter twenty one, David fled alone to Nob, and lied to Ahimelech the priest, who gave him the holy bread and the sword of Goliath. But Doeg the Edomite, one of Saul’s servants, was there and saw it all. David then fled to Gath, and pretended to be mad to escape. And in his fear, he cried to the LORD, and was heard.

Now, in chapter twenty two, the story shows two very different leaders. Listen for the people who gather to David. And listen for what Saul does when he hears where David has been. This chapter has a dark moment, which we will tell gently.

David escapes to the cave of Adullam. When his brothers and his father’s family hear about it, they come down to join him.

And others come too.

Then David goes to Moab, and asks the king of Moab, please let my father and mother stay with you, until I know what God will do for me. So they stay with the king of Moab.

Remember that David’s great grandmother was Ruth, a woman from Moab. Perhaps that is why David trusted the king of Moab to look after his parents.

Then a prophet named Gad speaks to David. Do not stay in the stronghold. Go into the land of Judah. So David leaves, and goes to the forest of Hareth.

Meanwhile, Saul hears that David and his men have been found. Saul is sitting under a tree on the hill at Gibeah, with his spear in his hand, and all his officers standing around him.

And Saul speaks to them, full of self pity. Listen, men of Benjamin. Will the son of Jesse give every one of you fields and vineyards, and make you commanders? Is that why you have all plotted against me? No one tells me that my son has made a covenant with the son of Jesse. None of you is sorry for me.

Saul sees enemies everywhere. Even his own officers. Even his own son. Jealousy and fear have made him suspicious of everyone.

Then Doeg the Edomite, standing among Saul’s servants, speaks up. I saw the son of Jesse at Nob, he says, with Ahimelech the priest. Ahimelech asked the LORD for guidance for him, gave him food, and gave him the sword of Goliath the Philistine.

So Saul sends for Ahimelech and all the priests of his family at Nob, and they come to the king.

Saul says, why have you plotted against me with the son of Jesse? You gave him bread and a sword, and asked God for him, so that he could rise up against me.

And Ahimelech answers honestly.

Ahimelech goes on. I did not know anything about any of this, small or great. Do not blame your servant or my family.

But Saul says, you will surely die, Ahimelech, you and all your father’s family.

Saul orders the guards standing around him to put the priests of the LORD to death. But the king’s servants will not lift a hand against the priests of the LORD.

Those guards showed real courage. They refused the king’s command, because it was wicked.

So Saul turns to Doeg. And Doeg the Edomite does what no Israelite would do. Many priests of the LORD lose their lives that day. And Doeg brings destruction on the whole town of Nob.

It is one of the darkest things Saul ever did. He turned against the priests of the very God who had made him king.

But one of Ahimelech’s sons, named Abiathar, escapes, and flees to David. He tells David that Saul has put the LORD’s priests to death.

And David says, I knew it, that day when Doeg the Edomite was there. I knew he would tell Saul. I have caused the death of your whole family.

David takes responsibility. He does not make excuses. His lie at Nob, told out of fear, had played a part in this terrible tragedy. And he admits it.

And David says to Abiathar.

From that day, Abiathar the priest stays with David. And he brings with him the ephod, so that David can ask the LORD for guidance.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 2. 'And every one that was in distress, and every one that was in debt, and every one that was discontented, gathered themselves unto him; and he became a captain over them: and there were with him about four hundred men.' The distressed, the indebted, the discontented. Not the kind of people you would choose for an army. But David took them in, and became their leader. And many of them would become his mighty men. It is a picture of Jesus, who welcomes the weary and burdened, and makes them his people.

Verse 14. 'Then Ahimelech answered the king, and said, And who is so faithful among all thy servants as David, which is the king’s son in law, and goeth at thy bidding, and is honourable in thine house?' Even facing death, Ahimelech spoke the truth about David. Who is so faithful as David? He did not try to save himself by blaming David. He told the truth, even to an angry king. Honesty takes courage, especially when it costs us.

Verse 23. 'Abide thou with me, fear not: for he that seeketh my life seeketh thy life: but with me thou shalt be in safeguard.' Abide with me. Fear not. With me you will be safe. David, the fugitive, offers safety to the grieving young priest. Even in his own danger, David cares for someone else. These words are a picture of what Jesus says to all who come to him. Stay with me. Do not be afraid. With me you are safe.

Jesus said, 'Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest.' And a psalm says, 'He that dwelleth in the secret place of the most High shall abide under the shadow of the Almighty.'

Do you know someone who is in distress, in debt, or discontented? Welcome them today, as David did. And if you are the one in distress, hear the words of Jesus: come unto me, and I will give you rest.

Here is today’s question. David took responsibility, saying, I have occasioned the death of all the persons of thy father’s house. Why is it important to admit our part when our choices hurt others?

Let us pray. Lord Jesus, thank you that you welcome the distressed and the weary. Thank you that with you I am safe. When my choices hurt others, help me to admit it honestly. And make me a person who welcomes and protects those in trouble. Amen.
