The Living Voice. 1 Samuel, chapter 24. Mine Hand Shall Not Be upon Thee.

A man is hiding deep in the darkness of a cave, with hundreds of his men pressed against the walls around him. And then the man who has been hunting him for years walks in, alone, and unaware. The king is completely at his mercy. His men are whispering, this is it. God has given him to you. And the man creeps forward in the dark, with a blade in his hand.

In chapter twenty three, David asked the LORD, shall I go? and saved the town of Keilah. Saul sought him every day, but God delivered him not into his hand. Jonathan came to David in the forest and strengthened his hand in God. And at the rock of escape, just as Saul was closing in, a messenger called him away to fight the Philistines.

Now, in chapter twenty four, David has the chance to end it all. Listen for what David does with that chance. And listen for what he says to Saul afterwards.

When Saul comes back from fighting the Philistines, he is told that David is in the wilderness of En-gedi. So Saul takes three thousand chosen men from all Israel, and goes to search for David among the rocks of the wild goats.

He comes to the sheepfolds by the road, where there is a cave. And Saul goes into the cave alone, for a private moment. But deep inside the cave, in the darkness, David and his men are hiding.

David’s men whisper to him. This is the day the LORD told you about, when he said, I will give your enemy into your hand, and you can do to him whatever you think best.

It seemed so obvious. Saul was right there, alone, helpless. Surely this was God’s doing. And David creeps up, and quietly cuts off a corner of Saul’s robe.

But afterwards, David’s conscience troubles him, because he has cut off the corner of Saul’s robe.

It was only a piece of cloth. No one was hurt. And yet David felt he had gone too far. He had treated the king with disrespect. David had a very tender conscience.

And David says to his men.

So David stops his men with these words, and does not let them attack Saul. And Saul gets up, leaves the cave, and goes on his way.

Then David goes out of the cave after him, and calls out, my lord the king! When Saul looks back, David bows down with his face to the ground.

Imagine Saul’s shock. The man he has been hunting is right behind him, bowing to him.

David says, why do you listen to people who say that David wants to harm you? Today you have seen with your own eyes that the LORD gave you into my hands in the cave. Some told me to take your life. But I spared you. I said, I will not lift my hand against my lord, for he is the LORD’s anointed.

And David holds up the piece of cloth. My father, look at this, the corner of your robe in my hand. I cut it off, but I did not harm you. Now you can see that I have done nothing wrong against you. And yet you are hunting me, to take my life.

Then David leaves it with God.

David goes on. As the old proverb says, wickedness comes from the wicked. But my hand will not be against you. Who is the king of Israel chasing? A dead dog? A flea?

David speaks of himself as nothing, as harmless as a dead dog or a flea. Why would the king of Israel waste his life hunting someone so small?

The LORD be judge, he says. May he see, and plead my cause, and rescue me from your hand.

When David finishes speaking, Saul says, is this your voice, my son David? And Saul weeps aloud.

And Saul admits the truth.

Saul says, you have shown me today how good you have been to me. The LORD gave me into your hand, and you did not harm me. If a man finds his enemy, does he let him go safely? May the LORD reward you well for what you did for me today.

And now I know for certain that you will be king, and that the kingdom of Israel will be established in your hands. So swear to me by the LORD that you will not cut off my descendants, or wipe out my name from my father’s family.

And David swears it to Saul.

Then Saul goes home. But David and his men go back up to the stronghold. David knew that Saul’s tears might not last.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 6. 'And he said unto his men, The LORD forbid that I should do this thing unto my master, the LORD’S anointed, to stretch forth mine hand against him, seeing he is the anointed of the LORD.' David had been anointed king too. But he would not take the throne by force. He would wait for God to give it to him, in God’s way and God’s time. David respected Saul, not because Saul deserved it, but because God had placed him there. It takes great faith to wait for God, instead of grabbing what he has promised.

Verse 12. 'The LORD judge between me and thee, and the LORD avenge me of thee: but mine hand shall not be upon thee.' David left justice to God. He did not pretend Saul had done nothing wrong. But he refused to take revenge himself. The LORD will judge. Mine hand shall not be upon thee. When someone wrongs us, we can hand them over to God, who judges rightly, and let go of our own desire for revenge.

Verse 17. 'And he said to David, Thou art more righteous than I: for thou hast rewarded me good, whereas I have rewarded thee evil.' Saul was overcome by David’s kindness. Good for evil. That is what melted Saul’s heart, at least for a moment. Returning good for evil does not always change our enemies. But it is the way of God, and it sometimes breaks through where nothing else can.

Paul wrote, 'Dearly beloved, avenge not yourselves, but rather give place unto wrath: for it is written, Vengeance is mine; I will repay, saith the Lord.' And Jesus said, 'Love your enemies, bless them that curse you, do good to them that hate you'.

Is there someone you would like to get back at? Today, choose to hand them over to God. Ask him to help you do good to them, instead of evil, as David did for Saul.

Here is today’s question. David’s men said, this is the day the LORD told you about. How can we tell the difference between an opportunity from God, and a temptation that only looks like one?

Let us pray. Lord God, you are the judge of all the earth. Help me to leave justice in your hands, and to let go of revenge. Give me a tender conscience, like David’s. And help me to wait for your timing, and to return good for evil, even to those who hurt me. Amen.
