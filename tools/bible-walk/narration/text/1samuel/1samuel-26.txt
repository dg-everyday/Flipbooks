The Living Voice. 1 Samuel, chapter 26. I Have Played the Fool.

Three thousand soldiers lie asleep in a wide circle in the dark. In the very middle, the king is sleeping, his great spear stuck in the ground beside his head. And two shadowy figures are creeping silently between the sleeping men, closer and closer to the king. One of them whispers, let me pin him to the ground with his own spear. Just one blow. I will not need a second.

In chapter twenty five, Samuel died. Nabal insulted David, and David strapped on his sword to take revenge. But Abigail rode out with gifts, and wise words, reminding David that his soul was bound in the bundle of life with the LORD. David said, blessed be thy advice, which hast kept me this day from avenging myself. The LORD struck Nabal, and Abigail became David’s wife.

Now, in chapter twenty six, David has a second chance to end Saul’s life. Listen for how David’s answer is the same as before. And listen for what Saul admits at the end.

The people of Ziph come to Saul again. Is not David hiding on the hill of Hachilah? So Saul goes down with three thousand chosen men to search for David in the wilderness of Ziph.

Remember, only two chapters ago, Saul wept and said, thou art more righteous than I. But his tears did not last. He is hunting David again.

David sends out spies, and learns that Saul has come. Then David goes to the place where Saul is camped. He sees where Saul and Abner, the commander of his army, are lying. Saul is sleeping inside the ring of the camp, with the soldiers all around him.

David asks, who will go down with me into Saul’s camp? And Abishai, the brother of Joab, says, I will go with you.

So David and Abishai go into the camp in the dark. And there is Saul, asleep, with his spear stuck in the ground near his head. And Abner and all the soldiers are lying asleep around him.

Abishai whispers to David. God has given your enemy into your hand today. Let me pin him to the ground with one thrust of the spear. I will not need to strike twice.

It is the same temptation as in the cave. And again, it comes with God’s name attached. God has given your enemy into your hand.

But David says no.

And David goes on. As the LORD lives, the LORD himself will deal with him. Either his time will come and he will die, or he will go into battle and fall. But the LORD forbid that I should lift my hand against the LORD’s anointed. Take the spear by his head, and the jar of water, and let us go.

So David takes the spear and the water jar from beside Saul’s head, and they leave. No one sees, no one knows, and no one wakes up. They are all asleep, because the LORD has sent a deep sleep upon them.

God was in control the whole time. The deep sleep came from the LORD. David did not need to fight his way out.

David crosses over to the other side, and stands on top of a hill, far away, with a wide distance between them. And he shouts to the army, and to Abner. Will you not answer, Abner?

Abner calls back, who are you, calling to the king?

David says, are you not a great man? Who is like you in Israel? Then why did you not guard your lord the king? Someone came in to destroy your king. You deserve to die, because you did not protect your master, the LORD’s anointed. Now look. Where is the king’s spear, and the water jar that was by his head?

Saul recognises David’s voice. Is that your voice, my son David?

And David says, it is my voice, my lord, O king. Why is my lord chasing his servant? What have I done? What evil is in my hand? The king of Israel has come out to search for a flea, like someone hunting a partridge in the mountains.

And then Saul speaks.

David answers, here is the king’s spear. Let one of your young men come over and get it.

Then David says.

Saul says, blessed are you, my son David. You will do great things, and you will surely succeed. So David goes on his way, and Saul goes back home.

It is the last time Saul and David will ever see each other.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 9. 'And David said to Abishai, Destroy him not: for who can stretch forth his hand against the LORD’S anointed, and be guiltless?' Twice now, David has had the perfect chance. Twice, someone has told him it was God’s will. And twice, David has said no. He would not take the throne by force. He trusted God to give it to him. Patience like this comes from deep trust in God’s timing.

Verse 21. 'Then said Saul, I have sinned: return, my son David: for I will no more do thee harm, because my soul was precious in thine eyes this day: behold, I have played the fool, and have erred exceedingly.' I have played the fool. What a sad confession. Saul knew exactly what he had done. He had wasted years hunting an innocent man, while the real enemies of Israel grew stronger. But knowing it was not enough. Saul never truly changed. It is possible to see our foolishness clearly, and still not turn from it.

Verse 23. 'The LORD render to every man his righteousness and his faithfulness: for the LORD delivered thee into my hand to day, but I would not stretch forth mine hand against the LORD’S anointed.' To render means to repay. David trusted that God sees and rewards faithfulness. He did not need Saul’s praise or promises. He left his life in God’s hands. The LORD render to every man his righteousness. God sees what we do in the dark, and he rewards faithfulness.

A psalm says, 'Rest in the LORD, and wait patiently for him: fret not thyself because of him who prospereth in his way'. And Peter wrote of Jesus, 'when he was reviled, reviled not again; when he suffered, he threatened not; but committed himself to him that judgeth righteously'.

Is there a situation where you are tempted to take matters into your own hands, instead of waiting for God? Commit it to him today, as David did. Trust that God sees, and will repay faithfulness.

Here is today’s question. Saul said, I have played the fool, but he never really changed. What is the difference between being sorry for what we have done, and truly turning away from it?

Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that you see everything, even in the dark, and that you reward faithfulness. Give me patience to wait for your timing, and to leave justice in your hands. Keep me from playing the fool. Help me not only to see my sin, but to turn from it. Amen.
