The Living Voice. 1 Samuel, chapter 30. David Encouraged Himself in the LORD His God.

Six hundred tired men come over the hill toward home. And they stop. Where their town should be, there is only smoke. The houses are burned. And their wives, their sons and their daughters are gone. The men weep until they cannot weep any more. And then their grief turns to anger. And they look at their leader.

In chapter twenty nine, David marched at the back of the Philistine army, about to fight his own people. But the Philistine commanders did not trust him, and Achish said, return, and go in peace. So David and his men rose up early, and went back to Ziklag, while the Philistines went up to Jezreel.

Now, in chapter thirty, David reaches the lowest point of his life. Listen for where he finds strength. And listen for how he treats the men who could not keep up.

On the third day, David and his men reach Ziklag. But the Amalekites have raided the south, and attacked Ziklag, and burned it. They have taken captive the women and everyone else in the town, small and great. They have not harmed anyone, but carried them all off, and gone on their way.

When David and his men see the town burned, and their wives and children taken, they lift up their voices and weep, until they have no strength left to weep. David’s own two wives, Ahinoam and Abigail, are among the captives.

And then it gets worse.

David was alone in his grief. His friends were turning against him. Everything had gone wrong. And much of it was the result of his own choices. He had brought these families to live in Philistine territory, and left the town unguarded.

But in his lowest moment, David turns back to God.

David says to Abiathar the priest, bring me the ephod. And David does what he had not done for a long time.

So David sets off with all six hundred men. They come to the brook Besor. But two hundred of the men are too exhausted to cross it. So David goes on with four hundred.

Out in the field, they find an Egyptian, and bring him to David. They give him bread to eat, water to drink, a piece of a cake of figs, and two clusters of raisins. After he has eaten, his strength comes back. He has had nothing to eat or drink for three days.

David asks, who do you belong to? Where are you from? He says, I am an Egyptian, the servant of an Amalekite. My master left me behind three days ago, because I fell sick. We raided the south, and burned Ziklag.

The Amalekite master had left his sick servant to die. But David’s men fed him and cared for him. And that act of kindness led them straight to their families.

David asks, can you lead me to this raiding party? The Egyptian says, swear to me by God that you will not harm me, or hand me back to my master, and I will lead you to them.

So he leads them down. And there are the Amalekites, spread out all over the land, eating, drinking and celebrating, because of all the plunder they have taken from the Philistines and from Judah.

David attacks them, 'from the twilight even unto the evening of the next day'. Only some young men on camels escape.

And David recovers everything the Amalekites had taken. He rescues his two wives. Nothing is missing, small or great, no sons, no daughters, no possessions. David recovers it all.

Then David comes back to the two hundred men who were too tired to cross the brook. They come out to meet him, and David greets them warmly.

But some of the wicked and worthless men who went with David say, they did not go with us, so we will not give them any of the plunder. They can take their wives and children and go.

And David answers.

David says, the share of the man who went down to battle shall be the same as the share of the man who stayed with the supplies. They shall share alike. And from that day on, it became a rule in Israel.

When David gets back to Ziklag, he sends some of the plunder as gifts to the elders of Judah, his friends, in town after town. Here is a gift for you, he says, from the plunder of the enemies of the LORD.

The man who had been hiding among the Philistines is beginning to turn back toward his own people. And they will soon need a king.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 6. 'And David was greatly distressed;' 'but David encouraged himself in the LORD his God.' The men were talking of turning on him, every man grieving for his sons and daughters. David had no one to encourage him. No Jonathan, no Samuel. So he encouraged himself in the LORD. He remembered who God was, and what God had promised. When everyone else turns away, we can still turn to God. And he is enough.

Verse 8. 'And David enquired at the LORD, saying, Shall I pursue after this troop? shall I overtake them? And he answered him, Pursue: for thou shalt surely overtake them, and without fail recover all.' For the first time in a long while, David asks God what to do. And God answers immediately, with a wonderful promise. Without fail recover all. David had wandered, and made poor choices. But the moment he turned back to God, God was there, ready to answer. It is never too late to turn back.

Verse 24. 'For who will hearken unto you in this matter? but as his part is that goeth down to the battle, so shall his part be that tarrieth by the stuff: they shall part alike.' David would not let the strong look down on the weak. The men who stayed behind, too exhausted to go on, would receive the same reward as those who fought. In God’s kingdom, everyone matters. Those who serve quietly behind the scenes are just as valued as those on the front line.

A psalm says, 'Why art thou cast down, O my soul? and why art thou disquieted in me? hope thou in God: for I shall yet praise him'. And Paul wrote, 'And whether one member suffer, all the members suffer with it; or one member be honoured, all the members rejoice with it.'

Are you discouraged today? Encourage yourself in the LORD your God. Remember who he is, and what he has done. And think of someone who serves quietly behind the scenes. Thank them, and let them know they matter.

Here is today’s question. David encouraged himself in the LORD when everyone else turned against him. What helps you to find strength in God when you feel alone?

Let us pray. Lord God, when I am greatly distressed, and everyone seems against me, help me to encourage myself in you. Thank you that it is never too late to turn back and ask you. Thank you that in your kingdom, everyone matters. Help me to treat others with the same generosity. Amen.
