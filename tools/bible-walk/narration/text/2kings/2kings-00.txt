The Living Voice. Meet the Second Book of Kings.

Two men walk along the bank of the river Jordan, talking. One is old, wrapped in a rough cloak. The other is younger, and will not leave his side. Suddenly, a chariot of fire, pulled by horses of fire, sweeps down between them. A whirlwind lifts the old man up into the sky, higher and higher, until he is gone. The younger man is left alone, holding the old man’s cloak. And so begins the second book of Kings.

Welcome to the second book of Kings, on The Living Voice. Second Kings has twenty five chapters, so on this walk it is twenty five steps, one chapter at a time.

At the end of the first book of Kings, the wicked king Ahab had died, just as the prophets had said. Since then we have walked through the short first letter of John, which ended with the words, little children, keep yourselves from idols. Second Kings shows us, very sadly, what happens to a people who do not.

The book begins with Elijah being carried up to heaven, and Elisha taking his place. The first part of the book is full of Elisha’s miracles. A widow’s jar of oil that keeps on pouring. A boy brought back to life. A foreign army commander healed of leprosy. An iron axe head that floats. And a hillside full of horses and chariots of fire, that only God’s eyes can see.

But the kings do not change. In the northern kingdom of Israel, one king after another walks in the way of Jeroboam. And in the middle of the book, Israel falls. The great empire of Assyria captures Samaria, and carries the people away. The ten tribes are scattered.

In the southern kingdom of Judah, David’s family still rules in Jerusalem. Some of its kings are wicked. But two of them stand out as the best kings since David. Hezekiah, who trusts God when the mighty Assyrian army surrounds Jerusalem. And young Josiah, who finds the lost book of God’s law in the temple, and leads the people back to God.

But in the end, Judah turns away too. And the book ends with the army of Babylon burning the temple, breaking down the walls of Jerusalem, and carrying the people away. You have already heard what happened next, when we walked through Daniel and Esther, God’s people living far from home.

Here are three things to watch for as we walk.

Watch for Elisha’s mercies. Many of his miracles are small and kind: helping a poor widow, healing poisoned soup, feeding hungry people with a few loaves. Some of them will remind you of what Jesus did, many years later.

Watch for God’s word coming true. Again and again, a prophet says something, and later the writer tells us, according to the word of the LORD. Things spoken in first Kings come true in second Kings. God’s word never fails.

And watch for God’s patience. He sends prophet after prophet. He waits, and warns, and rescues, again and again, before judgment finally comes. And even at the very end, there is a small glimmer of hope.

A gentle word before we begin. Second Kings tells honestly of wars, sieges, and kings who did terrible things. Some chapters, especially chapters six, nine and ten, and the last chapter, include hard and violent events. We will tell them gently, and we will not dwell on the details. If you are listening with children, you may want to listen to those chapters yourself first.

Here is a question to carry with you. Second Kings shows a people who drifted from God, little by little, until it was too late. What helps you to stay close to God, so that you do not drift?

Let us pray. Lord God, as I walk through the second book of Kings, show me your patience and your mercy. Thank you that your word never fails. Open my eyes, like Elisha’s servant, to see that you are always with me. And keep me from idols, so that my heart stays true to you. Amen.
