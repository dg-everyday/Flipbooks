The Living Voice. 2 Kings, chapter 2. A Double Portion of Thy Spirit.

An old teacher says to his student, stay here. I have to go on alone. And the student says, no. As the LORD lives, I will not leave you. They walk on. Again, the teacher says, stay here. Again, the student says, I will not leave you. Three times. Because the student knows that today is the last day he will ever walk with his master.

In chapter one, king Ahaziah fell from his upstairs window, and sent to ask Baal-zebub, the god of Ekron, if he would recover. Elijah met his messengers and asked, is it not because there is not a God in Israel? Two proud captains came to seize Elijah, and fire fell from heaven. But a third came humbly, and was spared. And Ahaziah died, as the LORD had said.

Now, in chapter two, Elijah’s work on earth comes to an end. Listen for what Elisha asks for. And listen for how his first miracles show that God is with him.

'And it came to pass, when the LORD would take up Elijah into heaven by a whirlwind, that Elijah went with Elisha from Gilgal.' Everyone seems to know what is going to happen today.

Elijah says to Elisha, stay here, for the LORD has sent me to Bethel. But Elisha says, 'As the LORD liveth, and as thy soul liveth, I will not leave thee.' So they go to Bethel together.

At Bethel, a group of prophets, called the sons of the prophets, come out to Elisha. Do you know that the LORD will take your master away today? Yes, I know, Elisha says. Be quiet.

Again Elijah says, stay here, for the LORD has sent me to Jericho. And again Elisha says, I will not leave you. At Jericho, the prophets say the same thing, and Elisha gives the same answer.

A third time, Elijah says, stay here, for the LORD has sent me to the Jordan. And a third time, Elisha says, I will not leave you. So the two of them walk on, and fifty of the prophets stand at a distance to watch.

At the river Jordan, Elijah takes off his cloak, his mantle, rolls it up, and strikes the water with it. And the river divides, this way and that, and the two of them cross over on dry ground. Do you remember when the Jordan parted for Joshua and the people of Israel, with the ark of the covenant? Here it happens again.

On the other side, Elijah turns to Elisha.

Elijah says, you have asked a hard thing. But if you see me when I am taken from you, it will be yours. If not, it will not.

And then it happens.

Elisha sees it. And he cries out, 'My father, my father, the chariot of Israel, and the horsemen thereof.' He meant that Elijah had been worth more to Israel than all its chariots and horsemen. And then Elijah is gone. Elisha takes hold of his own clothes, and tears them in two, in grief.

But Elijah’s mantle has fallen to the ground. Elisha picks it up, and walks back to the bank of the Jordan.

The prophets from Jericho, watching from far off, see it all. 'The spirit of Elijah doth rest on Elisha.' And they come and bow down before him.

They insist on sending fifty men to search for Elijah. They search for three days, and find nothing. Elijah was not on a mountain. He was with God.

Then Elisha’s first miracle. The men of Jericho say, our city is in a pleasant place, but the water is bad, and the land does not produce. So Elisha asks for a new bowl with salt in it. He throws the salt into the spring, and says, thus saith the LORD, I have healed these waters. And the water was healed, to that very day. The city that Joshua had cursed was now healed. Elisha’s ministry began with mercy.

As Elisha goes up to Bethel, a gang of youths comes out of the city and mocks him. Go up, you bald head! Go up, you bald head! The King James Bible says little children, but the word can also mean young men. They were not just teasing an old man. Bethel was the centre of Jeroboam’s golden calf worship, and they were mocking God’s new prophet, perhaps sneering, go up, like Elijah. Elisha turns and curses them in the name of the LORD. And two bears come out of the woods and attack them. This is a hard story, and Christians have understood it in different ways. But it shows that mocking God and his messengers is a serious thing.

Then Elisha goes on to Mount Carmel, and from there back to Samaria. His work as a prophet has begun.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 9. 'And it came to pass, when they were gone over, that Elijah said unto Elisha, Ask what I shall do for thee, before I be taken away from thee. And Elisha said, I pray thee, let a double portion of thy spirit be upon me.' In Israel, the eldest son received a double portion of his father’s inheritance, to carry on the family. Elisha was not being greedy. He was asking to be Elijah’s true heir, to carry on his work. And he knew he could not do it in his own strength. He needed the Spirit of God. That is a prayer God loves to answer.

Verse 11. 'And it came to pass, as they still went on, and talked, that, behold, there appeared a chariot of fire, and horses of fire, and parted them both asunder; and Elijah went up by a whirlwind into heaven.' As they still went on, and talked. Right to the end, they were walking together, talking. And then Elijah was taken up, without dying. Only one other person in the Bible did not die: Enoch, who walked with God, and was not, for God took him. Elijah’s story reminds us that death is not the end for those who walk with God.

Verse 14. 'And he took the mantle of Elijah that fell from him, and smote the waters, and said, Where is the LORD God of Elijah? and when he also had smitten the waters, they parted hither and thither: and Elisha went over.' Where is the LORD God of Elijah? Elijah was gone. But Elijah’s God was not. The same God was with Elisha. Great leaders come and go. But God remains. And the God who worked through those who went before us is ready to work through us too.

Luke wrote of Jesus, 'while they beheld, he was taken up; and a cloud received him out of their sight.' And Jesus said, 'He that believeth on me, the works that I do shall he do also'.

Think of someone who has passed on their faith to you, a parent, a teacher, a friend. Thank God for them. And ask him for his Spirit, so that you can carry on the work of faith for those who come after you.

Here is today’s question. Elisha asked, where is the LORD God of Elijah? When someone you depended on is no longer there, how can you find that God is still with you?

Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that you remain, when people come and go. Thank you for those who have walked with me and taught me about you. Like Elisha, I ask for your Spirit, because I cannot serve you in my own strength. Help me to stay faithful, and to say, I will not leave thee. Amen.

Next on the walk, second Kings chapter three. Three kings and their armies run out of water in the desert. Elisha tells them to dig ditches. And without wind or rain, the valley fills with water.
