The Living Voice. 2 Kings, chapter 4. It Is Well.

A mother rides a donkey as fast as it will go, across the hills to Mount Carmel. Her servant runs alongside. Back at home, her little boy is lying very still on a bed. When she gets to the prophet, his servant asks her, is everything all right? Is it well with you? With your husband? With your child? And with a breaking heart, she answers, it is well.

In chapter three, three kings and their armies ran out of water in the desert. Elisha told them, make this valley full of ditches. Without wind or rain, the valley filled with water, and God gave them victory over Moab too. It was but a light thing in the sight of the LORD.

Now, in chapter four, we see Elisha’s miracles of kindness in ordinary homes. Listen for a jar that will not run dry. And listen for a mother’s faith.

The chapter begins with a woman crying out to Elisha. She was the wife of one of the prophets. My husband is dead, she says. You know he feared the LORD. And now the man we owe money to is coming to take my two sons as slaves.

Elisha asks, what can I do for you? What do you have in the house? And she says, nothing, except a small jar of oil.

So Elisha says, go round to all your neighbours, and borrow empty jars. 'borrow not a few.' Then go inside, shut the door behind you and your sons, and start pouring oil into the jars.

She does exactly that. Her sons bring her jar after jar, and she pours, and pours. Her little jar keeps on pouring, and filling one big jar after another.

When she tells Elisha, he says, 'Go, sell the oil, and pay thy debt, and live thou and thy children of the rest.' She had enough to pay everything she owed, and to live on the rest.

Then the story moves to the town of Shunem. A wealthy woman lives there. Whenever Elisha passes through, she invites him in for a meal. One day she says to her husband, this man is a holy man of God. Let us build him a little room on the roof, with a bed, a table, a stool and a lamp, so he has somewhere to stay when he comes.

Elisha wants to thank her. Shall I speak to the king for you? But she answers simply, 'I dwell among mine own people.' I do not need anything.

But Gehazi, Elisha’s servant, notices that she has no son, and her husband is old. So Elisha tells her, about this time next year, you will hold a son in your arms. She hardly dares to hope. But a year later, she has a baby boy.

Years pass. The boy grows. One day, he goes out to the fields where his father is with the harvesters. Suddenly he cries out, my head, my head! His father tells a servant, carry him to his mother. And the boy sits on his mother’s lap for a few hours, and then he dies.

The mother carries him upstairs, lays him on Elisha’s bed, and shuts the door. Then she asks her husband for a donkey, and says, it shall be well.

She rides hard to Mount Carmel. Elisha sees her coming, and sends Gehazi to meet her.

When she reaches Elisha, she falls down and takes hold of his feet. Did I ask you for a son? she says. Did I not say, do not deceive me? Elisha sends Gehazi ahead with his staff. But the mother says, I will not leave you. So Elisha goes with her.

When Elisha arrives, he goes into the room, shuts the door, and prays to the LORD. Then he stretches himself out on the child, and the boy’s body grows warm. Elisha walks up and down the room, and stretches out on him again. 'and the child sneezed seven times, and the child opened his eyes.'

Elisha calls the mother. 'Take up thy son.' And she falls at his feet, bows to the ground, and then picks up her son, and goes out.

Two more small miracles end the chapter. There is a famine, and Elisha’s servant makes a big pot of stew for the prophets. Someone gathers wild gourds, not knowing they are poisonous, and drops them in. As the men start eating, they cry out, man of God, there is death in the pot! Elisha throws some flour into the pot, and says, serve it. And there is no harm in it.

Then a man arrives with twenty small barley loaves, a gift of the first fruits of his harvest. Elisha says, give them to the people to eat. His servant says, how can I set this before a hundred men? But Elisha says, give them, for thus saith the LORD, they shall eat, and have some left over.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 6. 'And it came to pass, when the vessels were full, that she said unto her son, Bring me yet a vessel. And he said unto her, There is not a vessel more. And the oil stayed.' The oil only stopped when there were no more jars. God’s supply was not the limit. The number of jars was. If she had borrowed more, she would have had more. God’s grace is like that. He is always ready to give. The question is how much room we make for it.

Verse 26. 'Run now, I pray thee, to meet her, and say unto her, Is it well with thee? is it well with thy husband? is it well with the child? And she answered, It is well.' Her son had died. How could she say, it is well? She was not pretending. She was holding on to God, before she knew what he would do. She did not tell everyone her trouble. She went straight to the man of God. Sometimes faith says, it is well, with tears, because it trusts the One who holds all things.

Verse 44. 'So he set it before them, and they did eat, and left thereof, according to the word of the LORD.' Twenty loaves, a hundred men, and food left over. Does that remind you of something? Jesus fed five thousand with five loaves and two fish, and twelve baskets were left over. Elisha’s miracle was a small picture of what Jesus would do in a far greater way. God always has enough, and more.

Matthew wrote, 'And they did all eat, and were filled: and they took up of the fragments that remained twelve baskets full.' And the letter to the Hebrews says, 'Be not forgetful to entertain strangers'.

Is there a need in your life, where you feel you have only a little jar of oil? Bring it to God. Make room for him to work. And look for a way to show kindness to someone today, as the woman of Shunem did for Elisha.

Here is today’s question. The woman of Shunem said, it is well, even when her heart was breaking. What helps you to trust God when things go wrong?

Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that your supply never runs dry. Help me to make room for your grace. When hard things happen, help me to run to you first, and to say, it is well, because you are with me. Thank you that you care about widows and mothers, children and hungry people, and that you always have more than enough. Amen.
