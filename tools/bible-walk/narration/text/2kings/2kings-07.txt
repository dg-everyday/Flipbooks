The Living Voice. 2 Kings, chapter 7. This Day Is a Day of Good Tidings.

Four sick, starving men sit outside the gate of a city under siege. They are lepers, so they are not allowed inside. And inside, everyone is starving anyway. They look at each other. Why sit here until we die? Let us go over to the enemy camp. If they let us live, we live. If not, we die anyway. And as the light fades, they get up and walk towards the enemy tents.

In chapter six, an iron axe head floated. Elisha prayed, LORD, open his eyes, and his servant saw the mountains full of horses and chariots of fire. Elisha led a blinded Syrian army into Samaria, and fed them instead of harming them. But then the Syrians besieged Samaria, and the famine became terrible. The king blamed Elisha, and sent a man to arrest him.

Now, in chapter seven, God answers in a way no one expected. Listen for a promise that sounds impossible. And listen for four unlikely messengers of good news.

The king’s messenger arrives at Elisha’s house, with the king close behind. And Elisha gives them the word of the LORD. 'To morrow about this time shall a measure of fine flour be sold for a shekel, and two measures of barley for a shekel, in the gate of Samaria.'

That was an astonishing promise. In a city where people were starving, and food cost a fortune, Elisha said that within a day, good flour would be cheap.

One of the king’s officers, the lord on whose arm the king leaned, scoffs.

Now the story moves outside the city gate. Four men with leprosy are sitting there. They are not allowed in the city, because of their disease. And there is no food inside anyway. So they say to one another, why sit here until we die? If we go into the city, we will starve. If we stay here, we will starve. Let us go over to the Syrian camp. If they spare us, we live. If not, we only die.

So as the light fades, they get up and go to the edge of the Syrian camp. And there is no one there.

Here is what had happened. 'For the Lord had made the host of the Syrians to hear a noise of chariots, and a noise of horses, even the noise of a great host'. The Syrians thought, the king of Israel has hired the armies of the Hittites and the Egyptians to attack us! And they ran for their lives, as the light faded, leaving everything behind. Their tents, their horses, their donkeys, and the whole camp, just as it was.

God did not need an army. He only needed a noise.

The four lepers walk into the first tent. They eat and drink. They pick up silver and gold and clothes, and go and hide them. Then they go into another tent, and do the same.

And then they stop. And they look at each other.

So they go back to the city, and call out to the gatekeepers. We went to the Syrian camp, and there is no one there. Not a sound. Only the horses and donkeys tied up, and the tents just as they were. The gatekeepers shout the news, and it reaches the king’s house.

But the king does not believe it. He gets up and says to his officers, I know what the Syrians are doing. They know we are hungry. So they have left their camp to hide in the fields. When we come out, they will capture us, and take the city.

One of his officers has a sensible idea. Let us send some men with a few of our last horses, and see. So the king sends two chariots after the Syrians. They follow the road all the way to the Jordan. And the whole road is littered with clothes and equipment that the Syrians threw away as they ran. The scouts come back, and tell the king.

Then the people of Samaria pour out of the gate, and plunder the Syrian camp.

And the officer who had scoffed at Elisha? The king put him in charge of the gate. And as the hungry crowds rushed out, he was trampled in the gateway, and died. He saw the flour with his own eyes, as Elisha had said. But he did not eat it. Unbelief had shut him out of the blessing.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 2. 'Then a lord on whose hand the king leaned answered the man of God, and said, Behold, if the LORD would make windows in heaven, might this thing be? And he said, Behold, thou shalt see it with thine eyes, but shalt not eat thereof.' Even if God opened windows in the sky, this could not happen. That is what the officer said. He was mocking. He thought God was limited by what he could see. But God did not need windows in the sky. He needed only a noise in the Syrian camp. Unbelief does not stop God from working. But it can stop us from sharing in what he does.

Verse 9. 'Then they said one to another, We do not well: this day is a day of good tidings, and we hold our peace: if we tarry till the morning light, some mischief will come upon us: now therefore come, that we may go and tell the king’s household.' Good tidings means good news. These four men had found food and life, while the whole city was starving. And they realised it was wrong to keep it to themselves. We who have found the good news of Jesus are like those lepers. We have found bread for hungry people. It would be wrong to keep it to ourselves.

Verse 16. 'And the people went out, and spoiled the tents of the Syrians. So a measure of fine flour was sold for a shekel, and two measures of barley for a shekel, according to the word of the LORD.' According to the word of the LORD. Exactly what Elisha had said, at exactly the time he said it. The starving city was fed. It seemed impossible, but God did it. When God makes a promise, it may seem impossible, but it will come true.

Paul wrote, 'How beautiful are the feet of them that preach the gospel of peace, and bring glad tidings of good things!' And the prophet Malachi heard God say, 'if I will not open you the windows of heaven, and pour you out a blessing'.

Who do you know who needs to hear the good news of God’s love? Do not keep it to yourself. And if there is a promise of God that seems impossible to you, trust him anyway. He keeps his word.

Here is today’s question. The four lepers said, this day is a day of good tidings, and we hold our peace. Who could you share good news with this week?

Let us pray. Lord God, nothing is impossible for you. Forgive me when I doubt your word, like the officer at the gate. Thank you for the good news that feeds hungry hearts. Give me the courage of the four lepers, not to keep it to myself, but to go and tell. Amen.
