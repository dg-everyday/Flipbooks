The Living Voice. 2 Kings, chapter 8. The Man of God Wept.

Two men stand face to face in a room. One is a prophet of God. The other is a powerful officer of a foreign king. The prophet looks at him, and keeps looking, until the officer feels uncomfortable and looks away. And then the prophet begins to cry. Why are you weeping, my lord? the officer asks. Because, says the prophet, I know what you are going to do.

In chapter seven, Elisha promised that food would be cheap in starving Samaria. An officer scoffed, if the LORD would make windows in heaven. But God made the Syrian army hear a great noise, and they fled. Four lepers found the empty camp, and said, this day is a day of good tidings. The city was fed, according to the word of the LORD.

Now, in chapter eight, we hear the end of one story, and the start of another. Listen for how God looks after a faithful woman. And listen for why Elisha weeps.

Do you remember the woman of Shunem, whose son Elisha brought back to life? Some time before, Elisha had warned her. Leave the land with your family, and live somewhere else, because the LORD has called for a famine. 'and it shall also come upon the land seven years.' So she trusted his word, and went to live in the land of the Philistines.

When the famine is over, she comes back. But someone has taken over her house and her land. So she goes to the king, to ask for it back.

And at that very moment, something remarkable is happening. The king is talking with Gehazi, Elisha’s old servant. Tell me, the king says, all the great things Elisha has done. And just as Gehazi is telling the story of how Elisha brought a dead boy back to life, in walks the very woman, with that very son, to ask the king for her land. My lord, O king, Gehazi says, this is the woman, and this is her son, whom Elisha restored to life!

What perfect timing. That was not chance. God had arranged it.

The king asks the woman, and she tells him the whole story.

Then the scene changes. Elisha goes to Damascus, the capital of Syria. Ben-hadad, the king of Syria, the same king who had fought Israel so many times, is sick. When he hears that the man of God has come, he sends his officer Hazael with a huge gift, forty camels loaded with the best things of Damascus. Ask him, the king says, shall I recover from this sickness?

Hazael stands before Elisha. Your son Ben-hadad, king of Syria, has sent me to ask, shall I recover? Elisha tells him, the illness itself will not kill him. But the LORD has shown me that he will surely die.

And then something strange happens.

Why are you weeping, my lord? Hazael asks. And Elisha tells him. Because I know the terrible harm you will do to the people of Israel. You will burn their cities, and bring great suffering on their families.

And Hazael says, what, is your servant a dog, that I would do such a thing? He could not imagine himself being so cruel. But Elisha says, the LORD has shown me that you will be king over Syria.

Hazael goes back to his master. What did Elisha say? Ben-hadad asks. And Hazael tells him only half. He said you will surely recover. But the very next day, Hazael takes the king’s life, and makes himself king of Syria instead. The man who said, is thy servant a dog, did exactly what Elisha had said.

Then the story turns to Judah. Jehoram, the son of good king Jehoshaphat, becomes king in Jerusalem. But he marries a daughter of Ahab and Jezebel, named Athaliah. 'And he walked in the way of the kings of Israel, as did the house of Ahab'. Ahab’s family has now married into David’s family, and brought its idols with it.

And yet.

In Jehoram’s days, Edom, which had been under Judah, rebels, and makes its own king. When Jehoram dies, his son Ahaziah becomes king of Judah. His mother is Athaliah, Ahab’s daughter. And he too does evil, like the house of Ahab.

Ahaziah of Judah joins his uncle, Joram of Israel, to fight against Hazael, the new king of Syria. Joram is wounded in the battle, and goes back to Jezreel to recover. And Ahaziah goes down to visit him there. The two kings are together in Jezreel. And in the next chapter, we will see why that matters.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 6. 'And when the king asked the woman, she told him. So the king appointed unto her a certain officer, saying, Restore all that was hers, and all the fruits of the field since the day that she left the land, even until now.' Not just her land, but all the harvests she had missed for all those years. The woman had trusted God’s word and left her home. And God made sure she lost nothing. God sees the faithfulness of ordinary people, and he takes care of them, often in ways that show his perfect timing.

Verse 11. 'And he settled his countenance stedfastly, until he was ashamed: and the man of God wept.' Elisha could see what was coming: war, cruelty and suffering for his people. And it broke his heart. Prophets did not enjoy announcing bad news. They wept over it. Many years later, Jesus looked over Jerusalem, knowing what would happen to it, and he wept too. God does not take pleasure in suffering. His heart is grieved by it.

Verse 19. 'Yet the LORD would not destroy Judah for David his servant’s sake, as he promised him to give him alway a light, and to his children.' Yet. Even when Judah’s king followed the ways of Ahab, God did not wipe out David’s family. Because of his promise. We have heard it before: a light for David. However dark things got, God kept that lamp burning, until the true Light of the world was born in Bethlehem, from David’s family.

Luke wrote of Jesus, 'And when he was come near, he beheld the city, and wept over it'. And Jeremiah wrote, 'The heart is deceitful above all things, and desperately wicked: who can know it?'

Is there a situation where you have stepped out in faith, and now you are wondering if God will take care of you? Trust his timing. And ask God to give you a heart that grieves over what grieves him.

Here is today’s question. Hazael said, is thy servant a dog, that he should do this? Why do you think people often cannot see what they are capable of, and how can we guard our hearts?

Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that you see faithful people, and that your timing is perfect. Thank you that you are not pleased by suffering, but weep over it. Search my heart, and show me what is there, so that I do not deceive myself. And thank you that you kept a light burning for David, even in the darkest days. Amen.
