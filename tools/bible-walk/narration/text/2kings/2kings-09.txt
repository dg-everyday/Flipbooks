The Living Voice. 2 Kings, chapter 9. He Driveth Furiously.

A watchman stands on top of a tower, staring into the distance. A cloud of dust is coming along the road. Chariots, moving fast. Too fast. He squints. Two riders have been sent to meet them, and neither has come back. Then he recognises the way the leader drives, wildly, recklessly, like a man on fire. And he calls down to the king, it must be Jehu. He drives like a madman.

In chapter eight, the woman of Shunem had her land restored, with all its harvests. Elisha wept when he saw the harm Hazael would do, and Hazael became king of Syria. In Judah, the kings married into Ahab’s family and followed its ways. Yet the LORD kept a light for David. And the two kings, Joram of Israel and Ahaziah of Judah, were together in Jezreel.

Now, in chapter nine, God’s judgment on the house of Ahab finally arrives, just as Elijah had said. This is a hard chapter, and we will tell it gently. Listen for how God’s word is fulfilled. And listen for the name of Naboth.

Elisha calls one of the young prophets. Tuck up your robe, he says, and take this flask of oil, and go to Ramoth-gilead. Find Jehu, take him into an inner room, and pour the oil on his head, and say, thus saith the LORD, I have anointed you king over Israel. Then open the door and run.

Do you remember? On Mount Horeb, God told Elijah to anoint Jehu as king of Israel. Now, years later, it is happening.

The young prophet arrives at the army camp. The officers are sitting together. I have a message for you, commander, he says. For which of us? asks Jehu. For you, commander. They go inside, and the young man pours the oil on Jehu’s head.

He tells Jehu that God is giving him a task. The house of Ahab will be brought to an end, because of the blood of God’s prophets and servants, shed by Jezebel. And then the young prophet opens the door, and runs away.

Jehu comes out to his fellow officers. Is everything all right? they ask. Why did that madman come to you? At first Jehu tries to brush it off. But they press him, and he tells them. He said, thus saith the LORD, I have anointed you king over Israel.

And at once the officers take off their cloaks, and spread them on the bare steps under Jehu’s feet, as a carpet for a king. They blow the trumpet, and shout, Jehu is king!

Jehu wastes no time. He climbs into his chariot and races towards Jezreel, where Joram, king of Israel, is recovering from his wounds, with Ahaziah, king of Judah.

The watchman on the tower in Jezreel sees them coming. The king sends a rider to ask, is it peace? Jehu says, what have you to do with peace? Get behind me. And the rider joins him. A second rider is sent, and the same thing happens.

Then the watchman says the famous words.

So Joram and Ahaziah each get into their chariots and ride out to meet Jehu. And where do they meet him? In the field that once belonged to Naboth, the man whose vineyard Ahab and Jezebel had stolen. Joram calls, is it peace, Jehu? And Jehu answers that there can be no peace while Jezebel’s idolatry and evil continue.

Joram turns his chariot and flees, crying, treachery, Ahaziah! But Jehu draws his bow, and an arrow strikes Joram, and he falls in his chariot.

And then Jehu says something very important to his officer.

So Joram’s body is left in Naboth’s field. Ahaziah, king of Judah, tries to escape, but he too is wounded, and dies at Megiddo. His servants carry him back to Jerusalem to be buried.

Then Jehu comes to Jezreel. Jezebel hears that he is coming. She paints her eyes, does her hair, and looks out of an upstairs window. Proud to the very end, she calls down to him, mocking him as a traitor.

Jehu looks up at the window. Who is on my side? Who? Two or three palace officials look down at him. And at his command, they throw Jezebel down. She dies there.

Later, when Jehu sends men to bury her, because she was a king’s daughter, they find almost nothing left of her. And Jehu says, this is the word of the LORD, which he spoke by his servant Elijah. Long before, in the vineyard of Naboth, Elijah had said that Jezebel would meet her end in Jezreel. And so it happened.

This is a very hard chapter. The Bible does not tell it to make us enjoy violence. It tells it to show that God sees injustice, that he does not forget the blood of the innocent, and that his word always comes true, even when it takes many years.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 6. 'And he arose, and went into the house; and he poured the oil on his head, and said unto him, Thus saith the LORD God of Israel, I have anointed thee king over the people of the LORD, even over Israel.' The people of the LORD. Even after years of Baal worship, God still called Israel his people. He had not given up on them. Jehu was anointed to clear away the evil of Ahab’s house, because God still cared about his people, and wanted them back.

Verse 20. 'And the watchman told, saying, He came even unto them, and cometh not again: and the driving is like the driving of Jehu the son of Nimshi; for he driveth furiously.' Furiously means wildly, like a madman. Jehu was a man of fierce energy. He did not waste time. That energy could be used for God. But as we will see in the next chapter, zeal and energy are not the same as a heart that truly loves God.

Verse 26. 'Surely I have seen yesterday the blood of Naboth, and the blood of his sons, saith the LORD; and I will requite thee in this plat, saith the LORD. Now therefore take and cast him into the plat of ground, according to the word of the LORD.' A plat means a plot of land. Requite means repay. Years had passed since Naboth was falsely accused and put to death. Perhaps people had forgotten. But God had not. He had seen. And in the very same field, justice came. God never forgets the innocent who suffer.

Paul wrote, 'Vengeance is mine; I will repay, saith the Lord.' And David wrote, 'Fret not thyself because of evildoers'.

Is there an injustice that troubles you, something wrong that seems to have gone unpunished? Bring it to God, and leave it with him. He sees. He has not forgotten. And he will put things right in his time.

Here is today’s question. God remembered the blood of Naboth, even years later. How does it help you to know that God sees every injustice, even when no one else does?

Let us pray. Lord God, you see everything, and you never forget the innocent who suffer. When I see injustice, help me to trust you to put things right, and not to take revenge myself. Thank you that your word always comes true. And keep me from pride like Jezebel’s, that refuses to turn to you even at the end. Amen.
