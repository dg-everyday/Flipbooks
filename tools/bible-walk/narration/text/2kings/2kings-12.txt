The Living Voice. 2 Kings, chapter 12. Jehoiada the Priest Instructed Him.

A wooden box sits beside the altar in the temple, with a hole cut in its lid. People come in, one after another, and drop their coins through the hole. Clink. Clink. Rich people, poor people, children with a single small coin. Before long, the box is so full that it is heavy to lift. And with that money, the broken house of God is going to be made whole again.

In chapter eleven, wicked queen Athaliah tried to destroy the whole royal family of David. But a brave princess, Jehosheba, hid the baby Joash in the house of the LORD. When he was seven, the priest Jehoiada brought him out and crowned him king. The people shouted, God save the king, and made a covenant to be the LORD’s people.

Now, in chapter twelve, the boy king grows up. Listen for who taught him. And listen for what happens to the house of God.

Joash, also called Jehoash, reigns for a long time in Jerusalem. And the writer tells us something important about him.

As long as Jehoiada guided him, Joash did what was right. But the high places were still not removed. The people still offered sacrifices on the hilltops.

Now, the temple was in poor condition. Solomon had built it more than a century before. And Athaliah’s family had damaged it, and taken its holy things for Baal. The walls had cracks and gaps. Joash wanted to repair it.

So he tells the priests, collect all the money people bring to the house of the LORD, the money they give when they are counted, and the money they give freely from their hearts. Take it from the people you know, and use it to repair the temple, wherever there is damage.

But years go by, and nothing happens. 'But it was so, that in the three and twentieth year of king Jehoash the priests had not repaired the breaches of the house.' The money was coming in, but the walls were still cracked.

So the king calls Jehoiada and the other priests. Why have you not repaired the temple? From now on, do not take the money for yourselves. Hand it over for the repairs. And the priests agree. They will not collect the money any more, and they will not be in charge of the repairs.

And then Jehoiada has a simple, wise idea.

When the chest is full, the king’s scribe and the high priest come and count the money, and put it in bags. Then they give it to the men in charge of the work. And they pay the carpenters and builders, the stonemasons and stone cutters. They buy wood and cut stone, and everything needed to repair the house of the LORD.

The writer adds that none of this money was used to make silver bowls or gold vessels or trumpets for the temple. It all went to the workmen, to repair the building.

And here is a lovely detail.

But then danger comes. Hazael, the king of Syria, the man Elisha had wept over, captures the Philistine city of Gath, and then turns towards Jerusalem. And Joash panics. He takes all the holy things that the kings of Judah before him had dedicated to God, and his own, and all the gold in the treasuries of the temple and the palace. And he sends it all to Hazael. And Hazael goes away from Jerusalem.

Joash spent years repairing the temple. And then, when trouble came, he emptied its treasury to buy off an enemy, instead of trusting God.

Another book of the Bible, the second book of Chronicles, tells us more. After Jehoiada died, Joash listened to other advisers, and turned away from the LORD. And when Jehoiada’s own son, Zechariah, warned him, Joash had him put to death. Joash began well. But when the one who instructed him was gone, his faith faded.

At the end of his life, Joash’s own servants plot against him, and take his life. And his son Amaziah becomes king.

It is a sad end, for a boy who was saved as a baby, crowned in the temple, and who repaired the house of God. His story asks us a searching question. Is our faith our own, or does it depend on someone else holding us up?

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 2. 'And Jehoash did that which was right in the sight of the LORD all his days wherein Jehoiada the priest instructed him.' All his days wherein Jehoiada instructed him. That phrase is both good and sad. Good, because a wise teacher made a great difference to a young king. Sad, because when that teacher was gone, Joash did not stand firm. Good teachers and parents are a gift from God. But in the end, each of us needs a faith of our own, rooted in God himself.

Verse 9. 'But Jehoiada the priest took a chest, and bored a hole in the lid of it, and set it beside the altar, on the right side as one cometh into the house of the LORD: and the priests that kept the door put therein all the money that was brought into the house of the LORD.' A simple box. A simple idea. Everyone could give, and everyone could see where the money went. Giving to God is not only for the rich. Every coin counted. And the work got done because many people gave what they could, together.

Verse 15. 'Moreover they reckoned not with the men, into whose hand they delivered the money to be bestowed on workmen: for they dealt faithfully.' Reckoned not means they did not need to check the accounts. The men in charge of the money were so honest that no one needed to watch them. What a thing to be said about someone: they dealt faithfully. Honesty with money is a way of honouring God, even when no one is checking.

Paul wrote, 'Every man according as he purposeth in his heart, so let him give; not grudgingly, or of necessity: for God loveth a cheerful giver.' And the letter to the Hebrews says, 'Remember them which have the rule over you, who have spoken unto you the word of God'.

Think of someone who has taught you about God. Thank God for them, and if you can, thank them too. And ask yourself honestly, is my faith my own? Spend some time with God today, just you and him.

Here is today’s question. Joash did right as long as Jehoiada instructed him. What helps your faith to be your own, so that it stays strong even when others are not there to hold you up?

Let us pray. Lord God, thank you for the people who have taught me about you. Help my faith to be my own, rooted in you, so that I stand firm all my days. Make me a cheerful giver, and someone who deals faithfully, with money and with everything you trust to me. Amen.
