The Living Voice. 2 Kings, chapter 15. And So It Came to Pass.

Imagine a game where every time someone sits in the big chair, someone else pushes them off and sits there instead. Over and over. That is what Israel’s throne was like in these years. One king plots against another, takes his throne, and then someone plots against him. Meanwhile, on the horizon, a great empire is watching, and getting ready to march.

In chapter fourteen, Amaziah of Judah grew proud after defeating Edom. Jehoash of Israel warned him with the story of the thistle and the cedar: tarry at home. But Amaziah would not listen, and Judah was defeated. In Israel, God used king Jeroboam to win back Israel’s land, just as the prophet Jonah had said.

Now, in chapter fifteen, the story speeds up. Many kings come and go. Do not worry about remembering all their names. Listen for one king in Judah who was struck with leprosy. And listen for the first shadow of Assyria.

The chapter begins in Judah. Azariah, the son of Amaziah, becomes king when he is only a teenager, and he reigns for a very long time. He is also called Uzziah. 'And he did that which was right in the sight of the LORD, according to all that his father Amaziah had done'. But again, the high places are not removed.

And then something happens to this king.

Another book, the second book of Chronicles, tells us why. Uzziah became proud, and went into the temple to burn incense, which only the priests were allowed to do. And as he stood there, angry at the priests who tried to stop him, leprosy broke out on his forehead.

The prophet Isaiah lived at this time. His great vision of God begins with the words, in the year that king Uzziah died. When the earthly king died, Isaiah saw the true King, high and lifted up.

Now the chapter turns to Israel, and the kings come and go very fast.

First, Zachariah, the son of Jeroboam. He reigns for only a few months, and does evil. Then a man named Shallum plots against him and takes his life in front of the people, and becomes king.

And the writer pauses to tell us something.

Shallum reigns for just one month. Then Menahem comes from Tirzah, takes Shallum’s life, and becomes king. Menahem is cruel, and does evil, like those before him.

And now a new name appears. 'And Pul the king of Assyria came against the land'. Assyria was a huge and powerful empire to the north east, with its capital at Nineveh. Menahem pays Pul a huge amount of silver, to make him go away, and to keep himself on the throne. He makes every wealthy man in Israel pay towards it. And for now, the king of Assyria goes home.

When Menahem dies, his son Pekahiah becomes king. After a short time, one of his own officers, Pekah, plots against him and takes his life in the palace, and becomes king.

Pekah too does evil. And in his days, Assyria comes back. This time, they do not go home with silver.

Finally, a man named Hoshea plots against Pekah, takes his life, and becomes king. Hoshea will be the last king of Israel.

Count them. Zachariah, Shallum, Menahem, Pekahiah, Pekah, Hoshea. Six kings. Four of them came to the throne by taking the life of the king before. No one was safe. Everyone was grabbing power. And not one of them turned back to God.

Meanwhile, in Judah, Jotham, the son of Uzziah, becomes king. He did what was right in the sight of the LORD, like his father. He built the upper gate of the house of the LORD. But the high places were still not removed.

And the chapter ends with a warning. 'In those days the LORD began to send against Judah Rezin the king of Syria, and Pekah the son of Remaliah.' Trouble is coming for Judah too.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 5. 'And the LORD smote the king, so that he was a leper unto the day of his death, and dwelt in a several house. And Jotham the king’s son was over the house, judging the people of the land.' A several house means a separate house. Uzziah had been a good and strong king. But pride brought him low, and he spent the rest of his life apart from others, unable even to go into the temple. Even good people can fall through pride. And notice, his son Jotham stepped in, and ruled well. God always has someone ready.

Verse 12. 'This was the word of the LORD which he spake unto Jehu, saying, Thy sons shall sit on the throne of Israel unto the fourth generation. And so it came to pass.' God told Jehu that his family would rule for four generations. Jehoahaz, Joash, Jeroboam, Zachariah. Four generations. And then it ended, exactly as God had said. And so it came to pass. Those words appear again and again in the Bible. When God speaks, it comes to pass.

Verse 29. 'In the days of Pekah king of Israel came Tiglath-pileser king of Assyria, and took Ijon, and Abel-beth-maachah, and Janoah, and Kedesh, and Hazor, and Gilead, and Galilee, all the land of Naphtali, and carried them captive to Assyria.' This was the beginning of the end for Israel. Assyria took the northern part of the land, including Galilee, and carried its people away. But listen to what Isaiah later said about that very land: the people that walked in darkness have seen a great light. Galilee, the first land to fall, would be the place where Jesus would grow up and begin to preach.

Isaiah wrote, 'In the year that king Uzziah died I saw also the Lord sitting upon a throne, high and lifted up'. And the psalmist wrote, 'Put not your trust in princes'.

Earthly leaders come and go. Some are good, and some are bad. But God is always on his throne. If you are worried about the leaders of your country, or the state of the world, pray for them, and remember who is really in charge.

Here is today’s question. In the year that king Uzziah died, Isaiah saw the Lord on his throne. When things around you feel unstable, how does it help to remember that God is still King?

Let us pray. Lord God, kings and leaders come and go, but you are on your throne for ever. Keep me from pride, which brought down even good king Uzziah. Thank you that what you say always comes to pass. And thank you that in the land of darkness, you sent a great light, your Son Jesus. Amen.
