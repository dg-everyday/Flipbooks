The Living Voice. 2 Kings, chapter 16. I Am Thy Servant and Thy Son.

Two enemy armies are marching on Jerusalem. The young king is terrified. His heart shakes, like trees in the wind. A prophet comes to him with a message from God. Do not be afraid. Trust the LORD, and he will save you. Ask God for a sign, any sign. But the king has already made up his mind. He has another plan. He will send a letter, and a great deal of money, to the most powerful empire on earth.

In chapter fifteen, kings in Israel came and went quickly, one plotting against another. Azariah, also called Uzziah, was struck with leprosy, and Isaiah saw the Lord in the year that he died. Assyria began to march, and carried away the people of Galilee. And the LORD began to send the kings of Syria and Israel against Judah.

Now, in chapter sixteen, a king of Judah faces those two kings. Listen for where he turns for help. And listen for what he does to the house of the LORD.

Ahaz, the son of Jotham, becomes king of Judah. And the writer gives his verdict straight away.

Ahaz walked in the ways of the kings of Israel. He even did the most terrible things the nations around him did, things God had forbidden, even to his own son. He offered sacrifices at the high places, on the hills, and under every green tree.

Then trouble comes. Rezin, king of Syria, and Pekah, king of Israel, join together and march against Jerusalem. They surround the city, but they cannot capture it.

The book of Isaiah tells us more about this moment. When Ahaz and his people heard that the two kings were coming, their hearts shook, like trees in the wind. And God sent Isaiah to meet Ahaz. Do not be afraid, Isaiah said. These two kings are like the smoking stubs of burnt out torches. Trust in the LORD. And God offered Ahaz a sign, anything he liked, in the depths below or the heights above. But Ahaz refused. I will not ask, he said. So Isaiah said that God would give a sign anyway. A child would be born, and his name would be Immanuel, which means, God with us. Many years later, Matthew wrote that this promise pointed to Jesus.

But Ahaz did not want God’s help. He had already decided what to do.

To pay for this help, Ahaz takes the silver and gold from the house of the LORD and from the palace, and sends it as a gift to the king of Assyria. And it works, for now. The king of Assyria marches against Damascus, captures it, carries its people away, and puts Rezin to death.

Then Ahaz travels to Damascus, to meet his new master, Tiglath-pileser. And there he sees something that catches his eye.

So Urijah the priest builds a copy of the Damascus altar in Jerusalem, and has it ready by the time Ahaz gets home. And when the king returns, he goes up to the new altar, and offers sacrifices on it.

Then Ahaz moves things around in the house of the LORD. The bronze altar that Solomon had made, the altar that stood before the LORD, is pushed aside to the north. The new foreign altar takes its place. All the regular offerings, Ahaz says, will be made on the great new altar. The old bronze altar, he says, will be for me to use when I want to seek guidance.

And he goes further. He cuts away the panels of the bronze stands that Hiram had made, and removes the basins. He takes down the great bronze sea from the backs of the twelve bronze oxen, and sets it on a stone floor. He changes the covered way and the king’s entrance in the temple. Why? 'for the king of Assyria.' Perhaps he needed the bronze to pay the Assyrians. Perhaps he wanted to please them. Either way, the house of the LORD was being changed to suit a foreign king.

Think back to the temple Solomon built, with its golden cherubims and its bronze sea on twelve oxen, made exactly as God wanted. Now a king of David’s family was taking it apart, piece by piece, to please the king of Assyria.

Then Ahaz dies, and his son Hezekiah becomes king. And Hezekiah will be very different from his father.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 2. 'Twenty years old was Ahaz when he began to reign, and reigned sixteen years in Jerusalem, and did not that which was right in the sight of the LORD his God, like David his father.' Ahaz was young when he became king. His father Jotham and his grandfather Uzziah had both done right in God’s sight. Ahaz had good examples. But he chose a different path. Having a good family is a great gift. But each of us still has to choose for ourselves whether we will follow God.

Verse 7. 'So Ahaz sent messengers to Tiglath-pileser king of Assyria, saying, I am thy servant and thy son: come up, and save me out of the hand of the king of Syria, and out of the hand of the king of Israel, which rise up against me.' I am thy servant and thy son. Those words should have been said to God. Ahaz refused God’s help, and gave himself to a cruel foreign king instead. And Assyria did help, for a while. But it cost Judah dearly. When we refuse to trust God, we end up serving something else.

Verse 10. 'And king Ahaz went to Damascus to meet Tiglath-pileser king of Assyria, and saw an altar that was at Damascus: and king Ahaz sent to Urijah the priest the fashion of the altar, and the pattern of it, according to all the workmanship thereof.' The fashion means the design. God had given Moses and Solomon the pattern for his house. But Ahaz liked the pattern of a foreign altar better. He changed God’s worship to suit the fashions of the world. We can do the same, shaping our faith to fit what is popular, instead of what God has said.

Isaiah told Ahaz, 'If ye will not believe, surely ye shall not be established.' And David wrote, 'Some trust in chariots, and some in horses: but we will remember the name of the LORD our God.'

When you are afraid, where do you turn first? Is there an Assyria in your life, something you run to for help instead of God? Bring your fears to him today. He is Immanuel, God with us.

Here is today’s question. Ahaz refused God’s sign and turned to Assyria instead. Why do you think it can be easier to trust something we can see, than to trust God?

Let us pray. Lord God, forgive me for the times I have trusted other things more than you. When I am afraid, help me to turn to you first. Keep me from changing my worship to fit the fashions of the world. And thank you that you are Immanuel, God with us, and that your Son Jesus came to save. Amen.
