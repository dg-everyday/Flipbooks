The Living Voice. 2 Kings, chapter 22. I Have Found the Book of the Law.

Workers are busy in an old building, clearing out dusty rooms and broken things. In a forgotten corner, the man in charge finds an old scroll. He brushes off the dust, unrolls it, and begins to read. And his eyes grow wide. He knows what this is. He runs to find the king’s secretary. I have found the book of the law in the house of the LORD!

In chapter twenty one, Manasseh became the worst king Judah ever had. He rebuilt the idols, set an idol in the very house of the LORD, and led the people to do more evil than the nations before them. God said that Jerusalem would be wiped like a dish. But later, Manasseh humbled himself in captivity, and God heard him. Then his son Amon was killed, and the boy Josiah became king.

Now, in chapter twenty two, Josiah grows up. Listen for what happens when God’s Word is found again. And listen for how Josiah responds.

Josiah became king when he was just a boy of eight. And the writer tells us what kind of king he was.

Josiah did not have a good father to learn from. His father and grandfather had been among the worst kings in Judah’s history. And yet Josiah chose to walk in the ways of David.

When Josiah is a young man, he sends his secretary, Shaphan, to the house of the LORD. Go to Hilkiah the high priest, he says. Let him count the money that the doorkeepers have collected from the people. And give it to the men in charge of repairing the temple, for the carpenters, builders and stonemasons, and to buy timber and cut stone.

Does that sound familiar? It is just like king Joash, long before, with his chest with a hole in the lid. And just as then, 'there was no reckoning made with them of the money that was delivered into their hand, because they dealt faithfully.'

And then, while the temple is being repaired, something wonderful is found.

The book of the law was God’s Word, given through Moses. Many think it was the book of Deuteronomy, or part of it. During the dark years of Manasseh, it seems it had been lost, or hidden, or simply forgotten. For years, the people of God had not even heard what God had said.

Shaphan goes back to the king and reports on the repairs. And then he says, Hilkiah the priest has given me a book. And he reads it aloud to the king.

'And it came to pass, when the king had heard the words of the book of the law, that he rent his clothes.' Josiah tore his clothes, a sign of deep grief and alarm. As he listened, he understood how far his people had wandered from God. He heard God’s commands, and God’s warnings about what would happen to a people who turned away. And he realised that his nation had broken every one.

Josiah commands Hilkiah, Shaphan and other officials, go and ask the LORD for me, and for the people, and for all Judah, about what is written in this book. For great is the anger of the LORD against us, because our fathers have not listened to the words of this book.

So they go to a prophetess named Huldah, who lives in Jerusalem. And Huldah gives them the word of the LORD.

First, a hard word. Thus saith the LORD, I will bring disaster on this place, and on its people, everything written in the book the king has read. Because they have forsaken me, and burned incense to other gods, my anger will be kindled against this place, and will not be quenched.

The people had gone too far. The judgment would still come.

But then Huldah has a personal word for Josiah himself.

Therefore, God says, you will be gathered to your fathers in peace, and your eyes will not see all the disaster I will bring on this place.

And the officials take Huldah’s answer back to the king.

So the judgment was still coming on Jerusalem. But because of Josiah’s tender heart, it would not come in his lifetime. And as we will see in the next chapter, Josiah did not just sit back. He acted.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 2. 'And he did that which was right in the sight of the LORD, and walked in all the way of David his father, and turned not aside to the right hand or to the left.' Turned not aside to the right hand or to the left. Like walking along a straight path, without wandering off on one side or the other. Josiah came from a family that had gone badly wrong. But he chose a different way. No matter what family we come from, we can choose to walk with God.

Verse 8. 'And Hilkiah the high priest said unto Shaphan the scribe, I have found the book of the law in the house of the LORD. And Hilkiah gave the book to Shaphan, and he read it.' God’s Word had been lost, in God’s own house. Imagine a church where the Bible has been forgotten. It is possible to keep going through religious routines while God’s Word gathers dust. But when God’s Word was found and read, everything began to change. Revival always begins with God’s Word.

Verse 19. 'Because thine heart was tender, and thou hast humbled thyself before the LORD, when thou heardest what I spake against this place, and against the inhabitants thereof, that they should become a desolation and a curse, and hast rent thy clothes, and wept before me; I also have heard thee, saith the LORD.' A tender heart is a soft heart, one that is easily moved by God’s Word, not hard or stubborn. When Josiah heard God’s Word, he did not argue or ignore it. He wept and humbled himself. And God heard him. God loves a tender heart.

The psalmist wrote, 'Thy word have I hid in mine heart, that I might not sin against thee.' And Isaiah heard God say, 'but to this man will I look, even to him that is poor and of a contrite spirit, and trembleth at my word.'

Is your Bible gathering dust? Pick it up today, and read a little. Ask God to give you a tender heart, that listens and responds to his Word. And if your family has not followed God, remember Josiah. You can choose a different way.

Here is today’s question. When Josiah heard God’s Word, he tore his clothes and humbled himself. How do you usually respond when God’s Word shows you something you need to change?

Let us pray. Lord God, thank you for your Word. Forgive me for the times I have let it gather dust. Give me a tender heart, like Josiah’s, that hears your Word and responds. Help me to walk in your ways, without turning to the right hand or to the left. Amen.
