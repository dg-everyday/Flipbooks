The Living Voice. 2 Samuel, chapter 6. David Danced Before the LORD with All His Might.

A great procession winds up the road toward the city. Trumpets blast. People shout for joy. And in front of it all, a king is dancing, leaping and spinning with all his strength, wearing a simple linen garment like a priest. He is not dancing for the crowd. He is dancing for God. And from a window above, his wife watches him, and her heart fills with scorn.

In chapter five, all the tribes of Israel came to Hebron and anointed David king over Israel. He took the stronghold of Zion, and it became the city of David. And David went on, and grew great, and the LORD God of hosts was with him. He asked the LORD twice before facing the Philistines, and God gave him victory both times.

Now, in chapter six, David wants to bring the ark of God to Jerusalem. Listen for what goes wrong the first time, and what David does differently the second time. And listen for his answer to Meekal.

David gathers the chosen men of Israel, and goes to bring up the ark of God from the house of Abinadab. Remember, the ark had stayed there for many years, ever since the Philistines sent it back.

They put the ark on a new cart. Two sons of Abinadab, Uzzah and Ahio, drive the cart. And David and all Israel celebrate before the LORD, with all kinds of instruments, harps, lyres, tambourines and cymbals.

It is a joyful day. But something is wrong. God’s law said that the ark must be carried on poles, on the shoulders of the Levites. Not on a cart, like the Philistines had done.

When they come to a threshing floor, the oxen stumble. And Uzzah reaches out his hand and takes hold of the ark of God. And the anger of the LORD burns against Uzzah, and God strikes him there, and he dies beside the ark.

It is a shocking moment. Uzzah may have meant well, trying to steady the ark. But God’s law said that no one was to touch the holy things. God is holy, and he cannot be treated carelessly, even with good intentions.

David is upset, because the LORD had broken out against Uzzah. And David is afraid of the LORD that day. He says, how can the ark of the LORD ever come to me?

So David does not take the ark into the city of David. He takes it instead to the house of a man named Obed-edom.

Then someone tells King David that the LORD has blessed the house of Obed-edom because of the ark of God. So David goes and brings up the ark from Obed-edom’s house to the city of David, with great joy.

This time, they do it God’s way. The ark is carried by men, not on a cart. And when those carrying it have gone six steps, David offers sacrifices of an ox and a fattened calf.

So David and all the house of Israel bring up the ark of the LORD with shouting, and with the sound of trumpets.

But as the ark comes into the city of David, Meekal, Saul’s daughter, looks out of a window. She sees King David leaping and dancing before the LORD. And she despises him in her heart.

They bring the ark of the LORD and set it in its place, inside the tent David had set up for it. And David offers burnt offerings and peace offerings before the LORD. Then he blesses the people in the name of the LORD of hosts. And he gives every person in the crowd, men and women alike, a loaf of bread, a good piece of meat and a flagon of wine. And everyone goes home.

Then David goes home to bless his own family. But Meekal comes out to meet him, full of scorn. How glorious was the king of Israel today, she says sarcastically, dancing in front of the servant girls, like a common fool, with no dignity at all.

Meekal thought a king should be dignified and proud. She was ashamed of a king who would make himself humble before God. Perhaps she was thinking of her father Saul, who always cared about how he looked to the people.

But David answers her.

David goes on. I will be even more humble than this, and lowly in my own eyes. But the servant girls you spoke of will honour me.

And the chapter ends sadly. Meekal, the daughter of Saul, had no children to the day of her death.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 11. 'And the ark of the LORD continued in the house of Obed-edom the Gittite three months: and the LORD blessed Obed-edom, and all his household.' The same ark that brought judgment on Uzzah brought blessing on Obed-edom. God’s presence is both holy and good. When we treat God carelessly, it is dangerous. But when we welcome him with reverence, his presence brings blessing to our whole household.

Verse 14. 'And David danced before the LORD with all his might; and David was girded with a linen ephod.' With all his might. David held nothing back in his worship. He did not care about looking like a king. He laid aside his royal robes, and put on a simple linen garment, like a priest. He was full of joy that God’s presence was coming to his city. Worship is meant to come from the whole heart.

Verse 21. 'And David said unto Meekal, It was before the LORD, which chose me before thy father, and before all his house, to appoint me ruler over the people of the LORD, over Israel: therefore will I play before the LORD.' It was before the LORD. David’s dancing was not for the crowd, or for Meekal. It was for God, who had chosen him. When we worship, we do it for an audience of one. What others think matters far less than what God thinks.

A psalm says, 'Let them praise his name in the dance: let them sing praises unto him with the timbrel and harp.' And Jesus said, 'thou shalt love the Lord thy God with all thy heart, and with all thy soul, and with all thy mind, and with all thy strength'.

Worship God today with all your heart, without worrying about what others think. Sing, pray, give thanks, with all your might. And ask God to show you if there is any way you have been treating him carelessly.

Here is today’s question. David danced before the LORD with all his might, while Meekal despised him. What holds you back from worshipping God wholeheartedly?

Let us pray. Lord God, you are holy, and your presence is good. Forgive me for the times I have treated you carelessly. Teach me to worship you with all my heart and all my might, caring more about what you think than what others think. It is before you that I live. Amen.
