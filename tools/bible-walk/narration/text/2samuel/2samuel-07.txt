The Living Voice. 2 Samuel, chapter 7. I Took Thee from the Sheepcote.

A king sits in his beautiful new palace, with walls of fragrant cedar wood. And he feels uncomfortable. He has a fine house, but the ark of God is still in a tent. So he decides to build God a house, the most beautiful building in the world. But God has other ideas. God is going to build David a house. And it will be a house that lasts for ever.

In chapter six, David brought the ark of God to Jerusalem. The first time, they carried it on a cart, and Uzzah died when he touched it. The second time, they carried it God’s way, and David danced before the LORD with all his might. Michal despised him, but David said, it was before the LORD.

Now, in chapter seven, we come to one of the most important chapters in the whole Bible. Listen for what David wants to do for God. And listen for what God promises to do for David.

The king is settled in his palace, and the LORD has given him rest from all his enemies around him.

And David says to Nathan the prophet, look, I live in a house of cedar, but the ark of God stays inside a tent.

It is a good thought. David loves God, and wants to honour him. And Nathan says, go and do whatever is in your heart, for the LORD is with you.

But before long, the word of the LORD comes to Nathan.

Go and tell my servant David, God says. Are you the one to build me a house to live in? I have not lived in a house since the day I brought Israel up out of Egypt. I have moved from place to place with a tent as my dwelling. Did I ever ask any of the leaders of Israel, why have you not built me a house of cedar?

Nathan had said yes too quickly. Even a good prophet needed to wait and listen to what God actually said.

And then God reminds David where he came from.

I have been with you wherever you have gone. I have cut off all your enemies before you. I will make your name great, like the names of the greatest men on earth. And I will give my people Israel a place of their own, where they can live in peace, and not be troubled any more.

And then comes the great reversal. David wanted to build God a house. But God says, the LORD will build you a house.

Not a house of wood and stone. A house in the sense of a family, a dynasty, a line of kings.

God says, when your days are over, and you rest with your fathers, I will raise up your son after you, and I will establish his kingdom.

God goes on. I will be his father, and he will be my son. If he does wrong, I will correct him, as a father corrects his son. But my mercy will not depart from him, as I took it from Saul.

And God makes the promise that runs through the whole rest of the Bible. Your house and your kingdom shall be established for ever before me. Your throne shall be established for ever.

For ever. Not for a few generations. For ever.

Nathan tells David everything God has said.

Then King David goes in, and sits before the LORD. And he prays one of the most humble prayers in the Bible. Who am I, O Lord GOD, and what is my family, that you have brought me this far? And as if that were not enough, you have spoken about my family far into the future. What more can David say to you? For you know your servant, Lord GOD.

David is overwhelmed. He came to give God something. And instead, God has given him more than he could ever have imagined.

David goes on praising God.

And David remembers what God has done for Israel. What other nation on earth is like your people Israel, whom God went to rescue as his own people, from Egypt and from the nations and their gods?

And David ends by asking God to do just what he has promised. Now, O LORD God, keep the promise you have made, for ever. Do as you have said. You are God, and your words are true. Bless the house of your servant, that it may continue for ever before you.

This promise was partly fulfilled in David’s son Solomon, who built the temple. But Solomon’s kingdom did not last for ever. And so God’s people kept waiting for a greater son of David. The New Testament begins by calling Jesus the son of David. And the angel told Mary that God would give her son the throne of his father David, and of his kingdom there shall be no end.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 8. 'Now therefore so shalt thou say unto my servant David, Thus saith the LORD of hosts, I took thee from the sheepcote, from following the sheep, to be ruler over my people, over Israel:' The sheepcote was the sheep pen. God reminds David, I found you with the sheep. Everything you have, I gave you. It is good for us to remember where we came from, and what God has done. Pride forgets. Humility remembers.

Verse 13. 'He shall build an house for my name, and I will stablish the throne of his kingdom for ever.' David’s son would build the temple, God’s house. And God would establish his throne for ever. David wanted to build for God, but God wanted to build for David. That is how God’s grace works. We do not earn God’s blessing by what we do for him. He blesses us first, out of his love.

Verse 22. 'Wherefore thou art great, O LORD God: for there is none like thee, neither is there any God beside thee, according to all that we have heard with our ears.' David responds to God’s promise with worship. There is none like thee. When we truly understand God’s grace to us, it fills us with praise. God is great, not just because he is powerful, but because he is so generous to undeserving people like us.

The angel said to Mary, 'the Lord God shall give unto him the throne of his father David: And he shall reign over the house of Jacob for ever; and of his kingdom there shall be no end.' And Paul wrote that Jesus 'was made of the seed of David according to the flesh'.

Take time today to sit before the LORD, as David did. Remember where God found you, and what he has done for you. And pray David’s prayer: who am I, O Lord GOD, that you have brought me this far?

Here is today’s question. David wanted to build a house for God, but God said he would build a house for David. What does this teach us about how God’s grace works?

Let us pray. O Lord GOD, who am I, that you have brought me this far? Thank you for all you have done for me, which I could never earn. Thank you for your promise to David, fulfilled in Jesus, whose kingdom will have no end. There is none like you. Amen.
