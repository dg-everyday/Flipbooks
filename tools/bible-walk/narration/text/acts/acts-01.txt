The Living Voice. Acts, chapter 1. Ye Shall Be Witnesses unto Me.

The disciples stand on a hill, staring up at the sky. Jesus has just been lifted up before their eyes, and a cloud has hidden him. They keep looking up, as if he might come straight back. Then two men in white clothing are standing beside them, with a question: why are you standing here, gazing up into heaven?

The Gospel of Luke ended with the risen Jesus telling his disciples to wait in Jerusalem until they were clothed with power from on high. Then he blessed them, and was carried up into heaven. The book of Acts begins by telling that same moment again, with more detail. Luke writes again to Theophilus, and picks up the story right where he left it.

This chapter is a bridge. Jesus is leaving, and the Holy Ghost has not yet come. The disciples are in between, waiting. Watch what they do while they wait.

Luke reminds Theophilus that his first book told of all that Jesus began to do and to teach, until the day he was taken up. After his suffering, Jesus showed himself alive to the apostles 'by many infallible proofs', appearing to them over forty days, and speaking about the kingdom of God.

He commands them not to leave Jerusalem, but to wait for the promise of the Father. John baptized with water, he says, but you shall be baptized with the Holy Ghost not many days from now.

The disciples ask him, 'Lord, wilt thou at this time restore again the kingdom to Israel?' They are still thinking of a kingdom on earth, and wondering when. Jesus answers, 'It is not for you to know the times or the seasons, which the Father hath put in his own power.' And then he gives them their task instead.

When he has spoken these things, while they are watching, he is taken up, and a cloud receives him out of their sight. As they gaze up toward heaven, two men stand by them in white clothing, and they speak.

So the disciples return to Jerusalem from the Mount of Olives, a short walk from the city. They go up into an upper room, where they are staying. Luke names them: Peter, James, John and Andrew; Philip and Thomas; Bartholomew and Matthew; James the son of Alphaeus, Simon the Zealot, and Judas the brother of James. Eleven names. And they are not alone. The women are with them, and Mary the mother of Jesus, and his brothers, who once did not believe in him.

In those days Peter stands up among about a hundred and twenty believers. He speaks about Judas, who guided those who arrested Jesus. Judas had been one of them, but he bought a field with the reward of his wickedness, and died a terrible death there. Everyone in Jerusalem heard about it, and they called that field the field of blood.

Peter says the Scriptures spoke of this, in the book of Psalms: 'his bishoprick let another take.' Bishoprick here means his office, his place of service. So someone must take Judas’ place among the twelve. It must be a man who was with them the whole time Jesus went in and out among them, from the baptism of John until the day he was taken up. He must be a witness with them of the resurrection.

They put forward two men: Joseph called Barsabas, who was also called Justus, and Matthias. Then they pray, 'Thou, Lord, which knowest the hearts of all men, shew whether of these two thou hast chosen'. They cast lots, and the lot falls on Matthias. And he is counted with the eleven apostles.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 8. 'But ye shall receive power, after that the Holy Ghost is come upon you: and ye shall be witnesses unto me both in Jerusalem, and in all Judea, and in Samaria, and unto the uttermost part of the earth.' This verse is the map of the whole book of Acts. First Jerusalem, then the land around it, then Samaria, then the ends of the earth. Notice what Jesus promises: power. Not their own strength, but the Spirit’s. And notice what he calls them: witnesses. A witness simply tells what he has seen and heard. You do not need to be clever to be a witness. You need to know Jesus.

Verse 11. 'Which also said, Ye men of Galilee, why stand ye gazing up into heaven? this same Jesus, which is taken up from you into heaven, shall so come in like manner as ye have seen him go into heaven.' The angels give two messages. First, stop staring; there is work to do. Second, this same Jesus will come again. Not a different Jesus, not just a feeling or an idea, but this same Jesus, the one they knew, the one with the nail prints in his hands. Christians live between his going and his coming, with work to do and a hope to hold.

Verse 14. 'These all continued with one accord in prayer and supplication, with the women, and Mary the mother of Jesus, and with his brethren.' Supplication means earnest asking. Jesus told them to wait, and they waited by praying, together, with one accord, which means with one mind and heart. Men and women, the apostles and the family of Jesus, all praying together. The great things in Acts begin in a room full of people praying.

Long before, the prophet Joel had spoken for God: 'I will pour out my spirit upon all flesh'. And Jesus himself had said, near the end of Luke’s Gospel, 'behold, I send the promise of my Father upon you'. Acts chapter one is a room full of people waiting for that promise to be kept.

Are you waiting for something from God? Do what the disciples did: keep praying, and keep praying with others. And remember what Jesus calls you: a witness. You do not have to explain everything. Just tell someone what Jesus means to you.

Here is today’s question. Jesus said, ye shall be witnesses unto me. What is one thing you have seen Jesus do in your life that you could tell someone?

Let us pray. Lord Jesus, thank you that you are alive, and that you will come again, this same Jesus. While I wait, teach me to pray with others, fill me with your Spirit, and make me your witness, close to home and far away. Amen.
