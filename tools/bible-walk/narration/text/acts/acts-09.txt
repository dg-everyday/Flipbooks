The Living Voice. Acts, chapter 9. He Is a Chosen Vessel unto Me.

A man is riding toward the city of Damascus, carrying letters from the high priest. The letters give him permission to arrest anyone there who follows Jesus, men or women, and bring them back to Jerusalem in chains. He is breathing out threats with every step. Then, suddenly, a light from heaven flashes all around him, brighter than the sun, and he falls to the ground.

In chapter eight, a great persecution scattered the believers, and Saul made havoc of the church, dragging men and women off to prison. But the scattered believers went everywhere preaching the word. Philip took the good news to Samaria, and to an official from Ethiopia on a desert road.

Now, in chapter nine, the persecutor himself meets Jesus. This is one of the most important moments in the whole book of Acts. The fiercest enemy of the church is about to become its greatest missionary.

Saul, still breathing out threatenings and slaughter against the disciples of the Lord, goes to the high priest and asks for letters to the synagogues of Damascus. If he finds any of the Way, as the followers of Jesus are called, he will bring them bound to Jerusalem.

As he comes near Damascus, a light shines around him from heaven. He falls to the earth, and hears a voice. Saul asks, 'Who art thou, Lord?' And the Lord says, 'I am Jesus whom thou persecutest'. Saul, trembling and astonished, says, Lord, what wilt thou have me to do? Jesus tells him, arise, go into the city, and you will be told what you must do.

The men travelling with Saul stand speechless, hearing a voice but seeing no one. Saul gets up from the ground, but when his eyes are opened, he sees nothing. They lead him by the hand into Damascus. For three days he cannot see, and he neither eats nor drinks.

In Damascus there is a disciple named Ananias. The Lord speaks to him in a vision. Go to the street called Straight, he says, and ask in the house of Judas for a man of Tarsus called Saul. He is praying, and he has seen in a vision a man named Ananias coming in and laying his hand on him, so that he might receive his sight.

Ananias is afraid. Lord, he says, I have heard from many about this man, how much evil he has done to your saints in Jerusalem. And here he has authority from the chief priests to arrest all who call on your name. But the Lord answers him. And he adds, 'For I will shew him how great things he must suffer for my name’s sake.'

So Ananias goes. He enters the house, lays his hands on Saul, and says, 'Brother Saul'. Brother. The man who came to arrest the believers is welcomed as family. The Lord Jesus, who appeared to you on the road, has sent me, Ananias says, that you might receive your sight and be filled with the Holy Ghost. Immediately something like scales falls from Saul’s eyes, and he can see. He gets up and is baptized. He eats, and his strength returns.

Straight away Saul preaches Christ in the synagogues, that he is the Son of God. Everyone who hears him is amazed. Is not this the man who destroyed those who called on this name in Jerusalem? But Saul grows stronger and stronger, and confounds the Jews in Damascus, proving that Jesus is the Christ.

After many days, his enemies plot to kill him. They watch the city gates constantly, to catch him. But the disciples take him and let him down through an opening in the wall, in a basket.

When Saul comes to Jerusalem, the disciples are all afraid of him. But Barnabas, the son of encouragement, brings him to the apostles, and tells them how Saul saw the Lord on the road, and preached boldly at Damascus. So Saul stays with them, until some try to kill him, and the believers send him home to Tarsus.

Then Luke turns back to Peter. Peter travels through the country, and comes to the believers at Lydda. There he finds a man named Aeneas, who has been bedridden for eight years, paralysed. Peter says, 'Aeneas, Jesus Christ maketh thee whole: arise, and make thy bed.' And he rises immediately. All who live in Lydda and Saron see him, and turn to the Lord.

At Joppa, on the coast, a disciple named Tabitha, or Dorcas, who is full of good works and makes clothes for the widows, falls sick and dies. The disciples send for Peter, and the widows show him, weeping, the coats she made. Peter puts them all out of the room, kneels down, and prays. Then he turns to the body and says, 'Tabitha, arise.' She opens her eyes, sees Peter, and sits up. He gives her his hand, lifts her up, and presents her alive to the believers and the widows. It becomes known throughout Joppa, and many believe in the Lord.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 4. 'And he fell to the earth, and heard a voice saying unto him, Saul, Saul, why persecutest thou me?' Notice the word me. Saul had never laid a hand on Jesus himself. He was hunting Jesus’ followers. But Jesus says, when you persecute them, you persecute me. Jesus is so closely joined to his people that what happens to them happens to him. If you belong to him, he feels your pain as his own.

Verse 15. 'But the Lord said unto him, Go thy way: for he is a chosen vessel unto me, to bear my name before the Gentiles, and kings, and the children of Israel:' A vessel is a container, something made to carry. God chose Saul, of all people, to carry the name of Jesus to the nations. No one is beyond the reach of God’s grace. The person you think is the least likely to follow Jesus may be the very one he has chosen.

Verse 31. 'Then had the churches rest throughout all Judea and Galilee and Samaria, and were edified; and walking in the fear of the Lord, and in the comfort of the Holy Ghost, were multiplied.' Edified means built up, strengthened. With Saul now a believer, the persecution eased, and the churches grew. Notice the two things they walked in: the fear of the Lord, deep respect for God, and the comfort of the Holy Ghost. Both together. Reverence and comfort, side by side.

Ananias called Saul brother, and Barnabas stood by him when everyone else was afraid. Long before, God had said through the prophet Isaiah, 'my thoughts are not your thoughts, neither are your ways my ways'. No one expected God to choose the persecutor. But God’s ways are higher than ours.

Is there someone you have written off, someone you think will never turn to God? Pray for them. And be like Ananias and Barnabas. When someone new comes to Jesus, welcome them as family, even if you are unsure of them at first.

Here is today’s question. Jesus chose Saul, the enemy of the church, to carry his name. Who is someone you find hard to imagine following Jesus, that you could pray for today?

Let us pray. Lord Jesus, thank you that no one is beyond your reach. You turned Saul from your enemy into your chosen vessel. I pray for those who seem far from you. Make me brave like Ananias and kind like Barnabas, ready to welcome anyone you call. Amen.
