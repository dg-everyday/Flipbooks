The Living Voice. Acts, chapter 15. Through the Grace of the Lord Jesus Christ We Shall Be Saved.

A serious argument is shaking the young church. Some teachers have come down to Antioch from Judea, and they are telling the Gentile believers, unless you are circumcised and keep the law of Moses, you cannot be saved. Paul and Barnabas strongly disagree. The question is too important to leave unanswered. What must a person do to be saved?

In chapter fourteen, Paul and Barnabas finished their first great journey. At Lystra they were mistaken for gods, and then Paul was stoned and left for dead. They went back to strengthen the new believers, and returned to Antioch to tell how God had opened the door of faith unto the Gentiles.

But now that door is being questioned. In chapter fifteen, the leaders of the church meet in Jerusalem to settle the matter. It is one of the most important meetings in the history of the church.

Certain men come down from Judea and teach the believers in Antioch that unless they are circumcised, in the way Moses commanded, they cannot be saved. Circumcision was the sign of God’s covenant with Abraham, given to every Jewish boy. Paul and Barnabas have no small argument with them. So the church sends Paul, Barnabas and some others up to Jerusalem, to the apostles and elders, about this question.

On their way, they pass through Phenice and Samaria, telling about the conversion of the Gentiles, and bringing great joy to all the believers. In Jerusalem, the church, the apostles and the elders welcome them, and they tell all that God has done with them. But some believers who belong to the Pharisees stand up and say, the Gentiles must be circumcised, and commanded to keep the law of Moses.

The apostles and elders come together to consider the matter. After much discussion, Peter stands up. Brothers, you know that some time ago God chose me to speak the gospel to the Gentiles, so that they might hear and believe. God, who knows the heart, bore them witness, giving them the Holy Ghost, just as he did to us. 'Now therefore why tempt ye God, to put a yoke upon the neck of the disciples, which neither our fathers nor we were able to bear?' A yoke is the wooden bar laid across the neck of an ox. The law had been a heavy load that no one could carry perfectly.

Then the whole crowd keeps silent, and listens to Barnabas and Paul, as they tell what miracles and wonders God has done among the Gentiles through them.

When they finish, James speaks. This is James the brother of Jesus, a leader of the church in Jerusalem. Simeon, he says, using Peter’s Hebrew name, has told us how God first visited the Gentiles, to take out of them a people for his name. And this agrees with the words of the prophets. James quotes the prophet Amos, who said God would rebuild the fallen house of David, so that the rest of mankind might seek the Lord, and all the Gentiles who are called by his name.

So James gives his judgment: we should not trouble the Gentiles who are turning to God. Instead, let us write to them, asking them to keep away from food offered to idols, from sexual immorality, from meat of strangled animals, and from blood. These were things that would most offend their Jewish neighbours, and things tied to the worship of idols.

The apostles and elders, with the whole church, agree. They choose two leading men, Judas called Barsabas and Silas, to go back to Antioch with Paul and Barnabas, carrying a letter. The letter says, we have heard that some who went out from us have troubled you with words, unsettling your souls, though we gave them no such command. So we have sent you our beloved Barnabas and Paul, men who have risked their lives for the name of our Lord Jesus Christ.

In Antioch, they gather the believers and deliver the letter. When the believers read it, they rejoice at the encouragement. Judas and Silas, who are prophets themselves, encourage and strengthen them with many words.

Some days later, Paul says to Barnabas, let us go back and visit the believers in every city where we have preached. Barnabas wants to take John Mark with them. But Paul thinks it is not wise, because Mark left them in the middle of the first journey. The disagreement becomes so sharp that they part company. Barnabas takes Mark and sails to Cyprus. Paul chooses Silas, and goes through Syria and Cilicia, strengthening the churches. God uses both teams. And later in his life, Paul will write warmly of Mark, as a helper who is useful to him.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 9. 'And put no difference between us and them, purifying their hearts by faith.' Peter says God made no difference between Jewish and Gentile believers. Both had their hearts made pure the same way: by faith. Not by a sign on the body, or by keeping a list of rules, but by trusting God. Faith is the great equaliser. At the foot of the cross, everyone stands on level ground.

Verse 11. 'But we believe that through the grace of the Lord Jesus Christ we shall be saved, even as they.' Notice how Peter turns it around. You might expect him to say, the Gentiles will be saved just like us. Instead he says, we Jews will be saved just like them, through grace. Grace means God’s kindness that we do not deserve and cannot earn. No one is saved by being good enough. Everyone is saved by grace.

Verse 28. 'For it seemed good to the Holy Ghost, and to us, to lay upon you no greater burden than these necessary things;' Notice the words the Holy Ghost, and to us. The leaders prayed, listened to each other, looked at the Scriptures, and paid attention to what God was doing. And they reached a decision together, guided by the Spirit. And they chose not to lay heavy burdens on new believers. The church at its best works this way: seeking God together, and keeping the gospel free.

James quoted the prophet Amos: 'In that day will I raise up the tabernacle of David that is fallen'. And Amos went on, 'all the heathen, which are called by my name'. God always planned for the nations to be part of his people. The meeting in Jerusalem simply recognised what God had promised long before.

Is there any part of you that still thinks you have to earn God’s love? Rest in his grace today. And if you disagree with another believer, follow the pattern of this chapter: listen, look to the Scriptures, seek the Spirit together, and remember that even good people sometimes go separate ways.

Here is today’s question. Peter said, through the grace of the Lord Jesus Christ we shall be saved. What does it mean to you that you cannot earn God’s love, only receive it?

Let us pray. Lord Jesus, thank you that I am saved by your grace, not by my own goodness. Purify my heart by faith. Help me not to lay heavy burdens on others, but to share your free gift. And when I disagree with others, give me humility and love. Amen.
