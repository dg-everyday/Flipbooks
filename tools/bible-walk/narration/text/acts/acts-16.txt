The Living Voice. Acts, chapter 16. Believe on the Lord Jesus Christ, and Thou Shalt Be Saved.

Two men sit in the deepest part of a prison, their backs torn open by a beating, their feet locked tight in wooden stocks. It is dark, and they are in pain. But instead of groaning, they are singing. The other prisoners lie listening in the darkness. And then the ground begins to shake.

In chapter fifteen, the apostles and elders met in Jerusalem and agreed that Gentiles are saved through the grace of the Lord Jesus Christ, without keeping the law of Moses. Paul and Barnabas disagreed about taking John Mark, and parted ways. Paul chose Silas, and set out to strengthen the churches.

Now, in chapter sixteen, the good news crosses the sea into Europe for the first time. And in this chapter, Luke starts saying we. He has joined Paul on the journey.

Paul and Silas come to Derbe and Lystra. There they meet a young disciple named Timothy, whose mother is a Jewish believer and whose father is a Greek. The believers speak well of him, and Paul takes him along. As they travel through the cities, delivering the decisions from Jerusalem, the churches are strengthened in the faith and grow in number every day.

Then something strange happens. They try to preach in the province of Asia, but the Holy Ghost forbids them. They try to go into Bithynia, but the Spirit does not allow them. Door after door seems shut. So they come down to Troas, by the sea. There Paul sees a vision: a man of Macedonia standing and pleading with him, 'Come over into Macedonia, and help us.' Immediately, Luke writes, we tried to go into Macedonia, sure that God had called us to preach the gospel there.

They sail across, and come to Philippi, a leading city of that part of Macedonia, and a Roman colony. On the sabbath they go out of the city gate, down by a river, where people gather to pray. They sit down and speak to the women who have come together there. One of them is a woman named Lydia.

Lydia and her household are baptized, and she urges them to stay at her home. 'If ye have judged me to be faithful to the Lord, come into my house, and abide there.' And she will not take no for an answer.

As they go to the place of prayer, they are met by a slave girl with a spirit of divination, a fortune-telling spirit. She earns a lot of money for her masters by telling fortunes. She follows Paul and his friends, crying out, 'These men are the servants of the most high God, which shew unto us the way of salvation.' She does this for many days. Finally Paul, grieved, turns and says to the spirit, I command thee in the name of Jesus Christ to come out of her. And it comes out that same hour.

When her masters see that their hope of making money is gone, they seize Paul and Silas and drag them before the magistrates. These men are Jews, they say, and they are troubling our city with customs that are not lawful for Romans. The crowd rises up against them. The magistrates tear the clothes off Paul and Silas, and order them to be beaten with rods. After many blows, they are thrown into prison. The jailer is charged to keep them safely, so he puts them in the inner prison, and fastens their feet in the stocks.

Then comes the singing in the dark, and suddenly there is a great earthquake. The foundations of the prison are shaken, every door flies open, and everyone’s chains come loose. The jailer wakes, sees the prison doors open, and draws his sword to kill himself, thinking the prisoners have escaped. But Paul cries out with a loud voice, 'Do thyself no harm: for we are all here.'

The jailer calls for a light, rushes in, and falls trembling before Paul and Silas. He brings them out and says, 'Sirs, what must I do to be saved?' And they answer him. They speak the word of the Lord to him and to everyone in his house. That same hour he washes their wounds, and he and all his family are baptized immediately. He brings them into his house, sets food before them, and rejoices, believing in God with all his household.

At first light, the magistrates send officers to say, let those men go. But Paul says, they have beaten us openly, uncondemned, though we are Romans, and thrown us into prison. Now do they want to put us out secretly? No. Let them come themselves and bring us out. The magistrates are afraid when they hear that Paul and Silas are Roman citizens. They come and plead with them, and ask them to leave the city. Paul and Silas go to Lydia’s house, encourage the believers there, and then depart.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 14. 'And a certain woman named Lydia, a seller of purple, of the city of Thyatira, which worshipped God, heard us: whose heart the Lord opened, that she attended unto the things which were spoken of Paul.' Lydia was a businesswoman who sold expensive purple cloth. She already worshipped God. But notice who opened her heart: the Lord. Paul spoke, but God did the opening. That is how every person comes to faith. We speak, and God opens hearts.

Verse 25. 'And at midnight Paul and Silas prayed, and sang praises unto God: and the prisoners heard them.' They had been beaten and locked in the stocks, for doing good. And they sang. Not because they felt fine, but because God was still worthy of praise. And notice the last words: the prisoners heard them. When we praise God in hard times, others are listening, and our faith can point them to him.

Verse 31. 'And they said, Believe on the Lord Jesus Christ, and thou shalt be saved, and thy house.' The jailer asked the most important question anyone can ask: what must I do to be saved? And the answer was simple. Believe on the Lord Jesus Christ. Not do enough good things, not become a better person first. Believe on him, trust in him. And the promise reached his whole family, who heard the word and believed too.

Long before, a psalm writer said that God gives 'songs in the night'. And Isaiah wrote that God would send his Servant 'To open the blind eyes, to bring out the prisoners from the prison'. In Philippi, God opened a prison, and opened a jailer’s heart.

Is there a dark place in your life right now? Try doing what Paul and Silas did: pray, and sing praise to God, even there. And if anyone ever asks you, what must I do to be saved, you know the answer. Believe on the Lord Jesus Christ.

Here is today’s question. The jailer asked, Sirs, what must I do to be saved? How would you answer that question in your own words?

Let us pray. Lord Jesus, I believe on you. Thank you that whoever believes on you shall be saved. Open the hearts of the people I love, as you opened Lydia’s heart. And when I am in a dark place, give me a song of praise, so that others may hear and come to you. Amen.
