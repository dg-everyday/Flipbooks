The Living Voice. Acts, chapter 17. In Him We Live, and Move, and Have Our Being.

Paul walks alone through the streets of Athens, the most famous city of learning in the world. Everywhere he looks there are statues and altars to gods, on every corner, in every square. And then he notices one altar with a strange inscription carved on it: TO THE UNKNOWN GOD. The people of Athens are worshipping a God they do not know. And Paul knows exactly who he is.

In chapter sixteen, Paul was called across the sea to Macedonia. At Philippi, Lydia believed, and Paul and Silas were beaten and thrown into prison, where they sang praises to God. An earthquake opened the doors, and the jailer and his household believed on the Lord Jesus Christ.

Now, in chapter seventeen, Paul travels on through three very different cities. In each one, people respond to the gospel in different ways. Watch how Paul speaks to each audience.

Paul and Silas come to Thessalonica, where there is a synagogue. As his custom is, Paul goes in, and for three sabbath days reasons with them from the Scriptures. He explains and shows that the Christ had to suffer and rise again from the dead, and he says, this Jesus, whom I preach to you, is the Christ. Some of the Jews believe, along with a great crowd of devout Greeks and many leading women.

But others, moved with envy, gather some troublemakers from the marketplace, form a mob, and set the whole city in an uproar. They attack the house of a believer named Jason, looking for Paul and Silas. When they cannot find them, they drag Jason before the city rulers, shouting. They say these men are acting against the decrees of Caesar, saying that there is another king, one Jesus. The rulers are troubled, and make Jason pay a bond before letting him go.

The believers send Paul and Silas away to Berea. There, too, they go into the synagogue. And the Bereans respond in a way that Luke wants every reader to notice. Many of them believe, along with honourable Greek women and men. But when the troublemakers from Thessalonica hear that Paul is preaching in Berea, they come there too and stir up the people. So the believers send Paul to the coast, and on to Athens, while Silas and Timothy stay behind.

While Paul waits in Athens, his spirit is stirred within him when he sees the city wholly given to idolatry. He reasons in the synagogue, and every day in the marketplace with whoever he meets. Some philosophers meet him, Epicureans and Stoics. Some say, 'What will this babbler say?' Others say, he seems to be preaching strange gods, because he preaches Jesus and the resurrection. They take him to the Areopagus, also called Mars’ hill, where the council of Athens meets. May we know what this new teaching is? they ask. For all the Athenians and visitors there spend their time doing nothing but telling or hearing some new thing.

Paul stands in the middle of Mars’ hill and speaks. Men of Athens, I see that in every way you are very religious. As I passed by and looked at your objects of worship, I found an altar with this inscription, TO THE UNKNOWN GOD. 'Whom therefore ye ignorantly worship, him declare I unto you.'

And Paul tells them about the true God. God made the world and everything in it. He is Lord of heaven and earth, and does not live in temples made with hands. He does not need anything from us, for he gives to all life, and breath, and all things. He made every nation from one man, and set the times and places where they would live, so that they would seek the Lord, and perhaps reach out for him and find him, though he is not far from any one of us. Then Paul quotes one of their own poets.

Since we are God’s offspring, Paul says, we should not think that God is like gold or silver or stone, carved by human skill. In the past God overlooked such ignorance, but now he commands all people everywhere to repent. He has fixed a day when he will judge the world in righteousness by the man he has appointed. And he has given assurance of this to everyone by raising him from the dead.

When they hear of the resurrection of the dead, some mock. Others say, we will hear you again about this. So Paul leaves them. But some people join him and believe, among them Dionysius, a member of the council of the Areopagus, and a woman named Damaris, and others with them.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 6. 'And when they found them not, they drew Jason and certain brethren unto the rulers of the city, crying, These that have turned the world upside down are come hither also;' This was meant as an accusation. But it is one of the greatest compliments ever paid to the church. A small group of ordinary people, with no power or wealth, were turning the world upside down. Or perhaps, right side up. The good news of Jesus changes everything it touches.

Verse 11. 'These were more noble than those in Thessalonica, in that they received the word with all readiness of mind, and searched the scriptures daily, whether those things were so.' The Bereans did two things. They listened eagerly, with readiness of mind. And they checked what Paul said against the Scriptures, every day. They did not believe everything they heard, and they did not reject it either. They went to God’s Word to see if it was true. Luke calls that noble. It is a good habit for every listener, including listeners to The Living Voice.

Verse 28. 'For in him we live, and move, and have our being; as certain also of your own poets have said, For we are also his offspring.' Paul starts where the Athenians are, with words from their own poets, and points them to the true God. Every breath you take, every step, every heartbeat, is held in God’s hands. He is not far away. He is closer than the air you breathe. And he made you to seek him, and to find him.

Paul told the Athenians that God does not live in temples made with hands. When King Solomon dedicated the temple in Jerusalem, he prayed, 'behold, the heaven and heaven of heavens cannot contain thee; how much less this house that I have builded?' The God of Solomon is the God of Mars’ hill, the maker of all things, who is not far from any one of us.

Be like the Bereans. When you hear teaching about God, open your Bible and check it for yourself. And remember that God is not far from you. In him you live, and move, and have your being. Take a moment today simply to thank him for your life and breath.

Here is today’s question. The Bereans searched the scriptures daily, whether those things were so. How could you make searching the Scriptures a daily habit?

Let us pray. Lord God, maker of heaven and earth, in you I live, and move, and have my being. Thank you that you are not far from me. Give me a ready mind and a searching heart, like the Bereans, and help me to seek you and find you every day. Amen.
