The Living Voice. Acts, chapter 25. I Appeal unto Caesar.

Paul has been a prisoner for a long time, waiting for a decision that never comes. Now a new governor wants to send him back to Jerusalem for trial, where enemies are already planning to ambush him on the road. Paul has one right left, a right given to every Roman citizen. He stands up and says four words that will change the rest of his life: I appeal unto Caesar.

In chapter twenty-four, Paul stood trial before the governor Felix, and spoke to him about righteousness, self-control, and the judgment to come. Felix trembled, but put off his decision, hoping for a bribe. A long time passed, and Felix left Paul in prison when a new governor, Porcius Festus, took his place.

Now, in chapter twenty-five, Festus hears Paul’s case. And a king comes to visit. Remember what the Lord told Paul in chapter twenty-three: you must bear witness also at Rome. Watch how God begins to make that happen.

Three days after arriving in his province, Festus goes up from Caesarea to Jerusalem. The high priest and the chief men of the Jews tell him their case against Paul. They ask him, as a favour, to bring Paul to Jerusalem, planning to kill him on the way. But Festus answers that Paul is being kept at Caesarea, and that he himself will be going there shortly. Let those of you who are able come down with me, he says, and accuse the man, if there is anything wrong in him.

After staying more than ten days, Festus goes down to Caesarea. The next day he sits on the judgment seat, and orders Paul to be brought. The Jewish leaders from Jerusalem stand around him, and bring many serious charges against Paul, which they cannot prove.

But Festus, wanting to do the Jewish leaders a favour, asks Paul, will you go up to Jerusalem, and be judged there before me about these things?

Paul answers, 'I stand at Caesar’s judgment seat, where I ought to be judged: to the Jews have I done no wrong, as thou very well knowest.'

Festus speaks with his council, and then answers, 'Hast thou appealed unto Caesar? unto Caesar shalt thou go.'

Some days later, King Agrippa and his sister Bernice come to Caesarea to welcome Festus. This is Herod Agrippa the Second, the son of the Herod who killed James and imprisoned Peter, and the great-grandson of the Herod who ruled when Jesus was born. He knows the Jewish customs well. As they stay many days, Festus tells the king about Paul.

There is a man left in prison by Felix, Festus says. When I was in Jerusalem, the chief priests and elders asked me to condemn him. I told them it is not the Roman way to hand over any man to die, before the accused meets his accusers face to face, and has a chance to answer the charge. So when they came here, I sat on the judgment seat the very next day. But when his accusers stood up, they brought no charge of the kind of crimes I expected.

Because I was unsure how to investigate such questions, Festus says, I asked him whether he would go to Jerusalem and be judged there. But Paul appealed to be kept for the decision of Augustus, the emperor. So I ordered him to be kept until I could send him to Caesar.

Agrippa says to Festus, 'I would also hear the man myself.' You shall hear him, Festus says.

The next day Agrippa and Bernice come with great pomp, and enter the hall of hearing with the military commanders and the leading men of the city. At Festus’ command, Paul is brought in. Festus says, King Agrippa, and all of you here, you see this man, about whom the whole Jewish people have pleaded with me, both in Jerusalem and here, crying that he ought not to live any longer. But I found that he had done nothing worthy of death. And since he himself has appealed to Augustus, I have decided to send him.

But I have nothing definite to write to my lord the emperor about him. That is why I have brought him before you, and especially before you, King Agrippa, so that after examining him, I may have something to write. 'For it seemeth to me unreasonable to send a prisoner, and not withal to signify the crimes laid against him.'

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 8. 'While he answered for himself, Neither against the law of the Jews, neither against the temple, nor yet against Caesar, have I offended any thing at all.' Paul answered every charge, calmly and clearly. He had not broken the law of Moses, he had not harmed the temple, and he had not rebelled against Rome. Christians are called to live in such a way that, when accusations come, they are simply not true. Paul’s life was his best defence.

Verse 11. 'For if I be an offender, or have committed any thing worthy of death, I refuse not to die: but if there be none of these things whereof these accuse me, no man may deliver me unto them. I appeal unto Caesar.' Paul was not afraid to die. He had said so many times. But he was not going to throw his life away either, by walking into an ambush. He used his rights as a Roman citizen wisely. And through it, the Lord’s promise was kept: Paul would bear witness at Rome. God can use even courts and laws to accomplish his plans.

Verse 19. 'But had certain questions against him of their own superstition, and of one Jesus, which was dead, whom Paul affirmed to be alive.' Festus, a Roman, did not understand the case at all. But without meaning to, he summed up the whole message of Acts in one sentence. One Jesus, who was dead, whom Paul affirmed to be alive. That is the whole question. Everything depends on whether Jesus rose from the dead. Paul said he did. And Paul was willing to stake his life on it.

Long before, the prophet Daniel told a king, 'he removeth kings, and setteth up kings'. Proverbs says, 'The king’s heart is in the hand of the LORD, as the rivers of water: he turneth it whithersoever he will.' Governors and kings were making their decisions. But God was guiding every step of Paul’s road to Rome.

If someone asked you what you believe, could you say it as simply as Festus did? Jesus was dead, and he is alive. Think about how that truth changes your life today. And if you are caught in a situation you cannot control, remember that God is still guiding every step.

Here is today’s question. Festus said Paul affirmed that Jesus, who was dead, is alive. What difference does it make in your life today that Jesus is alive?

Let us pray. Lord Jesus, you were dead, and you are alive for evermore. Help me to live so that my life is my best defence. When I am caught in things I cannot control, help me to trust that you are guiding every step, as you guided Paul to Rome. Amen.
