The Living Voice. The One Great Story.

The Bible is not one book. It is sixty-six books, written by kings and shepherds, fishermen and farmers, a tax collector and a doctor, across many centuries and in three languages. You might expect a library of different stories. But when you step back far enough, something wonderful appears. It is one story. And at the very end of Luke’s Gospel, the risen Jesus walked with two friends on a road, and 'beginning at Moses and all the prophets, he expounded unto them in all the scriptures the things concerning himself.' The whole Bible, he was saying, is about me.

Welcome to The Big Picture, on The Living Voice. In thirty chapters, about eight minutes each, we will walk from the first page of the Bible to the last. We will not read every chapter. We will stop at the great turning points, the places where the story changes direction, so that you can see the whole road from beginning to end.

Here is the shape of the story, in seven movements.

First, creation. God makes a good world, and makes people in his own image, to know him and to care for it.

Second, the fall. People turn away from God, and sin and death come into the world. Everything good is now broken.

Third, the promise. God does not give up. He calls one man, Abraham, and promises that through his family all the families of the earth will be blessed.

Fourth, the rescue. Abraham’s family becomes a nation of slaves in Egypt, and God sets them free, gives them his law, and brings them into a land of their own.

Fifth, the kingdom. God gives his people a king, David, and promises that one of David’s sons will reign for ever. But the kings turn away, and the people are carried off into exile.

Sixth, the hope. In the darkest days the prophets speak of a new covenant, of a servant who will suffer for the sins of many, and of a King who will come.

And seventh, the King comes. Jesus is born, teaches, dies and rises again. His Spirit sends his followers into all the world. And the story ends where it began, with God making all things new.

Each chapter in this plan is walked the same way. First, where we are in the great story. Then the chapter itself, told as a story, with every speaker in the King James words. Then three verses held and explained, a thread to the rest of the Bible, something for today, a question, and a prayer.

As you listen, watch for threads that run through the whole Bible. Watch for a lamb. Watch for a king. Watch for a promise that God keeps, even when his people do not. And watch for God coming to live with his people, from a garden, to a tent in the desert, to a temple, to a manger, and at last to a city where he will dwell with them for ever.

Here is a question to carry with you. Before we begin, where do you see yourself in God’s great story today?

Let us pray. Lord God, you are the author of the whole story, from the beginning to the end. As I walk through these chapters, open my eyes to see your plan, your promises, and your Son on every page. Write my life into your story. Amen.
