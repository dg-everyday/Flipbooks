The Living Voice. Exodus, chapter 14. Stand Still, and See.

In front of them is the sea. Behind them, coming fast, is the army of Egypt, six hundred chosen chariots and more, dust rising, horses thundering. There are no boats. There is nowhere to run. A whole nation of freed slaves, with their children and their animals, is trapped on the shore. And they turn to Moses in terror.

In Exodus chapter three, God spoke to Moses from the burning bush and sent him to bring his people out of Egypt. Moses went to Pharaoh with God’s message: 'Let my people go'. Pharaoh refused. So God sent plague after plague on Egypt: water turned to blood, frogs, lice, flies, sickness, boils, hail, locusts and darkness. Ten plagues in all.

Before the last one, God told every family of Israel to kill a lamb and put its blood on the doorposts of their houses. God said, 'when I see the blood, I will pass over you'. That was the first Passover. The firstborn of Egypt died, but in every house marked with the blood of the lamb, the firstborn lived. And Pharaoh finally let them go.

Now, in chapter fourteen, the rescue is tested. In The Big Picture, this is the moment that Israel would remember for ever as God’s great salvation.

God leads his people by a pillar of cloud and a pillar of fire. And he tells Moses to turn back and camp by the sea. It looks like a mistake. But God says Pharaoh will think they are lost in the land, and come after them, and God will be honoured through it.

Pharaoh does change his mind. Why did we let our slaves go? He gathers his chariots and his horsemen, and chases them.

When the people of Israel look up and see the Egyptians marching after them, they are terrified. They cry out to the LORD, and they turn on Moses: 'Because there were no graves in Egypt, hast thou taken us away to die in the wilderness?' It would have been better to stay slaves than to die out here.

Moses answers them. Then God speaks to Moses: 'Wherefore criest thou unto me? speak unto the children of Israel, that they go forward'. Lift up your rod, stretch your hand over the sea, and divide it.

The pillar of cloud moves from in front of them to behind them, standing between the camp of Israel and the army of Egypt. To the Egyptians it is cloud and darkness. To Israel it gives light.

Moses stretches out his hand over the sea, and the LORD drives the sea back with a strong east wind, and makes the sea dry land. The waters are divided. And the people of Israel walk into the middle of the sea on dry ground, with the waters standing like a wall on their right hand and on their left.

The Egyptians follow them in, horses, chariots and horsemen. But God throws their army into confusion. Their chariot wheels come off, and they drive heavily. And the Egyptians cry out, 'Let us flee from the face of Israel; for the LORD fighteth for them'.

Then God tells Moses to stretch out his hand again. The sea returns to its strength. The waters cover the chariots and the horsemen, the whole army of Pharaoh that had followed them into the sea. Not one of them is left. And Israel sees the Egyptians dead on the seashore.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 13. 'And Moses said unto the people, Fear ye not, stand still, and see the salvation of the LORD, which he will shew to you to day: for the Egyptians whom ye have seen to day, ye shall see them again no more for ever.' Three commands: fear not, stand still, and see. Sometimes the hardest thing to do is nothing, to stop panicking, stop running, stop trying to save ourselves, and watch God work. Salvation here is not something the people did. It is something they saw the LORD do.

Verse 14. 'The LORD shall fight for you, and ye shall hold your peace.' Hold your peace means be quiet. The battle was the LORD’s. Israel had no army, no weapons, and no way of escape. They did not need one. When you face something far too big for you, remember who fights for his people.

Verse 31. 'And Israel saw that great work which the LORD did upon the Egyptians: and the people feared the LORD, and believed the LORD, and his servant Moses.' Before the sea opened, they were full of fear of Pharaoh. Afterward, they feared the LORD, which means they were filled with awe and reverence for him, and they believed. Seeing what God had done turned their fear into faith. That is still how faith grows: by remembering what God has done.

The Passover lamb and the crossing of the sea became the great picture of salvation in the whole Old Testament. And the New Testament says, 'For even Christ our passover is sacrificed for us'. Just as the blood of the lamb saved Israel’s firstborn, and God brought them through the waters out of slavery, Jesus’ death sets his people free from the slavery of sin.

What are you facing that feels like an army behind and a sea in front? Bring it to God. Do the next thing he tells you, go forward, and then stand still and let him fight for you. And take a moment to remember something God has already done for you.

Here is today’s question. Moses said, fear ye not, stand still, and see the salvation of the LORD. What fear will you hand over to God today, and let him fight for you?

Let us pray. LORD, you made a way through the sea when there was no way. Thank you for Jesus, my Passover Lamb, who sets me free. When I am afraid, help me to stand still and see your salvation, and to remember all that you have done. Fight for me. Amen.
