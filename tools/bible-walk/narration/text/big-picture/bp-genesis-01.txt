The Living Voice. Genesis, chapter 1. In the Beginning.

Before there was anything, there was God. The earth was 'without form, and void; and darkness was upon the face of the deep.' No light. No life. No sound. Only 'the Spirit of God moved upon the face of the waters.' And then God speaks, and the first words of the story ring out across the darkness: 'Let there be light: and there was light.'

This is the first step of The Big Picture, and the first page of the Bible. The word Genesis means beginnings, and here everything begins: the world, the people in it, and the story that runs all the way to the last page.

Christians have understood the days of this chapter in different ways. But the chapter is perfectly clear about the things that matter most: who made everything, how he made it, and what he thinks of it. Listen for a pattern that repeats like a song. God said. And it was so. And God saw that it was good.

On the first day, God speaks light into the darkness. He divides the light from the darkness, and 'God called the light Day, and the darkness he called Night. And the evening and the morning were the first day.'

On the second day, God makes the firmament, the wide sky, and separates the waters below from the waters above.

On the third day, God gathers the waters below into one place, and the dry land appears. He calls the dry land Earth, and the waters Seas. Then he speaks again, and the earth bursts into green: grass, plants with seed, and trees with fruit, each one 'after his kind'. And God saw that it was good.

On the fourth day, God sets lights in the sky: the sun to rule the day, the moon to rule the darkness, and then, almost in passing, Genesis adds, 'he made the stars also.' The countless stars that fill the heavens take five words.

On the fifth day, God fills the seas with living creatures, from great whales to the smallest fish, and fills the sky with birds. And he blesses them: 'Be fruitful, and multiply'.

On the sixth day, God makes the animals of the land, cattle and creeping things and wild beasts. And then something new happens. Until now God has said, let there be. Now he says, 'Let us make man in our image, after our likeness'. God makes people to be like him in a way no animal is, and he gives them a task: to rule over the earth and care for it, as he would. He blesses them, and gives them every plant and tree for food.

Then God looks at everything he has made. Seven times in this chapter Genesis has said that God saw that it was good. Now, at the end of the sixth day, it says something more.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 1. 'In the beginning God created the heaven and the earth.' The very first sentence of the Bible answers the biggest question anyone can ask: where did everything come from? The Bible does not begin by arguing that God exists. It begins with God already there, before anything else. He is not part of the world; he made it. Everything you can see, from the farthest star to the ground under your feet, is here because God spoke it into being. Nothing exists that he did not make, and nothing he made is beyond his care. And the God who made it all is the God who speaks to you today.

Verse 27. 'So God created man in his own image, in the image of God created he him; male and female created he them.' Three times in one verse Genesis says that God created people. And it says we are made in his image. That means every person, man or woman, young or old, rich or poor, strong or weak, carries something of God. This is why every human life has worth. It is also why we can know God, speak with him, and love him. You are not an accident. You were made on purpose, to reflect your Maker.

Verse 31. 'And God saw every thing that he had made, and, behold, it was very good. And the evening and the morning were the sixth day.' Not just good. Very good. Hold on to that, because in the next chapter of this plan you will see how the goodness was broken. The world we live in now has sickness, sin and death in it. That was not how it began, and the rest of the Bible tells how God is working to make it very good again.

The Gospel of John begins with the same three words: 'In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God.' And John says, 'All things were made by him; and without him was not any thing made that was made.' The Word is Jesus. The God who spoke light into darkness in Genesis is the same God who came into the world as light. And the last chapters of the Bible end where this one begins, with a new heaven and a new earth.

Look at something God made today: the sky, a tree, a bird, a face. Thank him for it. And remember that the people around you, even the ones who are hard to love, are made in the image of God. Treat them that way.

Here is today’s question. God made you in his own image. How will that change the way you see yourself, or someone else, today?

Let us pray. Lord God, Creator of heaven and earth, thank you for the light, for the world, and for my life. Thank you that I was made in your image, on purpose. Help me to see your goodness in what you have made, and to honour your image in every person I meet. Amen.
