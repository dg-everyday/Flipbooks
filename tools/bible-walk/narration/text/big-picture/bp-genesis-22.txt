The Living Voice. Genesis, chapter 22. God Will Provide Himself a Lamb.

A father and his son are climbing a mountain together. The son carries a bundle of wood on his back. The father carries fire and a knife. Halfway up, the boy turns and asks a question that must have pierced his father’s heart: 'Behold the fire and the wood: but where is the lamb for a burnt offering?'

In Genesis chapter twelve, God called Abram to leave his home and promised that through his family all the families of the earth would be blessed. But Abram and Sarai had no children. God changed their names to Abraham and Sarah, and promised again. They waited for many years, until Abraham was a hundred years old and Sarah was ninety. And then, just as God said, Sarah had a son. They named him Isaac, which means laughter.

Isaac is the son of the promise. Every promise God made to Abraham depends on this boy. And now, in chapter twenty-two, God asks Abraham to do the unthinkable. In The Big Picture, this chapter is a picture of what God himself will one day do.

Genesis begins by telling us something Abraham did not know: 'God did tempt Abraham'. In the old English of the King James Bible, tempt here means test. God is going to test Abraham’s trust. He calls, 'Abraham', and Abraham answers, 'Behold, here I am.'

Then God gives the command. Abraham is to take Isaac to the land of Moriah and offer him there as a burnt offering, on a mountain God will show him.

Abraham does not argue. He rises up early, saddles his donkey, takes two young servants and Isaac, cuts the wood, and sets out. On the third day he sees the place far off. He tells the servants, 'Abide ye here with the ass; and I and the lad will go yonder and worship, and come again to you.' Listen to those words. Not I will come back, but we will.

Abraham lays the wood on Isaac, and takes the fire and the knife, and the two of them walk on together. Then Isaac asks his question: where is the lamb? And Abraham answers.

They reach the place. Abraham builds an altar, lays the wood in order, binds Isaac, and lays him on the altar. He stretches out his hand and takes the knife.

And the angel of the LORD calls from heaven: 'Abraham, Abraham'. 'Here am I.' 'Lay not thine hand upon the lad, neither do thou any thing unto him: for now I know that thou fearest God, seeing thou hast not withheld thy son, thine only son from me.'

Abraham looks up, and behind him is a ram, caught in a thicket by its horns. He takes the ram and offers it up instead of his son. And he gives the place a name.

Then the angel calls again. Because you have done this, God says, I will bless you, and multiply your descendants as the stars of the heaven and as the sand on the seashore. 'And in thy seed shall all the nations of the earth be blessed; because thou hast obeyed my voice.'

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 2. 'And he said, Take now thy son, thine only son Isaac, whom thou lovest, and get thee into the land of Moriah; and offer him there for a burnt offering upon one of the mountains which I will tell thee of.' Listen to how God describes Isaac: thy son, thine only son, whom thou lovest. God knew exactly how much he was asking. This is hard to hear, and it should be. But God never meant Isaac to die, and he stopped Abraham’s hand. The test showed that Abraham trusted God more than anything else, even more than the gift God had given him. The New Testament says Abraham believed that God was able to raise Isaac up, even from the dead.

Verse 8. 'And Abraham said, My son, God will provide himself a lamb for a burnt offering: so they went both of them together.' Abraham did not know how God would do it. But he trusted that God would provide. And God did, with a ram in the thicket. But the words go further than Abraham knew. God would provide himself a lamb. One day God would provide his own Lamb, on a hill in that same region, for the whole world.

Verse 14. 'And Abraham called the name of that place Jehovah-jireh: as it is said to this day, In the mount of the LORD it shall be seen.' Jehovah-jireh means the LORD will provide, or the LORD will see to it. Abraham named the place not after his own faith, but after God’s faithfulness. When you look back on your hardest moments, the most important thing to remember is not how strong you were, but how God provided.

Long after, John the Baptist saw Jesus coming and cried out, 'Behold the Lamb of God, which taketh away the sin of the world.' And Paul wrote of God, 'He that spared not his own Son, but delivered him up for us all, how shall he not with him also freely give us all things?' God stopped Abraham from giving his only son. But God did not hold back his own.

Is there something you love so much that it has become more important to you than God? Hold it with an open hand. And whatever you are facing today, remember the name Abraham gave that mountain. The LORD will provide.

Here is today’s question. Abraham trusted that God would provide, even when he could not see how. Where do you need to trust God to provide today?

Let us pray. Lord, you are Jehovah-jireh, the LORD who provides. Thank you that you did not spare your own Son, but gave him up for me. Help me to love you above every gift you give, and to trust you to provide, even when I cannot see how. Amen.
