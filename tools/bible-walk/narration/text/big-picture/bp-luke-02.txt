The Living Voice. Luke, chapter 2. Good Tidings of Great Joy.

Shepherds are sitting in a field outside a small town, watching their sheep in the dark. It is quiet, and cold, and ordinary. Then suddenly an angel is standing in front of them, and the glory of the Lord shines all around them, brighter than anything they have ever seen. They are terrified. And the angel says, do not be afraid. I have news for you.

In Nehemiah chapter two, God’s people rebuilt the walls of Jerusalem. They were home again. But no king from David’s family sat on the throne, and the prophets went silent. For a long time, God’s people waited. Empires came and went. Now the Romans ruled the land.

Then the silence broke. In Luke chapter one, the angel Gabriel appeared to an old priest named Zacharias and told him that he and his wife would have a son, John, who would prepare the way of the Lord. Then Gabriel went to a young woman in Nazareth named Mary, engaged to a man named Joseph, of the family of David. He told her, 'thou shalt conceive in thy womb, and bring forth a son, and shalt call his name JESUS.' He would be called the Son of the Highest, and God would give him the throne of his father David.

In The Big Picture, this is the moment every promise has been waiting for. The King is born.

The Roman emperor, Caesar Augustus, orders a census of the whole empire. Everyone must go to his own city to be registered. So Joseph travels from Nazareth, in Galilee, to Bethlehem, the city of David, because he belongs to David’s family. Mary goes with him, and she is expecting her child.

While they are in Bethlehem, the time comes for the baby to be born. 'And she brought forth her firstborn son, and wrapped him in swaddling clothes, and laid him in a manger; because there was no room for them in the inn.' Swaddling clothes were strips of cloth wrapped snugly around a newborn. A manger is a feeding trough for animals. The King of kings begins his life in a place made for cattle.

Then the angel appears to the shepherds. And he gives them a sign: you will find the baby wrapped in swaddling clothes, lying in a manger. And suddenly the angel is joined by a great crowd of angels, praising God: 'Glory to God in the highest, and on earth peace, good will toward men.'

When the angels are gone, the shepherds say, let us go to Bethlehem and see this thing. They hurry, and they find Mary and Joseph, and the baby lying in the manger, just as they were told. They tell everyone what the angel said about the child, and all who hear it wonder. 'But Mary kept all these things, and pondered them in her heart.' The shepherds go back, glorifying and praising God.

When he is eight days old, the baby is named Jesus, the name the angel gave. Later, Mary and Joseph bring him to the temple in Jerusalem. There an old man named Simeon, who has been waiting for the comfort of Israel, takes the baby in his arms and blesses God. 'Lord, now lettest thou thy servant depart in peace, according to thy word: For mine eyes have seen thy salvation'. This child, he says, will be 'A light to lighten the Gentiles, and the glory of thy people Israel.' An old widow named Anna, who served God in the temple with fasting and prayers, also gives thanks and speaks of him to everyone waiting for redemption.

The chapter ends when Jesus is twelve. His family goes to Jerusalem for the Passover, and on the way home they realise he is missing. After three days of searching they find him in the temple, sitting among the teachers, listening and asking questions. Everyone is amazed at his understanding. His mother says they have been searching for him anxiously. And Jesus answers, 'wist ye not that I must be about my Father’s business?' Then he goes home with them to Nazareth, and is obedient to them.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 10. 'And the angel said unto them, Fear not: for, behold, I bring you good tidings of great joy, which shall be to all people.' Good tidings means good news. That is what the word gospel means. Notice who heard it first: not kings or priests, but shepherds, ordinary working men out in the fields with their sheep. And notice who it is for: to all people. The promise to Abraham, that all families of the earth would be blessed, is arriving.

Verse 11. 'For unto you is born this day in the city of David a Saviour, which is Christ the Lord.' Three titles for one baby. Saviour: the one who rescues. Christ: the anointed King God promised David. Lord: God himself. And notice the words, unto you. This birth is not just news about someone else. It is a gift for you.

Verse 52. 'And Jesus increased in wisdom and stature, and in favour with God and man.' Jesus did not skip childhood. He grew up as a real boy in a real family, learning, growing taller, obeying his parents. The Son of God shared every stage of human life. That means he understands ours.

Long before, the prophet Micah had written, 'But thou, Bethlehem Ephratah, though thou be little among the thousands of Judah, yet out of thee shall he come forth unto me that is to be ruler in Israel; whose goings forth have been from of old, from everlasting.' A ruler born in little Bethlehem, whose beginnings are from everlasting. The King promised to David had come.

God sent the greatest news in the world to ordinary people first. You do not have to be important to receive it. Like Mary, take time to ponder what God has done. And like the shepherds, tell someone.

Here is today’s question. The angel said, unto you is born a Saviour. What does it mean to you today that Jesus came for you?

Let us pray. Glory to God in the highest. Thank you, Father, for sending your Son, born in a manger, to be my Saviour, Christ the Lord. Like Mary, help me to treasure your works in my heart, and like the shepherds, to tell others the good tidings. Amen.
