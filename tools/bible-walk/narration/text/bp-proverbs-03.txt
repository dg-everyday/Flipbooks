The Living Voice. Proverbs, chapter 3. Trust in the LORD with All Thine Heart.

A father sits down with his son. He is not giving him money, or land, or a sword. He is giving him something he says is worth more than silver, more than gold, more than rubies. He leans in and says, my son, do not forget what I am about to tell you. Write it on the table of your heart. And then he gives him some of the best known words in the whole Bible.

In Psalm twenty-three, David sang that the LORD was his shepherd. When David died, his son Solomon became king. Early in his reign, God appeared to Solomon in a dream and said, ask what I shall give thee. Solomon could have asked for riches or a long life. Instead he asked, 'Give therefore thy servant an understanding heart to judge thy people, that I may discern between good and bad'. God was pleased, and gave Solomon wisdom greater than anyone’s, and riches and honour as well.

Solomon built the temple in Jerusalem, the house for God’s name that God had promised David his son would build. And he wrote many wise sayings, which are gathered in the book of Proverbs. A proverb is a short, wise saying about how to live. The book begins with its great theme: 'The fear of the LORD is the beginning of knowledge'.

In The Big Picture, this is the golden age of Israel’s kingdom. And Proverbs chapter three shows what it means to live wisely under God.

The chapter is a father’s teaching to his son. 'My son, forget not my law; but let thine heart keep my commandments'. If you do, he says, they will add long life and peace to you.

Let mercy and truth never leave you. Tie them around your neck, and write them on the table of your heart. Then you will find favour with God and with people.

Then come the famous words about trust. And right after them, a warning: 'Be not wise in thine own eyes: fear the LORD, and depart from evil.' Do not think you have it all worked out on your own.

Honour the LORD with your wealth, and with the first part of everything you earn. Give God the first, not the leftovers, and he will fill your barns.

Do not despise the LORD’s correction, or be weary of it, because the LORD corrects those he loves, as a father corrects the son he delights in.

Then the father begins to sing the praises of wisdom, almost like a person. 'Happy is the man that findeth wisdom'. Wisdom is worth more than silver and gold, and more precious than rubies. Nothing you could ever want compares with her. 'Her ways are ways of pleasantness, and all her paths are peace.' And then a phrase that takes us all the way back to Eden: 'She is a tree of life to them that lay hold upon her'. The way to the tree of life was closed in Genesis chapter three. Here, true wisdom brings a taste of that life back.

The LORD made the earth by wisdom, the father says, and set the heavens in place by understanding. So keep wisdom close. Then you will walk safely, and your foot will not stumble. When you lie down, you will not be afraid, and your sleep will be sweet.

And the chapter ends with practical wisdom about our neighbours. 'Withhold not good from them to whom it is due, when it is in the power of thine hand to do it.' Do not plot harm against your neighbour. Do not argue with someone who has done you no harm. Do not envy the violent. For the LORD is near to the upright, and he gives grace to the humble.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 5. 'Trust in the LORD with all thine heart; and lean not unto thine own understanding.' To lean on something is to put your whole weight on it. Our own understanding is like a walking stick that snaps when we need it most. We do not see the whole picture. God does. Trusting him with all your heart means bringing all of you, not just the easy parts, and putting your full weight on him.

Verse 6. 'In all thy ways acknowledge him, and he shall direct thy paths.' To acknowledge God means to recognise him, to bring him in, to ask him. In all thy ways: not only in church, or in big decisions, but in your work, your money, your friendships, your plans. And his promise: he shall direct thy paths. He will make the road straight, or show you the way. This does not mean the path will always be easy, but it will be the right one.

Verse 11. 'My son, despise not the chastening of the LORD; neither be weary of his correction:' Chastening means discipline or correction. Nobody enjoys being corrected. But the next verse gives the reason: whom the LORD loveth he correcteth. When God corrects us, through his Word, through the people around us, or through hard experiences, it is a sign that we belong to him. A father who did not care would not bother.

Solomon was the wisest king who ever lived, but even he did not keep his own advice. In his old age his heart turned away to other gods. True wisdom needed more than a wise king. Long after, Paul wrote that in Christ 'are hid all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge.' And James promised, 'If any of you lack wisdom, let him ask of God, that giveth to all men liberally, and upbraideth not; and it shall be given him.'

Where are you leaning on your own understanding right now? Bring that area to God in prayer, and acknowledge him in it. And look for one person you can do good to today, while it is in the power of your hand.

Here is today’s question. Proverbs says, in all thy ways acknowledge him. What part of your life will you bring to God and trust him with fully today?

Let us pray. Lord, I want to trust you with all my heart, and not lean on my own understanding. In all my ways I acknowledge you; direct my paths. Give me your wisdom, and when you correct me, help me to receive it as a sign of your love. Amen.
