The Living Voice. Meet the Letter to the Colossians.

Imagine a jigsaw puzzle with a thousand pieces, and no picture on the box. You could spend hours turning the pieces over, and never see how they fit. Then someone hands you the picture, and suddenly everything makes sense. Every piece has its place. Paul’s letter to the Colossians is like that picture. And the picture on the box is Jesus.

Welcome to the letter to the Colossians, on The Living Voice. Colossians has four chapters, so on this walk it is four steps, one chapter at a time.

We have just walked through the letter to the Ephesians. Colossians is like its little sister. Paul wrote them at about the same time, from prison, and sent them with the same messenger, his friend Tychicus. Many of the same ideas appear in both, sometimes in almost the same words. If you enjoyed Ephesians, you will feel at home here.

Colosse was a small town, in what is now Turkey, not far from the city of Laodicea. Paul had never been there himself. He says so in the letter. The church had begun through a man named Epaphras, who came from Colosse, heard the good news, and took it home. Now Epaphras had come to visit Paul in prison, bringing news of the church. Much of the news was good. The believers were growing in faith and love.

But there was a worry. Some teachers had arrived in Colosse with a new and dangerous message. They used clever sounding ideas, which Paul calls philosophy. They made strict rules about what to eat, and which days to keep. They talked about worshipping angels. And without saying it openly, they made Jesus seem small, as if he were just one part of the picture, and believers needed something more.

So Paul writes to show the Colossians how great Jesus really is. He is not one piece of the puzzle. He is the whole picture. He made everything. He holds everything together. He is the head of the church. And in him, we are complete. We do not need anything more.

When Tychicus carried this letter to Colosse, someone travelled with him. A man named Onesimus, who had come from Colosse too, and had a story of his own. We will hear his story in the very next book on our walk, the short letter to Philemon.

Here are four things to watch for as we walk.

Watch for the word all. Paul uses it again and again about Jesus: all things were made by him, all fulness dwells in him, Christ is all, and in all.

Watch for the word complete. Paul says, ye are complete in him. We do not need to add anything to Jesus.

Watch for things above. Paul calls us to set our hearts on things above, where Christ is, not on things on the earth.

And watch for thanksgiving. In this short letter, Paul mentions giving thanks again and again. A grateful heart is a sign of a life that knows Jesus.

Here is a question to carry with you. Paul wrote to show that Jesus is not just one part of life, but above everything. What would it look like for Jesus to be first in every part of your life?

Let us pray. Lord Jesus, as I walk through the letter to the Colossians, show me how great you are. Help me to see that you are above all things, and that in you I am complete. Set my heart on things above, and fill my life with thanksgiving. Amen.
