The Living Voice. Colossians, chapter 1. The Image of the Invisible God.

Imagine trying to describe the sun to someone who has lived their whole life in a cave. You could say it is bright, and warm, and huge. But words are not enough. Then one day, you lead them outside, and they see it for themselves. Paul says that no one has ever seen God. But in Jesus, God has stepped into the light, where we can see him.

In the introduction, we heard that Colosse was a small town Paul had never visited, where the church began through Epaphras. False teachers were making Jesus seem small, as if believers needed something more. So Paul writes to show how great Jesus is. We heard four things to watch for: all, complete, things above, and thanksgiving.

Now, in chapter one, Paul begins. Listen for what he prays for the Colossians. And listen to his amazing description of who Jesus is.

Paul and Timothy write. 'To the saints and faithful brethren in Christ which are at Colosse: Grace be unto you, and peace'.

And straight away, he gives thanks. We give thanks to God, Paul says, praying always for you, since we heard of your faith in Christ Jesus, and your love for all God’s people. Faith, love, and hope. 'For the hope which is laid up for you in heaven'. The good news has come to you, Paul says, just as it is growing and bearing fruit all over the world.

Then Paul tells them what he prays for them. 'For this cause we also, since the day we heard it, do not cease to pray for you, and to desire that ye might be filled with the knowledge of his will in all wisdom and spiritual understanding'. And why? 'That ye might walk worthy of the Lord unto all pleasing, being fruitful in every good work, and increasing in the knowledge of God'.

He prays they will be 'Strengthened with all might, according to his glorious power, unto all patience and longsuffering with joyfulness'. Notice what God’s great power is for. Not to do amazing feats, but to give patience, endurance and joy, in ordinary life.

And giving thanks to the Father, who has made us fit to share in the inheritance of his people in the light. 'Who hath delivered us from the power of darkness, and hath translated us into the kingdom of his dear Son'. Translated means carried across, moved. God has rescued us from the kingdom of darkness, and carried us into the kingdom of his beloved Son. 'In whom we have redemption through his blood, even the forgiveness of sins'.

And then Paul writes some of the greatest words ever written about Jesus. Many think the first Christians may have sung them, like a hymn.

He is the image of the invisible God.

'For by him were all things created, that are in heaven, and that are in earth, visible and invisible'. Everything we can see, and everything we cannot see. Thrones, powers, rulers, authorities, even the angels. 'all things were created by him, and for him'. Jesus is not part of creation. He is the Creator. And everything exists for him.

And then.

'And he is the head of the body, the church: who is the beginning, the firstborn from the dead'. Jesus was the first to rise from the dead, never to die again. And one day, all his people will rise too. Why? 'that in all things he might have the preeminence.' Preeminence means first place. In everything, Jesus comes first.

'For it pleased the Father that in him should all fulness dwell'. Everything God is, is in Jesus. And through Jesus, God has made peace, bringing everything back to himself, 'having made peace through the blood of his cross'.

Then Paul turns to the Colossians. You were once far from God, enemies in your minds because of the wrong things you did. But now God has reconciled you, brought you back into friendship with him, through Christ’s death, to present you holy and blameless and without fault in his sight. As long as you keep going in the faith, firm and steady, and do not drift away from the hope of the good news.

Then Paul talks about his own sufferings. He is in prison, and he rejoices in his sufferings for them. He says he is filling up in his own body what remains of the afflictions of Christ, for the sake of the church. Christians have understood these words in different ways. But all agree that Christ’s work on the cross is complete, and nothing needs to be added to it. Paul is sharing in Christ’s sufferings, as he serves the church.

And Paul tells them about the mystery, the secret hidden for ages, but now revealed to God’s people.

'Whom we preach, warning every man, and teaching every man in all wisdom; that we may present every man perfect in Christ Jesus'. Perfect here means mature, complete. Every man, every person. Paul wants every single believer to grow up in Christ. And he works hard at it, struggling, he says, with all the energy Christ powerfully works in him.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 15. 'Who is the image of the invisible God, the firstborn of every creature'. No one has ever seen God. He is invisible. But Jesus is his image, the exact picture of God. If you want to know what God is like, look at Jesus. Firstborn here does not mean that Jesus was created. It means he is first in rank and honour, the heir over all creation. As the next verse says, all things were created by him.

Verse 17. 'And he is before all things, and by him all things consist.' Consist means hold together. Jesus existed before anything else was made. And right now, he holds everything together: every star, every atom, every heartbeat. If he let go, everything would fall apart. The same Jesus who holds the universe together can hold your life together too.

Verse 27. 'To whom God would make known what is the riches of the glory of this mystery among the Gentiles; which is Christ in you, the hope of glory'. Here is the great secret, now revealed. Christ in you. Not just Christ beside you, or Christ above you, but Christ living in you, by his Spirit. And because he is in you, you have the hope of glory, the sure hope of sharing in his glory one day. This is for everyone who believes, from every nation.

John wrote, 'No man hath seen God at any time; the only begotten Son, which is in the bosom of the Father, he hath declared him.' And the letter to the Hebrews says of Jesus, 'Who being the brightness of his glory, and the express image of his person, and upholding all things by the word of his power'.

When things feel like they are falling apart, remember that Jesus holds all things together. Bring your worries to him today. And thank God that, if you belong to Jesus, Christ is in you, the hope of glory.

Here is today’s question. Paul says Jesus is the image of the invisible God. What do you learn about what God is like, when you look at Jesus?

Let us pray. Lord Jesus, you are the image of the invisible God. All things were made by you and for you, and by you all things hold together. You are first in everything. Thank you that you made peace through your cross, and that you live in me, the hope of glory. Fill me with the knowledge of your will, and strengthen me with patience and joy. Amen.
