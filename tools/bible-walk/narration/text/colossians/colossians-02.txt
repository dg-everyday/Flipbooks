The Living Voice. Colossians, chapter 2. Rooted and Built Up in Him.

Two trees stand side by side in a field. They look the same above the ground. Then a great storm comes. The wind howls, and the rain pours. One tree bends, and cracks, and crashes to the ground, roots and all. The other bends, but stays standing. Why? Its roots go deep into the soil. Paul says believers need roots like that, deep in Jesus.

In chapter one, Paul thanked God for the Colossians’ faith, love and hope, and prayed that they would be filled with the knowledge of God’s will. He described Jesus as the image of the invisible God, the Creator of all things, who holds all things together. And he revealed the great mystery: Christ in you, the hope of glory.

Now, in chapter two, Paul warns about the false teaching in Colosse. Listen for why we do not need anything more than Jesus. And listen for something nailed to the cross.

Paul begins by telling them how much he cares about them. 'For I would that ye knew what great conflict I have for you, and for them at Laodicea, and for as many as have not seen my face in the flesh'. Conflict here means struggle, especially in prayer. Paul had never met them, but he wrestled in prayer for them.

He wants their hearts to be encouraged, and knit together in love, and to understand fully the mystery of God, which is Christ himself. 'In whom are hid all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge.' Everything worth knowing is found in Jesus.

'And this I say, lest any man should beguile you with enticing words.' Beguile means trick, deceive. Enticing words means clever, persuasive arguments. Paul is warning them about the false teachers.

Then comes Paul’s great encouragement.

And then a warning. 'Beware lest any man spoil you through philosophy and vain deceit, after the tradition of men, after the rudiments of the world, and not after Christ.' Spoil you here means carry you off, like a captive. Vain deceit means empty tricks. The false teachers had ideas that sounded deep and wise. But they were based on human ideas, not on Christ.

And then Paul tells them why they do not need anything else.

'And ye are complete in him, which is the head of all principality and power'. Complete means filled full. If you have Jesus, you have everything you need. You do not need extra secret knowledge, or special rules, or angels. You are complete in him.

Then Paul tells them what Jesus has done for them. God has changed their hearts, Paul says, in a way that no human hands could do. 'Buried with him in baptism, wherein also ye are risen with him through the faith of the operation of God, who hath raised him from the dead.' When we trust in Jesus, it is as if we died with him, were buried with him, and rose again with him to a new life.

You were dead in your sins, Paul says, but God has made you alive together with Christ, 'having forgiven you all trespasses'. All. Every one.

And then a powerful picture.

And Jesus has disarmed the evil powers, Paul says, and made a public show of them, triumphing over them by the cross. What looked like Jesus’ defeat was really his victory.

So, Paul says, 'Let no man therefore judge you in meat, or in drink, or in respect of an holyday, or of the new moon, or of the sabbath days'. The false teachers were making rules about what to eat and drink, and which special days to keep, and judging people who did not follow them. 'Which are a shadow of things to come; but the body is of Christ.' A shadow is the outline something casts. These things were shadows, pointing ahead to the real thing. And the real thing is Christ.

Do not let anyone rob you of your reward, Paul says, by insisting on false humility, or the worship of angels, or boasting about visions. Such people are puffed up with pride, and they have lost connection with the Head, who is Christ. It is from Christ that the whole body, joined and held together, grows as God makes it grow.

And then Paul asks a sharp question. If you died with Christ, and are free from the world’s ways, why do you still let people tie you up with rules? 'Touch not; taste not; handle not'. Do not touch this, do not taste that, do not handle the other thing. These rules are about things that will soon be used up and gone. They are only human commands and teachings. They look wise, with their strict discipline and harsh treatment of the body. But they cannot change the heart. Only Christ can do that.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 6. 'As ye have therefore received Christ Jesus the Lord, so walk ye in him'. The next verse says, rooted and built up in him, and stablished in the faith, abounding therein with thanksgiving. You started your Christian life by receiving Jesus, by faith. Now keep going the same way. Keep walking with him, trusting him. Be rooted in him, like a tree, and built up in him, like a house. And let thanksgiving overflow.

Verse 9. 'For in him dwelleth all the fulness of the Godhead bodily.' The Godhead means God, all that God is. Bodily means in a real human body. All of God lives in Jesus, in a real human body. Jesus is not just a good teacher, or an angel, or a part of God. He is fully God, and fully human. That is why we need nothing more than him.

Verse 14. 'Blotting out the handwriting of ordinances that was against us, which was contrary to us, and took it out of the way, nailing it to his cross'. The handwriting means a written record, like a list of debts we owed. Every sin we ever committed was written down against us. But Jesus blotted it out, wiped it clean. He took that record of debt, and nailed it to his cross. Paid in full. Nothing is left against us.

Jesus said, 'I am the vine, ye are the branches'. And the psalmist wrote of the godly man, 'he shall be like a tree planted by the rivers of water'.

Is there any rule, or idea, or worry that has made you feel you need something more than Jesus? Bring it to him. You are complete in him. And thank God today that the record of your sins has been nailed to the cross.

Here is today’s question. Paul says, as ye have received Christ Jesus the Lord, so walk ye in him. What helps your roots grow deeper in Jesus each day?

Let us pray. Lord Jesus, in you dwells all the fulness of God, and in you I am complete. Thank you that you forgave all my sins, and nailed the record of my debt to your cross. Keep me from clever ideas and empty rules that would lead me away from you. Root me deep in you, build me up, and let my life overflow with thanksgiving. Amen.

Next on the walk, Colossians chapter three. If you are risen with Christ, seek those things which are above. Put off the old ways, and put on love. And whatever you do, do it heartily, as to the Lord.
