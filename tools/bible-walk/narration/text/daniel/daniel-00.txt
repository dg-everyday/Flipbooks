The Living Voice. Meet the Book of Daniel.

A teenage boy is marched hundreds of miles from home, a prisoner of war. His city has been conquered. His temple has been robbed. Now he is in the greatest city in the world, Babylon, and they give him a new name, a new language and a new school. They want him to forget who he is. But he decides, quietly and firmly, that whatever else changes, he will stay faithful to his God.

Welcome to the book of Daniel, on The Living Voice. Daniel has twelve chapters. On this walk we will take the first six, one chapter at a time. These are the stories of Daniel and his friends, some of the best loved stories in the whole Bible. The second half of the book is made up of visions of the future, and those can wait for another walk. Six chapters, finished in a week or less. Another short win.

The book is named after Daniel, whose name means God is my judge. He was a young man of Judah, from a noble family, who was carried away to Babylon when King Nebuchadnezzar conquered Jerusalem. He lived the rest of his long life in exile, serving under foreign kings, first in Babylon, and then in Persia, after the Persians conquered Babylon.

Jesus called him Daniel the prophet. And in the second half of the book, Daniel speaks of himself, telling what he saw.

The stories in chapters one to six all ask the same question. How do you stay faithful to God when you live in a world that does not know him? When the people in power want you to worship their gods, eat their food, follow their ways? Daniel and his three friends, Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego, show us how. Not by running away, and not by giving in, but by serving faithfully where God has put them, and standing firm when it matters.

Here are three things to watch for as we walk.

Watch for the kings. Nebuchadnezzar, Belshazzar and Darius were some of the most powerful men who ever lived. And again and again, they learn that there is a greater King. Listen for what each of them comes to say about the God of Daniel.

Watch for the phrase, the most High ruleth in the kingdom of men. It is the message of the whole book. Empires rise and fall. Kings come and go. But God is in charge of history, and his kingdom will never end.

And watch for faithfulness. Daniel did not become faithful on the day he faced the lions. He was faithful in small things, in what he ate, and in how he prayed, every day, for years. When the big tests came, he was ready.

Daniel’s world was not so different from ours. Many people today live where faith in God is not popular, or even allowed. Daniel shows us that it is possible to live and work in such a world, to do your job well, to be respected, and still to belong completely to God.

Here is a question to carry with you. Daniel stayed faithful to God in a world that did not know him. Where do you find it hardest to stay faithful to God in your everyday life?

Let us pray. Lord God, as I walk through the stories of Daniel, teach me to be faithful to you in small things and in big things. Thank you that you rule over the kingdoms of this world, and that your kingdom will never end. Give me courage to stand for you, wherever you have placed me. Amen.
