The Living Voice. Daniel, chapter 3. Our God Whom We Serve Is Able.

A vast crowd stands on a plain, facing a golden statue as tall as a nine storey building. The music begins to play. And thousands upon thousands of people fall flat on their faces to worship it. All except three young men, who remain standing. Behind them, a furnace roars. They know exactly what it is for.

In chapter two, King Nebuchadnezzar dreamed of a great statue, with a head of gold, and Daniel, after praying with his friends, told the king the dream and its meaning. The king said, your God is a God of gods, and he gave Daniel and his three friends high positions.

Now, in chapter three, Daniel is not mentioned. The spotlight turns to his three friends, Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego. Listen for their answer to the king, one of the bravest speeches in the Bible. And listen for who is with them in the fire.

King Nebuchadnezzar makes an image of gold, sixty cubits high and six cubits wide, and sets it up on the plain of Dura, in the province of Babylon.

Remember the king’s dream. He was the head of gold. Now he builds a statue that is gold from top to bottom.

The king gathers all the officials of his empire to the dedication of the image.

Then a herald cries aloud, to you it is commanded, O peoples, nations and languages. When you hear the sound of the horn, flute, harp, and all kinds of music, you must fall down and worship the golden image that King Nebuchadnezzar has set up. And whoever does not fall down and worship will be thrown that same hour into the middle of a burning fiery furnace.

So when the music plays, all the people fall down and worship the golden image.

But some of the Chaldeans come forward and accuse the Jews. There are certain Jews whom you have set over the affairs of Babylon, Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego. These men, O king, have not regarded you. They do not serve your gods, or worship the golden image you have set up.

In a furious rage, Nebuchadnezzar commands that the three men be brought to him. He says, is it true, Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego, that you do not serve my gods, or worship the golden image I have set up? Now, if you are ready, when you hear the music, to fall down and worship the image, good. But if you do not, you will be thrown that same hour into the burning fiery furnace. 'and who is that God that shall deliver you out of my hands?'

The three young men answer, O Nebuchadnezzar, we do not need to answer you in this matter.

And then they speak with quiet, unshakeable courage.

Nebuchadnezzar is filled with fury. He commands that the furnace be heated seven times hotter than usual. He commands the strongest men in his army to tie up Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego, and throw them into the furnace.

So the three men are tied up, fully clothed, and thrown into the middle of the burning fiery furnace. The king’s command is so urgent, and the furnace so hot, that the flames overcome the soldiers who throw them in. And Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego fall down, tied up, into the middle of the fire.

Then King Nebuchadnezzar leaps to his feet in astonishment. He says to his advisers, did we not throw three men, tied up, into the fire? They answer, true, O king.

And the king says what he sees.

Then Nebuchadnezzar comes near to the door of the burning fiery furnace and calls out, Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego, ye servants of the most high God, come out, and come here!

And they walk out of the fire.

The officials gather around. The fire had no power over their bodies. Not a hair of their heads is singed. Their clothes are not scorched. They do not even smell of smoke. The only thing the fire burned was the ropes that tied them.

Then Nebuchadnezzar says, blessed be the God of Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego, who has sent his angel, and rescued his servants who trusted in him. They defied the king’s command, and were willing to give up their lives, rather than serve or worship any god except their own God.

And the king makes a new decree, that no one in any nation may speak against the God of Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego, 'because there is no other God that can deliver after this sort.' And the king promotes the three men in the province of Babylon.

The king who asked, who is that God that shall deliver you out of my hands? has his answer.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 17. 'If it be so, our God whom we serve is able to deliver us from the burning fiery furnace, and he will deliver us out of thine hand, O king.' The king had asked, who is that God that shall deliver you? And the young men answered, our God is able. They knew who their God was. He was the living God, the maker of heaven and earth. And no fire, and no king, was too strong for him.

Verse 18. 'But if not, be it known unto thee, O king, that we will not serve thy gods, nor worship the golden image which thou hast set up.' But if not. These may be the three bravest words in the Bible. The young men believed God could save them. But they did not know if he would. And it did not matter. Even if God did not rescue them, they would still not bow. Their faith did not depend on getting what they wanted. They trusted God, and obeyed him, whatever the result. That is real faith.

Verse 25. 'He answered and said, Lo, I see four men loose, walking in the midst of the fire, and they have no hurt; and the form of the fourth is like the Son of God.' Three men were thrown in, tied up. Four men are walking around, free. Who was the fourth? The king himself later calls him God’s angel. Many Christians have believed it was the Son of God himself, appearing long before he was born in Bethlehem. Christians have understood this in different ways. But all agree on this: God did not keep his servants out of the fire. He met them in it. And he walked with them there.

Isaiah wrote, 'when thou walkest through the fire, thou shalt not be burned; neither shall the flame kindle upon thee.' And Peter and the apostles said, 'We ought to obey God rather than men.'

Is there a fire you are facing right now? Remember that God can deliver you. And if he does not take you out of it, he will walk with you in it. Today, tell God that you trust him, even if things do not go the way you hope.

Here is today’s question. The three young men said, our God is able to deliver us, but if not, we will not bow. Is your trust in God strong enough to say, but if not? What would help it grow?

Let us pray. Lord God, you are able to deliver me from every fire. Give me faith like Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego, to trust you and obey you, even if things do not turn out as I hope. Thank you that you never leave me alone in the fire, but walk with me there. I will not bow to anyone but you. Amen.
