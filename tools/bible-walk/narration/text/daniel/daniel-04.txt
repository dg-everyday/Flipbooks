The Living Voice. Daniel, chapter 4. The Most High Ruleth in the Kingdom of Men.

A king walks on the roof of his royal palace, looking out over the greatest city in the world. He says to himself, look at all this. I built it, by my own power, for my own glory. And before the words have even left his mouth, a voice speaks from heaven.

In chapter three, Nebuchadnezzar set up a golden image and commanded everyone to worship it. Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego refused. They were thrown into the fiery furnace, but a fourth man walked with them in the fire, and they came out without even the smell of smoke. And Nebuchadnezzar said, there is no other God that can deliver after this sort.

Now, in chapter four, something unusual happens. The whole chapter is a letter written by Nebuchadnezzar himself, telling his own story. It is the story of how the proudest man in the world learned to be humble.

Nebuchadnezzar the king, unto all people, nations and languages that dwell in all the earth: peace be multiplied unto you. I thought it good to show the signs and wonders that the high God has done for me. How great are his signs, and how mighty are his wonders! His kingdom is an everlasting kingdom, and his dominion is from generation to generation.

The king begins at the end, with praise. Then he tells how he got there.

I, Nebuchadnezzar, was at rest in my house, and flourishing in my palace. I saw a dream which made me afraid. I called all the wise men of Babylon, but they could not tell me what it meant. At last Daniel came in, and I told him the dream.

In my dream I saw a tree in the middle of the earth. It grew strong, and its top reached to heaven, and it could be seen to the ends of the earth. Its leaves were beautiful and its fruit plentiful, and there was food in it for everyone. The animals rested in its shade, and the birds lived in its branches.

Then I saw a watcher, a holy one, coming down from heaven. He cried aloud, cut down the tree, and cut off its branches. Shake off its leaves, and scatter its fruit. But leave the stump of its roots in the earth, bound with a band of iron and bronze. Let him be wet with the dew of heaven, and live with the animals in the grass. Let his mind be changed from a man’s, and let seven times pass over him. The holy ones have decreed it, so that the living may know that the most High ruleth in the kingdom of men, and giveth it to whomsoever he will.

When Daniel hears the dream, he is stunned for a while, and his thoughts trouble him. The king says, do not let the dream trouble you. Daniel answers, my lord, I wish this dream were about your enemies.

Daniel cares about this king, even though he is the king who conquered his people.

Then Daniel explains. The tree is you, O king. You have grown great and strong, and your greatness reaches to heaven, and your rule to the ends of the earth. But you will be driven away from people to live with the wild animals, until you know that the most High ruleth in the kingdom of men. But the stump is left, so your kingdom will be given back to you, once you know that heaven rules.

Then Daniel pleads with him. Break off your sins by doing what is right, and your wrongs by showing mercy to the poor. Perhaps then your peace will last longer.

All this comes upon King Nebuchadnezzar. Twelve months later, he is walking on the roof of the royal palace of Babylon.

And then the king speaks.

While the words are still in the king’s mouth, a voice falls from heaven. O king Nebuchadnezzar, to thee it is spoken: the kingdom is departed from thee.

That same hour, it happens. Nebuchadnezzar is driven away from people. He eats grass like an ox. His body is wet with the dew of heaven, until his hair grows long like eagles’ feathers, and his nails like birds’ claws. The greatest king on earth is living like an animal in the field.

At the end of those days, I, Nebuchadnezzar, lifted up my eyes to heaven, and my understanding returned to me. And I blessed the most High, and I praised and honoured him that liveth for ever, whose dominion is an everlasting dominion, and his kingdom is from generation to generation.

The moment he looked up to heaven, his mind came back.

At the same time, my reason returned to me, my honour and splendour were given back, and I was established again in my kingdom.

And then the king finishes his letter.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 30. 'The king spake, and said, Is not this great Babylon, that I have built for the house of the kingdom by the might of my power, and for the honour of my majesty?' I have built. My power. My majesty. Nebuchadnezzar had been warned. He had seen God’s power in chapter two, and in the fiery furnace. And he had been given a whole year to change. But his heart was still full of himself. Pride says, I did this. I deserve this. It forgets that every good thing we have is a gift from God.

Verse 35. 'And all the inhabitants of the earth are reputed as nothing: and he doeth according to his will in the army of heaven, and among the inhabitants of the earth: and none can stay his hand, or say unto him, What doest thou?' This is a king speaking. Now he says that compared with God, all the people of the earth are as nothing. God does as he pleases in heaven and on earth, and no one can stop his hand. Nebuchadnezzar finally saw himself as he really was, small, before a great God.

Verse 37. 'Now I Nebuchadnezzar praise and extol and honour the King of heaven, all whose works are truth, and his ways judgment: and those that walk in pride he is able to abase.' To abase means to bring low. The proud king became a humble worshipper. And notice, he did not hide his story. He wrote it to the whole world, so that everyone could learn from it. God humbled him, not to destroy him, but to save him. And the last words of his letter are a warning for us all: those that walk in pride, God is able to bring low.

James wrote, 'God resisteth the proud, but giveth grace unto the humble.' And Paul asked, 'and what hast thou that thou didst not receive?'

Is there something in your life you are proud of, as if you did it all yourself? Take a moment today to thank God for it, and to give him the credit. Look up to heaven, as Nebuchadnezzar did, and praise the King of heaven.

Here is today’s question. Nebuchadnezzar said, is not this great Babylon, that I have built? What helps you to remember that everything good in your life is a gift from God?

Let us pray. King of heaven, all your works are truth, and your ways are just. Forgive me for the times I have been proud, and taken credit for what you have given me. Keep my heart humble. I lift up my eyes to you, and I praise and honour you, who lives for ever. Amen.
