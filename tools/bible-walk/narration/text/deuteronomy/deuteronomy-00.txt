The Living Voice. Meet the Book of Deuteronomy.

An old man stands before a huge crowd. He knows he is about to die. Across the river behind them lies the land he has spent his whole life leading them toward. He will never set foot in it. So he gathers everyone together, and he begins to speak. Not just a few words. He speaks for days, pouring out everything he wants them to remember.

Welcome to the book of Deuteronomy, on The Living Voice. Deuteronomy has thirty four chapters, so on this walk it is thirty four steps, one chapter at a time.

At the end of the book of Numbers, Israel was camped on the plains of Moab, by the river Jordan, across from Jericho. A new generation had grown up in the wilderness. Their parents, who had refused to enter the land, were gone. Now this new generation was about to cross over. And before they did, Moses spoke to them, one last time.

Deuteronomy is that speech. Or really, a series of speeches, Moses’ farewell sermons. Most of the book is Moses talking, from the beginning almost to the end.

The name Deuteronomy means second law. It is not a new law, but the same law God gave at Mount Sinai, told again, to a new generation, with all of Moses’ love and urgency. Moses looks back over the journey. He repeats the Ten Commandments. He explains God’s laws for life in the land. And again and again, he pleads with the people to love God and follow him.

Here are three things to watch for as we walk.

Watch for the word remember. Moses keeps saying, remember what the LORD did for you. Remember Egypt. Remember the wilderness. Do not forget. He knew that the greatest danger for a people living in a good land would be to forget the God who gave it to them.

Watch for the word heart. Deuteronomy is not just about rules. It is about love. In chapter six, Moses says, thou shalt love the LORD thy God with all thine heart, and with all thy soul, and with all thy might. Jesus called that the first and great commandment.

And watch for the word choose. Near the end of the book, Moses sets two ways before the people: life and blessing, or death and curse. And he pleads with them, choose life.

Deuteronomy is one of the books Jesus quoted most often. When he was tempted in the wilderness, every time he answered the devil, he quoted Deuteronomy. Man shall not live by bread alone. Thou shalt not tempt the Lord thy God. Thou shalt worship the Lord thy God, and him only shalt thou serve. Jesus had hidden these words in his heart.

There are some hard parts in Deuteronomy too, especially in the laws in the middle of the book, and in the warnings near the end. Some of those laws belonged to Israel’s life in that time, and Christians have understood how they apply today in different ways. We will tell them honestly and gently, and listen for what they show us about God.

And the book ends with something very moving. Moses climbs a mountain, and God shows him the whole promised land, spread out below. And there, Moses dies, and God himself buries him.

Here is a question to carry with you. Moses spent his last days reminding the people what God had done, and pleading with them to love him. If you could say one last thing to the people you love most about God, what would it be?

Let us pray. Lord God, as I walk through the book of Deuteronomy, teach me to remember what you have done for me. Teach me to love you with all my heart, and soul, and might. And when I face a choice between your way and my own, help me to choose life. Amen.
