The Living Voice. Deuteronomy, chapter 7. Because the LORD Loved You.

Imagine a king walking through his kingdom, looking for someone to adopt as his own child. He passes the strongest, the cleverest, the most beautiful. And then he stops, and chooses the smallest, weakest child in the whole land. Why? Not because of anything special about the child. Simply because he loves them. That is what Moses tells Israel in chapter seven.

In chapter six, Moses spoke the words at the heart of the book: Hear, O Israel: The LORD our God is one LORD: and thou shalt love the LORD thy God with all thine heart, and with all thy soul, and with all thy might. These words were to be in their hearts, and taught to their children, in everyday life.

Now, in chapter seven, Moses tells the people why God chose them. Listen for the reason he gives. It is not what you might expect.

When the LORD your God brings you into the land, Moses says, he will drive out many nations before you, seven nations greater and mightier than you.

Moses names them: the Hittites, the Girgashites, the Amorites, the Canaanites, the Perizzites, the Hivites and the Jebusites.

Then Moses gives hard commands about these nations. Israel was to bring God’s judgment on them, make no treaty with them, and not intermarry with them. This is one of the hard parts of Deuteronomy, and Christians have understood these chapters in different ways. The Bible says elsewhere that these nations were judged for their great wickedness, and that God had waited a long time before judging them.

And Moses explains the main reason for not marrying them. For they will turn away your children from following me, to serve other gods.

The great danger was not the armies of these nations. It was their gods. If Israel lived alongside them, married them, and shared their worship, they would be drawn away from the LORD. And as we saw in the book of Judges, that is exactly what happened.

So Moses says, break down their altars, smash their sacred stones, cut down their idols, and burn their images with fire.

And then Moses tells them who they are.

Special here means treasured, a precious possession. Israel was God’s treasure.

And then Moses tells them why God chose them.

Not because Israel was big, or strong, or good. They were small, and weak, and often rebellious. God chose them simply because he loved them.

But because the LORD loved you, Moses says, and because he would keep the promise he swore to your fathers, he brought you out with a mighty hand, and rescued you from slavery, from the hand of Pharaoh king of Egypt.

God’s love did not depend on how impressive Israel was. It came first. It came freely. And it was rooted in his own faithfulness to his promises.

And so Moses says.

Then Moses tells them about the blessings of obedience. If you keep these laws, the LORD will keep his covenant and his love with you. He will love you and bless you, and make you many. He will bless your families, your fields, your grain and wine and oil, your herds and flocks. He will keep you from the diseases you knew in Egypt.

Then Moses speaks to their fears. If you say in your heart, these nations are bigger than us, how can we drive them out? Do not be afraid of them. Remember what the LORD your God did to Pharaoh and all Egypt. The great trials you saw with your own eyes, the signs and wonders, the mighty hand. The LORD your God will do the same to all the peoples you fear.

The LORD your God is among you, Moses says, a great and awesome God.

And God will drive out these nations little by little. Not all at once, or the wild animals would multiply and overrun the land.

God had a plan, and a pace. Little by little. Sometimes God’s victories come slowly, step by step, not all at once.

And the chapter ends with a warning. Burn the images of their gods. Do not covet the silver or gold on them, or take it for yourself, or you will be trapped by it. Do not bring a detestable thing into your house.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 6. 'For thou art an holy people unto the LORD thy God: the LORD thy God hath chosen thee to be a special people unto himself, above all people that are upon the face of the earth.' Holy means set apart, belonging to God. Chosen, special, treasured. Israel was not chosen to be proud, but to belong to God, and to show the world what he is like. Peter wrote the same of God’s people in Christ: ye are a chosen generation, a royal priesthood, an holy nation, a peculiar people.

Verse 7. 'The LORD did not set his love upon you, nor choose you, because ye were more in number than any people; for ye were the fewest of all people:' The fewest of all people. God did not choose the greatest. He chose the smallest. God’s love is not earned by being impressive. That is good news for us. God does not love us because we are strong or clever or good. He loves us because he loves us.

Verse 9. 'Know therefore that the LORD thy God, he is God, the faithful God, which keepeth covenant and mercy with them that love him and keep his commandments to a thousand generations;' The faithful God. To a thousand generations. God’s faithfulness does not run out. It stretches on and on, from parents to children to grandchildren, far beyond what we can imagine.

Peter wrote, 'But ye are a chosen generation, a royal priesthood, an holy nation, a peculiar people'. And John wrote, 'We love him, because he first loved us.'

Thank God today that he loves you, not because of anything you have done, but simply because he loves you. And ask him whether there is anything in your life, like the idols’ gold, that is pulling your heart away from him.

Here is today’s question. Moses said God chose Israel not because they were great, but simply because he loved them. How does it change you to know that God’s love for you does not depend on how impressive you are?

Let us pray. Lord God, you are the faithful God, keeping your covenant and mercy to a thousand generations. Thank you that you love me, not because I am great, but because you are love. Keep me from anything that would draw my heart away from you. I want to belong to you alone. Amen.
