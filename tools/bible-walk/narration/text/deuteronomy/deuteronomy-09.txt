The Living Voice. Deuteronomy, chapter 9. Not for Thy Righteousness.

Imagine winning a great prize, and thinking, I must have deserved this. I must be better than everyone else. And then someone who knows you well sits you down and says, gently but firmly, no. You did not earn this. In fact, you have been stubborn and rebellious from the very start. This is a gift. In Deuteronomy chapter nine, Moses has that conversation with Israel.

In chapter eight, Moses reminded the people that God let them hunger in the wilderness, and fed them with manna, to teach them that man doth not live by bread only. And he warned that in the good land, when they were full and rich, they must remember that it is God who giveth thee power to get wealth.

Now, in chapter nine, Moses warns against another danger: pride. Listen for why God is really giving them the land. And listen for how Moses prayed for them, when they least deserved it.

Hear, O Israel, Moses says. You are about to cross the Jordan, to take possession of nations greater and stronger than you, with great cities walled up to the sky. The descendants of Anak, tall and powerful. You have heard it said, who can stand against the sons of Anak?

But understand this, Moses says. The LORD your God is the one who goes across ahead of you, like a consuming fire. He will defeat them before you.

And then Moses gives a warning they might not expect.

When the LORD your God has driven them out, Moses says, do not say in your heart, the LORD brought me here because I am so good.

And Moses explains.

And then Moses says it again, even more plainly.

Stiffnecked means stubborn, like an ox that will not turn its neck to follow the plough. And to prove it, Moses reminds them of their history.

Remember, and do not forget, Moses says, how you provoked the LORD your God in the wilderness. From the day you left Egypt until you came here, you have been rebellious against the LORD.

Even at Horeb, Moses says. When I went up the mountain to receive the tablets of stone, the tablets of the covenant, I stayed on the mountain, without eating bread or drinking water. And the LORD gave me the two tablets, written with the finger of God.

Written with the finger of God. Not carved by any human hand. God himself wrote his law.

But then the LORD said to me, get down quickly. Your people have corrupted themselves. They have quickly turned away, and made an idol. I have seen this people, God said, and they are stubborn. Leave me alone, so that I can destroy them, and make of you a greater nation.

So I came down from the mountain, while it was burning with fire, with the two tablets in my hands. And I looked, and there it was. You had made yourselves a calf of metal. You had turned aside so quickly.

So I took the two tablets, and threw them down, and broke them before your eyes.

Then I fell down before the LORD, Moses says, as at the first, without bread or water, because of all the sin you had done. I was afraid of the LORD’s anger. But the LORD listened to me that time too. And the LORD was very angry with Aaron, and I prayed for Aaron as well.

And I took your sin, the calf you had made, and burned it, and crushed it, and ground it into dust, and threw the dust into the stream that came down from the mountain.

And it was not only the calf. At Taberah, at Massah, and at the graves of craving, you provoked the LORD. And at Kadesh-barnea, when the LORD said go up and possess the land, you rebelled, and did not believe him.

You have been rebellious against the LORD, Moses says, from the day that I knew you.

Then Moses tells them what he prayed, when he lay on his face before God.

O Lord GOD, do not destroy your people, your own inheritance, whom you rescued with your greatness, and brought out of Egypt with a mighty hand. Remember your servants, Abraham, Isaac and Jacob. Do not look at the stubbornness of this people, or their wickedness, or their sin. Otherwise the land you brought us out of will say, the LORD was not able to bring them into the land he promised, or he hated them, so he brought them out to destroy them in the wilderness.

And Moses ends his prayer with these words.

Moses did not argue that the people were good. He knew they were not. He appealed to God’s promises, and God’s name, and God’s mercy. And God listened.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 5. 'Not for thy righteousness, or for the uprightness of thine heart, dost thou go to possess their land: but for the wickedness of these nations the LORD thy God doth drive them out from before thee, and that he may perform the word which the LORD sware unto thy fathers, Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob.' Two reasons, and neither of them was Israel’s goodness. The wickedness of the nations, and God’s faithfulness to his promise. Israel received the land as a gift of grace, not a reward.

Verse 6. 'Understand therefore, that the LORD thy God giveth thee not this good land to possess it for thy righteousness; for thou art a stiffnecked people.' The New Testament says the same of every believer. By grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God. We cannot boast of earning God’s blessing. All we can do is receive it with thanks.

Verse 29. 'Yet they are thy people and thine inheritance, which thou broughtest out by thy mighty power and by thy stretched out arm.' Yet they are thy people. In spite of everything, Moses said, they still belong to you. That is the heart of Moses’ prayer. And it is the heart of the gospel. Not because we are good, but because God has made us his own.

Paul wrote, 'For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man should boast.' And the letter to the Hebrews says that Jesus 'ever liveth to make intercession for them.'

Is there any part of you that thinks God blesses you because you are good? Thank him today that everything you have from him is grace. And pray for someone who is struggling, as Moses prayed for the people.

Here is today’s question. Moses said, not for thy righteousness. Why is it so important to remember that God’s blessings are a gift, not a reward for being good?

Let us pray. Lord God, I have not earned your blessings. Everything I have from you is grace. Forgive my pride, and my stubbornness. Thank you that, in spite of my sin, I am yours. And thank you for Jesus, who prays for us, as Moses prayed for the people. Amen.
