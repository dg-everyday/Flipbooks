The Living Voice. Deuteronomy, chapter 13. Ye Shall Serve Him, and Cleave unto Him.

Imagine someone comes to you claiming to speak for God. And to prove it, they do something amazing. A sign. A miracle. Something they predicted actually happens. And then they say: now, come and follow a different god. What would you do? It is a harder question than it sounds. And Moses gives a clear answer.

In chapter twelve, Moses began the laws for life in the land. God’s people were to worship at the place the LORD shall choose, not every man whatsoever is right in his own eyes. And there they were to rejoice before the LORD, with their whole households, the servants and the Levite too.

Now, in chapter thirteen, Moses warns about those who would lead God’s people away from him. Listen for how they are to test those who claim to speak for God. And listen for the reason God allows such tests.

If a prophet or a dreamer of dreams appears among you, Moses says, and gives you a sign or a wonder, and the sign or wonder comes true, and then he says, let us follow other gods, gods you have not known, and let us serve them.

Do not listen to that prophet, Moses says.

This is important. A miracle on its own was not proof that someone came from God. The real test was this: does this person lead you toward the LORD, or away from him? Even impressive signs could not make a false message true.

And then Moses explains why God would allow such a thing.

God does not tempt anyone to sin. But he allows our love for him to be tested. Will we hold on to him, even when something dazzling pulls us the other way?

And Moses tells them what to do instead.

Walk after God. Fear him. Keep his commandments. Obey his voice. Serve him. And cleave to him. Six things, all pointing in one direction: toward the LORD.

Then Moses says that such a false prophet was to be put to death, because he had tried to turn the people away from the LORD who brought them out of Egypt.

These are some of the hardest laws in Deuteronomy, and Christians have understood them in different ways. They belonged to Israel as a nation under God’s covenant, in a land where turning to other gods would destroy the whole people, as it did again and again in the book of Judges. The New Testament does not call Christians to punish false teachers this way. But it does call us to take them just as seriously, and not to follow them.

Then Moses says something even harder. What if the one who tempts you is someone close to you? Your brother, or your son or daughter, or your wife, or your closest friend, someone you love like your own soul. What if they secretly say, let us go and serve other gods, the gods of the nations around us?

Do not give in to them, Moses says. Do not listen to them. Even love for family must not come before love for God.

Jesus said something similar. He that loveth father or mother more than me is not worthy of me. Not because family does not matter, but because God must come first.

Moses goes on to speak about a whole town being drawn away to other gods by worthless men. The people were to investigate carefully, and ask diligently, to be sure it was true, before acting. God did not want anyone judged on rumour.

And Moses ends the chapter with the heart of the matter.

All of this was about one thing: keeping Israel’s heart faithful to the LORD alone. Idolatry was not a small matter. It would turn God’s people away from the one who gave them life.

The New Testament gives the same warning in its own way. John wrote, believe not every spirit, but try the spirits whether they are of God. And Paul wrote that even if an angel from heaven preached a different gospel, it should not be believed. The question is always the same: does this lead me toward the true God, or away from him?

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 3. 'Thou shalt not hearken unto the words of that prophet, or that dreamer of dreams: for the LORD your God proveth you, to know whether ye love the LORD your God with all your heart and with all your soul.' The test was about love. Do you love God with all your heart? Then you will not be drawn away by anyone, however impressive. Love for God protects us from being led astray.

Verse 4. 'Ye shall walk after the LORD your God, and fear him, and keep his commandments, and obey his voice, and ye shall serve him, and cleave unto him.' Cleave means to hold on tight, to cling. Like a child clinging to a parent in a crowd. When other voices call, the answer is to hold on to God even more tightly.

Verse 18. 'When thou shalt hearken to the voice of the LORD thy God, to keep all his commandments which I command thee this day, to do that which is right in the eyes of the LORD thy God.' Right in the eyes of the LORD. Not right in our own eyes, or in the eyes of a persuasive leader, or even in the eyes of people we love. Right in God’s eyes. That is the standard.

John wrote, 'Beloved, believe not every spirit, but try the spirits whether they are of God: because many false prophets are gone out into the world.' And Jesus said, 'He that loveth father or mother more than me is not worthy of me'.

Is there any voice in your life, a teacher, a friend, a book, or a trend, that is slowly pulling you away from God? Test it today against God’s word. And choose to cleave to the LORD.

Here is today’s question. Moses said that even a miracle was not proof that someone came from God. How can we test whether a teaching or voice is leading us toward God or away from him?

Let us pray. Lord God, I want to love you with all my heart and with all my soul. When other voices try to pull me away, even impressive ones, even voices I love, help me to cleave to you. Give me wisdom to test what I hear, and to do what is right in your eyes. Amen.
