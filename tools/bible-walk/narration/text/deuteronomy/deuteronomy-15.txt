The Living Voice. Deuteronomy, chapter 15. Thou Shalt Open Thine Hand Wide.

Imagine owing someone more money than you could ever repay. Every year, the debt hangs over you. And then one day, the person you owe comes to your door, and says: it is gone. Cancelled. You owe me nothing. In Israel, God built a day like that into the life of the nation, again and again.

In chapter fourteen, Moses told the people, ye are the children of the LORD your God, a holy people, chosen as his special treasure. Even what they ate reminded them they belonged to God. And every third year, the tithe was stored so that the Levite, the stranger, the fatherless and the widow could come, and eat, and be satisfied.

Now, in chapter fifteen, Moses speaks about debts, the poor, and servants. Listen for how God wants his people to give. And listen for the reason he gives them.

At the end of every seven years, Moses says, you shall make a release.

This is how it works. Every lender shall cancel what his neighbour owes him, his fellow Israelite. He shall not demand payment, because the LORD’s release has been proclaimed.

Every seventh year, debts between Israelites were cancelled. No one would be crushed by debt for ever. The poor would get a fresh start.

And then Moses says something surprising. There should be no poor among you, for the LORD will greatly bless you in the land, if only you carefully obey the voice of the LORD your God.

God’s plan was for a people so generous, and so faithful, that poverty would be overcome.

But Moses is realistic too. If there is a poor man among you, one of your brothers, in any of your towns, do not harden your heart, or shut your hand against him.

Instead, Moses says, open your hand wide to him, and freely lend him enough for whatever he needs.

Then Moses warns against a sneaky thought. Be careful, he says, that you do not think in your heart, the seventh year, the year of release, is almost here, so I will not lend to him, because I will never get it back. Do not look on your poor brother with a grudging eye, and give him nothing. He may cry out to the LORD against you, and it will be counted as sin.

God saw through the clever excuse. Lending only when you are sure to get it back is not generosity at all.

And then Moses says how to give.

Give freely, and do not be sorry afterwards. God sees, and God will bless.

And then Moses says something Jesus later quoted.

Then Moses speaks about servants. In those days, a poor Israelite might sell himself as a servant to pay his debts. But Moses says, if your brother, a Hebrew man or woman, is sold to you, and serves you six years, then in the seventh year you must let them go free.

And do not send them away empty handed, Moses says. Give to them generously, from your flock, your threshing floor and your winepress. Give as the LORD your God has blessed you.

A servant set free with nothing would soon fall back into poverty. So God told his people to send them out with enough to start a new life.

And Moses gives the reason.

You know what it is like, Moses says. You were slaves once. And God set you free, and sent you out with gifts. Now do the same for others.

Moses adds that if a servant does not want to leave, because he loves his master and his household, and is happy there, he may choose to stay, as a servant for life. And Moses says, do not think it hard to set a servant free. He has served you well, worth twice a hired worker. And the LORD your God will bless you in all you do.

And the chapter ends with the firstborn of the flocks and herds, set apart for God, and eaten with rejoicing before him, at the place he chooses.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 10. 'Thou shalt surely give him, and thine heart shall not be grieved when thou givest unto him: because that for this thing the LORD thy God shall bless thee in all thy works, and in all that thou puttest thine hand unto.' Thine heart shall not be grieved. Not grudgingly, not with regret. God cares about the heart behind our giving. Paul wrote the same: not grudgingly, or of necessity: for God loveth a cheerful giver.

Verse 11. 'For the poor shall never cease out of the land: therefore I command thee, saying, Thou shalt open thine hand wide unto thy brother, to thy poor, and to thy needy, in thy land.' The poor shall never cease. Not an excuse to do nothing, but a reason to keep giving. Jesus quoted these words, saying, ye have the poor always with you. There will always be people in need. So there will always be a call to open our hands wide.

Verse 15. 'And thou shalt remember that thou wast a bondman in the land of Egypt, and the LORD thy God redeemed thee: therefore I command thee this thing to day.' Remember that thou wast a bondman. A bondman is a slave. Israel’s generosity was to flow from their memory of being rescued. When we remember how God has rescued us, it moves us to be generous to others.

Paul wrote, 'Every man according as he purposeth in his heart, so let him give; not grudgingly, or of necessity: for God loveth a cheerful giver.' And Jesus said, 'Give, and it shall be given unto you; good measure, pressed down, and shaken together, and running over'.

Is there someone in need whom you could help today, with money, food, time or practical help? Open your hand wide, and give cheerfully, remembering how God has rescued and blessed you.

Here is today’s question. God told his people to give without a grudging heart, remembering that they had once been slaves. How does remembering what God has done for you change the way you give?

Let us pray. Lord God, you rescued me, and gave me more than I could ever deserve. Open my hand wide to those in need. Take away any grudging in my heart, and make me a cheerful giver. Help me to remember your kindness, and to show it to others. Amen.
