The Living Voice. Deuteronomy, chapter 17. He Shall Read Therein All the Days of His Life.

Imagine a new king, sitting down on his throne for the very first time. Servants bring him a crown, a sceptre, a royal robe. And then, before he does anything else, they bring him a blank book, and a pen. His first task as king is not to give orders, or plan a war. It is to sit down and copy out, by hand, the whole law of God.

In chapter sixteen, Moses described Israel’s three great feasts, where everyone rejoiced before the LORD, even the stranger, the fatherless and the widow. Every man was to give as he was able. And Moses said of the judges, that which is altogether just shalt thou follow.

Now, in chapter seventeen, Moses speaks about justice, and looks far ahead to a day when Israel will have a king. Listen for what the king must not do. And listen for what he must do every day.

The chapter begins with a short rule. Do not offer to the LORD your God any ox or sheep that has a blemish or defect. God deserved the best, not the leftovers.

Then Moses speaks about a man or woman in Israel found worshipping other gods, the sun or the moon or the stars. Such a person was to be judged. But Moses insists on something important. The matter must be investigated carefully, and found to be certainly true. And no one could be condemned on the word of only one witness. There had to be two or three.

God protected people from false accusations. Justice required evidence, not rumour, and not one person’s word against another’s.

Then Moses speaks about difficult cases. If a case is too hard for your local judges, take it to the place the LORD chooses, to the priests and the judge serving there. They will give you their decision, and you must follow it.

And then Moses looks ahead. One day, he says, when you have come into the land and settled there, you will say, let us set a king over us, like all the nations around us.

Remember, in the first book of Samuel, that is exactly what the people said. Make us a king to judge us like all the nations. Moses saw it coming, long before.

And Moses says, you may set a king over you, but he must be the one the LORD your God chooses. He must be one of your own people, not a foreigner.

Then Moses gives three warnings for the king.

He must not gather great numbers of horses for himself, or send people back to Egypt to get more horses. Horses meant chariots and military power. The king was to trust in God, not in his army.

He must not take many wives for himself, or his heart will be turned away from God.

And he must not pile up great amounts of silver and gold for himself.

Power, pleasure and riches. The three great temptations of a king. And sadly, the Bible tells us that King Solomon, for all his wisdom, did all three. He gathered thousands of horses, many from Egypt. He took many wives, who turned his heart to other gods. And he made silver as common as stones in Jerusalem. And it led to the breaking of his kingdom.

But Moses does not only tell the king what not to do. He tells him what he must do.

The king was to write out the law himself, by his own hand. Not have a servant do it. Writing it out would help him understand it, and remember it.

And the copy was not to sit on a shelf.

Every day of his life. The most powerful man in the land was to sit down every day, and read God’s word, and learn from it.

And Moses says why.

The king was not to think of himself as better than everyone else. He was a brother among brothers, under God’s law, just like everyone else.

What a picture of a good leader. Humble. Reading God’s word every day. Remembering that he answers to God. Not grabbing power or pleasure or wealth for himself.

Many years later, a King came who was everything this chapter describes, and more. Jesus, who knew God’s word perfectly, who did not grasp at power, who lived humbly among his brothers and sisters, and who said, the Son of man came not to be ministered unto, but to minister.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 18. 'And it shall be, when he sitteth upon the throne of his kingdom, that he shall write him a copy of this law in a book out of that which is before the priests the Levites:' The first thing a king was to do was write out God’s law. Before ruling others, he had to be ruled by God’s word. Anyone who leads others, in a family, a church, or a workplace, needs first to be shaped by God’s word themselves.

Verse 19. 'And it shall be with him, and he shall read therein all the days of his life: that he may learn to fear the LORD his God, to keep all the words of this law and these statutes, to do them:' All the days of his life. Not once, but every day. Reading God’s word is not a task you finish. It is a daily habit, for life. And notice why: that he may learn. Even a king never stopped being a learner.

Verse 20. 'That his heart be not lifted up above his brethren, and that he turn not aside from the commandment, to the right hand, or to the left: to the end that he may prolong his days in his kingdom, he, and his children, in the midst of Israel.' That his heart be not lifted up. God’s word keeps us humble. When we read it every day, we are reminded that we are under God, not above others. Pride is the great danger of power. God’s word is the cure.

Jesus said, 'For even the Son of man came not to be ministered unto, but to minister, and to give his life a ransom for many.' And a psalm says, 'Blessed is the man… his delight is in the law of the LORD; and in his law doth he meditate day and night.'

Read a passage of God’s word today, even a short one. If you can, write out one verse by hand, as the king was told to do. Let it humble you, and shape your heart.

Here is today’s question. The king was to read God’s law every day, so that his heart would not be lifted up above his brothers. How does reading God’s word help to keep us humble?

Let us pray. Lord God, thank you for your word. Help me to read it every day of my life, and to learn from it. Keep my heart from being lifted up above others. Thank you for Jesus, the true King, who came not to be served, but to serve. Make me like him. Amen.
