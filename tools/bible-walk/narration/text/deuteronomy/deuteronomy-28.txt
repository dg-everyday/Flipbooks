The Living Voice. Deuteronomy, chapter 28. Blessed Shalt Thou Be When Thou Comest In.

Imagine blessings chasing after you. Not you chasing them, but blessings running after you, catching up with you, overtaking you, wherever you go. In the city, in the field, at home, on the road. That is the picture Moses paints at the start of Deuteronomy chapter twenty eight. But the chapter has another side too. And it is one of the most sobering chapters in the Bible.

In chapter twenty seven, Moses commanded that the law be written very plainly on great plastered stones, and that the tribes stand on mount Gerizim and mount Ebal. The Levites called out curses on secret sins, on those who mislead the blind, and on those who twist justice for the stranger, the fatherless and the widow. And after each, all the people said, Amen.

Now, in chapter twenty eight, Moses describes the blessings that come with obedience, and the warnings for those who turn away. This is a long chapter, and the warnings are very hard, so we will tell them briefly. Listen for how the blessings are described. And listen for the reason Moses gives for the curses.

If you listen carefully to the voice of the LORD your God, Moses says, and are careful to do all his commandments, the LORD your God will set you high above all the nations of the earth.

And then Moses says.

Overtake. The blessings would run after them and catch them.

And Moses describes them.

Blessed shall you be in the city, and blessed shall you be in the field.

Blessed shall be your children, and your crops, and your herds and flocks.

Blessed shall be your basket and your kneading bowl.

Wherever they lived, wherever they worked, whatever they ate. Every part of life, touched by God’s blessing.

The LORD will cause your enemies to be defeated before you, Moses says. They will come out against you one way, and flee before you seven ways.

The LORD will command his blessing on your barns, and on everything you put your hand to. He will make you a holy people for himself, as he promised. And all the peoples of the earth will see that you are called by the name of the LORD.

The LORD will open the heavens, his good storehouse, and send rain on your land in its season. You will lend to many nations, and borrow from none. The LORD will make you the head, and not the tail. You will be on top, and not underneath, if you listen to his commands, and do not turn aside from them, to the right or to the left, to follow other gods.

These were wonderful promises. A land blessed by God, a people blessed by God, a witness to the whole world.

But then the chapter turns.

If you will not listen to the voice of the LORD your God, Moses says, and will not do his commandments, then all these curses will come upon you, and overtake you.

The same word. Overtake. Just as blessings would chase after the obedient, curses would chase after the disobedient.

What follows is a long, dark list. Many more verses of warning than of blessing. Disease and drought. Crops failing. Enemies winning. Cities besieged, and terrible suffering. And finally, being taken away from the land altogether, and scattered among the nations. The details are among the hardest in the whole Bible, and we will not dwell on them.

But Moses gives a reason for these warnings that is worth hearing.

God did not only want obedience. He wanted joy. He had given them so much. And he longed for them to serve him gladly, with grateful hearts.

And Moses warns of the final curse. The LORD will scatter you among all peoples, from one end of the earth to the other. And there you will serve other gods, of wood and stone. Among those nations you will find no rest, and your heart will tremble, and your eyes will fail, and your soul will be in sorrow.

Sadly, this is exactly what happened, many years later. Israel turned away from God, again and again, despite the warnings of the prophets. And in the end, they were defeated, and carried away into exile, to Assyria and Babylon. In the book of Daniel, we heard about young men far from home in Babylon. And in Nehemiah, we heard how a small group returned to rebuild Jerusalem’s walls.

These warnings came true. But so did God’s mercy. In chapter thirty, we will hear that even after all this, if the people turned back to God with all their heart, he would bring them home.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 2. 'And all these blessings shall come on thee, and overtake thee, if thou shalt hearken unto the voice of the LORD thy God.' Overtake thee. God’s blessings are not something we have to chase down. They chase us, when we walk with him. A psalm says, surely goodness and mercy shall follow me all the days of my life. The word follow there means pursue.

Verse 6. 'Blessed shalt thou be when thou comest in, and blessed shalt thou be when thou goest out.' Coming in and going out means the whole of life. Setting out to start the day, and coming home when the work is done. Every journey, every task. God’s blessing surrounds the whole of life for those who follow him.

Verse 47. 'Because thou servedst not the LORD thy God with joyfulness, and with gladness of heart, for the abundance of all things;' God wanted his people to serve him with joy, out of thankfulness for all he had given. Grumbling, ungrateful service was not what he wanted. A psalm says, serve the LORD with gladness. When we remember how much God has given us, joy comes naturally.

A psalm says, 'Surely goodness and mercy shall follow me all the days of my life: and I will dwell in the house of the LORD for ever.' And another psalm says, 'The LORD shall preserve thy going out and thy coming in from this time forth, and even for evermore.'

Count the blessings God has given you today, in the city and in the field, coming in and going out. Then serve him today with joy and gladness of heart, out of thankfulness for all he has given.

Here is today’s question. Moses said the curses came because the people did not serve God with joyfulness and gladness of heart. Why do you think God cares so much about the joy in our hearts when we serve him?

Let us pray. Lord God, thank you for every blessing you have poured out on me. Thank you that your goodness and mercy follow me. Forgive me for the times I have served you grudgingly, without joy. Fill my heart with gladness, and help me to serve you with joy, coming in and going out. Amen.
