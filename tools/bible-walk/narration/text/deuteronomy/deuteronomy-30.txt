The Living Voice. Deuteronomy, chapter 30. Therefore Choose Life.

Two roads stretch out in front of you. One leads to life. The other leads to death. Someone you trust stands at the fork, pointing, and calling out to you: choose this one. Choose life. That is what Moses does at the end of Deuteronomy chapter thirty. And he says it is not too hard. The word is not far away. It is right here, in your mouth, and in your heart.

In chapter twenty nine, Moses gathered the whole people to renew the covenant, from the leaders to the hewer of wood and the drawer of water. He reminded them that their clothes and sandals had not worn out in the wilderness. And he said, the secret things belong unto the LORD our God, but those things which are revealed belong unto us.

Now, in chapter thirty, Moses looks far ahead, past the warnings, to a day of coming home. Listen for how close God’s word is. And listen for the choice Moses sets before the people.

The chapter begins with hope.

When all these things have happened to you, Moses says, the blessings and the curses I have set before you, and you are scattered among the nations, and you remember these words there.

And when you return to the LORD your God, and obey his voice, you and your children, with all your heart and with all your soul.

Then a promise.

Even if you have been driven to the farthest corner under heaven, Moses says, from there the LORD your God will gather you, and from there he will bring you back. He will bring you into the land your fathers possessed, and you will possess it.

Remember the warnings of chapter twenty eight? Scattering among the nations. Here, God promises that scattering would not be the end. If they turned back, he would bring them home. And that is what happened, long after. In the books of Ezra and Nehemiah, we heard how God brought his people back from Babylon.

And then Moses says something even more wonderful. The LORD your God will change your heart, and the heart of your children, so that you will love the LORD your God with all your heart and with all your soul, and live.

God would not only bring them home. He would change their hearts from the inside, so they would truly love him. The prophets later spoke of this too. Through Ezekiel, God said, a new heart also will I give you.

And the LORD will again delight in you, Moses says, and do you good, just as he delighted in your fathers.

Then Moses speaks about the commandment itself.

This commandment that I give you today, he says, is not too hard for you, and it is not far away.

It is not up in heaven, so that you would have to say, Who will go up to heaven for us, and bring it down to us, so that we can hear it and do it?

It is not across the sea, so that you would have to say, Who will cross the sea for us, and bring it back to us?

God had not hidden his word in the sky, or across the ocean, where only heroes could reach it. He had brought it near.

Long after, Paul quoted these very words in his letter to the Romans. He said that the word that is near you is the message of faith in Christ. That if you confess with your mouth the Lord Jesus, and believe in your heart that God raised him from the dead, you will be saved. God did not make us climb to heaven. Christ came down to us.

And then Moses comes to the great choice.

Look, I have set before you today life and good, and death and evil.

If you love the LORD your God, walk in his ways, and keep his commandments, you will live and grow, and the LORD your God will bless you in the land.

But if your heart turns away, and you will not listen, but are drawn away to worship other gods, then you will surely perish, and will not live long in the land.

And then Moses calls on witnesses. Not people, but the whole of creation.

Then Moses explains what choosing life means. Love the LORD your God, obey his voice, and hold fast to him. For he is your life.

Choosing life was not choosing a set of rules. It was choosing a person. God himself is life.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 3. 'That then the LORD thy God will turn thy captivity, and have compassion upon thee, and will return and gather thee from all the nations, whither the LORD thy God hath scattered thee.' Have compassion upon thee. Even when the people had wandered far and suffered the consequences, God’s heart was full of compassion. He would gather them back. No one is too far away for God to bring home.

Verse 14. 'But the word is very nigh unto thee, in thy mouth, and in thy heart, that thou mayest do it.' Very nigh means very near. God’s word is not out of reach. We do not have to be scholars, or climb to heaven. He has brought his word close, and through Jesus, he has come close himself.

Verse 19. 'I call heaven and earth to record this day against you, that I have set before you life and death, blessing and cursing: therefore choose life, that both thou and thy seed may live:' Choose life. God does not force us. He sets the choice before us, and pleads with us to choose well. And our choice affects not just us, but our children too.

Paul wrote, 'But what saith it? The word is nigh thee, even in thy mouth, and in thy heart: that is, the word of faith, which we preach'. And Jesus said, 'I am the way, the truth, and the life'.

Every day brings choices that lead toward life, or away from it. Choose life today, in a decision, a word, or a habit. And remember that God’s word is near you, ready to help.

Here is today’s question. Moses said God’s word is not far away, but very near, in your mouth and in your heart. How can you keep God’s word close to you every day?

Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that you are full of compassion, and that no one is too far away for you to bring home. Thank you that your word is near me, and that Jesus came close. Change my heart, so that I love you with all of it. Today, I choose life. I choose you. Amen.
