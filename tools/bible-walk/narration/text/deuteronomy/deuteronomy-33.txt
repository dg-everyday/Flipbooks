The Living Voice. Deuteronomy, chapter 33. Underneath Are the Everlasting Arms.

A child is falling. Maybe from a tree, or from the top of a slide. For one frightening moment, there is nothing underneath. And then, strong arms catch them. Their father was there all along, standing just below, ready. In Deuteronomy chapter thirty three, Moses gives one of the most comforting pictures in the whole Bible. Underneath are the everlasting arms.

In chapter thirty two, Moses sang his great song. He is the Rock, his work is perfect. God found his people in the howling wilderness, and kept them as the apple of his eye. As an eagle stirreth up her nest, he carried them on his wings. And then God told Moses to go up mount Nebo, to see the land he would not enter.

Now, in chapter thirty three, before he goes, Moses blesses each of the tribes of Israel. Listen for the blessing given to Benjamin. And listen for the words Moses saves for the very end.

The chapter begins, 'And this is the blessing, wherewith Moses the man of God blessed the children of Israel before his death.'

The man of God. This is the first time the Bible calls Moses by that name. After all the years, all the struggles, all the times he spoke for God, that is how he is remembered.

Moses begins by remembering how God came to his people. The LORD came from Sinai, and shone on them like the dawning sun, from mount Paran.

'Yea, he loved the people'. Before anything else, before the law, before the blessings, God loved his people.

Then Moses blesses the tribes, one by one, like a father blessing his children.

Of Reuben, Moses says, Let Reuben live, and not die, and let his men be many.

Of Judah, he says, Hear, LORD, the voice of Judah, and bring him to his people. Be a help to him against his enemies.

Of Levi, the tribe of the priests, Moses says, they shall teach Jacob thy judgments, and Israel thy law. They will offer incense before you, and whole offerings on your altar. Bless, LORD, his work.

And then Moses comes to Benjamin, the youngest tribe.

Between his shoulders. Like a father carrying his small child on his back, the child resting safely between his shoulders. That is how God would carry Benjamin.

Of Joseph, Moses gives a long blessing, full of good things. His land would be blessed with the precious things of heaven, the dew, and the deep waters beneath, the precious fruits brought out by the sun, the best of the ancient mountains, and 'the good will of him that dwelt in the bush.'

The one who dwelt in the bush. Do you remember? God spoke to Moses from the burning bush, long ago, at the very beginning of his calling. Now, at the end of his life, Moses remembers that moment. The same God who called him from the bush would bless Joseph.

Of Zebulun and Issachar, he says, Rejoice, Zebulun, in your going out, and Issachar, in your tents.

Of Gad, he says, Blessed be he that enlargeth Gad.

Of Dan, he says, Dan is a lion’s whelp.

Of Naphtali, he says, O Naphtali, satisfied with favour, and full with the blessing of the LORD.

And of Asher, he says, Let Asher be blessed with children, and let him be acceptable to his brethren.

And then Moses gives Asher a promise that has encouraged people ever since.

Shoes of iron and brass were strong, made for rough and rocky paths. And as thy days, so shall thy strength be. Whatever each day brings, God would give strength for that day. Not strength for next year, or next week. Strength for today.

And then Moses turns from the tribes, to God himself.

There is none like the God of Jeshurun, who rides through the heavens to help you, and on the clouds in his majesty.

And then he says the words that this chapter is loved for.

And Moses ends his blessing with joy. 'Happy art thou, O Israel: who is like unto thee, O people saved by the LORD, the shield of thy help'.

These are the last recorded words of Moses to the people. Not a warning. Not a law. A blessing. Happy are you, O people saved by the LORD.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 12. 'And of Benjamin he said, The beloved of the LORD shall dwell in safety by him; and the LORD shall cover him all the day long, and he shall dwell between his shoulders.' The beloved of the LORD. Covered all the day long, carried between his shoulders. That is how God carries those he loves. Safe, close, held.

Verse 25. 'Thy shoes shall be iron and brass; and as thy days, so shall thy strength be.' God gives strength for each day as it comes. We do not need to worry about having enough strength for everything ahead. When the day comes, the strength will come with it.

Verse 27. 'The eternal God is thy refuge, and underneath are the everlasting arms' The eternal God is our refuge, our safe place. And underneath us, always, are his everlasting arms. However far we fall, we can never fall below them. They will hold us, and they will never tire.

Isaiah wrote, 'He shall feed his flock like a shepherd: he shall gather the lambs with his arm, and carry them in his bosom'. And a psalm says, 'God is our refuge and strength, a very present help in trouble.'

Is today a hard day? Remember: as thy days, so shall thy strength be. Ask God for strength for today, just today. And rest in the knowledge that underneath you are the everlasting arms.

Here is today’s question. Moses said, underneath are the everlasting arms. How does that picture help you when you feel like you are falling, or afraid?

Let us pray. Eternal God, you are my refuge. Thank you that underneath me are your everlasting arms. Carry me, as you carried Benjamin, between your shoulders. Give me strength for this day, as you have promised. I am happy to be one of your people, saved by you. Amen.
