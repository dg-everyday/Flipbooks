The Living Voice. Ephesians, chapter 2. By Grace Are Ye Saved.

A man lies at the bottom of a deep, dark pit. He has fallen in, and he cannot climb out. He has tried everything. He is too weak. And then, a rope comes down from above, and a voice says, hold on. I will pull you up. The man does not climb out by his own strength. He is lifted out by someone else. That is what grace means.

In chapter one, Paul praised God for all spiritual blessings in Christ. We are chosen, adopted, accepted in the beloved, forgiven through his blood, and sealed with the Holy Spirit. And Paul prayed that the eyes of our understanding would be enlightened, to see our hope, our riches, and God’s great power.

Now, in chapter two, Paul tells us what we were, and what God has done. Listen for two of the most important words in the Bible: But God. And listen for a wall that comes tumbling down.

Paul begins with a hard truth. 'And you hath he quickened, who were dead in trespasses and sins'. Quickened means made alive. Before God stepped in, we were dead, spiritually dead, because of our sins. A dead person cannot help themselves.

Paul says we used to follow the ways of this world, and the evil one, the ruler of the spiritual powers of darkness, who is still at work in those who disobey God. We all lived like that once, Paul says, following our own selfish desires and thoughts. And we deserved God’s anger, just like everyone else.

And then come two wonderful words.

Even when we were dead in our sins, God made us alive together with Christ. And Paul cannot help adding, in brackets, 'by grace ye are saved'. He raised us up with Christ, and seated us with him in the heavenly places. Why? So that in the ages to come, he could show the exceeding riches of his grace, his kindness to us in Christ Jesus. We will spend eternity discovering how kind God has been to us.

And then come the most famous verses in the letter.

'Not of works, lest any man should boast.' If we could earn our salvation, we would boast about it. But we cannot. So no one can boast. All the glory goes to God.

But that does not mean good works do not matter.

Then Paul turns to a second great change. Remember, he tells the Ephesian believers, who were not Jews by birth, what you used to be. You were outsiders. You were looked down on by God’s people. 'That at that time ye were without Christ, being aliens from the commonwealth of Israel, and strangers from the covenants of promise, having no hope, and without God in the world'. Aliens means foreigners. No hope, and without God. That is the saddest description of anyone.

'But now in Christ Jesus ye who sometimes were far off are made nigh by the blood of Christ.' Nigh means near. Those who were far away have been brought near, through Jesus’ death.

And then Paul describes something amazing.

In the temple in Jerusalem, there was a real wall, which separated the outer court, where non-Jews could go, from the inner courts, where only Jews could go. A sign on that wall warned outsiders to go no further. Paul says that Jesus has broken down the wall that divided Jews and non-Jews. Not just the stone wall, but the hostility between them.

Jesus made the two groups into one new people, one new man, making peace. He brought them both back to God, in one body, through the cross. He came and preached peace to those who were far away, and to those who were near. 'For through him we both have access by one Spirit unto the Father.' Access means we can come close. Every believer, whatever their background, can come straight to God the Father, through Jesus, by the Spirit.

And Paul ends with a picture of a building. 'Now therefore ye are no more strangers and foreigners, but fellowcitizens with the saints, and of the household of God'. No longer outsiders. Citizens. Family members. Built on the foundation of the apostles and prophets, with Jesus Christ himself as the chief cornerstone, the most important stone that holds the whole building together. And the whole building grows into a holy temple for the Lord. 'In whom ye also are builded together for an habitation of God through the Spirit.' An habitation is a home. God’s people, together, are where God lives.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 8. 'For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God'. Grace is God’s kindness that we do not deserve. Saved means rescued, from sin and its punishment. Through faith means by trusting God. And not of yourselves means we cannot do it ourselves. Salvation is a gift. You do not earn a gift. You simply receive it, with thanks. That is the heart of the good news.

Verse 10. 'For we are his workmanship, created in Christ Jesus unto good works, which God hath before ordained that we should walk in them.' Workmanship means something made by a craftsman, a masterpiece. You are God’s masterpiece. We are not saved by good works. But we are saved for good works. God has already prepared good things for us to do. When we do them, we are not earning his love. We are living out the new life he has given us.

Verse 14. 'For he is our peace, who hath made both one, and hath broken down the middle wall of partition between us'. A partition is a dividing wall. People build all kinds of walls between themselves: walls of race, nationality, rich and poor, background. Jesus breaks them down. In him, people who were enemies become one family. Jesus does not only give peace. He is our peace.

Paul wrote to Titus, 'Not by works of righteousness which we have done, but according to his mercy he saved us'. And to the Galatians, 'for ye are all one in Christ Jesus.'

Thank God today that you are saved by grace, as a gift, not by anything you have done. And look for one good work God has prepared for you to do. Is there also a wall between you and someone else that Jesus wants to break down?

Here is today’s question. Paul says, by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God. Why do you think it is good news that we cannot earn our salvation?

Let us pray. Lord God, I was dead in my sins, but you, rich in mercy, made me alive with Christ. Thank you that I am saved by grace, through faith, as your gift. Thank you that I am your workmanship. Show me the good works you have prepared for me. And thank you that Jesus is our peace, and has broken down the walls between us. Amen.

Next on the walk, Ephesians chapter three. Paul tells of the mystery revealed to him. And he kneels and prays that we would know the love of Christ, which passes knowledge.
