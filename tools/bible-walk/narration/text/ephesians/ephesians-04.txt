The Living Voice. Ephesians, chapter 4. Speaking the Truth in Love.

A boy takes off his muddy, torn, smelly clothes, after a long day playing in the rain. He throws them in the wash. Then he puts on fresh, clean clothes, soft and warm. He feels like a new person. Paul says that following Jesus is a bit like that. We take off our old ways, like dirty clothes, and put on new ones.

In chapter three, Paul told of the mystery revealed to him: that people from every nation are fellowheirs and one body in Christ. He called himself less than the least of all saints. And he knelt and prayed that we would know the love of Christ, which passeth knowledge, praising God who is able to do exceeding abundantly above all that we ask or think.

Now, in chapter four, the second half of the letter begins. Paul has told us who we are. Now he tells us how to walk. Listen for the word one. And listen for what we should take off, and what we should put on.

Paul begins with an appeal. 'I therefore, the prisoner of the Lord, beseech you that ye walk worthy of the vocation wherewith ye are called'. Beseech means beg. Vocation means calling. Because of everything God has done for you, live in a way that matches it.

And how? 'With all lowliness and meekness, with longsuffering, forbearing one another in love'. Lowliness is humility. Meekness is gentleness. Longsuffering is patience. Forbearing one another means putting up with each other’s faults.

And especially this.

Then Paul lists seven things that God’s people share. 'There is one body, and one Spirit, even as ye are called in one hope of your calling; One Lord, one faith, one baptism, One God and Father of all, who is above all, and through all, and in you all.' One, one, one, seven times. We may be very different, but what we share is far greater.

But being one does not mean being the same. 'But unto every one of us is given grace according to the measure of the gift of Christ.' Each of us has been given different gifts.

Paul says Jesus gave gifts to his church: some to be apostles, some prophets, some evangelists, who share the good news, and some pastors and teachers. Why? 'For the perfecting of the saints, for the work of the ministry, for the edifying of the body of Christ'. Perfecting means equipping, preparing. Edifying means building up. Leaders are not meant to do all the work. They are meant to equip all God’s people to serve.

And the goal is that we all grow up, together, in faith and in knowing Jesus, until we become mature, like Christ himself. 'That we henceforth be no more children, tossed to and fro, and carried about with every wind of doctrine'. Like a little boat thrown about by every wave, immature believers are pushed this way and that by every new teaching. God wants us to grow up, and stand firm.

And here is how we grow.

From Christ, the whole body, joined and held together by every part doing its work, grows and builds itself up in love.

Then Paul tells them how not to live. Do not live like those who do not know God, he says, whose minds are empty and darkened, cut off from the life of God, whose hearts have grown hard, and who have given themselves over to every kind of impurity and greed. 'But ye have not so learned Christ'.

Instead, take off the old self, with its deceitful desires, like old clothes. Let your minds be made new. 'And that ye put on the new man, which after God is created in righteousness and true holiness.'

And then Paul gives very practical examples of what that looks like.

'Wherefore putting away lying, speak every man truth with his neighbour: for we are members one of another.' Take off lying. Put on truth.

'Be ye angry, and sin not: let not the sun go down upon your wrath: Neither give place to the devil.' Anger itself is not always sin. But do not let it lead you into sin, and do not hold on to it. Deal with it quickly, before the day is over. Otherwise, you give the devil a foothold.

'Let him that stole steal no more: but rather let him labour, working with his hands the thing which is good, that he may have to give to him that needeth.' Take off stealing. Put on hard work, and generosity.

'Let no corrupt communication proceed out of your mouth, but that which is good to the use of edifying, that it may minister grace unto the hearers.' Take off rotten words. Put on words that build others up, and give them grace.

'And grieve not the holy Spirit of God, whereby ye are sealed unto the day of redemption.' The Holy Spirit is a person, and he can be grieved, made sad, by how we live.

'Let all bitterness, and wrath, and anger, and clamour, and evil speaking, be put away from you, with all malice'. Clamour means shouting and quarrelling. Malice means wanting to hurt others. Take it all off.

And then, what to put on instead.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 3. 'Endeavouring to keep the unity of the Spirit in the bond of peace.' Endeavouring means making every effort. Notice, Paul does not say, create unity. The Spirit has already made us one. Our job is to keep it, to protect it. And peace is like a bond, a cord, that ties us together. Unity among God’s people does not happen by accident. It takes effort, humility and patience.

Verse 15. 'But speaking the truth in love, may grow up into him in all things, which is the head, even Christ'. Truth and love belong together. Truth without love can be harsh and hurtful. Love without truth can be weak, and let people go wrong. But truth spoken in love helps people grow. That is how the whole body of Christ grows up to be like him.

Verse 32. 'And be ye kind one to another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, even as God for Christ’s sake hath forgiven you.' Kind, tenderhearted, forgiving. This is what we put on, instead of bitterness and anger. And why should we forgive? Because God, for Christ’s sake, has forgiven us. When we remember how much we have been forgiven, we can forgive others.

The psalmist sang, 'Behold, how good and how pleasant it is for brethren to dwell together in unity!' And Jesus prayed, 'That they all may be one'.

Is there anger you have been holding on to? Deal with it today, before the sun goes down. And think of one way to put on kindness today, with your words or your actions, especially towards someone you find hard to love.

Here is today’s question. Paul says to speak the truth in love. Why do you think truth and love need each other, and which one do you find harder?

Let us pray. Lord God, help me to walk worthy of your calling, with humility, gentleness and patience. Help me to keep the unity of the Spirit. Help me to take off my old ways, and put on the new. Let my words build others up. And make me kind, tenderhearted and forgiving, as you have forgiven me in Christ. Amen.
