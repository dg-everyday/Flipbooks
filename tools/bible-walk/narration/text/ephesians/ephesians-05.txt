The Living Voice. Ephesians, chapter 5. Walk as Children of Light.

A little girl watches her father carefully. When he sits down, she sits down. When he stretches, she stretches. When he laughs, she laughs. When he puts his hands in his pockets, she puts her hands in her pockets. She wants to be just like him. Paul says that is how God’s children should be with their heavenly Father.

In chapter four, Paul began to tell us how to walk. Walk worthy of your calling. Keep the unity of the Spirit in the bond of peace. Speak the truth in love. Put off the old self, and put on the new. And be kind one to another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, as God for Christ’s sake has forgiven you.

Now, in chapter five, Paul goes on. Listen for three ways to walk: in love, in light, and in wisdom. And listen to what Paul says about husbands and wives.

Paul begins with a call to imitate God. 'Be ye therefore followers of God, as dear children'. Followers here means imitators. Like children who copy a parent they love, we should copy God.

And the first way to do that is love.

Then Paul warns about the opposite of love. Do not let any kind of sexual impurity, or greed, even be talked about among you, he says. Do not use dirty language, foolish talk or crude jokes. These do not belong in the lives of God’s people. 'but rather giving of thanks.' Instead of coarse talk, let your words be full of thankfulness.

'Let no man deceive you with vain words'. Vain words means empty words. Some people will tell you these things do not matter. Do not believe them.

Then comes the second way to walk.

'For the fruit of the Spirit is in all goodness and righteousness and truth'. Light produces good fruit. Find out what pleases the Lord.

'And have no fellowship with the unfruitful works of darkness, but rather reprove them.' Do not take part in what is done in darkness. Instead, let your light show it up for what it is. When light shines, everything is seen clearly.

'Wherefore he saith, Awake thou that sleepest, and arise from the dead, and Christ shall give thee light.' This may have been an early Christian song. Wake up, sleeper! Christ will shine on you.

Then the third way to walk: in wisdom. 'See then that ye walk circumspectly, not as fools, but as wise'. Circumspectly means carefully, looking around, like someone walking along a narrow path. 'Redeeming the time, because the days are evil.' Redeeming the time means making the most of every opportunity. Our time is precious. Do not waste it.

'Wherefore be ye not unwise, but understanding what the will of the Lord is.'

Then Paul contrasts two ways of being filled. Do not be filled with wine, which leads to wild living, he says. 'but be filled with the Spirit'. Instead of letting strong drink control you, let the Holy Spirit fill you and guide you.

And what does a Spirit filled life look like? 'Speaking to yourselves in psalms and hymns and spiritual songs, singing and making melody in your heart to the Lord'. Singing together. Making music in our hearts to God.

And then, thankfulness.

And then, 'Submitting yourselves one to another in the fear of God.' Submitting means putting others before yourself. All of us, to one another, out of respect for Christ.

Then Paul turns to marriage. And here we need to listen carefully, because faithful Christians have understood these verses in different ways. Paul writes, 'Wives, submit yourselves unto your own husbands, as unto the Lord.' And then he gives husbands an even longer instruction.

'Husbands, love your wives, even as Christ also loved the church, and gave himself for it'. How did Christ love the church? He gave his life for it. He died for it. Husbands are called to love their wives like that: not to rule over them selfishly, but to lay down their lives for them, to serve them, care for them, and put them first.

Christ loves the church, Paul says, to make her holy and beautiful, without spot or wrinkle. So husbands should love their wives as they love their own bodies, feeding and caring for them, as Christ does for the church.

Then Paul quotes from Genesis, from the very beginning. 'For this cause shall a man leave his father and mother, and shall be joined unto his wife, and they two shall be one flesh.' And then he says something surprising. 'This is a great mystery: but I speak concerning Christ and the church.' Marriage, at its best, is a living picture of something greater: the love between Jesus and his people.

'Nevertheless let every one of you in particular so love his wife even as himself; and the wife see that she reverence her husband.' Love and respect, given to each other, in a marriage that reflects the love of Christ.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 2. 'And walk in love, as Christ also hath loved us, and hath given himself for us an offering and a sacrifice to God for a sweetsmelling savour.' A sweetsmelling savour is a pleasing fragrance, like the smoke of the offerings that rose up to God. Christ’s love was not just a feeling. He gave himself, completely, on the cross. That is the kind of love we are to walk in. Not love that only takes, but love that gives.

Verse 8. 'For ye were sometimes darkness, but now are ye light in the Lord: walk as children of light'. Sometimes here means once, in the past. Notice that Paul does not just say, you were in darkness. He says, you were darkness. And now, you are light, in the Lord. God has changed who we are. So we should live like it, as children of light, letting our lives show goodness, righteousness and truth.

Verse 20. 'Giving thanks always for all things unto God and the Father in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ'. Always, for all things. That is a big challenge. Not just when things go well, but always. Not just for good things, but for all things. Even in hard times, we can thank God for his presence, his love, and the way he works all things for good. A thankful heart is one of the clearest signs of a Spirit filled life.

John wrote, 'But if we walk in the light, as he is in the light, we have fellowship one with another'. And Paul wrote to the Colossians, 'teaching and admonishing one another in psalms and hymns and spiritual songs'.

Try to give thanks always today, for all things, even the small or hard ones. And look for a way to walk in love by giving something of yourself to someone else, as Christ gave himself for us.

Here is today’s question. Paul says, ye were sometimes darkness, but now are ye light in the Lord. What difference does it make to know that God has changed who you are, not just what you do?

Let us pray. Lord God, help me to imitate you, as a dear child. Help me to walk in love, as Christ loved me and gave himself for me. Help me to walk as a child of light, and to make the most of my time. Fill me with your Spirit, and put a song in my heart. And teach me to give thanks always, for all things. Amen.

Next on the walk, Ephesians chapter six, the last chapter of the letter. Children and parents, servants and masters. And then the call to be strong in the Lord, and put on the whole armour of God.
