The Living Voice. Ephesians, chapter 6. The Whole Armour of God.

A Roman soldier stands guard outside a prison cell. Inside, a prisoner is writing a letter. Every day, the prisoner looks at the soldier: his belt, his breastplate, his sturdy sandals, his big shield, his helmet, his sword. And one day, the prisoner has an idea. That is what God has given us, he thinks. Armour. And he begins to write.

In chapter five, Paul told us to be followers of God, as dear children. Walk in love, as Christ loved us and gave himself for us. Walk as children of light. Walk wisely, redeeming the time, filled with the Spirit, giving thanks always for all things. And he spoke of husbands and wives, and the great mystery of Christ and the church.

Now we come to the last chapter of the letter. Listen for what Paul says to families, and to workers. And listen for the six pieces of the armour of God.

Paul begins with children. 'Children, obey your parents in the Lord: for this is right.' And he quotes one of the Ten Commandments. 'Honour thy father and mother; (which is the first commandment with promise;) That it may be well with thee, and thou mayest live long on the earth.' It is the first commandment that comes with a promise of blessing.

Then he speaks to parents.

Then Paul speaks to servants and masters. In the Roman world, many people were servants, or slaves, belonging to a master. Paul told servants to work sincerely, from the heart, not just when someone was watching, but 'as the servants of Christ, doing the will of God from the heart'. Do your work as if you were serving the Lord, not people. God will reward every good thing anyone does, whether they are a servant or free.

And then he spoke to masters, which was surprising in those days. Treat your servants in the same way, he said. Stop threatening them. Remember that you too have a Master in heaven, and with him there is no favouritism. In God’s eyes, master and servant are equal. Today, these words help anyone who works: work with all your heart, as for the Lord, and treat those who work for you fairly.

And then Paul comes to his last great theme. 'Finally, my brethren, be strong in the Lord, and in the power of his might.'

Why do we need strength? Because there is a battle. 'Put on the whole armour of God, that ye may be able to stand against the wiles of the devil.' Wiles means tricks and schemes. The devil does not usually attack openly. He uses lies, temptations and discouragement.

And Paul tells us who the real enemy is.

'Wherefore take unto you the whole armour of God, that ye may be able to withstand in the evil day, and having done all, to stand.' Four times in this passage, Paul says, stand. Not run away, and not necessarily charge forward, but stand firm.

And then he describes the armour, piece by piece, like the soldier he could see.

'Stand therefore, having your loins girt about with truth'. A soldier’s belt held everything together. Truth holds our lives together: knowing God’s truth, and being truthful.

'and having on the breastplate of righteousness'. A breastplate protected the heart. Righteousness, being made right with God through Jesus, and living rightly, protects our hearts.

'And your feet shod with the preparation of the gospel of peace'. Roman soldiers had strong sandals, so they could stand firm and march far. The good news of peace with God gives us firm footing, and makes us ready to go and share it.

'Above all, taking the shield of faith, wherewith ye shall be able to quench all the fiery darts of the wicked.' Roman shields were large, and were sometimes soaked in water, so that flaming arrows would be put out when they hit. When the enemy fires his burning arrows of lies, doubts and temptations, faith, trusting God, puts them out.

'And take the helmet of salvation'. A helmet protects the head. Knowing that we are saved, and belong to God, protects our minds from fear and doubt.

'and the sword of the Spirit, which is the word of God'. The only weapon in the armour is the Word of God. When Jesus was tempted in the wilderness, he answered the devil every time by quoting Scripture. God’s Word is our sword.

And then, the most important thing of all.

And Paul asks them to pray for him, that he may speak boldly, and make known the mystery of the gospel. 'For which I am an ambassador in bonds'. An ambassador in chains! Paul was in prison, and still all he wanted was to speak boldly about Jesus.

And the letter ends. 'Peace be to the brethren, and love with faith, from God the Father and the Lord Jesus Christ. Grace be with all them that love our Lord Jesus Christ in sincerity. Amen.'

Grace at the beginning, and grace at the end. Who we are in Christ, and how to walk, in the strength of the Lord.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 10. 'Finally, my brethren, be strong in the Lord, and in the power of his might.' Notice where the strength comes from. Not be strong in yourself, but be strong in the Lord. We are not expected to win the battle in our own strength. The power of his might is the same mighty power that raised Jesus from the dead. When we feel weak, we can lean on his strength.

Verse 12. 'For we wrestle not against flesh and blood, but against principalities, against powers, against the rulers of the darkness of this world, against spiritual wickedness in high places.' Wrestle means struggle. Our real battle is not against people. The people who annoy or hurt us are not the true enemy. Behind the troubles of this world are spiritual powers of evil. That is why we need spiritual armour, not anger or revenge against people.

Verse 18. 'Praying always with all prayer and supplication in the Spirit, and watching thereunto with all perseverance and supplication for all saints'. Supplication means asking. Perseverance means keeping on. After all the armour, Paul says, pray. Always. In every situation. And keep watching, keep going, and pray for all God’s people. Prayer is how a soldier stays in touch with his commander. Without it, even the best armour is not enough.

Paul wrote to the Corinthians, 'For the weapons of our warfare are not carnal, but mighty through God'. And James wrote, 'Resist the devil, and he will flee from you.'

Go through the armour of God today, piece by piece, in prayer: truth, righteousness, the gospel of peace, faith, salvation, and God’s Word. Ask God to help you stand firm. And pray today for other believers, especially those facing hard times.

Here is today’s question. Paul says we wrestle not against flesh and blood. How might it change the way you treat people who hurt you, to remember that they are not the real enemy?

Let us pray. Lord God, thank you for walking with me through the letter to the Ephesians. Thank you that I am chosen, loved, forgiven and saved by grace. Make me strong in you, and in the power of your might. I put on your whole armour: truth, righteousness, peace, faith, salvation, and your Word. Help me to stand firm, and to pray always. Grace be with all who love our Lord Jesus Christ. Amen.

That is the end of our walk through the letter to the Ephesians. Well walked. You have finished another whole book of the Bible. Next on the walk, another short letter Paul wrote from prison, about Jesus, who is above everything: the letter to the Colossians.
