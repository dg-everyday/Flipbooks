The Living Voice. Meet the Book of Esther.

A young orphan girl grows up in a foreign land, raised by her older cousin. She has a secret: she belongs to a people that are scattered and far from home. One day, she is taken to the palace of the most powerful king on earth, and made his queen. And then a law is signed that means death for her whole people. She is the only one close enough to the king to stop it. But to go to him uninvited could cost her life.

Welcome to the book of Esther, on The Living Voice. Esther has ten chapters, so on this walk it is ten steps, one chapter at a time. It is another short win. And it is one of the most exciting stories in the whole Bible, full of danger, courage, and surprising turns.

Esther is set in the great empire of Persia. Long before, the people of Judah had been carried away to Babylon. Then Persia conquered Babylon, and the Persian king allowed the Jewish people to go home to Jerusalem. Some went. But many stayed, scattered across the empire. Esther is about those who stayed.

The king in this story is Ahasuerus. History also knows him by his Greek name, Xerxes. His empire stretched from India to Ethiopia. His palace was in a city called Shushan.

We do not know who wrote the book of Esther. But we know it was written to tell how God’s people were saved from destruction, and how a feast called Purim began.

Here is something remarkable about the book of Esther. The name of God never appears in it. Not once. There are no miracles. No angels. No voice from heaven. No prophet saying, thus saith the LORD. And yet, as you listen, you will see God’s hand everywhere. In a queen who refuses a king. In a king who cannot sleep. In a book of records read at just the right moment. In a gallows built for one man, and used for another. God is hidden in Esther, but he is never absent.

Here are three things to watch for as we walk.

Watch for the turns. Again and again in this book, things turn out the very opposite of what people planned. The proud are brought low, and the humble are lifted up. The book itself says that what was meant for sorrow was turned to joy.

Watch for feasts. There are banquets all through Esther, at the beginning, in the middle and at the end. Some are for showing off. Some are for a dangerous plan. And the last is a feast of thanksgiving.

And watch for courage. Esther was young, and afraid, and she had every reason to stay silent and stay safe. Listen for the moment when her cousin Mordecai asks her the question that changes everything: who knoweth whether thou art come to the kingdom for such a time as this?

Here is a question to carry with you. In the book of Esther, God is never named, yet he is at work in every turn of the story. Can you think of a time when God was at work in your life, even though you could not see him at the time?

Let us pray. Lord God, as I walk through the book of Esther, open my eyes to see your hand at work, even when you seem hidden. Thank you that you watch over your people, wherever they are. Give me courage, like Esther, to do what is right, even when it is hard. Amen.
