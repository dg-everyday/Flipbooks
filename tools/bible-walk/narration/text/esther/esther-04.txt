The Living Voice. Esther, chapter 4. For Such a Time as This.

A message passes back and forth between the palace and the city gate. On one side, a queen, safe behind palace walls. On the other, her cousin, dressed in sackcloth, weeping in the street. He is asking her to do something that could cost her her life. She says she cannot. And then he sends back words that people have remembered for thousands of years.

In chapter three, Haman was promoted above all the princes, and everyone bowed to him except Mordecai. In his fury, Haman plotted to destroy all the Jews in the empire. He cast lots to choose the day, and persuaded the king to sign a law, sealed with the king’s ring, that could not be changed. And the king and Haman sat down to drink, while the city was perplexed.

Now, in chapter four, God’s people mourn. And Esther must make the hardest choice of her life. This is the turning point of the whole book. Listen for Mordecai’s great question, and for Esther’s brave answer.

When Mordecai learns all that has been done, he tears his clothes, and puts on sackcloth and ashes, and goes out into the middle of the city, crying with a loud and bitter cry. He comes as far as the king’s gate, but no further. No one dressed in sackcloth was allowed to enter the king’s gate.

And in every province, wherever the king’s decree arrives, there is great mourning among the Jews, with fasting, weeping and wailing. Many lie in sackcloth and ashes.

Esther’s maids and her chamberlains come and tell her about Mordecai. The queen is deeply distressed. She sends clothes for Mordecai to wear instead of his sackcloth. But he will not take them.

It seems Esther does not yet know about the decree. Shut away in the palace, she has not heard the news.

So Esther calls Hatach, one of the king’s chamberlains who serves her, and sends him to Mordecai to find out what is wrong, and why.

Hatach goes out to Mordecai in the city square in front of the king’s gate. And Mordecai tells him everything that has happened, even the exact amount of silver Haman promised to pay to destroy the Jews. He gives Hatach a copy of the decree, to show Esther. And he asks Hatach to urge her to go to the king, and beg him for mercy, and plead with him for her people.

Hatach comes back and tells Esther all that Mordecai said.

But Esther sends Hatach back with a reply.

When Esther’s words are given to Mordecai, he sends back an answer. Think not with thyself that thou shalt escape in the king’s house, more than all the Jews. Do not think that because you are in the palace, you alone will be safe.

Then Mordecai says more.

Esther makes her decision, and sends back her answer to Mordecai.

So Mordecai goes his way, and does everything Esther has commanded him.

Notice how things have changed. At the beginning of the story, Esther always obeyed Mordecai. Now, Mordecai obeys Esther. She has become the leader. The frightened young queen has found her courage.

And notice what she asks for: fasting. In the Bible, fasting goes together with prayer. The book does not use the word prayer, and it does not name God. But why else would Esther ask the people to fast for her? She is asking them to seek God for her, before she risks her life. Even when God is not named, his people are turning to him.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 11. 'All the king’s servants, and the people of the king’s provinces, do know, that whosoever, whether man or woman, shall come unto the king into the inner court, who is not called, there is one law of his to put him to death, except such to whom the king shall hold out the golden sceptre, that he may live: but I have not been called to come in unto the king these thirty days.' Esther’s fear was real. Anyone who came to the king uninvited could be put to death, unless he held out his golden sceptre. Even the queen. And the king had not asked for her in a month. Perhaps she had fallen out of his favour. Esther was not being cowardly. She was being honest about the danger. Courage does not mean having no fear. It means doing what is right in spite of it.

Verse 14. 'For if thou altogether holdest thy peace at this time, then shall there enlargement and deliverance arise to the Jews from another place; but thou and thy father’s house shall be destroyed: and who knoweth whether thou art come to the kingdom for such a time as this?' Mordecai was sure that God would save his people somehow. Deliverance will come from another place. God does not need Esther. But he was giving her the privilege of being part of his plan. And then the great question: who knows whether you have become queen for such a time as this? All the strange turns of her life, the loss of her parents, being taken to the palace, being made queen, had brought her to this moment. Nothing in her life had been an accident.

Verse 16. 'Go, gather together all the Jews that are present in Shushan, and fast ye for me, and neither eat nor drink three days, night or day: I also and my maidens will fast likewise; and so will I go in unto the king, which is not according to the law: and if I perish, I perish.' If I perish, I perish. Esther has counted the cost, and decided. She will go to the king, even if it costs her life. She has placed herself in God’s hands. But she does not go alone. She asks God’s people to fast with her, and she fasts herself. Courage and prayer belong together.

Paul wrote, 'And we know that all things work together for good to them that love God, to them who are the called according to his purpose.' And Jesus said, 'Greater love hath no man than this, that a man lay down his life for his friends.'

God may have placed you where you are for such a time as this. Is there someone you could speak up for, or help, because of where God has put you? Ask God to show you, and to give you courage to act.

Here is today’s question. Mordecai asked Esther, who knoweth whether thou art come to the kingdom for such a time as this? Where might God have placed you for such a time as this?

Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that nothing in my life is an accident, and that you have placed me where I am for a reason. When you ask me to do something hard, give me courage like Esther’s. Help me to count the cost, to seek you in prayer, and then to act, trusting my life into your hands. Amen.
