The Living Voice. Esther, chapter 7. The Adversary and Enemy Is This Wicked Haman.

Three people sit at a table. A king, a queen and the king’s most powerful servant. The wine is poured. The king turns to his queen, and asks her once more: what is your request? It shall be granted. The queen looks across the table at the man who has planned to destroy her people. And she begins to speak.

In chapter six, the king could not sleep, and the book of records was read to him. He found that Mordecai had saved his life and had never been rewarded. Haman came to ask for Mordecai’s death, but instead the king made him lead Mordecai through the city on the king’s horse, in the king’s robes. Haman’s own wife said, thou shalt not prevail against him, but shalt surely fall before him. And the king’s servants hurried Haman to Esther’s banquet.

Now, in chapter seven, comes the moment Esther has been waiting for. This short chapter is the climax of the whole story. Listen for Esther’s courage, and for how Haman’s own plan comes back on his head.

So the king and Haman come to banquet with Esther the queen.

On this second day, as they drink wine at the banquet, the king says again to Esther, what is thy petition, queen Esther? And it shall be granted thee. And what is thy request? It shall be performed, even to the half of the kingdom.

This is the moment. Esther finally speaks.

And then she says why. For we are sold, I and my people, to be destroyed, and to perish. If we had only been sold as slaves, I would have kept quiet. But this is far worse.

Esther reveals at last the secret she has kept for years. She is one of the people under the death sentence. And she uses the very words of Haman’s decree, to be destroyed and to perish, so that the king will understand exactly what has been done.

The king is shocked. He says, 'Who is he, and where is he, that durst presume in his heart to do so?' Who would dare to do such a thing?

It seems the king does not even remember signing the decree. He had given Haman his ring and said, do as you please, without even asking who the people were. Now he discovers that the people he signed away include his own queen.

And Esther, with all her courage, points across the table.

Then Haman is terrified before the king and the queen.

The king gets up from the banquet in a rage, and goes out into the palace garden. Perhaps he needs a moment to think. He has been tricked by his most trusted servant. And the law he signed cannot be changed.

Haman stays behind, to beg Queen Esther for his life. He sees that the king has already decided against him.

In those days, people reclined on couches at banquets. In his panic, Haman throws himself down on the couch where Esther is lying. Just then, the king comes back from the garden, and sees him there. And the king believes that Haman is attacking the queen, in his own house. As soon as the king speaks, the servants cover Haman’s face. His fate is sealed.

Then one of the king’s chamberlains, named Harbonah, speaks up. Look, there is a gallows fifty cubits high, standing at Haman’s house. Haman made it for Mordecai, the man who spoke up to save the king.

The king says, hang him thereon.

Think back over the last day. Haman built a gallows for Mordecai. He went to ask for Mordecai’s death. Instead, he had to honour Mordecai through the streets. Then he was named as the enemy at the queen’s table. And at the end of the day, the gallows he built for Mordecai is used for Haman himself.

The Bible often shows this pattern. Those who dig a pit for others fall into it themselves. The evil that Haman planned came back on his own head.

But notice: the danger is not over. Haman is gone, but his decree is still law. It cannot be changed. The day of destruction is still coming. Esther has won a great victory, but the battle for her people is not finished yet.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 3. 'Then Esther the queen answered and said, If I have found favour in thy sight, O king, and if it please the king, let my life be given me at my petition, and my people at my request:' For years, Esther had hidden who she was. Now she stood with her people. She did not ask only for her own life, but for her people’s lives too. She could have kept her secret and tried to save herself. Instead, she pleaded for them. Christians have long seen in Esther a picture of Jesus, who stands before the King of heaven and pleads for his people.

Verse 6. 'And Esther said, The adversary and enemy is this wicked Haman. Then Haman was afraid before the king and the queen.' This took enormous courage. Haman was the second most powerful man in the empire, and he was sitting right there. But Esther named the evil plainly. There is a time to be patient, and there is a time to speak the truth boldly. Esther had waited for the right moment. And when it came, she did not hold back.

Verse 10. 'So they hanged Haman on the gallows that he had prepared for Mordecai. Then was the king’s wrath pacified.' The gallows Haman had prepared for Mordecai became his own. In the end, evil destroys itself. God is not mentioned, but his justice is at work. Haman’s pride had raised him higher and higher, and from that height, he fell. And the king’s anger was calmed.

A psalm says, 'He made a pit, and digged it, and is fallen into the ditch which he made. His mischief shall return upon his own head'. And the letter to the Hebrews says of Jesus, 'Wherefore he is able also to save them to the uttermost that come unto God by him, seeing he ever liveth to make intercession for them.'

Esther spoke up for her people when it would have been safer to stay silent. Is there someone who needs you to speak up for them? Pray for courage, and wisdom to know the right time. And thank Jesus that he pleads for you before the Father.

Here is today’s question. Esther pleaded for her people before the king. What does it mean to you that Jesus ever lives to make intercession, to plead, for you?

Let us pray. Lord Jesus, thank you that you stand before the Father and plead for me. Thank you that no evil plan can stand against you. Give me courage like Esther’s, to speak the truth, and to stand with those who are in danger. And help me to trust you for justice. Amen.
