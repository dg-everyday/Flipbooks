The Living Voice. Meet the Book of Exodus.

A nation of slaves, making bricks under the whip of the most powerful king on earth. They cry out, and it seems that no one is listening. Then, in a lonely desert, a bush begins to burn, and does not burn up. And a voice speaks from the fire: I have seen. I have heard. I know their sorrows. I am come down to deliver them.

Welcome to the book of Exodus, on The Living Voice. Exodus has forty chapters. On this walk we will take the first twenty, one chapter at a time. They tell the great story of the rescue, from slavery in Egypt to the mountain of God. The later chapters, about the tabernacle, can wait for another walk.

The word Exodus means going out, or departure. It is the story of how God brought his people out of Egypt. Exodus carries on straight from Genesis. Genesis ended with Jacob’s family safe in Egypt, seventy people, and Joseph’s promise: God will surely visit you. Exodus begins many years later. The family has become a great people. But a new king has risen, and they have become slaves.

Like Genesis, Exodus has long been credited to Moses, and the book itself tells us that Moses wrote down the words of the LORD.

Here is the shape of our walk. In chapters one to six, Israel suffers in Egypt, and God raises up a deliverer. A baby is saved from the river, grows up in Pharaoh’s palace, runs away, and meets God at the burning bush. In chapters seven to twelve, Moses stands before Pharaoh and says, let my people go. Pharaoh refuses, and God sends plagues on Egypt, until the last plague, and the first Passover. In chapters thirteen to eighteen, Israel walks out of Egypt, through the Red Sea on dry ground, and into the wilderness, where God gives them bread from heaven and water from the rock. And in chapters nineteen and twenty, they come to Mount Sinai, and God speaks the Ten Commandments.

Here are four things to watch for as we walk.

Watch for the name of God. At the burning bush, God tells Moses his name: I AM THAT I AM. The whole book shows what that name means. Again and again, God says, ye shall know that I am the LORD.

Watch for God hearing his people. Exodus is the story of a God who sees suffering, hears cries, and comes down to help.

Watch for the lamb. At the first Passover, the blood of a lamb on the doorposts keeps God’s people safe. The New Testament says that Jesus is 'our passover' who 'is sacrificed for us'.

And watch for the purpose of the rescue. God does not only bring his people out of slavery. He brings them to himself. He says, 'I bare you on eagles’ wings, and brought you unto myself.'

Exodus is the great rescue story of the Old Testament. The prophets and psalms look back to it again and again. And it points forward to an even greater rescue: Jesus setting people free from sin.

Here is a question to carry with you. God heard the cry of his people in Egypt and came down to deliver them. What would you ask God to set you free from as we walk through this book?

Let us pray. Lord God, you heard the cry of your people in Egypt, and you came down to deliver them. As I walk through the book of Exodus, show me who you are, the great I AM. Set me free from everything that holds me, and bring me close to you. Amen.
