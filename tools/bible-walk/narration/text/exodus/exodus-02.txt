The Living Voice. Exodus, chapter 2. Because I Drew Him out of the Water.

A mother kneels at the edge of the river Nile, hidden among the tall reeds. In her arms is her baby son, three months old. She has hidden him as long as she can. Now she lays him in a little basket, sealed with tar, and sets it among the reeds. His sister stands a little way off, watching. And then, the daughter of Pharaoh comes down to the river.

In chapter one, the children of Israel grew into a great people in Egypt. A new king, who knew not Joseph, made them slaves and made their lives bitter. He ordered the midwives to put the baby boys to death, but the midwives feared God. So Pharaoh commanded that every Hebrew son should be thrown into the river.

Now, in chapter two, a baby is born under that terrible command. This chapter covers many years, from Moses’ birth to his middle age. Watch how God protects him, and prepares him, even when Moses himself gets things badly wrong.

A man of the house of Levi marries a daughter of Levi. Later we learn their names, Amram and Jochebed. She has a son, and when she sees that he is a goodly child, she hides him three months.

When she can no longer hide him, she takes an ark of bulrushes, a little basket woven from reeds. She covers it with slime and pitch to make it watertight, and puts the child in it, and lays it in the reeds by the river’s edge. The word for this basket is the same word used for Noah’s ark. Once again, God is about to save a life through an ark on the water.

His sister stands far off, to see what will be done to him.

The daughter of Pharaoh comes down to wash herself at the river, and her maidens walk along the river’s side. She sees the ark among the reeds, and sends her maid to fetch it. When she opens it, she sees the child, and behold, the baby weeps. And she has compassion on him, and says, this is one of the Hebrews’ children.

Then the baby’s sister steps forward, bravely, and asks Pharaoh’s daughter, shall I go and call a nurse of the Hebrew women, to nurse the child for you? Pharaoh’s daughter says, go. And the girl goes and calls the child’s own mother.

Pharaoh’s daughter says to her, take this child away, and nurse it for me, and I will give thee thy wages. So the mother takes her own son home, and nurses him, and is even paid to do it. The same river that was meant to destroy him has brought him back to his mother’s arms.

Then the boy is brought to the palace.

Moses grows up as a prince in Pharaoh’s household. Later, the book of Acts tells us that he was learned in all the wisdom of the Egyptians, and was mighty in words and in deeds.

When Moses has grown up, he goes out to his own people, and looks on their burdens. He sees an Egyptian beating a Hebrew, one of his brothers. Moses looks this way and that way, and when he sees no one, he strikes down the Egyptian, and hides him in the sand. Moses knew he was a Hebrew. He wanted to help his people. But he tried to do it in his own way, in his own strength, and in secret.

The next day he goes out, and sees two Hebrews fighting. He says to the one who did the wrong, why are you striking your fellow? And the man answers, 'Who made thee a prince and a judge over us? intendest thou to kill me, as thou killedst the Egyptian?' Moses is afraid. The thing is known. When Pharaoh hears of it, he seeks to put Moses to death. So Moses flees from Pharaoh, and goes to live in the land of Midian, and sits down by a well.

The priest of Midian has seven daughters. They come to draw water for their father’s flock, but shepherds come and drive them away. Moses stands up and helps them, and waters their flock. When they go home early, their father Reuel, also called Jethro, asks, how is it that you are come so soon today? They say, an Egyptian delivered us from the shepherds, and drew water for us. Jethro says, where is he? Call him, that he may eat bread. Moses is content to live with the man, and Jethro gives him his daughter Zipporah as his wife. She has a son, and Moses calls him Gershom, which means a stranger there. 'for he said, I have been a stranger in a strange land.'

In process of time, the king of Egypt dies. But the slavery goes on. The children of Israel sigh by reason of the bondage, and they cry, and their cry comes up to God.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 10. 'And the child grew, and she brought him unto Pharaoh’s daughter, and he became her son. And she called his name Moses: and she said, Because I drew him out of the water.' The name Moses sounds like the Hebrew word for drawing out. Pharaoh had ordered Hebrew boys to be thrown into the river. Instead, his own daughter drew one out, and raised him in the palace. God used Pharaoh’s own household to protect the boy who would one day lead Israel out of Egypt. And Moses, who was drawn out of the water, would one day lead his people out through the water.

Verse 24. 'And God heard their groaning, and God remembered his covenant with Abraham, with Isaac, and with Jacob.' For many years, it may have seemed that God was silent. The slavery went on. Moses was far away in the desert. But God was not deaf. He heard every groan. And he remembered his covenant. In the Bible, when God remembers, it means he is about to act. God had not forgotten the promise he made to Abraham, long before.

Verse 25. 'And God looked upon the children of Israel, and God had respect unto them.' God looked, and God knew. The Hebrew word means he took notice of them, he cared about them, he knew them closely. Four verbs in two verses: God heard, God remembered, God looked, God knew. Whatever you are going through, God hears, remembers, sees and knows. You are not forgotten.

The letter to the Hebrews says, 'By faith Moses, when he was come to years, refused to be called the son of Pharaoh’s daughter; Choosing rather to suffer affliction with the people of God, than to enjoy the pleasures of sin for a season'. And a psalm says, 'This poor man cried, and the LORD heard him, and saved him out of all his troubles.'

Have you tried to fix something in your own strength, like Moses, and made it worse? Bring it to God, and wait for his way. And if you are crying out to God and it feels as though nothing is happening, remember: God hears, God remembers, God sees, and God knows.

Here is today’s question. God heard, remembered, looked upon and knew his people. Which of those four do you most need to remember today, and why?

Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that you protected baby Moses, and that you protect your people still. Thank you that you hear my groaning, remember your promises, see my troubles and know my heart. Teach me to wait for your way, and not to rush ahead in my own strength. Amen.
