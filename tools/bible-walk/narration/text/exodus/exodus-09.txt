The Living Voice. Exodus, chapter 9. That Thou Mayest Know That the Earth Is the LORD’S.

The sky over Egypt turns black. Thunder rolls. Then the hail begins to fall, huge stones of ice, with fire running along the ground between them. Trees are shattered. Crops are flattened. Anyone caught outside is in terrible danger. And yet a few Egyptians are safe indoors, with their animals beside them. They had listened to the warning.

In chapter eight, frogs covered Egypt, then lice, then swarms of flies. The magicians could not make the lice, and they told Pharaoh, this is the finger of God. God put a division between his people and the Egyptians, so that there were no flies in Goshen. Pharaoh bargained, promised, and broke his promise.

Now, in chapter nine, come the fifth, sixth and seventh plagues. They grow more serious each time. And for the first time, God explains to Pharaoh exactly why he is doing all this. Listen also for some Egyptians who begin to fear the word of the LORD.

The LORD tells Moses to go to Pharaoh with the same message. Let my people go, that they may serve me. If you refuse, and hold them still, the hand of the LORD will be upon your cattle in the field: upon the horses, the donkeys, the camels, the oxen and the sheep. There will be a very grievous murrain, a deadly disease among the animals.

And once again, the LORD makes a difference. Not one animal belonging to the children of Israel will die. And God sets a time: the next day the LORD shall do this thing in the land.

The next day, the LORD does it. All the cattle of Egypt in the field die. But of the cattle of Israel, not one dies. Pharaoh sends to find out, and behold, not one of the cattle of the Israelites is dead. But the heart of Pharaoh is hardened, and he does not let the people go.

Then comes the sixth plague. The LORD tells Moses and Aaron to take handfuls of ashes from a furnace, and Moses is to sprinkle them toward the heaven in the sight of Pharaoh. It becomes fine dust over all the land of Egypt, and causes painful boils to break out on people and animals. The magicians cannot even stand before Moses, because the boils are on them too. And the LORD hardens the heart of Pharaoh, and he does not listen, as the LORD had told Moses.

Then the LORD sends Moses to Pharaoh with a longer message. For this time I will send all my plagues upon your heart, and upon your servants, and upon your people, so that you may know that there is none like me in all the earth. God tells Pharaoh that he could have cut him off from the earth already. But he has a reason for letting him live.

Then God warns of a hail so heavy that nothing like it has been seen in Egypt since its founding. And God gives a merciful warning. Send now, and gather your cattle, and all that you have in the field. Every man and beast that is found in the field, and not brought home, will be struck when the hail comes down.

And the Egyptians are divided.

'And he that regarded not the word of the LORD left his servants and his cattle in the field.'

Moses stretches out his rod toward heaven, and the LORD sends thunder and hail, and fire runs along the ground. There is hail, and fire mingled with the hail, very grievous, such as there was none like it in all the land of Egypt since it became a nation. The hail strikes everything in the field, man and beast. It strikes every plant, and breaks every tree. Only in the land of Goshen, where the children of Israel live, there is no hail.

Pharaoh sends for Moses and Aaron, and says, I have sinned this time. 'the LORD is righteous, and I and my people are wicked.' Intreat the LORD, for it is enough, that there be no more mighty thunderings and hail. And I will let you go.

Moses answers him.

But Moses also says, as for thee and thy servants, I know that ye will not yet fear the LORD God.

The flax and the barley are struck, because they were ripe. But the wheat and the rye are not struck, because they had not grown up yet. God’s judgment is measured. He leaves something.

Moses goes out of the city, and spreads out his hands to the LORD. The thunders and hail cease, and the rain stops. But when Pharaoh sees that the rain and the hail and the thunders have ceased, he sins yet more, and hardens his heart, he and his servants. He does not let the children of Israel go, as the LORD had spoken by Moses.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 16. 'And in very deed for this cause have I raised thee up, for to shew in thee my power; and that my name may be declared throughout all the earth.' God was not only dealing with Pharaoh. He was making himself known to the whole world. The story of the plagues and the Red Sea would be told for generations, in many nations. Even Pharaoh’s stubbornness was used by God to spread his name. God can use even those who oppose him to accomplish his purpose.

Verse 20. 'He that feared the word of the LORD among the servants of Pharaoh made his servants and his cattle flee into the houses:' Not every Egyptian was like Pharaoh. Some of his own servants believed God’s warning, and acted on it. They were saved. Their king had not changed his mind, but they changed theirs. God’s mercy was open to anyone who would listen, even Egyptians. Faith is taking God at his word, and acting on it before the storm arrives.

Verse 29. 'that thou mayest know how that the earth is the LORD’S.' Pharaoh thought Egypt was his, and its river, and its land, and its people. But the earth is the LORD’S. The hail fell where God allowed it, and stopped when God said so. Not one part of creation is outside his control. Every field, every river, every storm belongs to him. And that means that we belong to him too.

Paul quoted God’s words to Pharaoh in his letter to the Romans: 'Even for this same purpose have I raised thee up, that I might shew my power in thee, and that my name might be declared throughout all the earth.' And a psalm says, 'The earth is the LORD’S, and the fulness thereof; the world, and they that dwell therein.'

Pharaoh said, I have sinned, but he did not really change. Is there something you have said sorry to God for, but have not truly turned away from? Ask him to change your heart. And remember that the whole earth is the LORD’S, including your life and your troubles.

Here is today’s question. Some of Pharaoh’s servants feared the word of the LORD and acted on his warning. What would it look like for you to take God at his word this week?

Let us pray. Lord God, the earth is yours, and everything in it. Thank you that your mercy was open even to the Egyptians who listened. Give me a heart that fears your word and acts on it. Keep me from saying sorry without truly turning back to you. Amen.
