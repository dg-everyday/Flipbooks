The Living Voice. Exodus, chapter 15. The LORD Is My Strength and Song.

On the far shore of the sea, a million people begin to sing. Moses leads them, and the sound rises over the water where Pharaoh’s army has just disappeared. Then Moses’ sister Miriam picks up a tambourine, and all the women follow her, dancing. It is the first great song of praise in the Bible. But three days later, the singing has stopped.

In chapter fourteen, Pharaoh chased Israel to the edge of the sea. Moses said, fear ye not, stand still, and see the salvation of the LORD. God divided the waters, and Israel walked through on dry ground, with the waters a wall on their right hand and on their left. The Egyptians followed, and the sea returned and covered them. And the people feared the LORD, and believed.

Now, in chapter fifteen, Israel responds to their rescue with a song. Then the journey continues, and they meet their first test in the wilderness. Listen for how quickly praise can turn to complaint, and for a new name of God.

Then sing Moses and the children of Israel this song unto the LORD. 'I will sing unto the LORD, for he hath triumphed gloriously: the horse and his rider hath he thrown into the sea.'

The song goes on.

The LORD is a man of war, they sing. The LORD is his name. Pharaoh’s chariots and his army he has cast into the sea. His chosen captains are drowned in the Red Sea. The depths have covered them. They sank into the bottom as a stone.

Thy right hand, O LORD, is become glorious in power. With the blast of your nostrils the waters were gathered together. The floods stood upright as a heap. The enemy said, I will pursue, I will overtake. But you blew with your wind, and the sea covered them.

Then the song asks a question.

The song looks forward, too. Thou in thy mercy hast led forth the people which thou hast redeemed. The nations will hear and be afraid, until thy people pass over, O LORD. You shall bring them in, and plant them in the mountain of your inheritance, in the place you have made to dwell in. 'The LORD shall reign for ever and ever.'

Then Miriam the prophetess, the sister of Aaron, takes a timbrel, a tambourine, in her hand. All the women go out after her with timbrels and with dances. And Miriam answers them, 'Sing ye to the LORD, for he hath triumphed gloriously'.

So Moses brings Israel on from the Red Sea, and they go out into the wilderness of Shur. They go three days in the wilderness, and find no water.

When they come to a place called Marah, they cannot drink the waters there, for they are bitter. That is why it is called Marah, which means bitter. And the people murmur against Moses, saying, what shall we drink?

Three days before, they were singing. Now they are complaining. It is easy to praise God on the shore of a miracle. It is harder to trust him in a dry desert.

Moses cries to the LORD. And the LORD shows him a tree. When Moses casts it into the waters, the waters are made sweet. There God makes a statute and an ordinance for them, and there he proves them, he tests them.

And God gives them a promise, and a new name.

Then they come to Elim, where there are twelve wells of water, and seventy palm trees. And they camp there by the waters. After the bitter water of Marah, God leads them to a place of rest and refreshment.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 2. 'The LORD is my strength and song, and he is become my salvation: he is my God, and I will prepare him an habitation; my father’s God, and I will exalt him.' Notice the words my and mine. My strength. My song. My salvation. My God. This is not just a song about what God did for a nation. It is personal. When God rescues us, he becomes our strength when we are weak, our song when we are glad, and our salvation when we are lost. And our response is to exalt him.

Verse 11. 'Who is like unto thee, O LORD, among the gods? who is like thee, glorious in holiness, fearful in praises, doing wonders?' Egypt had many gods: the river, the sun, the frog, Pharaoh himself. Not one of them could stand before the LORD. Who is like you? No one. God is glorious in holiness, so great that even our praises are full of awe. He does wonders. There is no one in heaven or on earth like him.

Verse 26. 'If thou wilt diligently hearken to the voice of the LORD thy God, and wilt do that which is right in his sight, and wilt give ear to his commandments, and keep all his statutes, I will put none of these diseases upon thee, which I have brought upon the Egyptians: for I am the LORD that healeth thee.' I am the LORD that healeth thee. In Hebrew, this name is often spoken of as Jehovah Rapha. God had just healed the bitter waters. Now he says that he is the one who heals his people. He heals bodies, and he heals hearts that have grown bitter. Listening to him and walking in his ways is the path to wholeness.

In the book of Revelation, John sees those who have overcome, standing by a sea of glass, and 'they sing the song of Moses the servant of God, and the song of the Lamb'. And Peter wrote of Jesus, 'by whose stripes ye were healed.'

Take a moment to sing, or say, your own song of praise for something God has done for you. And if you are at a Marah, a place of bitterness, do not grumble. Cry to the LORD, as Moses did. He is the LORD that heals.

Here is today’s question. Israel sang God’s praise, and then three days later complained at Marah. How can you keep trusting God after the excitement of a blessing has passed?

Let us pray. Lord God, you are my strength and my song, and you have become my salvation. There is no one like you. When I come to bitter places, help me to cry to you instead of complaining. You are the LORD that heals. Heal my body, and heal any bitterness in my heart. Amen.
