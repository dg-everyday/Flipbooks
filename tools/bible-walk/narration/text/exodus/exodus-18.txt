The Living Voice. Exodus, chapter 18. Thou Art Not Able to Perform It Thyself Alone.

A long line of people stands outside a tent in the desert, waiting. They have been waiting since the day began, and they will still be waiting when it ends. Inside, one man is trying to settle every quarrel among a million people. An older man watches all day, and then takes him aside and says, what you are doing is not good.

In chapter seventeen, God brought water out of a rock at Horeb. Then the Amalekites attacked. Joshua fought in the valley, while Moses held up his hands on the hill, with Aaron and Hur holding them up when he grew tired. Israel won the battle, and Moses built an altar and called it Jehovah-nissi, the LORD is my banner.

Now, in chapter eighteen, there is a family reunion, and a lesson in leadership. Moses’ father-in-law Jethro, a priest from Midian, comes to visit. He rejoices at what God has done, and gives Moses advice that will help him for the rest of his life.

Jethro, the priest of Midian, Moses’ father-in-law, hears of all that God has done for Moses and for Israel his people, and that the LORD has brought Israel out of Egypt.

At some point, Moses had sent his wife Zipporah and their two sons back to Jethro. Now Jethro brings them to him. The first son is Gershom, for Moses said, I have been an alien in a strange land. The second is Eliezer, which means my God is help, for Moses said, the God of my father was my help, and delivered me from the sword of Pharaoh.

Jethro comes with Zipporah and the boys into the wilderness, where Moses is camped at the mountain of God. Moses goes out to meet his father-in-law. He bows and kisses him, and they ask each other of their welfare, and come into the tent.

Moses tells his father-in-law all that the LORD has done to Pharaoh and to the Egyptians for Israel’s sake. He tells him all the travail, the hardship, that came upon them on the way, and how the LORD delivered them.

And Jethro rejoices for all the goodness which the LORD has done to Israel. He says, blessed be the LORD, who has delivered you out of the hand of the Egyptians, and out of the hand of Pharaoh.

Then Jethro makes a declaration of faith.

Jethro brings a burnt offering and sacrifices for God. And Aaron and all the elders of Israel come to eat bread with Moses’ father-in-law before God. A priest from another nation, who has heard what God has done, worships the LORD together with Israel.

The next day, Moses sits to judge the people. And the people stand by Moses from the start of the day to its end. When Jethro sees all that Moses does for the people, he says, what is this thing that you do to the people? Why do you sit alone, and all the people stand by you all day long?

Moses explains. Because the people come unto me to enquire of God. When they have a dispute, they come to me, and I judge between one and another, and I make them know the statutes of God and his laws.

Jethro says to him, 'The thing that thou doest is not good.'

Then Jethro gives his advice. Hearken now unto my voice, I will give thee counsel, and God shall be with thee. Be thou for the people to God-ward, so that you may bring their causes to God. Teach them the ordinances and the laws, and show them the way wherein they must walk, and the work that they must do.

Then Jethro tells Moses to share the work with others.

Let them judge the people at all times, Jethro says. Every great matter they shall bring to you, but every small matter they shall judge themselves. So shall it be easier for yourself, and they shall bear the burden with you. If you do this thing, and God commands you so, then you shall be able to endure, and all this people shall go to their place in peace.

So Moses hearkens to the voice of his father-in-law, and does all that he has said. Moses chooses able men out of all Israel, and makes them heads over the people. They judge the people at all times. The hard cases they bring to Moses, but every small matter they judge themselves.

Then Moses lets his father-in-law depart, and he goes his way into his own land.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 11. 'Now I know that the LORD is greater than all gods: for in the thing wherein they dealt proudly he was above them.' Jethro was a priest from another nation. But when he heard what God had done, he said, now I know. This is exactly what God said would happen: the nations would hear, and know that he is the LORD. Telling others what God has done for us is one of the most powerful ways to help them come to know him.

Verse 18. 'Thou wilt surely wear away, both thou, and this people that is with thee: for this thing is too heavy for thee; thou art not able to perform it thyself alone.' Moses was doing good work, God’s work. But he was doing too much, and doing it alone. He would wear himself out, and the people would wear out from waiting. Even good work can become a burden if we try to carry it all ourselves. God never meant for one person to do everything. It takes humility to admit, I cannot do this alone.

Verse 21. 'Moreover thou shalt provide out of all the people able men, such as fear God, men of truth, hating covetousness; and place such over them, to be rulers of thousands, and rulers of hundreds, rulers of fifties, and rulers of tens:' Jethro described what to look for in a leader. Able: they can do the job. Such as fear God: they honour God above all. Men of truth: they are honest. Hating covetousness: they cannot be bribed and are not greedy. Those four qualities still describe a good leader, in a family, a church, a workplace or a nation.

In the book of Acts, when the apostles were overwhelmed, they said, 'look ye out among you seven men of honest report, full of the Holy Ghost and wisdom, whom we may appoint over this business.' And Proverbs says, 'Where no counsel is, the people fall: but in the multitude of counsellors there is safety.'

Are you trying to carry something on your own that is too heavy for you? Be humble enough to ask for help, and to share the work. And be open, like Moses, to wise advice, even when it is hard to hear.

Here is today’s question. Jethro told Moses, thou art not able to perform it thyself alone. What burden are you carrying that you need to share with others?

Let us pray. Lord God, you are greater than all gods. Thank you for the people you have placed around me, to help me and to give me wise counsel. Give me humility to accept advice and to share my burdens. And help me to be a person who fears you, loves the truth, and hates greed. Amen.
