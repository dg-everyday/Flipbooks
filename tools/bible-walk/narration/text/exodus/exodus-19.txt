The Living Voice. Exodus, chapter 19. I Bare You on Eagles’ Wings.

A great mountain rises out of the desert. Around its base, a million people wait, washed and ready. On the third day, it begins. Thunder rolls. Lightning flashes. A thick cloud covers the mountain. Then a trumpet sounds, so loud and long that everyone in the camp trembles. The whole mountain is smoking, and shaking. God has come down.

In chapter eighteen, Moses’ father-in-law Jethro visited the camp. He rejoiced at all God had done, and said, now I know that the LORD is greater than all gods. Then he saw Moses judging the people alone, and told him to choose able men to share the work.

Now, in chapter nineteen, Israel comes to Mount Sinai, the mountain where God first spoke to Moses from the burning bush. God had promised, when you have brought the people out of Egypt, you shall serve God upon this mountain. That promise is now coming true. And God tells Israel why he rescued them.

In the third month after the children of Israel left Egypt, they come into the wilderness of Sinai, and camp there before the mount. They will stay here for about a year.

Moses goes up to God, and the LORD calls to him out of the mountain. Thus shalt thou say to the house of Jacob, and tell the children of Israel.

And God tells them what he has done.

Then God tells them what he wants them to be.

God says, these are the words which you shall speak unto the children of Israel.

Moses comes down and calls the elders of the people, and lays before them all these words which the LORD commanded him. And all the people answer together. 'All that the LORD hath spoken we will do.' Moses brings the words of the people back to the LORD.

The LORD says to Moses, lo, I come unto thee in a thick cloud, that the people may hear when I speak with thee, and believe thee for ever.

Then God tells Moses to prepare the people. Go unto the people, and sanctify them today and the next day, and let them wash their clothes. Be ready for the third day. For on the third day the LORD will come down in the sight of all the people upon mount Sinai.

God also tells Moses to set bounds, limits, around the mountain. Take heed to yourselves, that you go not up into the mount, or touch the border of it. Whoever touches the mount shall surely be put to death. Only when the trumpet sounds long may they come near the mountain.

Moses goes down from the mount to the people, and sanctifies them, and they wash their clothes.

And on the third day, as the day begins, there are thunders and lightnings, and a thick cloud upon the mount, 'and the voice of the trumpet exceeding loud; so that all the people that was in the camp trembled.'

Moses brings the people out of the camp to meet with God, and they stand at the foot of the mountain. Mount Sinai is altogether on a smoke, because the LORD descends upon it in fire. The smoke goes up like the smoke of a furnace, and the whole mount quakes greatly.

When the voice of the trumpet sounds long, and grows louder and louder, Moses speaks, and God answers him by a voice.

The LORD comes down upon mount Sinai, on the top of the mount. And the LORD calls Moses up to the top, and Moses goes up. Then God tells Moses to go down and warn the people again, so that they do not break through to gaze, and many of them die. Even the priests who come near must sanctify themselves.

Moses says, the people cannot come up to mount Sinai, for you charged us, set bounds about the mount, and sanctify it. The LORD says, away, get thee down, and come up, thou, and Aaron with thee. But let not the priests and the people break through.

So Moses goes down unto the people, and speaks unto them. And in the next chapter, God himself speaks.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 4. 'Ye have seen what I did unto the Egyptians, and how I bare you on eagles’ wings, and brought you unto myself.' What a beautiful picture. A mother eagle teaching her young to fly would swoop down beneath them and carry them on her wings when they fell. God had carried his people through the plagues, through the sea, through the wilderness. And notice where he carried them: unto myself. The goal of the exodus was not just a place. It was a person. God brought them to himself.

Verse 5. 'Now therefore, if ye will obey my voice indeed, and keep my covenant, then ye shall be a peculiar treasure unto me above all people: for all the earth is mine:' In the King James Bible, peculiar does not mean strange. It means a special personal possession, like a king’s private treasure. All the earth belongs to God. And yet he chose this people to be his own treasure. God rescued them first, and then called them to obey. Obedience was not the way to earn his love. It was the way to live in it.

Verse 6. 'And ye shall be unto me a kingdom of priests, and an holy nation. These are the words which thou shalt speak unto the children of Israel.' A priest stands between God and people, bringing people to God and God to people. Israel was called to be a whole nation of priests, showing the world what God is like. And they were to be holy, set apart for God. God’s people are rescued not just for their own sake, but so that they can bring God’s blessing to the world.

The apostle Peter wrote to believers in Jesus, 'But ye are a chosen generation, a royal priesthood, an holy nation, a peculiar people; that ye should shew forth the praises of him who hath called you out of darkness into his marvellous light'. And the letter to the Hebrews says that we have not come to a mountain that burned with fire, but to Jesus, the mediator of the new covenant.

Remember that God has carried you, as on eagles’ wings, and brought you to himself. You are his treasure. Today, think about how you can show the people around you what God is like.

Here is today’s question. God said, I bare you on eagles’ wings, and brought you unto myself. When have you felt God carrying you through a hard time?

Let us pray. Lord God, thank you for carrying me on eagles’ wings and bringing me to yourself. Thank you that I am your treasure. Help me to live as one of your holy people, a priest who shows the world what you are like. Teach me to obey your voice, not to earn your love, but because I am loved. Amen.
