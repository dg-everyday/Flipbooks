The Living Voice. Exodus, chapter 20. Thou Shalt Have No Other Gods Before Me.

The mountain is covered in smoke and fire. The trumpet has sounded. A million people stand at its foot, trembling. And then, from the top of the mountain, God himself speaks, in a voice that every one of them can hear. These are the most famous words of the Old Testament, the Ten Commandments.

In chapter nineteen, Israel came to Mount Sinai. God said, I bare you on eagles’ wings, and brought you unto myself. He called them to be his peculiar treasure, a kingdom of priests, and an holy nation. The people said, all that the LORD hath spoken we will do. And on the third day, the LORD came down upon the mountain in fire, smoke and thunder.

Now, in chapter twenty, the last step of our walk through these chapters, God speaks. The Ten Commandments show what it looks like to love God, and to love our neighbour. But listen to how God begins. Before a single command, he reminds his people of who he is, and what he has done.

God speaks all these words, saying.

The first four commandments are about our love for God.

The first commandment is about who comes first.

Second. You shall not make any graven image, or any likeness of anything in heaven above, or in the earth beneath, or in the water, to bow down to them or serve them. 'for I the LORD thy God am a jealous God'. God’s jealousy is not petty. It is the love of a husband who will not share his wife’s heart with another. And he shows mercy unto thousands of them that love him, and keep his commandments.

Third. 'Thou shalt not take the name of the LORD thy God in vain'. That means not using his name lightly, carelessly or falsely. God’s name stands for who he is.

Fourth. 'Remember the sabbath day, to keep it holy.' Six days you shall labour, and do all your work. But the seventh day is the sabbath of the LORD your God. In it you shall not do any work, you, your children, your servants, your animals, or the stranger among you. For in six days the LORD made heaven and earth, the sea, and all that is in them, and rested the seventh day. A people who had been slaves, working without rest, were now given a day to stop, and to remember that their lives were in God’s hands.

The next six commandments are about our love for other people.

Fifth. 'Honour thy father and thy mother: that thy days may be long upon the land which the LORD thy God giveth thee.' Paul later called this the first commandment with a promise.

Sixth. 'Thou shalt not kill.' Every human life is precious, because every person is made in the image of God.

Seventh. 'Thou shalt not commit adultery.' Marriage is to be kept faithful.

Eighth. 'Thou shalt not steal.'

Ninth. 'Thou shalt not bear false witness against thy neighbour.' Do not lie about other people.

Tenth. You shall not covet, or long to have, your neighbour’s house, or his wife, or his servants, or his ox, or his donkey, or anything that is your neighbour’s. This last commandment goes beyond our actions, into our hearts.

All the people see the thunderings, and the lightnings, and the noise of the trumpet, and the mountain smoking. When they see it, they move back, and stand far off. They say to Moses, speak thou with us, and we will hear. But let not God speak with us, lest we die.

Moses answers them.

The people stand far off, and Moses draws near unto the thick darkness where God is.

Then the LORD tells Moses to say to the children of Israel, you have seen that I have talked with you from heaven. You shall not make gods of silver or gold to set beside me. God tells them to make him a simple altar of earth, or of uncut stones. 'in all places where I record my name I will come unto thee, and I will bless thee.'

That is where our walk through Exodus pauses. The rest of the book tells how God gave Israel more of his laws, and how they built the tabernacle, so that God could dwell among them.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 2. 'I am the LORD thy God, which have brought thee out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of bondage.' Before God gave a single commandment, he reminded his people of his grace. I am your God. I have already rescued you. The commandments were not a ladder to climb up to God. They were a way of life for people God had already set free. We do not obey God to earn his love. We obey him because he has already loved us and saved us.

Verse 3. 'Thou shalt have no other gods before me.' This is the first commandment, and it comes first for a reason. Everything else flows from it. When God is first, everything else falls into its proper place. Other gods are not always statues. They can be money, success, pleasure, people, or anything we love and trust more than God. God asks for first place in our hearts, because he alone deserves it.

Verse 20. 'And Moses said unto the people, Fear not: for God is come to prove you, and that his fear may be before your faces, that ye sin not.' Fear not, and yet, let his fear be before you. Moses meant two different kinds of fear. Do not be terrified, as if God came to destroy you. But do have a deep respect and awe for God, which keeps you from sin. The right kind of fear of God is not running away from him. It is loving him too much to want to grieve him.

When Jesus was asked which commandment was the greatest, he said, 'Thou shalt love the Lord thy God with all thy heart, and with all thy soul, and with all thy mind.' And, 'Thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself. On these two commandments hang all the law and the prophets.' Paul wrote, 'love is the fulfilling of the law.'

Think about the first commandment. Is there anything in your life that has taken God’s place? Give him first place again today. And choose one of the commandments about loving others, and ask God to help you live it out this week.

Here is today’s question. We have walked through the first twenty chapters of Exodus. God said, I am the LORD thy God, which have brought thee out. How has this walk helped you understand what God has done for you, and how you can live for him?

Let us pray. Lord God, you are the LORD my God, who has brought me out of bondage. Thank you for rescuing me before you ever asked anything of me. Help me to love you with all my heart, and to love my neighbour as myself. Be first in my life, above everything else. Amen.

That is the end of our walk through the first twenty chapters of Exodus. Well walked. God brought his people out of Egypt, carried them on eagles’ wings, and brought them to himself. And he has done the same, through Jesus, for everyone who trusts in him.
