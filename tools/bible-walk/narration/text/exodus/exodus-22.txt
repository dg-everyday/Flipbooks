The Living Voice. Exodus, chapter 22. For I Am Gracious.

A poor farmer needs to borrow some money. He has nothing to offer as a promise that he will pay it back, except the cloak he wears. So he hands it over. The man who lends him the money could keep that cloak until the debt is paid. But as the sun begins to go down, there is a knock at the farmer’s door. It is the lender, holding out the cloak. You will need this to keep warm while you sleep, he says. God commanded that. Because God is gracious.

In chapter twenty one, God began to give Moses laws for daily life, called the judgments. A Hebrew servant was to go free in the seventh year. Human life was precious, and there was a place of refuge for someone who killed by accident. The law of an eye for an eye limited revenge, so that the punishment fitted the crime. And a master who injured his servant had to set him free.

Now, in chapter twenty two, the laws continue. Listen for how a thief must make things right. And listen for three kinds of people God watches over especially.

The chapter begins with stealing.

Notice that the thief did not just give back what he had stolen. He paid back more. If he stole a sheep and still had it, he gave back two. If he had already sold it or killed it, he had to give four sheep for one, or five oxen for one ox. An ox was especially valuable, because it pulled the plough.

This is called restitution. It means making things right, and more than right. That way, the victim was helped, and the thief learned how costly his wrong was.

Do you remember Zacchaeus, the tax collector who climbed a tree to see Jesus? He had cheated many people. But when Jesus came to his house, Zacchaeus stood up and said, if I have taken any thing from any man by false accusation, I restore him fourfold. He was following this very law, paying back four times over. And Jesus said, this day is salvation come to this house.

Then come laws about damage. 'If a man shall cause a field or vineyard to be eaten, and shall put in his beast, and shall feed in another man’s field; of the best of his own field, and of the best of his own vineyard, shall he make restitution.' If you let your animals eat your neighbour’s crops, you must pay him back with the best of your own. 'If fire break out, and catch in thorns' and burns up a neighbour’s grain, 'he that kindled the fire shall surely make restitution.'

Then laws about looking after other people’s things. If you looked after your neighbour’s money or animals, and they were stolen, the judges would decide whether you had been honest. If you borrowed something, and it was damaged, you had to make it good. Take care of what belongs to others, as if it were your own.

Then there are a few short laws about serious matters, about marriage, and about worshipping other gods. God’s people were to worship the LORD only.

And then the chapter turns to some of the most beautiful laws in the whole Bible. Laws about kindness.

'Ye shall not afflict any widow, or fatherless child. If thou afflict them in any wise, and they cry at all unto me, I will surely hear their cry'. Afflict means mistreat, take advantage of. Widows, women whose husbands had died, and fatherless children, often had no one to protect them, and no one to earn money for them. But God says, if anyone mistreats them, and they cry out to me, I will hear. God himself is their protector. And he warns that his anger will burn against anyone who harms them.

Then a law about lending. 'If thou lend money to any of my people that is poor by thee, thou shalt not be to him as an usurer, neither shalt thou lay upon him usury.' Usury means charging interest, making the poor person pay back much more than they borrowed. When a neighbour is poor and in need, God says, help him. Do not make money out of his trouble.

And then, the law about the cloak.

Then a few more laws. God’s people were to respect those in authority. They were to bring God the first of their harvest, and their firstborn, as a sign that everything belonged to him. 'And ye shall be holy men unto me'.

Can you see the heart of God in this chapter? He cares about honesty. He cares about responsibility. And most of all, he cares about the weak and the poor. The stranger, the widow, the orphan, the poor borrower. No one else noticed them. But God did. And he wanted his people to notice them too.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 1. 'If a man shall steal an ox, or a sheep, and kill it, or sell it; he shall restore five oxen for an ox, and four sheep for a sheep.' Saying sorry is important. But when we have taken something, sorry is not enough. We must put it right, and more. Restitution shows that we are truly sorry. If you have ever taken something that was not yours, or damaged something that belongs to someone else, the best thing to do is to give it back, and make it right.

Verse 21. 'Thou shalt neither vex a stranger, nor oppress him: for ye were strangers in the land of Egypt.' A stranger was a foreigner, someone from another land, living among God’s people. Vex means trouble, mistreat. God reminds Israel, you know what it is like to be foreigners. You were strangers in Egypt, and you were treated cruelly. So do not treat others that way. When we remember how God has been kind to us, it helps us to be kind to others.

Verse 27. 'For that is his covering only, it is his raiment for his skin: wherein shall he sleep? and it shall come to pass, when he crieth unto me, that I will hear; for I am gracious.' If a poor man gave his cloak as a pledge, a promise to repay, it had to be returned before he lay down to sleep. It was his only blanket. And notice why. For I am gracious. God’s laws are not cold and harsh. They come from his kind, generous heart. He thinks about whether a poor man will be cold. That is how much he cares.

Zacchaeus said, 'if I have taken any thing from any man by false accusation, I restore him fourfold.' And James wrote, 'Pure religion and undefiled before God and the Father is this, To visit the fatherless and widows in their affliction'.

Is there someone around you who is new, or lonely, or left out? A stranger in your class, or a widow in your neighbourhood? Show them kindness today, as God does. And if you have taken or damaged anything, make it right.

Here is today’s question. God reminds Israel, ye were strangers in the land of Egypt, so do not mistreat strangers. How can remembering God’s kindness to us help us to be kind to others?

Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that you are gracious. Thank you that you notice the stranger, the widow, the fatherless and the poor, and that you hear their cry. Help me to notice them too, and to be kind. Make me honest and careful with what belongs to others. And when I have done wrong, help me to make it right. Amen.
