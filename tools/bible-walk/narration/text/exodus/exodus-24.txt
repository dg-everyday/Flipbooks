The Living Voice. Exodus, chapter 24. Behold the Blood of the Covenant.

Two kings stand facing each other. Between them lies a written agreement. Each king reads the promises aloud. Then they seal the agreement in a way that everyone will remember. In ancient times, such agreements were often sealed with a sacrifice. At Mount Sinai, God makes an agreement with his people, a covenant. And it is sealed with blood.

In chapter twenty three, the laws of the book of the covenant came to an end. God said, thou shalt not follow a multitude to do evil. Help even your enemy, and bring back his ox or his donkey when it goes astray. Every seventh year the land would rest, so the poor could eat. And God promised, behold, I send an Angel before thee, to keep thee in the way.

Now, in chapter twenty four, the people say yes to God’s covenant. Listen for the promise they make, twice. And listen for what the leaders of Israel see on the mountain.

God tells Moses to come up the mountain, with Aaron, and Aaron’s two oldest sons, Nadab and Abihu, and seventy of the elders, the leaders of Israel. They are to worship from a distance. Only Moses may come near.

But first, Moses goes down to the people, and tells them everything God has said, all the words and all the laws. And the people answer together. 'All the words which the LORD hath said will we do.' With one voice. The whole nation agrees.

Then Moses writes down all the words of the LORD. And he builds an altar at the foot of the mountain, with twelve stone pillars around it, one for each of the twelve tribes of Israel.

Young men offer sacrifices on the altar. Burnt offerings, which were completely burned up, a sign of giving everything to God. And peace offerings, which were shared in a meal, a sign of friendship with God.

Then Moses does something solemn. He takes the blood of the sacrifices, and pours half of it into bowls. The other half he sprinkles on the altar. The altar stands for God.

Then he reads the book of the covenant aloud to the people.

And then, with the other half of the blood, Moses sprinkles the people.

What a strange and serious ceremony! Half of the blood on the altar, standing for God. Half on the people. God and his people are joined together by one covenant, sealed with one blood. Blood stands for life.

Then something amazing happens. Moses, Aaron, Nadab, Abihu and the seventy elders go up the mountain. 'And they saw the God of Israel: and there was under his feet as it were a paved work of a sapphire stone, and as it were the body of heaven in his clearness.'

They saw the God of Israel! The Bible does not describe God himself. It only tells us what was under his feet. A pavement like sapphire, a brilliant blue jewel, as clear and bright as the sky itself.

And here is something wonderful. 'And upon the nobles of the children of Israel he laid not his hand: also they saw God, and did eat and drink.' Sinful people standing in God’s presence might expect to be destroyed. But God did not harm them. Instead, they ate and drank, a covenant meal, in the presence of God.

Then God calls Moses higher. 'Come up to me into the mount, and be there: and I will give thee tables of stone, and a law, and commandments which I have written; that thou mayest teach them.' Tables of stone are flat slabs of stone, like tablets. God himself would write his commandments on them.

So Moses sets off up the mountain, with his young helper, Joshua. Joshua would one day lead Israel into the promised land. Moses tells the elders, wait here for us. Aaron and Hur are with you. If anyone has a problem, go to them. We will hear more about Aaron later, and it will not be good news.

'And Moses went up into the mount, and a cloud covered the mount. And the glory of the LORD abode upon mount Sinai, and the cloud covered it six days: and the seventh day he called unto Moses out of the midst of the cloud.' For six days, Moses waited. Then, on the seventh day, God called him.

And then.

'And Moses went into the midst of the cloud, and gat him up into the mount: and Moses was in the mount forty days and forty nights.' Moses walked right into the fire and the cloud. And there he stayed, with God, for forty days. The next chapters tell us what God said.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 7. 'And he took the book of the covenant, and read in the audience of the people: and they said, All that the LORD hath said will we do, and be obedient.' In the audience of the people means so that everyone could hear. The people made their promise a second time. All that the LORD hath said will we do. They meant it. But as we will see, in only a few weeks, they would break it. It is easy to make promises to God. It is much harder to keep them. We all need God’s help to obey.

Verse 8. 'And Moses took the blood, and sprinkled it on the people, and said, Behold the blood of the covenant, which the LORD hath made with you concerning all these words.' Many years later, just before he died, Jesus took a cup of wine at supper with his disciples. And he said, this is my blood of the new testament, which is shed for many for the remission of sins. Testament means covenant. Jesus was saying, a new covenant is being made, and it will be sealed, not with the blood of animals, but with my own blood.

Verse 17. 'And the sight of the glory of the LORD was like devouring fire on the top of the mount in the eyes of the children of Israel.' From the camp below, the people looked up and saw the mountaintop blazing, as if it were on fire. Devouring means consuming, eating everything up. God’s glory is beautiful, but it is also awesome and holy. And Moses walked right into it. Because God had called him. We can only come close to a holy God when he invites us in.

Jesus said, 'For this is my blood of the new testament, which is shed for many for the remission of sins.' And the letter to the Hebrews says, 'For our God is a consuming fire.'

The people promised, all that the LORD hath said will we do. Is there something God has asked you to do, in his Word, that you have been putting off? Ask him to help you obey it today. And thank Jesus for the new covenant, sealed with his own blood.

Here is today’s question. The elders saw God, and ate and drank in his presence, and God did not harm them. What does that tell you about the kind of relationship God wants with his people?

Let us pray. Lord God, thank you for making a covenant with your people, and for keeping your promises. I want to say, all that you have said I will do. But I know I need your help. Thank you for Jesus, whose blood seals a new covenant, for the forgiveness of sins. Help me to come close to you, because you have invited me. Amen.
