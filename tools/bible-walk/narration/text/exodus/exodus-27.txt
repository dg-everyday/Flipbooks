The Living Voice. Exodus, chapter 27. To Cause the Lamp to Burn Always.

Imagine walking towards the tabernacle in the desert. All around it is a tall white fence of linen curtains, higher than your head. You walk all the way round, looking for a way in. On three sides, there is no opening at all. Then, on the east side, you find it. One wide gate, hung with curtains of blue, purple and scarlet. It is the only way in. And the first thing you see inside is a great altar, with smoke rising from it.

In chapter twenty six, God described the tent of the tabernacle. Its ten beautiful curtains of linen were embroidered with cherubim, and joined with golden clasps, so that it shall be one tabernacle. Its walls were boards covered with gold, standing on silver. And a veil divided the holy place from the most holy place, where the ark of the covenant would stand.

Now, in chapter twenty seven, God describes what stood outside the tent. Listen for the altar, the first thing a worshipper would come to. And listen for the gate, and how many there were.

Chapter twenty seven describes three things. An altar, a courtyard, and oil for the lamp.

First, the altar.

It was made of acacia wood, covered with brass, which means bronze. It was a large square box, five cubits long and five cubits wide, and three cubits high. That is about as tall as a child’s shoulder, and wider than a bed. It was hollow inside, with a bronze grate, like a metal net, halfway down, where the fire burned.

'And thou shalt make the horns of it upon the four corners thereof'. On each corner stood a horn, pointing upwards. Horns were a sign of strength. Later in Israel’s history, a person in danger would run to the altar, and take hold of its horns, asking for mercy.

It had bronze tools, and poles to carry it.

This was the altar of burnt offering. It was the first thing a worshipper came to when they entered the courtyard. Before anyone could come near to God, a sacrifice had to be made. An animal would die in their place. This taught God’s people a very important lesson. Sin is serious. It brings death. And the only way to come to a holy God is through a sacrifice.

All of these sacrifices pointed forward to one great sacrifice. When John the Baptist saw Jesus coming towards him, he said, behold the Lamb of God, which taketh away the sin of the world. Jesus was the true sacrifice, offered once, for all. Because of him, there is no more need for an altar of burnt offering.

Second, the courtyard. Around the tabernacle and the altar was a large open courtyard, called the court. It was a hundred cubits long and fifty cubits wide. That is about as long as a sports field, and half as wide. Around it stood a fence of fine white linen curtains, five cubits high, taller than a man. The curtains hung from pillars standing on bronze bases, with hooks and bands of silver.

The white linen stood for purity, for holiness. It told everyone that God is holy.

And there was only one way in.

The gate was on the east side, facing the rising sun. It was wide, twenty cubits, so many people could come in. And it was beautiful, made in the same colours as the veil and the door of the tent. Only one gate. If you wanted to come to God, you had to come through that one gate.

So the way to God in the tabernacle went like this. In through the one gate. Then to the bronze altar, where a sacrifice was made. Then, as we will hear later, to a bronze basin for washing. Then into the holy place, with the lampstand, the table and the altar of incense. And finally, behind the veil, to the most holy place, where God dwelt above the mercy seat.

Third, the oil.

'In the tabernacle of the congregation without the vail, which is before the testimony, Aaron and his sons shall order it from evening to morning before the LORD'. Aaron and his sons, the priests, were to keep the lamps of the golden lampstand burning, through all the hours of darkness.

Inside the tabernacle, there were no windows. The only light in the holy place came from the golden lampstand.

Jesus said, I am the light of the world. And he also said to his followers, ye are the light of the world. Let your light so shine before men, that they may see your good works, and glorify your Father which is in heaven. Like the lamps in the tabernacle, we are meant to keep shining, and never let our light go out.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 1. 'And thou shalt make an altar of shittim wood, five cubits long, and five cubits broad; the altar shall be foursquare: and the height thereof shall be three cubits.' Foursquare means square, the same on every side. This altar was where the blood of sacrifices was shed, day after day. It was the place of death, and also the place of forgiveness. It reminds us that forgiveness is costly. It cost the life of an animal then. And it cost the life of God’s own Son, Jesus, on the cross.

Verse 16. 'And for the gate of the court shall be an hanging of twenty cubits, of blue, and purple, and scarlet, and fine twined linen, wrought with needlework'. There was only one gate. Not many ways in, but one. Jesus said, I am the door: by me if any man enter in, he shall be saved. And he said, I am the way, the truth, and the life: no man cometh unto the Father, but by me. But notice that the gate was wide. Twenty cubits wide. Anyone could come in, through that one gate.

Verse 20. 'And thou shalt command the children of Israel, that they bring thee pure oil olive beaten for the light, to cause the lamp to burn always.' Pure olive oil, beaten, crushed by hand, gave the clearest, brightest light, with very little smoke. The people had to keep bringing it, so the lamp would never go out. In the Bible, oil is often a picture of the Holy Spirit. It is God’s Spirit who keeps our light burning.

Jesus said, 'I am the door: by me if any man enter in, he shall be saved'. And he said, 'Let your light so shine before men, that they may see your good works, and glorify your Father which is in heaven.'

Let your light shine today. Think of one good thing you can do for someone, to show them God’s love. And thank Jesus that he is the one door, the one way, to God, and that the door is open wide for anyone to come in.

Here is today’s question. The courtyard of the tabernacle had only one gate, but it was wide. What do you think that teaches us about the way to God?

Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that there is a way to come to you. Thank you for Jesus, the Lamb of God, the one sacrifice for sin. Thank you that he is the door, and that the door is open wide. Fill me with your Spirit, like oil in the lamp, so that my light will keep shining for you. Amen.
