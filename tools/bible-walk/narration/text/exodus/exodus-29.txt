The Living Voice. Exodus, chapter 29. I Will Dwell Among the Children of Israel.

A king has a son. On the day the son becomes a prince, there is a special ceremony. He is washed, and dressed in royal robes. Oil is poured on his head. And from that day on, everyone knows he belongs to the king, set apart for royal service. In Exodus chapter twenty nine, God describes a ceremony like that, to set apart Aaron and his sons as priests.

In chapter twenty eight, God gave Aaron the high priest holy garments for glory and for beauty. On his shoulders, two onyx stones with the names of the twelve tribes. Over his heart, a breastplate with twelve precious stones, one for each tribe. Golden bells rang on the hem of his blue robe. And on his forehead, a golden plate said, HOLINESS TO THE LORD.

Now, in chapter twenty nine, the priests are washed, dressed, anointed and set apart. Listen for what Aaron and his sons do with their hands. And listen for three places where the blood is put on them.

God tells Moses how to hallow Aaron and his sons. To hallow means to make holy, to set apart for God.

Moses was to bring a young bull, two rams without any defect, and a basket of bread made without yeast. Only the best was given to God.

The first step was washing. 'And Aaron and his sons thou shalt bring unto the door of the tabernacle of the congregation, and shalt wash them with water.' Before anyone could serve a holy God, they needed to be clean.

The second step was dressing. Moses was to put on Aaron the coat, the blue robe, the ephod, and the breastplate, and the turban with the holy crown, the golden plate. Then Aaron’s sons were dressed in their coats, sashes and caps.

The third step was anointing. 'Then shalt thou take the anointing oil, and pour it upon his head, and anoint him.' Do you remember Psalm one hundred and thirty three? It is like the precious ointment upon the head, that ran down upon the beard, even Aaron’s beard. This is the moment that psalm remembers.

Then came the sacrifices. And there were three.

First, the bull, as a sin offering.

Moses killed the bull, put some of its blood on the horns of the altar, and poured the rest at the bottom of the altar. The priests were not perfect. Before they could stand between God and the people, their own sins had to be dealt with.

Second, a ram as a burnt offering. Again, Aaron and his sons laid their hands on its head. Then the whole ram was burned on the altar. 'it is a sweet savour, an offering made by fire unto the LORD.' A sweet savour means a pleasing smell. It was a picture of giving yourself completely to God.

Third, the ram of consecration. And here comes a very unusual ceremony.

Then Moses was to sprinkle blood and anointing oil on Aaron and his sons, and on their garments. They were now holy, set apart for God.

Parts of the ram, and some of the bread, were placed in the priests’ hands, and waved before the LORD, as an offering. Then the priests ate a holy meal of the rest.

This ceremony lasted seven days.

Then God gives a command that would go on for ever. 'Now this is that which thou shalt offer upon the altar; two lambs of the first year day by day continually.' Two young lambs, every single day. 'The one lamb thou shalt offer in the morning; and the other lamb thou shalt offer at even'. One at the beginning of the day, and one at the end. Every day, for generations, the smoke would rise from the altar. It was a constant reminder that God’s people needed forgiveness, and that God had provided a way.

Do you remember what John the Baptist said when he saw Jesus? Behold the Lamb of God, which taketh away the sin of the world. All those lambs, offered every day, year after year, pointed to the one Lamb who would take away sin once and for all.

And then God tells Moses the purpose behind all of it. 'This shall be a continual burnt offering throughout your generations at the door of the tabernacle of the congregation before the LORD: where I will meet you, to speak there unto thee. And there I will meet with the children of Israel, and the tabernacle shall be sanctified by my glory.'

The altar, the priests, the sacrifices, the garments, all of it was so that God could meet with his people.

And the chapter ends with a wonderful promise.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 10. 'And thou shalt cause a bullock to be brought before the tabernacle of the congregation: and Aaron and his sons shall put their hands upon the head of the bullock.' Laying their hands on the animal’s head was a sign. It was as if they were saying, this animal stands in my place. My sin is passed onto it. It will die instead of me. That is what we call a substitute. And it is a picture of what Jesus did for us. He took our place, and died the death we deserved.

Verse 20. 'Then shalt thou kill the ram, and take of his blood, and put it upon the tip of the right ear of Aaron, and upon the tip of the right ear of his sons, and upon the thumb of their right hand, and upon the great toe of their right foot'. Why the ear, the thumb and the toe? The ear, to listen to God. The hand, to work for God. The foot, to walk in God’s ways. Everything about the priests, what they heard, what they did, and where they went, was to belong to God. Paul wrote that we too should give ourselves to God, as a living sacrifice.

Verse 45. 'And I will dwell among the children of Israel, and will be their God.' This is the heart of the whole book of Exodus. God rescued his people from Egypt, not just to set them free, but so that he could live with them, and be their God. And the very last chapter of the Bible ends with the same promise. Behold, the tabernacle of God is with men, and he will dwell with them, and they shall be his people.

John the Baptist said, 'Behold the Lamb of God, which taketh away the sin of the world.' And Paul wrote, 'present your bodies a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable unto God, which is your reasonable service.'

Think about your ears, your hands and your feet. Ask God to help you use them for him today. Listen to what is good. Do what is kind. And go where God would want you to go.

Here is today’s question. Blood was put on the priests’ right ear, right thumb and right big toe. What might it look like for you to give your ears, your hands and your feet to God?

Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that you want to dwell among your people, and be our God. Thank you for Jesus, the Lamb of God, who took my place and died for my sin. I give myself to you. Let my ears listen to you, my hands work for you, and my feet walk in your ways. Make me holy, set apart for you. Amen.
