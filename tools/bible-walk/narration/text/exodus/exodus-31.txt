The Living Voice. Exodus, chapter 31. Written with the Finger of God.

A boy loves to make things. He carves little animals out of wood. He builds model boats. He draws beautiful pictures. Sometimes people say, that is nice, but it is not as important as being clever at school. But in Exodus chapter thirty one, God picks out a craftsman, a man who works with his hands, and fills him with his Holy Spirit. Because God loves beautiful, skilful work.

In chapter thirty, God described a golden altar of incense, where sweet smoke would rise like prayer. Every man gave the same half shekel offering, for the rich shall not give more, and the poor shall not give less. A bronze basin of water stood ready for the priests to wash their hands and feet. And God gave the recipes for the holy anointing oil and the holy incense.

Now, in chapter thirty one, we come to the end of Moses’ forty days on the mountain. Listen for the man God fills with his Spirit. And listen for what God gives Moses to carry down the mountain.

For seven chapters, God has been describing the tabernacle. The ark, the mercy seat, the table, the lampstand, the curtains, the boards, the veil, the altars, the courtyard, the priests’ garments, the oil, the incense. It was a huge and complicated project. Who could possibly make all of this?

God already had the answer.

Bezaleel was the grandson of Hur. Do you remember Hur? When Israel fought the Amalekites, Aaron and Hur held up Moses’ hands on the hilltop until the battle was won. And Hur was one of the two leaders left in charge while Moses was on the mountain. Bezaleel came from a faithful family, from the tribe of Judah.

I have called by name, God says. God knew Bezaleel personally. He had chosen him for this very job.

And then.

This is one of the first times in the whole Bible that anyone is said to be filled with the Spirit of God. And it is not a prophet, or a priest, or a king. It is a craftsman. 'To devise cunning works, to work in gold, and in silver, and in brass, And in cutting of stones, to set them, and in carving of timber, to work in all manner of workmanship.' Devise cunning works means design clever, skilful things. Bezaleel could design, and work with metal, and cut precious stones, and carve wood. And every one of those skills was a gift from God’s Spirit.

But Bezaleel would not work alone.

Then God lists everything they would make. The tabernacle, the ark, the mercy seat, the table, the lampstand, the altar of incense, the altar of burnt offering, the laver, the priests’ garments, the oil and the incense. 'according to all that I have commanded thee shall they do.'

Then God speaks about the sabbath. 'Verily my sabbaths ye shall keep: for it is a sign between me and you throughout your generations; that ye may know that I am the LORD that doth sanctify you.' The sabbath was the seventh day, a day of rest. It was a special sign of the covenant between God and Israel. Even the building of the tabernacle was to stop on the sabbath.

God gave Israel very serious warnings about breaking the sabbath. And he reminds them why it matters. 'It is a sign between me and the children of Israel for ever: for in six days the LORD made heaven and earth, and on the seventh day he rested, and was refreshed.' God did not rest because he was tired. He rested because his work was finished, and it was good. And he gave his people rest too, to remember that they were not slaves any more. Slaves in Egypt never had a day off. God’s people did.

Christians have understood the sabbath in different ways. But all agree that God made us to need rest, and that rest is a gift from him. And Jesus said, come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest.

And then the chapter ends with one of the most amazing verses in the whole book.

For forty days, Moses had been on the mountain, in the cloud of God’s glory. Now he would carry down two stone tablets, with the Ten Commandments written on them, not by a scribe, not by Moses, but by God himself.

But while Moses had been on the mountain, something terrible had been happening down in the camp. And that is where our walk goes next.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 3. 'And I have filled him with the spirit of God, in wisdom, and in understanding, and in knowledge, and in all manner of workmanship'. Workmanship means skill in making things. God’s Spirit does not only help people to preach and pray. He gives skill to artists, builders, designers, musicians, and craftsmen too. Whatever gifts you have, whether you are good with your hands, or with numbers, or with music, or with people, they come from God. And you can use them for him.

Verse 6. 'And I, behold, I have given with him Aholiab, the son of Ahisamach, of the tribe of Dan: and in the hearts of all that are wise hearted I have put wisdom, that they may make all that I have commanded thee'. Aholiab came from the tribe of Dan, one of the smallest tribes. Bezaleel came from Judah, one of the largest. God brought them together, big tribe and small, to work side by side. And many others helped too. God’s work is never meant to be done alone. He gives different gifts to different people, so that we need each other.

Verse 18. 'And he gave unto Moses, when he had made an end of communing with him upon mount Sinai, two tables of testimony, tables of stone, written with the finger of God.' The finger of God. God himself wrote the Ten Commandments. That is how important they were. And Paul wrote that God now writes his law, not on tables of stone, but on our hearts, by his Spirit. When we trust Jesus, God changes us from the inside, so that we want to obey him.

Jesus said, 'Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest.' And Paul wrote, 'written not with ink, but with the Spirit of the living God; not in tables of stone, but in fleshy tables of the heart.'

Think about the gifts and skills God has given you. Thank him for them, and use one of them today to serve him or help someone else. And make time to rest, and to thank God that rest is his gift.

Here is today’s question. The first person in the Bible described as filled with the spirit of God was a craftsman. What does that tell you about the gifts God gives, and how he can use them?

Let us pray. Lord God, thank you for every gift and skill you have given me. Fill me with your Spirit, as you filled Bezaleel, so that I can use my gifts for you. Help me to work well with others. Thank you for the gift of rest. And write your law on my heart, so that I will love to obey you. Amen.
