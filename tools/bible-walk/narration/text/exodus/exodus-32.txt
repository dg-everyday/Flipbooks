The Living Voice. Exodus, chapter 32. Who Is on the LORD’S Side?.

A crowd gathers in the camp, restless and impatient. Moses has been gone for weeks. Has he died up there on the mountain? Where is the God who brought them out of Egypt? We cannot wait any longer, someone shouts. Make us a god we can see! And so, at the foot of the very mountain where God spoke the Ten Commandments, the people begin to collect gold.

In chapter thirty one, God filled Bezaleel the craftsman with his Spirit, to make the tabernacle and everything in it. He gave Israel the sabbath as a sign of his covenant. And at the end of forty days on the mountain, God gave Moses two tables of stone, written with the finger of God.

Now, in chapter thirty two, while Moses is still on the mountain, the people break the covenant they promised to keep. Listen for Aaron’s excuse. And listen for Moses’ astonishing prayer.

The people had promised, twice, all that the LORD hath said will we do. But now, Moses had been gone a long time. And they grew impatient.

They went to Aaron, Moses’ brother, who had been left in charge. 'Up, make us gods, which shall go before us; for as for this Moses, the man that brought us up out of the land of Egypt, we wot not what is become of him.' We wot not means we do not know.

And Aaron did not say no. He told them to bring their gold earrings. And Aaron melted it down, and shaped it into a calf.

Then Aaron built an altar in front of the calf, and announced, 'To morrow is a feast to the LORD.' He used God’s own name. As if they could worship the LORD and the calf at the same time. They offered sacrifices, 'and the people sat down to eat and to drink, and rose up to play.' A wild party around a golden idol.

Up on the mountain, God saw everything. 'Go, get thee down; for thy people, which thou broughtest out of the land of Egypt, have corrupted themselves'.

'I have seen this people, and, behold, it is a stiffnecked people'. Stiffnecked means stubborn. God said, let me alone, and I will destroy them, and make a great nation from you, Moses, instead.

What would Moses do? He could have become the father of a great nation. Instead, he pleaded for the people. Why should the Egyptians say that you brought them out only to destroy them? Remember your promises to Abraham, Isaac and Israel. 'And the LORD repented of the evil which he thought to do unto his people.' God turned from his anger, because Moses prayed.

Then Moses went down the mountain, carrying the two tables of stone.

Joshua, waiting partway down, heard the noise from the camp. That sounds like war, he said. No, said Moses, it is the sound of singing.

And when Moses came near, and saw the calf and the dancing, his anger burned. He threw the tables of stone to the ground, and they shattered at the foot of the mountain.

Moses took the calf, burned it, ground it to powder, scattered it on the water, and made the people drink it. Their precious god was nothing but dust.

Then Moses turned to Aaron. What did these people do to you, that you led them into such a great sin? And Aaron made excuses. You know these people, they are set on mischief. They asked me to make them gods. 'So they gave it me: then I cast it into the fire, and there came out this calf.' As if the calf had jumped out of the fire all by itself!

Then Moses stood at the gate of the camp, and called out.

The men of the tribe of Levi came to Moses, and God used them to bring his judgment on the rebellion. That day, about three thousand people died. It was a terrible day, showing how serious it is to turn away from the living God.

The next day, Moses said to the people, 'Ye have sinned a great sin: and now I will go up unto the LORD; peradventure I shall make an atonement for your sin.' Peradventure means perhaps. Moses went back up the mountain, to plead for them again.

And then Moses prayed one of the most amazing prayers in the Bible.

God replied, whoever has sinned against me, him will I blot out of my book. Moses could not take the people’s place. But God told him to lead them on, and promised, 'behold, mine Angel shall go before thee'.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 4. 'And he received them at their hand, and fashioned it with a graving tool, after he had made it a molten calf: and they said, These be thy gods, O Israel, which brought thee up out of the land of Egypt.' A molten calf means a calf made of melted metal. What a terrible thing to say! A lump of gold, made a few hours ago, had not brought anyone out of Egypt. God had. Idols are not just statues. An idol is anything we put in God’s place, anything we trust or love more than him. We must be careful not to make golden calves of our own.

Verse 26. 'Then Moses stood in the gate of the camp, and said, Who is on the LORD’S side? let him come unto me. And all the sons of Levi gathered themselves together unto him.' Moses drew a line. Everyone had to choose. Are you with the LORD, or with the calf? There was no middle way. The same question comes to each of us. Will we stand with God, even when the crowd is going the other way? Choosing God’s side takes courage. But it is always the right side.

Verse 32. 'Yet now, if thou wilt forgive their sin--; and if not, blot me, I pray thee, out of thy book which thou hast written.' Moses loved the people so much that he was willing to be cut off himself, if only they could be forgiven. God’s book is the record of those who belong to him. Moses could not take their place. But many years later, Jesus did. He took our sin on himself, and died in our place, so that our names could be written in God’s book for ever.

John wrote, 'Little children, keep yourselves from idols.' And Paul wrote, 'Who gave himself for our sins, that he might deliver us from this present evil world'.

Is there anything in your life that you love or trust more than God? Ask him to show you, and to help you put him first again. And when you have done wrong, do not make excuses as Aaron did. Own up, and ask God to forgive you.

Here is today’s question. Aaron said, I cast it into the fire, and there came out this calf. Why do you think people make excuses instead of owning up to what they have done?

Let us pray. Lord God, you alone are God, and you alone brought your people out of Egypt. Keep me from putting anything in your place. When the crowd turns away from you, help me to stand on your side. Thank you for Moses, who prayed for his people. And thank you for Jesus, who gave himself for me, so that my name could be written in your book. Amen.
