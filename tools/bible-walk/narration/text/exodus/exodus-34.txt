The Living Voice. Exodus, chapter 34. The LORD, Merciful and Gracious.

A boy has broken his father’s favourite thing. He has disobeyed, and lied, and hurt his father deeply. He expects to be sent away. But instead, his father comes to him, puts an arm around his shoulders, and says, let us start again. His love is bigger than the boy’s wrong. Exodus chapter thirty four is the story of a new beginning like that.

In chapter thirty three, God said he would not go up in the midst of the people, because they were stiffnecked. But in the tent of meeting, the LORD spake unto Moses face to face, as a man speaketh unto his friend. God promised, my presence shall go with thee, and I will give thee rest. And when Moses asked, shew me thy glory, God hid him in a clift of the rock, and passed by.

Now, in chapter thirty four, God does what he promised. Listen for the words God uses to describe himself. And listen for what happens to Moses’ face.

God said to Moses, 'Hew thee two tables of stone like unto the first: and I will write upon these tables the words that were in the first tables, which thou brakest.' Hew means cut out of the rock. The first tables had been broken, just as the people had broken the covenant. But God was willing to start again. He would write his words a second time.

What a wonderful God! God gives second chances.

So Moses cut two new tables of stone, and went up Mount Sinai alone, as God commanded, carrying them in his hands.

'And the LORD descended in the cloud, and stood with him there, and proclaimed the name of the LORD.' And as God passed by, he did not describe his power, or his size, or his strength. He described his heart.

And God went on. 'Keeping mercy for thousands, forgiving iniquity and transgression and sin, and that will by no means clear the guilty'. God forgives sin, every kind of sin. But he will not pretend that sin does not matter. He is merciful, and he is just. God also warned that the effects of sin can pass down through families. When parents turn away from God, their children and grandchildren often suffer for it. But notice the difference. Sin’s effects reach to the third and fourth generation. God’s mercy reaches to thousands.

How could God forgive sin, and still be just? The full answer would only come many centuries later, at the cross. There, Jesus took the punishment for sin on himself, so that God could forgive us and still be completely just.

'And Moses made haste, and bowed his head toward the earth, and worshipped.' When Moses heard God’s name, all he could do was bow down and worship.

And then Moses prayed again.

And God answered with a promise. 'Behold, I make a covenant: before all thy people I will do marvels, such as have not been done in all the earth, nor in any nation'. The covenant that had been broken was renewed.

Then God repeated some of the most important laws. Do not make any covenant with the people of the land you are going to. Break down their altars, and smash their idols. 'For thou shalt worship no other god: for the LORD, whose name is Jealous, is a jealous God'. God’s jealousy is not like ours, petty and selfish. It is like the love of a faithful husband for his wife. He loves his people so much that he will not share them with idols that would harm them.

'Thou shalt make thee no molten gods.' No more golden calves.

Then God told Moses to write down these words. 'And he was there with the LORD forty days and forty nights; he did neither eat bread, nor drink water. And he wrote upon the tables the words of the covenant, the ten commandments.'

And then something remarkable happened, as Moses came down the mountain.

When Aaron and the people saw Moses’ face shining, they were afraid to come near him. But Moses called them, and they came, and he told them everything God had said. When he finished speaking, he put a veil over his face. And whenever he went in to speak with the LORD, he took the veil off, until he came out again.

Moses had been in the presence of God, and some of God’s glory rubbed off on him. Paul wrote about this in his letter to the Corinthians. He said that when we look at the glory of the Lord, like looking in a mirror, we are changed into the same image, from glory to glory. The more time we spend with God, the more we become like him.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 6. 'And the LORD passed by before him, and proclaimed, The LORD, The LORD God, merciful and gracious, longsuffering, and abundant in goodness and truth'. This is how God describes himself. Merciful, full of tender compassion. Gracious, kind to people who do not deserve it. Longsuffering, patient, slow to get angry. Abundant in goodness and truth, overflowing with faithful love and faithfulness. The psalmists repeat these words again and again. We heard them in Psalm one hundred and three, and Psalm one hundred and forty five. This is who God is.

Verse 9. 'And he said, If now I have found grace in thy sight, O Lord, let my Lord, I pray thee, go among us; for it is a stiffnecked people; and pardon our iniquity and our sin, and take us for thine inheritance.' Notice what Moses says. Go among us, for it is a stiffnecked people. Not, go with us because we are good. Go with us because we are stubborn, and we need you. And notice the word us and our. Moses does not separate himself from the people. Pardon our sin. Take us for your inheritance, your own special possession.

Verse 29. 'And it came to pass, when Moses came down from mount Sinai with the two tables of testimony in Moses’ hand, when he came down from the mount, that Moses wist not that the skin of his face shone while he talked with him.' Wist not means did not know. Moses’ face was shining with God’s glory, and he did not even realise it. He was not trying to look holy. He had simply been with God. When we spend time with God, others may notice something different about us, even when we do not notice it ourselves.

Paul wrote, 'But we all, with open face beholding as in a glass the glory of the Lord, are changed into the same image from glory to glory'. And John wrote, 'And of his fulness have all we received, and grace for grace.'

Read God’s description of himself again: merciful and gracious, longsuffering, and abundant in goodness and truth. Thank him for each of these. And spend time with him today, so that some of his glory can shine through you.

Here is today’s question. After the golden calf, God gave his people a second chance, and wrote the commandments again. What does that tell you about God, and how might it help you when you have failed?

Let us pray. The LORD, the LORD God, merciful and gracious, longsuffering, and abundant in goodness and truth. Thank you, Lord, that you forgive iniquity and transgression and sin. Thank you for second chances. Go among us, because we need you. And as I spend time with you, change me, from glory to glory, to be more like you. Amen.
