The Living Voice. Exodus, chapter 35. Every One Whose Heart Stirred Him Up.

A neighbourhood has been hit by a flood. Homes are damaged, and families have lost everything. A call goes out for help. And then something wonderful happens. People come from everywhere. One brings blankets. One brings food. One brings tools. A grandmother sits and knits warm clothes. A builder offers to fix roofs for free. Nobody is forced. They just want to help. In Exodus chapter thirty five, God’s people respond like that.

In chapter thirty four, God gave his people a second chance. Moses cut two new tables of stone, and God proclaimed his name: The LORD, The LORD God, merciful and gracious, longsuffering, and abundant in goodness and truth. The covenant was renewed. And when Moses came down from the mountain, the skin of his face shone, and he wist it not.

Now, in chapter thirty five, the work of building the tabernacle begins at last. Listen for the kind of heart God is looking for. And listen for who brings what.

For seven chapters, God gave Moses instructions for the tabernacle. Then came the sad story of the golden calf. Then the covenant was renewed. And now, at last, the people are ready to build.

Moses gathered all the people together. And the very first thing he spoke about was rest. 'Six days shall work be done, but on the seventh day there shall be to you an holy day, a sabbath of rest to the LORD'. Even the work of building God’s house was to stop on the sabbath. Before they began the great work, God reminded them that he comes first, even before work done for him.

Then Moses told them what God wanted.

'Take ye from among you an offering unto the LORD: whosoever is of a willing heart, let him bring it, an offering of the LORD; gold, and silver, and brass'. And Moses listed everything needed. Coloured threads and fine linen, goats’ hair, rams’ skins, acacia wood, oil, spices and precious stones.

'And every wise hearted among you shall come, and make all that the LORD hath commanded'. Not only materials were needed. Skilled hands were needed too. Anyone with a gift for making things was invited to come and use it.

Then the people went back to their tents. And what happened next is one of the happiest moments in the whole book of Exodus.

'And they came, both men and women, as many as were willing hearted, and brought bracelets, and earrings, and rings, and tablets, all jewels of gold'.

Do you remember chapter thirty two? Then, the people took off their golden earrings, and gave them to Aaron, to make a golden calf. Now, they take off their gold again. But this time, they give it to God, for his house. The same people, the same gold. But their hearts had changed.

Everyone brought what they had. Some brought coloured thread, or linen, or goats’ hair, or skins. Some brought silver and bronze. Some brought acacia wood.

And then.

The rulers, the leaders of the tribes, brought the precious stones for the high priest’s ephod and breastplate, and the spices and oil.

'The children of Israel brought a willing offering unto the LORD, every man and woman, whose heart made them willing to bring for all manner of work'. Rich and poor, men and women, leaders and ordinary people. Everyone had a part.

Then Moses introduced the two master craftsmen. 'See, the LORD hath called by name Bezaleel the son of Uri, the son of Hur, of the tribe of Judah; And he hath filled him with the spirit of God, in wisdom, in understanding, and in knowledge, and in all manner of workmanship'. Bezaleel could work in gold, silver and bronze, cut stones, and carve wood.

And God had given him another gift.

'Them hath he filled with wisdom of heart, to work all manner of work, of the engraver, and of the cunning workman, and of the embroiderer, in blue, and in purple, in scarlet, and in fine linen, and of the weaver'. Engravers, designers, embroiderers, weavers. Every kind of skill was needed.

The tabernacle was not built by one person. It was built by a whole people, each bringing what they had, each doing what they could. Paul wrote that God’s people are like a body, with many parts. The eye cannot say to the hand, I have no need of thee. Every part is needed.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 21. 'And they came, every one whose heart stirred him up, and every one whom his spirit made willing, and they brought the LORD’S offering to the work of the tabernacle of the congregation'. Their hearts were stirred up. Something moved inside them, and they wanted to give. That is the kind of giving God loves. Not because we have to. Not to show off. But because our hearts have been stirred by his love. And remember, these were the same people who had made the golden calf. God had forgiven them. And forgiven people love to give.

Verse 25. 'And all the women that were wise hearted did spin with their hands, and brought that which they had spun, both of blue, and of purple, and of scarlet, and of fine linen.' To spin means to twist fibres into thread. It was slow, patient work, done by hand. These women did not have gold to give, perhaps. But they had skilful hands and willing hearts. And their work became the beautiful curtains of God’s house. Whatever you can do, however small it seems, God can use it.

Verse 34. 'And he hath put in his heart that he may teach, both he, and Aholiab, the son of Ahisamach, of the tribe of Dan.' God did not only give Bezaleel and Aholiab skill. He gave them the desire and the ability to teach others. They did not keep their skills to themselves. They passed them on. That is a wonderful way to use any gift. When God has taught you something, share it with others.

Paul wrote, 'And the eye cannot say unto the hand, I have no need of thee'. And he told Timothy, 'commit thou to faithful men, who shall be able to teach others also.'

What has God given you that you could give back to him? It might be money, or time, or a skill. Give it today, willingly, from a stirred up heart. And if you know how to do something well, think of someone you could teach.

Here is today’s question. The same people who gave their gold for the golden calf now gave it for God’s house. What do you think changed their hearts?

Let us pray. Lord God, stir up my heart to give to you willingly. Thank you that you have given me gifts and skills. Help me to use them for you, and to teach others what I know. Thank you that you forgive, and that forgiven people love to give. Use me, together with your people, to build what you want built. Amen.
