The Living Voice. Exodus, chapter 36. Much More Than Enough.

Have you ever heard of a collection where the organisers had to say, please stop giving, we have too much? It hardly ever happens. Usually, people need to be reminded again and again to give. But in Exodus chapter thirty six, God’s people are so generous that Moses has to send a message through the whole camp. Stop! No more! We have more than enough!

In chapter thirty five, Moses called for an offering from everyone with a willing heart. And they came, every one whose heart stirred him up. Men and women brought gold, silver, bronze, linen and wood. The women who were wise hearted spun thread with their hands. And Bezaleel and Aholiab were filled with the spirit of God, and given hearts to teach.

Now, in chapter thirty six, the craftsmen begin to work. Listen for what the workmen tell Moses. And listen for how carefully they follow God’s instructions.

The chapter begins with the workers.

Moses gave them all the offerings the people had brought. And then something remarkable happened. 'And they brought yet unto him free offerings every morning.' Free offerings means gifts given freely. Day after day, the people kept coming, bringing more and more.

Finally, the wise men who were doing the work left what they were making, and came to Moses.

So Moses gave a command, and it was announced all through the camp. 'Let neither man nor woman make any more work for the offering of the sanctuary. So the people were restrained from bringing.' Restrained means held back. They had to be stopped from giving!

'For the stuff they had was sufficient for all the work to make it, and too much.' Sufficient means enough. Enough, and too much.

What a change from the grumbling people who complained about food and water in the wilderness! What a change from the people who made the golden calf! When God’s people truly understand how much he has given them, they become generous.

Many years later, the apostle Paul wrote about some poor believers in Macedonia. Even in deep poverty, they gave so generously that Paul was amazed. They gave beyond their power, he said, and first gave their own selves to the Lord. When we give ourselves to God first, giving everything else becomes easier.

Then the work began. And the rest of the chapter describes how the craftsmen made the tabernacle itself.

They made the ten curtains of fine linen, woven with blue, purple and scarlet, and embroidered with cherubim. They joined them in two sets of five, with fifty loops of blue.

They made the curtains of goats’ hair for the tent over the tabernacle, eleven of them, joined with fifty clasps of bronze. They made the covering of rams’ skins dyed red, and the outer covering of badgers’ skins.

They made the boards of acacia wood, standing up, each one ten cubits tall, with two pegs at the bottom, fitting into sockets of silver. Twenty boards for the south side, twenty for the north, six for the west, and two for the corners. They made the bars, and the middle bar to shoot through the boards from one end to the other. And they covered the boards and bars with gold.

They made the veil, of blue, purple, scarlet and fine linen, with cherubim embroidered on it, and hung it on four pillars covered with gold. And they made the hanging for the door of the tent.

If you listened carefully to chapter twenty six, you will notice that this chapter sounds almost the same. In fact, chapters thirty six to thirty nine repeat chapters twenty five to thirty one, almost word for word. Why would the Bible repeat it all?

Because the repetition shows us something important. In chapters twenty five to thirty one, God said, make it like this. In chapters thirty six to thirty nine, we hear, and they made it like this. Exactly as God had said. Every curtain, every loop, every board, every socket. Nothing was left out, and nothing was changed.

After the disaster of the golden calf, when the people did what they wanted instead of what God commanded, this is a beautiful picture of obedience. Careful, detailed, joyful obedience.

Jesus said, if ye love me, keep my commandments. Obedience is not about following rules to earn God’s love. It is about loving God so much that we want to do exactly what he says.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 2. 'And Moses called Bezaleel and Aholiab, and every wise hearted man, in whose heart the LORD had put wisdom, even every one whose heart stirred him up to come unto the work to do it'. Notice two things about the workers. First, God had put wisdom in their hearts. Their skill came from him. Second, their hearts stirred them up to come. They were willing. God uses people who are gifted and willing. And if you are willing, God will give you whatever gifts you need.

Verse 5. 'And they spake unto Moses, saying, The people bring much more than enough for the service of the work, which the LORD commanded to make.' Much more than enough. This is what happens when God’s people give from willing hearts. There is no shortage. God is generous, and when his people become like him, they are generous too. Paul wrote that God loveth a cheerful giver, and that God is able to make all grace abound toward you.

Verse 13. 'And he made fifty taches of gold, and coupled the curtains one unto another with the taches: so it became one tabernacle.' So it became one tabernacle. Many pieces, made by many hands, joined together into one dwelling place for God. The people who made it came from different tribes, with different skills. But together they made one house for God. God’s people today are like that too. Different, but joined together into one home for him.

Jesus said, 'If ye love me, keep my commandments.' And Paul wrote of the Macedonians, 'And this they did, not as we hoped, but first gave their own selves to the Lord'.

Think about one thing God has asked you to do in his Word. Do it carefully and joyfully today, exactly as he says, because you love him. And be generous with what you have.

Here is today’s question. Chapters thirty six to thirty nine repeat God’s instructions almost word for word, to show that the people did exactly what God said. Why do you think careful obedience matters to God?

Let us pray. Lord God, thank you for the generous hearts of your people. Make me generous too, giving much more than enough. Help me to obey you carefully and joyfully, exactly as you say, because I love you. And join your people together, like the curtains of the tabernacle, into one home for you. Amen.
