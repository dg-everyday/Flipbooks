The Living Voice. Exodus, chapter 37. Bezaleel Made the Ark.

In a workshop in the desert, a craftsman bends over his work. In front of him lies a block of gold. With a small hammer, he taps it, again and again, slowly shaping it. Hour after hour, the gold begins to take shape. Wings appear. Faces. Two angels, facing each other, their wings spread wide. He wipes his brow, and keeps going. This is the most important thing he will ever make.

In chapter thirty six, the people brought so many gifts that the workmen told Moses, the people bring much more than enough. Moses had to tell them to stop giving. Then the craftsmen made the curtains, the boards, the bars and the veil, exactly as God had commanded, so it became one tabernacle.

Now, in chapter thirty seven, Bezaleel makes the holiest objects of all, the furniture for inside the tabernacle. Listen for the word beaten. And listen for how the angels on the mercy seat are made.

In chapter thirty six, we heard about many workmen making the curtains and boards. But in chapter thirty seven, one name stands out.

It seems that Bezaleel, the master craftsman, made these most holy objects with his own hands. The ark of the covenant, the most sacred object in all Israel, was not left to an apprentice. The chief craftsman made it himself.

Bezaleel covered the ark with pure gold, inside and out, and put a gold rim around the top. He made four gold rings for the corners, and two poles of acacia wood covered with gold, and slid the poles through the rings, so the ark could be carried.

Then he made the mercy seat, the lid of the ark, of pure gold. And on each end, a cherub, made of beaten gold.

Beaten work means the cherubim were not cast in a mould. They were hammered into shape, by hand, out of the same piece of gold as the mercy seat itself. It was slow, careful, difficult work. Every blow of the hammer had to be just right.

Then the table for the bread of the presence. Acacia wood, covered with pure gold, with a gold border, rings and poles. And Bezaleel made the dishes, spoons, bowls and covers for the table, all of pure gold.

Then the lampstand. It too was beaten work, hammered out of one solid piece of pure gold. A central shaft, six branches, three on each side, each decorated with cups shaped like almond blossoms, with buds and flowers. 'And he made his seven lamps, and his snuffers, and his snuffdishes, of pure gold.' Snuffers were tools for trimming the wicks. 'Of a talent of pure gold made he it, and all the vessels thereof.' A talent was a very large weight, about as heavy as a child. All that gold, hammered into one beautiful lampstand.

Then the altar of incense, a small square altar of acacia wood, covered with pure gold, with horns on its corners and a gold rim around the top, and rings and poles to carry it.

And finally, 'he made the holy anointing oil, and the pure incense of sweet spices, according to the work of the apothecary.' An apothecary was someone who mixed perfumes and medicines.

Every single thing was made exactly as God had shown Moses on the mountain.

What happened to these objects? The ark of the covenant went on an amazing journey. The priests carried it ahead of the people when they crossed the river Jordan, and the waters stopped flowing. It was carried around the walls of Jericho, before the walls fell down. Later, it was captured by the Philistines, but God sent it back. King David brought it up to Jerusalem, dancing before it with all his might. And Solomon placed it in the most holy place of the great temple. The ark Bezaleel made with his own hands was at the centre of Israel’s story for a very long time.

Bezaleel probably never knew all that would happen to it. He simply did his work, faithfully and carefully, for God. Paul wrote, whatsoever ye do, do it heartily, as to the Lord, and not unto men. That is what Bezaleel did. And God used his work far beyond anything he could have imagined.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 1. 'And Bezaleel made the ark of shittim wood: two cubits and a half was the length of it, and a cubit and a half the breadth of it, and a cubit and a half the height of it'. Exactly the measurements God gave in chapter twenty five. Not a little bigger, to make it more impressive. Not a little smaller, to save gold. Exactly what God said. Bezaleel did not add his own ideas to God’s design. He used his great skill to do exactly what God asked. That is true faithfulness.

Verse 9. 'And the cherubims spread out their wings on high, and covered with their wings over the mercy seat, with their faces one to another; even to the mercy seatward were the faces of the cherubims.' The angels looked down towards the mercy seat. Peter wrote that the good news of Jesus is something which the angels desire to look into. Like the golden cherubim, the angels in heaven look with wonder at God’s mercy to sinners. They can hardly believe how kind God is to us.

Verse 22. 'Their knops and their branches were of the same: all of it was one beaten work of pure gold.' Knops are the round buds on the branches. The whole lampstand, all its branches and flowers, was made from one piece of gold, beaten into shape. It became beautiful, and gave light, by being hammered. Sometimes God shapes us through hard times, like a craftsman with a hammer. It hurts. But he is making us into something beautiful, that will shine with his light.

Paul wrote, 'And whatsoever ye do, do it heartily, as to the Lord, and not unto men'. And Peter wrote of the good news, 'which things the angels desire to look into.'

Whatever work you do today, whether schoolwork, chores, or helping someone, do it carefully and heartily, as if you were doing it for God himself. Because you are.

Here is today’s question. Bezaleel never knew all the places the ark he made would go. How does it help you to know that God can use faithful work in ways we may never see?

Let us pray. Lord God, thank you for Bezaleel, who made the ark faithfully and carefully. Help me to do my work heartily, as for you. When hard times come, help me to trust that you are shaping me, like gold in the craftsman’s hands, into something beautiful that shines for you. Amen.
