The Living Voice. Exodus, chapter 38. Of the Lookingglasses of the Women.

A woman sits in her tent, holding her most precious possession. It is a mirror, made of polished bronze, smooth and shining. She brought it all the way from Egypt. Every day, she looks into it. Then she hears that bronze is needed for God’s house. She looks at her reflection one last time. Then she stands up, walks to the tabernacle, and gives her mirror away.

In chapter thirty seven, Bezaleel made the holiest objects for the tabernacle with his own hands. The ark of the covenant and the mercy seat, with two cherubim of beaten gold, facing each other. The golden table, and the lampstand, all of one beaten work of pure gold. The altar of incense, and the holy oil and incense.

Now, in chapter thirty eight, Bezaleel makes the things for the courtyard. Listen for what the bronze basin was made from. And listen for what happened to the silver from the half shekel offering.

First, Bezaleel made the altar of burnt offering. Acacia wood, covered with bronze, five cubits square and three cubits high, with horns on its four corners. He made all its tools of bronze: pots, shovels, bowls, forks and firepans. He made the bronze grate, and the rings and poles to carry it.

Then he made the laver, the bronze basin where the priests washed their hands and feet. And here the Bible tells us something we were not told before.

In those days, there was no glass for mirrors. A mirror was a flat piece of bronze, polished until it shone, so that you could see your reflection in it. Mirrors like these were precious, and many women had brought them from Egypt.

These women gave up their mirrors, so that the priests could wash before serving God. Think about what that means. They gave up something they used to look at themselves, so that others could be made clean for God. Instead of looking at their own reflection, they turned their eyes to God.

Then Bezaleel made the courtyard. Curtains of fine white linen, a hundred cubits long on the south and north sides, fifty cubits on the west and east. Pillars of bronze, with hooks and bands of silver. And the gate, on the east, twenty cubits wide, made of blue, purple, scarlet and fine linen, with needlework.

Then the chapter does something unusual. It counts up everything. 'This is the sum of the tabernacle, even of the tabernacle of testimony, as it was counted, according to the commandment of Moses, for the service of the Levites, by the hand of Ithamar, son to Aaron the priest.' Ithamar, Aaron’s son, kept careful records. Everything given to God was counted, and accounted for. Nothing was wasted or stolen.

And then the craftsmen are honoured.

And with him was Aholiab, from the tribe of Dan, 'an engraver, and a cunning workman, and an embroiderer in blue, and in purple, and in scarlet, and fine linen.'

Then comes the account. How much gold was used? Twenty nine talents and seven hundred and thirty shekels. That is a huge amount of gold, more than a ton.

How much silver? A hundred talents and more. Where did the silver come from? Do you remember chapter thirty? Every man of twenty years old and over was to give half a shekel, the rich no more and the poor no less, as a ransom for his soul. And the number of men counted was six hundred and three thousand, five hundred and fifty. Every one of them gave his half shekel.

And what was that silver used for?

The bronze, seventy talents and more, was used for the bronze altar, the grate, the sockets for the door and the courtyard, and all the tent pegs.

So the tabernacle was built from the gifts of all the people. The gold, given freely by those with willing hearts. The silver, given equally by every man as a ransom. And the bronze, including the mirrors of the women. Every gift had its place.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 8. 'And he made the laver of brass, and the foot of it of brass, of the lookingglasses of the women assembling, which assembled at the door of the tabernacle of the congregation.' Lookingglasses means mirrors. These women gathered at the door of the tabernacle, perhaps to serve, perhaps to pray. They loved God’s house. And they gave up their mirrors, so that the priests could be made clean. When we give up something for God, he turns it into something that blesses others.

Verse 22. 'And Bezaleel the son of Uri, the son of Hur, of the tribe of Judah, made all that the LORD commanded Moses.' All that the LORD commanded. Not some of it. Not most of it. All of it. Bezaleel finished the work God gave him. Near the end of his life, Paul wrote, I have finished my course. And Jesus, on the cross, said, it is finished. What a wonderful thing it is to finish the work God has given us.

Verse 27. 'And of the hundred talents of silver were cast the sockets of the sanctuary, and the sockets of the vail; an hundred sockets of the hundred talents, a talent for a socket.' The silver from the ransom money became the foundation of the tabernacle. Every board stood on silver sockets. The whole of God’s house rested on the price of redemption. Peter wrote that we were not redeemed with corruptible things, as silver and gold, but with the precious blood of Christ. Our salvation rests on the price Jesus paid.

Peter wrote, 'Forasmuch as ye know that ye were not redeemed with corruptible things, as silver and gold'. And Paul wrote, 'I have fought a good fight, I have finished my course, I have kept the faith'.

Is there something you love that you could give up for God, or use to bless someone else? Think of the women and their mirrors. And whatever work God has given you, ask him to help you finish it.

Here is today’s question. The women gave up their mirrors so that the priests could wash before serving God. What do you think it means to look away from ourselves, and towards God and others?

Let us pray. Lord God, thank you for the women who gave up their mirrors, and for every gift that built your house. Help me to give up what I love most, if you ask me to. Help me to finish the work you give me. And thank you that I was redeemed, not with silver and gold, but with the precious blood of Jesus. Amen.
