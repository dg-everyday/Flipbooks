The Living Voice. Exodus, chapter 39. As the LORD Commanded Moses.

A teacher gives her class a big project. Each child has a part to do. For weeks, they work hard, measuring, cutting, gluing and painting. Then comes the day to show the finished work. The children carry it all to the front, and set it out on the tables. The teacher walks slowly along, looking carefully at every piece. And then she smiles, and says, well done. You have done exactly what I asked. In Exodus chapter thirty nine, Moses has a moment like that.

In chapter thirty eight, Bezaleel made the bronze altar, and the courtyard with its one gate. He made the bronze laver from the mirrors of the women who assembled at the door of the tabernacle. And the silver from the half shekel ransom money of every man was made into the sockets, the foundation of the whole tabernacle.

Now, in chapter thirty nine, the priests’ holy garments are made, and the whole work is finished. Listen for a phrase that is repeated seven times. And listen for what Moses does when he sees the finished work.

The first part of the chapter describes the making of the priests’ garments, the clothes for glory and for beauty that God described in chapter twenty eight.

First, the ephod. It was made of gold, blue, purple, scarlet and fine linen. And here we learn how they got the gold into the cloth.

Then they made the two onyx stones for the shoulders of the ephod, engraved with the names of the twelve sons of Israel, 'that they should be stones for a memorial to the children of Israel; as the LORD commanded Moses.'

Then the breastplate. Four rows of precious stones, three in each row. A sardius, a topaz and a carbuncle. An emerald, a sapphire and a diamond. A ligure, an agate and an amethyst. A beryl, an onyx and a jasper. Each one engraved with the name of a tribe, set in gold, and fastened to the ephod with gold chains and a blue cord.

Then the robe, all of blue, with a strong woven edge around the neck, so it would not tear. And around the hem, pomegranates of blue, purple and scarlet, and bells of pure gold. 'A bell and a pomegranate, a bell and a pomegranate, round about the hem of the robe to minister in; as the LORD commanded Moses.'

Then the coats of fine linen for Aaron and his sons, the turban, the caps, and the embroidered sash.

And last of all, 'they made the plate of the holy crown of pure gold, and wrote upon it a writing, like to the engravings of a signet, HOLINESS TO THE LORD.' They tied it with a blue cord to the front of the turban, 'as the LORD commanded Moses.'

Did you notice the phrase? As the LORD commanded Moses. In this chapter, it is repeated seven times. After each part of the work, the Bible tells us, they did it as the LORD commanded. In the Bible, seven is often a number of completeness. The work was complete, and completely obedient.

Then comes a great moment.

So much work. Thousands of willing hands. Gold, silver, bronze, linen, wood, skins, stones, spices. And now, it was finished.

Then the people brought everything to Moses. The tent, with all its clasps, boards, bars, pillars and sockets. The coverings of red rams’ skins and badgers’ skins, and the veil. The ark of the testimony, with its poles, and the mercy seat. The table, with all its dishes, and the bread. The pure lampstand, with its lamps, and the oil for the light. The golden altar, the anointing oil, the sweet incense, and the curtain for the door. The bronze altar, with its grate, poles and tools, and the laver. The hangings of the courtyard, the pillars, the gate, the cords and the pegs. And the holy garments for Aaron and his sons.

It must have been an amazing sight. All the pieces of God’s house, laid out before Moses, ready to be put together.

'According to all that the LORD commanded Moses, so the children of Israel made all the work.'

And then.

Do you remember the very first chapter of the Bible? When God had finished creating the world, the Bible says, God saw every thing that he had made, and, behold, it was very good. Then God blessed the seventh day. Now, the people have finished making God’s house. Moses looks at all of it, and, behold, they had done it as God commanded. And Moses blessed them.

It is a beautiful ending to a long story. These were the same people who had grumbled in the wilderness, and made the golden calf. But God had forgiven them, and given them a second chance. And this time, they obeyed, completely and joyfully.

Jesus told a story about servants who were given work to do while their master was away. When the master returned, he said to the faithful servant, well done, thou good and faithful servant. One day, all who have served Jesus faithfully will hear those words.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 3. 'And they did beat the gold into thin plates, and cut it into wires, to work it in the blue, and in the purple, and in the scarlet, and in the fine linen, with cunning work.' What patient, skilful work! They hammered gold until it was as thin as paper, then cut it into fine threads, and wove those threads into the cloth, so the ephod would shimmer with gold. Nobody would notice each tiny thread. But God did. He cares about the small details, done faithfully for him.

Verse 32. 'Thus was all the work of the tabernacle of the tent of the congregation finished: and the children of Israel did according to all that the LORD commanded Moses, so did they.' All the work finished. And they did according to all that the LORD commanded. Not half. Not most. All. Starting something for God is good. But finishing it, and finishing it his way, is even better.

Verse 43. 'And Moses did look upon all the work, and, behold, they have done it as the LORD had commanded, even so had they done it: and Moses blessed them.' Moses looked carefully at everything. And everything was right. So he blessed them. He prayed God’s goodness on them, and thanked them. When we serve God faithfully, he sees. And he delights to bless us. And good leaders, like Moses, notice faithful work, and encourage the people who did it.

Genesis tells us, 'And God saw every thing that he had made, and, behold, it was very good.' And Jesus said, 'Well done, thou good and faithful servant: thou hast been faithful over a few things, I will make thee ruler over many things'.

Finish something today that you have started, and do it carefully, as for God. And think of someone who has worked hard, at home, at school, or among God’s people. Encourage them, and say well done.

Here is today’s question. The phrase as the LORD commanded Moses is repeated seven times in this chapter. Why do you think God wanted the Bible to show so clearly that the people obeyed him exactly?

Let us pray. Lord God, thank you for the faithful work of your people, as they made your house just as you commanded. Help me to be faithful in small things, and to finish the work you give me, your way. Thank you that you see what I do for you. And one day, I long to hear you say, well done, thou good and faithful servant. Amen.
