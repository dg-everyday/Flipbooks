The Living Voice. Ezra, chapter 1. The LORD Stirred Up the Spirit of Cyrus.

In a great palace in Persia, a king sits on his throne. He rules the largest empire the world has ever seen. He does not worship the God of Israel. And yet, one day, something stirs inside him. He calls his scribes, and dictates a message to be sent to every corner of his kingdom. It begins, The LORD God of heaven. And it will bring a whole people home.

In the introduction, we heard that the book of Ezra tells how God’s people came home from exile, rebuilt the temple, and rebuilt their lives around God’s Word. We heard three things to watch for: how God moves the hearts of kings, the good hand of God, and the place of God’s Word.

Now, in chapter one, the exile comes to an end. Listen for why it happened. And listen for what goes home with the people.

The chapter begins with one long, wonderful sentence.

Persia had conquered Babylon. The book of Daniel tells how Babylon fell, after the hand wrote on the wall during Belshazzar’s feast. And now Cyrus, the Persian king, rules over all the lands Babylon once held, including the Jewish exiles.

Jeremiah had promised that after a set time in Babylon, God would bring his people home. And here it was, happening, exactly as God had said.

Even more amazing, the prophet Isaiah, long before Cyrus was born, had named him. God said of Cyrus, he is my shepherd, and he shall say to Jerusalem, thou shalt be built, and to the temple, thy foundation shall be laid. God knew Cyrus’s name before Cyrus knew God’s name.

And here is what Cyrus said in his proclamation. 'Thus saith Cyrus king of Persia, The LORD God of heaven hath given me all the kingdoms of the earth; and he hath charged me to build him an house at Jerusalem, which is in Judah.'

Then Cyrus invites the Jews to go.

And Cyrus adds something else. Those who stay behind should help those who go, with silver and gold, goods and animals, as well as freewill offerings for the house of God in Jerusalem. Do you remember when the Israelites left Egypt, and the Egyptians gave them silver and gold? It is like a second exodus.

But not everyone wanted to go. Many had been born in Babylon. They had homes and businesses and families there. Jerusalem was a ruin, far away across the desert. Going home would be hard and dangerous.

So who went?

The heads of families from the tribes of Judah and Benjamin, the priests, and the Levites, who served in God’s house. And all their neighbours helped them, with silver, gold, goods, animals and precious gifts, as well as all the freewill offerings.

And then Cyrus does something remarkable. He brings out the vessels of the house of the LORD, the gold and silver bowls and dishes that Nebuchadnezzar had carried away from the temple in Jerusalem, and put in the temple of his own gods.

Do you remember? In second Kings, we heard how Nebuchadnezzar carried away the treasures of the temple. And in the book of Daniel, king Belshazzar used those very vessels at his feast, drinking from them and praising his idols, just before the writing appeared on the wall. Now, those same vessels are counted out, one by one, by the king’s treasurer.

The treasurer gives them to Sheshbazzar, the prince of Judah, who will lead the people home. And the writer counts them carefully: dishes of gold and silver, bowls, basins and many other vessels. Thousands of them. Every one counted, and every one going home.

'All these did Sheshbazzar bring up with them of the captivity that were brought up from Babylon unto Jerusalem.'

The things that had been taken away were coming back. The people who had been taken away were coming back. God had not forgotten his house, or his people.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 1. 'Now in the first year of Cyrus king of Persia, that the word of the LORD by the mouth of Jeremiah might be fulfilled, the LORD stirred up the spirit of Cyrus king of Persia, that he made a proclamation throughout all his kingdom, and put it also in writing, saying'. That the word of the LORD might be fulfilled. Cyrus thought he was simply making a wise political decision. But behind it all, God was keeping his promise through Jeremiah. And God did it by stirring up the heart of a foreign king. God can move anyone’s heart, even the most powerful ruler on earth.

Verse 3. 'Who is there among you of all his people? his God be with him, and let him go up to Jerusalem, which is in Judah, and build the house of the LORD God of Israel, (he is the God,) which is in Jerusalem.' Who is there among you? It was an invitation, not a command. Each person had to decide whether to stay in comfort in Babylon, or go up to the hard work of rebuilding. God still asks that question. Who will go? Who will help build his house?

Verse 5. 'Then rose up the chief of the fathers of Judah and Benjamin, and the priests, and the Levites, with all them whose spirit God had raised, to go up to build the house of the LORD which is in Jerusalem.' Notice the words, whose spirit God had raised. God stirred the heart of Cyrus to send them. And God stirred the hearts of his people to go. When God wants something done, he moves hearts on every side. If you feel a desire to serve God, that desire may be God stirring your spirit.

Isaiah heard God say of Cyrus, 'He is my shepherd, and shall perform all my pleasure'. And Solomon wrote, 'The king’s heart is in the hand of the LORD, as the rivers of water: he turneth it whithersoever he will.'

Is God stirring your heart about something, a place to serve, a person to help, a step of faith? Pay attention to that stirring. And if you are worried about people in power, remember that their hearts are in God’s hand. Pray for them.

Here is today’s question. Cyrus asked, who is there among you, that will go up and build? If God asked you that question today, how would you answer?

Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that you keep your promises, and that you can move the hearts of kings. Thank you that nothing that belongs to you is ever truly lost. Stir up my spirit, as you stirred up your people, to serve you and to build what you want built. Here I am. Amen.
