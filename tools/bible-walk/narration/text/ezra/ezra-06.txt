The Living Voice. Ezra, chapter 6. With Joy.

In a dusty room in a far-off royal palace, officials search through shelf after shelf of old scrolls. Records of laws, and taxes, and decisions made long ago. They are looking for one particular scroll. Did the old king really give permission to build a temple in Jerusalem? Hours pass. And then, one of them pulls out a scroll, unrolls it, and reads. Here it is!

In chapter five, God sent the prophets Haggai and Zechariah, and the people started building the temple again. The governor Tatnai came and asked, who commanded you to build this? But the eye of their God was upon them, and they kept building. They said, we are the servants of the God of heaven and earth. And Tatnai wrote to king Darius, asking him to search the records.

Now, in chapter six, the answer comes. Listen for what king Darius decides. And listen for the joy when the house of God is finally finished.

King Darius orders a search of the royal records. They search in Babylon. And at last, in a palace in another city, called Achmetha, they find a scroll.

It is the very decree that Cyrus had made, years before. Let the house of God at Jerusalem be built, the place where they offer sacrifices. Let its foundations be firmly laid. Let the costs be paid from the king’s treasury. And let the gold and silver vessels that Nebuchadnezzar took from the temple be returned, every one to its place.

The Jews had told the truth. And now king Darius writes his own decree to Tatnai, the governor.

But Darius goes even further. He orders that the cost of the building should be paid in full, from the king’s own taxes from that very region. And whatever the priests need for their sacrifices, young bulls, rams, lambs, wheat, salt, wine and oil, should be given to them every day, without fail. Why? 'That they may offer sacrifices of sweet savours unto the God of heaven, and pray for the life of the king, and of his sons.' And Darius adds a stern warning to anyone who tries to change his decree.

Think about that. The enemies of the Jews had tried to stop the building. Instead, their own taxes now had to pay for it! God had turned the whole thing around.

So Tatnai and his companions do exactly what Darius commanded, and quickly.

And the building goes on.

And then, at last, the house is finished. The writer gives us the exact date, in the reign of king Darius. After all the years of waiting, all the opposition, all the discouragement, the house of God stands again in Jerusalem.

And the people celebrate.

They offer sacrifices: bulls, rams and lambs. And as a sin offering for all Israel, they offer twelve goats, one for each tribe of Israel. Not just for Judah and Benjamin, who had come home, but for all twelve tribes. They still saw themselves as one people, the whole family of Israel.

They organise the priests and Levites in their groups, for the service of God, just as it is written in the book of Moses.

And then they keep the passover. The passover remembered how God brought his people out of slavery in Egypt. Now, they had been brought out of exile in Babylon. It was like a second exodus, and they celebrated it the way God had commanded.

And who joined them? Not only those who had come back from exile. Also, 'all such as had separated themselves unto them from the filthiness of the heathen of the land, to seek the LORD God of Israel'. People from the land who turned away from idols to seek the LORD were welcomed in. God’s people were never closed to those who truly wanted to follow him.

And they kept the feast of unleavened bread for seven days, with joy.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 14. 'And the elders of the Jews builded, and they prospered through the prophesying of Haggai the prophet and Zechariah the son of Iddo. And they builded, and finished it, according to the commandment of the God of Israel, and according to the commandment of Cyrus, and Darius, and Artaxerxes king of Persia.' They prospered through the prophesying. God’s Word kept them going. And notice the order: first, the commandment of the God of Israel, then the commandments of the kings. The kings gave permission. But it was God who commanded it, and God who made it happen.

Verse 16. 'And the children of Israel, the priests, and the Levites, and the rest of the children of the captivity, kept the dedication of this house of God with joy'. With joy. After tears, after waiting, after discouragement, joy. The children of the captivity, people who had lost everything, were now rejoicing in the house of God. Joy often comes at the end of a long, hard road of faithfulness.

Verse 22. 'And kept the feast of unleavened bread seven days with joy: for the LORD had made them joyful, and turned the heart of the king of Assyria unto them, to strengthen their hands in the work of the house of God, the God of Israel.' The LORD had made them joyful. Their joy was a gift from God. And the king of Assyria here means the Persian king, who now ruled the old lands of Assyria. Once, enemies had weakened their hands. Now, God turned a king’s heart to strengthen their hands. God can turn our discouragement into joy.

The psalmist wrote, 'I was glad when they said unto me, Let us go into the house of the LORD.' And Paul wrote, 'he which hath begun a good work in you will perform it'.

Is there something you have been waiting a long time for God to finish? Keep trusting him. He finishes what he starts. And thank God today for something he has brought to completion in your life.

Here is today’s question. Enemies tried to stop the building, but in the end their taxes helped pay for it. When have you seen God turn something bad into something good?

Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that you finish what you begin. Thank you that you can turn the hearts of kings, and turn opposition into help. Make me joyful in you, as you made your people joyful. Strengthen my hands for your work. And thank you that your house is open to all who turn to seek you. Amen.
