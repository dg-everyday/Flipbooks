The Living Voice. Genesis, chapter 6. Noah Found Grace in the Eyes of the LORD.

Far from the sea, a man is building a boat. Not a small boat, but a huge one, longer than a city street. Day after day he cuts wood, fits beams together, and covers them with pitch. People must have stared. There is no water anywhere near. But God has spoken, and Noah is listening.

In chapter five, we followed the family line from Adam to Noah. Generation after generation lived, and died. But Enoch walked with God, and God took him. And Lamech named his son Noah, hoping that he would bring comfort from the toil of the cursed ground.

Now, in chapter six, we see how far the world has fallen since the garden. It is one of the darkest pictures in the Bible. And yet, in the middle of it, there is one of the most beautiful words in Scripture: grace.

People begin to multiply on the face of the earth. The chapter opens with a strange and difficult passage. The sons of God take wives of the daughters of men, whichever they choose. There are giants in the earth in those days, mighty men, men of renown. Bible readers have understood these verses in different ways. But the point is clear: people are crossing the lines God set, and the world is going badly wrong.

God says, 'My spirit shall not always strive with man'. He gives a limit, a hundred and twenty years.

Then God looks at the earth, and what he sees is terrible.

'And it repented the LORD that he had made man on the earth, and it grieved him at his heart.' This is not a cold, distant God. It is a God whose heart is broken. The people he made in his own image have filled his world with evil. And God says he will destroy man, whom he has created, from the face of the earth.

But then comes a single word that changes everything: but.

Noah is a just man, and perfect in his generations, which means blameless among the people of his time. And like Enoch before him, Noah walks with God. Noah has three sons: Shem, Ham and Japheth.

The earth is corrupt before God, and filled with violence. So God tells Noah what he is going to do. The end of all flesh has come before him, he says, because the earth is filled with violence through them. And God gives Noah a task.

Make thee an ark of gopher wood, God says. Make rooms in it, and cover it inside and out with pitch. He gives the measurements: three hundred cubits long, fifty cubits wide, and thirty cubits high. A cubit is about the length of a forearm, so the ark was about one hundred and fifty metres long. It is to have a window, a door in its side, and three decks.

God says that he will bring a flood of waters upon the earth, to destroy all flesh that has the breath of life. Everything on the earth shall die. 'But with thee will I establish my covenant'. This is the first time the word covenant appears in the Bible. A covenant is a solemn promise, a binding agreement. God is binding himself to Noah.

Noah is to go into the ark with his sons, his wife and his sons’ wives. And he is to bring two of every kind of living creature, male and female, to keep them alive. Birds, cattle and every creeping thing, two of every sort. And he is to gather food of every kind, for himself and for them.

Then the chapter ends with one simple sentence about Noah.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 5. 'And GOD saw that the wickedness of man was great in the earth, and that every imagination of the thoughts of his heart was only evil continually.' Notice how deep the problem goes. Not just evil actions, but evil thoughts. Not just sometimes, but continually. The trouble with the world was not out there somewhere. It was in the human heart. The flood would wash the earth, but it could not wash the heart. Only God could do that, and the rest of the Bible shows how he does.

Verse 8. 'But Noah found grace in the eyes of the LORD.' This is the first time the word grace appears in the Bible. Grace means God’s kindness to people who do not deserve it. Noah was a good man, but he was not sinless, as later chapters will show. He did not earn God’s favour. He found it. In a world under judgment, God’s grace rested on one man and his family. And that grace is still offered to anyone who will receive it.

Verse 22. 'Thus did Noah; according to all that God commanded him, so did he.' Noah’s faith was not just words. He obeyed, completely, even when it made no sense to the people around him. He built the ark exactly as God said, for a flood no one had seen. Faith listens to God, and then does what he says, all of it.

The letter to the Hebrews says, 'By faith Noah, being warned of God of things not seen as yet, moved with fear, prepared an ark to the saving of his house'. And Jesus said, 'But as the days of Noe were, so shall also the coming of the Son of man be.' People were eating and drinking, not knowing, until the flood came. Jesus calls us to be ready.

Is God asking you to do something that does not make sense to the people around you? Trust him, and obey. Whatever the world around you is like, you can walk with God, as Noah did, one day at a time.

Here is today’s question. Noah found grace in the eyes of the LORD, and then he obeyed all that God commanded him. Is there anything God has asked you to do that you have not yet done?

Let us pray. Lord God, you see the wickedness of the world, and the evil in every heart, including mine. Thank you for your grace, which I could never earn. Help me, like Noah, to walk with you and to do all that you command, even when others do not understand. Amen.
