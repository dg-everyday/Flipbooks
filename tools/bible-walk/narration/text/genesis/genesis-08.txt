The Living Voice. Genesis, chapter 8. God Remembered Noah.

For months, there has been nothing outside the ark but water. No land, no trees, no sound but the creak of timber and the cries of the animals. Then Noah opens a window, and lets a little dove fly out over the endless sea. Will it find anywhere to land?

In chapter seven, Noah and his family went into the ark, with the animals, two by two. The LORD shut him in. The fountains of the great deep broke open, and the rain fell 'forty days and forty nights.' The waters rose above the highest mountains, and every living thing on the land died. Only Noah, and those with him in the ark, remained alive.

Now, in chapter eight, the story turns. It turns on four small words: God remembered Noah. Listen for how slowly and carefully the waters go down, and for the very first thing Noah does when he steps onto dry ground.

God remembers Noah, and every living thing with him in the ark. He makes a wind pass over the earth, and the waters begin to go down. The fountains of the deep and the windows of heaven are stopped, and the rain is restrained.

Little by little the waters return from off the earth. After a hundred and fifty days they have gone down, and in the seventh month, on the seventeenth day of the month, the ark comes to rest upon the mountains of Ararat. The waters keep going down, until in the tenth month the tops of the mountains can be seen.

After forty days more, Noah opens the window of the ark, and sends out a raven. It flies to and fro, until the waters are dried up from the earth.

Then Noah sends out a dove, to see if the waters have gone down from the face of the ground. But the dove finds no rest for the sole of her foot, and she returns to him in the ark. Noah puts out his hand, takes her, and pulls her in to him.

He waits another seven days, and sends out the dove again.

Noah waits another seven days, and sends out the dove once more. This time she does not return.

In the six hundred and first year, in the first month, on the first day of the month, Noah removes the covering of the ark, and looks out. The face of the ground is dry. But Noah does not rush out. He waits until God speaks. In the second month, on the twenty-seventh day, the earth is dried.

Then God speaks to Noah. Go forth of the ark, he says, you and your wife, your sons and your sons’ wives. Bring out every living thing with you, that they may breed abundantly in the earth, and be fruitful, and multiply. So Noah goes out, with his family, and every beast, every creeping thing, and every bird, by their kinds.

And the very first thing Noah does is build an altar to the LORD. He takes of every clean beast and every clean bird, and offers burnt offerings on the altar. It is an act of worship and thanksgiving.

'And the LORD smelled a sweet savour'. God is pleased. And the LORD says in his heart that he will never again curse the ground for man’s sake, even though the imagination of man’s heart is evil from his youth. Nor will he ever again destroy every living thing, as he has done. The flood has not changed the human heart. But God promises to be patient.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 1. 'And God remembered Noah, and every living thing, and all the cattle that was with him in the ark: and God made a wind to pass over the earth, and the waters asswaged;' God had never forgotten Noah. In the Bible, when God remembers someone, it means he acts on their behalf. The long days on the waters were not wasted, and Noah was never out of God’s mind. If you feel forgotten in a long season of waiting, remember this verse. God remembers you, and in his time, he will act.

Verse 11. 'And the dove came in to him in the evening; and, lo, in her mouth was an olive leaf pluckt off: so Noah knew that the waters were abated from off the earth.' One small, fresh leaf. It was not dry land yet, but it was a sign that life was returning. God often gives us small signs of hope before the full answer comes. Ever since, the dove and the olive leaf have been a picture of peace. Watch for the olive leaves God sends into your life.

Verse 22. 'While the earth remaineth, seedtime and harvest, and cold and heat, and summer and winter, and day and night shall not cease.' This is God’s promise of faithfulness. Every harvest, every change of season, every new day is a sign that God keeps his word. The world is not ruled by chance. It is held steady by the promise of God. And the God who keeps the seasons turning can be trusted to keep every other promise he has made.

The prophet Jeremiah wrote, 'It is of the LORD’S mercies that we are not consumed, because his compassions fail not. They are new every morning: great is thy faithfulness.' Every new day is a reminder of the promise God made to Noah.

Are you waiting for God to act? Hold on, like Noah, and watch for the olive leaf. And when God brings you through, do what Noah did first. Stop, and give him thanks.

Here is today’s question. God remembered Noah, and sent a dove with an olive leaf as a sign of hope. What signs of God’s faithfulness have you seen in your own life?

Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that you remember me, even when I feel forgotten. Thank you for every sign of hope, and for your faithfulness in every season. When you bring me through the storm, help me to stop and worship you first. Amen.
