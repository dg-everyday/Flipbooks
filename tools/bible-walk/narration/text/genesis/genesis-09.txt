The Living Voice. Genesis, chapter 9. I Do Set My Bow in the Cloud.

The storm clouds have passed. The ground is drying. Noah and his family stand on a mountainside, in a world that has been washed clean. Then, across the clouds, something appears that no one has ever looked at in quite this way: a great arch of colour, stretching from one side of the sky to the other. And God tells them what it means.

In chapter eight, God remembered Noah. The waters went down, the ark came to rest on the mountains of Ararat, and a dove returned with an olive leaf. Noah came out of the ark, built an altar, and worshipped. And God promised that while the earth remains, seedtime and harvest, and summer and winter, shall not cease.

Now, in chapter nine, God speaks to Noah and his sons, and gives them a new beginning. It sounds a lot like chapter one. But it also shows us that sin has not been washed away.

God blesses Noah and his sons, with the same words he spoke to Adam and Eve.

Then God tells them that the animals will now fear them, and that every moving thing that lives shall be food for them, just as he once gave them the green plants. But they are not to eat meat with its blood, the life of it, still in it. Life belongs to God, and must be treated with respect.

Above all, human life is precious. God says he will require a reckoning for the life of every man. 'Whoso sheddeth man’s blood, by man shall his blood be shed: for in the image of God made he man.' Even after the flood, every person still carries the image of God. That is why taking a human life is so serious.

Then God makes a covenant, a solemn promise, with Noah and his sons, and with their descendants after them. And not only with people. The covenant is with every living creature that came out of the ark: the birds, the cattle, and every beast of the earth. God promises that never again will all flesh be cut off by the waters of a flood, and never again will there be a flood to destroy the earth.

Then God gives a sign of his promise, something they will see again and again.

God says that when he brings a cloud over the earth, the bow will be seen in the cloud. And he will remember his covenant.

The bow will be in the cloud, God says, and he will look upon it, and remember the everlasting covenant between God and every living creature. He says it again to Noah: 'This is the token of the covenant'.

Then the chapter moves on, and gives a sad and honest story. The sons of Noah who went out of the ark are Shem, Ham and Japheth, and from them the whole earth is filled with people. Ham is the father of Canaan.

Noah begins to be a farmer, and plants a vineyard. He drinks too much of the wine, and lies uncovered in his tent. Ham sees his father’s shame, and goes and tells his two brothers outside. But Shem and Japheth take a garment, lay it on their shoulders, walk in backward, and cover their father, with their faces turned away. They will not look on their father’s shame.

When Noah wakes from his wine, and learns what his younger son has done, he speaks a curse on Canaan, Ham’s son, and a blessing on Shem and Japheth. 'Blessed be the LORD God of Shem'. Later, the promised family line of Abraham will come from Shem.

Noah lives three hundred and fifty years after the flood. All the days of Noah are nine hundred and fifty years: and he died.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 1. 'And God blessed Noah and his sons, and said unto them, Be fruitful, and multiply, and replenish the earth.' These are almost the same words God spoke to Adam and Eve in chapter one. The flood was not the end. It was a new beginning. God did not give up on his plan to fill the earth with people who bear his image. He is the God of second chances, and of fresh starts.

Verse 13. 'I do set my bow in the cloud, and it shall be for a token of a covenant between me and the earth.' A token is a sign, something you can see that reminds you of a promise. Every time you see a rainbow after rain, you are looking at a promise God made long ago, and has never broken. Notice also when the rainbow appears. Not on a clear day, but when the clouds are there. God’s promises shine most brightly when the storm clouds gather.

Verse 15. 'And I will remember my covenant, which is between me and you and every living creature of all flesh; and the waters shall no more become a flood to destroy all flesh.' Notice who does the remembering. Not Noah, but God. The rainbow is for us to see, but it is also a sign God himself looks upon. Our faith may be weak, and our memories short, but God never forgets his promises. The covenant depends on his faithfulness, not ours.

The prophet Isaiah heard God say, 'For this is as the waters of Noah unto me: for as I have sworn that the waters of Noah should no more go over the earth; so have I sworn that I would not be wroth with thee'. And in the book of Revelation, John sees around the throne of God 'a rainbow round about the throne, in sight like unto an emerald.'

The next time you see a rainbow, stop and remember God’s promise. And think of a promise of God from his Word that you need to hold on to today, especially if clouds have gathered in your life.

Here is today’s question. God set his bow in the cloud as a sign that he would remember his promise. What promise of God do you need to remember today?

Let us pray. Lord God, thank you for your covenant, and for the sign of the rainbow. Thank you that you never forget your promises, even when I forget. Help me to see every person as made in your image, and to trust your faithfulness when the clouds gather. Amen.
