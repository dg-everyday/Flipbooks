The Living Voice. Genesis, chapter 10. By These Were the Nations Divided.

Imagine a family tree so large that it covers the whole world. At the top are three brothers who stepped out of an ark. Below them, the branches spread out: to the islands and the coastlands, to the deserts of Africa, to the river valleys of the east. Every nation on earth, Genesis says, belongs to this one family.

In chapter nine, God blessed Noah and his sons, and told them to be fruitful and fill the earth. He made a covenant with every living creature, and set his bow in the cloud as a sign of his promise. And the sons of Noah who went out of the ark were Shem, Ham and Japheth.

Now, in chapter ten, we see that blessing coming true. This chapter is often called the table of nations. It lists about seventy peoples who came from Noah’s three sons. It may be one of the less familiar chapters of the Bible. But it carries a big message: all the peoples of the earth are one family, and God cares about every one of them.

The chapter begins with a title, and then follows each son in turn. It starts with the youngest, Japheth, then Ham, and ends with Shem, because the story of Genesis will follow Shem’s line.

First, the sons of Japheth. Among them are Gomer, Magog, Madai, Javan, Tubal, Meshech and Tiras. Many of these names were later linked with peoples to the north and west of Israel. Javan is the Hebrew name for the Greeks. From Japheth’s family come the peoples of the isles of the Gentiles, the coastlands, each with their own land, their own language, their own families and their own nations.

Next, the sons of Ham: Cush, Mizraim, Phut and Canaan. Cush was linked with the lands south of Egypt. Mizraim is the Hebrew name for Egypt itself. And Canaan is the father of the peoples who will live in the land God later promises to Abraham.

Among the descendants of Cush is a remarkable man named Nimrod. He begins to be a mighty one in the earth.

Nimrod becomes a builder of kingdoms. The beginning of his kingdom is Babel, and Erech, and Accad, and Calneh, in the land of Shinar, the plain between the great rivers. From there he goes out into Assyria, and builds Nineveh and other cities. These names will come back again and again in the Bible. Babel will become Babylon. Nineveh will become the capital of Assyria. Both will be great powers that trouble God’s people for centuries.

The chapter goes on to list the families of Mizraim, including the Philistines, and the many families of Canaan: the Hittites, the Jebusites, the Amorites, the Girgasites, the Hivites and others. Their border stretches from Sidon, toward Gerar and Gaza, and toward Sodom and Gomorrah. These are the peoples Israel will meet when they enter the promised land.

Last of all comes Shem, called the father of all the children of Eber. Shem’s descendants include Elam, Asshur, Arphaxad, Lud and Aram. Aram is the father of the Aramaeans, or Syrians. Arphaxad is the father of Salah, and Salah is the father of Eber. Many believe the word Hebrew comes from Eber’s name.

Eber has two sons. One is named Peleg, which means division, 'for in his days was the earth divided'. The next chapter will tell us how that happened. Peleg’s brother is Joktan, who has thirteen sons, peoples who settle in the lands of Arabia.

Through all three families, the same phrase keeps repeating: after their families, after their tongues, in their lands, in their nations. The people of the earth are spreading out, just as God commanded.

Then the chapter closes with a summary of the whole family tree.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 1. 'Now these are the generations of the sons of Noah, Shem, Ham, and Japheth: and unto them were sons born after the flood.' The phrase these are the generations appears again and again in Genesis, like chapter headings. It reminds us that the Bible is not a collection of myths set in no time and no place. It is rooted in real families and real history. Every name in this chapter was a real person, known to God. Your name, too, is known to him.

Verse 9. 'He was a mighty hunter before the LORD: wherefore it is said, Even as Nimrod the mighty hunter before the LORD.' Nimrod was strong, famous and successful. People made sayings about him. He built great cities, including Babel and Nineveh. But those cities would become symbols of human pride, standing against God. Being mighty is not the same as being godly. The Bible asks not how great we became, but whether we used our strength for God.

Verse 32. 'These are the families of the sons of Noah, after their generations, in their nations: and by these were the nations divided in the earth after the flood.' Every nation on earth comes from one family. There is no nation that is not related to the others. In God’s eyes, there is one human race. That truth cuts against every kind of pride and prejudice. The people of every nation are our relatives, made in God’s image, and loved by him.

In Athens, the apostle Paul said that God 'hath made of one blood all nations of men for to dwell on all the face of the earth'. And at the end of the Bible, John sees 'a great multitude, which no man could number, of all nations, and kindreds, and people, and tongues,' standing before the throne. The table of nations ends up around the throne of God.

Think of a person from another nation, language or culture. Remember that they are part of your family, the family of Noah, made in the image of God. Pray for them today, and ask God how you can show them kindness.

Here is today’s question. Genesis ten shows that all nations come from one family. How should that change the way you think about people who are different from you?

Let us pray. Lord God, you made all nations of one blood, and you know every name on the family tree of the world. Take away any pride or prejudice in my heart. Help me to love people of every nation, and to long for the day when every tongue will praise you. Amen.
