The Living Voice. Genesis, chapter 12. Get Thee Out of Thy Country.

An old man packs up everything he owns. His tents, his flocks, his servants, his wife. He is leaving his homeland and his father’s family for ever. His neighbours ask him where he is going. And he gives them an answer that makes no sense: I do not know. God has told me to go, and he will show me where.

In chapter eleven, people built the tower of Babel to make a name for themselves, and God scattered them over the earth. Then the family line of Shem led down to Terah and his son Abram. Abram’s wife Sarai was barren; she had no child. Terah set out from Ur toward Canaan, but stopped at Haran, and died there.

Now, in chapter twelve, everything changes. For eleven chapters, Genesis has told the story of the whole human race, a story of sin spreading and judgment falling. Now God begins a rescue plan. And it starts with one man, and a promise.

The LORD speaks to Abram. He tells him to leave three things behind: his country, his kindred, and his father’s house. And God does not give him a map. He simply says, go to the land that I will show you.

Then God gives Abram a great promise. I will make of thee a great nation. I will bless thee. I will make thy name great. Thou shalt be a blessing. Notice the difference from Babel. The builders of the tower wanted to make a name for themselves. Here, God says, I will make thy name great. And the promise does not stop with Abram.

So Abram departs, as the LORD has spoken to him. Lot goes with him. Abram is seventy and five years old when he leaves Haran. He takes Sarai his wife, Lot his nephew, all their possessions, and the people they have gathered in Haran, and they set out for the land of Canaan. And into the land of Canaan they come.

Abram passes through the land as far as Shechem, to the oak of Moreh. The Canaanites are living in the land. Then the LORD appears to Abram, and says, 'Unto thy seed will I give this land'. Abram has no seed, no children, and the land is full of other people. But he believes God. And there he builds an altar to the LORD, who appeared to him.

From there he moves to a mountain east of Bethel, and pitches his tent. And again he builds an altar.

Then Abram journeys on, still going toward the south.

But then trouble comes. There is a famine in the land, so severe that Abram goes down to Egypt to live there for a while. And as they come near to Egypt, Abram’s faith seems to fail. He says to Sarai, I know that you are a beautiful woman to look upon. When the Egyptians see you, they will say, this is his wife, and they will kill me, but keep you alive. So he asks her to say that she is his sister, so that it will go well with him for her sake.

Sarai was in fact his half-sister, as a later chapter explains. But it is a half-truth meant to deceive. And it goes badly. The princes of Pharaoh see Sarai and praise her to Pharaoh, and she is taken into Pharaoh’s house. Pharaoh treats Abram well for her sake, giving him sheep, oxen, donkeys, camels and servants.

But the LORD protects Sarai. He plagues Pharaoh and his house with great plagues because of Sarai, Abram’s wife. Pharaoh calls Abram, and asks him, what is this that thou hast done unto me? Why did you not tell me that she was your wife? Why did you say, she is my sister? Now therefore, here is your wife. Take her, and go.

Pharaoh commands his men, and they send Abram away, with his wife and all that he has.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 1. 'Now the LORD had said unto Abram, Get thee out of thy country, and from thy kindred, and from thy father’s house, unto a land that I will shew thee:' God asked Abram to leave everything familiar, without telling him where he was going. That is what faith often looks like. Not seeing the whole road, but trusting the One who calls. God does not always show us the destination before we start. He asks us to take the first step, and promises to show us the rest along the way.

Verse 3. 'And I will bless them that bless thee, and curse him that curseth thee: and in thee shall all families of the earth be blessed.' This is one of the most important verses in the whole Bible. After Babel, the families of the earth were scattered. Now God promises that through Abram, every one of those families will be blessed. God chose one man not to forget the rest of the world, but to reach it. And that promise came true in Jesus, a descendant of Abram, through whom the blessing of God has gone out to every nation.

Verse 8. 'And he removed from thence unto a mountain on the east of Bethel, and pitched his tent, having Bethel on the west, and Hai on the east: and there he builded an altar unto the LORD, and called upon the name of the LORD.' Wherever Abram went, he did two things. He pitched a tent, and he built an altar. His tent showed that he was a traveller, not settling down for good. His altar showed that God was at the centre of his life. A tent and an altar: a light hold on this world, and a firm hold on God.

The apostle Paul wrote that 'the scripture, foreseeing that God would justify the heathen through faith, preached before the gospel unto Abraham, saying, In thee shall all nations be blessed.' And the letter to the Hebrews says, 'By faith Abraham, when he was called to go out… obeyed; and he went out, not knowing whither he went.'

Is God calling you to take a step of faith, without showing you the whole road? Take the first step today. And wherever you are, build an altar, a place and a time to call on his name.

Here is today’s question. God called Abram to leave what was familiar and follow him, not knowing where he was going. What might God be asking you to leave behind, or to step into, in faith?

Let us pray. Lord God, thank you for your promise to Abram, that all the families of the earth would be blessed through him, and for the blessing that has come to me through Jesus. Give me faith to follow you, even when I cannot see the road. And when I am afraid, help me to trust you, not my own plans. Amen.
