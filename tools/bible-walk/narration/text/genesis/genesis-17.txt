The Living Voice. Genesis, chapter 17. I Am the Almighty God.

An old man falls on his face in the dust. God has just spoken to him, and given him a new name. And then God tells him something so astonishing that he falls on his face again, and laughs. His wife is ninety years old. And God says she is going to have a baby.

In chapter sixteen, Sarai grew tired of waiting, and gave her servant Hagar to Abram as a wife. Hagar ran away, and the angel of the LORD found her in the desert. She called him the God who sees me. She returned, and bore Abram a son, Ishmael.

Now, in chapter seventeen, thirteen more years have passed. Abram is ninety-nine years old. Ishmael is growing up, and Abram may think that he is the promised son. But God has not forgotten his promise. And he is about to show Abram that nothing is impossible for him.

The LORD appears to Abram, and gives him a new name for God, and a new call for Abram.

Abram falls on his face, and God talks with him. As for me, God says, behold, my covenant is with thee, and thou shalt be a father of many nations. Then God changes Abram’s name.

God promises to make him exceeding fruitful. Nations and kings will come from him. And God says, 'I will establish my covenant between me and thee and thy seed after thee in their generations for an everlasting covenant, to be a God unto thee, and to thy seed after thee.' That is the heart of the covenant. I will be your God. God gives himself to Abraham’s family for ever. And he promises them all the land of Canaan for an everlasting possession.

Then God gives Abraham a token, a sign of the covenant, to be kept by his family in every generation. Every male in Abraham’s household is to receive a mark in his body, from the oldest man to a baby boy eight days old, including the servants born in the house or bought from strangers. God says, 'my covenant shall be in your flesh for an everlasting covenant.' This outward sign would set Abraham’s family apart as belonging to God. Later, Moses and the prophets would say that God wanted more than an outward mark. He wanted a changed heart.

Then God speaks about Sarai. Her name is changed too. From now on she will be called Sarah, which means princess. God says, 'I will bless her, and give thee a son also of her: yea, I will bless her, and she shall be a mother of nations; kings of people shall be of her.'

Abraham falls on his face, and laughs. He says in his heart, shall a child be born to a man a hundred years old? And shall Sarah, who is ninety years old, bear a child? It seems impossible. Abraham loves the son he already has. He says to God, 'O that Ishmael might live before thee!'

But God answers clearly.

The name Isaac means he laughs. Every time they call their son by name, they will remember Abraham’s laughter, and how God turned it into joy.

God has not forgotten Ishmael either. As for Ishmael, I have heard thee, God says. I will bless him, and make him fruitful, and multiply him exceedingly. Twelve princes shall he beget, and I will make him a great nation. 'But my covenant will I establish with Isaac, which Sarah shall bear unto thee at this set time in the next year.'

God finishes speaking with Abraham, and goes up from him.

And Abraham obeys, that very same day. He takes Ishmael his son, and all the men and boys of his household, and gives them the sign of the covenant, as God said to him. Abraham is ninety-nine years old, and Ishmael is thirteen. In the selfsame day, every man of his house receives the sign with him.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 1. 'And when Abram was ninety years old and nine, the LORD appeared to Abram, and said unto him, I am the Almighty God; walk before me, and be thou perfect.' This is the first time God calls himself the Almighty God. In Hebrew, El Shaddai. He reveals this name to an old man who has waited for decades and cannot have children. When we come to the end of our strength, God shows us that his strength has no end. And his call is simple: walk before me. Live your whole life in my presence, with a whole heart.

Verse 5. 'Neither shall thy name any more be called Abram, but thy name shall be Abraham; for a father of many nations have I made thee.' Abram means exalted father. Abraham means father of a multitude. Imagine introducing yourself as father of a multitude, when you have only one son, and he is not the son of the promise. Notice too that God says, have I made thee. Not I will make thee. It is so certain that God speaks as if it has already happened. When God gives a promise, it is as good as done.

Verse 19. 'And God said, Sarah thy wife shall bear thee a son indeed; and thou shalt call his name Isaac: and I will establish my covenant with him for an everlasting covenant, and with his seed after him.' God would not let Abraham settle for second best. Abraham was ready to accept Ishmael as the answer. But God had something better. Sarah herself would bear a son. Sometimes, when we ask God to bless our own plans, he says no, because he has a far greater plan than we can imagine.

The apostle Paul wrote that Abraham is 'the father of us all', of everyone who believes. And he wrote that Abraham, 'being not weak in faith… considered not his own body now dead, when he was about an hundred years old,' but was 'fully persuaded that, what he had promised, he was able also to perform.'

Is there something in your life that seems impossible? Remember that God is the Almighty God. Bring it to him, and trust his promise. And whatever you face today, hear his call to walk before him, living in his presence with a whole heart.

Here is today’s question. God revealed himself to Abraham as the Almighty God, when Abraham’s own strength had run out. Where do you need to trust God’s strength instead of your own?

Let us pray. Almighty God, nothing is too hard for you. Thank you for keeping your promises, even when they seem impossible. Help me to walk before you with my whole heart, and to trust your plan, even when it is different from mine. Amen.
