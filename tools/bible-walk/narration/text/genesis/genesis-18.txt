The Living Voice. Genesis, chapter 18. Is Any Thing Too Hard for the LORD?.

It is the hottest part of the day. An old man sits in the doorway of his tent, in the shade of great oak trees. He looks up, and sees three men standing near him. He does not know who they are. But he jumps up, runs to meet them, and bows down to the ground. He is about to have the most important meal of his life.

In chapter seventeen, God appeared to Abram as the Almighty God, and changed his name to Abraham, father of many nations. He changed Sarai’s name to Sarah, and promised that she would bear a son, to be called Isaac. Abraham laughed, because he was nearly a hundred years old, and Sarah was ninety.

Now, in chapter eighteen, the LORD visits Abraham again, this time face to face. Listen for two conversations. One is about a baby. The other is about a city. Both show us what kind of God Abraham knows.

The LORD appears to Abraham by the oaks of Mamre, as he sits in the tent door in the heat of the day. Abraham sees three men, and runs to meet them. He bows, and says, my Lord, if I have found favour in thy sight, pass not away from thy servant. Let a little water be brought, and wash your feet, and rest under the tree, and I will bring a morsel of bread.

A morsel of bread turns out to be a feast. Abraham hurries into the tent to Sarah, and tells her to make cakes from three measures of fine meal. He runs to the herd, chooses a tender calf, and gives it to a young man to prepare. Then he sets butter, milk and the calf before his guests, and stands by them under the tree while they eat. The letter to the Hebrews later says, 'Be not forgetful to entertain strangers: for thereby some have entertained angels unawares.'

The visitors ask, where is Sarah thy wife? Abraham says, behold, in the tent. Then one of them, the LORD himself, says, I will certainly return to you at the time of life, and Sarah your wife shall have a son.

Sarah is listening behind the tent door. She and Abraham are old, well past the age of having children. So Sarah laughs within herself, and says, after I am waxed old, shall I have pleasure, my lord being old also?

The LORD says to Abraham, wherefore did Sarah laugh, saying, shall I surely bear a child, when I am old?

Sarah is afraid, and denies it. I laughed not, she says. But he says, nay; but thou didst laugh. God knows even the laughter we keep inside.

Then the men rise up, and look toward Sodom, and Abraham walks with them to send them on their way. And the LORD says, shall I hide from Abraham that thing which I do? God treats Abraham as a friend, and shares his plans with him.

The LORD says that the cry of Sodom and Gomorrah is great, and their sin is very grievous. He will go down and see whether they have done altogether according to the cry that has come to him. Two of the men turn and go toward Sodom. But Abraham stands yet before the LORD.

Abraham draws near and asks, wilt thou also destroy the righteous with the wicked? Perhaps there are fifty righteous within the city. Will you not spare the place for the fifty?

The LORD says, if I find fifty righteous in Sodom, I will spare all the place for their sakes.

Abraham speaks again, humbly. 'Behold now, I have taken upon me to speak unto the Lord, which am but dust and ashes'. What if there are forty-five? God says, if I find forty-five, I will not destroy it. Forty? I will not do it for forty’s sake. Thirty? I will not do it, if I find thirty there. Twenty? I will not destroy it for twenty’s sake. And finally, oh let not the Lord be angry, and I will speak yet but this once. Perhaps ten shall be found there. And God says, I will not destroy it for ten’s sake.

The LORD goes his way, when he has finished speaking with Abraham. And Abraham returns to his place.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 14. 'Is any thing too hard for the LORD? At the time appointed I will return unto thee, according to the time of life, and Sarah shall have a son.' This is a question that echoes through the whole Bible. Sarah laughed because the promise seemed impossible. And God answered with a question. Is anything too hard for me? The answer is no. Not old age, not barrenness, not any problem we face. What is impossible for us is not too hard for God.

Verse 19. 'For I know him, that he will command his children and his household after him, and they shall keep the way of the LORD, to do justice and judgment; that the LORD may bring upon Abraham that which he hath spoken of him.' God chose Abraham so that he would teach his children and his household to walk in God’s ways. God’s promises are passed on through families who keep the way of the LORD. What we teach those who come after us matters. Faith is meant to be handed down.

Verse 25. 'Shall not the Judge of all the earth do right?' Abraham did not pray because he doubted God’s goodness. He prayed because he trusted it. He knew that God is just, and would never treat the righteous like the wicked. And notice how boldly Abraham prayed, and how patiently God listened. Abraham kept asking, and God kept saying yes. God welcomes prayers for others, even for cities full of people who do not know him.

Mary, too, heard a promise that seemed impossible, and the angel Gabriel told her, 'For with God nothing shall be impossible.' And the apostle James wrote that Abraham 'was called the Friend of God.'

What seems impossible in your life right now? Bring it to God, remembering that nothing is too hard for him. And think of a place or a person who needs your prayers. Like Abraham, stand before the LORD for them, and keep asking.

Here is today’s question. God asked, is any thing too hard for the LORD? What would you bring to God in prayer if you truly believed that nothing is too hard for him?

Let us pray. Lord God, nothing is too hard for you. Forgive me for laughing at your promises, or doubting your power. Thank you that you are the Judge of all the earth, and you always do what is right. Teach me to pray boldly for others, as Abraham did, and to hand on your ways to those who come after me. Amen.
