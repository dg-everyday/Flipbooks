The Living Voice. Genesis, chapter 19. Escape for Thy Life.

The sky is just beginning to grow light. Two strangers are pulling a man, his wife and his two daughters by the hand, out through the gates of a city. Hurry, they say. Do not look back. Do not stop. The man keeps dragging his feet. Everything he owns is behind him. But the city is about to be destroyed.

In chapter eighteen, three visitors came to Abraham’s tent. The LORD promised that Sarah would have a son, and asked, is any thing too hard for the LORD? Then the LORD told Abraham that the sin of Sodom was very grievous. Abraham pleaded for the city, and God promised that if even ten righteous people were found there, he would not destroy it.

Now, in chapter nineteen, two angels come to Sodom, where Lot, Abraham’s nephew, has made his home. Back in chapter thirteen, Lot pitched his tent toward Sodom. Now he is living inside it. This is one of the most serious chapters in Genesis. It shows how deep the wickedness of Sodom had become, and how merciful God is, even to those who drag their feet.

As the day ends, two angels come to Sodom. Lot is sitting in the gate of the city, the place where leaders and judges sat. He has become an important man in Sodom. When he sees the strangers, he rises to meet them, and bows with his face to the ground. He invites them to his house, to wash their feet and stay until they go on their way. They say, no, we will stay in the street. But Lot presses them greatly, because he knows how dangerous the streets of Sodom are. So they come in, and he makes them a feast.

Before they lie down to sleep, the men of the city surround the house, young and old, from every part of the city. They shout to Lot, and demand that he bring his guests out to them, so that they can do them great harm. Lot goes out to them, and shuts the door behind him. He says, 'I pray you, brethren, do not so wickedly.' But they will not listen. They say, this one fellow came in to sojourn, and he wants to be a judge over us. And they press hard against Lot, and come near to break the door.

But the angels reach out, pull Lot into the house, and shut the door. Then they strike the men at the door with blindness, both small and great, so that they weary themselves trying to find the door.

The angels say to Lot, have you anyone else here? Sons-in-law, sons, daughters? Bring them out of this place. The LORD has sent us to destroy it, because the cry against it has become great before the LORD.

Lot goes out and speaks to the men who are to marry his daughters. Up, get you out of this place, he says, for the LORD will destroy this city. But they think he is joking.

When the day begins to break, the angels hurry Lot. Arise, they say, take your wife and your two daughters, lest you be consumed in the iniquity of the city. And still Lot lingers.

The angels bring them outside the city, and say, 'Escape for thy life; look not behind thee, neither stay thou in all the plain; escape to the mountain, lest thou be consumed.' But Lot is afraid of the mountains. He begs to go instead to a little city nearby. The angel agrees, and spares that small city for Lot’s sake. It is called Zoar, which means little.

The sun has risen upon the earth when Lot enters Zoar. 'Then the LORD rained upon Sodom and upon Gomorrah brimstone and fire from the LORD out of heaven'. He overthrows those cities, and all the plain, and all who live there, and everything that grows on the ground.

Abraham rises early, and goes back to the place where he stood before the LORD. He looks toward Sodom and Gomorrah, and sees the smoke of the land going up like the smoke of a furnace. There were not even ten righteous people there.

The chapter ends sadly. Lot is afraid to stay in Zoar, and goes to live in a cave in the mountains with his two daughters. There, the daughters, who had grown up in Sodom, deceive their father in a shameful way. From them come two nations, the Moabites and the Ammonites. Lot had left Sodom, but Sodom had not left his family. And this is the last we hear of Lot in Genesis.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 16. 'And while he lingered, the men laid hold upon his hand, and upon the hand of his wife, and upon the hand of his two daughters; the LORD being merciful unto him: and they brought him forth, and set him without the city.' Lot lingered. Even with destruction coming, he could not bring himself to leave. His heart was still tied to Sodom. But God did not leave him behind. The angels took him by the hand. Lot was saved, not because he was quick or strong, but because the LORD was merciful to him. That is how all of us are saved: by mercy.

Verse 26. 'But his wife looked back from behind him, and she became a pillar of salt.' She had been told not to look back. She was out of the city, on the road to safety. But her heart was still in Sodom, and she turned. Jesus himself said, 'Remember Lot’s wife.' It is possible to leave a place with your feet, while your heart stays behind. When God calls us out of sin, he calls us not to look back.

Verse 29. 'And it came to pass, when God destroyed the cities of the plain, that God remembered Abraham, and sent Lot out of the midst of the overthrow, when he overthrew the cities in the which Lot dwelt.' Why was Lot saved? Because God remembered Abraham. Abraham’s prayers for Sodom in chapter eighteen were not wasted. Sodom was not spared, but Lot was. We may never know, in this life, how God has answered our prayers for others. But he remembers them.

The apostle Peter wrote that God 'delivered just Lot, vexed with the filthy conversation of the wicked'. He concluded, 'The Lord knoweth how to deliver the godly out of temptations'. And Jesus warned that as it was in the days of Lot, so it will be when the Son of man is revealed.

Is there anything that your heart is still clinging to, even though God has called you away from it? Do not linger. Take his hand, and do not look back. And keep praying for the people you love. God remembers your prayers.

Here is today’s question. Lot lingered, and his wife looked back. Is there anything in your life that is pulling your heart back, when God is calling you forward?

Let us pray. Lord God, thank you for your mercy, which takes me by the hand even when I linger. Forgive me for the times my heart has looked back. Help me to follow you without holding on to what you have called me to leave. And thank you that you remember the prayers we pray for others. Amen.
