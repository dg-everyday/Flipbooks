The Living Voice. Genesis, chapter 21. God Hath Made Me to Laugh.

An old woman holds a newborn baby in her arms, and laughs. Not the doubtful laugh she once hid behind a tent door, but a laugh of pure joy. For twenty-five years she has waited for this moment. She is ninety years old, and she is a mother. And everyone who hears about it laughs with her.

In chapter twenty, Abraham went to Gerar, and once again said that Sarah was his sister. God warned King Abimelech in a dream, and Sarah was returned to Abraham. Then Abraham prayed, and God healed Abimelech’s household.

Now, in chapter twenty-one, the promise God made long ago finally comes true. It is a chapter of great joy. But it is also a chapter of pain, because the choices made in chapter sixteen come back to trouble Abraham’s family. And in the middle of that pain, God once again hears a cry in the wilderness.

The chapter begins with a joyful statement of God’s faithfulness.

Sarah conceives, and bears Abraham a son in his old age, at the set time of which God had spoken to him. Abraham calls his son’s name Isaac, which means he laughs, just as God had told him. On the eighth day, Abraham gives Isaac the sign of the covenant, as God had commanded. Abraham is a hundred years old when his son Isaac is born.

And Sarah laughs.

She says, who would have said to Abraham that Sarah would nurse a child? For I have borne him a son in his old age.

The child grows, and is weaned. In those days, that would be when he was about two or three years old. Abraham makes a great feast on the day Isaac is weaned.

But at the feast, Sarah sees Ishmael, the son of Hagar the Egyptian, mocking. Ishmael is now a teenager. The apostle Paul later describes this as persecution, as the older son treating the younger with scorn. Sarah says to Abraham, cast out this bondwoman and her son, for the son of this bondwoman shall not be heir with my son, with Isaac.

This is very grievous in Abraham’s sight, because of his son. He loves Ishmael. But God tells Abraham not to be grieved. Listen to Sarah, God says, for in Isaac shall thy seed be called. And also of the son of the bondwoman will I make a nation, because he is thy seed.

So Abraham rises early, takes bread and a bottle of water, and gives them to Hagar, putting them on her shoulder, and sends her away with the boy. She wanders in the wilderness of Beersheba.

When the water in the bottle is gone, Hagar puts the boy under one of the shrubs. She goes and sits down a good way off, about a bowshot away, because she says, let me not see the death of the child. And she sits there, and lifts up her voice, and weeps.

But God hears.

The angel says, arise, lift up the lad, and hold him in thine hand; for I will make him a great nation. Then God opens her eyes, and she sees a well of water. She goes and fills the bottle with water, and gives the boy a drink.

God is with the boy, and he grows. He lives in the wilderness and becomes an archer. He lives in the wilderness of Paran, and his mother finds him a wife from the land of Egypt.

The chapter ends with Abraham and King Abimelech making a covenant of peace. Abimelech says to Abraham, God is with thee in all that thou doest. They settle a quarrel about a well that Abraham had dug, and swear an oath together. The place is called Beersheba, the well of the oath. And Abraham plants a grove there, and calls on the name of the LORD, the everlasting God.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 1. 'And the LORD visited Sarah as he had said, and the LORD did unto Sarah as he had spoken.' Notice the repetition. As he had said. As he had spoken. God did exactly what he promised, exactly when he promised. It took twenty-five years, and many times Abraham and Sarah struggled to believe. But God was never late, and he never forgot. What God says, he does. You can build your life on his word.

Verse 6. 'And Sarah said, God hath made me to laugh, so that all that hear will laugh with me.' Sarah once laughed in disbelief. Now God had turned her laughter into joy. And her joy spilled over to everyone around her. That is what God does. He takes our doubts and our sorrows, and turns them into joy so great that others share in it too. The name Isaac would remind her, every day, that God had the last laugh.

Verse 17. 'And God heard the voice of the lad; and the angel of God called Hagar out of heaven, and said unto her, What aileth thee, Hagar? fear not; for God hath heard the voice of the lad where he is.' Once again, God heard. Ishmael’s name means God hears, and here God lives up to that name. And notice the words, where he is. God met the boy right where he was, under a bush in the desert, dying of thirst. Wherever you are, however far from where you should be, God can hear you there.

In his letter to the Romans, Paul wrote that Abraham 'staggered not at the promise of God through unbelief; but was strong in faith, giving glory to God'. And a psalm says, 'He maketh the barren woman to keep house, and to be a joyful mother of children. Praise ye the LORD.'

Is there a promise of God that you have been waiting for a long time to see? Do not give up. God is never late. And if you are crying out in a wilderness today, remember that God hears you, right where you are.

Here is today’s question. Sarah said, God hath made me to laugh. When has God turned your sorrow or your doubt into joy?

Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that you always do what you have said. Thank you for turning Sarah’s laughter of doubt into laughter of joy, and for hearing Ishmael’s cry in the wilderness. Help me to wait for your promises with patience, and to trust that you hear me wherever I am. Amen.
