The Living Voice. Genesis, chapter 22. God Will Provide.

A father and his son climb a mountain together. The son carries the wood on his back. The father carries the fire. They walk in silence for a while. Then the boy asks a question that must have broken his father’s heart: my father, here is the fire and the wood, but where is the lamb?

In chapter twenty-one, God kept his promise. Sarah gave birth to Isaac in her old age, and she laughed for joy. Hagar and Ishmael were sent away, and God heard the boy’s cry in the wilderness. And Abraham called on the name of the LORD, the everlasting God, at Beersheba.

Now, in chapter twenty-two, comes the greatest test of Abraham’s life. It is one of the hardest chapters in the Bible to read. But it is also one of the most important. Listen closely, because it points far ahead, to another Father, another Son, and another hill.

After these things, God tests Abraham. He says, Abraham. And Abraham answers, here I am.

Then God says, 'Take now thy son, thine only son Isaac, whom thou lovest, and get thee into the land of Moriah; and offer him there for a burnt offering upon one of the mountains which I will tell thee of.'

Notice how God describes Isaac. Thy son. Thine only son. Whom thou lovest. Each phrase goes deeper. This is the son Abraham waited twenty-five years for. The son through whom every promise of God is to come true. And God asks him to give him up.

The chapter does not tell us what Abraham felt. It only tells us what he did. Abraham rises early, saddles his donkey, takes two young men and Isaac his son, cuts the wood for the offering, and sets out for the place God told him of.

On the third day, Abraham lifts up his eyes, and sees the place far off. He says to the young men, stay here with the donkey, and 'I and the lad will go yonder and worship, and come again to you.' Notice those words. We will come again. Abraham expects to come back with Isaac.

Abraham lays the wood on Isaac, his son. He takes the fire in his hand, and they go both of them together. Then Isaac speaks. My father. And Abraham says, here am I, my son. Isaac says, behold the fire and the wood: but where is the lamb for a burnt offering?

And Abraham gives his answer.

They come to the place God told him of. Abraham builds an altar, lays the wood in order, binds Isaac his son, and lays him on the altar, on the wood. Isaac is old enough to carry a load of wood up a mountain. He could have resisted his old father. But he does not. Abraham stretches out his hand.

And the angel of the LORD calls to him out of heaven. Abraham, Abraham. And he says, here am I. The angel says, lay not thine hand upon the lad, neither do thou any thing unto him: 'for now I know that thou fearest God, seeing thou hast not withheld thy son, thine only son from me.'

Abraham lifts up his eyes, and behind him he sees a ram, caught in a thicket by his horns. Abraham goes and takes the ram, and offers him up for a burnt offering, in the place of his son.

Then Abraham gives that place a name.

The angel of the LORD calls to Abraham out of heaven a second time. God swears an oath by himself, because there is no one greater to swear by. Because you have done this thing, and have not withheld your son, your only son, I will bless you, and multiply your seed as the stars of the heaven, and as the sand upon the sea shore. And your seed shall possess the gate of his enemies.

So Abraham returns to his young men, and they go together to Beersheba. And the chapter ends with news of Abraham’s brother’s family, including a girl named Rebekah. We will meet her soon.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 8. 'And Abraham said, My son, God will provide himself a lamb for a burnt offering: so they went both of them together.' Abraham did not know how God would do it. But he trusted that God would provide. And his words were more true than he knew. God would provide a ram that day on Moriah. And long after, God would provide himself a Lamb, his own Son, for the sins of the world. Notice again, they went both of them together. Father and son, walking up the hill as one.

Verse 14. 'And Abraham called the name of that place Jehovah-jireh: as it is said to this day, In the mount of the LORD it shall be seen.' Jehovah-jireh means the LORD will provide, or the LORD will see to it. God saw Abraham’s need, and met it, at the very last moment. Many believe that this mountain in the land of Moriah is the place where the temple was later built in Jerusalem. And on a hill near Jerusalem, God provided once and for all.

Verse 18. 'And in thy seed shall all the nations of the earth be blessed; because thou hast obeyed my voice.' God repeated the promise he first made in chapter twelve. And the promise would come through Isaac, the son Abraham was willing to give up. Through Isaac’s line, Jesus would one day come, the seed through whom all nations are blessed. Abraham’s obedience did not earn the promise. It showed that he truly trusted the God who made it.

The letter to the Hebrews says that Abraham offered up Isaac, 'Accounting that God was able to raise him up, even from the dead'. And John the Baptist, seeing Jesus, cried out, 'Behold the Lamb of God, which taketh away the sin of the world.' The question Isaac asked, where is the lamb, is answered in Jesus. God 'spared not his own Son, but delivered him up for us all'.

Is there something in your life that you are holding on to more tightly than you hold on to God? Offer it to him, trusting that he will provide. And thank God that he did not spare his own Son, but gave him for you.

Here is today’s question. Abraham told Isaac, God will provide himself a lamb. Where do you need to trust God to provide for you today?

Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that you provided a ram for Abraham, and that you provided your own Son, the Lamb of God, for me. Help me to trust you with everything I love, and to hold nothing back from you. You are Jehovah-jireh, the LORD who provides. Amen.
