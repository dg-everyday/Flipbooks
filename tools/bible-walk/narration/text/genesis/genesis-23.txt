The Living Voice. Genesis, chapter 23. I Am a Stranger and a Sojourner.

An old man kneels beside the body of his wife, and weeps. They have travelled together for decades, from Ur to Haran, from Canaan to Egypt and back. God promised them this whole land. And yet now, at the end of her life, Abraham does not own enough ground even to bury her.

In chapter twenty-two, God tested Abraham, and told him to offer Isaac on a mountain in Moriah. Abraham said, God will provide himself a lamb. At the last moment, the angel of the LORD stopped him, and God provided a ram in Isaac’s place. Abraham called the place Jehovah-jireh, the LORD will provide.

Now, in chapter twenty-three, we come to a quieter chapter, and a sad one. Sarah dies. And Abraham, who has been promised the whole land, has to buy a small piece of it. It may seem like a chapter about a property deal. But it is really a chapter about faith that looks beyond death.

Sarah lives to be a hundred and twenty-seven years old. She is the only woman in the Bible whose age at death is recorded.

Abraham does not hide his grief. He mourns, and he weeps. Faith in God does not mean we do not feel sorrow. It means we do not sorrow without hope.

Then Abraham stands up from before his dead, and speaks to the sons of Heth, the Hittites who live in the land.

The Hittites answer him with great respect. Hear us, my lord, they say. Thou art a mighty prince among us. Choose the best of our burying places, and bury your dead. None of us will refuse you his tomb.

Abraham stands up, and bows himself to the people of the land. He asks them to speak for him to a man named Ephron, the son of Zohar. Ephron owns a field with a cave at the end of it, the cave of Machpelah. Abraham wants to buy it, for the full price, as a burying place among them.

Ephron is sitting among the people at the city gate, where business was done in front of witnesses. He answers loudly, so that everyone can hear. Nay, my lord, hear me. The field I give you, and the cave that is in it, I give it to you. In the presence of my people I give it to you. Bury your dead.

This sounds generous. But it is the polite bargaining of the time. Abraham understands, and bows again. He says, if you will give it, I pray you, hear me. I will give you money for the field. Take it of me, and I will bury my dead there.

Ephron answers, my lord, hearken unto me. The land is worth four hundred shekels of silver. What is that between me and thee? Bury therefore thy dead.

It is a very high price. But Abraham does not argue. He weighs out for Ephron the silver, four hundred shekels, current money with the merchant, in the hearing of the sons of Heth. He wants no doubt, ever, that this land is truly his.

So the field of Ephron in Machpelah, before Mamre, the field and the cave in it, and all the trees in the field and around its borders, are made sure to Abraham, in the presence of all who come in at the gate of the city.

And after this, Abraham buries Sarah his wife in the cave of the field of Machpelah, before Mamre, which is Hebron, in the land of Canaan.

Later, Abraham himself will be buried there. So will Isaac and Rebekah, and Jacob and Leah. This small field is the first piece of the promised land that Abraham’s family ever owned.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 2. 'And Sarah died in Kirjath-arba; the same is Hebron in the land of Canaan: and Abraham came to mourn for Sarah, and to weep for her.' Abraham was the friend of God, a man of great faith. And he wept. Grief is not a lack of faith. It is the price of love. Abraham had walked with Sarah through every promise and every failure. Jesus himself wept at the grave of his friend Lazarus. God does not ask us to hide our tears. He meets us in them.

Verse 4. 'I am a stranger and a sojourner with you: give me a possession of a buryingplace with you, that I may bury my dead out of my sight.' God had promised Abraham the whole land. Yet here he calls himself a stranger and a sojourner, a foreigner, someone just passing through. He did not own the land yet. He was living by a promise. Every believer is like that. We belong to God’s kingdom, but we are not yet home. We live in this world as travellers, looking forward to what God has promised.

Verse 20. 'And the field, and the cave that is therein, were made sure unto Abraham for a possession of a buryingplace by the sons of Heth.' Why did Abraham pay so much, and so carefully, for a grave? Because he believed God’s promise. He wanted Sarah buried in the land God would give to their descendants, not taken back to Haran or Ur. This grave was a statement of faith. One day, this land would belong to their family. And beyond that, Abraham looked for something more.

The letter to the Hebrews says that Abraham and Sarah 'died in faith, not having received the promises, but having seen them afar off'. They 'confessed that they were strangers and pilgrims on the earth.' And it says of them, 'God is not ashamed to be called their God: for he hath prepared for them a city.'

If you are grieving someone you love, do not be afraid to weep. Bring your sorrow to God. And remember that, if you belong to him, this world is not your final home. Live today as a traveller, with your eyes on what God has promised.

Here is today’s question. Abraham called himself a stranger and a sojourner, living by God’s promise. What helps you remember that this world is not your final home?

Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that you are close to the brokenhearted. Comfort those who are grieving today. Help me to live as a pilgrim, holding lightly to this world, and holding firmly to your promises, until I come home to the city you have prepared. Amen.
