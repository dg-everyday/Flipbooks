The Living Voice. Genesis, chapter 24. I Being in the Way, the LORD Led Me.

A tired traveller sits by a well outside a city, with ten thirsty camels kneeling beside him. He has come hundreds of miles on a mission for his master. And he has no idea how to complete it. So he bows his head and prays. Before he has even finished, a young woman comes walking toward the well, with a water jar on her shoulder.

In chapter twenty-three, Sarah died, and Abraham wept for her. He called himself a stranger and a sojourner, and bought the cave of Machpelah from the Hittites to bury her, the first piece of the promised land his family owned.

Now, in chapter twenty-four, Abraham is very old, and Isaac is still not married. God’s promise depends on Isaac having children. So Abraham sends his oldest servant to find Isaac a wife. This is the longest chapter in Genesis, and one of the loveliest. Watch how God guides each step.

Abraham is old, and the LORD has blessed him in all things. He calls the eldest servant of his house, who rules over all that he has. Abraham makes him swear an oath by the LORD, the God of heaven and earth, that he will not take a wife for Isaac from the Canaanites, but will go to Abraham’s own country and kindred.

The servant asks, what if the woman will not come with me? Must I take Isaac back there? Abraham says, beware that thou bring not my son thither again. The LORD, who took me from my father’s house, will send his angel before thee. And if the woman will not come, you are free from this oath.

So the servant takes ten of his master’s camels and many good things, and goes to the city of Nahor, in Mesopotamia. He makes the camels kneel down by a well outside the city, at the time when the women go out to draw water. And he prays.

He asks God for a sign. Let the young woman to whom I say, let down thy pitcher, I pray thee, that I may drink, answer, drink, and I will give thy camels drink also. Let her be the one you have chosen for Isaac. It is a wise sign. A thirsty camel can drink a great deal of water. A woman who offers to water ten camels would be kind, hardworking and generous.

Before he has done speaking, Rebekah comes out with her pitcher on her shoulder. She is the granddaughter of Abraham’s brother Nahor. She goes down to the well and fills her pitcher. The servant runs to meet her and asks for a little water. She says, drink, my lord, and quickly lets down her pitcher. When he has finished drinking, she says, I will draw water for thy camels also, until they have done drinking. And she runs back and forth to the well, until all the camels are satisfied.

The servant watches in silence, wondering whether the LORD has made his journey prosperous. Then he gives her a gold earring and two bracelets, and asks whose daughter she is, and whether there is room in her father’s house. She tells him, and says there is plenty of straw and room to stay. And the man bows his head and worships the LORD.

Rebekah runs home and tells her family. Her brother Laban sees the gold, and hurries out to welcome the servant. Food is set before him. But the servant says, I will not eat, until I have told mine errand. And he tells the whole story, from Abraham’s oath to the prayer at the well. He asks them to tell him plainly whether they will deal kindly and truly with his master.

Laban and Bethuel answer, the thing proceedeth from the LORD. Behold, Rebekah is before thee. Take her, and go. The servant bows to the earth before the LORD. He brings out jewels of silver and gold, and clothing, and gives them to Rebekah, and precious things to her brother and mother.

The next day, the servant asks to leave. Her family wants her to stay at least ten days more. But the servant says, hinder me not, seeing the LORD hath prospered my way. So they ask Rebekah herself.

They send Rebekah away with her nurse, and bless her: be thou the mother of thousands of millions.

Isaac has gone out into the field to meditate. He lifts up his eyes, and sees the camels coming. Rebekah lifts up her eyes, and sees Isaac. She gets down from the camel, and asks, who is this man walking in the field to meet us? The servant says, it is my master. So she takes a veil, and covers herself. The servant tells Isaac everything he has done. And Isaac brings Rebekah into his mother Sarah’s tent, and she becomes his wife, and he loves her. And Isaac is comforted after his mother’s death.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 12. 'And he said, O LORD God of my master Abraham, I pray thee, send me good speed this day, and shew kindness unto my master Abraham.' This is the first recorded prayer for guidance in the Bible. The servant did not rely on his own cleverness. He brought the task to God, simply and specifically. We can pray like that too, about the ordinary decisions of everyday life. God cares about the details.

Verse 27. 'And he said, Blessed be the LORD God of my master Abraham, who hath not left destitute my master of his mercy and his truth: I being in the way, the LORD led me to the house of my master’s brethren.' I being in the way, the LORD led me. The servant was already moving, doing what he had been asked, when God guided him. God often guides us as we walk in obedience, not while we stand still. And notice that the servant stopped to thank God straight away.

Verse 58. 'And they called Rebekah, and said unto her, Wilt thou go with this man? And she said, I will go.' Three short words, full of faith. Rebekah would leave her home and family, to travel far away and marry a man she had never met. Like Abraham before her, she was willing to go where God was leading. God still asks each of us the same question. Will you go? And the right answer is still, I will go.

Proverbs says, 'In all thy ways acknowledge him, and he shall direct thy paths.' And the psalm says, 'The steps of a good man are ordered by the LORD: and he delighteth in his way.'

What decision are you facing? Bring it to God in prayer, simply and honestly, as the servant did. Then keep walking in what you know is right, and watch for him to guide you on the way. When he does, stop and give him thanks.

Here is today’s question. The servant said, I being in the way, the LORD led me. How have you seen God guide you as you walked in obedience to him?

Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that you care about every detail of my life. Guide me in the decisions I face, as you guided Abraham’s servant. Give me a willing heart, like Rebekah, to say, I will go, wherever you lead. And help me never to forget to thank you. Amen.
