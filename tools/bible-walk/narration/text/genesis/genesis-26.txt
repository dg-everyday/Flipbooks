The Living Voice. Genesis, chapter 26. The LORD Hath Made Room for Us.

A man and his servants dig in the dry ground until, at last, water bubbles up. A well. In that dry land, it is like finding treasure. But almost at once, angry neighbours arrive. This water is ours, they say. So the man walks away, and digs again. And again. And again.

In chapter twenty-five, Abraham died in a good old age, and Isaac and Ishmael buried him. Isaac prayed for Rebekah, and she gave birth to twins, Esau and Jacob. And Esau, hungry from the field, sold his birthright to Jacob for a bowl of red stew.

Now, in chapter twenty-six, we spend some time with Isaac. He is the quietest of the great fathers of Israel. There are fewer stories about him than about Abraham or Jacob. But in this chapter, we see God keeping his promise to Abraham’s son, and we see Isaac as a man of peace.

There is a famine in the land, like the famine in Abraham’s days. Isaac goes to Abimelech, king of the Philistines, in Gerar. Perhaps he is on his way to Egypt, as his father once went. But the LORD appears to him, and says, go not down into Egypt. Live in the land I will tell you of.

Then God repeats to Isaac the promises he made to Abraham. I will multiply thy seed as the stars of heaven, and in thy seed shall all the nations of the earth be blessed, because Abraham obeyed my voice, and kept my commandments.

So Isaac lives in Gerar. But there, he makes the same mistake as his father. When the men of the place ask about his wife, he says, she is my sister, because he is afraid they will kill him to take Rebekah, who is beautiful. After a long time, King Abimelech looks out of a window, and sees Isaac being affectionate with Rebekah. He calls Isaac, and says, behold, of a surety she is thy wife. Why did you say, she is my sister? One of the people might easily have taken her, and you would have brought guilt upon us. So Abimelech commands all his people, he that toucheth this man or his wife shall surely be put to death.

Then Isaac sows seed in that land, in a time of famine, and in the same year he receives a hundredfold. 'and the LORD blessed him.' Isaac grows very great. He has flocks, herds and many servants. And the Philistines envy him.

The Philistines had filled up with earth all the wells that Abraham’s servants had dug. Abimelech says to Isaac, go from us, for thou art much mightier than we. So Isaac moves to the valley of Gerar, and there he digs again the wells of his father Abraham, and calls them by the names his father had given them.

Isaac’s servants dig in the valley, and find a well of springing water. But the herdmen of Gerar quarrel with Isaac’s herdmen, saying, the water is ours. So Isaac calls the well Esek, which means strife. They dig another well, and they quarrel over that one too. He calls it Sitnah, which means hatred, or opposition.

Isaac does not fight back. He moves on.

From there Isaac goes up to Beersheba.

There Isaac builds an altar, and calls upon the name of the LORD, and pitches his tent. And there his servants dig another well.

Then Abimelech comes to visit Isaac, with his friend and the captain of his army. Isaac asks, why come ye to me, seeing ye hate me, and have sent me away? They answer, 'We saw certainly that the LORD was with thee'. Let there be an oath between us, they say, that you will do us no harm, as we have done nothing but good to you. So Isaac makes them a feast. They rise up early, and swear an oath together, and they depart from him in peace.

The same day, Isaac’s servants come and tell him about the well they have dug. We have found water. He calls it Shebah, the oath. And the city is called Beersheba to this day.

The chapter ends with a note of sorrow. Esau, at forty years old, marries two Hittite women, who are a grief of mind to Isaac and to Rebekah.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 3. 'Sojourn in this land, and I will be with thee, and will bless thee; for unto thee, and unto thy seed, I will give all these countries, and I will perform the oath which I sware unto Abraham thy father;' God’s promise did not die with Abraham. It passed to Isaac. And God tells Isaac to stay, even in a time of famine. Sometimes God’s blessing is found not by running away from hard places, but by staying where he has put us. I will be with thee is the promise that makes any place bearable.

Verse 22. 'And he removed from thence, and digged another well; and for that they strove not: and he called the name of it Rehoboth; and he said, For now the LORD hath made room for us, and we shall be fruitful in the land.' Isaac could have fought for every well. Instead, he kept moving, and kept digging, and trusted God. And at last God gave him Rehoboth, which means wide spaces, or room. When we refuse to fight for our own way, and trust God instead, he makes room for us in his time.

Verse 24. 'And the LORD appeared unto him the same night, and said, I am the God of Abraham thy father: fear not, for I am with thee, and will bless thee, and multiply thy seed for my servant Abraham’s sake.' After all the quarrels over wells, God spoke words of comfort: fear not, for I am with thee. Isaac had been pushed from place to place. Perhaps he felt tired and alone. God met him with the same promise he gave his father. The God of Abraham is the God of every generation that trusts him.

Jesus said, 'Blessed are the meek: for they shall inherit the earth.' And Proverbs says, 'When a man’s ways please the LORD, he maketh even his enemies to be at peace with him.' That is exactly what happened to Isaac, when Abimelech came to make peace.

Is there a quarrel you are tempted to fight to the bitter end? Ask God whether it is worth it, or whether he is calling you to move on and dig another well. And trust that he will make room for you.

Here is today’s question. Isaac kept moving and digging new wells until the LORD made room for him. Where do you need to trust God, instead of fighting for your own way?

Let us pray. Lord God of Abraham and Isaac, thank you that you are with me, and that I need not fear. Make me a person of peace, who does not have to win every quarrel. When I am pushed from place to place, help me to keep trusting you, until you make room for me. Amen.
