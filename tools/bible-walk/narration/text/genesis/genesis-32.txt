The Living Voice. Genesis, chapter 32. I Will Not Let Thee Go, Except Thou Bless Me.

In the dark, by the bank of a river, two figures grapple on the ground. Hour after hour they struggle, neither one giving way. One of them is Jacob. The other is a mysterious stranger. As the first light appears in the sky, the stranger touches Jacob’s hip, and it is thrown out of joint. But Jacob will not let go.

In chapter thirty-one, God told Jacob to return to the land of his fathers. Jacob slipped away from Laban with his family and flocks. Laban chased him, but God warned Laban in a dream. The two men made a covenant at Mizpah, saying, the LORD watch between me and thee, and parted in peace.

Now, in chapter thirty-two, Jacob is heading home. But home means facing Esau, the brother he cheated twenty years ago, the brother who swore to kill him. This is the meeting that changes Jacob for ever.

As Jacob goes on his way, the angels of God meet him. When Jacob sees them, he says, this is God’s host, God’s army, and he calls the place Mahanaim, which means two camps. Once again, as at Bethel, God shows Jacob that he is not alone.

Jacob sends messengers ahead to Esau, in the land of Edom. He tells them to say, thy servant Jacob saith thus: I have stayed with Laban until now. I have oxen, donkeys, flocks and servants. I have sent to tell my lord, that I may find grace in thy sight.

The messengers return with alarming news. We came to your brother Esau, and he is coming to meet you, with four hundred men.

Jacob is greatly afraid and distressed. He divides the people, flocks and herds into two camps. If Esau comes to one camp and attacks it, he thinks, the other camp may escape.

Then Jacob prays. It is the first real prayer we hear from him. He begins, O God of my father Abraham, and God of my father Isaac, the LORD which saidst unto me, return unto thy country, and to thy kindred, and I will deal well with thee.

Then Jacob asks plainly for help. Deliver me, I pray thee, from the hand of my brother, from the hand of Esau, for I fear him. And he reminds God of his promise: thou saidst, I will surely do thee good, and make thy seed as the sand of the sea.

After praying, Jacob prepares a large gift for Esau: two hundred she goats and twenty he goats, two hundred ewes and twenty rams, thirty milk camels with their colts, forty cows and ten bulls, twenty she donkeys and ten foals. He sends them ahead in separate droves, with space between each one. Each servant is to say, these belong to thy servant Jacob; it is a present sent unto my lord Esau. Jacob hopes, I will appease him with the present that goeth before me, and afterward I will see his face.

Then Jacob takes his two wives, his two maidservants and his eleven sons, and sends them across the ford of the river Jabbok, with everything he has. 'And Jacob was left alone; and there wrestled a man with him until the breaking of the day.'

When the man sees that he cannot prevail against Jacob, he touches the hollow of Jacob’s thigh, and the hip is put out of joint as they wrestle. And the man says, let me go, for the day is breaking.

The man asks, what is thy name? And Jacob answers, Jacob. For Jacob to say his own name is a confession. Jacob means the supplanter, the heel-grabber, the cheat. The last time someone asked who he was, Jacob lied and said, I am Esau. This time, he tells the truth.

Then the man gives him a new name.

Jacob asks, tell me, I pray thee, thy name. The man says, wherefore is it that thou dost ask after my name? And he blesses him there.

Jacob calls the name of the place Peniel, which means the face of God. 'for I have seen God face to face, and my life is preserved.' And as he passes over Peniel, the sun rises upon him, and he limps upon his thigh.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 10. 'I am not worthy of the least of all the mercies, and of all the truth, which thou hast shewed unto thy servant; for with my staff I passed over this Jordan; and now I am become two bands.' This is a very different Jacob. Twenty years before, he crossed the Jordan with nothing but a staff. Now he has a large family and great wealth. And instead of boasting, he says, I am not worthy. Jacob is learning that everything he has is a gift of God’s mercy. True prayer begins with humility.

Verse 26. 'And he said, Let me go, for the day breaketh. And he said, I will not let thee go, except thou bless me.' Jacob had spent his whole life grabbing for blessing, by trickery and scheming. Now, crippled and unable to fight, he simply clings, and asks. This is the moment Jacob stops trying to win, and starts holding on to God. Sometimes God has to weaken us, so that we stop relying on ourselves and cling to him.

Verse 28. 'And he said, Thy name shall be called no more Jacob, but Israel: for as a prince hast thou power with God and with men, and hast prevailed.' Israel means he struggles with God, or God prevails. Jacob prevailed not by overpowering God, but by refusing to let go of him. From now on, his descendants would carry this name. The whole nation of Israel is named after a limping man who held on to God and would not let go.

The prophet Hosea looked back on this struggle: 'Yea, he had power over the angel, and prevailed: he wept, and made supplication unto him'. And Paul heard the Lord say, 'My grace is sufficient for thee: for my strength is made perfect in weakness.'

Is there something you are struggling with God about? Do not give up. Hold on to him, and ask for his blessing. And if God has weakened you in some way, remember that he may be teaching you to lean on him instead of on yourself.

Here is today’s question. Jacob said, I will not let thee go, except thou bless me. What is one thing you need to keep bringing to God in prayer, without letting go?

Let us pray. Lord God, I am not worthy of the least of your mercies. Thank you for all you have given me. When I am afraid, teach me to pray. When I am weak, teach me to cling to you. Change me, as you changed Jacob, and bless me, Lord, for I will not let you go. Amen.
