The Living Voice. Genesis, chapter 38. She Hath Been More Righteous Than I.

A man sits in judgment, angry. He has just heard that his widowed daughter-in-law is expecting a child, and he demands that she be punished. Then a servant brings him three objects: a seal, a cord, and a walking staff. He looks at them, and his face goes pale. They are his own.

In chapter thirty-seven, Joseph’s brothers hated him for his coat and his dreams. They threw him into a pit, and Judah suggested selling him to traders going to Egypt. They dipped his coat in blood, and Jacob wept, believing his son was dead. And Joseph was sold to Potiphar, an officer of Pharaoh.

Now, in chapter thirty-eight, the story leaves Joseph in Egypt, and follows Judah, the brother who sold him. It is an uncomfortable chapter. But it is in the Bible for a reason. It shows how far Judah had fallen, and the beginning of his change. And it is part of the family story that leads, in the end, to Jesus.

At that time, Judah goes down from his brothers, and turns aside to stay with a man named Hirah, from Adullam. There he marries a Canaanite woman, the daughter of a man named Shuah. They have three sons: Er, Onan and Shelah.

Judah finds a wife for Er, his firstborn. Her name is Tamar. But Er is wicked in the sight of the LORD, and he dies.

In those days, there was a custom that if a man died without children, his brother should marry the widow, so that she could have a son to carry on her husband’s name and inheritance. So Judah tells his second son, Onan, to marry Tamar. But Onan knows that the child would not count as his own. He selfishly refuses to give Tamar a child, while still taking her as his wife. What he does displeases the LORD, and he dies too.

Judah has one son left, Shelah. He tells Tamar, go back and live as a widow in your father’s house, until Shelah my son is grown. But secretly Judah is afraid that Shelah will die also, like his brothers. He has no intention of keeping his promise. So Tamar goes and lives in her father’s house.

Years pass. Judah’s wife dies. After he is comforted, he goes up to Timnath with his friend Hirah, to the shearing of his sheep. Tamar hears of it. She has seen that Shelah is grown, and she has not been given to him as his wife. She has been left with no husband, no child and no future.

So Tamar takes off her widow’s clothes, covers herself with a veil, and sits by the road to Timnath. When Judah sees her, he does not recognise her, because her face is covered. He thinks she is a woman of the roadside, and he goes to her. As payment, he promises to send her a young goat from his flock. She asks for a pledge until it comes, and he gives her his signet, the seal he used to sign his name, with its cord, and the staff in his hand. Then she goes home, and puts on her widow’s clothes again.

Judah sends the young goat by his friend, to get back his pledge. But the woman cannot be found. The people of the place say there has been no such woman there. Judah says, let her keep the things, lest we be shamed.

About three months later, Judah is told, Tamar your daughter-in-law has been unfaithful, and she is expecting a child. And Judah, without a moment’s thought for his own behaviour, says, bring her forth, and let her be put to death.

As she is brought out, Tamar sends a message to her father-in-law.

Judah recognises them. And the man who has been so quick to judge someone else is suddenly confronted with his own sin.

When the time comes for Tamar to give birth, there are twins. As she is in labour, one baby puts out his hand, and the midwife ties a scarlet thread around it, saying, this came out first. But he draws back his hand, and his brother comes out first instead.

The second twin, with the scarlet thread on his hand, is called Zarah.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 25. 'When she was brought forth, she sent to her father in law, saying, By the man, whose these are, am I with child: and she said, Discern, I pray thee, whose are these, the signet, and bracelets, and staff.' Tamar did not shame Judah in public. She sent the items privately, and let him decide what to do. The word discern is the same word used in the last chapter, when the brothers brought Jacob the coat dipped in blood and said, know now whether it be thy son’s coat. Judah had deceived his father with a piece of clothing. Now he is confronted by his own signet and staff.

Verse 26. 'And Judah acknowledged them, and said, She hath been more righteous than I; because that I gave her not to Shelah my son.' This is the turning point in Judah’s life. For the first time, he admits that he was wrong. He had broken his promise, and treated Tamar unjustly. Instead of hiding his sin, he confesses it in front of everyone. This is the beginning of a changed man. In a few chapters, we will see Judah offer his own life to save his brother Benjamin. Real change begins with honest confession.

Verse 29. 'And it came to pass, as he drew back his hand, that, behold, his brother came out: and she said, How hast thou broken forth? this breach be upon thee: therefore his name was called Pharez.' Pharez means breaking forth. Once again in Genesis, the younger comes before the elder. And from this child, born out of a sad and messy story, would come the family line of King David. God’s grace broke through. He did not wait for a perfect family to carry his promise. He worked through broken people, and made something beautiful.

The book of Ruth ends with these words: 'Now these are the generations of Pharez: Pharez begat Hezron', and the line goes on to David. And Matthew’s list of the family of Jesus includes this very name: 'And Judas begat Phares and Zara of Thamar'. Tamar, a wronged and overlooked woman, is named in the family line of the Messiah.

Is there a sin you have been quick to judge in others, but slow to see in yourself? Ask God to show you honestly. And if you need to say, I was wrong, say it today. Confession is the beginning of change.

Here is today’s question. Judah said, she hath been more righteous than I. Is there someone you need to admit you have treated unfairly?

Let us pray. Lord God, forgive me for being quick to judge others and slow to see my own sin. Give me the honesty to say, I was wrong. Thank you that you work through broken people and broken families, and that your grace breaks through, even into the messiest stories. Amen.
