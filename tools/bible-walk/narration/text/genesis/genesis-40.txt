The Living Voice. Genesis, chapter 40. Do Not Interpretations Belong to God?.

Two of Pharaoh’s officials sit in a prison cell, looking troubled. Each one has had a strange dream, and neither can understand it. A young Hebrew prisoner, who serves them, notices their sad faces. He asks a simple question: why do you look so sad today? It is a question that will change his life, though not for two more long years.

In chapter thirty-nine, Joseph served in Potiphar’s house, and the LORD was with him. He refused to sin against God, and fled from temptation. But he was falsely accused and thrown into prison. Even there, the LORD was with Joseph, and the keeper of the prison put him in charge of all the prisoners.

Now, in chapter forty, God begins to open a door for Joseph, though Joseph cannot see it yet. Dreams first got Joseph into trouble with his brothers. Now dreams will begin to lead him out.

After these things, two officials of the king of Egypt offend their lord. One is the chief of the butlers, who served the king his wine. The other is the chief of the bakers. Pharaoh is angry with them, and puts them in the prison where Joseph is bound. The captain of the guard puts Joseph in charge of them, and he serves them. They stay in custody for some time.

Then, at the same time, each of them dreams a dream, each dream with its own meaning. When Joseph comes in to them the next day, he sees that they are sad. He asks them, wherefore look ye so sadly to day? Even in his own suffering, Joseph notices the sorrow of others.

They tell him the reason.

So the chief butler tells his dream. In my dream, behold, a vine was before me. In the vine were three branches. It seemed to bud, and its blossoms shot forth, and the clusters brought forth ripe grapes. Pharaoh’s cup was in my hand. I took the grapes, pressed them into Pharaoh’s cup, and gave the cup into Pharaoh’s hand.

Joseph says, this is the interpretation of it. The three branches are three days. Within three days Pharaoh will lift up your head, and restore you to your place. You will give Pharaoh’s cup into his hand, as you did before, when you were his butler.

Then Joseph makes a request.

Joseph explains, 'For indeed I was stolen away out of the land of the Hebrews: and here also have I done nothing that they should put me into the dungeon.' He does not speak bitterly about his brothers, or about Potiphar’s wife. He simply states the truth: I have done nothing wrong.

When the chief baker sees that the interpretation is good, he tells his dream too. I also was in my dream, and behold, I had three white baskets on my head. In the top basket was all manner of baked food for Pharaoh, and the birds ate them out of the basket on my head.

Joseph answers him honestly, though the news is hard. The three baskets are three days. Within three days Pharaoh will lift up your head from off you. Pharaoh will put the baker to death, and the birds will come. Joseph does not soften the truth, or pretend it is good news. He tells it plainly.

On the third day, which is Pharaoh’s birthday, Pharaoh makes a feast for all his servants. He lifts up the head of the chief butler and the head of the chief baker among his servants. He restores the chief butler to his butlership, and the butler gives the cup into Pharaoh’s hand again. But the chief baker is put to death, just as Joseph had said.

Everything happens exactly as Joseph said it would. And now, surely, the butler will speak up for him.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 8. 'And they said unto him, We have dreamed a dream, and there is no interpreter of it. And Joseph said unto them, Do not interpretations belong to God? tell me them, I pray you.' Joseph did not claim any special power for himself. He pointed straight to God. Even in prison, even after everything that had happened to him, Joseph still trusted God and gave him the glory. And notice that Joseph’s own dreams had not yet come true. He was still waiting. But he still believed that God speaks, and that God is in control.

Verse 14. 'But think on me when it shall be well with thee, and shew kindness, I pray thee, unto me, and make mention of me unto Pharaoh, and bring me out of this house:' Joseph trusted God, but he also asked for help. There is nothing wrong with that. Faith does not mean doing nothing. Joseph saw an opportunity, and asked to be remembered. He had been forgotten by his family, and he longed for someone to speak up for him. It is a very human cry: please, do not forget me.

Verse 23. 'Yet did not the chief butler remember Joseph, but forgat him.' This may be one of the saddest verses in Genesis. Once he was free and comfortable, the butler forgot all about the young man who had helped him. Joseph would wait two more full years in prison. People often forget us. Even people we have helped. But God never forgets. While the butler forgot, God was still preparing the perfect moment to bring Joseph out.

Isaiah heard God say that even a mother might forget her child, 'yea, they may forget, yet will I not forget thee.' And a psalm says, 'Wait on the LORD: be of good courage, and he shall strengthen thine heart: wait, I say, on the LORD.'

Has someone forgotten you, or forgotten a kindness you showed them? Bring that disappointment to God, who never forgets. And think of someone who helped you. Is there a way you could remember them today, and show them kindness in return?

Here is today’s question. Joseph helped the butler, but the butler forgot him. How do you keep trusting God when people forget you, or when his answer seems slow in coming?

Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that even when people forget me, you never do. Help me, like Joseph, to notice the sorrow of others, to give you the glory, and to keep trusting you while I wait. And help me to remember those who have been kind to me. Amen.
