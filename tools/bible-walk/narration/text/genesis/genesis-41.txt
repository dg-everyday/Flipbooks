The Living Voice. Genesis, chapter 41. God Shall Give Pharaoh an Answer of Peace.

When the day begins, a prisoner is in a dungeon. By the end of the day, he is the second most powerful man in Egypt. He has been shaved, dressed in fine linen, given a gold chain and the king’s own ring, and is riding in a royal chariot while crowds bow before him. Thirteen years after his brothers sold him, everything changes in a single day.

In chapter forty, Joseph interpreted the dreams of Pharaoh’s butler and baker in prison. Just as he said, the butler was restored, and the baker was put to death. Joseph asked the butler to remember him. But the butler forgot him.

Now, in chapter forty-one, two full years have passed. Joseph is still in prison. But God has not forgotten him. Watch how God brings together a king’s dreams, a butler’s memory, and a prisoner’s faith, at exactly the right moment.

At the end of two full years, Pharaoh dreams. He stands by the river Nile, and out of the river come seven cows, fat and fine looking, and they feed in a meadow. Then seven other cows come up after them, ugly and thin, and they eat up the seven fat cows. Pharaoh wakes. He sleeps again, and dreams a second time. Seven ears of corn come up on one stalk, plump and good. Then seven thin ears, blasted by the east wind, spring up after them, and swallow up the seven good ears.

Pharaoh’s spirit is troubled. He calls for all the magicians and wise men of Egypt, and tells them his dreams. But no one can interpret them.

Then the chief butler speaks. I do remember my faults this day, he says. And he tells Pharaoh about the young Hebrew in prison who interpreted his dream, and how it all came true.

Pharaoh sends and calls Joseph, and they bring him hastily out of the dungeon. Joseph shaves himself, changes his clothes, and comes in to Pharaoh. Pharaoh says, I have dreamed a dream, and there is none that can interpret it. And I have heard it said of you, that you can understand a dream and interpret it.

Joseph’s answer is the first thing he says to Pharaoh.

Pharaoh tells Joseph his dreams. And Joseph says, the dream of Pharaoh is one. God has shewed Pharaoh what he is about to do. The seven good cows and the seven good ears are seven years. The seven thin cows and the seven empty ears are seven years of famine. Behold, there come seven years of great plenty throughout all the land of Egypt. And after them shall arise seven years of famine, so severe that all the plenty will be forgotten. The dream was given twice, because the thing is established by God, and God will shortly bring it to pass.

Then Joseph, the prisoner, gives the king advice. Let Pharaoh look for a man discreet and wise, and set him over the land of Egypt. Let him appoint officers to gather a fifth part of the harvest in the good years, and store up corn in the cities, so the people will not die in the famine.

The plan is good in the eyes of Pharaoh and all his servants.

Pharaoh says to Joseph, forasmuch as God hath shewed thee all this, there is none so discreet and wise as thou art. 'Thou shalt be over my house, and according unto thy word shall all my people be ruled: only in the throne will I be greater than thou.'

Pharaoh takes off his ring and puts it on Joseph’s hand. He dresses him in fine linen, and puts a gold chain around his neck. He makes him ride in the second chariot, and they cry before him, bow the knee. Pharaoh gives him an Egyptian name, and a wife, Asenath. Joseph is thirty years old when he stands before Pharaoh.

In the seven plenteous years, the earth brings forth by handfuls. Joseph gathers up the food in every city. He gathers corn as the sand of the sea, so much that they stop counting it.

Before the famine comes, two sons are born to Joseph. He calls the firstborn Manasseh, which sounds like making to forget. 'For God, said he, hath made me forget all my toil, and all my father’s house.' And he names the second son too.

The seven years of plenty come to an end, and the seven years of famine begin, just as Joseph said. The famine is in all lands, but in Egypt there is bread. When the people cry to Pharaoh for bread, he says, go unto Joseph; what he saith to you, do. Joseph opens the storehouses and sells to the Egyptians. 'And all countries came into Egypt to Joseph for to buy corn'. And among them, soon, will be ten men from Canaan.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 16. 'And Joseph answered Pharaoh, saying, It is not in me: God shall give Pharaoh an answer of peace.' This was Joseph’s big chance. He could have tried to impress the king. Instead, his very first words pointed away from himself. It is not in me. Thirteen years of slavery and prison had not made Joseph bitter or proud. They had made him humble, and made him trust God even more. When you have the opportunity to shine, remember to point to God.

Verse 38. 'And Pharaoh said unto his servants, Can we find such a one as this is, a man in whom the Spirit of God is?' A pagan king recognised that the Spirit of God was in Joseph. Joseph’s faith was not hidden. It showed in his wisdom, his humility and his character. When God’s Spirit is at work in someone, other people notice, even people who do not know God. Joseph’s life spoke for his God.

Verse 52. 'And the name of the second called he Ephraim: For God hath caused me to be fruitful in the land of my affliction.' Joseph did not pretend that Egypt had been easy. He called it the land of my affliction. But he also saw that God had made him fruitful there. Both things were true. God does not always take us out of hard places. Sometimes he makes us fruitful right in the middle of them.

Joseph was raised from the prison to the right hand of the king, to save many lives. Paul wrote of Jesus, who humbled himself, 'Wherefore God also hath highly exalted him, and given him a name which is above every name: That at the name of Jesus every knee should bow'. And Peter wrote, 'Humble yourselves therefore under the mighty hand of God, that he may exalt you in due time'.

Are you in a long season of waiting, like Joseph in prison? God’s timing is perfect, even when it feels slow. Keep trusting him, and keep being faithful where you are. And when good things come, remember to say, it is not in me. It is God.

Here is today’s question. Joseph named his son Ephraim, saying, God hath caused me to be fruitful in the land of my affliction. How has God made you fruitful in a hard place?

Let us pray. Lord God, your timing is perfect, even when it feels slow. Thank you that you never forgot Joseph, and you have not forgotten me. Keep me humble, so that I always point to you. And make me fruitful, even in the land of my affliction. Amen.
