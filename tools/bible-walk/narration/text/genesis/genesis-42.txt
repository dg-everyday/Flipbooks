The Living Voice. Genesis, chapter 42. We Are Verily Guilty Concerning Our Brother.

Ten weary travellers from Canaan stand before the governor of Egypt, and bow with their faces to the ground. He is dressed like an Egyptian lord, and he speaks to them through an interpreter. They have no idea who he is. But he knows exactly who they are. These are the brothers who sold him as a slave, twenty years ago.

In chapter forty-one, Joseph interpreted Pharaoh’s dreams: seven years of plenty, then seven years of famine. Pharaoh made Joseph ruler over all Egypt. Joseph stored up grain like the sand of the sea, and when the famine came, all countries came to Egypt to buy corn.

Now, in chapter forty-two, the famine reaches Canaan, and Joseph’s family is hungry. His dreams are about to come true. But Joseph does not rush to reveal himself. He wants to know whether his brothers have changed. And God is about to stir up something in the brothers’ hearts that has been buried for twenty years.

Jacob sees that there is corn in Egypt. He says to his sons, why do you look at one another? I have heard that there is corn in Egypt. Go down and buy for us from there, that we may live, and not die.

So Joseph’s ten brothers go down to buy corn in Egypt. But Jacob does not send Benjamin, Joseph’s younger brother, Rachel’s other son. He says, lest peradventure mischief befall him. Jacob is still holding on to his favourite.

Then the brothers arrive.

Joseph sees his brothers, and knows them, but makes himself strange to them, and speaks roughly. Where do you come from? From the land of Canaan, to buy food. Joseph knows his brothers, but they do not know him. He was a teenager when they last saw him. Now he is a grown man, with an Egyptian name, Egyptian clothes, and an Egyptian voice.

And Joseph remembers the dreams which he dreamed of them.

He says to them, you are spies. You have come to see the weakness of the land. They say, no, my lord, we are come to buy food. We are all one man’s sons. We are true men. We are twelve brothers, the sons of one man in the land of Canaan. The youngest is with our father today, and one is not.

One is not. That is how they speak of Joseph, to Joseph himself.

Joseph says, by this you shall be proved. Send one of you to bring your youngest brother here, and the rest stay in prison. And he puts them all together in custody for three days.

On the third day, Joseph says, do this, and live, for I fear God. Let one of you be bound in prison, and the rest carry corn home to your hungry families. But bring your youngest brother to me. So shall your words be verified.

And then the brothers say something to one another, in Hebrew, not knowing that Joseph understands every word.

Reuben answers them, did I not tell you, do not sin against the child? And you would not hear. Therefore, behold, his blood is required.

They do not know that Joseph understands them, because he has been speaking through an interpreter. And Joseph turns away from them, and weeps. Then he comes back, and takes Simeon from among them, and binds him before their eyes.

Joseph commands his servants to fill their sacks with corn, to restore every man’s money into his sack, and to give them food for the way. They load their donkeys and set off. At the inn, one of them opens his sack to feed his donkey, and sees his money in the mouth of his sack. Their hearts fail them. They turn trembling to one another, and say, 'What is this that God hath done unto us?'

They come home to Jacob in Canaan, and tell him everything. As they empty their sacks, every man finds his bundle of money in his sack. And they and their father are afraid.

Then Jacob speaks in his grief.

Reuben says to his father, you may put my two sons to death if I do not bring Benjamin back to you. Put him in my hands, and I will bring him to you again. But Jacob says, my son shall not go down with you. His brother is dead, and he alone is left. If harm comes to him on the way, then you will bring down my grey hairs with sorrow to the grave.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 6. 'And Joseph was the governor over the land, and he it was that sold to all the people of the land: and Joseph’s brethren came, and bowed down themselves before him with their faces to the earth.' Twenty years before, Joseph dreamed that his brothers’ sheaves bowed down to his sheaf. They mocked him, and sold him to stop his dreams. Now here they are, bowing before him with their faces to the earth. God’s word came true, exactly as he had shown. What God has spoken, he will do, even if it takes many years.

Verse 21. 'And they said one to another, We are verily guilty concerning our brother, in that we saw the anguish of his soul, when he besought us, and we would not hear; therefore is this distress come upon us.' For twenty years, the brothers had carried a secret. Now, under pressure, it all came flooding back. They remembered Joseph’s face, his cries from the pit. Guilt that is buried does not go away. But God was not stirring up their guilt to destroy them. He was bringing it to the surface so that it could be healed.

Verse 36. 'And Jacob their father said unto them, Me have ye bereaved of my children: Joseph is not, and Simeon is not, and ye will take Benjamin away: all these things are against me.' Jacob saw only loss. All these things are against me, he said. But in fact, everything was working for him. Joseph was alive. Simeon was safe. And God was about to bring the whole family back together. Sometimes, when everything seems to be against us, God is quietly working everything together for our good.

Paul wrote, 'And we know that all things work together for good to them that love God, to them who are the called according to his purpose.' And a psalm says, 'When I kept silence, my bones waxed old through my roaring all the day long.' Then, 'I acknowledged my sin unto thee… and thou forgavest the iniquity of my sin.'

Is there a guilt you have been carrying for a long time? Bring it to God. He is not stirring it up to crush you, but to heal you. And if it feels as if everything is against you, ask God to help you trust that he is at work, even now.

Here is today’s question. Jacob said, all these things are against me, but God was working everything for good. Where do you need to trust that God is working, even when it looks like everything is against you?

Let us pray. Lord God, you see the guilt I carry and the sorrow I feel. Thank you that you bring hidden things to light, not to crush me, but to heal me. When it seems that everything is against me, help me to trust that you are working all things together for good. Amen.
