The Living Voice. Genesis, chapter 44. How Shall I Go Up to My Father?.

Eleven brothers are on the road home, laughing with relief. They have food, Simeon is free, and Benjamin is safe. Then they hear horses behind them. The governor’s steward rides up and accuses them of stealing their master’s silver cup. They are sure it is a mistake. They open their sacks, one by one, from the oldest to the youngest. And the cup is in Benjamin’s sack.

In chapter forty-three, Jacob finally let Benjamin go to Egypt, after Judah promised, I will be surety for him. The brothers were brought to Joseph’s house for a feast. Joseph wept in secret when he saw Benjamin, and gave Benjamin five times as much as the others. And the brothers ate and were merry with him.

Now, in chapter forty-four, comes Joseph’s final test. Twenty years before, the brothers sold a favoured son of Rachel into slavery. Now Joseph gives them a chance to do it again. Will they abandon Benjamin to save themselves? The answer comes in one of the most moving speeches in the Bible.

Joseph commands the steward of his house. Fill the men’s sacks with food, as much as they can carry, and put every man’s money in his sack’s mouth. And put my cup, the silver cup, in the sack’s mouth of the youngest.

As soon as it is light, the men are sent away with their donkeys. When they are not yet far from the city, Joseph tells his steward, follow after the men, and when you overtake them, say, wherefore have ye rewarded evil for good?

The steward overtakes them, and says these words. They answer, why does my lord say this? God forbid that thy servants should do such a thing. We brought back the money we found in our sacks. How then would we steal silver or gold out of your lord’s house? With whomever of us it is found, let him die, and we also will be my lord’s slaves.

The steward says, let it be as you say. But only the one with whom it is found shall be my servant, and the rest of you shall be blameless.

They quickly take down every man’s sack and open it. The steward searches, beginning at the eldest, and ending at the youngest. And the cup is found in Benjamin’s sack.

Then they tear their clothes. Every man loads his donkey, and they all return to the city.

Notice what they do not do. They could have let Benjamin go back alone, and gone home with their food. Twenty years before, they would have done exactly that. But this time, they all go back together.

Judah and his brothers come to Joseph’s house. He is still there, and they fall before him on the ground. Joseph says, what deed is this that ye have done? Did you not know that such a man as I can certainly find things out?

And Judah answers.

Joseph says, God forbid that I should do so. Only the man in whose hand the cup is found shall be my servant. As for you, go up in peace to your father.

Then Judah comes near to him, and speaks. Oh my lord, let thy servant, I pray thee, speak a word in my lord’s ears.

He tells the whole story. How the governor asked about their father and brother. How their father did not want to let Benjamin go. Judah repeats his father’s words: you know that my wife bore me two sons. One went out from me, and I said, surely he is torn in pieces, and I have not seen him since. If you take this one also, and harm comes to him, you will bring down my grey hairs with sorrow to the grave.

Judah speaks of his father with tenderness. His life is bound up in the lad’s life, Judah says. When he sees that the lad is not with us, he will die. For thy servant became surety for the lad unto my father.

And then Judah makes his offer.

This is the brother who once said, let us sell him. Now he says, let me stay instead.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 16. 'And Judah said, What shall we say unto my lord? what shall we speak? or how shall we clear ourselves? God hath found out the iniquity of thy servants:' The brothers had not stolen the cup. But Judah was not thinking about the cup. He was thinking about the sin they had committed twenty years before. God hath found out our iniquity. He did not make excuses. He confessed. Sometimes God allows hard things to come into our lives so that we will face sin we have hidden for years.

Verse 33. 'Now therefore, I pray thee, let thy servant abide instead of the lad a bondman to my lord; and let the lad go up with his brethren.' Judah offered to become a slave in Benjamin’s place. He would give up his freedom, his family and his future, so that his younger brother could go home. This is what real change looks like. The man who sold one brother as a slave now offered to become a slave for another. It is one of the clearest pictures in the Old Testament of someone taking another’s place.

Verse 34. 'For how shall I go up to my father, and the lad be not with me? lest peradventure I see the evil that shall come on my father.' Twenty years before, the brothers did not care about their father’s grief. They watched him tear his clothes and weep, and said nothing. Now Judah cannot bear the thought of his father’s sorrow. His heart has been changed. He has learned to love his father, and even to accept his father’s special love for Rachel’s sons. God had softened a hard heart.

The apostle Peter wrote of Jesus, 'For Christ also hath once suffered for sins, the just for the unjust, that he might bring us to God'. Judah, from whose tribe Jesus would come, offered to take his brother’s place. Jesus, the Lion of the tribe of Judah, did take our place.

Is there an old sin that God is bringing back to your mind? Do not hide it. Confess it, as Judah did. And ask God to change your heart, so that you care more about others than about yourself.

Here is today’s question. Judah offered to take Benjamin’s place. How does his offer help you understand what Jesus has done for you?

Let us pray. Lord Jesus, thank you that you took my place, the just for the unjust, to bring me to God. Change my heart, as you changed Judah’s. Help me to confess my sin honestly, and to love others enough to put them first. Amen.
