The Living Voice. Genesis, chapter 45. I Am Joseph Your Brother.

The most powerful man in Egypt orders everyone out of the room. The doors close. Eleven frightened men stand before him, waiting to hear their fate. And then the governor begins to weep, so loudly that the Egyptians outside can hear it. Through his tears, he speaks to them in their own language, for the first time. I am Joseph.

In chapter forty-four, Joseph’s silver cup was found in Benjamin’s sack. The brothers all returned to the city, and fell before Joseph. Judah confessed, God hath found out the iniquity of thy servants. Then he offered to stay as a slave in Benjamin’s place, saying, how shall I go up to my father, and the lad be not with me?

Now, in chapter forty-five, Joseph has seen enough. His brothers have changed. This is the moment the whole story has been building toward, for twenty-two years. And it is one of the great moments of forgiveness in the whole Bible.

Joseph cannot hold back any longer before all those who stand by him. He cries, cause every man to go out from me. And no one stands with him while he makes himself known to his brothers.

He weeps aloud, and the Egyptians and the house of Pharaoh hear it. Then Joseph says to his brothers, I am Joseph; doth my father yet live? And his brothers cannot answer him. They are terrified at his presence. The brother they sold, the brother they thought was dead, is standing in front of them, and he has the power of life and death.

But Joseph speaks gently.

Then Joseph says something astonishing.

For two years the famine has been in the land, Joseph says, and there are still five years to come, in which there will be no ploughing or harvest. God sent me before you to preserve you a posterity in the earth, and to save your lives by a great deliverance.

And Joseph says it a third time.

Then Joseph tells them to hurry home. Go up to my father, and say to him, thus saith thy son Joseph, God hath made me lord of all Egypt. Come down unto me, tarry not. You shall live in the land of Goshen, and be near me, you and your children and your children’s children, your flocks and your herds and all that you have. And there I will nourish you, for there are yet five years of famine.

Your eyes see, and the eyes of my brother Benjamin, that it is my mouth that speaketh unto you. Tell my father of all my glory in Egypt, and of all that you have seen, and hurry, and bring my father down here.

And Joseph falls upon his brother Benjamin’s neck, and weeps. And Benjamin weeps upon his neck. Then Joseph kisses all his brothers, and weeps upon them. 'and after that his brethren talked with him.' After twenty-two years of silence and fear, they finally talk.

The news reaches Pharaoh’s house: Joseph’s brothers have come. It pleases Pharaoh well. He tells Joseph, have your brothers load their animals and go to Canaan, and bring your father and your households to me. I will give you the good of the land of Egypt. Take wagons for your little ones and your wives, and bring your father. And do not worry about your belongings, for the good of all the land of Egypt is yours.

Joseph gives them wagons, food for the way, and changes of clothing. To Benjamin he gives three hundred pieces of silver and five changes of clothing. To his father he sends ten donkeys loaded with the good things of Egypt, and ten female donkeys loaded with corn and bread and food. As he sends his brothers away, he says, see that ye fall not out by the way. Do not quarrel on the journey. Joseph knows his brothers well.

They go up out of Egypt, and come to Jacob their father in Canaan. They tell him, Joseph is yet alive, and he is governor over all the land of Egypt. And Jacob’s heart faints, for he does not believe them.

They tell him all the words of Joseph. And when he sees the wagons Joseph has sent to carry him, the spirit of Jacob their father revives.

And Israel says, 'It is enough; Joseph my son is yet alive: I will go and see him before I die.'

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 4. 'And Joseph said unto his brethren, Come near to me, I pray you. And they came near. And he said, I am Joseph your brother, whom ye sold into Egypt.' Joseph’s brothers were terrified. They had every reason to expect punishment. But Joseph said, come near. He did not pretend that what they had done was not wrong. He named it: whom ye sold into Egypt. But he named it while drawing them close. True forgiveness does not pretend there was no wrong. It names the wrong, and then welcomes the wrongdoer anyway.

Verse 5. 'Now therefore be not grieved, nor angry with yourselves, that ye sold me hither: for God did send me before you to preserve life.' Joseph saw his whole painful story in a new light. His brothers sold him. But God sent him. Both were true. The brothers meant harm, but God had a purpose for every step: the pit, the slavery, the prison. Joseph did not become bitter, because he saw the hand of God in his story. That is what made it possible to forgive.

Verse 8. 'So now it was not you that sent me hither, but God: and he hath made me a father to Pharaoh, and lord of all his house, and a ruler throughout all the land of Egypt.' Three times Joseph says that God sent him. He wanted his brothers to understand that God had been in control all along. That does not make their sin any less wrong. But it means that their sin did not have the last word. God did. When we look back on our lives, we may see that even the hardest parts were part of God’s plan to bring good.

The letter to the Romans says, 'all things work together for good to them that love God'. And Paul wrote, 'be ye kind one to another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, even as God for Christ’s sake hath forgiven you.' Jesus, too, was rejected by his own, and became the one who saves them.

Is there someone who has hurt you deeply? Ask God to help you see your story through his eyes. And ask him for the grace to say to that person, in your heart if not in words, come near. Forgiveness is a gift you can give, because God has given it to you.

Here is today’s question. Joseph said, it was not you that sent me hither, but God. How might seeing God at work in your story help you to forgive someone who has hurt you?

Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that you were with Joseph in every step of his painful journey, and that you are with me in mine. Help me to see your hand in my story. Give me the grace to forgive as Joseph forgave, and as you have forgiven me in Jesus. Amen.
