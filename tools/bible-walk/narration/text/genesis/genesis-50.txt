The Living Voice. Genesis, chapter 50. God Meant It unto Good.

Joseph’s father is dead. And now his brothers are afraid. For seventeen years they have lived safely in Egypt, under Joseph’s protection. But perhaps, they think, he was only being kind for their father’s sake. Now that Jacob is gone, will Joseph finally take his revenge? They send a message, and then come and fall down before him. Behold, we be thy servants.

In chapter forty-nine, Jacob gathered his twelve sons and blessed them, one by one. He promised that the sceptre would not depart from Judah, until Shiloh came. In the middle of it all, he prayed, I have waited for thy salvation, O LORD. Then he asked to be buried with his fathers in Canaan, and he died.

Now we come to the last chapter of Genesis. The book that began with God creating life ends with a funeral, and a coffin in Egypt. But it does not end in despair. It ends with forgiveness, and with a promise.

Joseph falls upon his father’s face, and weeps upon him, and kisses him. He commands the physicians to prepare his father’s body for burial, in the Egyptian way. It takes forty days. And the Egyptians mourn for Jacob seventy days, almost as long as they would mourn for a king.

When the days of mourning are past, Joseph asks Pharaoh’s permission to bury his father in Canaan, as he promised. Pharaoh says, go up, and bury thy father, according as he made thee swear.

So Joseph goes up to bury his father. With him go all the servants of Pharaoh, the elders of his house, all the elders of Egypt, all of Joseph’s household, his brothers, and his father’s household. Only the little ones and the flocks are left in Goshen. Chariots and horsemen go with them. It is a very great company.

They come to the threshing floor of Atad, beyond the Jordan, and there they mourn with a great and very sore lamentation, for seven days. The Canaanites see it, and say, this is a grievous mourning to the Egyptians. Then Jacob’s sons do as he commanded. They carry him into the land of Canaan, and bury him in the cave of the field of Machpelah, with Abraham and Isaac. Then Joseph returns to Egypt, with his brothers and all who went with him.

And now the brothers’ old fear comes back.

When Joseph’s brothers see that their father is dead, they say, Joseph will perhaps hate us, and will certainly repay us all the evil we did to him. So they send a messenger to Joseph, saying, thy father did command before he died, so shall ye say unto Joseph: forgive, I pray thee now, the trespass of thy brethren, and their sin, for they did unto thee evil. And now, we pray thee, forgive the trespass of the servants of the God of thy father.

And Joseph weeps when they speak to him. It hurts him that, after all these years, they still do not believe he has forgiven them.

His brothers come and fall down before his face, and say, behold, we be thy servants. One last time, Joseph’s dream comes true. And Joseph says to them, fear not: for am I in the place of God? It is not my place to take revenge. That belongs to God alone.

Then Joseph says the words that sum up his whole life.

Joseph lives in Egypt, he and his father’s house. Joseph lives a hundred and ten years. He sees Ephraim’s children to the third generation. The children of Machir, the son of Manasseh, are brought up on Joseph’s knees.

As he comes near to death, Joseph speaks to his brothers.

Then Joseph makes the children of Israel swear an oath. God will surely visit you, he says, and ye shall carry up my bones from hence. Joseph had become a great lord of Egypt. But he did not want to stay in Egypt for ever. Even in death, he wanted to be part of God’s promise.

So Joseph dies, a hundred and ten years old. They prepare his body for burial, and he is put in a coffin in Egypt.

And that is the last word of Genesis. A coffin in Egypt. But it is a coffin waiting to go home.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 20. 'But as for you, ye thought evil against me; but God meant it unto good, to bring to pass, as it is this day, to save much people alive.' This is the heart of the whole story of Joseph, and perhaps of the whole book of Genesis. Joseph did not pretend that what his brothers did was good. Ye thought evil against me. But God was greater than their evil. He took the worst thing they did and used it to save many lives. God can do the same with the wrongs done to us. He does not waste our pain.

Verse 21. 'Now therefore fear ye not: I will nourish you, and your little ones. And he comforted them, and spake kindly unto them.' Joseph did not just say, I forgive you. He promised to take care of them and their children. And he comforted them, and spoke kindly to their hearts. Real forgiveness is not only giving up revenge. It is choosing kindness, again and again, to people who once hurt us. That is how God forgives us.

Verse 24. 'And Joseph said unto his brethren, I die: and God will surely visit you, and bring you out of this land unto the land which he sware to Abraham, to Isaac, and to Jacob.' Joseph’s last words were about God’s promise. He would not see it come true. But he was sure it would. God will surely visit you. And he did. Long after, Moses led the people out of Egypt, and they carried Joseph’s bones with them, all the way to the promised land. God’s promises outlive the people who first hear them.

The letter to the Hebrews says, 'By faith Joseph, when he died, made mention of the departing of the children of Israel; and gave commandment concerning his bones.' And Paul wrote, 'And we know that all things work together for good to them that love God'. The God of Genesis, who brings good out of evil, did it once for all at the cross.

Is there something painful in your story that you cannot understand? Bring it to God, and ask him to show you how he can bring good out of it. And if you have forgiven someone, show it with kindness, as Joseph did.

Here is today’s question. We have walked through the whole book of Genesis. Joseph said, ye thought evil against me; but God meant it unto good. How has this book helped you trust that God is at work in your story?

Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that you are greater than every evil, and that you bring good out of the hardest parts of our lives. Thank you for keeping your promises from generation to generation. Help me to forgive as Joseph forgave, to trust your promises to the end, and to know that you are working all things for good. Amen.

That is the end of our walk through the book of Genesis. Well walked. Genesis ends with God’s people in Egypt, and a promise that God will surely visit them. The book of Exodus tells how he kept that promise.
