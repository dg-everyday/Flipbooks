The Living Voice. John, chapter 3. For God So Loved the World.

A respected teacher, one of the rulers of his people, slips quietly through the streets of Jerusalem in the dark. He does not want to be seen. He knocks at the place where a young rabbi from Galilee is staying. He has questions. And the answer he receives will become the most famous sentence in the Bible.

In chapter two, Jesus turned water into wine at a wedding in Cana, the first of his signs, and his disciples believed on him. Then he drove the money changers out of the temple and said, destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up. He was speaking of his body. And many believed when they saw his miracles, but Jesus knew what was in man.

Now, in chapter three, we meet one of those people. Nicodemus has seen the miracles. He is interested. But he does not yet understand. Jesus tells him that to see the kingdom of God, he needs something far more than interest. He needs a new birth.

There is a man of the Pharisees, named Nicodemus, a ruler of the Jews. 'The same came to Jesus by night'. He says, Rabbi, we know that thou art a teacher come from God, for no man can do these miracles that you do, except God be with him.

Jesus goes straight to the heart of the matter.

Nicodemus does not understand. How can a man be born when he is old? Can he be born a second time? Jesus answers, verily, verily, I say unto thee, except a man be born of water and of the Spirit, he cannot enter into the kingdom of God. That which is born of the flesh is flesh, and that which is born of the Spirit is spirit. 'Marvel not that I said unto thee, Ye must be born again.'

Jesus gives a picture. 'The wind bloweth where it listeth, and thou hearest the sound thereof, but canst not tell whence it cometh, and whither it goeth: so is every one that is born of the Spirit.' You cannot see the wind, but you can see what it does. So it is with the Spirit of God, bringing new life.

Nicodemus asks, how can these things be? Jesus answers, art thou a master of Israel, and knowest not these things? Nicodemus is a great teacher, but he does not understand the most important thing of all.

Then Jesus reminds him of a story from the book of Numbers. In the wilderness, the people of Israel were bitten by fiery serpents, and many died. God told Moses to make a serpent of brass and set it on a pole. Whoever looked at it lived. And Jesus says, as Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, even so must the Son of man be lifted up, that whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have eternal life.

And then come the words that sum up the whole Bible.

For God sent not his Son into the world to condemn the world, Jesus says, but that the world through him might be saved. He that believeth on him is not condemned. But he that believeth not is condemned already, because he has not believed in the name of the only begotten Son of God.

'And this is the condemnation, that light is come into the world, and men loved darkness rather than light, because their deeds were evil.' Everyone who does evil hates the light, and will not come to it. But he that doeth truth cometh to the light. Nicodemus came in the dark. Jesus invites him into the light.

After this, Jesus and his disciples go into the land of Judea, and baptize. John the Baptist is baptizing nearby. Some of John’s disciples come to him, worried. Rabbi, the man you spoke of is baptizing, and all men come to him. They seem to feel that John is losing his followers.

But John answers them with joy. A man can receive nothing, except it be given him from heaven. You yourselves heard me say, I am not the Christ, but I am sent before him. John compares himself to the friend of the bridegroom, the best man at a wedding. The best man does not want the attention. He rejoices greatly when he hears the bridegroom’s voice. This my joy therefore is fulfilled.

And John says something every follower of Jesus needs to learn.

The chapter ends with these words. The Father loveth the Son, and hath given all things into his hand. 'He that believeth on the Son hath everlasting life'.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 3. 'Jesus answered and said unto him, Verily, verily, I say unto thee, Except a man be born again, he cannot see the kingdom of God.' Nicodemus was religious, educated and respected. If anyone could earn his way into God’s kingdom, it was him. But Jesus said that even he needed to be born again. The words can also mean born from above. We cannot make ourselves new, any more than we could cause our own first birth. It is God’s Spirit who gives new life to everyone who believes.

Verse 16. 'For God so loved the world, that he gave his only begotten Son, that whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have everlasting life.' This verse has been called the gospel in a nutshell. God so loved, that is the reason. The world, that is everyone, every nation, every person. He gave his only begotten Son, that is the cost. Whosoever believeth, that is the invitation, open to anyone. Should not perish, but have everlasting life, that is the gift. If you remember one verse from the whole Bible, remember this one.

Verse 30. 'He must increase, but I must decrease.' John the Baptist was famous. Crowds came to him. But when Jesus came, John was glad to step aside. He knew his job was to point to Jesus, not to himself. This is a wonderful prayer for every follower of Jesus: may Jesus become greater in my life, and may I become less. Less of my pride, my self, my need for attention. More of him.

Nicodemus came in secret. But later, he stood up for Jesus before the council, and after the cross, he came openly, bringing spices to bury Jesus’ body. And the apostle Paul wrote, 'if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature: old things are passed away; behold, all things are become new.'

Read John chapter three, verse sixteen, slowly today, and put your own name in it. God so loved you, that he gave his only begotten Son. And ask him to help you say, with John the Baptist, he must increase, but I must decrease.

Here is today’s question. Jesus said, ye must be born again. What difference has God’s new life made in you, or what difference would you like it to make?

Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that you so loved the world, and so loved me, that you gave your only begotten Son. I believe in him. Give me new life by your Spirit, and help me to walk in the light. May Jesus increase in my life, and may I decrease. Amen.
