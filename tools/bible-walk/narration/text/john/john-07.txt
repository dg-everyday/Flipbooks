The Living Voice. John, chapter 7. If Any Man Thirst, Let Him Come unto Me.

Jerusalem is full of people, camping in shelters made of branches for the great autumn feast. On the last day, the priests pour out water at the temple, in a joyful ceremony, remembering how God gave water in the wilderness. Suddenly a voice rings out over the crowd. A man is standing, and crying out: if any man thirst, let him come unto me, and drink.

In chapter six, Jesus fed five thousand people with five loaves and two fishes, and walked on the sea. Then he said, I am the bread of life. Many found his words hard, and walked away. But Peter said, Lord, to whom shall we go? thou hast the words of eternal life.

Now, in chapter seven, Jesus goes to Jerusalem for the Feast of Tabernacles. Everyone is talking about him, and arguing about him. Some say he is a good man. Some say he deceives the people. Some say he is the Christ. Listen for how one question runs through this chapter: who is he?

After these things, Jesus walks in Galilee. He will not walk in Judea, because the leaders there seek to kill him. The Jews’ feast of tabernacles is near. This was a joyful harvest festival, when for a week the people lived in shelters made of branches, to remember how God cared for them in the wilderness.

Jesus’ own brothers say to him, depart from here, and go into Judea, so that your disciples may see your works. If you do these things, show yourself to the world. They are mocking him. 'For neither did his brethren believe in him.' Even his own family did not yet believe. Later, after the resurrection, they would.

Jesus says, my time is not yet come. You go up to the feast. Then, after his brothers have gone, he goes up too, not openly, but quietly.

At the feast, the leaders look for him, and ask, where is he? There is much murmuring among the people about him. Some say, he is a good man. Others say, nay, he deceives the people. But no one speaks openly of him, for fear of the leaders.

About the middle of the feast, Jesus goes up into the temple, and teaches. The leaders marvel, saying, how does this man know letters, having never learned? He has not studied in their schools. Jesus answers, my doctrine is not mine, but his that sent me. If any man will do his will, he shall know of the doctrine, whether it be of God, or whether I speak of myself.

Jesus asks them why they seek to kill him for healing a man on the sabbath. He says, judge not according to the appearance, but judge righteous judgment.

The people of Jerusalem argue. Is not this he, whom they seek to kill? Do the rulers know indeed that this is the very Christ? But we know where this man comes from. When Christ comes, no man knows where he comes from. Then Jesus cries out in the temple, you know me, and you know where I come from. But I am not come of myself. He that sent me is true, whom you know not. But I know him, for I am from him, and he has sent me.

They seek to take him, but no man lays hands on him, because his hour is not yet come. And many of the people believe on him, and say, when Christ comes, will he do more miracles than these which this man has done?

The Pharisees and chief priests send officers to arrest him.

Then comes the last day of the feast, the great day.

This he spake of the Spirit, John tells us, which they that believe on him should receive. For the Holy Ghost was not yet given, because Jesus was not yet glorified.

Many of the people, when they hear this saying, say, of a truth this is the Prophet. Others say, this is the Christ. But some say, shall Christ come out of Galilee? Has not the scripture said, that Christ comes of the seed of David, and out of the town of Bethlehem, where David was? So there is a division among the people because of him.

Then the officers come back to the chief priests and Pharisees, without Jesus. The leaders ask, why have ye not brought him?

The Pharisees answer, are ye also deceived? Have any of the rulers or of the Pharisees believed on him? But this crowd, who know not the law, are cursed.

Then Nicodemus speaks up, the one who came to Jesus in secret, being one of them. 'Doth our law judge any man, before it hear him, and know what he doeth?' It is a brave question. They answer, art thou also of Galilee? Search, and look: for out of Galilee ariseth no prophet. And every man goes to his own house.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 37. 'In the last day, that great day of the feast, Jesus stood and cried, saying, If any man thirst, let him come unto me, and drink.' At the very moment the people were celebrating God’s gift of water in the wilderness, Jesus stood up and said, I am the one who satisfies thirst. Any man means anyone. Thirst is all you need to come. Not goodness, not knowledge, not religion. If you are thirsty for God, for meaning, for forgiveness, come to Jesus, and drink.

Verse 38. 'He that believeth on me, as the scripture hath said, out of his belly shall flow rivers of living water.' The word belly here means the innermost being, the heart. Jesus does not only satisfy our thirst. He makes us a source of life for others. John explains that he was speaking of the Holy Spirit. When the Spirit lives in us, his life flows out of us like rivers, to bless the people around us.

Verse 46. 'The officers answered, Never man spake like this man.' The officers were sent to arrest Jesus. They came back empty handed. They had heard him speak, and they could not do it. They were not scholars. They did not argue theology. They simply said, never man spake like this man. When people truly listen to Jesus, they find there is no one else like him.

The prophet Isaiah wrote, 'Therefore with joy shall ye draw water out of the wells of salvation.' And in the last chapter of the Bible, the Spirit and the bride say, come. 'And let him that is athirst come.'

Are you thirsty, in your heart, for something more? Come to Jesus, and drink. And pray that the life of his Spirit would flow out of you, like rivers, to someone who needs it today.

Here is today’s question. The officers said, never man spake like this man. What words of Jesus have most moved or changed you?

Let us pray. Lord Jesus, I am thirsty, and I come to you to drink. Fill me with your Holy Spirit, and let rivers of living water flow out of me to others. Thank you that no one ever spoke like you. Give me courage, like Nicodemus, to speak up for you. Amen.
