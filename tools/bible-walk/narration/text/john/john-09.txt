The Living Voice. John, chapter 9. Whereas I Was Blind, Now I See.

A man sits begging by the road, as he has done all his life. He was born blind. He has never seen a face, or a colour. Then he hears footsteps stop beside him. Someone spits on the ground, makes mud, and spreads it on his eyes. A voice tells him, go, wash in the pool. He gropes his way there, and washes. And for the first time in his life, he sees.

In chapter eight, a woman was brought to Jesus to be condemned. Jesus said, he that is without sin among you, let him first cast a stone at her, and then, neither do I condemn thee: go, and sin no more. He said, I am the light of the world, and the truth shall make you free. And when he said, before Abraham was, I am, they took up stones against him.

Now, in chapter nine, Jesus shows what it means to be the light of the world. He gives sight to a man born blind. But the more the man sees, the more blind the religious leaders turn out to be. Listen for how the man’s understanding of Jesus grows, step by step.

As Jesus passes by, he sees a man which was blind from his birth. His disciples ask him, Master, who did sin, this man, or his parents, that he was born blind? In those days, many people believed that suffering was always a punishment for someone’s sin.

Jesus answers.

Jesus says, 'I must work the works of him that sent me, while it is day: the night cometh, when no man can work.' And then, 'As long as I am in the world, I am the light of the world.'

He spits on the ground, makes clay of the spittle, and anoints the eyes of the blind man with the clay. And he says to him, go, wash in the pool of Siloam, which means sent. The man goes his way, and washes, and comes back seeing.

His neighbours ask, is not this he that sat and begged? He says, I am he. They ask, how were thine eyes opened? He answers, a man that is called Jesus made clay, and anointed mine eyes, and said to me, go to the pool of Siloam, and wash. And I went and washed, and I received sight.

They bring him to the Pharisees. It is the sabbath day when Jesus made the clay and opened his eyes. The Pharisees ask him how he received his sight. He tells them. Some of the Pharisees say, this man is not of God, because he keeps not the sabbath day. Others say, how can a man that is a sinner do such miracles? And there is a division among them.

They ask the blind man again what he says of Jesus. He says, he is a prophet.

The leaders do not believe he was ever blind, so they call his parents. Is this your son, who you say was born blind? How then does he now see? His parents answer, we know that this is our son, and that he was born blind. But by what means he now sees, we know not. He is of age; ask him. They say this because they are afraid. The leaders had already agreed that if anyone confessed that Jesus was the Christ, he would be put out of the synagogue.

So they call the man again, and say, give God the praise: we know that this man is a sinner.

They ask him again, what did he to thee? How opened he thine eyes? He answers, I have told you already, and ye did not hear. Why would you hear it again? Will ye also be his disciples? They revile him, and say, thou art his disciple; but we are Moses’ disciples. We know that God spake unto Moses. As for this fellow, we know not from whence he is.

The man answers with simple, powerful logic. Why herein is a marvellous thing, that ye know not from whence he is, and yet he hath opened mine eyes. Since the world began was it not heard that any man opened the eyes of one that was born blind. 'If this man were not of God, he could do nothing.'

They answer, thou wast altogether born in sins, and dost thou teach us? And they cast him out.

Jesus hears that they have cast him out. And when he finds him, he says, dost thou believe on the Son of God? He answers, who is he, Lord, that I might believe on him? And Jesus says, thou hast both seen him, and it is he that talketh with thee.

And the man responds.

Jesus says, for judgment I am come into this world, that they which see not might see, and that they which see might be made blind. Some of the Pharisees hear and ask, are we blind also? Jesus says, if ye were blind, ye should have no sin. But now ye say, we see. Therefore your sin remaineth.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 3. 'Jesus answered, Neither hath this man sinned, nor his parents: but that the works of God should be made manifest in him.' The disciples wanted someone to blame. Jesus refused to play that game. This man’s blindness was not a punishment. It was an opportunity for God to show his glory. Not every suffering in our lives is caused by a particular sin. Instead of asking whose fault it is, Jesus invites us to ask, how might God be glorified here?

Verse 25. 'He answered and said, Whether he be a sinner or no, I know not: one thing I know, that, whereas I was blind, now I see.' The man could not win a theological argument with the Pharisees. But he did not need to. He simply told what had happened to him. One thing I know. That is the power of a personal testimony. You may not have all the answers. But you can tell what Jesus has done for you, and no one can argue with that.

Verse 38. 'And he said, Lord, I believe. And he worshipped him.' Follow the man’s journey. First, a man called Jesus. Then, a prophet. Then, a man of God. And finally, Lord, I believe, and he worshipped him. He lost his place in the synagogue, but he found the Son of God. The man who was cast out by the religious leaders was found by Jesus himself.

The prophet Isaiah foretold that in the days of the Messiah, 'the eyes of the blind shall be opened'. And Paul wrote, 'For God, who commanded the light to shine out of darkness, hath shined in our hearts'.

Is there something in your life that you have been asking, whose fault is this? Ask instead, how might God show his works here? And think about your own story. What can you say, as the man did, one thing I know?

Here is today’s question. The man said, one thing I know, that, whereas I was blind, now I see. What is one thing you know Jesus has done for you?

Let us pray. Lord Jesus, you are the light of the world. Open my eyes to see you more clearly. Thank you for the things you have done in my life, that I can tell to others. When I suffer, help me to look for your works, and not for someone to blame. Lord, I believe, and I worship you. Amen.
