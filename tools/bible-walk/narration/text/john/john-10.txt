The Living Voice. John, chapter 10. I Am the Good Shepherd.

In the hills around Bethlehem and Jerusalem, shepherds lead their flocks out to pasture. The sheep do not follow just anyone. They know one voice, and they follow it. If a stranger calls, they scatter. Jesus looks at the crowd around him, many of whom have seen shepherds and sheep all their lives, and says, I am the good shepherd.

In chapter nine, Jesus gave sight to a man born blind. The religious leaders questioned him and cast him out of the synagogue. But Jesus found him, and the man said, Lord, I believe, and worshipped him. Jesus said that those who claimed to see were really blind.

Now, in chapter ten, Jesus keeps speaking to those same leaders. They have just thrown a helpless man out. In contrast, Jesus describes himself as a shepherd who knows his sheep by name, protects them, and gives his life for them. Two of the great I AM sayings are in this chapter.

Jesus says, verily, verily, I say unto you, he that enters not by the door into the sheepfold, but climbs up some other way, the same is a thief and a robber. But he that enters in by the door is the shepherd of the sheep.

To him the porter opens. The sheep hear his voice. 'and he calleth his own sheep by name, and leadeth them out.' When he puts forth his own sheep, he goes before them, and the sheep follow him, for they know his voice. And a stranger will they not follow, but will flee from him, for they know not the voice of strangers.

The people do not understand. So Jesus says it again, more plainly. Verily, verily, I say unto you, I am the door of the sheep. 'by me if any man enter in, he shall be saved, and shall go in and out, and find pasture.' In those days, a shepherd would sometimes lie down across the opening of the sheepfold, so that he himself was the door. Nothing could get in or out without passing him.

Then Jesus contrasts himself with a thief.

Then comes the second great I AM.

Jesus describes the hireling, the hired hand, who is not the shepherd, and whose own the sheep are not. When he sees the wolf coming, he leaves the sheep, and flees. And the wolf catches them, and scatters the sheep. The hireling flees, because he cares not for the sheep.

But Jesus says, I am the good shepherd, and know my sheep, and am known of mine. As the Father knoweth me, even so know I the Father. And I lay down my life for the sheep.

And other sheep I have, Jesus says, which are not of this fold. Them also I must bring, and they shall hear my voice, and there shall be one fold, and one shepherd. Jesus is speaking of the Gentiles, people of every nation, who will join his flock.

Then Jesus says something no other shepherd could say. Therefore doth my Father love me, because I lay down my life, that I might take it again. No man taketh it from me, but I lay it down of myself. I have power to lay it down, and I have power to take it again. Jesus is not a victim. He chooses to give his life, and he will take it up again.

There is a division among the people because of these sayings. Many say, he has a devil, and is mad; why hear ye him? Others say, these are not the words of him that has a devil. Can a devil open the eyes of the blind?

Some time later, it is the feast of the dedication at Jerusalem, and it is winter. Jesus walks in the temple, in Solomon’s porch. The leaders surround him, and say, how long dost thou make us to doubt? If thou be the Christ, tell us plainly.

Jesus answers, I told you, and ye believed not. The works that I do in my Father’s name, they bear witness of me. But ye believe not, because ye are not of my sheep. My sheep hear my voice, and I know them, and they follow me.

And then he gives them a wonderful promise.

My Father, which gave them me, is greater than all, Jesus says, and no man is able to pluck them out of my Father’s hand. 'I and my Father are one.'

Then the leaders take up stones again to stone him. Jesus asks, many good works have I shewed you from my Father. For which of those works do ye stone me? They say, for a good work we stone thee not, but for blasphemy, because that thou, being a man, makest thyself God. They try again to take him, but he escapes out of their hand. And he goes away beyond Jordan, to the place where John first baptized. And many come to him there, and believe on him.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 10. 'The thief cometh not, but for to steal, and to kill, and to destroy: I am come that they might have life, and that they might have it more abundantly.' Jesus contrasts two kinds of leaders. Some take from people. Jesus gives. The thief comes to take life away. Jesus comes to give life, not just survival, but life to the full. Following Jesus does not make life smaller. It makes it overflowing.

Verse 11. 'I am the good shepherd: the good shepherd giveth his life for the sheep.' Not a hired hand who runs at the first sign of danger. A good shepherd, who loves his sheep enough to die for them. Jesus said this knowing that the cross was ahead. And he does more than protect us from the wolf. He takes the full attack himself, so that his sheep can be safe.

Verse 28. 'And I give unto them eternal life; and they shall never perish, neither shall any man pluck them out of my hand.' Our safety does not depend on how tightly we hold on to Jesus. It depends on how tightly he holds on to us. No one, no power, no enemy, can snatch his sheep out of his hand. And his hand is held in his Father’s hand. That is as safe as anyone can ever be.

Long before, David wrote, 'The LORD is my shepherd; I shall not want.' And the prophet Ezekiel heard God say, 'I, even I, will both search my sheep, and seek them out.' Peter later called Jesus 'the Shepherd and Bishop of your souls.'

Take a few quiet moments today to listen for your Shepherd’s voice, in his Word and in prayer. He knows you by name. And if you are afraid, remember that you are held in his hand, and no one can pluck you out.

Here is today’s question. Jesus said, my sheep hear my voice, and I know them, and they follow me. How do you listen for the voice of your Shepherd?

Let us pray. Lord Jesus, you are the good shepherd, who gave your life for me. Thank you that you know me by name, and that no one can pluck me out of your hand. Help me to hear your voice, to follow you, and to enjoy the abundant life you came to give. Amen.
