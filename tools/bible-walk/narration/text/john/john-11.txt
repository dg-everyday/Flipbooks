The Living Voice. John, chapter 11. Thou Shouldest See the Glory of God.

A message arrives for Jesus from two sisters: Lord, the one you love is sick. Everyone expects Jesus to hurry to Bethany. Instead, he stays where he is for two more days. By the time he arrives, his friend has been in the grave for four days. And both sisters say the same painful words to him: Lord, if you had been here.

In chapter ten, Jesus said, I am the good shepherd: the good shepherd giveth his life for the sheep. He promised that no one could pluck his sheep out of his hand, and said, I and my Father are one. The leaders took up stones again, and Jesus went away beyond the Jordan.

Now, in chapter eleven, comes the seventh and greatest sign in John’s Gospel. Jesus raises a man who has been dead four days. It is a story of grief and delay, of faith and tears, and of the glory of God. And it is the sign that leads the leaders to plan his death.

A certain man named Lazarus is sick. He lives in Bethany, the town of Mary and her sister Martha. The sisters send word to Jesus: Lord, behold, he whom thou lovest is sick.

When Jesus hears that, he says, this sickness is not unto death, but for the glory of God, that the Son of God might be glorified thereby.

Then John tells us something important, before the strange part of the story.

When he has heard that Lazarus is sick, he stays two days still in the same place where he is. It seems so strange. If he loves them, why does he wait? But Jesus’ delay is not a lack of love. It is part of his plan.

After that, he says to his disciples, let us go into Judea again. They say, Master, the Jews of late sought to stone thee; and goest thou thither again? Then Jesus says to them plainly, Lazarus is dead. And I am glad for your sakes that I was not there, to the intent ye may believe. Thomas says to the others, 'Let us also go, that we may die with him.'

When Jesus comes, he finds that Lazarus has lain in the grave four days already.

Martha, as soon as she hears that Jesus is coming, goes and meets him. Mary sits still in the house. Martha says to Jesus, Lord, if thou hadst been here, my brother had not died. But I know, that even now, whatsoever thou wilt ask of God, God will give it thee. Jesus says, thy brother shall rise again. Martha says, I know that he shall rise again in the resurrection at the last day.

Then Jesus says something astonishing.

Martha answers, yea, Lord: I believe that thou art the Christ, the Son of God, which should come into the world.

Then Martha goes and calls Mary, saying secretly, the Master is come, and calleth for thee. Mary rises quickly, and comes to him. When she sees him, she falls down at his feet, and says the same words: Lord, if thou hadst been here, my brother had not died.

When Jesus sees her weeping, and the friends weeping who came with her, he groans in the spirit, and is troubled. He says, where have ye laid him? They say, Lord, come and see. 'Jesus wept.' Then the people say, 'Behold how he loved him!'

Jesus comes to the grave. It is a cave, and a stone lies upon it. Jesus says, take ye away the stone. Martha says, Lord, by this time there will be a bad smell, for he hath been dead four days.

And Jesus answers her.

They take away the stone. And Jesus lifts up his eyes, and says, Father, I thank thee that thou hast heard me. And I knew that thou hearest me always. But because of the people which stand by I said it, that they may believe that thou hast sent me.

Then he cries with a loud voice, 'Lazarus, come forth.' And he that was dead comes out, bound hand and foot with grave clothes, his face bound about with a napkin. Jesus says to them, 'Loose him, and let him go.'

Many who saw what Jesus did believe on him. But some go and tell the Pharisees, and the leaders gather a council. If we let him alone, they say, all men will believe on him, and the Romans will come.

Caiaphas, the high priest that year, says, ye know nothing at all, nor consider that it is expedient for us, that one man should die for the people, and that the whole nation perish not. John tells us that Caiaphas did not say this of himself. Being high priest, he prophesied that Jesus should die for that nation, and not for that nation only, but that he should gather together in one the children of God that were scattered abroad.

From that day forth, they take counsel together to put him to death.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 5. 'Now Jesus loved Martha, and her sister, and Lazarus.' John puts this verse right before telling us that Jesus waited two days. He wants us to know that the delay did not mean Jesus did not care. He loved them. When God does not answer our prayers as quickly as we hoped, it is not because he does not love us. His timing is part of a bigger plan, and his love never fails.

Verse 25. 'Jesus said unto her, I am the resurrection, and the life: he that believeth in me, though he were dead, yet shall he live:' Martha believed in a resurrection one day, far in the future. Jesus told her that the resurrection is not just an event. It is a person, and he was standing in front of her. Death is not the end for anyone who believes in him. Then he asked her, believest thou this? He asks us the same question.

Verse 40. 'Jesus saith unto her, Said I not unto thee, that, if thou wouldest believe, thou shouldest see the glory of God?' Martha was being practical. Four days. The smell. It is too late. But Jesus asked her to trust him one more step: take away the stone. Sometimes faith means removing the stone, doing the small thing Jesus asks, even when it seems pointless. And then we see the glory of God.

Paul wrote, 'For the Lord himself shall descend from heaven with a shout… and the dead in Christ shall rise first'. And he wrote, 'O death, where is thy sting? O grave, where is thy victory?' The voice that called Lazarus out of the tomb will one day call every believer home.

Is there a prayer God seems slow to answer? Remember that Jesus loved Martha, Mary and Lazarus, even when he waited. And if you are grieving, remember that Jesus wept. He does not stand far from your sorrow. He weeps with you, and he is the resurrection and the life.

Here is today’s question. Jesus asked Martha, believest thou this? What does it mean to you that Jesus is the resurrection and the life?

Let us pray. Lord Jesus, you are the resurrection and the life. Thank you that you love me, even when your answers seem slow. Thank you that you wept at your friend’s grave, and that you understand my grief. Help me to believe, and to see the glory of God. Amen.
