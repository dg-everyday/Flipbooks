The Living Voice. John, chapter 12. Sir, We Would See Jesus.

At a dinner in Bethany, a woman breaks open a jar of very costly perfume, and pours it over Jesus’ feet. Then she wipes his feet with her hair. The whole house is filled with the fragrance. One of the disciples is angry. What a waste, he says. That could have been sold for a year’s wages. But Jesus says, let her alone.

In chapter eleven, Jesus raised Lazarus from the dead, after he had been in the tomb four days. Jesus said, I am the resurrection, and the life, and at the grave he wept. Many believed. But the chief priests and Pharisees gathered a council, and from that day they planned to put him to death.

Now, in chapter twelve, Jesus’ public ministry comes to an end. His hour has come. In this chapter we see costly love, a royal welcome, and some visitors from far away who want to see Jesus. And Jesus explains what his death will mean.

Six days before the Passover, Jesus comes to Bethany, where Lazarus lives, whom he raised from the dead. They make him a supper there. Martha serves, and Lazarus sits at the table with him.

Then Mary takes a pound of ointment of spikenard, very costly, and anoints the feet of Jesus, and wipes his feet with her hair. 'and the house was filled with the odour of the ointment.'

Judas Iscariot, the one who will betray him, says, why was not this ointment sold for three hundred pence, and given to the poor? John tells us that Judas said this, not because he cared for the poor, but because he was a thief. He kept the money bag, and took what was put in it.

Jesus says, let her alone. She has kept this for the day of my burying. For the poor always ye have with you; but me ye have not always.

Many people come to Bethany, not only to see Jesus, but to see Lazarus too. So the chief priests plan to put Lazarus to death as well, because many are believing on Jesus because of him.

The next day, a great crowd that has come to the feast hears that Jesus is coming to Jerusalem. They take branches of palm trees, and go out to meet him, and cry, 'Hosanna: Blessed is the King of Israel that cometh in the name of the Lord.' Jesus finds a young donkey and sits on it, as the prophet Zechariah had written: Fear not, daughter of Sion: behold, thy King cometh, sitting on an ass’s colt. His disciples do not understand this at first. But after Jesus is glorified, they remember.

The Pharisees say among themselves, perceive ye how ye prevail nothing? Behold, the world is gone after him.

Among those who come up to worship at the feast are some Greeks, people from outside Israel. They come to Philip.

Philip tells Andrew, and Andrew and Philip tell Jesus. And Jesus answers, the hour is come, that the Son of man should be glorified.

Then Jesus explains how he will be glorified. Through a seed falling into the ground.

He that loveth his life shall lose it, Jesus says, and he that hateth his life in this world shall keep it unto life eternal. If any man serve me, let him follow me. And where I am, there shall also my servant be.

Then Jesus says, now is my soul troubled. And what shall I say? Father, save me from this hour? But for this cause came I unto this hour. 'Father, glorify thy name.' Then a voice comes from heaven, saying, I have both glorified it, and will glorify it again. Some of the crowd say it thundered. Others say, an angel spake to him.

Jesus says, this voice came not because of me, but for your sakes. Now is the judgment of this world. Now shall the prince of this world be cast out.

Then he speaks of how he will die.

The people say, we have heard out of the law that Christ abideth for ever. Who is this Son of man? Jesus says, yet a little while is the light with you. Walk while ye have the light, lest darkness come upon you. While ye have light, believe in the light, that ye may be the children of light.

But though he has done so many miracles before them, many still do not believe on him. John says this fulfils the words of Isaiah: Lord, who hath believed our report? Yet even among the chief rulers, many believe on him. But because of the Pharisees, they do not confess him, lest they be put out of the synagogue. 'For they loved the praise of men more than the praise of God.'

Jesus cries out one last public message. He that believeth on me, believeth not on me, but on him that sent me. I am come a light into the world, that whosoever believeth on me should not abide in darkness.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 21. 'The same came therefore to Philip, which was of Bethsaida of Galilee, and desired him, saying, Sir, we would see Jesus.' These were Greeks, outsiders, who had come a long way. They did not ask to see the temple, or a miracle. They wanted to see Jesus. That is still the deepest longing of many hearts. And every follower of Jesus is like Philip: someone others come to, hoping we will bring them to him.

Verse 24. 'Verily, verily, I say unto you, Except a corn of wheat fall into the ground and die, it abideth alone: but if it die, it bringeth forth much fruit.' A seed that stays in a jar stays just one seed. A seed that is buried in the ground springs up into a plant with many seeds. Jesus was speaking of his own death. Through his death, life would come to multitudes. And he calls us to the same pattern. When we give our lives to God, they bear much fruit.

Verse 32. 'And I, if I be lifted up from the earth, will draw all men unto me.' John tells us that Jesus said this to signify what death he should die. Lifted up on a cross. The cross, which looked like defeat, would become the place where Jesus draws people of every nation to himself. The Greeks wanted to see Jesus. Soon, all the world would see him, lifted up.

Isaiah wrote of the suffering servant, 'Behold, my servant shall deal prudently, he shall be exalted and extolled, and be very high.' And Paul wrote, 'God forbid that I should glory, save in the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ'.

Is there someone around you who wants to see Jesus, even if they would not say it that way? Pray that they will see him in you. And think about Mary’s costly love. What could you give to Jesus today, not because you have to, but because you love him?

Here is today’s question. Jesus said that a corn of wheat must fall into the ground and die to bear much fruit. What might God be asking you to let go of, so that your life can bear fruit?

Let us pray. Lord Jesus, I want to see you. Thank you that you were lifted up on the cross to draw all people to yourself, including me. Help me to love you as Mary did, with costly devotion. And help me to bring others to you, so that they too may see Jesus. Amen.
