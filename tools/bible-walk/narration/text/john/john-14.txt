The Living Voice. John, chapter 14. I Am the Way, the Truth, and the Life.

A small group of frightened men sit around a table in an upstairs room. Their leader has just told them that he is going away, that one of them will betray him, and that their bravest friend will deny him. Their world is falling apart. And into that fear, Jesus speaks some of the most comforting words ever spoken.

In chapter thirteen, Jesus washed his disciples’ feet, and gave them a new commandment: love one another, as I have loved you. Judas went out into the dark to betray him. And Jesus told Peter that he would deny him three times before the cock crowed.

Now, in chapter fourteen, Jesus continues talking with his disciples at the supper. They are troubled and confused. Jesus speaks to their fears with promises about heaven, about himself, about the Holy Spirit, and about peace. Listen as though he were speaking to you.

Jesus begins with words of comfort.

In my Father’s house are many mansions, many rooms, Jesus says. If it were not so, I would have told you. 'I go to prepare a place for you.' And if I go and prepare a place for you, I will come again, and receive you unto myself, that where I am, there ye may be also. And whither I go ye know, and the way ye know.

Thomas says to him, Lord, we know not whither thou goest. And how can we know the way? Thomas is honest. He does not pretend to understand.

And Jesus gives one of his greatest answers.

If ye had known me, ye should have known my Father also, Jesus says. And from henceforth ye know him, and have seen him.

Philip says, Lord, shew us the Father, and it sufficeth us. That is all we need. Jesus says to him, have I been so long time with you, and yet hast thou not known me, Philip? 'he that hath seen me hath seen the Father'. How then can you say, show us the Father? Believe me that I am in the Father, and the Father in me. Or else believe me for the very works’ sake.

Jesus goes on. He that believeth on me, the works that I do shall he do also, and greater works than these shall he do, because I go unto my Father. And whatsoever ye shall ask in my name, that will I do, that the Father may be glorified in the Son.

Then Jesus promises a helper. If ye love me, keep my commandments. And I will pray the Father, and he shall give you another Comforter, that he may abide with you for ever, even the Spirit of truth. The world cannot receive him, because it seeth him not, neither knoweth him. 'but ye know him; for he dwelleth with you, and shall be in you.'

The word Comforter means one who is called alongside to help. Jesus was going away, but he would not leave them alone. 'I will not leave you comfortless: I will come to you.' Yet a little while, and the world seeth me no more, but ye see me. Because I live, ye shall live also.

He that hath my commandments, and keepeth them, Jesus says, he it is that loveth me. And he that loveth me shall be loved of my Father, and I will love him, and will manifest myself to him.

Another disciple named Judas, not Iscariot, asks, Lord, how is it that thou wilt manifest thyself unto us, and not unto the world? Jesus answers, if a man love me, he will keep my words, and my Father will love him, and we will come unto him, and make our abode with him. God himself will make his home with those who love Jesus.

The Comforter, which is the Holy Ghost, whom the Father will send in my name, Jesus says, he shall teach you all things, and bring all things to your remembrance, whatsoever I have said unto you.

Then Jesus gives them a parting gift.

Ye have heard how I said unto you, I go away, and come again unto you. If ye loved me, ye would rejoice, because I said, I go unto the Father. And now I have told you before it come to pass, that, when it is come to pass, ye might believe. The prince of this world cometh, and hath nothing in me. But that the world may know that I love the Father, even so I do. 'Arise, let us go hence.'

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 1. 'Let not your heart be troubled: ye believe in God, believe also in me.' Jesus knew that in a few hours he would be arrested and crucified. His own heart was troubled. And yet his concern was for his disciples’ hearts. He did not tell them their troubles were not real. He told them where to put their trust. When your heart is troubled, Jesus says the same to you. Believe in God. Believe also in me.

Verse 6. 'Jesus saith unto him, I am the way, the truth, and the life: no man cometh unto the Father, but by me.' Thomas asked for a map. Jesus said, I am the way. Not, I will show you the way, but I am the way. He is the truth, the one in whom we see what God is really like. And he is the life, the source of life that death cannot end. This is one of the boldest claims Jesus ever made. The way to God is not a set of rules. It is a person.

Verse 27. 'Peace I leave with you, my peace I give unto you: not as the world giveth, give I unto you. Let not your heart be troubled, neither let it be afraid.' The world’s peace depends on circumstances. When everything is calm, we feel at peace. But Jesus gives his own peace, the peace he had even on the way to the cross. It does not depend on what is happening around us. It comes from knowing that we are held by him.

Paul wrote, 'And the peace of God, which passeth all understanding, shall keep your hearts and minds through Christ Jesus.' And Isaiah wrote, 'Thou wilt keep him in perfect peace, whose mind is stayed on thee: because he trusteth in thee.'

What is troubling your heart today? Name it before Jesus, and hear him say, let not your heart be troubled. Believe in God; believe also in me. And remember that he has gone to prepare a place for you.

Here is today’s question. Jesus said, I am the way, the truth, and the life. What does it mean to you that Jesus is the way to the Father?

Let us pray. Lord Jesus, when my heart is troubled, help me to believe in you. Thank you that you are the way, the truth, and the life, and that you have gone to prepare a place for me. Thank you for the Holy Spirit, my Comforter. Give me your peace, not as the world gives, but your own peace. Amen.
