The Living Voice. John, chapter 15. I Am the Vine, Ye Are the Branches.

On the hillsides of Israel, grapevines grow everywhere. A gardener walks along the rows with a pruning hook, cutting away dead branches, and trimming the healthy ones so they will grow even more grapes. A branch lying on the ground, cut off from the vine, quickly withers. But a branch joined to the vine is full of life. Jesus points to this picture, and says, I am the vine.

In chapter fourteen, Jesus told his troubled disciples, let not your heart be troubled. I go to prepare a place for you. He said, I am the way, the truth, and the life. He promised them the Comforter, the Holy Spirit, and said, peace I leave with you. Then he said, arise, let us go hence.

Now, in chapter fifteen, Jesus and his disciples may be walking toward the garden, perhaps past vineyards. Jesus gives the last of his seven I AM sayings. It is about staying close to him. And he speaks about love, about friendship, and about the hatred of the world.

Jesus says, 'I am the true vine, and my Father is the husbandman.' The husbandman is the gardener, the one who cares for the vine.

Every branch in me that beareth not fruit he taketh away, Jesus says. And every branch that beareth fruit, he purgeth it, he prunes it, that it may bring forth more fruit. Pruning hurts. But a good gardener prunes because he wants more fruit, not less.

Now ye are clean through the word which I have spoken unto you, Jesus says. Abide in me, and I in you. As the branch cannot bear fruit of itself, except it abide in the vine, no more can ye, except ye abide in me.

Then Jesus says it plainly.

If a man abide not in me, he is cast forth as a branch, and is withered. If ye abide in me, and my words abide in you, ye shall ask what ye will, and it shall be done unto you. Herein is my Father glorified, that ye bear much fruit. So shall ye be my disciples.

The word abide means to stay, to remain, to live in. It is a word for home. Jesus is not asking his disciples to visit him from time to time. He is asking them to make their home in him.

Then Jesus speaks about love.

If ye keep my commandments, ye shall abide in my love, Jesus says, even as I have kept my Father’s commandments, and abide in his love. 'These things have I spoken unto you, that my joy might remain in you, and that your joy might be full.' Jesus wants his followers to have joy, his own joy, and to have it in full.

This is my commandment, Jesus says, that ye love one another, as I have loved you. And then he shows how much he loves them.

Ye are my friends, Jesus says, if ye do whatsoever I command you. Henceforth I call you not servants, for the servant knoweth not what his lord doeth. But I have called you friends, for all things that I have heard of my Father I have made known unto you. Abraham was called the friend of God. Now Jesus calls his disciples his friends.

Ye have not chosen me, but I have chosen you, and ordained you, that ye should go and bring forth fruit, and that your fruit should remain. Jesus chose them, and he sends them out.

Then Jesus warns them that following him will not always be easy. If the world hate you, ye know that it hated me before it hated you. If ye were of the world, the world would love his own. But because ye are not of the world, but I have chosen you out of the world, therefore the world hateth you. Remember the word that I said unto you, the servant is not greater than his lord. If they have persecuted me, they will also persecute you.

They hated me without a cause, Jesus says, quoting the psalms.

But Jesus does not leave them alone in the face of the world’s hatred. When the Comforter is come, whom I will send unto you from the Father, even the Spirit of truth, he shall testify of me. 'And ye also shall bear witness, because ye have been with me from the beginning.'

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 5. 'I am the vine, ye are the branches: He that abideth in me, and I in him, the same bringeth forth much fruit: for without me ye can do nothing.' A branch does not strain and struggle to produce grapes. It simply stays joined to the vine, and the life of the vine flows through it. The fruit comes from the connection. Without me ye can do nothing. Not a little. Nothing. But joined to Jesus, our lives become fruitful, full of love, joy, peace, and good works that last.

Verse 9. 'As the Father hath loved me, so have I loved you: continue ye in my love.' Think about how much the Father loves the Son. That is how much Jesus loves you. Not a little less. The same. And Jesus calls us to continue, to remain, in that love. We do not have to earn it. We are invited to live in it, every day, like a branch living in the vine.

Verse 13. 'Greater love hath no man than this, that a man lay down his life for his friends.' Jesus spoke these words just hours before he laid down his life on the cross. He was not just talking about love. He was about to show it, in the greatest way possible. And he called his disciples, and he calls us, his friends.

Isaiah sang a song about God’s vineyard, Israel, which brought forth wild grapes. Jesus is the true vine, who bears the fruit God always looked for. And Paul wrote, 'But the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, longsuffering, gentleness, goodness, faith'.

How can you abide in Jesus today? Spend time in his Word, talk with him in prayer, and remember throughout the day that you are joined to him. And if God is pruning something in your life, trust that he is doing it so that you will bear more fruit.

Here is today’s question. Jesus said, without me ye can do nothing. What would it look like for you to abide in Jesus, instead of trying to bear fruit in your own strength?

Let us pray. Lord Jesus, you are the true vine, and I am a branch. Help me to abide in you, and to let your words abide in me. Thank you that you love me as the Father loves you, and that you call me your friend. Bear much fruit through my life, for the glory of the Father. Amen.

Next on the walk, John chapter sixteen. Jesus tells his disciples about the work of the Holy Spirit, and says, in the world ye shall have tribulation: but be of good cheer; I have overcome the world.
