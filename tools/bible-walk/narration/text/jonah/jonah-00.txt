The Living Voice. Meet the Book of Jonah.

God gives a man a job. Go east, to the great city of your enemies, and tell them to turn from their wickedness. The man gets up, and goes straight down to the harbour, and buys a ticket on a ship sailing west, as far away as he can go. He thinks he can run from God. He is about to find out that he cannot.

Welcome to the book of Jonah, on The Living Voice. Jonah has only four chapters, so on this walk it is four steps, one chapter at a time. It is another short win. You can finish the whole book in less than a week.

Most of the prophets in the Bible are books of messages, the words God gave them to speak. Jonah is different. It is almost all story. And the surprising thing is that the prophet is not the hero. Jonah runs away. He sulks. He gets angry with God. Again and again, the people around him, sailors and a pagan king, behave better than he does.

Jonah was a real prophet. The second book of Kings mentions him, Jonah the son of Amittai, from a town in Galilee. He lived in the time of the kings of Israel. In those days, the great power in the world was Assyria, and its capital was Nineveh, a huge city on the river Tigris. The Assyrians were feared for their cruelty in war. To an Israelite, Nineveh was the enemy.

And that is where God sent Jonah.

Most people know about the great fish. But the fish is only part of the story. The book of Jonah is really about the heart of God. About a God who cares even for the people we would rather he did not care about.

Here are three things to watch for as we walk.

Watch for the word great. A great city, a great wind, a great storm, a great fish. Everything in this book is big, except Jonah’s heart.

Watch for the word prepared. God prepares a fish, and later a plant, a worm and a wind. God is in charge of the sea and the creatures in it, and the weather. Even when Jonah runs, God is in control.

And watch for mercy. Jonah knew that God is gracious and merciful, slow to anger and of great kindness. That was exactly why he ran. He did not want God to be merciful to Nineveh. Listen for the question God asks at the very end of the book. It is a question for Jonah, and for us.

Jesus himself spoke about Jonah. He called his three days in the fish a sign, pointing to Jesus’ own death and rising again on the third day. And he said that the people of Nineveh turned to God when Jonah preached to them, and 'behold, a greater than Jonas is here.'

Here is a question to carry with you. Jonah ran from what God asked him to do. Have you ever wanted to run from something God was asking of you?

Let us pray. Lord God, as I walk through the book of Jonah, show me your heart. Thank you that you are gracious and merciful, slow to anger and of great kindness. When I want to run from you, bring me back. And give me a heart that cares for the people you care for. Amen.
