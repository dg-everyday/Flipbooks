The Living Voice. Jonah, chapter 3. The People of Nineveh Believed God.

A lone foreigner walks into the biggest city in the world. He does not bring an army. He does not perform a miracle. He simply walks through the streets, calling out one short sentence. And the whole city, from the king on his throne to the poorest family, stops what it is doing, and turns to God.

In chapter two, Jonah prayed from inside the great fish. He said, I am cast out of thy sight, yet I will look again toward thy holy temple. He remembered the LORD when his soul fainted, and he said, salvation is of the LORD. And the LORD spoke to the fish, and it brought Jonah up onto the dry land.

Now, in chapter three, God gives Jonah his job again. Listen for how quickly Nineveh responds, and for what the king says. And listen for what this chapter tells us about the God who sent Jonah there.

The word of the LORD comes to Jonah the second time.

Arise, God says, go unto Nineveh, that great city, and preach unto it the preaching that I bid thee.

This time, Jonah goes. 'So Jonah arose, and went unto Nineveh, according to the word of the LORD.'

Nineveh is an exceeding great city, so large that it takes three days to walk through it and its surrounding towns. Jonah goes in a day’s journey into the city, and he cries out, 'Yet forty days, and Nineveh shall be overthrown.'

That is all we are told that he said. In the Hebrew, it is only five words. No explanation. No offer of hope. Just a warning: in forty days, this city will be destroyed.

And then something astonishing happens.

The people of Nineveh, this great, proud, violent city, take God at his word. They call a fast, going without food, as a sign of sorrow. And everyone, from the greatest to the least, puts on sackcloth. Sackcloth was rough, scratchy material, like a sack. People wore it to show grief and repentance.

The news reaches the king of Nineveh. And the king gets up from his throne. He takes off his royal robe. He covers himself with sackcloth, and sits down in the ashes. The most powerful man in the city humbles himself before God.

Then the king and his nobles send out a decree through all of Nineveh. Let neither man nor beast, herd nor flock, taste any thing. Let them not eat, nor drink water. But let man and beast be covered with sackcloth, and cry mightily unto God.

Even the animals are to wear sackcloth. It is a striking picture of how seriously the whole city takes the warning.

But the king knows that fasting and sackcloth are not enough on their own. He goes on. 'yea, let them turn every one from his evil way, and from the violence that is in their hands.' Nineveh was known for its violence. The king tells his people that they must actually change how they live.

And then he asks a question. 'Who can tell if God will turn and repent, and turn away from his fierce anger, that we perish not?'

Who can tell? The king does not know if God will spare them. Jonah had not promised them anything. But he hopes that the God who sent the warning might also be a God of mercy. If God only meant to destroy them, why would he send a warning at all? Maybe the warning itself was a door left open.

And the king is right.

Think about how this story is turning out. In chapter one, the pagan sailors feared the LORD. Now, in chapter three, a whole pagan city turns to him, with their king leading the way. The only one in the story who has struggled to obey God is his own prophet.

And think about how little Jonah had to do. He did not need to be clever or persuasive. He only needed to go, and to speak the word God gave him. God did the rest.

The chapter ends with a city saved. Over a hundred thousand people, spared. You would think that this is the happy ending, and that Jonah would be overjoyed. Jonah’s preaching has done what most preachers only dream of. But the book of Jonah is not finished yet. In the next chapter, we will see how Jonah feels about what God has done.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 1. 'And the word of the LORD came unto Jonah the second time, saying,' The second time. Jonah had run away. He had disobeyed God completely. And God did not give up on him, or find someone else. He came back to Jonah, with the same call. God is the God of second chances. When we fail him, he does not throw us away. He comes to us again, and says, arise, go.

Verse 5. 'So the people of Nineveh believed God, and proclaimed a fast, and put on sackcloth, from the greatest of them even to the least of them.' The people of Nineveh believed God. That is where it all began. Not with good deeds, but with believing. They took God’s word seriously. And their faith showed itself in action, from the greatest to the least. Real belief always changes the way we live.

Verse 10. 'And God saw their works, that they turned from their evil way; and God repented of the evil, that he had said that he would do unto them; and he did it not.' In the old English of the King James Bible, the evil here means the disaster, the destruction God had warned of. And repented means that God turned from it. God does not change in his character. But his warnings are invitations. When people turn from their sin, God turns from his judgment. That is who God is. He would rather show mercy than judge.

Jesus said, 'The men of Nineveh shall rise in judgment with this generation, and shall condemn it: because they repented at the preaching of Jonas; and, behold, a greater than Jonas is here.' And Peter wrote that the Lord is 'not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance.'

Is there something in your life that you know you need to turn from? You do not have to wait. Like the king of Nineveh, you can humble yourself before God today. And like Jonah, if you have failed God, hear him calling you again, the second time.

Here is today’s question. God came to Jonah the second time, and he spared Nineveh when they turned to him. Where in your life do you need God’s second chance today?

Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that you are the God of second chances. Thank you that you came to Jonah again, and that you showed mercy to Nineveh when they turned to you. I turn to you now, from my own wrong ways. Help me to believe your word, and to obey it. Amen.
