The Living Voice. Joshua, chapter 3. They Passed Over on Dry Ground.

A river is in full flood, spilling over its banks, fast and brown and deep. On one side stands a whole nation: old people, children, families with their animals and tents. There is no bridge. There are no boats. And at the front, a handful of priests are walking straight toward the water, carrying a golden chest on their shoulders.

In chapter two, Joshua sent two spies into Jericho. A woman named Rahab hid them on her roof, and told them, the LORD your God, he is God in heaven above, and in earth beneath. She tied a scarlet line in her window, and the spies came back saying, truly the LORD hath delivered into our hands all the land.

Now, in chapter three, Israel comes to the river Jordan. Listen for where the ark goes, and what happens the moment the priests step into the water.

Joshua gets up early, and the whole people of Israel set out from Shittim, and come to the Jordan. There they camp for three days, looking at the river they must cross.

Then the officers go through the camp with instructions. When you see the ark of the covenant of the LORD your God, carried by the priests, set out from where you are, and follow it.

The ark of the covenant was a wooden chest covered in gold. Inside were the stone tablets of the law. It was the sign of God’s presence with his people. Where the ark went, God was leading.

And there is a strange detail. Keep a space between you and the ark, about two thousand cubits. That is about a kilometre. Do not come near it. Why? 'that ye may know the way by which ye must go: for ye have not passed this way heretofore.' If the ark is far ahead, everyone can see it, even at the back. This is a path no one has walked before. So they must keep their eyes on God’s presence, and follow.

Then Joshua speaks to the people.

And Joshua tells the priests, take up the ark of the covenant, and go before the people. So they do.

Then the LORD speaks to Joshua.

God tells Joshua what the priests must do. When you come to the edge of the water, stand still in the Jordan.

So Joshua gathers the people. Come here, he says, and hear the words of the LORD your God. 'Hereby ye shall know that the living God is among you'. Here is how you will know. 'Behold, the ark of the covenant of the Lord of all the earth passeth over before you into Jordan.' The Lord of all the earth. Not just the God of one river, or one land. And Joshua tells them that as soon as the feet of the priests rest in the water, the Jordan will be cut off, and the water coming down from above will stand up in a heap.

He also tells them to choose twelve men, one from each tribe. We will find out why in the next chapter.

So the people set out from their tents, with the priests carrying the ark in front.

Now the writer adds a detail. This is the time of harvest, when the Jordan overflows all its banks. The river is not low and gentle. It is at its very highest.

And the priests walk to the edge. And the moment their feet dip into the water, the river stops.

Far upstream, at a town called Adam, the water stands and rises up in a heap. And the water flowing down toward the Salt Sea is cut off completely. The riverbed empties. And the people cross over, right opposite Jericho.

Think of those priests. They had to step into a flooding river before anything happened. God did not part the water first, and then ask them to walk. He asked them to walk first. The miracle came as their feet touched the water.

That is often how faith works. God gives us his word, and asks us to take the first step. And sometimes we only see the way open as we walk.

Remember the Red Sea, long before, when Moses led Israel out of Egypt. Then, God opened the water to let his people escape from slavery. Now, God opens the water again, this time to bring his people into their home. The same God, at the beginning and at the end of the journey.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 5. 'And Joshua said unto the people, Sanctify yourselves: for to morrow the LORD will do wonders among you.' To sanctify means to make yourself ready and set apart for God. Before the miracle, Joshua asks the people to prepare their hearts. God was about to do wonders. They needed to be ready to meet him. When we expect God to work, it is good to come to him with clean hands and an open heart.

Verse 7. 'And the LORD said unto Joshua, This day will I begin to magnify thee in the sight of all Israel, that they may know that, as I was with Moses, so I will be with thee.' The people had seen God part the sea for Moses. Now they would see God open the river for Joshua. God was showing everyone that the same presence was with the new leader. Joshua did not have to prove himself. God would do it.

Verse 17. 'And the priests that bare the ark of the covenant of the LORD stood firm on dry ground in the midst of Jordan, and all the Israelites passed over on dry ground, until all the people were passed clean over Jordan.' Picture the priests, standing in the middle of the riverbed, holding the ark, while the whole nation walks past them. They do not move until every last person is across. The ark of God stood in the place of danger, so that the people could pass safely through. God goes before his people, and he stays with them until all are safely home.

A psalm remembers this day: 'The sea saw it, and fled: Jordan was driven back.' And through the prophet Isaiah, God says, 'When thou passest through the waters, I will be with thee; and through the rivers, they shall not overflow thee'.

Is there a step of obedience God is asking you to take, before you can see how it will work out? Keep your eyes on him, as Israel kept their eyes on the ark. Take the first step today, and trust him with the rest.

Here is today’s question. The priests had to step into the flooding river before the water stopped. Have you ever had to take a step of faith before you could see the way ahead? What happened?

Let us pray. Lord God, Lord of all the earth, thank you that you go before your people, and that you stay with us until we are safely through. When the way ahead looks impossible, help me to keep my eyes on you, and to take the next step in faith. Amen.
