The Living Voice. Joshua, chapter 5. As Captain of the Host of the LORD.

Joshua is alone, near the walls of Jericho, perhaps planning the attack. He looks up, and there is a man standing in front of him, with a sword drawn in his hand. Joshua walks straight up to him and asks the obvious question. Are you on our side, or on the side of our enemies? And the answer he gets is not the one he expects.

In chapter four, twelve men carried twelve stones out of the middle of the Jordan, and Joshua set them up at Gilgal. When your children ask, what mean these stones, he said, tell them, Israel came over this Jordan on dry land. And as soon as the priests came up out of the river, the waters returned.

Now, in chapter five, before any battle is fought, God prepares his people. Listen for what they remember, what they eat, and who Joshua meets near Jericho.

News of the river crossing spreads fast. All the kings of the land, west of the Jordan and along the coast, hear that the LORD has dried up the river for Israel. And their hearts melt. They have no courage left.

You might expect Joshua to attack straight away, while his enemies are afraid. But God has other plans first. Before Israel fights, they must be made ready as God’s people.

God tells Joshua to renew the sign of the covenant, the sign he first gave to Abraham, long before. The writer explains why. The generation that came out of Egypt had all been given that sign. But they had disobeyed God, and they had died in the wilderness, during the long years of wandering. Their children had grown up on the journey, and had never received the sign.

So now, in the land at last, the whole new generation receives it. They stay in the camp until they are well again.

And the LORD says to Joshua, 'This day have I rolled away the reproach of Egypt from off you.' A reproach is a shame, a disgrace. The shame of slavery in Egypt, and the shame of the wilderness years, is rolled away. And so the place is called Gilgal, which sounds like the Hebrew word for rolling.

Then, camped at Gilgal on the plains of Jericho, the people keep the Passover, at the time God appointed. The Passover was the feast that remembered how God brought them out of Egypt. Remember how it began, with the blood of a lamb on the doorposts, and God’s people saved. The last time Israel kept the Passover was in the wilderness. Now they keep it in the promised land.

The next day, they eat food grown in the land: unleavened bread and roasted grain.

And then something else happens. Something that had not changed for many years.

Think about that. For their whole lives, this generation had eaten manna. Every day, God had provided bread from heaven. It was all they had ever known. Now it stops. Not because God has stopped caring for them, but because he has brought them to a land full of food. God provides in different ways at different times.

Then the scene changes. Joshua is near Jericho, and he looks up, and sees a man standing opposite him, with his sword drawn.

Joshua goes up to him, and asks, 'Art thou for us, or for our adversaries?'

The answer is surprising.

Not for us, and not for our enemies. That is not the right question. The real question is not whether God is on our side. The real question is whether we are on God’s side.

Joshua understands at once. He falls with his face to the ground, and worships, and asks, 'What saith my lord unto his servant?' What does my Lord want me to do?

And the captain of the LORD’s host tells him.

Who is this captain? The chapter does not say. Some Christians have understood him as an angel, sent by God. Others have understood him as the Lord himself, appearing to Joshua, since he accepts Joshua’s worship. Christians have understood this in different ways. Either way, Joshua learns that he is not the real commander of Israel’s army. God is.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 12. 'And the manna ceased on the morrow after they had eaten of the old corn of the land; neither had the children of Israel manna any more; but they did eat of the fruit of the land of Canaan that year.' The manna had been God’s gift for the wilderness. Now that they were in the land, God fed them from the land itself. The same God who sent bread from heaven now gives them fields and harvests. Sometimes God provides through miracles. Sometimes he provides through ordinary work and ordinary food. Both come from his hand.

Verse 14. 'And he said, Nay; but as captain of the host of the LORD am I now come. And Joshua fell on his face to the earth, and did worship, and said unto him, What saith my lord unto his servant?' Joshua was the leader of Israel. But here he learns that he serves under someone greater. The host of the LORD means the army of the LORD. Before the battle of Jericho, Joshua needed to know who was really in charge. And his response is perfect. He bows down, and asks for orders. What saith my lord unto his servant?

Verse 15. 'And the captain of the LORD’S host said unto Joshua, Loose thy shoe from off thy foot; for the place whereon thou standest is holy. And Joshua did so.' These are almost the same words God spoke to Moses at the burning bush. Joshua, standing near an enemy city, is standing on holy ground, because God is there. Before Joshua is told how to win the battle, he is told to worship. And he simply obeys. Joshua did so.

God spoke to Moses at the bush with almost the same words: 'put off thy shoes from off thy feet, for the place whereon thou standest is holy ground.' And Paul wrote, 'If God be for us, who can be against us?'

Before you plan your day, or face your own battles, stop and worship. Ask God the question Joshua asked: what saith my lord unto his servant? Then listen, and do what he says.

Here is today’s question. Joshua asked, are you for us, or for our adversaries, and the answer was, nay. Why do you think it matters more that we are on God’s side, than that God is on ours?

Let us pray. Lord God, you are the captain of the host, and I am your servant. Forgive me for the times I have asked you to follow my plans. Thank you for rolling away my shame, and for feeding me every day. Teach me to bow before you, and to ask, what saith my lord unto his servant? Amen.
