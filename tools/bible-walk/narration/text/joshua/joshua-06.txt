The Living Voice. Joshua, chapter 6. Shout; for the LORD Hath Given You the City.

For six days, the people of Jericho have watched the same strange thing from the top of their walls. An army marches silently round the city, once, with priests blowing trumpets and a golden chest carried in the middle. No one shouts. No one attacks. They just walk, and go back to their camp. On the seventh day, they come back. And this time they do not stop after one lap.

In chapter five, Israel renewed the sign of the covenant at Gilgal, and God rolled away the reproach of Egypt. They kept the Passover in the land, and the manna stopped. Then Joshua met the captain of the host of the LORD, and took off his shoes, for the place where he stood was holy.

Now, in chapter six, comes the battle of Jericho. Listen for how strange God’s battle plan is. And listen for the scarlet line, and what happens to Rahab.

Jericho is shut up tight because of the children of Israel. No one goes out, and no one comes in. The gates are barred. The people inside are afraid.

Then the LORD speaks to Joshua.

And God gives him the plan. It is not a plan any army would choose.

All your fighting men are to march round the city once a day, for six days. Seven priests are to carry seven trumpets made of rams’ horns, in front of the ark. On the seventh day, march round the city seven times, with the priests blowing the trumpets. And when they make a long blast with the ram’s horn, all the people are to shout with a great shout. And the wall of the city will fall down flat, and the people will go straight in.

No battering rams. No ladders. No siege. Just walking, trumpets, and a shout.

So Joshua calls the priests, and gives the orders. The armed men go first, then the seven priests blowing their trumpets, then the ark of the LORD, and then the rear guard. And Joshua gives the people one more command. 'Ye shall not shout, nor make any noise with your voice, neither shall any word proceed out of your mouth, until the day I bid you shout; then shall ye shout.'

Silence. Thousands of people walking round a city, and not one word. Only the sound of feet, and of the trumpets.

The first day, they march round once, and go back to the camp. The second day, the same. And so for six days.

Imagine how it felt, day after day. Perhaps some people wondered if this was really going to work. Perhaps the people of Jericho laughed at them from the walls. But Israel kept walking, and kept silent, and kept obeying.

On the seventh day, they rise early, and march round the city in the same way, but this time seven times. And on the seventh time round, as the priests blow the trumpets, Joshua says to the people, 'Shout; for the LORD hath given you the city.'

Joshua also gives a solemn command. The city is to be devoted completely to the LORD, given over to him in judgment. No one is to take anything from it for themselves. The silver and gold and the vessels of brass and iron are to go into the treasury of the LORD. Only one household will be spared. Rahab, and everyone with her in her house, will live, because she hid the messengers.

This is the hard part of the chapter. Jericho fell under God’s judgment, and Scripture tells us that the people of the city did not survive. It is right to feel the weight of that. The Bible tells us that this judgment was not sudden or careless. God had waited a long time, and the wickedness of the land was very great. And even in Jericho, there was a way to be saved. Rahab heard about God, and trusted him, and she was spared.

Joshua tells the two spies, go into the woman’s house, and bring her out, and everyone with her, as you promised. So the young men go in, to the house with the scarlet line in the window. And they bring out Rahab, and her father and her mother, and her brothers, and all her family. Not one of them is lost. They keep their word, just as Rahab kept hers.

And the writer tells us that Rahab lived among the people of Israel from then on. An outsider from Jericho became part of God’s people.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 2. 'And the LORD said unto Joshua, See, I have given into thine hand Jericho, and the king thereof, and the mighty men of valour.' Notice the words, I have given. Not, I will give. The battle has not started, the walls are still standing, and yet God speaks as if it is already done. When God makes a promise, it is as good as done. Joshua’s job was not to win the city. It was to trust God, and do what he said.

Verse 20. 'So the people shouted when the priests blew with the trumpets: and it came to pass, when the people heard the sound of the trumpet, and the people shouted with a great shout, that the wall fell down flat, so that the people went up into the city, every man straight before him, and they took the city.' The walls did not fall because of the noise. They fell because God brought them down. The shout was a shout of faith, trusting that God would do what he had promised. Strong walls are nothing to God. And the people walked straight in, every man straight before him.

Verse 27. 'So the LORD was with Joshua; and his fame was noised throughout all the country.' This is the promise of chapter one, coming true. God had said, as I was with Moses, so I will be with thee. Now everyone in the land is talking about Joshua. But notice the order. First, the LORD was with Joshua. Then, his fame spread. Joshua’s success came from God’s presence, not from his own skill.

The letter to the Hebrews says, 'By faith the walls of Jericho fell down, after they were compassed about seven days.' And Paul wrote, 'For the weapons of our warfare are not carnal, but mighty through God to the pulling down of strong holds'.

Is there a wall in your life that seems too strong to come down? A habit, a fear, or a problem that will not move? Keep walking with God, and keep obeying, even when nothing seems to change. Bring that wall to him in prayer today.

Here is today’s question. Israel walked round Jericho for seven days before anything happened. What helps you to keep trusting and obeying God when you cannot yet see any change?

Let us pray. Lord God, nothing is too hard for you. No wall is too strong for you. Thank you that you saved Rahab and her family, and that you save all who trust in you. Help me to keep walking in obedience, even when I cannot see what you are doing, and to trust that what you promise, you will do. Amen.
