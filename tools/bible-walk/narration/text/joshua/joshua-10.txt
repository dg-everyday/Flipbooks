The Living Voice. Joshua, chapter 10. Sun, Stand Thou Still.

An army has been marching uphill in the dark, all through the hours when they should have been asleep. Now they burst upon their enemies by surprise. The enemy runs. Huge hailstones crash down from the sky. And in the middle of it all, the leader of the army looks up at the sun, and the moon, and speaks to them. And the Bible says that they obeyed.

In chapter nine, the Gibeonites tricked Israel with mouldy bread and worn-out shoes, pretending to come from a far country. Israel asked not counsel at the mouth of the LORD, and made a league with them. When the truth came out, Israel kept its promise anyway, because they had sworn by the LORD.

Now, in chapter ten, that promise is put to the test. Gibeon is attacked, and calls on Israel for help. Listen for what Joshua does, and for the most famous prayer in the book.

Adoni-zedek, the king of Jerusalem, hears that Joshua has taken Jericho and Ai. And he hears that Gibeon, a great city with mighty men, has made peace with Israel. He is very afraid.

So he sends messages to four other kings, the kings of Hebron, Jarmuth, Lachish and Eglon. Come and help me, he says, and let us attack Gibeon, because it has made peace with Joshua.

The five kings gather their armies, and camp around Gibeon, and make war against it.

The men of Gibeon send an urgent message to Joshua at Gilgal. 'Slack not thy hand from thy servants; come up to us quickly, and save us, and help us'.

Joshua could have said, you tricked us. Why should we help you? But he had made a promise. And keeping a promise means more than just not harming someone. It means standing by them when they are in danger.

So Joshua goes up from Gilgal, with all his fighting men.

And the LORD says to Joshua, 'Fear them not: for I have delivered them into thine hand; there shall not a man of them stand before thee.'

Joshua marches all through the dark hours, uphill from Gilgal, and comes upon the enemy suddenly. The LORD throws them into confusion before Israel. They run, down the road to Beth-horon. And as they run, the LORD throws down great stones from heaven, huge hailstones. The writer tells us that more of the enemy fell from the hailstones than from the swords of Israel.

The battle belonged to the LORD. Israel fought, but God was fighting for them.

Then Joshua prays.

And God answers.

Many people have asked exactly how this happened. Christians have understood it in different ways. But the Bible is clear about the main thing. God heard the prayer of his servant, and gave Israel the time they needed to finish the battle. The God who made the sun and moon is Lord over them.

After the battle, the five kings flee, and hide in a cave at a place called Makkedah. Joshua is told, and he says, roll great stones over the mouth of the cave, and set men to guard it. Do not stop. Chase your enemies, and do not let them reach their cities.

When the fighting is over, the people come back to Joshua in peace. And no one dares to speak a word against Israel.

Then Joshua has the five kings brought out of the cave. He calls the captains of his army, and tells them to put their feet on the necks of the kings. In those days, that was a sign of complete victory.

And Joshua says to his captains the words God had said to him.

Then the five kings are put to death, and their bodies are placed back in the cave, and great stones are laid at its mouth.

After this, Joshua leads Israel through the southern part of the land, city after city: Makkedah, Libnah, Lachish, Eglon, Hebron and Debir. The writer tells it in a solemn, repeated pattern. These cities came under the same judgment as Jericho. It is hard to hear. But the writer wants us to see who was really at work. The chapter ends with these words. 'And all these kings and their land did Joshua take at one time, because the LORD God of Israel fought for Israel.'

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 12. 'Then spake Joshua to the LORD in the day when the LORD delivered up the Amorites before the children of Israel, and he said in the sight of Israel, Sun, stand thou still upon Gibeon; and thou, Moon, in the valley of Ajalon.' This is a bold prayer. Joshua does not ask for a little help. He asks for the sun to stand still. And he prays it in the sight of Israel, out loud, in front of everyone. Joshua believed that the God of the battle was also the God of the heavens. No request is too big for him.

Verse 14. 'And there was no day like that before it or after it, that the LORD hearkened unto the voice of a man: for the LORD fought for Israel.' To hearken means to listen. The amazing thing in this verse is not just that the sun stood still. It is that the LORD listened to a man. The God of all creation heard the voice of Joshua, and answered. And the same God still listens to his people when they pray.

Verse 25. 'And Joshua said unto them, Fear not, nor be dismayed, be strong and of good courage: for thus shall the LORD do to all your enemies against whom ye fight.' These are the same words God spoke to Joshua in chapter one. Now Joshua passes them on to his captains. What God gives us, we can share with others. Joshua had learned that God keeps his word. And now he wanted his men to know it too.

James wrote, 'The effectual fervent prayer of a righteous man availeth much.' And a psalm says, 'The heavens declare the glory of God; and the firmament sheweth his handywork.'

Is there something you have been afraid to ask God for, because it seems too big? Bring it to him today. He is the God who made the sun and moon, and he still hearkens to the voice of his people.

Here is today’s question. Joshua kept his promise to Gibeon, even though they had tricked him. What does it look like to stand by someone we have made a promise to, even when it is costly?

Let us pray. Lord God, maker of the sun and moon, thank you that you hear the prayers of your people. Thank you that you fight for us, and that no request is too big for you. Help me to pray boldly, to keep my promises, and to be strong and of good courage. Amen.
