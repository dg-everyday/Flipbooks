The Living Voice. Joshua, chapter 12. All the Kings Thirty and One.

Have you ever looked back through an old photo album, or a diary, and remembered all the things that happened in a year? Some pages bring back hard times. Some bring back wonderful moments. And when you reach the end, you realise how much you have been through, and how far you have come. Joshua chapter twelve is a page like that.

In chapter eleven, the kings of the north gathered at the waters of Merom, with horses and chariots as many as the sand on the seashore. God said, be not afraid, and Israel won the battle. Joshua burned the chariots, so Israel would trust in the LORD alone. Joshua made war a long time, and at last, the land rested from war.

Now, in chapter twelve, the writer stops and looks back. It is a list of names. But listen carefully, because every name tells a story. And listen for how the list ends.

The chapter begins on the east side of the Jordan, toward the rising of the sun. That was where the story of the conquest began, while Moses was still alive.

First the writer names two kings. Sihon, king of the Amorites, who ruled from Heshbon. And Og, king of Bashan, who was one of the last of the giants. These two kings came out to fight Israel, before Israel ever crossed the Jordan. Do you remember? Rahab spoke of them in chapter two. We have heard, she said, what ye did unto the two kings of the Amorites, Sihon and Og.

Then the writer crosses the river, and lists the kings on the west side of the Jordan, the land Joshua conquered.

He describes the land first. The mountains and the valleys. The plains and the springs. The wilderness and the south country. The land of the Hittites, the Amorites, the Canaanites, the Perizzites, the Hivites and the Jebusites.

And then the list begins. It is written very simply. After each king’s name comes one word: one.

'The king of Jericho, one; the king of Ai, which is beside Bethel, one;'

Jericho, where the walls fell down flat at the shout of the people. Ai, where Israel was first defeated because of Achan’s hidden sin, and then won, after the sin was dealt with.

'The king of Jerusalem, one; the king of Hebron, one;'

These were two of the five kings who attacked Gibeon, on the day the sun stood still.

The list goes on. Jarmuth and Lachish. Eglon and Gezer. Debir and Geder. Hormah and Arad. Libnah and Adullam. Makkedah, where the five kings hid in the cave. Bethel, where long before Jacob dreamed of a ladder reaching up to heaven. Tappuah and Hepher. Aphek and Lasharon. Madon and Hazor, the great city of the north. Shimron-meron and Achshaph. Taanach and Megiddo. Kedesh and Jokneam. Dor on the coast, and Gilgal of the nations. And last of all, Tirzah.

Why would the Bible include a list like this? It might seem dull to us. But to Israel, it was precious. Every name was a place where God had kept his promise. Every one was a reason to give thanks.

Think about how easy it is to forget what God has done for us. We remember our problems clearly. But God’s past kindness slips out of our minds. This chapter is a way of counting blessings, one by one. One. One. One.

Some of these names will come back later in the Bible. Hebron will be given to Caleb, the old man of faith, in chapter fourteen. Jerusalem will one day become the city of King David. And Bethel will appear again and again in Israel’s story.

But there is something else to notice. Although the king of Jerusalem was defeated, the city itself was not yet taken. We will find out later in the book that the Jebusites still lived there. The kings were defeated, but there was still land to be possessed. The next chapter begins with exactly those words.

Here is a lesson for us too. God gives great victories. But there is always more to grow in, more ground to take, more of his promise to walk into. A victory is not a reason to stop. It is a reason to keep going.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 6. 'Them did Moses the servant of the LORD and the children of Israel smite: and Moses the servant of the LORD gave it for a possession unto the Reubenites, and the Gadites, and the half tribe of Manasseh.' Twice in one verse, Moses is called the servant of the LORD. That is his greatest title. Not Moses the great leader, or Moses the lawgiver. Moses the servant. The victories east of the Jordan were not Moses’ victories. They were the LORD’s, won through his servant.

Verse 7. 'And these are the kings of the country which Joshua and the children of Israel smote on this side Jordan on the west, from Baal-gad in the valley of Lebanon even unto the mount Halak, that goeth up to Seir; which Joshua gave unto the tribes of Israel for a possession according to their divisions;' Moses on one side of the river. Joshua on the other. Two leaders, but one God, and one promise. The work God began through Moses, he finished through Joshua. When one servant of God passes on, God’s work does not stop. He raises up another.

Verse 24. 'The king of Tirzah, one: all the kings thirty and one.' After the long list, the total. Thirty and one. Every single one counted. Not one forgotten. God keeps careful count of his faithfulness, even when we do not. And this verse reminds us that God’s promises are not vague. They are real, and specific, and every one of them comes true.

A psalm says, 'Bless the LORD, O my soul, and forget not all his benefits'. And another psalm says, 'Many, O LORD my God, are thy wonderful works which thou hast done'.

Make your own list today. Write down as many things as you can remember that God has done for you. Answered prayers, times of help, people he has sent. Count them, one by one, and give thanks for each one.

Here is today’s question. The writer counted the kings one by one, each one a sign of God’s faithfulness. If you made a list of God’s faithfulness in your own life, what would be the first three things on it?

Let us pray. Lord God, many are your wonderful works. Forgive me for how quickly I forget what you have done. Thank you for every promise you have kept, every one counted, not one forgotten. Help me to remember your benefits, and to keep going into all that you have for me. Amen.
