The Living Voice. Joshua, chapter 14. Give Me This Mountain.

An old man walks up to Joshua. The two of them go back a long way. They were young men together, long ago, when they stood almost alone against a whole nation’s fear. Now the old man has come to ask for something. Not a quiet field, or a peaceful valley. He wants the hardest piece of land in the country, a mountain full of giants and walled cities. And he wants it now.

In chapter thirteen, God told Joshua, thou art old, and there remaineth yet very much land to be possessed. The land of the eastern tribes was described, and we heard that some peoples were not driven out. And we heard that the tribe of Levi received no land, because the LORD God of Israel was their inheritance.

Now, in chapter fourteen, the sharing out of the land west of the Jordan begins. And before any tribe’s borders are described, one man steps forward. Listen for what Caleb says about himself, and about God.

The writer tells us how the land was divided. Eleazar the priest, Joshua, and the heads of the tribes shared it out. And it was done by lot, as the LORD had commanded through Moses, for the nine and a half tribes.

He reminds us again that the Levites received no land, except cities to live in, with fields around them for their animals. And that Joseph had become two tribes, Manasseh and Ephraim. So there were still twelve shares of land.

Then the men of Judah come to Joshua at Gilgal. And with them is Caleb, the son of Jephunneh.

Caleb says, you know what the LORD said to Moses about you and me, at Kadesh-barnea.

To understand what Caleb means, we need to go back many years. When Israel first came close to the promised land, Moses sent twelve spies to look at it. Joshua and Caleb were two of them. The spies came back after forty days, carrying a huge bunch of grapes. The land was wonderful, they said. It flowed with milk and honey. But ten of the spies said, the people there are strong, the cities are walled, and there are giants. We were like grasshoppers in their sight. We cannot go in.

Only Caleb and Joshua said, let us go up at once, for we are well able to overcome it. If the LORD delights in us, he will bring us into this land.

But the people listened to the ten, and not to the two. They wept, and complained, and wanted to go back to Egypt. And so that whole generation wandered in the wilderness until they died. Only Joshua and Caleb, of all the adults who left Egypt, lived to enter the land.

Now Caleb reminds Joshua of that day. I was forty years old, he says, when Moses sent me to spy out the land. And I brought back word as it was in my heart.

Then Caleb tells Joshua what Moses promised. 'Surely the land whereon thy feet have trodden shall be thine inheritance, and thy children’s for ever, because thou hast wholly followed the LORD my God.'

And now look at me, Caleb says. The LORD has kept me alive all these years, as he said. 'and now, lo, I am this day fourscore and five years old.' Fourscore and five means eighty five.

And then he says something astonishing. I am as strong this day as I was on the day Moses sent me. As my strength was then, so is my strength now, for war, both to go out and to come in.

And then comes his request.

Notice what Caleb asks for. Hebron was the very place where the spies had seen the giants. The place that had terrified the ten spies. The place that had kept a whole generation out of the land. Caleb does not ask for an easy place to retire. He asks for the mountain that once frightened everyone else, so that he can show what God can do.

And Joshua blesses him, and gives him Hebron for his inheritance.

The writer adds that Hebron used to be called Kirjath-arba, after Arba, the greatest man among the giants. And the chapter ends with the words, 'And the land had rest from war.'

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 8. 'Nevertheless my brethren that went up with me made the heart of the people melt: but I wholly followed the LORD my God.' Caleb was in a small minority. Ten against two. The crowd went one way, and Caleb went another. He did not follow the LORD halfway, or when it was easy. He wholly followed him. That word, wholly, is used three times about Caleb in this chapter. It was the secret of his whole life.

Verse 12. 'Now therefore give me this mountain, whereof the LORD spake in that day; for thou heardest in that day how the Anakims were there, and that the cities were great and fenced: if so be the LORD will be with me, then I shall be able to drive them out, as the LORD said.' Give me this mountain. Caleb is eighty five, and he is still asking God for big things. But notice where his confidence comes from. If so be the LORD will be with me. Caleb does not trust in his own strength. He trusts in the promise God made, long ago. Faith does not retire.

Verse 14. 'Hebron therefore became the inheritance of Caleb the son of Jephunneh the Kenezite unto this day, because that he wholly followed the LORD God of Israel.' The Kenezites were not originally Israelites. Caleb’s family seems to have joined Israel from outside, like Rahab. And yet he became one of the great men of faith in Israel’s story. God does not care where we come from. He cares whether we wholly follow him. Caleb waited many long years for this promise. And God kept it.

Isaiah wrote, 'But they that wait upon the LORD shall renew their strength; they shall mount up with wings as eagles'. And a psalm says of the righteous, 'They shall still bring forth fruit in old age'.

Is there a promise of God that you have been waiting for, a long time? Do not give up. Caleb waited for many years. Keep wholly following the LORD today, and trust him to keep his word.

Here is today’s question. Caleb asked for the very mountain that had frightened everyone else. What is a mountain in your life, something that seems too big, that you could ask God to help you face?

Let us pray. Lord God, thank you for Caleb, who wholly followed you, even when everyone else turned back. Give me a heart like his, that follows you fully, at every age. When I face mountains that frighten me, help me to say, if the LORD be with me, I shall be able. Amen.
