The Living Voice. Joshua, chapter 17. The Mountain Shall Be Thine.

Five sisters stand before the most powerful men in the nation: the high priest, the leader of the army, and all the heads of the tribes. They have no father and no brothers to speak for them. In their world, land was passed down from father to son. But these five women have come to remind the leaders of a promise. A promise God himself had made to them.

In chapter sixteen, the lot fell to the children of Joseph. We remembered how Jacob adopted Joseph’s two sons, and crossed his hands to bless the younger, Ephraim. Ephraim received its land in the hill country. But they did not drive out the Canaanites of Gezer, and made them serve under tribute instead.

Now, in chapter seventeen, we hear about the other half of Joseph’s family, the tribe of Manasseh. Listen for the five sisters, and what they ask. And listen for how Joshua answers a complaint.

There is a lot for the tribe of Manasseh, Joseph’s firstborn son. Part of Manasseh had already received land on the east side of the Jordan, in Gilead and Bashan, back in the days of Moses. Now the rest of the tribe receives its share, on the west side.

The writer names the families of Manasseh. Abiezer, Helek, Asriel, Shechem, Hepher and Shemida.

And then he tells us about a man named Zelophehad. Zelophehad had no sons, only daughters. Five of them: Mahlah, Noah, Hoglah, Milcah and Tirzah.

In those days, if a man had no sons, his family’s land could be lost. The daughters would have no share, and the family name would disappear.

But these five sisters had done something brave, back in the days of Moses. They had come to Moses, and to the whole assembly, at the door of the tabernacle. Why should our father’s name be lost from among his family, they asked, just because he had no son? Give us a share among our father’s brothers.

Moses did not know the answer. So he brought their case before the LORD. And God said, the daughters of Zelophehad are right. Give them an inheritance among their father’s brothers.

Now, in the land at last, the sisters come forward again.

And Joshua and the leaders give them their share, just as the LORD commanded. So Manasseh receives ten portions of land, because the daughters of Manasseh received an inheritance among the sons.

The writer describes Manasseh’s land. It runs north of Ephraim, from near Shechem toward the sea, with the brook Kanah between Ephraim and Manasseh. It reaches up toward Asher and Issachar. Manasseh is also given some great cities in the valleys, like Beth-shean, Taanach and Megiddo.

But then comes the familiar sad note. 'Yet the children of Manasseh could not drive out the inhabitants of those cities; but the Canaanites would dwell in that land.' Later, when Israel grows strong, they put the Canaanites to forced labour. But they do not drive them out.

Then the children of Joseph come to Joshua with a complaint. Why have you given us only one lot, one share, when we are a great people, and the LORD has blessed us so much?

Joshua is from the tribe of Ephraim himself. He could have favoured his own family. But he does not.

The children of Joseph are not satisfied. The hill country is not enough for us, they say. And the Canaanites in the valleys have chariots of iron.

They wanted more land, but they wanted easy land. They did not want to clear forests, or face iron chariots.

So Joshua answers them again. You are a great people, and you have great power. You will not have only one lot.

Think about Joshua’s wisdom here. He agrees that they are a great people. But a great people should do great work. If you are as strong as you say, then use your strength. Do not just complain about what you do not have. Go and take hold of what God has given you.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 4. 'And they came near before Eleazar the priest, and before Joshua the son of Nun, and before the princes, saying, The LORD commanded Moses to give us an inheritance among our brethren. Therefore according to the commandment of the LORD he gave them an inheritance among the brethren of their father.' The five sisters did not demand something new. They held on to God’s word, and asked for what he had promised. And the leaders listened. This story shows that God cares for those who could easily be overlooked. He made sure these women had a place in the land.

Verse 15. 'And Joshua answered them, If thou be a great people, then get thee up to the wood country, and cut down for thyself there in the land of the Perizzites and of the giants, if mount Ephraim be too narrow for thee.' The wood country was the forest on the hills. If you want more land, Joshua says, there is plenty. But you will have to work for it. Sometimes we ask God for more, when he has already given us what we need. We just have not taken hold of it yet.

Verse 18. 'But the mountain shall be thine; for it is a wood, and thou shalt cut it down: and the outgoings of it shall be thine: for thou shalt drive out the Canaanites, though they have iron chariots, and though they be strong.' Though they have iron chariots, and though they be strong. Joshua does not pretend the task is easy. But he says, you can do it. The same God who brought down the walls of Jericho could deal with iron chariots. The question was whether they would trust him, and do the work.

Paul wrote, 'I can do all things through Christ which strengtheneth me.' And Jesus said, 'Ask, and it shall be given you; seek, and ye shall find; knock, and it shall be opened unto you'.

Is there something God has already given you, that you have not yet taken hold of? A gift, an opportunity, a job to do? Instead of complaining about what you do not have, ask God for strength to use what you do have.

Here is today’s question. Joshua told the children of Joseph, if you are a great people, go and clear the forest. Is there a forest in your life that God might be asking you to clear?

Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that you care for those who are easily overlooked, like the daughters of Zelophehad. Thank you for all you have already given me. Forgive me for complaining, when I have not yet taken hold of your gifts. Give me courage and strength for the work you have set before me. Amen.
