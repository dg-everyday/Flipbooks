The Living Voice. Joshua, chapter 24. As for Me and My House.

A whole nation stands in a valley between two mountains. Before them stands an old man. He has told them the whole story of what God has done for them. Now he asks them a question, and waits. Will you serve the LORD, or not? You must choose. And then he tells them what he has chosen.

In chapter twenty three, Joshua gathered the leaders of Israel for his farewell. One man of you shall chase a thousand, he said, for the LORD your God fighteth for you. Take good heed that ye love the LORD your God. And he said, not one thing hath failed of all the good things which the LORD your God spake concerning you.

Now we come to the last chapter of Joshua. All Israel gathers at Shechem. Listen for the story God tells, and for the choice Joshua puts before the people. And listen for the end of Joshua’s life, and the end of a much older story.

Joshua gathers all the tribes of Israel to Shechem, with their elders, their heads, their judges and their officers. And they present themselves before God.

Shechem was a special place. It was where God first promised the land to Abraham, long before, and where Abraham built an altar. Now, at the end of the conquest, the people return to where the promise began.

Then Joshua speaks, and he speaks God’s words. Thus saith the LORD God of Israel.

And God tells them their own story. Your fathers lived on the other side of the river, long ago, Terah, the father of Abraham. And they served other gods. But I took your father Abraham from there, and led him through the land of Canaan, and gave him Isaac. To Isaac I gave Jacob and Esau. And Jacob and his children went down into Egypt.

I sent Moses and Aaron. I brought you out of Egypt. When the Egyptians chased you to the Red Sea, I put darkness between you, and brought the sea over them. You lived in the wilderness a long time. I brought you to the land of the Amorites, and gave them into your hand. You crossed the Jordan, and came to Jericho, and I gave the land into your hand. Not with your sword, nor with your bow.

Listen to how many times God says, I. I took. I gave. I sent. I brought. The whole story of Israel is the story of what God did.

And then God says this. 'And I have given you a land for which ye did not labour, and cities which ye built not, and ye dwell in them; of the vineyards and oliveyards which ye planted not do ye eat.' Everything they had was a gift.

Then Joshua turns to the people.

The people answer quickly. God forbid that we should leave the LORD to serve other gods! For the LORD our God brought us out of Egypt, and did great signs in our sight, and kept us safe on the whole journey. So we will serve the LORD, for he is our God.

But Joshua surprises them. 'Ye cannot serve the LORD: for he is an holy God; he is a jealous God'. Joshua is not trying to turn them away. He wants them to understand how serious this promise is. God is holy. Serving him is not a light thing, or something to say without thinking.

And the people answer again. 'Nay; but we will serve the LORD.'

Then you are witnesses against yourselves, Joshua says, that you have chosen the LORD. And they say, we are witnesses.

Then put away the foreign gods that are among you, Joshua says, and turn your hearts to the LORD God of Israel.

And the people answer a third time.

So Joshua makes a covenant with the people that day at Shechem. He writes these words in the book of the law of God. And he takes a great stone, and sets it up under an oak tree, by the sanctuary of the LORD. This stone shall be a witness, he says, for it has heard all the words of the LORD. One more stone, to remember.

And after these things, Joshua the son of Nun, the servant of the LORD, dies. They bury him in his own land, at Timnath-serah, in the hill country of Ephraim. At the start of the book, Moses was called the servant of the LORD. Now, at the end, Joshua is given the same title.

And then the writer tells us one more thing. The bones of Joseph, which the children of Israel had carried up out of Egypt, are buried at Shechem, in the piece of ground that Jacob bought long ago. Joseph had asked for this before he died, believing that God would bring his people home. Now, at last, Joseph is home too. The story that began with Abraham at Shechem ends with Joseph laid to rest at Shechem. God kept his word, to the very end.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 15. 'And if it seem evil unto you to serve the LORD, choose you this day whom ye will serve; whether the gods which your fathers served that were on the other side of the flood, or the gods of the Amorites, in whose land ye dwell: but as for me and my house, we will serve the LORD.' Choose you this day. Not someday. This day. Everyone serves something. The question is what. Joshua does not wait to see what the crowd will do. He makes his choice first, and he makes it for his whole household. As for me and my house, we will serve the LORD.

Verse 24. 'And the people said unto Joshua, The LORD our God will we serve, and his voice will we obey.' Three times the people say it. We will serve the LORD. Serving God and obeying his voice go together. It is not enough to say the right words. We show that we serve him by listening to his voice, and doing what he says.

Verse 31. 'And Israel served the LORD all the days of Joshua, and all the days of the elders that overlived Joshua, and which had known all the works of the LORD, that he had done for Israel.' Joshua’s faithfulness shaped a whole generation. As long as Joshua lived, and the elders who had seen God’s works, Israel served the LORD. One faithful life can influence many others. But this verse also contains a quiet warning. What would happen when that generation was gone? Each new generation must know the LORD for itself.

Elijah would one day ask Israel, 'How long halt ye between two opinions? if the LORD be God, follow him'. And Jesus said, 'No man can serve two masters'.

Joshua said, choose you this day. Make your choice again today. Tell God, as for me and my house, we will serve the LORD.

Here is today’s question. Joshua chose to serve the LORD, no matter what anyone else decided. What does it mean for you to say, as for me and my house, we will serve the LORD?

Let us pray. Lord God, thank you for the whole story of your faithfulness, from Abraham to Joshua, and to me. Everything I have is a gift from you. Today I choose to serve you. As for me and my house, we will serve the LORD, and your voice we will obey. Amen.

That is the end of our walk through the book of Joshua. Well walked. You have finished another whole book of the Bible. Not one thing hath failed of all the good things God promised. Next, the story goes on in the book of Judges, as a new generation rises up in the land.
