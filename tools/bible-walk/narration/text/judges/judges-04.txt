The Living Voice. Judges, chapter 4. Is Not the LORD Gone Out Before Thee?.

Under a palm tree in the hill country sits a woman. People come from all over Israel to bring her their problems, and to hear what God says. One day she sends for a soldier, and tells him that God has a job for him: to fight an army with nine hundred iron chariots. And the soldier says, I will go, but only if you come with me.

In chapter three, we met the first judges. When Israel cried unto the LORD, he raised up Othniel, Caleb’s brother, and the Spirit of the LORD came upon him. Then Ehud, a man lefthanded, set Israel free from Eglon, king of Moab. And Shamgar fought the Philistines with an ox goad.

Now, in chapter four, the circle turns again. Listen for Deborah, and what she says to Barak. And listen for who wins the final victory.

After Ehud dies, the children of Israel again do evil in the sight of the LORD. And the LORD lets them fall into the hand of Jabin, a king of Canaan, who reigns in Hazor. The captain of his army is a man named Sisera.

Sisera has nine hundred chariots of iron. For many years, he cruelly oppresses the children of Israel. And they cry out to the LORD.

This time, God’s answer is a woman.

Deborah sits under a palm tree between Ramah and Bethel, in the hill country of Ephraim, and the people come to her for judgment.

One day, Deborah sends for a man named Barak, from Kedesh in the north. And she gives him God’s message. 'Hath not the LORD God of Israel commanded, saying, Go and draw toward mount Tabor, and take with thee ten thousand men of the children of Naphtali and of the children of Zebulun?' And God says, I will draw Sisera to you, by the river Kishon, with his chariots and his army, and I will give him into your hand.

It is a clear command, and a clear promise. But Barak hesitates. 'If thou wilt go with me, then I will go: but if thou wilt not go with me, then I will not go.'

Why does Barak say this? Perhaps he was afraid. Perhaps he wanted the prophetess near him, as a sign that God was with them. Either way, he is willing to go, but not on his own.

And Deborah answers.

So Deborah goes with Barak to Kedesh. Barak calls together the men of Zebulun and Naphtali, and ten thousand men follow him up to mount Tabor. And Deborah goes with him.

Sisera hears that Barak has gone up to mount Tabor. So he gathers all his chariots, all nine hundred, and all his soldiers, and brings them to the river Kishon.

Picture the scene. On the mountain, ten thousand men on foot. In the valley below, the most powerful army in the land, with its iron chariots. On flat ground, chariots were almost unstoppable.

And then Deborah speaks to Barak.

So Barak goes down from the mountain, with ten thousand men behind him. And the LORD throws Sisera and all his chariots and all his army into confusion. In the song in the next chapter, we will hear that the river Kishon swept them away. Sisera jumps down from his chariot, and runs away on foot. And Barak chases the chariots and the army, and Sisera’s army is defeated completely.

But Sisera himself escapes, running alone, to the tent of a woman named Jael. Her husband, Heber, belonged to a family that was at peace with Sisera’s king.

Jael goes out to meet him. Turn in, my lord, she says, turn in to me. Do not be afraid. So he goes into the tent, and she covers him with a blanket. He asks for a little water, because he is thirsty. She opens a skin of milk, and gives him a drink, and covers him again. He tells her to stand at the door, and if anyone asks whether there is a man inside, to say no.

Sisera falls fast asleep, worn out. And while he sleeps, Jael takes a tent peg and a hammer, and Sisera dies.

When Barak comes chasing after Sisera, Jael goes out to meet him. Come, she says, and I will show you the man you are looking for. And Barak goes into the tent, and there lies Sisera, dead.

Deborah’s words have come true. The LORD sold Sisera into the hand of a woman. Not Deborah, but Jael, a woman no one expected.

This is a fierce story, and the Bible tells it without softening. Sisera had cruelly oppressed God’s people for many years. And in that dark time, God used two women, Deborah and Jael, to set his people free.

'So God subdued on that day Jabin the king of Canaan before the children of Israel.'

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 4. 'And Deborah, a prophetess, the wife of Lapidoth, she judged Israel at that time.' A prophetess is someone who speaks God’s word. In a time when the men of Israel were not leading as they should, God raised up Deborah. She listened to God, and spoke his word, and the people came to her for wisdom. God calls whom he chooses. And Deborah’s strength came from listening to him.

Verse 9. 'And she said, I will surely go with thee: notwithstanding the journey that thou takest shall not be for thine honour; for the LORD shall sell Sisera into the hand of a woman. And Deborah arose, and went with Barak to Kedesh.' Deborah agrees to go. But because Barak would not go alone, the honour of capturing Sisera would go to someone else. Barak did obey in the end, and the letter to the Hebrews lists him among the people of faith. But he missed something, because he hesitated.

Verse 14. 'And Deborah said unto Barak, Up; for this is the day in which the LORD hath delivered Sisera into thine hand: is not the LORD gone out before thee? So Barak went down from mount Tabor, and ten thousand men after him.' Is not the LORD gone out before thee? Barak was not going into the valley alone. The LORD had already gone ahead of him. That is why he could go down the mountain to face nine hundred chariots. When God goes before us, we can face what frightens us most.

The letter to the Hebrews names Barak among those 'Who through faith subdued kingdoms'. And Moses told Israel, 'And the LORD, he it is that doth go before thee; he will be with thee, he will not fail thee, neither forsake thee'.

Is there something God is asking you to do, but you are holding back, waiting for someone else to go with you? Remember Deborah’s words: is not the LORD gone out before thee? Take one step forward today.

Here is today’s question. Barak would only go if Deborah went with him. Is it wrong to want others with us when God calls us, or can it be a good thing? What do you think?

Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that you go out before your people. Thank you for Deborah, who listened to you and spoke your word. When I am afraid, remind me that you have gone ahead of me. Help me to obey you quickly, and not to hold back. Amen.
