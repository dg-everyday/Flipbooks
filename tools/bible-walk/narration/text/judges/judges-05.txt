The Living Voice. Judges, chapter 5. The Stars in Their Courses Fought.

The battle is over. The chariots lie broken in the mud. The enemy is gone. And the first thing the people do is sing. Not a quiet hymn, but a loud, wild, joyful song, full of storms and stars and rushing rivers. It is one of the oldest songs in the Bible. And it tells the story of the battle the way only a song can.

In chapter four, Deborah the prophetess called Barak to fight Sisera and his nine hundred chariots. Barak said, if thou wilt go with me, I will go. Deborah said, is not the LORD gone out before thee? The LORD threw Sisera’s army into confusion, and Sisera fled on foot to the tent of Jael, where he died. And God subdued Jabin the king of Canaan.

Now, in chapter five, Deborah and Barak sing about it. Listen for who gets the praise. And listen for the tribes who came to help, and the tribes who stayed at home.

On that day, Deborah and Barak sing.

The song begins with praise. 'Praise ye the LORD for the avenging of Israel, when the people willingly offered themselves.' Notice that. The leaders led, and the people willingly offered themselves. Nobody forced them. They chose to step forward.

Then Deborah calls out to the kings and princes of the earth.

She remembers how God came to help his people long before. LORD, when you marched out from Seir, from the land of Edom, the earth trembled, and the heavens poured, and the clouds poured water. The mountains melted before the LORD, even Sinai, before the LORD God of Israel.

Then she remembers how bad things had been. In the days of Shamgar, and in the days of Jael, the main roads were empty. Travellers crept along back paths, afraid. The villages were deserted. 'until that I Deborah arose, that I arose a mother in Israel.'

A mother in Israel. Deborah does not call herself a general or a queen. She calls herself a mother, someone who cares for her people.

She tells us why things had become so bad. 'They chose new gods; then was war in the gates'. When Israel turned from the LORD, trouble came right to their doors.

Then the song calls out to everyone. You who ride on white donkeys, you who sit in judgment, you who walk along the road. Tell of the righteous acts of the LORD. At the wells where the women draw water, let them repeat the story of what God has done.

'Awake, awake, Deborah: awake, awake, utter a song: arise, Barak, and lead thy captivity captive, thou son of Abinoam.'

Then comes a roll call of the tribes. Some came gladly. Ephraim, Benjamin and Machir. Zebulun, and the princes of Issachar, who went with Deborah. 'Zebulun and Naphtali were a people that jeoparded their lives unto the death in the high places of the field.' They risked everything.

But others stayed away. Reuben had great searchings of heart, but stayed among the sheepfolds, listening to the bleating of the flocks. Gilead stayed beyond the Jordan. Dan stayed with the ships. Asher sat by the seashore. They thought about it, they talked about it, but they did not come.

There is a challenge here. When God’s people are in need, it is easy to have great thoughts of heart, and still do nothing.

Then the song tells of the battle. The kings of Canaan came and fought, by the waters of Megiddo.

And then, 'The river of Kishon swept them away, that ancient river, the river Kishon.' It seems that a great storm came, and the river flooded, and the iron chariots were stuck and swept away. The weapon Sisera trusted most became useless.

The song praises Jael as most blessed among the women who live in tents. And it tells again, in poetry, how Sisera fell at her feet.

Then the song turns, unexpectedly, to Sisera’s mother. She looks out of her window, waiting. Why is his chariot so long in coming? Her wise ladies comfort her. They must be dividing the spoil, they say. She is imagining the treasure her son will bring home. But we know that he will never come home.

It is a strange and moving moment. Even in a song of victory, the writer stops to show us the other side, a mother waiting for a son who is not coming back. War is never only glory.

And the song ends with a prayer.

'And the land had rest forty years.'

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 3. 'Hear, O ye kings; give ear, O ye princes; I, even I, will sing unto the LORD; I will sing praise to the LORD God of Israel.' Deborah wants the whole world to hear. But she does not sing about herself, or about Barak, or about Israel’s army. She sings to the LORD. When God gives us a victory, the first thing to do is to praise him, and to tell others what he has done.

Verse 20. 'They fought from heaven; the stars in their courses fought against Sisera.' This is poetry. The stars did not leave the sky. But the song is saying that all of creation was on God’s side. The storm, the flooding river, the very heavens, all fought against Sisera. When God fights for his people, even the weather obeys him. Sisera trusted in iron chariots. But nothing can stand against the God who made the stars.

Verse 31. 'So let all thine enemies perish, O LORD: but let them that love him be as the sun when he goeth forth in his might. And the land had rest forty years.' Let them that love him be as the sun when he goeth forth in his might. What a beautiful picture. The sun rising, strong and bright, chasing away the dark. That is how Deborah prays for the people who love God. Not weak and fading, but shining brighter and brighter.

Moses and Israel sang after the Red Sea, 'I will sing unto the LORD, for he hath triumphed gloriously'. And a proverb says, 'But the path of the just is as the shining light, that shineth more and more unto the perfect day.'

Think of something God has done for you recently. Sing about it today, or say a psalm of praise out loud. And think about whether God is calling you to step forward and help someone, rather than staying among the sheepfolds.

Here is today’s question. Some tribes came to help, and some stayed at home with great searchings of heart. What helps you to move from thinking about doing good, to actually doing it?

Let us pray. Lord God, I will sing praise to you, for you have done great things. Thank you that all creation obeys you. Make me willing to offer myself when you call. And let me, and all who love you, be like the sun when it rises in its strength. Amen.
