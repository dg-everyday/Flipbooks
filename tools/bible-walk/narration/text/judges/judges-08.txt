The Living Voice. Judges, chapter 8. The LORD Shall Rule over You.

The people come to the hero of the hour with an offer. Be our king. You, and your son, and your grandson after you. It is what many leaders dream of. Power, honour, a royal family. And the hero says no. It is one of the finest moments of his life. And then, just a moment later, he makes one of the worst decisions of his life.

In chapter seven, God made Gideon’s army smaller and smaller, until only three hundred were left, so that Israel could not boast. With trumpets, clay jars and torches, they surrounded the camp of Midian in the dark, and cried, the sword of the LORD, and of Gideon. And the whole army of Midian fled.

Now, in chapter eight, Gideon chases the last of the enemy. Listen for how he answers anger, and how he answers the offer of a crown. And listen for the gold.

The chapter begins with a quarrel. The men of Ephraim are angry with Gideon. Why did you not call us when you went to fight Midian? And they argue with him fiercely.

Gideon could have argued back. Instead, he answers gently. What have I done, compared with you? God gave the two princes of Midian, Oreb and Zeeb, into your hands. What was I able to do, compared with you? And when he says that, their anger cools.

A soft answer turns away wrath, says the book of Proverbs. Gideon shows us how.

Then Gideon crosses the Jordan, still chasing the two kings of Midian, Zebah and Zalmunna.

Gideon asks the men of the town of Succoth for bread for his tired men. But the leaders of Succoth refuse. Have you caught the kings yet, they sneer, that we should feed your army? They were afraid to help, in case Midian came back. The town of Penuel gives the same answer. Gideon warns them both that he will come back.

Gideon and his men go on, and they catch up with the rest of the Midianite army, which thinks it is safe. Gideon attacks, and the army is defeated, and he captures the two kings.

On the way back, Gideon punishes the leaders of Succoth for refusing to help, and pulls down the tower of Penuel. These are harsh acts. The writer tells them plainly, without praising them. Gideon, the gentle man who calmed Ephraim, is becoming harder.

Then Gideon speaks to the two kings. He learns that they were the ones who had taken the lives of his own brothers at Tabor. And so the two kings are put to death.

Then the men of Israel come to Gideon with their offer. 'Rule thou over us, both thou, and thy son, and thy son’s son also: for thou hast delivered us from the hand of Midian.'

Notice what they say. Thou hast delivered us. They have already forgotten that it was the LORD who won the battle with three hundred men and some clay jars.

And Gideon gives a wonderful answer.

If the story ended there, Gideon would be one of the greatest heroes in the Bible. But it does not end there.

Gideon says, I have one request. Give me the gold earrings you took from the enemy. The Midianites wore gold earrings. And the people gladly spread out a cloak, and each man throws in his earrings. The gold weighs a great deal, besides the ornaments and purple clothes of the kings, and the gold chains on their camels’ necks.

And Gideon makes the gold into an ephod. An ephod was a special garment worn by the high priest, used when seeking God’s guidance. Gideon puts it in his home town, Ophrah.

Perhaps Gideon meant well. Perhaps he wanted a way to remember the victory, or to ask God for guidance. But it was not what God had commanded. And soon all Israel began to worship it, as an idol. The writer says it became a snare to Gideon and to his family.

The man who pulled down his father’s altar to Baal ends up setting up something that leads the people astray. Even the faithful can stumble, especially after great success.

Still, Midian is defeated, and never troubles Israel again. 'And the country was in quietness forty years in the days of Gideon.'

Gideon goes home and lives in his own house. He has many wives, and seventy sons. And a woman in Shechem also bears him a son, whom he names Abimelech. Keep that name in mind. It means my father is king. Remember that Gideon had said, I will not rule over you.

Gideon dies in a good old age, and is buried in his father’s tomb.

And as soon as Gideon is dead, the people turn again. They make a Baal their god. And they show no kindness to Gideon’s family, after all the good he had done for Israel.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 4. 'And Gideon came to Jordan, and passed over, he, and the three hundred men that were with him, faint, yet pursuing them.' Faint, yet pursuing. They were exhausted. They had no food. The people who should have helped them would not. And still they kept going. Sometimes following God means carrying on when we are tired, and others will not help. Faint, yet pursuing. That is what faithfulness often looks like.

Verse 23. 'And Gideon said unto them, I will not rule over you, neither shall my son rule over you: the LORD shall rule over you.' This is Gideon at his best. He knew that Israel already had a king: the LORD. He refused to take God’s place. Every leader, and every one of us, is tempted to take the credit and the control that belong to God. Gideon points the people back to the true King.

Verse 34. 'And the children of Israel remembered not the LORD their God, who had delivered them out of the hands of all their enemies on every side:' They remembered not. God had rescued them again and again. And as soon as the judge was gone, they forgot. How quickly we forget God’s goodness. That is why the Bible tells us again and again to remember.

A proverb says, 'A soft answer turneth away wrath: but grievous words stir up anger.' And Paul wrote, 'Wherefore let him that thinketh he standeth take heed lest he fall.'

Think about your greatest successes. Have you given God the credit for them? And is there anything in your life, even something good, that is starting to take God’s place? Bring it to him today.

Here is today’s question. Gideon refused to be king, but then made the golden ephod that became a snare. Why do you think people are often most in danger right after a great success?

Let us pray. Lord God, you are the true King. Rule over me. Thank you for every victory you have given. Keep me from taking the glory for myself, and from setting up anything in my life that takes your place. When I am faint, help me to keep pursuing. Amen.
