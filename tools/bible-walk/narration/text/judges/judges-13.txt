The Living Voice. Judges, chapter 13. Why Askest Thou After My Name?.

A woman is alone in a field. For years she has longed for a child, and none has come. Then a stranger appears, with a face that shines with a terrible glory. And he tells her that she will have a son. A special son, set apart for God from the very beginning, who will begin to rescue Israel.

In chapter twelve, the men of Ephraim quarrelled with Jephthah. At the fords of the Jordan, one word, Shibboleth, showed who was from Ephraim, and brothers fell at the river. Jephthah died, and three more judges came and went: Ibzan, Elon and Abdon.

Now, in chapter thirteen, we come to the last of the great judges. His story begins before he is even born. Listen for the angel’s message, and the questions Manoah asks. And listen for what the angel says about his own name.

The children of Israel do evil again in the sight of the LORD. And the LORD gives them into the hand of the Philistines, for a long time.

Notice something missing. This time, the writer does not say that Israel cried out to the LORD. They seem to have settled down under the Philistines, and stopped even asking for help. But God has not stopped caring for them. He begins to act before they ask.

In the town of Zorah lives a man of the tribe of Dan, named Manoah. His wife has never been able to have children.

And then the angel of the LORD appears to the woman.

This was the dearest wish of her heart. And the angel goes on. Be careful. Drink no wine or strong drink, and eat nothing unclean. For you will have a son, and no razor shall ever touch his head. He will be a Nazarite to God from his birth. And he will begin to rescue Israel from the hand of the Philistines.

A Nazarite was a person set apart for God in a special way. The law of Moses described it. A Nazarite did not drink wine or eat anything from the grapevine, did not touch anything dead, and did not cut his hair. Usually this was a promise made for a short time. But this boy would be a Nazarite all his life, from before he was born.

The woman runs to tell her husband. A man of God came to me, she says. He looked like an angel of God, very awesome. I did not ask where he came from, and he did not tell me his name. And she tells Manoah everything the man said.

Then Manoah prays. And it is a beautiful prayer. O my Lord, let the man of God whom you sent come again to us, and teach us what we should do for the child who will be born.

Manoah does not ask for proof. He asks for help to raise the child well.

And God hears Manoah’s prayer. The angel of God comes again to the woman, while she is sitting in the field. Her husband is not with her, so she runs and tells him. The man has come back!

Manoah goes with her, and asks, are you the man who spoke to my wife? And he says, I am. Manoah says, now let your words come true. How shall we bring up the child, and what shall we do for him? And the angel repeats his instructions.

Then Manoah wants to show hospitality. Please let us keep you here, until we have prepared a young goat for you. The angel says, even if you keep me, I will not eat your food. But if you offer a burnt offering, offer it to the LORD. For Manoah did not yet know that he was an angel of the LORD.

Then Manoah asks, what is your name, so that when your words come true, we may honour you?

The angel answers.

So Manoah takes a young goat and a grain offering, and offers it on a rock to the LORD. And the angel does a wonderful thing. As the flame goes up from the altar toward heaven, the angel of the LORD goes up in the flame. Manoah and his wife watch, and fall on their faces to the ground.

Then Manoah understands that he has seen the angel of the LORD. And he says to his wife, we shall surely die, because we have seen God.

But his wife is calm and wise. If the LORD wanted to destroy us, she says, he would not have accepted our offering. He would not have shown us all these things, or told us such wonderful news.

Manoah is frightened. His wife sees clearly. Her faith reasons from what God has done. God has been kind to us. He has made us a promise. He will not take it away now.

And the promise comes true.

The chapter ends with these words. 'And the Spirit of the LORD began to move him at times in the camp of Dan between Zorah and Eshtaol.'

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 3. 'And the angel of the LORD appeared unto the woman, and said unto her, Behold now, thou art barren, and bearest not: but thou shalt conceive, and bear a son.' Again and again in the Bible, God works through women who could not have children: Sarah, Rebekah, Rachel, and later Hannah and Elizabeth. Their sons were gifts that only God could give. This woman is not even named. But God saw her, and knew her sorrow, and came to her first.

Verse 18. 'And the angel of the LORD said unto him, Why askest thou thus after my name, seeing it is secret?' The word secret here can also mean wonderful, beyond understanding. Some Christians have understood this angel to be the Lord himself, appearing before he came as a man. Christians have understood this in different ways. But we can all hear the wonder in it. Isaiah would later say that the coming child’s name would be called Wonderful.

Verse 24. 'And the woman bare a son, and called his name Samson: and the child grew, and the LORD blessed him.' The child grew, and the LORD blessed him. Samson’s life began with every advantage. Godly parents who prayed for him. A special calling from God. And the blessing of the LORD. As we will see, the question would be what Samson did with all he had been given.

Isaiah wrote, 'and his name shall be called Wonderful, Counsellor, The mighty God'. And of another promised son, the angel said to Mary, 'For with God nothing shall be impossible.'

Manoah prayed, teach us what we shall do unto the child. Pray today for children you know, your own, or in your family or church. Ask God to help those who raise them, and to bless them.

Here is today’s question. Manoah was afraid, but his wife reasoned from God’s kindness to them. When you are afraid, how can remembering what God has already done help you to trust him?

Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that you see those who are overlooked, and that you know the sorrows of our hearts. Thank you that your name is Wonderful. Teach us how to raise the children in our lives, and bless them, as you blessed Samson. Amen.
