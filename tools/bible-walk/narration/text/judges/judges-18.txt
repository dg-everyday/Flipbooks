The Living Voice. Judges, chapter 18. Ye Have Taken Away My Gods Which I Made.

Six hundred armed men stand at a gate, while five others slip inside a house and carry out its treasures. A young priest watches them take his master’s idols. And instead of trying to stop them, he smiles, because they have just offered him a better job.

In chapter seventeen, Micah gave back the silver he stole from his mother, and she used it to make an idol, dedicated to the LORD. Micah set up a house of gods, and hired a wandering Levite as his priest for ten pieces of silver a year. In those days there was no king in Israel, but every man did that which was right in his own eyes. And Micah said, now know I that the LORD will do me good.

Now, in chapter eighteen, the tribe of Dan arrives. Listen for what happens to Micah’s gods and his priest. And listen for Micah’s cry.

In those days there was no king in Israel. And the tribe of Dan is looking for land to live in.

Remember that Dan was given land in Joshua chapter nineteen, in the west. But in Judges chapter one, the Amorites pushed Dan back into the hills, and would not let them come down. They had not trusted God to give them their land.

So Dan sends out five brave men from Zorah and Eshtaol to spy out the land. These are the same towns where Samson grew up. On their way, they come to the hill country of Ephraim, to Micah’s house, and lodge there.

They hear the voice of the young Levite, and they recognise his accent. Who brought you here, they ask. What are you doing here?

He tells them. Micah has hired me, and I am his priest.

So they say, please ask God for us whether our journey will be successful.

And the priest says, 'Go in peace: before the LORD is your way wherein ye go.'

It sounds holy. But did he really ask God? The chapter does not say he prayed. He simply told them what they wanted to hear.

The five men go on, and come to a town called Laish, far in the north. The people there live quietly and securely, like the people of Zidon. They are peaceful. They have no strong ruler, and they are far from anyone who could help them.

The spies go back to their brothers. Come on, they say. We have seen the land, and it is very good. Why are you sitting still? Do not be slow to go and take it. The people are unsuspecting, and the land is large. God has given it into your hands.

So six hundred men of Dan set out, armed for war, with their families. And on the way, they come again to Micah’s house.

The five spies say to the others, did you know that in these houses there is an ephod, household gods, a carved image and a metal idol? Think about what you should do.

So the six hundred armed men stand at the gate, while the five spies go in and take the idols and the ephod. The priest is standing at the gate with the soldiers. And he asks, what are you doing?

And the men of Dan answer.

And the priest’s heart is glad. He takes the ephod, the household gods and the carved image, and goes along with them.

Micah’s neighbours gather, and chase after the men of Dan, and catch up with them. The men of Dan turn round and say to Micah, what is the matter with you, coming after us with such a crowd?

And Micah answers.

The men of Dan say, keep quiet, or some angry men may attack you, and you will lose your life, and your family’s lives too. And when Micah sees that they are too strong for him, he turns and goes home.

Then the men of Dan come to Laish, to a people living quietly and securely. And they take the city, and burn it, and no one comes to help. They build a city there, and call it Dan, after their father.

And they set up Micah’s carved image for themselves. The writer tells us that the young Levite was a grandson of Moses, through Gershom. And his family were priests to the tribe of Dan for a very long time. A grandson of Moses, leading God’s people in worshipping an idol. That shows how far Israel had fallen in just a few generations.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 19. 'And they said unto him, Hold thy peace, lay thine hand upon thy mouth, and go with us, and be to us a father and a priest: is it better for thee to be a priest unto the house of one man, or that thou be a priest unto a tribe and a family in Israel?' The Levite had already sold himself to Micah for ten pieces of silver. Now he is offered a bigger job, and he leaves without a second thought. He did not ask what God wanted. He asked what was best for himself. A faith built on what we gain will always move to the highest bidder.

Verse 24. 'And he said, Ye have taken away my gods which I made, and the priest, and ye are gone away: and what have I more? and what is this that ye say unto me, What aileth thee?' My gods which I made. Listen to how sad and how foolish that is. Micah’s gods could be carried off by a gang of men. They could not even protect themselves. A god you can make can also be taken away. But the living God cannot be stolen. No one can take him from those who trust in him.

Verse 31. 'And they set them up Micah’s graven image, which he made, all the time that the house of God was in Shiloh.' All the time that the house of God was in Shiloh. The true tabernacle was there, in Shiloh, with the ark of the covenant, not so far away. The true God could be worshipped the true way. But the tribe of Dan chose their stolen idol instead. It is a warning for us. It is possible to have the truth nearby, and still choose something else.

A psalm says of idols, 'They have mouths, but they speak not: eyes have they, but they see not'. And Paul wrote, 'For the love of money is the root of all evil'.

Is there anything in your life that could be taken away, and leave you saying, what have I more? Thank God today that he is your treasure, and that no one can take him from you.

Here is today’s question. Micah cried, ye have taken away my gods which I made, and what have I more? What are some things people today depend on, that could be taken away from them?

Let us pray. Lord God, you are the living God, and no one can take you away from those who trust in you. Forgive me for the times I have leaned on things I have made, or followed what is best for me instead of what is right. Be my treasure, and my all. Amen.
