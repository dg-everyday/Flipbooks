The Living Voice. Judges, chapter 19. Consider of It, Take Advice, and Speak Your Minds.

A traveller sits in the town square as darkness falls, with his wife and his servant. In those days, it was a duty and an honour to welcome travellers into your home. But in this town, no one opens their door. No one offers them a place to stay. And that cold welcome is the beginning of the darkest chapter in the book.

In chapter eighteen, the tribe of Dan took Micah’s idols and his priest, and Micah cried, ye have taken away my gods which I made, and what have I more? The men of Dan took the quiet city of Laish, and set up the stolen idol there, all the time that the house of God was in Shiloh.

Now we come to chapter nineteen. Before we begin, a gentle word. This chapter tells of a terrible crime against a woman. We will not describe it in detail. But if you are listening with children, you may want to stop here, and listen to this one yourself first. The Bible tells this story honestly, so that we can see how far people fall when they forget God. Listen for who shows kindness, and who does not.

In those days, when there was no king in Israel, there was a Levite living in the hill country of Ephraim. He took a wife from Bethlehem in Judah. She was a secondary wife, in the custom of those days.

The marriage was not happy. She left him, and went back to her father’s house in Bethlehem, and stayed there for months.

Then her husband goes after her, to speak kindly to her and bring her back. He takes his servant, and two donkeys. When her father sees him, he is glad to welcome him.

The father keeps him there for days, eating and drinking. Each time the Levite tries to leave, the father persuades him to stay a little longer.

But this time the Levite will not stay. He sets off late, with his wife, his servant and his donkeys.

As the day is ending, they come near Jebus, which is Jerusalem. The servant says, let us stop and stay in this city. But the Levite says, no, we will not stay in a city of foreigners, who are not Israelites. We will go on to Gibeah. So they go on, and the sun goes down as they reach Gibeah, a town of the tribe of Benjamin.

The Levite thought they would be safer among their own people. He was wrong.

They go into the town, and sit down in the open square.

At last an old man comes in from his work in the fields. He is from the hill country of Ephraim, living there as a stranger. He sees the traveller in the square, and asks, where are you going, and where do you come from?

The Levite explains that they are going home, and that no one will take them in, though they have food for themselves and their donkeys.

And the old man answers kindly.

So he brings them into his house, and feeds the donkeys. They wash their feet, and eat and drink.

But while they are enjoying themselves, some wicked men of the city surround the house, and beat on the door. They demand that the old man bring out his guest. They intend him great harm.

It is like what the men of Sodom did at Lot’s house, long before. Gibeah, a town in Israel, has become like Sodom.

The old man goes out and pleads with them not to do such a wicked thing. But they will not listen. And then the Levite does something shameful. To save himself, he puts his wife outside the door, into the hands of those men.

They do terrible things to her, all through the dark hours, until it is nearly light. Then they let her go. She comes back to the door of the house where her husband is, and falls down there, with her hands on the threshold.

When the Levite opens the door to go on his way, there she is. He says, get up, let us go. But there is no answer. She has died.

He puts her on his donkey, and goes home. And there he sends a shocking message to all the tribes of Israel, a sign of what had been done, so that no one could ignore it.

Everyone who sees it says, nothing like this has ever happened, or been seen, since the day Israel came up out of Egypt.

This is a story with no hero. The men of Gibeah were wicked. The Levite was cowardly and cold, and thought only of himself. The only kindness came from an old stranger. And the one who suffered most was a woman whose name we are never told. But God saw her. Not one of her tears was hidden from him.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 15. 'And they turned aside thither, to go in and to lodge in Gibeah: and when he went in, he sat him down in a street of the city: for there was no man that took them into his house to lodging.' No man took them in. God’s law told Israel to love the stranger, and to welcome travellers. But in Gibeah, no one cared. The terrible evil later in the chapter began with a closed door. When kindness disappears from a community, worse things soon follow.

Verse 20. 'And the old man said, Peace be with thee; howsoever let all thy wants lie upon me; only lodge not in the street.' In the middle of this dark chapter, here is a small light. An old man, a stranger himself, coming home tired from the fields, opens his door. Let all thy wants lie upon me. That is what God’s people are meant to be like. Even when everyone around us is cold, we can still be kind.

Verse 30. 'And it was so, that all that saw it said, There was no such deed done nor seen from the day that the children of Israel came up out of the land of Egypt unto this day: consider of it, take advice, and speak your minds.' Consider of it. Stop and think. Do not look away. The Bible does not hide this evil, and it asks us to face it, and to ask how God’s people could fall so low. The answer is in the sentence we keep hearing: there was no king in Israel, and every man did that which was right in his own eyes.

The letter to the Hebrews says, 'Be not forgetful to entertain strangers: for thereby some have entertained angels unawares.' And a psalm says, 'The LORD is nigh unto them that are of a broken heart'.

Is there someone around you who is a stranger, left out, or in need of a safe place? Open your door to them today,. And pray for those who suffer cruelty, especially women and children, that God would protect them and bring justice.

Here is today’s question. In this dark chapter, the only kindness came from an old man who was a stranger himself. What does it mean for you to be a light when the people around you are cold?

Let us pray. Lord God, this is a hard and painful story. You see every person who suffers, and not one of their tears is hidden from you. Protect those who are in danger today, and bring justice for the wronged. Give me a heart that welcomes the stranger, and courage to be kind, even when others are not. Amen.
