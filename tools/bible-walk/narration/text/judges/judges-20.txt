The Living Voice. Judges, chapter 20. Shall I Go Up Against Benjamin My Brother?.

An entire nation gathers in one place. They are united as never before. Every tribe, from the far north to the far south, stands together as one man. But they have not gathered to worship, or to fight a foreign enemy. They have gathered to go to war against their own brothers.

In chapter nineteen, a Levite and his wife stopped in Gibeah, a town of Benjamin, and no one but an old stranger took them in. The men of the town committed a terrible crime, and the Levite’s wife died. He sent a message to all the tribes, and everyone said, there was no such deed done nor seen since Israel came up out of Egypt. Consider of it, take advice, and speak your minds.

Now, in chapter twenty, Israel responds. Listen for how often they ask God for guidance, and how often they weep. And listen for the cost of sin, not only to one town, but to a whole tribe, and a whole nation.

All the children of Israel come out, from Dan in the north to Beer-sheba in the south, and from Gilead across the Jordan. They gather before the LORD at Mizpeh.

A vast army stands ready. The tribe of Benjamin hears that they have gathered.

Then the people say, tell us, how did this wicked thing happen?

The Levite tells them about the men of Gibeah, and his wife’s death. But he leaves out his own part, how he put her outside the door to save himself.

The people rise up as one man. None of us will go home, they say, until this is dealt with. We will go up against Gibeah, and repay them for the evil they have done in Israel.

But first, they try to settle it rightly. They send men through all the tribe of Benjamin. What is this wickedness that has been done among you? Now hand over the wicked men of Gibeah, so that we may put away evil from Israel.

That was a fair request. Only the guilty would be punished.

But Benjamin will not listen.

Instead, the men of Benjamin gather from all their towns to Gibeah, to fight against the rest of Israel. Among them are some remarkable soldiers, chosen men, all left-handed, who can sling a stone at a hair and not miss. Ehud, who rescued Israel long before, was a left-handed Benjamite too.

The children of Israel go up to the house of God, and ask, which of us shall go up first to fight against Benjamin? And the LORD says, Judah shall go up first.

So Israel attacks Gibeah. And the men of Benjamin come out, and Israel is badly defeated. Many of Israel’s men fall that day.

The people go up and weep before the LORD, and ask again. Shall I go up again to battle against the children of Benjamin my brother? And the LORD says, go up against him.

Notice the words, my brother. Even in their anger, they know who they are fighting.

They attack a second time. And again, they are badly defeated, and many more fall.

Then something changes. All the children of Israel go up to the house of God. And this time, they do more than ask.

At that time, the ark of the covenant is there, and Phinehas the priest, the grandson of Aaron, serves before it. And the people ask once more. Shall I go out again to battle against the children of Benjamin my brother, or shall I stop? And the LORD says, go up, for I will give them into your hand.

So Israel sets an ambush around Gibeah, much like Joshua did at Ai. The men of Benjamin come out, and are drawn away from the city, thinking they are winning again. Then the men in hiding rush into Gibeah, and set it on fire. The smoke rises up to heaven. The men of Benjamin look back, and see their city burning, and they know that disaster has come upon them.

The battle turns. Benjamin is defeated. The men of Israel go through the towns of Benjamin, and destroy them.

By the end, the tribe of Benjamin is almost wiped out. Only six hundred men escape, and flee to the wilderness, to the rock of Rimmon, and stay there for months.

Think about how this happened. One town committed a terrible crime. Instead of handing over the guilty, the whole tribe defended them. And in the end, a whole tribe was almost lost, and many from every tribe of Israel died too. Sin is never as small as it looks. When it is protected instead of dealt with, it spreads like fire.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 1. 'Then all the children of Israel went out, and the congregation was gathered together as one man, from Dan even to Beer-sheba, with the land of Gilead, unto the LORD in Mizpeh.' As one man. It is the only time in the whole book that all Israel acts together. How sad that it took a terrible crime to unite them. And how sad that their unity was used to fight their own brothers. God’s people should be united, not only against evil, but in loving and serving him.

Verse 13. 'Now therefore deliver us the men, the children of Belial, which are in Gibeah, that we may put them to death, and put away evil from Israel. But the children of Benjamin would not hearken to the voice of their brethren the children of Israel:' Children of Belial means worthless, wicked men. Benjamin could have ended the whole thing by handing them over. But they chose loyalty to their own tribe over doing what was right. When we defend people simply because they belong to our group, even when they are wrong, we share in their guilt.

Verse 26. 'Then all the children of Israel, and all the people, went up, and came unto the house of God, and wept, and sat there before the LORD, and fasted that day until even, and offered burnt offerings and peace offerings before the LORD.' At first, Israel asked God which tribe should go first. But they did not ask whether to go at all. After two defeats, they weep, fast, and offer sacrifices. They humble themselves. Perhaps God was teaching them that they too needed to come before him in humility, not only Benjamin. Before we judge others, we must humble ourselves before God.

Paul wrote, 'Know ye not that a little leaven leaveneth the whole lump?' And a psalm says, 'Behold, how good and how pleasant it is for brethren to dwell together in unity!'

Is there a situation where you have been tempted to defend someone just because they are on your side, even though they did wrong? Ask God today for the courage to stand for what is right, and for a humble heart before him.

Here is today’s question. Benjamin chose loyalty to its own people over doing what was right. Why is it sometimes so hard to admit that someone on our side has done wrong?

Let us pray. Lord God, you are holy and just. Forgive me for the times I have defended what was wrong because it was easier, or because it was my own side. Humble my heart before you. Help your people to deal with sin honestly, and to be united in loving and serving you. Amen.
