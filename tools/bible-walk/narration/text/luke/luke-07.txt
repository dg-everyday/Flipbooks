The Living Voice. Luke, chapter 7. She Loved Much.

A respectable man is hosting a dinner for a famous teacher. The guests are reclining around the table, as people did in those days. Suddenly a woman slips into the room, a woman the whole town knows has lived a sinful life. She kneels at the teacher’s feet and begins to weep, so much that her tears wet his feet. She wipes them with her hair, kisses them, and pours precious ointment on them. And the host is thinking, if this man were really a prophet, he would know what kind of woman is touching him.

In chapter six, Jesus spent long hours in prayer, chose his twelve apostles, and taught the crowds to love their enemies and be merciful as their Father is merciful. Now, in chapter seven, we see that mercy in action. Jesus meets a Roman soldier, a grieving mother, a doubting prophet in prison, and a sinful woman. Watch who has faith, and who has love.

In Capernaum, a centurion, a Roman officer over a hundred soldiers, has a servant he values dearly, who is sick and about to die. He sends Jewish elders to ask Jesus to come and heal him. They tell Jesus he deserves it, 'For he loveth our nation, and he hath built us a synagogue.'

Jesus goes with them. But before he reaches the house, the centurion sends friends with a message: Lord, do not trouble yourself, 'for I am not worthy that thou shouldest enter under my roof'. Just say the word, and my servant will be healed. For I too am a man under authority, with soldiers under me. I say to one, go, and he goes, and to another, come, and he comes. Jesus marvels at him, and turns to the crowd. When the messengers return to the house, they find the servant healed.

Soon afterward, Jesus comes to a town called Nain, with a large crowd. As he nears the town gate, a funeral is coming out: a young man, 'the only son of his mother, and she was a widow.' In those days a widow who lost her only son lost everything, her family, her protection and her support. When the Lord sees her, his heart goes out to her. He touches the coffin, and the bearers stand still. 'Young man, I say unto thee, Arise.' The dead man sits up and begins to speak, 'And he delivered him to his mother.' Fear comes on everyone, and they glorify God: 'God hath visited his people.'

In prison, John the Baptist hears what Jesus is doing, and sends two of his disciples to ask, 'Art thou he that should come? or look we for another?' Even John, the great prophet, has questions. Jesus heals many people right then, and tells the messengers to go and tell John what they have seen: the blind see, the lame walk, lepers are cleansed, the deaf hear, the dead are raised, and the poor have the gospel preached to them. Then Jesus praises John: among those born of women there is no greater prophet. And he says people are never satisfied. John came fasting, and they said he had a devil. The Son of man came eating and drinking, and they say, 'a friend of publicans and sinners!'

Then a Pharisee named Simon invites Jesus to eat with him, and the woman comes in with her alabaster box of ointment. Jesus knows what Simon is thinking. Simon, he says, I have something to tell you. Two men owed money to a lender. One owed five hundred pence, the other fifty. When they could not pay, he forgave them both. 'Tell me therefore, which of them will love him most?' Simon answers, the one he forgave most. You are right, says Jesus.

Then Jesus turns to the woman, and speaks to Simon. When I came into your house, you gave me no water for my feet, but she has washed them with her tears. You gave me no kiss, but she has not stopped kissing my feet. You did not anoint my head with oil, but she has anointed my feet with ointment. And Jesus says to her, 'Thy sins are forgiven.' The other guests begin to say among themselves, who is this that forgives sins? But Jesus says to the woman, 'Thy faith hath saved thee; go in peace.'

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 9. 'When Jesus heard these things, he marvelled at him, and turned him about, and said unto the people that followed him, I say unto you, I have not found so great faith, no, not in Israel.' Only twice do the Gospels say that Jesus marvelled, and this is one of them. The great faith he found was in a Roman, a foreign soldier. The centurion believed that Jesus’ word was enough, even from a distance. Great faith is simply trusting that what Jesus says, he can do.

Verse 13. 'And when the Lord saw her, he had compassion on her, and said unto her, Weep not.' No one asked Jesus to help the widow. She did not call out. He simply saw her, and his heart went out to her. Jesus notices grief. And when he said, weep not, he was not telling her to hide her sorrow. He was about to take its cause away.

Verse 47. 'Wherefore I say unto thee, Her sins, which are many, are forgiven; for she loved much: but to whom little is forgiven, the same loveth little.' The woman’s love did not earn her forgiveness. It came from knowing she had been forgiven so much. Simon felt he needed little forgiveness, so he showed little love. The more clearly we see how much God has forgiven us, the more we will love him.

Long before, near the same region, the prophet Elijah raised a widow’s only son and gave him back to his mother, saying, 'See, thy son liveth.' When the people of Nain saw what Jesus did, they cried, 'a great prophet is risen up among us'. But Jesus did not have to stretch himself over the boy or pray for long, as Elijah did. He simply spoke, and the dead obeyed.

Do you, like Simon, sometimes feel you need only a little forgiveness? Take a moment to remember how much God has forgiven you. And like the woman, show your love for Jesus in a way that costs you something.

Here is today’s question. Jesus said, to whom little is forgiven, the same loveth little. How will you show Jesus your love today, remembering how much he has forgiven you?

Let us pray. Lord Jesus, you saw the widow and had compassion, and you forgave the woman who loved much. Thank you that you see me, and that you have forgiven me so much. Give me faith like the centurion, and love like the woman at your feet. Amen.
