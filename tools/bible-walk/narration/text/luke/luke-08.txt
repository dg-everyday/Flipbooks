The Living Voice. Luke, chapter 8. Where Is Your Faith?.

A woman has been ill for twelve years. Luke, who was a doctor himself, tells us honestly that she 'had spent all her living upon physicians, neither could be healed of any.' Every doctor failed her, and all her money is gone. Now she is in a pushing crowd, trying to get close enough to touch just the edge of a teacher’s clothes.

In chapter seven, Jesus healed a centurion’s servant, raised a widow’s son at Nain, and forgave a woman who loved much. Now, in chapter eight, he travels from town to town preaching the kingdom of God. Luke shows us who travelled with him, tells one of his best known parables, and then shows Jesus’ power over a storm, over evil spirits, over sickness and over death. And all the way through, there is one question: where is your faith?

The twelve go with Jesus. And so do some women he had healed: Mary called Magdalene, out of whom he had cast seven devils, Joanna the wife of Herod’s steward, Susanna, and many others, 'which ministered unto him of their substance.' These women supported Jesus with their own money. Only Luke tells us this.

A great crowd gathers, and Jesus tells a parable. A sower goes out to sow. Some seed falls by the wayside and is trampled and eaten by birds. Some falls on rock, springs up, and withers because it has no moisture. Some falls among thorns, which choke it. And some falls on good ground and brings forth fruit a hundredfold. 'He that hath ears to hear, let him hear.'

His disciples ask what it means. 'The seed is the word of God.' The wayside is those who hear, but the devil takes the word from their hearts. The rock is those who receive it with joy but have no root, and fall away in a time of testing. The thorns are those who are choked with 'cares and riches and pleasures of this life'. And the good ground is those who hear, keep it, and bear fruit with patience.

Then Jesus says that a lit candle is not hidden under a bed, but set where everyone can see. Take heed how you hear. When his mother and brothers come and cannot reach him through the crowd, he says, 'My mother and my brethren are these which hear the word of God, and do it.'

One day Jesus gets into a boat with his disciples and says, let us go over to the other side of the lake. As they sail, he falls asleep. A storm of wind comes down on the lake, and the boat begins to fill with water. They wake him: 'Master, master, we perish.' He gets up, rebukes the wind and the raging water, and there is a calm. Then he asks them a question. And they are afraid and amazed, and say to one another, who is this, that commands even the winds and water, and they obey him?

On the other side, in the country of the Gadarenes, a man possessed by devils meets him. He has worn no clothes for a long time and lives among the tombs. When he sees Jesus, he cries out and falls down. Jesus asks his name. 'Legion', he says, because many devils had entered him. Jesus sends the devils into a herd of pigs, which rush down into the lake. The townspeople come and find the man 'sitting at the feet of Jesus, clothed, and in his right mind'. But they are afraid, and ask Jesus to leave. Jesus sends the healed man home to tell what great things God has done for him.

Back across the lake, a ruler of the synagogue named Jairus falls at Jesus’ feet. His only daughter, about twelve years old, is dying. As Jesus goes, the crowds press around him. And the woman who has been ill for twelve years comes up behind him and touches the border of his garment, and at once she is healed.

Jesus says, 'Who touched me?' Peter says, Master, the crowd is pressing all around you. But Jesus says, someone touched me, for 'I perceive that virtue is gone out of me.' The woman comes trembling, falls down, and tells everyone why she touched him and how she was healed. And Jesus says, 'Daughter, be of good comfort: thy faith hath made thee whole; go in peace.'

While he is still speaking, a messenger comes from Jairus’ house: your daughter is dead; do not trouble the Master. But Jesus answers Jairus.

At the house, everyone is weeping. Jesus says, weep not; she is not dead, but sleeping. They laugh at him. He takes her by the hand and calls, 'Maid, arise.' Her spirit comes back, she gets up at once, and Jesus tells them to give her something to eat.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 15. 'But that on the good ground are they, which in an honest and good heart, having heard the word, keep it, and bring forth fruit with patience.' Three things mark good ground. An honest heart, that does not pretend. Keeping the word, holding on to it, not letting it be snatched away or choked. And patience, because fruit takes time. If you feel your faith is growing slowly, do not give up. Good fruit grows with patience.

Verse 25. 'And he said unto them, Where is your faith? And they being afraid wondered, saying one to another, What manner of man is this! for he commandeth even the winds and water, and they obey him.' Jesus did not ask, do you have faith? He asked, where is it? Their faith was there, but in the storm they had lost track of it. Faith is not a feeling we hold on to. It is trust in a person, and the person was in the boat with them the whole time.

Verse 50. 'But when Jesus heard it, he answered him, saying, Fear not: believe only, and she shall be made whole.' The worst news had just arrived. And Jesus spoke into it at once. Fear not; believe only. Jairus had to keep walking with Jesus toward a house of mourning, holding on to nothing but Jesus’ word. And Jesus’ word proved stronger than death.

Long before, God said through Isaiah that his word is like rain and snow that water the earth and make it bring forth seed. 'So shall my word be that goeth forth out of my mouth: it shall not return unto me void'. The sower scatters seed on every kind of ground. God’s word always does its work. The question the parable asks is what kind of ground it falls on in us.

Take heed how you hear. As you listen to God’s word on this walk, ask him to make your heart honest and good ground. And if you are in a storm right now, hear Jesus’ question: where is your faith? Turn it back to him.

Here is today’s question. Jesus asked his frightened disciples, where is your faith? Where will you put your trust today, in the storm you are facing?

Let us pray. Lord Jesus, make my heart good ground for your word, honest and patient. When storms come and fear takes over, remind me that you are in the boat. Fear not; believe only. I put my faith in you. Amen.
