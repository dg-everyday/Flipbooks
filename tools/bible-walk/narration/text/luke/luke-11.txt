The Living Voice. Luke, chapter 11. Lord, Teach Us to Pray.

The disciples have watched Jesus pray many times. They have seen him slip away to quiet places, and pray before every big decision. And one day, as he finishes praying, one of them comes to him with a simple request. Not, teach us to preach, or teach us to do miracles. Just this: Lord, teach us to pray.

In chapter ten, Jesus sent out seventy workers, told the story of the good Samaritan, and told busy Martha that Mary had chosen the one thing needful: sitting at his feet. Now, in chapter eleven, he teaches about prayer, and then he faces growing opposition. Some say his power comes from the devil, and the Pharisees and lawyers grow more and more angry as he exposes their hypocrisy.

When the disciple asks him to teach them to pray, Jesus gives them a prayer. 'When ye pray, say, Our Father which art in heaven, Hallowed be thy name.' Hallowed means honoured as holy. Thy kingdom come, thy will be done, on earth as in heaven. 'Give us day by day our daily bread.' Forgive us our sins, as we forgive everyone who owes us. And lead us not into temptation, but deliver us from evil.

Then Jesus tells a story. Suppose you go to a friend’s house very late, and knock, and say, a friend of mine has just arrived from a journey and I have nothing to set before him. Lend me three loaves. And your friend calls from inside, do not trouble me; the door is locked, and my children are in bed with me. I cannot get up. Jesus says, he may not get up because he is your friend, but because you keep on asking without shame, he will get up and give you all you need.

Then he gives a promise. And he asks, if a son asks his father for bread, will he give him a stone? Or for a fish, will he give him a snake? Or for an egg, will he give him a scorpion? Of course not.

Later, Jesus casts out a devil from a man who could not speak, and the man speaks. But some in the crowd say, he casts out devils through Beelzebub, the chief of the devils. Jesus answers that a kingdom divided against itself falls. When a strong man, fully armed, guards his house, his goods are safe, until someone stronger comes, overpowers him, and takes away his armour. 'He that is not with me is against me'.

A woman in the crowd calls out, blessed is the mother who bore you! Jesus answers, 'yea rather, blessed are they that hear the word of God, and keep it.'

As the crowds grow, Jesus says this generation keeps asking for a sign, but the only sign they will get is the sign of Jonas, the prophet Jonah, who was a sign to the people of Nineveh. The queen of the south came from the ends of the earth to hear Solomon’s wisdom, and the men of Nineveh repented at Jonah’s preaching, 'and, behold, a greater than Solomon is here.'

Then a Pharisee invites Jesus to dinner, and is surprised that Jesus does not first wash his hands in the ceremonial way. Jesus says, you Pharisees clean the outside of the cup and the plate, but inside you are full of greed and wickedness. You carefully give God a tenth of your smallest garden herbs, but pass over justice and the love of God. You love the best seats and the greetings in the market. One of the lawyers says, Master, you insult us too. And Jesus says, woe to you lawyers too, for you load people with heavy burdens and do not lift a finger to help them. You have taken away the key of knowledge.

When he leaves, the scribes and Pharisees begin to press him hard, trying to catch him in something he might say.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 1. 'And it came to pass, that, as he was praying in a certain place, when he ceased, one of his disciples said unto him, Lord, teach us to pray, as John also taught his disciples.' The disciples did not ask Jesus to teach them about prayer. They asked him to teach them to pray. Prayer is learned by praying. And it is all right not to know how. Even those who walked with Jesus every day had to ask. You can ask him too.

Verse 9. 'And I say unto you, Ask, and it shall be given you; seek, and ye shall find; knock, and it shall be opened unto you.' Ask, seek, knock: each word is stronger than the one before. Jesus is encouraging us to keep praying and not give up. If even a sleepy friend will get up because you keep knocking, how much more will your Father, who never sleeps and who loves you, answer those who keep coming to him?

Verse 13. 'If ye then, being evil, know how to give good gifts unto your children: how much more shall your heavenly Father give the Holy Spirit to them that ask him?' Notice what the best answer to prayer is: the Holy Spirit, God himself, living in us. Earthly parents, even with all their faults, want to give good things to their children. Our heavenly Father is perfectly good. He will never give his children a stone when they ask for bread.

Long before, David wrote in a psalm, 'Like as a father pitieth his children, so the LORD pitieth them that fear him.' Pitieth means has tender compassion on. Jesus teaches us to begin our prayers with that same truth: Our Father. We do not pray to a stranger, or to a God who must be persuaded to care. We pray to a Father who already loves us.

Pray the prayer Jesus taught, slowly, thinking about each line. Then bring God one thing you have stopped asking him for. Ask again. Keep knocking.

Here is today’s question. Jesus said, ask, and it shall be given you; seek, and ye shall find. What will you ask your Father for today, and keep asking?

Let us pray. Our Father which art in heaven, hallowed be thy name. Lord, teach me to pray. Thank you that you are a good Father who gives good gifts. Give me your Holy Spirit, and help me to keep asking, seeking and knocking. Amen.
