The Living Voice. Luke, chapter 12. Fear Not, Little Flock.

A rich farmer looks out over his fields. The harvest is so huge he has nowhere to store it all. So he makes a plan. He will tear down his barns and build bigger ones, and then he will say to himself, relax, you have enough for many years. Eat, drink, and be merry. He has thought of everything. Except one thing.

In chapter eleven, Jesus taught his disciples to pray, Our Father, and promised that the Father gives good gifts to those who ask. He also exposed the hypocrisy of the Pharisees, and they began to press him hard.

Now, in chapter twelve, crowds of thousands are gathering, so many that they tread on one another. Jesus speaks first to his own disciples, and then to the crowd. His theme is fear and worry: what to fear, what not to fear, and what to treasure.

Jesus warns his disciples first. 'Beware ye of the leaven of the Pharisees, which is hypocrisy.' Leaven is yeast, which spreads quietly through the whole loaf. Nothing is covered that will not be revealed, he says. What you whisper in secret will be shouted from the housetops.

Do not be afraid of those who can only kill the body. Fear God, who has power over far more than that. And then Jesus speaks tenderly. Are not five sparrows sold for two farthings, two of the smallest coins? And not one of them is forgotten before God.

Whoever confesses me before men, Jesus says, the Son of man will confess before the angels of God. And when you are brought before rulers, do not worry about what to say, 'For the Holy Ghost shall teach you in the same hour what ye ought to say.'

Then a man in the crowd calls out, 'Master, speak to my brother, that he divide the inheritance with me.' Jesus will not be his judge in the quarrel. Instead he warns everyone about the real danger, and tells them the story of the rich farmer and his barns. But God said to him, 'Thou fool, this night thy soul shall be required of thee: then whose shall those things be, which thou hast provided?' 'So is he that layeth up treasure for himself, and is not rich toward God.'

Then Jesus turns to his disciples. Do not worry about your life, what you will eat, or about your body, what you will wear. Life is more than food, and the body more than clothes. 'Consider the ravens': they neither sow nor reap, they have no storehouse or barn, and God feeds them. How much more valuable are you than the birds! Which of you by worrying can add a single inch to his height? 'Consider the lilies how they grow'. They do not toil or spin, and yet Solomon in all his glory was not dressed like one of them. If God so clothes the grass, which is in the field one day and thrown into the oven the next, how much more will he clothe you, O ye of little faith? Your Father knows you need these things. 'But rather seek ye the kingdom of God; and all these things shall be added unto you.'

Then he speaks to his little group of followers. Sell what you have and give to those in need. Make for yourselves treasure in heaven, where no thief comes near and no moth destroys. 'For where your treasure is, there will your heart be also.'

Be ready, Jesus says, like servants waiting for their master to return from a wedding, with their clothes tied up for work and their lamps burning, ready to open the door the moment he knocks. Blessed are those servants. The master himself will serve them at the table. Be ready, for the Son of man comes at an hour when you do not expect him.

Peter asks, are you telling this parable to us, or to everyone? Jesus speaks of a faithful and wise steward who looks after his master’s household well, and a foolish one who beats the servants and gets drunk. 'For unto whomsoever much is given, of him shall be much required'.

Jesus ends with hard words. He has come to bring fire on the earth. His coming will divide families. You can read the signs of the weather, he tells the crowd, so why can you not read the signs of this time? Make peace with your adversary while you still can.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 7. 'But even the very hairs of your head are all numbered. Fear not therefore: ye are of more value than many sparrows.' Sparrows were the cheapest birds in the market. Yet God does not forget a single one. And he knows you so completely that he has numbered the hairs on your head. You are never overlooked, never lost in the crowd, never forgotten by God. That is why you need not be afraid.

Verse 15. 'And he said unto them, Take heed, and beware of covetousness: for a man’s life consisteth not in the abundance of the things which he possesseth.' Covetousness means always wanting more. Jesus says your life is not measured by what you own. The rich farmer was not a fool because he was rich or because he planned ahead. He was a fool because he lived as if this life were everything, and as if God did not exist. He was rich in things, and poor toward God.

Verse 32. 'Fear not, little flock; for it is your Father’s good pleasure to give you the kingdom.' Notice how tenderly Jesus speaks. Little flock. His followers were few and small and often afraid. But their Father does not give his kingdom grudgingly. It is his good pleasure, his joy, to give it. We do not need to grab and hoard and worry. Our Father delights to give.

Long before, the Preacher in Ecclesiastes noticed the same thing as the rich fool: 'He that loveth silver shall not be satisfied with silver; nor he that loveth abundance with increase'. More never satisfies. Jesus offers a treasure that does: the kingdom of God, given freely by a Father who knows what we need.

What are you most worried about today? Name it, and hand it to your Father, who feeds the ravens and clothes the lilies. And look at where your treasure is: what you spend your time, money and thoughts on. Is your heart there too?

Here is today’s question. Jesus said, where your treasure is, there will your heart be also. What is one way you will be rich toward God today?

Let us pray. Father, you number the hairs of my head and do not forget a single sparrow. Forgive me for worrying, and for wanting more and more. Help me to seek your kingdom first, to treasure what lasts, and to trust that it is your good pleasure to give. Fear not, little flock. Amen.
