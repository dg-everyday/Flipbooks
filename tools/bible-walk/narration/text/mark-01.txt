Bible Walk. Mark, chapter 1. Prepare Ye the Way.

A man with leprosy kneels in the dust in front of Jesus. In those days a leper had to live apart from everyone else. People kept their distance, and nobody touched him. But this man comes close, kneels down, and says, 'If thou wilt, thou canst make me clean.' He knows Jesus can. He does not know if Jesus wants to. Before this chapter ends, you will hear what Jesus said, and what he did with his hand.

This is the first step of our walk through Mark, and Mark is in a hurry. There is no birth story and no family tree. His very first line is 'The beginning of the gospel of Jesus Christ, the Son of God.' Gospel means good news. Hold on to those last words, the Son of God, because the whole book is going to show you who Jesus is.

We are in the land of Israel. In the north is Galilee, with its fishing villages around a large lake, the sea of Galilee. Down the river Jordan, near the dry and empty wilderness, our story begins.

Out in the wilderness, a voice is calling. It belongs to a man named John. He is dressed in clothes of camel’s hair, with a leather belt around his waist, and he lives on locusts and wild honey. John is calling people to turn back to God, and they come to him from all over the country and from Jerusalem. They confess their sins, and John baptizes them in the river Jordan.

But John says he is only getting things ready. 'There cometh one mightier than I after me, the latchet of whose shoes I am not worthy to stoop down and unloose.' A latchet is a sandal strap. Untying sandals was the job of the lowest servant, and John says he is not even worthy to do that. 'I indeed have baptized you with water: but he shall baptize you with the Holy Ghost.'

Then Jesus comes, from the little town of Nazareth in Galilee, and John baptizes him in the Jordan. As Jesus comes up out of the water, he sees the heavens opened, and the Spirit coming down upon him like a dove. And a voice speaks from heaven: 'Thou art my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased.'

At once the Spirit drives Jesus into the wilderness. For forty days he is tempted by Satan, among the wild beasts, and the angels take care of him.

After John is put in prison, Jesus comes to Galilee, preaching the good news of God. Walking along the shore of the lake, he sees two brothers, Simon and Andrew, throwing their net into the water, for they were fishermen. Jesus says to them, 'Come ye after me, and I will make you to become fishers of men.' And Mark says, 'straightway they forsook their nets, and followed him.' Straightway is Mark’s favourite word. It means at once. A little farther on, Jesus sees two more brothers, James and John, the sons of Zebedee, mending their nets in their boat. He calls them too, and they leave their father in the boat with the hired men, and go after him.

In the town of Capernaum, on the Sabbath, Jesus teaches in the synagogue, and the people are astonished, 'for he taught them as one that had authority, and not as the scribes.' A man with an unclean spirit cries out, 'I know thee who thou art, the Holy One of God.' Jesus says, 'Hold thy peace, and come out of him', and the spirit leaves him. Everyone is amazed, and the news about Jesus spreads all over Galilee.

At Simon’s house, Simon’s mother-in-law is in bed with a fever. Jesus takes her by the hand and lifts her up, and the fever leaves her at once. Then, 'at even, when the sun did set', the whole town gathers at the door, and Jesus heals many who are sick.

Rising 'a great while before day', Jesus goes out to a quiet place alone, and prays. When Simon finds him and says, 'All men seek for thee', Jesus answers, 'Let us go into the next towns, that I may preach there also: for therefore came I forth.'

And then the leper comes, kneeling in the road. Jesus is moved with compassion. He reaches out his hand, and touches the man. 'I will; be thou clean.' At once the leprosy is gone. Jesus tells him to say nothing, but to show himself to the priest, as the law of Moses said. Instead, the man tells everyone, and the crowds grow.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 15. 'The time is fulfilled, and the kingdom of God is at hand: repent ye, and believe the gospel.' These are the first words Jesus speaks in Mark, and they sum up his message. The time is fulfilled: the long wait is over. For hundreds of years the prophets had promised that God would come to save his people, and now the moment has come. The kingdom of God is at hand: God’s rule has come near, because the King himself is here. Then Jesus asks for two things. Repent: turn around. Stop walking away from God, and come back to him. And believe the gospel: trust the good news. Turning and trusting. That is how anyone, in any age, begins to walk with God.

Verse 17. 'Come ye after me, and I will make you to become fishers of men.' Jesus chooses fishermen in the middle of their work. He does not say, first become better men. He says, come and follow. And notice who does the work: I will make you. They knew how to catch fish; now he will teach them to bring people to God. Following Jesus can cost us something, but it always comes with a promise.

Verse 41. 'And Jesus, moved with compassion, put forth his hand, and touched him, and saith unto him, I will; be thou clean.' The leper said, if thou wilt. He was not sure that Jesus wanted to help someone like him. Jesus answers his doubt with two words: I will. And look at what Jesus does before he speaks. He touches him. Jesus could have healed him with a word from far away. Instead he touches a man nobody had touched in a long time. Moved with compassion means his heart went out to him. That is the heart of Jesus. He is not only able. He is willing.

Mark begins his book with words from the prophets. Malachi wrote, 'Behold, I will send my messenger, and he shall prepare the way before me'. And Isaiah wrote of a voice crying in the wilderness, 'Prepare ye the way of the LORD, make straight in the desert a highway for our God.' Hundreds of years later, John is that voice, out in that very wilderness. Isaiah said the way was being prepared for the LORD himself. Mark tells us who came down that road. It was Jesus.

You do not need to understand everything to take one step after Jesus. And remember the leper. Today, bring Jesus the one thing you cannot fix, and say it plainly. He is still willing.

Here is today’s question. Simon and Andrew left their nets straightway. What is one thing you will lay down today to follow Jesus?

Let us pray. Lord Jesus, like the leper, I come to you with what I cannot fix. Thank you that you are willing, and that you are not afraid to touch what others turn away from. When you call me, help me to follow you, straightway. Amen.
