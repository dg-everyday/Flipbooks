The Living Voice. Mark, chapter 3. Whosoever Shall Do the Will of God.

A man stands in the synagogue with a hand that is shrivelled and useless. All around him, people are watching. But they are not watching the man. They are watching Jesus. If Jesus heals on the sabbath, they will have something to accuse him of. Jesus knows exactly what they are thinking, and he says to the man, 'Stand forth.' Come out where everyone can see.

In chapter two, the questions began. Why does he forgive sins? Why does he eat with sinners? Why do his disciples break the sabbath? Now, in chapter three, the questions turn into a plot.

This chapter shows people answering Jesus in very different ways. Some plot against him. Some follow him. Some crowd around him to get what they need. And some, even in his own family, think he has lost his mind. As we walk through it, listen for where each one stands.

In the synagogue, Jesus asks the people watching him, 'Is it lawful to do good on the sabbath days, or to do evil? to save life, or to kill?' Nobody answers. Mark says Jesus looked round about on them with anger, 'being grieved for the hardness of their hearts'. Then he says to the man, 'Stretch forth thine hand.' The man stretches it out, and it is made whole, like the other.

You would think everyone would rejoice. Instead, the Pharisees go straight out and start planning with the Herodians, the supporters of King Herod, how they might destroy him.

Jesus goes back to the lake, and crowds come from everywhere: from Galilee, from Judea and Jerusalem, from beyond the Jordan, and even from Tyre and Sidon on the coast. So many push forward to touch him that he asks for a small boat to be kept ready, so that he will not be crushed.

Then Jesus goes up into a mountain and calls to him the ones he wants. He chooses twelve. Mark gives their names: Simon, whom Jesus names Peter; James and John, whom he calls 'The sons of thunder'; Andrew, Philip, Bartholomew, Matthew, Thomas, another James, Thaddaeus, another Simon, and Judas Iscariot, 'which also betrayed him'. Mark tells us that already, so we know that even among the twelve, not every heart was true.

Back in a house, the crowd is so large that they cannot even eat. And when his family hears of it, they come to take hold of him, 'for they said, He is beside himself.' They think he is out of his mind.

Then scribes come down from Jerusalem, and they have a worse explanation. They say he has Beelzebub, the prince of the devils, and that is how he casts out devils. Jesus answers with pictures. 'How can Satan cast out Satan?' A kingdom divided against itself cannot stand. A house divided against itself cannot stand. And no one can break into a strong man’s house and carry off his goods, unless he first ties up the strong man. Jesus is not working for the devil. He is the one tying him up, and setting his prisoners free.

Then Jesus gives a solemn warning. All sins can be forgiven, he says, but to blaspheme against the Holy Ghost, to call the work of God’s Spirit the work of the devil, is to shut the door on forgiveness. Mark tells us why he said it: 'Because they said, He hath an unclean spirit.'

At last his mother and his brethren arrive. They stand outside and send someone in to call him. The crowd tells him, 'Behold, thy mother and thy brethren without seek for thee.' Jesus asks, 'Who is my mother, or my brethren?' Then he looks around at the people sitting in a circle about him, listening, and he says, 'Behold my mother and my brethren!'

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 5. 'And when he had looked round about on them with anger, being grieved for the hardness of their hearts, he saith unto the man, Stretch forth thine hand.' Jesus felt anger and sorrow at the same moment. He was angry, not at the man, but at hard hearts that cared more about catching Jesus out than about a man’s hand. God’s anger is never cold; it comes mixed with grief. And look at the man. He was asked to stretch out the very hand he could not use. He obeyed, and as he obeyed, he found it healed.

Verse 14. 'And he ordained twelve, that they should be with him, and that he might send them forth to preach,' Notice the first reason Jesus chose the twelve: that they should be with him. Before the work comes friendship. Before they went out to preach, they spent time with Jesus, watching him and listening to him. It is the same for us. Being with Jesus, in his Word and in prayer, comes before doing things for him. Then he sends us out into our day.

Verse 35. 'For whosoever shall do the will of God, the same is my brother, and my sister, and mother.' Jesus is not pushing his family away. He is opening the door wider. Anyone can belong to the family of God, not by being born into the right home, but by hearing the will of God and doing it. If you feel alone today, hear what Jesus says about those who listen to him and obey: this is my brother, my sister.

When Jesus spoke of tying up the strong man, he was keeping a promise made long before. Through the prophet Isaiah, God asked, 'Shall the prey be taken from the mighty, or the lawful captive delivered?' And he answered his own question: 'Even the captives of the mighty shall be taken away, and the prey of the terrible shall be delivered'. Every time Jesus sets someone free, the strong man is losing his prisoners.

The people in this chapter stand in different places. Some plot. Some think Jesus has lost his mind. Some crowd in to get something from him. And some sit around him, listening. Where are you standing today? Find a quiet moment simply to be with him, as the twelve were.

Here is today’s question. Jesus chose the twelve first of all to be with him. When will you take time today simply to be with Jesus?

Let us pray. Lord Jesus, soften any hardness in my heart. Thank you that you call me to be with you, and that you welcome me into your family. Help me to do the will of God, and to stretch out whatever you tell me to stretch out. Amen.

Next on the walk, Mark chapter four. Jesus sits in a boat and teaches the crowd with stories of seeds and soil, and then, crossing the lake, his friends are caught in a terrible storm while he sleeps.
