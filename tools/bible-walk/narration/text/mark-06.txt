Bible Walk. Mark, chapter 6. Be of Good Cheer: It Is I.

The disciples are rowing hard in the middle of the lake. The wind is against them, and they are getting nowhere. Mark says Jesus 'saw them toiling in rowing'. And then, 'about the fourth watch of the night', they see a figure coming toward them, walking on top of the water. They scream. They think it is a ghost.

In chapter five, Jesus set a man free from a legion of devils, healed a woman who touched his clothes, and raised a little girl from death. Everywhere he goes, people are amazed.

But chapter six begins in the one place where people are not amazed: his home town of Nazareth. This is a long and busy chapter. Jesus is turned away by those who know him best, he sends his twelve friends out on their own, we hear how John the Baptist died, Jesus feeds a huge crowd, and he comes to his friends across the water.

On the sabbath, Jesus teaches in the synagogue at Nazareth, and the people are astonished, but not in a good way. 'Is not this the carpenter, the son of Mary,' they say, the brother of James and Joses and Juda and Simon? We know his family. Who does he think he is? Mark says, 'And they were offended at him.' Jesus says, 'A prophet is not without honour, but in his own country'. He heals only a few sick people there, and he marvels at their unbelief.

Then Jesus sends out the twelve, two by two. He gives them power over unclean spirits, and tells them to take nothing for the journey but a staff: no bag, no bread, no money. They must trust God to provide. They go out, preach that people should repent, cast out devils, and heal the sick.

News of all this reaches King Herod, and it frightens him. He says, 'It is John, whom I beheaded: he is risen from the dead.' Then Mark tells us what had happened. John had told Herod it was wrong for him to marry his brother’s wife, Herodias. So Herod put John in prison. Herodias hated John and wanted him dead. At Herod’s birthday feast, her daughter danced, and the king, pleased, promised her anything she asked, up to half his kingdom. She asked her mother what to ask for, and Herodias said, 'The head of John the Baptist.' The king was sorry, but he would not break his promise in front of his guests. And so John, the voice in the wilderness, was put to death in prison.

When the twelve come back and tell Jesus everything, he says, 'Come ye yourselves apart into a desert place, and rest a while'. So many people are coming and going that they have no time even to eat. They set off by boat to a quiet place. But the crowds see them, and run ahead around the shore, and get there first.

When Jesus steps out and sees the crowd, he does not send them away. He is moved with compassion, and he teaches them many things. As the day ends, the disciples say, send them away to buy food. Jesus answers, 'Give ye them to eat.' They have five loaves and two fish. Jesus has the people sit down in groups on the green grass. He looks up to heaven, blesses the food, breaks the loaves, and gives them to the disciples to hand out. 'And they did all eat, and were filled.' Afterward they pick up twelve baskets full of leftovers. About five thousand men were fed.

Then Jesus sends his disciples ahead by boat, and goes up a mountain alone to pray. Out on the lake, the wind is against them. And Jesus comes to them, walking on the sea. They cry out in fear. Then they hear his voice. He climbs into the boat, and the wind stops. Mark says they were amazed, because they still had not understood the miracle of the loaves.

When they reach the other shore, people recognise him at once. They carry the sick to him on beds, and all who touch even the edge of his clothes are made whole.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 31. 'And he said unto them, Come ye yourselves apart into a desert place, and rest a while: for there were many coming and going, and they had no leisure so much as to eat.' The twelve had just come back from their first mission, full of stories. And the first thing Jesus says is, come and rest. Jesus cares about tired people. Rest is not laziness; it is part of following him. If you are worn out from serving, hear his invitation: come apart with me, and rest a while.

Verse 34. 'And Jesus, when he came out, saw much people, and was moved with compassion toward them, because they were as sheep not having a shepherd: and he began to teach them many things.' Their quiet place was full of people. Jesus could have been annoyed. Instead, his heart went out to them. He saw people who were lost and hungry, like sheep with no one to lead them. And the first thing he gave them was not bread. It was teaching. A shepherd feeds his sheep with the Word, and then he feeds them with bread.

Verse 50. 'For they all saw him, and were troubled. And immediately he talked with them, and saith unto them, Be of good cheer: it is I; be not afraid.' Jesus does not leave them in their fear. Immediately he speaks. And notice the order: be of good cheer, it is I, be not afraid. The reason to take heart is in the middle: it is I. Not, the storm is small, or, you are strong. It is I. The answer to fear is the presence of Jesus.

Long before, Moses had prayed that God would give his people a leader, 'that the congregation of the LORD be not as sheep which have no shepherd.' And David sang, 'The LORD is my shepherd; I shall not want.' The shepherd of that psalm makes his sheep lie down in green pastures and spreads a table for them. On a hillside of green grass, Jesus makes the people sit down, and feeds them until they want nothing more.

Are you rowing hard against the wind? Jesus sees you toiling, as he saw his friends. Or are you tired from giving to others? Hear him say, come and rest a while. Whatever you are facing, the answer is not first a plan. It is a person: it is I.

Here is today’s question. Jesus said to his tired friends, come apart and rest a while. Where will you find a quiet moment with him today?

Let us pray. Lord Jesus, good Shepherd, thank you that you see me toiling and you come to me. When I am tired, give me rest. When I am afraid, let me hear you say, it is I, be not afraid. Amen.
