Bible Walk. Mark, chapter 8. Whom Say Ye That I Am?.

Jesus and his friends are walking on a road far in the north. He has been teaching them, healing in front of them, and feeding crowds with a few loaves. And now he asks them a question. First, what are other people saying about me? That is easy to answer. Then he asks a harder one, the question the whole book of Mark has been building to: 'But whom say ye that I am?'

In chapter seven, Jesus taught that what makes us unclean comes from the heart, he helped a mother from outside Israel, and he opened the ears of a deaf man. Mark is now halfway through his book, and this chapter is the turning point.

For seven chapters people have been asking, who is this? In this chapter, Peter gives the answer. And then, straightway, Jesus begins to tell them something that will change everything: where he is going, and what it will cost.

Once more a huge crowd has been with Jesus for three days, and they have nothing to eat. Jesus says, 'I have compassion on the multitude'. If he sends them away hungry, they will faint on the road. His disciples ask how anyone could feed so many out here in the wilderness. They seem to have forgotten what happened before. Jesus asks how many loaves they have. Seven, they say. He gives thanks, breaks them, and about four thousand people eat and are filled, with seven baskets left over.

Then the Pharisees come, asking for a sign from heaven to prove who he is. Jesus sighs deeply. No sign will be given to them. They have seen enough already.

In the boat, the disciples realise they have brought only one loaf. Jesus warns them, 'beware of the leaven of the Pharisees, and of the leaven of Herod.' Leaven is yeast, and a little of it spreads through the whole loaf. Jesus is warning them about the unbelief of the Pharisees and of Herod, which spreads the same way. But the disciples think he is talking about bread. Jesus asks, 'Having eyes, see ye not? and having ears, hear ye not? and do ye not remember?' How many baskets were left over? Twelve, and seven. 'How is it that ye do not understand?'

At Bethsaida, people bring Jesus a blind man. Jesus leads him by the hand out of the village, puts spit on his eyes, lays his hands on him, and asks if he sees anything. The man looks up and says, 'I see men as trees, walking.' He can see, but not clearly. Jesus lays his hands on his eyes again, and now he sees everything clearly.

Then, on the road to the towns of Caesarea Philippi, Jesus asks, 'Whom do men say that I am?' They tell him what people are saying: John the Baptist come back, or Elias, the prophet Elijah, or one of the prophets. Then he asks them the question. And Peter answers.

And now Jesus begins to teach them plainly that 'the Son of man must suffer many things', be rejected by the leaders, be killed, and after three days rise again. Peter takes him aside and begins to rebuke him. This cannot happen to you! But Jesus turns, looks at his disciples, and rebukes Peter: 'Get thee behind me, Satan: for thou savourest not the things that be of God, but the things that be of men.' Then he calls the crowd, and tells them what it means to follow him.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 29. 'And he saith unto them, But whom say ye that I am? And Peter answereth and saith unto him, Thou art the Christ.' The Christ means the anointed one, the King God had promised to send. Peter got the answer right. But a moment later, he showed he did not yet understand what kind of king Jesus would be. He was like the blind man who saw men as trees, walking: he could see, but not clearly yet. That is often how it is with us. We see Jesus truly, and then we keep learning to see him more clearly. And this question is not only for Peter. Jesus is asking you.

Verse 34. 'And when he had called the people unto him with his disciples also, he said unto them, Whosoever will come after me, let him deny himself, and take up his cross, and follow me.' In those days a cross was not jewellery. It was how Rome put people to death, and a man carrying his cross was walking to his death. Jesus says, if you want to follow me, say no to yourself, say yes to me, and follow wherever I go. Following Jesus is not adding him to our life. It is giving him our life.

Verse 36. 'For what shall it profit a man, if he shall gain the whole world, and lose his own soul?' Jesus does the sums for us. Put everything the world offers on one side: money, success, comfort, praise. Put your soul on the other side. Nothing balances it. The whole world would be a bad bargain. The life Jesus asks us to lose for his sake is the life we find with him for ever.

Jesus said he must be rejected. Long before, Isaiah had written of a servant of God who would suffer: 'He is despised and rejected of men; a man of sorrows, and acquainted with grief'. Peter expected a king who would win. Jesus knew that he was the servant Isaiah wrote about, and that the way to the crown went through the cross.

Like the blind man, maybe you see Jesus, but not yet clearly. Keep coming to him. And think about Peter’s answer. If someone asked you today who Jesus is, not what others say, but what you say, what would you answer?

Here is today’s question. Jesus asked, but whom say ye that I am? What is your own answer today, in your own words?

Let us pray. Lord Jesus, you are the Christ, the Son of the living God. Open my eyes to see you more clearly. Give me the courage to deny myself, take up my cross, and follow you, because nothing in the whole world is worth more than you. Amen.
