Bible Walk. Mark, chapter 15. Truly This Man Was the Son of God.

A Roman soldier stands at the foot of a cross. He has seen many men die this way. It is his job. But as he watches this man die, something about him is different. He has heard the loud cry. He has seen the darkness. And this hard, battle-worn soldier says what the religious leaders refused to say all through the book of Mark: 'Truly this man was the Son of God.'

In chapter fourteen, Jesus shared the Passover with his friends, prayed in Gethsemane, not what I will, but what thou wilt, and was betrayed with a kiss. At his trial, he said I am, and was condemned. And Peter denied him three times.

Chapter fifteen is the day of the cross. Mark tells it simply, without many words, and he tells it slowly, hour by hour. As we walk through it, remember what Jesus said in chapter ten: that he came to give his life a ransom for many. This is that ransom being paid.

At first light, the chief priests bind Jesus and hand him over to Pilate, the Roman governor. Pilate asks him, 'Art thou the King of the Jews?' Jesus answers, 'Thou sayest it.' The chief priests accuse him of many things, but Jesus says nothing more, and Pilate is amazed.

At the feast, Pilate would release one prisoner chosen by the people. In prison is a man named Barabbas, who had killed someone in a riot. Pilate knows the priests have handed Jesus over out of envy, so he asks the crowd, shall I release the King of the Jews? But the priests stir up the crowd to ask for Barabbas instead. 'What will ye then that I shall do unto him whom ye call the King of the Jews?' And they cry out, 'Crucify him.' Pilate asks, 'Why, what evil hath he done?' They only shout louder. So Pilate, wanting to please the crowd, sets Barabbas free, has Jesus whipped, and hands him over to be crucified. The guilty man goes free, and the innocent man dies in his place.

The soldiers take Jesus inside and call the whole troop together. They dress him in purple, twist a crown of thorns and press it on his head, and mock him: 'Hail, King of the Jews!' They hit him on the head with a stick, spit on him, and kneel in pretend worship. Then they lead him out to crucify him.

On the way, they force a man passing by, Simon from Cyrene, to carry his cross. They bring Jesus to Golgotha, which means the place of a skull. They offer him wine mixed with myrrh, to dull the pain, but he will not drink it. They crucify him, and divide his clothes by casting lots. Above him they put the charge against him: THE KING OF THE JEWS. Two thieves are crucified with him, one on each side.

People passing by shake their heads and mock him: come down from the cross and save yourself! The chief priests mock him too.

Then, at the sixth hour, darkness covers the whole land, for three hours. And at the ninth hour, Jesus cries out with a loud voice in his own language, 'Eloi, Eloi, lama sabachthani? which is, being interpreted, My God, my God, why hast thou forsaken me?' Some think he is calling Elijah. Someone gives him sour wine on a sponge. And Jesus cries out with a loud voice, and gives up the ghost. He dies.

At that moment, in the temple, the great veil, the curtain that closed off the most holy place, is torn in two from top to bottom. And the centurion speaks.

Women are watching from a distance: Mary Magdalene, Mary the mother of James and Joses, Salome, and many others who had followed him from Galilee. Then Joseph of Arimathaea, a respected member of the council, who was waiting for the kingdom of God, goes boldly to Pilate and asks for the body. He wraps Jesus in fine linen, lays him in a tomb cut out of the rock, and rolls a stone against the door. And the two Marys see where he is laid.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 31. 'Likewise also the chief priests mocking said among themselves with the scribes, He saved others; himself he cannot save.' They meant it as an insult, but they spoke a deep truth. Jesus could have come down from the cross. He calmed storms and raised the dead. But if he saved himself, he could not save us. He stayed on the cross, not because he was weak, but because he loved. He did not save himself, so that he could save others. So that he could save you.

Verse 38. 'And the veil of the temple was rent in twain from the top to the bottom.' The veil was a thick curtain in the temple. Behind it was the most holy place, where God’s presence dwelt, and only the high priest could go in, once a year. The curtain said, sinful people cannot come near a holy God. When Jesus died, it was torn from the top to the bottom, from heaven downward, as if by the hand of God. The way to God was opened. Because of Jesus, anyone can now come near.

Verse 39. 'And when the centurion, which stood over against him, saw that he so cried out, and gave up the ghost, he said, Truly this man was the Son of God.' Mark began his book with Jesus Christ, the Son of God. Now, at the cross, the first person to say it out loud is not a disciple, not a priest, but a Roman soldier, a man from outside. He saw Jesus die and knew who he was. The cross, which looked like the end of every hope, is where the truth about Jesus shines the clearest.

Jesus’ cry, My God, my God, why hast thou forsaken me, is the first line of Psalm twenty-two. That psalm describes a suffering man mocked by those who pass by, and it says, 'They part my garments among them, and cast lots upon my vesture.' Every Jew who heard that cry would know how the psalm continues: not in defeat, but with God’s rescue, and all the ends of the earth turning to the LORD.

Barabbas went free because Jesus took his place. That is the gospel. We are Barabbas. Stand at the cross today, and let it sink in that he did not save himself, so that he could save you. And because the veil is torn, you can come near to God, right now, in prayer.

Here is today’s question. Barabbas went free because Jesus took his place. What will you say to Jesus today, knowing that he took your place?

Let us pray. Lord Jesus, you did not save yourself, so that you could save me. Thank you for bearing my sin on the cross, for opening the way to God, and for loving me to the end. Truly, you are the Son of God. Amen.
