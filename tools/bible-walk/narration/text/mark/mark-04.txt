The Living Voice. Mark, chapter 4. Peace, Be Still.

The boat is filling with water. The wind is howling, and the waves are crashing over the sides. These men are fishermen. They know this lake, and they know they are about to drown. And Jesus is asleep, on a pillow at the back of the boat. They shake him awake and cry out, 'Master, carest thou not that we perish?'

In chapter three, people took sides about Jesus. Some plotted against him, his own family thought he was out of his mind, and he chose twelve to be with him. Now, in chapter four, Jesus teaches by the lake. So many people come that he gets into a boat, sits in it a little way from the shore, and teaches the crowd on the beach.

He teaches in parables. A parable is a short story from everyday life with a deeper meaning, a story that you have to listen to with your heart. And when the teaching is done and the day comes to an end, comes the storm.

'Hearken', Jesus begins. Listen. 'Behold, there went out a sower to sow'. As the farmer scatters his seed, some falls on the hard path, and the birds come and eat it. Some falls on stony ground where there is little soil. It springs up quickly, but when the sun comes up it is scorched, because it has no root. Some falls among thorns, and the thorns grow up and choke it. And some falls on good ground. It grows and gives a harvest, thirty, sixty, even a hundred times what was sown. Then Jesus says, 'He that hath ears to hear, let him hear.'

Later, when they are alone, the twelve ask him what it means. Jesus explains. 'The sower soweth the word.' The seed is God’s Word. The hard path is a heart where Satan comes at once and snatches the word away. The stony ground is someone who receives the word with gladness, but has no root, so when trouble comes for the word’s sake, they fall away. The thorns are 'the cares of this world, and the deceitfulness of riches, and the lusts of other things', and they choke the word. And the good ground is those who hear the word, receive it, and bear fruit.

Jesus tells more parables. A candle is not lit to be hidden under a basket or under a bed, but to be set on a candlestick. A farmer scatters seed, then goes to sleep and gets up, day after day, and the seed grows by itself, 'he knoweth not how': first the blade, then the ear, then the full grain. And the kingdom of God is like a grain of mustard seed. It is the smallest of seeds, but it grows into a plant so big that the birds can rest in its shade.

When the day is over, Jesus says, 'Let us pass over unto the other side.' They set out across the lake, and a great storm of wind comes down on them. The waves beat into the boat until it is almost full. And Jesus is asleep in the back. They wake him: 'Master, carest thou not that we perish?'

Jesus gets up. He rebukes the wind, and he speaks to the sea. The wind stops, and the lake is still. Then he turns to his friends and asks why they were so afraid. And now they are afraid in a new way. They say to one another, 'What manner of man is this, that even the wind and the sea obey him?'

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 20. 'And these are they which are sown on good ground; such as hear the word, and receive it, and bring forth fruit, some thirtyfold, some sixty, and some an hundred.' The same seed fell on all four kinds of ground. The difference was never the seed. It was the soil. Good ground does three things: it hears the word, it receives it, and it bears fruit. We cannot make a seed grow, but we can get the soil ready. We can soften a hard heart by asking God to speak, and pull out the thorns of worry and of always wanting more. Every step of this walk is seed being sown.

Verse 39. 'And he arose, and rebuked the wind, and said unto the sea, Peace, be still. And the wind ceased, and there was a great calm.' Jesus speaks to the storm as you might speak to a noisy child. Peace. Be still. And notice: a great storm, and then a great calm. The storm does not slowly die down. It stops at his word. The wind and the sea know the voice of the one who made them. That same voice can speak peace into our storms. Sometimes he calms the storm. Sometimes he calms us in the middle of it.

Verse 40. 'And he said unto them, Why are ye so fearful? how is it that ye have no faith?' They had asked, carest thou not? Fear makes us think God does not care. But Jesus was in the boat with them the whole time. And before they set out he had said, let us pass over unto the other side. He did not say, let us go to the middle and drown. His word was enough. Faith remembers what Jesus has said, even when the waves are saying something else.

Long before, a psalm told of sailors caught in a storm, who cried out to the LORD in their trouble. And the psalm says of the LORD, 'He maketh the storm a calm, so that the waves thereof are still.' The disciples knew that only God can do that. Now they have watched Jesus do it with a word. No wonder they ask, who is this?

Think about the four kinds of ground. Which one is most like your heart right now? Is there a worry, or a wanting, that is choking God’s Word in you? And if you are in a storm today, remember what Jesus has said to you, and that he is in the boat.

Here is today’s question. Which worry is choking God’s Word in your heart, and what will you do to pull it out today?

Let us pray. Lord Jesus, make my heart good ground for your Word. When storms come, remind me that you are in the boat with me. Speak peace to my fears, and help me to trust what you have said. Amen.
