The Living Voice. Mark, chapter 7. He Hath Done All Things Well.

A man is brought to Jesus who cannot hear, and can hardly speak. Jesus takes him away from the crowd, where it is just the two of them. He puts his fingers in the man’s ears. He touches his tongue. Then he looks up to heaven, and sighs, and says one word in his own language: 'Ephphatha'. Be opened.

In chapter six, Jesus was turned away in his home town, he fed five thousand people with five loaves and two fish, and he came to his friends walking on the sea.

Now in chapter seven, teachers come all the way from Jerusalem to watch him, and they find something to complain about. This chapter is about what makes a person clean or unclean before God. Is it what we wash, and what we eat? Or is it something deeper? Then Jesus travels north, outside the land of Israel, where he meets a mother who will not give up.

The Pharisees and some scribes notice that his disciples eat bread with unwashed hands. In those days this was not about germs. The Pharisees followed many traditions of washing hands, cups and pots, handed down from their elders, as a way of keeping themselves clean before God. So they ask Jesus, 'Why walk not thy disciples according to the tradition of the elders, but eat bread with unwashen hands?'

Jesus answers with words from Isaiah, whom the King James Bible here calls Esaias. Then he shows them how their traditions had pushed aside God’s own commands. God said, 'Honour thy father and thy mother'. But they had a rule called Corban: a man could declare his money a gift for God, and then refuse to use it to help his own parents. 'Making the word of God of none effect through your tradition', Jesus says.

Then he calls the crowd and says, 'Hearken unto me every one of you, and understand'. Nothing that goes into a person from outside can make them unclean. It is what comes out of a person that makes them unclean. Later, his disciples ask what he meant, and he explains. Food goes into the stomach, not into the heart. It is out of the heart that evil comes.

Then Jesus goes north, to the region of Tyre and Sidon, near the sea coast. He wants to stay hidden in a house, but he cannot be hid. A woman comes and falls at his feet. She is a Greek, not a Jew. Her young daughter has an unclean spirit, and she begs him to cast it out.

Jesus answers her in a picture. 'Let the children first be filled: for it is not meet to take the children’s bread, and to cast it unto the dogs.' The children are the people of Israel, whom Jesus came to first. She is from outside. But she does not walk away. She takes his picture and answers with it. And Jesus says, 'For this saying go thy way; the devil is gone out of thy daughter.' She goes home and finds her daughter lying on the bed, and the devil gone.

Coming back toward the sea of Galilee, Jesus meets the man who is deaf and can hardly speak. He takes him aside, touches his ears and tongue, looks up to heaven, sighs, and says, 'Ephphatha, that is, Be opened.' Straightway his ears are opened, and he speaks plainly. Jesus tells them to tell no one, but the more he tells them, the more they spread the news. The people are astonished beyond measure, and they say, 'He hath done all things well: he maketh both the deaf to hear, and the dumb to speak.'

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 6. 'He answered and said unto them, Well hath Esaias prophesied of you hypocrites, as it is written, This people honoureth me with their lips, but their heart is far from me.' A hypocrite was an actor, someone wearing a mask. The Pharisees said the right words and washed the right cups, but their hearts were far from God. It is possible to do all the outward things, to read, to pray, to sing, while our heart is somewhere else. God is not looking first at our lips or our hands. He is looking at our heart.

Verse 21. 'For from within, out of the heart of men, proceed evil thoughts, adulteries, fornications, murders,' The list goes on: theft, wanting what others have, deceit, pride, foolishness. Jesus is saying the problem is not out there; it is in here. We cannot wash our way clean, because the trouble is inside us. That is hard news, but it is honest news. And it is why we need more than rules. We need God to give us a new heart, and only he can do that.

Verse 28. 'And she answered and said unto him, Yes, Lord: yet the dogs under the table eat of the children’s crumbs.' What an answer. She does not argue that she deserves it. She agrees with Jesus, yes, Lord, and then she holds on. Even the crumbs from your table are enough for me. This is humble, clever, stubborn faith. She knew that the smallest crumb of the grace of Jesus was more than enough. And Jesus loved her answer.

The crowd said, he maketh both the deaf to hear, and the dumb to speak. They may have been thinking of the prophet Isaiah, who promised a day when God would come to save his people: 'Then the eyes of the blind shall be opened, and the ears of the deaf shall be unstopped.' And, 'the tongue of the dumb sing'. Mark wants us to see that this promised day has begun.

Ask God to look at your heart, not only at your habits. Is there a way you honour him with your lips while your heart is far away? And if you feel like an outsider, unworthy of his help, remember the woman from Tyre. Come anyway. Even his crumbs are enough.

Here is today’s question. Jesus said that evil comes from within, out of the heart. What is one thing in your heart that you will bring to God and ask him to make clean today?

Let us pray. Lord, I do not want to honour you only with my lips. Search my heart and make it clean. Give me the humble, holding-on faith of the woman who asked for crumbs. Open my ears to hear you, and my mouth to speak of you. Amen.
