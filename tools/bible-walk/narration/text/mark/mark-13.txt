The Living Voice. Mark, chapter 13. Take Ye Heed, Watch and Pray.

The disciples are walking out of the temple in Jerusalem, and one of them stops and stares. The stones are enormous, and the buildings gleam. 'Master, see what manner of stones and what buildings are here!' Jesus looks at the same great buildings, and says something that must have shocked them: 'there shall not be left one stone upon another, that shall not be thrown down.'

In chapter twelve, Jesus answered every trap the leaders set for him in the temple, named the greatest commandment, and praised a poor widow who gave everything.

Now he leaves the temple for the last time. Chapter thirteen is different from every other chapter in Mark. It is almost all teaching. Sitting on the Mount of Olives, looking across at the temple, Jesus tells four of his disciples about the troubles that will come, and about the day he will return.

Christians have understood parts of this chapter in different ways. Some of what Jesus said came true when the temple was destroyed, a generation later. Some of it still waits for the day he comes again. But what Jesus asks of his followers here is plain and simple, and that is what we will hold on to.

On the Mount of Olives, Peter, James, John and Andrew ask him privately, 'Tell us, when shall these things be? and what shall be the sign'? Jesus does not begin by giving them dates. He begins with a warning. Many will come claiming to be the Christ, and will deceive many.

You will hear of wars and rumours of wars, he says, but 'be ye not troubled'. Nations will rise against nations. There will be earthquakes and famines. 'These are the beginnings of sorrows.' Like the pains of a mother before her child is born, they are not the end, but they tell you something is coming.

Then he tells them what will happen to them. They will be handed over to councils, beaten in synagogues, and stand before rulers and kings because of him. But this will be their chance to tell of him, for 'the gospel must first be published among all nations.' When they are arrested, they should not worry beforehand about what to say. 'For it is not ye that speak, but the Holy Ghost.' Families will turn against each other. They will be hated for his name. 'But he that shall endure unto the end, the same shall be saved.'

Jesus speaks of the abomination of desolation, which the prophet Daniel wrote about, and of a time of great distress in Judea, when people must flee to the mountains without even going back for a coat. False christs and false prophets will rise and show signs and wonders, to deceive, if possible, even God’s chosen people. 'But take ye heed: behold, I have foretold you all things.'

Then, after that trouble, the sun and moon will be darkened, the stars will fall, 'And then shall they see the Son of man coming in the clouds with great power and glory.' He will send his angels to gather his own from the four winds, from one end of the earth to the other.

Jesus gives them a picture. When a fig tree puts out tender leaves, you know summer is near. In the same way, when you see these things, know that it is near, even at the door. But the exact day and hour, no one knows, not the angels, not even the Son, but only the Father.

So he ends with a story. A man goes on a long journey, gives each servant his work, and tells the doorkeeper to keep watch. No one knows when the master will come back. 'Lest coming suddenly he find you sleeping.' And his last words in the chapter are for everyone: 'And what I say unto you I say unto all, Watch.'

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 5. 'And Jesus answering them began to say, Take heed lest any man deceive you:' The disciples asked when. Jesus answered first with, be careful. He knew that the greatest danger was not wars or earthquakes, but being led astray. Many will claim to speak for God, or even to be the Christ. How do we take heed? By knowing his words well, so that we recognise anything that does not match them. That is one reason we walk through the Bible, chapter by chapter.

Verse 31. 'Heaven and earth shall pass away: but my words shall not pass away.' The disciples had just been amazed at stones that looked as if they would last for ever. Jesus says even heaven and earth will pass away. But his words will not. Buildings fall. Empires end. Everything we can see will one day be gone. But every word Jesus has spoken will still be standing. That is a solid place to build your life.

Verse 33. 'Take ye heed, watch and pray: for ye know not when the time is.' Jesus does not tell us to guess the date of his return. He tells us to be ready for it. Watching does not mean staring at the sky. It means living faithfully, doing the work he has given each of us, and praying, so that whenever he comes, he finds us awake, not asleep. A servant who is ready does not fear the master’s return. He looks forward to it.

When Jesus spoke of the Son of man coming in the clouds, he was using words from the prophet Daniel. Daniel saw a vision of 'one like the Son of man' who 'came with the clouds of heaven', and to him was given a kingdom that would never end. All through Mark, Jesus has called himself the Son of man. Now he tells them plainly: I am the one Daniel saw.

Many voices today claim to have the answers. Take heed. Hold on to the words of Jesus, which will never pass away. And do not be anxious about the future. Be faithful in the work he has given you today, and keep watching, and keep praying.

Here is today’s question. Jesus said, take heed, watch and pray. What is one thing you will do today to stay awake and ready for him?

Let us pray. Lord Jesus, thank you that your words will never pass away. Keep me from being deceived. Help me to be faithful in the work you have given me, to watch and pray, and to be glad, not afraid, when you come. Amen.
