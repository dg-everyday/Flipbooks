The Living Voice. Mark, chapter 14. Not What I Will, but What Thou Wilt.

In a garden called Gethsemane, Jesus falls with his face to the ground. He tells his closest friends, 'My soul is exceeding sorrowful unto death'. He asks them to watch with him. And he prays to his Father, asking if the hour ahead could pass from him. Three times he prays. And three times he comes back to find his friends asleep.

In chapter thirteen, Jesus sat on the Mount of Olives and taught his disciples to watch and pray, because no one knows when he will return.

Now, in chapter fourteen, the end of his earthly life is very near. It is two days before the feast of the Passover, when Israel remembered how God rescued them from slavery in Egypt, and the lamb whose blood marked their doors. The leaders are plotting to kill him quietly. This is a long chapter, full of people who love Jesus and people who fail him. Watch the faithfulness of Jesus, even as everyone around him falls away.

In the village of Bethany, Jesus is eating at the house of Simon the leper. A woman comes in with an alabaster jar of very costly perfume. She breaks the jar and pours it all on his head. Some there are angry. Why this waste? It could have been sold for more than a year’s wages and given to the poor. But Jesus says, 'Let her alone; why trouble ye her? she hath wrought a good work on me.' And he says that wherever the gospel is preached, what she has done will be told in memory of her.

Right after this, Judas Iscariot, one of the twelve, goes to the chief priests to hand Jesus over to them. They are glad, and promise to give him money.

On the day the Passover lambs are killed, Jesus sends two disciples into the city. They meet a man carrying a jar of water, follow him, and find a large upper room, ready, just as Jesus said. There they prepare the Passover. As Jesus and the twelve eat together, he says, 'One of you which eateth with me shall betray me.' One by one, they ask him sadly, 'Is it I?'

Then, as they eat, Jesus takes bread, blesses it, breaks it, and gives it to them: 'Take, eat: this is my body.' He takes the cup, gives thanks, and they all drink from it. 'This is my blood of the new testament, which is shed for many.' The ransom he spoke of is about to be paid.

After singing a hymn, they go out to the Mount of Olives. Jesus tells them they will all fall away. Peter insists, 'Although all shall be offended, yet will not I.' Jesus tells him that before the cock crows twice, he will deny him three times. Peter says he would rather die. And so do all the rest.

In Gethsemane, Jesus takes Peter, James and John further in, and prays. Coming back, he finds them sleeping. 'Simon, sleepest thou? couldest not thou watch one hour?' 'The spirit truly is ready, but the flesh is weak.' Three times he prays, and three times they sleep. Then he says, 'Rise up, let us go; lo, he that betrayeth me is at hand.'

Immediately Judas arrives with a crowd carrying swords and clubs, sent by the chief priests. He has told them, the one I kiss is the man. He walks up to Jesus, says, 'Master, master', and kisses him. They seize Jesus. One of his followers draws a sword and cuts off the ear of the high priest’s servant. Then all the disciples leave him and run away.

They take Jesus to the high priest, where the whole council is gathered. They look for witnesses against him, and many tell lies, but their stories do not agree. Jesus stays silent. Then the high priest asks him straight out, 'Art thou the Christ, the Son of the Blessed?' And Jesus says, 'I am'. The high priest tears his clothes. They all condemn him to death. Some spit on him, cover his face, and strike him.

Meanwhile, Peter has followed at a distance, and sits in the courtyard warming himself by the fire. A servant girl looks at him: 'And thou also wast with Jesus of Nazareth.' Peter says he does not know what she means, and the cock crows. She says it again to the people standing around. Peter denies it again. Then the bystanders say, surely you are one of them; you talk like a Galilean. And Peter begins to curse and swear, 'I know not this man of whom ye speak.' And the cock crows a second time.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 8. 'She hath done what she could: she is come aforehand to anoint my body to the burying.' Others saw waste. Jesus saw love. She may not have fully understood that he was about to die, but she gave him the most precious thing she had, while she still could. Jesus does not ask us to do everything. He asks us to do what we can, for him, out of love. Nothing given to Jesus is ever wasted.

Verse 36. 'And he said, Abba, Father, all things are possible unto thee; take away this cup from me: nevertheless not what I will, but what thou wilt.' Abba is the word a child uses for a father. In his darkest hour, Jesus speaks to God as his dear Father. He is honest: if there is another way, take this cup away. The cup was the suffering, and the sin of many, that he was about to carry. And then he chose his Father’s will over his own. This is the prayer that saved us: not what I will, but what thou wilt.

Verse 72. 'And the second time the cock crew. And Peter called to mind the word that Jesus said unto him, Before the cock crow twice, thou shalt deny me thrice. And when he thought thereon, he wept.' Peter, the bold one, who had said he would die with Jesus, had sworn he did not even know him. When he heard the cock, he remembered, and he broke down and wept. Peter’s story does not end in that courtyard. Wait until the last chapter. But his tears are the beginning of his way back. When we fail Jesus, the first step home is to remember his words, and weep honestly over what we have done.

When the false witnesses spoke against him, Jesus held his peace and said nothing. The prophet Isaiah had written of God’s suffering servant: 'he is brought as a lamb to the slaughter, and as a sheep before her shearers is dumb, so he openeth not his mouth.' At the Passover, when the lambs were killed, the Lamb of God stood silent before his accusers.

Like the woman, what can you give to Jesus today, out of love, even if others think it is a waste? And if you have failed him, as Peter did, do not run from him. Come back. His prayer in the garden was for you too.

Here is today’s question. Jesus prayed, not what I will, but what thou wilt. Where in your life will you pray that same prayer today?

Let us pray. Abba, Father, all things are possible unto thee. Thank you that Jesus chose your will, and drank the cup for me. When my spirit is willing and my flesh is weak, help me. When I fail you, bring me back. Not what I will, but what thou wilt. Amen.
