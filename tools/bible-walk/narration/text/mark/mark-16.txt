The Living Voice. Mark, chapter 16. He Is Risen; He Is Not Here.

Three women are walking to a tomb, carrying spices to anoint a body. Their hearts are broken. And as they walk, they worry about something practical: the stone. It was very large. 'Who shall roll us away the stone from the door of the sepulchre?' But when they look up, they see that the stone is already rolled away.

In chapter fifteen, Jesus was crucified. He did not save himself, so that he could save others. A Roman centurion said, truly this man was the Son of God. And Joseph of Arimathaea laid his body in a tomb cut out of the rock, and rolled a stone against the door. The two Marys watched where he was laid.

Then came the sabbath, the day of rest, and everything was still. Now it is the first day of the week. This is the last chapter of Mark, the sixteenth step of our walk through this book, and the best news of all.

When the sabbath is past, Mary Magdalene, Mary the mother of James, and Salome buy sweet spices to anoint the body of Jesus. Mark says they came 'very early in the morning the first day of the week', 'at the rising of the sun.' All the way there, they wonder who will move the stone. But when they arrive, it has been rolled away.

They go into the tomb, and see a young man sitting on the right side, dressed in a long white garment, and they are frightened. He speaks to them. Jesus of Nazareth, who was crucified, is not here. He is risen. Look, here is the place where they laid him. Then he gives them a message to take to the disciples. The women run from the tomb, trembling and amazed, and at first they say nothing to anyone, because they are afraid.

Then Mark tells us how the risen Jesus was seen. First he appeared to Mary Magdalene, out of whom he had cast seven devils. She went and told the disciples, who were mourning and weeping. But when they heard that he was alive and that she had seen him, they did not believe it.

After that he appeared to two of them, 'as they walked, and went into the country.' You may remember them. Their story was the very first step of our walk: the two friends on the road, when Jesus himself drew near and walked with them. They went back and told the others, and still the others did not believe.

Afterward Jesus appeared to the eleven as they sat at the table. He rebuked them for their unbelief and hard hearts, because they had not believed those who had seen him. And then, instead of giving up on them, he gave them the greatest task in the world. He sent them out. 'He that believeth and is baptized shall be saved; but he that believeth not shall be damned.' Signs would follow those who believe in his name.

Then the Lord Jesus 'was received up into heaven, and sat on the right hand of God.' And the last verse of the book tells us what his friends did next: 'And they went forth, and preached every where, the Lord working with them, and confirming the word with signs following. Amen.'

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 6. 'And he saith unto them, Be not affrighted: Ye seek Jesus of Nazareth, which was crucified: he is risen; he is not here: behold the place where they laid him.' These words change everything. Jesus said three times on the road that he would be killed and rise again on the third day, and no one understood. Now it has happened. He is risen. The tomb is empty. Death could not hold him. That means everything he said is true. His promises are true. His forgiveness is real. And death is not the end for those who belong to him.

Verse 7. 'But go your way, tell his disciples and Peter that he goeth before you into Galilee: there shall ye see him, as he said unto you.' Notice two words: and Peter. Peter, who boasted he would never fall away. Peter, who swore by the fire that he did not know Jesus, and then went out and wept. The risen Lord sends a message with Peter’s name in it. You are not finished. I still want you. If you have failed Jesus, hear your name in this verse. He goes before you, and he wants you back.

Verse 15. 'And he said unto them, Go ye into all the world, and preach the gospel to every creature.' Jesus gave this command to men who, only days before, had run away, and then did not believe the women. He did not wait for perfect people. He sent forgiven people. The gospel, the good news, was never meant to stay in one room or one country. It is for every creature, in all the world. And the last verse says the Lord worked with them. He still does, with us.

The prophet Isaiah, who described the suffering servant wounded for our transgressions, did not end his song at the grave. He wrote that when the servant’s soul was made an offering for sin, 'he shall prolong his days'. And then, 'He shall see of the travail of his soul, and shall be satisfied'. The servant who died would live again. On the first day of the week, the women found that promise kept.

At the start of our walk through Mark, the book asked you a question: who is Jesus? We have seen him heal, teach, serve, suffer, die, and rise. Mark’s first line said he is the Son of God, and the centurion said it at the cross. Mark began with the beginning of the gospel. The gospel goes on in us. Who will you tell?

Here is today’s question. Now that you have walked through the whole book of Mark, who do you say Jesus is, and who will you tell about him?

Let us pray. Lord Jesus, you are risen. You are not in the tomb. Thank you that death could not hold you, and that you call failures like Peter, and like me, back to yourself. Go before me, and send me out to tell others the good news. Amen.

That is the end of our walk through the book of Mark. Well walked. In our next plan, The Big Picture, we will step back and walk through the whole Bible in thirty chapters, from the first chapter of Genesis to the last chapters of Revelation, and see the one great story that every book belongs to.
