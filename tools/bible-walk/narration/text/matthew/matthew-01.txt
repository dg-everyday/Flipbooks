The Living Voice. Matthew, chapter 1. They Shall Call His Name Emmanuel.

Imagine opening a book and finding that the whole first page is a list of names. Most people would skip it. But if you know the stories behind those names, the list comes alive. A shepherd boy who became king. A woman from Moab who gleaned in a field. A governor called God’s signet. Matthew begins with a list like that. And every name on it is a step on the road to Jesus.

In the introduction, we heard that Matthew was a tax collector whom Jesus called to follow him. We heard that Matthew wrote for people who knew the Old Testament, and that he loves to say, that it might be fulfilled. And we heard that the book begins with Emmanuel, God with us.

Now, in chapter one, Matthew opens with a family line, and then tells us how Jesus was born, from the side of Joseph. Listen for the names you know. And listen for the two names the angel gives the baby.

Matthew begins with one sentence that sums up everything.

Then comes the family line. Abraham begat Isaac. Isaac begat Jacob. Jacob begat Judas, which is Judah, and his brothers. On it goes, down through the generations.

And there are surprises in this list. In those days, family lines usually named only the fathers. But Matthew names four women, and every one of them has a story. Thamar, which is Tamar, from the time of Judah. Rachab, which is Rahab, who hid the spies in Jericho. Ruth, the woman from Moab, the great-grandmother of David. And her that had been the wife of Urias, which is Bathsheba. Outsiders, and people with painful stories. Matthew seems to be saying, look who God brings into the family of Jesus.

The list goes on to 'David the king', and then down through the kings you have walked with. Solomon. Asa. Josaphat, which is Jehoshaphat. Ozias, which is Uzziah. Ezekias, which is Hezekiah. Josias, which is Josiah. Some of them were good, and some were very bad. But God kept his promise to David through all of them.

Then the list reaches the darkest moment, when the people were carried away to Babylon. And then, 'Jechonias begat Salathiel; and Salathiel begat Zorobabel'. That is Zerubbabel, the governor in Haggai, whom God called his signet. The line of David did not end in Babylon.

At last, the list comes to 'Joseph the husband of Mary, of whom was born Jesus, who is called Christ.' Christ is not a last name. It means the anointed one, the King God promised.

Matthew tells us there were fourteen generations from Abraham to David, fourteen from David to Babylon, and fourteen from Babylon to Christ. He is showing us the shape of God’s plan, steady and on time.

Then Matthew tells how Jesus was born. Mary was engaged to Joseph. Before they came together as husband and wife, she 'was found with child of the Holy Ghost.' Joseph did not yet understand. He was 'a just man', and he did not want to shame Mary in public, so he planned to end the engagement quietly.

But while he was thinking about it, an angel of the Lord appeared to him in a dream. Joseph, thou son of David, fear not to take Mary as your wife, for the child in her is of the Holy Ghost.

Then the angel tells Joseph what to name the baby.

And Matthew adds, all this happened that it might be fulfilled which was spoken by the prophet. And he gives the words of Isaiah.

When Joseph woke up, he did exactly what the angel told him. He took Mary as his wife, and when the baby was born, 'he called his name JESUS.'

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 1. 'The book of the generation of Jesus Christ, the son of David, the son of Abraham.' In one line, Matthew ties Jesus to the two greatest promises in the Old Testament. God promised Abraham that in him all the families of the earth would be blessed. And God promised David that his son would sit on the throne for ever. Matthew is telling us, here he is. The promised son of Abraham, and the promised son of David, is Jesus.

Verse 21. 'And she shall bring forth a son, and thou shalt call his name JESUS: for he shall save his people from their sins.' The name Jesus is the same as the Old Testament name Joshua, and it means the LORD saves. Joshua led God’s people into the promised land. Jesus came to do something even greater. Not to save his people from the Romans, as many hoped, but from their sins. That was the deepest rescue they needed. And it is the rescue we need too.

Verse 23. 'Behold, a virgin shall be with child, and shall bring forth a son, and they shall call his name Emmanuel, which being interpreted is, God with us.' Isaiah spoke these words to king Ahaz, long before. Now Matthew says they have been fulfilled in Jesus. Emmanuel means God with us. Not God far away, watching from heaven. God here, with us, as a baby in a carpenter’s home. In Haggai, God said, I am with you. In Jesus, God came to be with us in person.

God promised David, 'And thine house and thy kingdom shall be established for ever before thee: thy throne shall be established for ever.' And Isaiah said of the coming child, 'his name shall be called Wonderful, Counsellor, The mighty God, The everlasting Father, The Prince of Peace.'

Joseph did not understand everything, but when God spoke, he obeyed straight away. Is there something God has shown you to do, even though you do not understand it all? And remember the name Emmanuel today. Wherever you are, God is with you.

Here is today’s question. Matthew’s family line includes outsiders and people with painful stories. What does that tell you about who is welcome in God’s family?

Let us pray. Lord Jesus, thank you that you came to save your people from their sins. Thank you that you are Emmanuel, God with us. Help me, like Joseph, to trust you and obey, even when I do not understand. Amen.
