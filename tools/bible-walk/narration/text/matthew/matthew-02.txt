The Living Voice. Matthew, chapter 2. We Have Seen His Star.

Far away in the east, some learned men study the sky. One day, they see something new, a star that means a king has been born. So they pack their treasures and set out on a long journey. When they finally reach the great city, they ask a simple question. Where is the baby king? And with that question, they frighten the most powerful man in the land.

In chapter one, Matthew gave us the family line of Jesus, from Abraham, through David, through Babylon, to Joseph. An angel told Joseph in a dream to take Mary as his wife, and to call the baby Jesus, for he would save his people from their sins. And he would be Emmanuel, God with us.

Now, in chapter two, visitors come from far away to worship the child, while a king nearby wants to destroy him. Listen for the two ways people respond to Jesus. And listen for how God protects him, again and again.

Jesus was born in Bethlehem of Judea, in the days of Herod the king. Herod was a cruel and suspicious ruler, put in power by the Romans.

Then 'there came wise men from the east to Jerusalem'. Matthew does not tell us how many there were, or their names. He tells us they came from the east, and that they had been watching the sky. And they ask a question.

When Herod hears this, he is troubled, 'and all Jerusalem with him.' A new king of the Jews? Herod was the king, and he did not want a rival.

So Herod gathers the chief priests and scribes, the men who knew the Scriptures best. Where is the Christ to be born? They know the answer straight away. In Bethlehem, for this is what the prophet wrote.

Then Herod calls the wise men secretly, and asks them exactly when the star appeared. He sends them to Bethlehem. Go and search carefully for the young child, he says, and when you find him, bring me word, 'that I may come and worship him also.' But Herod did not want to worship. He wanted to destroy.

The wise men set out, and the star they had seen goes before them, until it stops over the place where the child is. 'When they saw the star, they rejoiced with exceeding great joy.'

And then they come into the house.

God warns the wise men in a dream not to go back to Herod. So they go home to their own country another way.

Then the angel of the Lord appears to Joseph in a dream. Arise, take the young child and his mother, and flee into Egypt, for Herod will seek the young child to destroy him. Joseph gets up at once and takes them to Egypt, and they stay there until Herod dies. Matthew says this fulfilled the words of the prophet Hosea. 'Out of Egypt have I called my son.' Long ago, God brought Israel out of Egypt. Now God’s own Son would come out of Egypt too.

When Herod realises the wise men have not come back, he is furious. He sends soldiers to Bethlehem, and the little boys there are put to death. It was a terrible, cruel act. Matthew does not hide it. He says it fulfilled the words of Jeremiah, about 'Rachel weeping for her children, and would not be comforted'. The Bible does not pretend the world is not dark. Jesus was born into a world where rulers did evil things, and where mothers wept. That is the very world he came to save.

After Herod dies, an angel tells Joseph in a dream that it is safe to go back. But Herod’s son is ruling in Judea, so Joseph is warned to go to Galilee instead. They settle in a small town called Nazareth. And Matthew says that fulfilled the prophets’ words, 'He shall be called a Nazarene.' Nazareth was a little, humble place. The King would grow up there, out of sight.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 2. 'Saying, Where is he that is born King of the Jews? for we have seen his star in the east, and are come to worship him.' These men were not Jews. They came from another land, perhaps without the Scriptures. Yet they were the first in Matthew’s Gospel to come and worship Jesus. From the very start, Matthew shows that Jesus is not only for one nation. People from far away would come to him too. And notice why they came. Not to see him, but to worship him.

Verse 6. 'And thou Bethlehem, in the land of Juda, art not the least among the princes of Juda: for out of thee shall come a Governor, that shall rule my people Israel.' These are the words of the prophet Micah, spoken long before. Bethlehem was a small town, but it was David’s town. And God said the Ruler of his people would come from there. The priests knew the verse by heart. But they did not walk the short road to Bethlehem to see if it was true. Knowing the Bible is good. But it is not the same as coming to Jesus.

Verse 11. 'And when they were come into the house, they saw the young child with Mary his mother, and fell down, and worshipped him: and when they had opened their treasures, they presented unto him gifts; gold, and frankincense, and myrrh.' Gold is a gift for a king. Frankincense was a sweet incense burned in worship. Myrrh was a perfume, and later it was used when a body was buried. Many Christians have seen meaning in these gifts: a King, worthy of worship, who would one day die for us. The wise men gave their best, and they bowed low before a little child.

The psalmist wrote of the King, 'Yea, all kings shall fall down before him: all nations shall serve him.' And Isaiah said, 'And the Gentiles shall come to thy light, and kings to the brightness of thy rising.'

The wise men travelled a long way to worship Jesus. The priests knew where he was, but did not go. Where are you in this story? Today, take a moment simply to worship Jesus, and give him something of your best.

Here is today’s question. The wise men gave Jesus gold, frankincense and myrrh. What could you give to Jesus, that would show he is your King?

Let us pray. Lord Jesus, you are the King the wise men came to worship. Thank you that you came for people from every nation. Keep me from only knowing about you, and help me to come to you and worship you with my best. Amen.
