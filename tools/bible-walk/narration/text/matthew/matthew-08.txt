The Living Voice. Matthew, chapter 8. Speak the Word Only.

A man stands at the edge of the crowd. His skin is covered with sores. For years, no one has touched him. If people see him coming, they move away. The law says he must live outside the town, alone. But today, he walks right up to Jesus and falls on his knees. And Jesus does something nobody else would dare to do.

In chapter seven, Jesus finished the Sermon on the Mount. He taught us to ask, seek and knock, to treat others as we would want to be treated, and to build our lives on the rock by hearing his words and doing them. The people were astonished, because he taught with authority.

Now, in chapter eight, Jesus comes down from the mountain, and we see that same authority in action. Over sickness, over storms, and over the powers of evil. Listen for the different people who come to Jesus. And listen for the question the disciples ask in the boat.

When Jesus comes down from the mountain, great crowds follow him. And a leper comes and kneels before him. Leprosy was a skin disease that made a person unclean under the law. Lepers had to live apart from everyone else. He says, 'Lord, if thou wilt, thou canst make me clean.' He had no doubt that Jesus could. He only wondered if Jesus would.

And Jesus answers him.

Jesus tells him to go and show himself to the priest, and offer the gift Moses commanded, so that everyone would know he was truly clean.

When Jesus comes into Capernaum, a centurion comes to him. A centurion was a Roman officer in charge of a hundred soldiers. He was a foreigner, and part of the army that ruled over the Jews. But he comes to Jesus humbly. Lord, my servant is lying at home, paralysed and suffering terribly. Jesus says, 'I will come and heal him.'

But the centurion answers in a way that amazes Jesus.

He explains. I am a man under authority, with soldiers under me. I say to one, go, and he goes, and to another, come, and he comes. The centurion understood that Jesus had that kind of authority over sickness. Jesus did not need to be there. He only needed to say the word.

When Jesus hears this, he marvels. 'Verily I say unto you, I have not found so great faith, no, not in Israel.' And he says that many will come from the east and the west, and sit down with Abraham, Isaac and Jacob in the kingdom of heaven. People from every nation would come in, like this Roman soldier.

Then Jesus says to the centurion, go your way, as you have believed, so be it. And his servant is healed at that very moment.

Jesus goes into Peter’s house, and finds Peter’s mother in law in bed with a fever. He touches her hand, the fever leaves her, and she gets up and serves them.

Later, people bring many who are troubled by evil spirits, and Jesus casts out the spirits with a word, and heals all the sick. Matthew says this fulfilled Isaiah’s words. 'Himself took our infirmities, and bare our sicknesses.'

When crowds press around him, Jesus decides to cross the lake. A scribe says, Master, I will follow you wherever you go. Jesus answers, 'The foxes have holes, and the birds of the air have nests; but the Son of man hath not where to lay his head.' Following Jesus would not be comfortable. Another says, let me first go and bury my father. Jesus says, 'Follow me; and let the dead bury their dead.' Jesus was not being unkind. He was saying that following him must come first, before every other duty.

Then Jesus gets into a boat, and his disciples follow. Suddenly a great storm rises on the sea, so that the waves cover the boat. 'but he was asleep.' The disciples wake him. 'Lord, save us: we perish.' Jesus says, 'Why are ye fearful, O ye of little faith?' Then he gets up, rebukes the winds and the sea, and there is a great calm.

And the disciples ask a question.

On the other side of the lake, in the country of the Gergesenes, two men come out of the tombs, troubled by devils. They are so fierce that no one dares to pass that way. They cry out, what have we to do with thee, Jesus, thou Son of God? The devils knew exactly who he was. Nearby, a large herd of pigs is feeding. The devils beg to be sent into the pigs. Jesus says one word. Go. And the whole herd rushes down a steep bank into the sea.

The men looking after the pigs run into the town and tell everything. And the whole town comes out to meet Jesus. But instead of welcoming him, they beg him to leave. They cared more about their pigs than about the two men set free.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 3. 'And Jesus put forth his hand, and touched him, saying, I will; be thou clean. And immediately his leprosy was cleansed.' Jesus could have healed this man with a word, from a distance. But he chose to touch him. For years, no one had touched this man. Under the law, touching a leper made a person unclean. But when Jesus touched him, the uncleanness did not spread to Jesus. Instead, Jesus’ cleanness spread to the man. And notice his answer to the question. I will. Jesus is willing.

Verse 8. 'The centurion answered and said, Lord, I am not worthy that thou shouldest come under my roof: but speak the word only, and my servant shall be healed.' This powerful officer felt unworthy to have Jesus in his house. And he trusted that the word of Jesus was enough. That is faith: taking Jesus at his word, even when we cannot see anything happening yet. Jesus praised this foreigner’s faith above everyone else’s.

Verse 27. 'But the men marvelled, saying, What manner of man is this, that even the winds and the sea obey him!' The disciples were fishermen. They knew storms. They knew no man could command the wind. Only God made the sea, and only God could tell it to be still. Without saying so, they were beginning to see the answer to their own question. This man was more than a man.

Isaiah wrote, 'Surely he hath borne our griefs, and carried our sorrows'. And a psalm says, 'He maketh the storm a calm, so that the waves thereof are still.'

The leper asked, Lord, if you will. Jesus answered, I will. Is there something you need to bring to Jesus today? Come to him humbly, like the centurion, and trust his word. And if a storm is raging in your life, remember who is in the boat with you.

Here is today’s question. The disciples asked, what manner of man is this? From what you have heard so far in Matthew, how would you answer their question?

Let us pray. Lord Jesus, thank you that you are willing to touch and heal. Thank you that your word has power over sickness, storms and every evil. Give me faith like the centurion’s, to trust your word. And when I am afraid, help me to remember that you are with me in the boat. Amen.
