The Living Voice. Matthew, chapter 16. Thou Art the Christ, the Son of the Living God.

Imagine someone asks you, who is Jesus? You might say what other people say. A good teacher. A prophet. A wise man. But then they look at you and ask, no, who do you say he is? That is a different question. And one day, far in the north of the land, Jesus asked his friends exactly that.

In chapter fifteen, Jesus taught that what makes us unclean comes from the heart. A woman of Canaan would not give up asking for her daughter, and Jesus said, O woman, great is thy faith. Then he fed four thousand people with seven loaves.

Now, in chapter sixteen, Jesus asks his disciples who they believe he is. Peter answers, and then Jesus tells them something they do not want to hear. Listen for Peter’s answer. And listen for what Jesus says it means to follow him.

The Pharisees and Sadducees come to Jesus, testing him. Show us a sign from heaven, they say. Jesus answers, you know how to read the sky and tell what the weather will be. But you cannot read the signs of the times. No sign will be given, except the sign of the prophet Jonah. And he leaves them.

When the disciples cross the lake, they realise they have forgotten to bring bread. Jesus says, 'Take heed and beware of the leaven of the Pharisees and of the Sadducees.' The disciples think he is talking about bread. Jesus says, O ye of little faith, do you not remember the five loaves for the five thousand, and the seven loaves for the four thousand? I was not talking about bread. Then they understand. He meant the teaching of the Pharisees and Sadducees, which spread like yeast and puffed people up with pride.

Then Jesus comes to the region of Caesarea Philippi, far in the north. And he asks his disciples, 'Whom do men say that I the Son of man am?'

They say, some say John the Baptist, some say Elijah, others Jeremiah, or one of the prophets. They were all great men. But none of them was the answer.

Then Jesus asks, 'But whom say ye that I am?'

And Simon Peter answers.

Jesus says, 'Blessed art thou, Simon Barjona: for flesh and blood hath not revealed it unto thee, but my Father which is in heaven.' Barjona means son of Jonah. Peter had not worked this out by himself. God the Father had shown him.

Then Jesus says, 'thou art Peter, and upon this rock I will build my church; and the gates of hell shall not prevail against it.' The name Peter means rock. And Jesus speaks of giving Peter the keys of the kingdom of heaven. Christians have understood these words in different ways. Some see the rock as Peter himself, and some as Peter’s confession of faith, or Christ himself. But all agree on the promise. Jesus builds his church, and nothing, not even death, will defeat it.

From that time, Jesus begins to tell his disciples plainly that he must go to Jerusalem, suffer many things from the elders, chief priests and scribes, be killed, 'and be raised again the third day.'

Peter cannot bear it. He takes Jesus aside and begins to scold him. Be it far from thee, Lord: this shall not be unto thee. But Jesus turns and says to Peter, 'Get thee behind me, Satan: thou art an offence unto me: for thou savourest not the things that be of God, but those that be of men.' Just a moment before, Peter spoke words from God. Now he was speaking the very temptation the devil had offered in the wilderness: a crown without a cross. Jesus would not take that road.

Then Jesus tells all his disciples what it means to follow him.

'For whosoever will save his life shall lose it: and whosoever will lose his life for my sake shall find it.'

And then Jesus asks a question to make everyone think.

Jesus says that the Son of man will come in the glory of his Father with his angels, and reward everyone according to what they have done.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 16. 'And Simon Peter answered and said, Thou art the Christ, the Son of the living God.' This is the heart of the whole Gospel. The Christ means the Messiah, the anointed King God promised through the prophets, the son of David. And the Son of the living God means far more than a man. Many people say nice things about Jesus. But Peter said who Jesus really is. And Jesus still asks each of us the same question. Whom say ye that I am?

Verse 24. 'Then said Jesus unto his disciples, If any man will come after me, let him deny himself, and take up his cross, and follow me.' To deny yourself means to say no to putting yourself first. In those days, someone carrying a cross was walking to their death. Jesus was saying, following me means giving up your own way, and putting me first in everything. It sounds hard. But Jesus never asks us to go anywhere he has not gone first. He carried his cross for us.

Verse 26. 'For what is a man profited, if he shall gain the whole world, and lose his own soul? or what shall a man give in exchange for his soul?' Profited means gained. Imagine owning everything in the whole world, every house, every phone, every bit of money. And then losing your soul. It would be the worst trade ever made. Your soul is worth more than the whole world. Nothing is worth losing it for. Jesus thought you were worth his life.

Isaiah heard God say, 'Behold, I lay in Zion for a foundation a stone, a tried stone, a precious corner stone, a sure foundation'. And John wrote, 'Whosoever shall confess that Jesus is the Son of God, God dwelleth in him, and he in God.'

Jesus asks you today, whom say ye that I am? How would you answer in your own words? And think about one way you could deny yourself today, choosing what Jesus wants over what you want.

Here is today’s question. Jesus asked, what shall a man give in exchange for his soul? What things in the world are tempting to chase, even if they might cost you your walk with God?

Let us pray. Lord Jesus, you are the Christ, the Son of the living God. Thank you that you went to the cross for me, and rose again on the third day. Help me to deny myself, take up my cross, and follow you. Keep me from trading my soul for anything this world offers. Amen.
