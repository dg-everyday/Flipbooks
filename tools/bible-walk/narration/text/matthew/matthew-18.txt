The Living Voice. Matthew, chapter 18. Except Ye Become as Little Children.

A group of grown men are arguing. Which one of us is the most important? Who will have the best place when Jesus becomes king? Jesus listens. Then, without a word, he calls a small child over, and stands the child right in the middle of them. And everyone goes quiet. What could a child have to teach them?

In chapter seventeen, Jesus was transfigured on the mountain, and the Father said, this is my beloved Son, hear ye him. Then Jesus healed a boy his disciples could not help, and said that faith as small as a mustard seed can move mountains.

Now, in chapter eighteen, comes the fourth great teaching in Matthew. It is about how God’s people should treat one another. Listen for what Jesus says about children. And listen for his answer when Peter asks how many times to forgive.

The disciples come to Jesus and ask, 'Who is the greatest in the kingdom of heaven?'

Jesus calls a little child to him, and sets the child in the middle of them.

And he goes on, 'Whosoever therefore shall humble himself as this little child, the same is greatest in the kingdom of heaven.' And whoever welcomes one such child in my name welcomes me.

Then Jesus gives a very serious warning. If anyone causes one of these little ones who believe in him to stumble and fall away, it would be better for him to be thrown into the depths of the sea. Jesus uses strong words, because he cares so much for children, and for anyone small and weak in faith. Hurting them is a very serious thing to God. And he uses strong pictures to show that we must cut out of our lives anything that leads us into sin.

'Take heed that ye despise not one of these little ones; for I say unto you, That in heaven their angels do always behold the face of my Father which is in heaven.' No child is unimportant to God.

Then Jesus tells a story you may remember from Luke. If a man has a hundred sheep, and one wanders away, will he not leave the ninety nine and go into the hills to look for the one that is lost? And if he finds it, he is happier about that one sheep than about the ninety nine that never wandered. 'Even so it is not the will of your Father which is in heaven, that one of these little ones should perish.'

Next, Jesus teaches what to do when someone sins against you. First, go to him and talk about it, just between the two of you. If he listens, you have won back your brother. If he will not listen, take one or two others with you. If he still will not listen, tell it to the church. And if he will not even listen to the church, treat him as an outsider. The goal at every step is to win the person back. Jesus also says that what his followers bind on earth is bound in heaven.

Then Jesus makes a wonderful promise.

Peter comes to Jesus with a question. 'Lord, how oft shall my brother sin against me, and I forgive him? till seven times?' Peter thought he was being very generous. Many teachers said three times was enough.

And Jesus answers.

Then Jesus tells a story. A king wanted to settle accounts with his servants. One servant owed him ten thousand talents. That was an enormous amount, more than a person could earn in many lifetimes. He could never pay it back. The king ordered that he and his family be sold to pay the debt. But the servant fell on his knees. Be patient with me, and I will pay you everything. 'Then the lord of that servant was moved with compassion, and loosed him, and forgave him the debt.' The whole debt, gone.

But that same servant went out and found a fellow servant who owed him a hundred pence, a small amount, the wages of a few months. He grabbed him by the throat. Pay me what you owe! His fellow servant fell down and begged, be patient with me, and I will pay you. But he would not. He had him thrown into prison until he could pay.

The other servants were very upset, and told the king everything. The king called the first servant in. You wicked servant. I forgave you all that debt because you begged me. 'Shouldest not thou also have had compassion on thy fellowservant, even as I had pity on thee?' And the angry king handed him over to be punished until he paid all he owed. Jesus says, so my heavenly Father will do to you, if you do not forgive your brother from your heart.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 3. 'And said, Verily I say unto you, Except ye be converted, and become as little children, ye shall not enter into the kingdom of heaven.' To be converted means to be turned around. The disciples wanted to be great. Jesus said, you need to become small. Little children do not pretend to have it all together. They trust, they depend on others, they know they need help. That is how we come into God’s kingdom. Not by being important, but by trusting our Father like a child.

Verse 20. 'For where two or three are gathered together in my name, there am I in the midst of them.' This is a promise for small groups. Not only for big crowds or grand buildings. Two or three people praying together, reading God’s Word together, or listening together, in Jesus’ name, and Jesus is there with them. A family at home, two friends praying, a small group. Jesus says, I am there.

Verse 22. 'Jesus saith unto him, I say not unto thee, Until seven times: but, Until seventy times seven.' Jesus was not saying, count up to four hundred and ninety, and then stop. He meant, do not keep count at all. Keep on forgiving. The story shows us why. God has forgiven us a debt we could never pay. When we remember how much we have been forgiven, we can forgive others the smaller things they owe us. Forgiving does not mean pretending something did not hurt. It means letting go of the debt, as God let go of ours.

The psalmist wrote, 'Surely I have behaved and quieted myself, as a child that is weaned of his mother'. And Paul wrote, 'And be ye kind one to another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, even as God for Christ’s sake hath forgiven you.'

Is there someone you need to forgive? It may take time, and it may be hard. Ask God to help you let go of the debt, as he let go of yours. And find one or two people to pray with this week, and remember that Jesus is there.

Here is today’s question. The servant was forgiven a huge debt, but would not forgive a small one. Why do you think it is sometimes hard to forgive, even when we have been forgiven so much?

Let us pray. Lord Jesus, help me to become like a little child, trusting my Father. Thank you that you came to find the lost sheep. Thank you that God has forgiven me a debt I could never pay. Give me a forgiving heart, so that I can forgive others, again and again. Amen.
