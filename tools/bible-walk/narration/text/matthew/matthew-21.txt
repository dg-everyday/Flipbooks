The Living Voice. Matthew, chapter 21. Hosanna to the Son of David.

The road into the city is packed with people. Some are pulling off their coats and spreading them on the ground. Others are cutting branches from the trees and laying them down like a carpet. Everyone is shouting and singing. And riding slowly through the middle of it all, not on a warhorse, but on a young donkey, comes Jesus.

In chapter twenty, Jesus told the story of the workers in the vineyard, and said, the last shall be first. He told his disciples that the greatest must be a servant, for he came to give his life a ransom for many. At Jericho, he opened the eyes of two blind men.

Now, in chapter twenty one, Jesus arrives in Jerusalem. The last week before the cross begins. Listen for what the crowds shout. Listen for what Jesus does in the temple. And listen for the stone the builders rejected.

As they come near Jerusalem, to the Mount of Olives, Jesus sends two disciples ahead. Go into the village, and you will find a donkey tied up, with her colt. Untie them and bring them to me. If anyone asks, say, the Lord needs them.

Matthew says this fulfilled the prophet Zechariah. 'Tell ye the daughter of Sion, Behold, thy King cometh unto thee, meek, and sitting upon an ass'. Kings who came to make war rode on horses. A king who came in peace rode on a donkey. Jesus was showing everyone what kind of King he was.

The disciples bring the donkey and the colt and lay their coats on them, and Jesus sits on them. The crowd spreads coats and branches on the road.

And the crowds shout.

When Jesus enters Jerusalem, the whole city is stirred. 'Who is this?' And the crowds answer, 'This is Jesus the prophet of Nazareth of Galilee.'

Jesus goes into the temple. In the outer courts, people were buying and selling animals for sacrifices, and changing money, and making a big profit. Jesus drives them all out, and turns over the tables of the money changers and the seats of those who sold doves.

And he tells them why.

Then the blind and the lame come to him in the temple, and he heals them. Children in the temple are shouting, Hosanna to the Son of David. The chief priests and scribes are furious. Do you hear what these children are saying? Jesus says, yes. Have you never read, 'Out of the mouth of babes and sucklings thou hast perfected praise?' Then he leaves the city and stays in Bethany.

On his way back into the city, Jesus finds a fig tree with nothing on it but leaves. He says, let no fruit ever grow on you again, and it withers. The disciples are amazed. Jesus says, if you have faith and do not doubt, even a mountain could be thrown into the sea. 'And all things, whatsoever ye shall ask in prayer, believing, ye shall receive.' The fig tree was a picture. It looked good from far away, but had no fruit, like religion that looks good but does not please God.

In the temple, the chief priests and elders ask him, by what authority do you do these things? Jesus answers with a question. John’s baptism, was it from heaven, or from men? If they say from heaven, why did they not believe John? If they say from men, the people will be angry. So they answer, we cannot tell. Then neither will I tell you, says Jesus.

Then Jesus tells a story. A man had two sons. He said to the first, go and work in my vineyard. The son said, I will not. But later he changed his mind, and went. The father said the same to the second son, who answered, I go, sir. But he did not go. Which one did his father’s will? The first, they say. Jesus says, tax collectors and sinners are going into the kingdom of God ahead of you. They said no at first, but when John came, they believed and turned back. You said yes to God, but did not do it.

Then another story. A landowner planted a vineyard, rented it to farmers, and went away. At harvest time, he sent servants to collect his share of the fruit. But the farmers beat one, killed another, and stoned another. He sent more servants, and they did the same. Last of all, he sent his son. Surely they will respect my son. But they said, this is the heir. Let us kill him and take his inheritance. And they threw him out of the vineyard and killed him. What will the owner do to those farmers? The listeners say, he will destroy those wicked men and give the vineyard to others.

Then Jesus asks them a question from the psalms.

The leaders realise he means them, but they are afraid to arrest him.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 9. 'And the multitudes that went before, and that followed, cried, saying, Hosanna to the Son of David: Blessed is he that cometh in the name of the Lord; Hosanna in the highest.' Hosanna means save us now. It was a cry of praise and a prayer at the same time. Son of David meant the promised King. The crowd was right about who Jesus was. But many of them expected him to drive out the Romans. Instead, within a few days, he would save them in a way no one expected, by giving his life.

Verse 13. 'And said unto them, It is written, My house shall be called the house of prayer; but ye have made it a den of thieves.' The temple was meant to be a place where people from every nation could come and pray. Instead, the outer court had become a noisy market where people were cheated. Jesus was angry, not with a bad temper, but with a holy anger at the way God’s house was being misused. God wants his people to be places of prayer, not profit.

Verse 42. 'Jesus saith unto them, Did ye never read in the scriptures, The stone which the builders rejected, the same is become the head of the corner: this is the Lord’s doing, and it is marvellous in our eyes?' Builders choose stones carefully. Here is a stone they threw away as useless. But God made it the cornerstone, the most important stone in the whole building, the one that holds everything together. Jesus was about to be rejected and killed by the leaders. But God would raise him up, and make him the cornerstone of everything.

Zechariah wrote, 'Rejoice greatly, O daughter of Zion; shout, O daughter of Jerusalem: behold, thy King cometh unto thee'. And Isaiah heard God say, 'for mine house shall be called an house of prayer for all people.'

The children in the temple shouted praise to Jesus, even when the grown ups were angry. Take a moment today to praise Jesus as your King. And ask yourself, like the two sons, am I only saying yes to God, or am I doing what he asks?

Here is today’s question. The crowd shouted Hosanna, which means save us now. What would you like Jesus to save you from, or help you with, today?

Let us pray. Lord Jesus, Hosanna to the Son of David. You are the King who came in peace, humble and riding on a donkey. You are the stone the builders rejected, and God made you the cornerstone. Make my life a house of prayer, and help me not only to say yes to you, but to do your will. Amen.
