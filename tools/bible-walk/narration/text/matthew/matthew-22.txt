The Living Voice. Matthew, chapter 22. Many Are Called, but Few Are Chosen.

A king is throwing the biggest wedding party anyone has ever seen, for his own son. The tables are set, the food is ready, the invitations have gone out. But one by one, the guests make excuses. I am busy with my farm. I have business to do. Nobody comes. So the king sends his servants out into the streets, to invite anyone they can find. And the banquet hall fills up with the most surprising guests.

In chapter twenty one, Jesus rode into Jerusalem on a donkey, while crowds shouted Hosanna to the Son of David. He drove the money changers out of the temple, and told stories that showed the religious leaders had rejected God’s messengers. He spoke of the stone the builders rejected, which became the cornerstone.

Now, in chapter twenty two, the leaders try to trap Jesus with hard questions. Listen for the wedding story. Listen for how Jesus answers the question about taxes. And listen for the greatest commandment.

Jesus tells another parable. The kingdom of heaven is like a king who prepared a wedding feast for his son. He sent his servants to call the invited guests, but they would not come. He sent more servants. Tell them, my dinner is ready, my oxen and fat calves are prepared, everything is ready, come to the wedding! But they made light of it. One went off to his farm, another to his business. Others mistreated the king’s servants, and even killed them. The king was angry, and sent his army to punish those who had done this.

Then the king said to his servants, the wedding is ready, but those who were invited were not worthy. Go out to the main roads, and invite everyone you find. So the servants went out and gathered everyone they could find, bad and good alike. And the wedding hall was filled with guests.

But when the king came in to see the guests, he saw a man who was not wearing wedding clothes. Friend, how did you get in here without wedding clothes? And the man was speechless. The king had him taken out of the feast. In those days, a host often provided wedding clothes for guests. To refuse them was to refuse the king’s gift, and dishonour his son.

And Jesus ends the story.

Then the Pharisees plot to trap Jesus in his words. They send some of their followers, along with supporters of Herod, who say, Master, we know you are honest and teach the way of God truthfully, and you do not care what anyone thinks. So tell us, is it lawful to pay taxes to Caesar, or not?

It was a clever trap. If Jesus said yes, the people would be angry, because they hated paying taxes to Rome. If he said no, the Romans could arrest him as a rebel.

Jesus sees through it. Why are you testing me, you hypocrites? Show me the tax money. They bring him a penny, a Roman coin. Whose picture and name are on this coin? Caesar’s, they say.

And then Jesus gives his answer.

They are amazed, and they leave him.

The same day, the Sadducees come. They did not believe in the resurrection, that the dead will rise. They tell him a story about a woman who married seven brothers, one after another, as each one died. In the resurrection, whose wife will she be? They thought the whole idea was ridiculous.

Jesus says, 'Ye do err, not knowing the scriptures, nor the power of God.' In the resurrection, people do not marry, but are like the angels in heaven. And about the dead rising, have you not read what God said? I am the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob. 'God is not the God of the dead, but of the living.' God said I am, not I was. Abraham, Isaac and Jacob are alive to God. The crowds are astonished.

When the Pharisees hear that he has silenced the Sadducees, they gather together. One of them, an expert in the law, asks him a question to test him. 'Master, which is the great commandment in the law?' The teachers counted six hundred and thirteen commandments in the law. Which one mattered most?

And Jesus answers.

'This is the first and great commandment. And the second is like unto it, Thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself. On these two commandments hang all the law and the prophets.'

Then Jesus asks the Pharisees a question. What do you think about the Christ? Whose son is he? The son of David, they say. Then how does David, in the psalms, call him Lord? 'The LORD said unto my Lord, Sit thou on my right hand'. If David calls him Lord, how can he be his son? No one can answer him a word. And from that day, no one dares to ask him any more questions. The answer was that the Christ is both. David’s son, and David’s Lord.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 14. 'For many are called, but few are chosen.' God’s invitation goes out to everyone, good and bad alike. The king wanted his hall full. But being invited is not the same as coming, and coming on our own terms is not the same as accepting the king’s gift. Many people hear God’s call, and make excuses. Those who come humbly, and accept what the king provides, find themselves at the feast. God’s invitation is open to you.

Verse 21. 'They say unto him, Caesar’s. Then saith he unto them, Render therefore unto Caesar the things which are Caesar’s; and unto God the things that are God’s.' Render means give back. The coin had Caesar’s image on it, so give Caesar his tax. Jesus says we should be good citizens, and pay what we owe. But there is something with God’s image on it. People. Genesis says God made us in his image. So give yourself to God. Caesar may have your coins, but God should have your whole heart.

Verse 37. 'Jesus said unto him, Thou shalt love the Lord thy God with all thy heart, and with all thy soul, and with all thy mind.' Jesus quoted the great words of Moses that every Jewish child learned. Love God with everything you are. Not just a part of your heart, or a part of your week, but all of it. And then the second command flows from the first: love your neighbour as yourself. If we love God with all our heart, we will love the people he made.

Moses told Israel, 'Hear, O Israel: The LORD our God is one LORD: And thou shalt love the LORD thy God with all thine heart, and with all thy soul, and with all thy might.' And God said in the beginning, 'Let us make man in our image, after our likeness'.

God’s invitation to his feast is open to you. Have you made any excuses? Think about the two great commandments today. Choose one way to show love for God, and one way to show love for a neighbour.

Here is today’s question. Jesus said, give to God the things that are God’s. If you bear God’s image, what does it mean to give yourself to him?

Let us pray. Lord God, thank you for inviting me to your feast, though I do not deserve it. Help me to come humbly, and accept your gift. Teach me to love you with all my heart, soul and mind, and to love my neighbour as myself. I give myself to you, for I bear your image. Amen.
