The Living Voice. Matthew, chapter 23. As a Hen Gathereth Her Chickens.

Imagine a cup that has been washed and polished on the outside until it shines. But inside, it is still full of old, dirty, sticky leftovers. Would you drink from it? Of course not. Jesus said some people are like that cup. Beautiful on the outside. And on this day in the temple, he spoke more strongly than at any other time in Matthew’s Gospel.

In chapter twenty two, the religious leaders tried to trap Jesus with hard questions. He told them to render unto Caesar the things that are Caesar’s, and unto God the things that are God’s. He named the greatest commandments: love God with all your heart, and love your neighbour as yourself.

Now, in chapter twenty three, Jesus speaks to the crowds and his disciples about the scribes and Pharisees. These are some of the strongest words he ever spoke. Before we listen, remember this. Jesus was Jewish. His disciples were Jewish. He was not warning against a people. He was warning against hypocrisy, and that warning is for everyone, including us. Listen for what God cares about most. And listen for how Jesus feels about Jerusalem.

Jesus says to the crowds and his disciples, the scribes and Pharisees sit in the seat of Moses. They teach the law. So do what they tell you. 'but do not ye after their works: for they say, and do not.'

They tie up heavy loads of rules and put them on people’s shoulders, but they will not lift a finger to help carry them. Everything they do is to be seen. They make their prayer boxes big and their robe tassels long. They love the best seats at feasts and in the synagogues, and they love being greeted in the markets and called Rabbi, which means teacher.

But it should not be like this for you, Jesus says. You have one Master, Christ, and you are all brothers and sisters. 'But he that is greatest among you shall be your servant.'

And then he gives a rule that turns the world upside down.

Then Jesus speaks a series of woes. Woe means great sorrow is coming. It is a warning, and a lament. Seven times he says, woe unto you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites. A hypocrite is someone who pretends, who wears a mask.

Woe to you, because you shut the door of the kingdom of heaven in people’s faces. You do not go in yourselves, and you stop others from going in.

Woe to you, blind guides, who make complicated rules about which promises count and which do not.

And then Jesus speaks about tithing.

'Ye blind guides, which strain at a gnat, and swallow a camel.' It was a funny picture. They would carefully strain their drinks so as not to swallow a tiny unclean insect, and then gulp down a whole camel without noticing. They worried about tiny details and missed the most important things.

Woe to you, because you clean the outside of the cup and dish, but inside you are full of greed. 'Thou blind Pharisee, cleanse first that which is within the cup and platter, that the outside of them may be clean also.'

Woe to you, because you are like tombs painted white. They look beautiful on the outside, but inside they are full of death and uncleanness. 'Even so ye also outwardly appear righteous unto men, but within ye are full of hypocrisy and iniquity.'

Woe to you, because you build fine tombs for the prophets and say, if we had lived back then, we would never have killed them. But you are just like those who did.

Jesus warns that he will send them prophets, wise men and teachers, and they will reject them too, as their fathers did.

And then, after all these hard words, Jesus’ voice changes. He looks out over the city, and his heart breaks.

'Behold, your house is left unto you desolate.' Desolate means empty and abandoned. Jesus was warning that the temple would be destroyed, as it was, a generation later. 'For I say unto you, Ye shall not see me henceforth, till ye shall say, Blessed is he that cometh in the name of the Lord.'

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 12. 'And whosoever shall exalt himself shall be abased; and he that shall humble himself shall be exalted.' To exalt yourself means to lift yourself up, to make yourself look important. To be abased means to be brought low. In God’s kingdom, the way up is down. Those who push to the front will end up at the back. Those who take the low place, and serve others, God himself will lift up. This is the way Jesus lived, all the way to the cross.

Verse 23. 'Woe unto you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites! for ye pay tithe of mint and anise and cummin, and have omitted the weightier matters of the law, judgment, mercy, and faith: these ought ye to have done, and not to leave the other undone.' A tithe was a tenth, given to God. These men even gave a tenth of the tiny herbs in their gardens. But they forgot the weightier, heavier, more important things: justice, mercy and faithfulness. Jesus did not say tithing was wrong. He said, do not miss the heart of it. God cares most about how we treat people.

Verse 37. 'O Jerusalem, Jerusalem, thou that killest the prophets, and stonest them which are sent unto thee, how often would I have gathered thy children together, even as a hen gathereth her chickens under her wings, and ye would not!' This is the heart behind all the warnings. Jesus did not speak these hard words because he hated these people. He spoke them because he loved them. A mother hen spreads her wings over her chicks to protect them from danger. That is what Jesus longed to do. And ye would not. God never forces us. But his arms are always open.

The psalmist wrote, 'He shall cover thee with his feathers, and under his wings shalt thou trust'. And the prophet Samuel said, 'Behold, to obey is better than sacrifice'.

Is there any part of your life where you are wearing a mask, looking good on the outside while something else is going on inside? Bring it honestly to God. He longs to gather you under his wings, not to shame you, but to make you clean inside.

Here is today’s question. Jesus said that justice, mercy and faith are the weightier matters. Which of these would you like God to grow in your life?

Let us pray. Lord Jesus, thank you that your hard words come from a heart of love. Keep me from pretending, and from caring more about how I look than about who I am. Clean me inside. Teach me justice, mercy and faith. Gather me under your wings, as a hen gathers her chickens. Amen.
