The Living Voice. Matthew, chapter 24. Heaven and Earth Shall Pass Away.

The disciples stand in front of the temple in Jerusalem, gazing up at it. Its walls are made of huge white stones, some as big as a bus, and parts of it glitter with gold. It looks as if it will stand for ever. Then Jesus says something that stops them in their tracks. Not one of these stones will be left on top of another. Every one will be thrown down.

In chapter twenty three, Jesus warned the scribes and Pharisees against hypocrisy, and said that justice, mercy and faith are the weightier matters of the law. Then he wept over Jerusalem, longing to gather her children as a hen gathers her chickens.

Now, in chapter twenty four, Jesus begins the fifth and last great teaching in Matthew, about the future. Christians have understood parts of this chapter in different ways. Some of what Jesus said came true when the temple was destroyed, a generation later. Some of it still waits for the day he comes again. But what Jesus asks of his followers here is plain and simple. Listen for what Jesus tells them to watch for. And listen for what will never pass away.

As Jesus leaves the temple, his disciples point out how magnificent its buildings are. And Jesus says, 'See ye not all these things? verily I say unto you, There shall not be left here one stone upon another, that shall not be thrown down.'

Later, as Jesus sits on the Mount of Olives, looking across at the temple, the disciples come to him privately. Tell us, when will these things happen? And what will be the sign of your coming, and of the end of the age?

Jesus begins, 'Take heed that no man deceive you.' Many will come using my name, saying, I am the Christ, and will fool many people. You will hear of wars and rumours of wars. 'see that ye be not troubled: for all these things must come to pass, but the end is not yet.' Nations will fight nations. There will be famines and earthquakes in many places. But all this is only the beginning, like the first pains before a baby is born.

Then you will be handed over to suffer, and hated by all nations because of me. Many will fall away and turn on each other. False prophets will deceive many. And because evil grows, the love of many will grow cold.

But Jesus gives a promise to those who keep going.

'And this gospel of the kingdom shall be preached in all the world for a witness unto all nations; and then shall the end come.'

Jesus speaks of terrible days in Judea, when people should flee to the mountains without stopping for anything. He warns that false Christs and false prophets will rise up, and do great signs, to deceive even God’s chosen people if they could. If anyone says, look, the Christ is out in the desert, or hidden in a room, do not believe it. For when the Son of man comes, it will be like lightning that flashes from the east to the west. Everyone will see it.

The sun and moon will be darkened, and the stars will fall. And then all the peoples of the earth will see the Son of man coming on the clouds of heaven, with power and great glory. He will send his angels with a loud trumpet call, and they will gather his chosen ones from the four winds, from one end of heaven to the other.

Learn from the fig tree, Jesus says. When its branches grow tender and put out leaves, you know summer is near. In the same way, when you see these things, know that it is near, right at the door.

And then Jesus says something about his own words.

But no one knows the day or the hour, not even the angels in heaven, but only the Father.

It will be like the days of Noah. Before the flood, people were eating and drinking and getting married, right up to the day Noah went into the ark. They did not know anything was coming until the flood swept them all away. So it will be when the Son of man comes. Two men will be working in a field. One will be taken, and the other left. Two women will be grinding grain. One will be taken, and the other left.

And so Jesus gives his command.

If the owner of a house knew when a thief was coming, he would stay awake and not let his house be broken into. 'Therefore be ye also ready: for in such an hour as ye think not the Son of man cometh.'

Who is the faithful and wise servant? The one the master leaves in charge of his household, to give them food at the right time. 'Blessed is that servant, whom his lord when he cometh shall find so doing.' But if a servant says, my master is taking a long time, and starts to mistreat the other servants and waste his time, his master will come on a day he does not expect, and he will face judgment.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 13. 'But he that shall endure unto the end, the same shall be saved.' To endure means to keep going, to hold on, to not give up. Jesus did not promise his followers an easy road. He spoke honestly about trouble ahead. But he also promised that those who keep trusting him, all the way to the end, will be saved. Following Jesus is not a sprint. It is a long walk. And every step of faithfulness counts.

Verse 35. 'Heaven and earth shall pass away, but my words shall not pass away.' The temple looked like it would last for ever, but it was thrown down. Mountains, nations, even the sky above us, will not last for ever. But the words of Jesus will. Everything else can change, and his words will still be true. That is why it is worth building our lives on them, like the wise man who built his house on the rock.

Verse 42. 'Watch therefore: for ye know not what hour your Lord doth come.' Watching does not mean staring at the sky, or trying to work out dates. Jesus said no one knows the day or the hour. Watching means living ready. Being faithful in the work he has given us, caring for others, keeping our hearts close to him, so that whenever he comes, he finds us doing what he asked.

Isaiah wrote, 'The grass withereth, the flower fadeth: but the word of our God shall stand for ever.' And Daniel saw one 'like the Son of man came with the clouds of heaven'.

Jesus told his followers not to be troubled, and not to be fooled. Do not be frightened by scary news. Instead, be like the faithful servant. Is there something Jesus has given you to do that you can be faithful in today?

Here is today’s question. Jesus said, blessed is the servant his lord finds faithfully at work when he comes. What would you like Jesus to find you doing when he comes?

Let us pray. Lord Jesus, thank you that your words will never pass away, even when everything else changes. Keep me from being troubled or deceived. Help me to endure to the end, and to watch, living ready and faithful every day, until you come. Amen.
