The Living Voice. Matthew, chapter 25. Well Done, Thou Good and Faithful Servant.

Ten young women are waiting for a wedding procession, holding their little oil lamps. The bridegroom is late. Very late. One by one, they fall asleep. Then suddenly, a shout rings out. He is coming! They jump up to light their lamps. But five of them find their oil has run out. And while they rush off to buy more, the door to the wedding feast is shut.

In chapter twenty four, Jesus sat on the Mount of Olives and spoke about the future. He said the temple would be thrown down, and that he would come again with power and great glory. He said, heaven and earth shall pass away, but my words shall not pass away. And he told his followers to watch, because no one knows the day or the hour.

Now, in chapter twenty five, Jesus finishes his teaching with three stories about being ready for his coming. Listen for the difference between the wise and the foolish. Listen for what the master says to his faithful servants. And listen for where Jesus says we can find him today.

Jesus says, the kingdom of heaven is like ten young women, bridesmaids, who took their lamps and went out to meet the bridegroom. Five of them were wise, and five were foolish. The foolish ones took their lamps, but no extra oil. The wise ones took flasks of oil with their lamps.

The bridegroom was a long time coming, and they all grew drowsy and fell asleep. Then, very late, a cry rang out. 'Behold, the bridegroom cometh; go ye out to meet him.' All the young women got up and trimmed their lamps. The foolish ones said to the wise, give us some of your oil, our lamps are going out. The wise answered, there may not be enough for us and for you. Go and buy some for yourselves.

While they were away buying oil, the bridegroom came. Those who were ready went in with him to the wedding, 'and the door was shut.' Later, the other young women came and called, Lord, Lord, open to us. But he answered, I do not know you.

And Jesus gives the lesson.

Then Jesus tells another story. A man was going on a long journey. He called his servants and put them in charge of his money. To one he gave five talents, to another two, and to another one, 'to every man according to his several ability'. A talent was a large amount of money, worth many years of wages. Then he left.

The servant with five talents went straight to work, traded with the money, and made five more. The one with two talents made two more. But the servant with one talent dug a hole in the ground and hid his master’s money.

After a long time, the master came back to settle accounts. The servant with five talents came and said, master, you gave me five talents. Look, I have made five more.

And the master answered him.

The servant with two talents heard exactly the same words. He had less, but he was just as faithful.

Then the servant with one talent came. Master, I knew you were a hard man. I was afraid, so I hid your talent in the ground. Here it is. The master said, you wicked and lazy servant. You could at least have put my money in the bank. Take the talent from him and give it to the one with ten. And the master sent the useless servant away from his presence. He had wasted what he was given, out of fear and laziness.

Then Jesus describes the day when he comes in his glory. When the Son of man comes in his glory, with all his angels, he will sit on his glorious throne. All the nations will be gathered before him, and he will separate the people from one another, as a shepherd separates the sheep from the goats. He will put the sheep on his right, and the goats on his left.

The King will say to those on his right, 'Come, ye blessed of my Father, inherit the kingdom prepared for you from the foundation of the world'. For I was hungry, and you gave me food. I was thirsty, and you gave me drink. I was a stranger, and you took me in. I needed clothes, and you clothed me. I was sick, and you visited me. I was in prison, and you came to me.

The righteous will be surprised. Lord, when did we see you hungry, or thirsty, or a stranger, or sick, or in prison?

And the King will answer.

Then he will turn to those on his left, who never helped anyone in need. Whatever you did not do for one of the least of these, he says, you did not do for me. And they will go away into punishment, but the righteous into eternal life.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 13. 'Watch therefore, for ye know neither the day nor the hour wherein the Son of man cometh.' The foolish young women were not bad people. They were invited, they had lamps, they even waited. But they were not ready when the moment came, and you cannot borrow readiness from someone else. Each of us must be ready for ourselves. Being ready means knowing Jesus, and keeping our faith alive, like a lamp kept full of oil.

Verse 21. 'His lord said unto him, Well done, thou good and faithful servant: thou hast been faithful over a few things, I will make thee ruler over many things: enter thou into the joy of thy lord.' God has given each of us different gifts, time, abilities and opportunities. Some have more, and some have less. God does not ask us to do what someone else can do. He asks us to be faithful with what we have. And the reward is not just more work. It is joy, the joy of our Lord himself.

Verse 40. 'And the King shall answer and say unto them, Verily I say unto you, Inasmuch as ye have done it unto one of the least of these my brethren, ye have done it unto me.' Inasmuch means in so far as. Jesus says that when we care for someone who is hungry, lonely, sick, or forgotten, we are caring for him. The righteous did not even know they were serving Jesus. They were simply being kind. Every act of kindness to someone in need is an act of love for Jesus himself.

The proverb says, 'He that hath pity upon the poor lendeth unto the LORD; and that which he hath given will he pay him again.' And Paul wrote, 'Moreover it is required in stewards, that a man be found faithful.'

What has God given you: a skill, some time, a kind word, something you own? Do not bury it. Use it for him today. And look around you for someone who is one of the least of these. Helping them is helping Jesus.

Here is today’s question. Jesus said that when we help the least of these, we help him. Who is someone in need near you that you could help this week?

Let us pray. Lord Jesus, help me to be ready for your coming, with my faith kept alive. Thank you for the gifts you have given me. Help me to be faithful with them, however small they seem. Open my eyes to see you in the hungry, the lonely, the sick and the forgotten, and to serve you by serving them. Amen.
