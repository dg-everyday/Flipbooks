The Living Voice. Matthew, chapter 26. Could Ye Not Watch with Me One Hour?.

A woman walks into a room full of men, carrying a beautiful stone jar. Before anyone can stop her, she breaks it open and pours the whole thing over Jesus’ head. The smell of costly perfume fills the house. The disciples are shocked. What a waste! That could have been sold for a fortune! But Jesus says, leave her alone. She has done a beautiful thing.

In chapter twenty five, Jesus told three stories about being ready for his coming: the wise and foolish young women, the faithful servants with their talents, and the sheep and the goats. He said that whatever we do for the least of these, we do for him.

Now, in chapter twenty six, Jesus’ teaching is finished, and his suffering begins. Listen for the woman’s gift, and Judas’s bargain. Listen for what Jesus asks his friends in the garden. And listen for Peter, and the rooster.

When Jesus finishes his teaching, he tells his disciples that in two days, at the Passover, he will be handed over to be crucified. Meanwhile, the chief priests meet with Caiaphas the high priest, and plot to arrest him secretly.

In Bethany, a woman pours a jar of very expensive ointment on Jesus’ head. The disciples are indignant. Why this waste? It could have been sold and given to the poor.

Jesus says, why are you troubling her? She has done a good thing to me. You will always have the poor with you, but you will not always have me. By pouring this on my body, she has prepared me for burial.

And then Jesus makes a promise about her.

Then one of the twelve, Judas Iscariot, goes to the chief priests. What will you give me if I hand him over to you? And they agree on thirty pieces of silver. Long before, the prophet Zechariah had spoken of a shepherd valued at thirty pieces of silver. From then on, Judas looks for a chance to betray him.

At the Passover meal, Jesus says, one of you will betray me. One after another they ask, 'Lord, is it I?' Judas asks too, and Jesus answers, 'Thou hast said.'

As they are eating, Jesus takes bread, blesses it, breaks it, and gives it to the disciples. 'Take, eat; this is my body.' Then he takes the cup, gives thanks, and gives it to them. 'Drink ye all of it; For this is my blood of the new testament, which is shed for many for the remission of sins.' Remission means forgiveness. Christians have understood this meal in different ways, and all around the world they still remember it together, as Jesus asked.

They sing a hymn and go out to the Mount of Olives. Jesus says, you will all fall away, as it is written. Peter says, I never will. Jesus says, before the rooster crows, you will deny me three times. Even if I must die with you, Peter says, I will never deny you.

Then Jesus comes with them to a garden called Gethsemane. He takes Peter, James and John with him, and begins to be very sorrowful and troubled. 'My soul is exceeding sorrowful, even unto death: tarry ye here, and watch with me.' He goes a little further, falls on his face, and prays. 'O my Father, if it be possible, let this cup pass from me: nevertheless not as I will, but as thou wilt.'

He comes back and finds the disciples asleep. 'What, could ye not watch with me one hour?'

And he tells them what they need.

Twice more he prays, and twice more finds them sleeping. Then he says, the hour has come. Here comes my betrayer.

Judas arrives with a crowd carrying swords and clubs, and greets Jesus with a kiss, the sign he had agreed. Jesus says, 'Friend, wherefore art thou come?' They seize Jesus. One disciple draws a sword, but Jesus says, put it away. I could ask my Father for more than twelve legions of angels. But how then would the Scriptures be fulfilled? Then all the disciples run away.

They take Jesus to Caiaphas. Peter follows at a distance into the courtyard. The council looks for false evidence, but finds nothing that agrees. Jesus stays silent. Then the high priest says, I command you by the living God, tell us whether you are the Christ, the Son of God. Jesus says, 'Thou hast said'. And you will see the Son of man sitting at the right hand of power, and coming on the clouds of heaven. The high priest tears his clothes. Blasphemy! And they say, he deserves death. Then they spit in his face and strike him.

Out in the courtyard, a servant girl says to Peter, you were with Jesus of Galilee. Peter denies it. Another says the same, and he denies it with an oath. Then others say, your accent gives you away. Peter begins to curse and swear. I do not know the man. 'And immediately the cock crew.'

And then Peter remembers.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 13. 'Verily I say unto you, Wheresoever this gospel shall be preached in the whole world, there shall also this, that this woman hath done, be told for a memorial of her.' The disciples called her gift a waste. Jesus called it beautiful, and promised it would be remembered all over the world. And it has been. Right now, on this walk, we are remembering her. Love for Jesus that gives its very best is never wasted, even when others do not understand.

Verse 41. 'Watch and pray, that ye enter not into temptation: the spirit indeed is willing, but the flesh is weak.' The disciples meant well. They really wanted to stay awake with Jesus. But they were tired, and they fell asleep. Jesus knew their hearts were willing but their bodies were weak. That is true of all of us. The answer is to watch and pray, to stay alert and keep talking to God, so that when temptation comes, we have his strength and not only ours.

Verse 75. 'And Peter remembered the word of Jesus, which said unto him, Before the cock crow, thou shalt deny me thrice. And he went out, and wept bitterly.' Peter, who said he would die for Jesus, said three times that he did not even know him. When he remembered, his heart broke. But Peter’s story does not end here. Judas was sorry too, but he did not come back. Peter’s tears led him back to Jesus, who would forgive him and restore him. Failing is not the end, if we turn back to Jesus.

Zechariah wrote, 'smite the shepherd, and the sheep shall be scattered'. And the psalmist wrote, 'The sacrifices of God are a broken spirit: a broken and a contrite heart, O God, thou wilt not despise.'

Is there something you have done that makes you feel like Peter, ashamed and afraid? Jesus knows, and he still loves you. Turn back to him. And like Jesus in the garden, pray today, not my will, but yours be done.

Here is today’s question. Jesus asked, could ye not watch with me one hour? What helps you stay close to Jesus when you feel tired or weak?

Let us pray. Lord Jesus, thank you for going to the cross for me, though your friends fell asleep and ran away. When my spirit is willing but my flesh is weak, help me to watch and pray. And when I fail you, as Peter did, bring me back to you, and forgive me. Not my will, but yours be done. Amen.
