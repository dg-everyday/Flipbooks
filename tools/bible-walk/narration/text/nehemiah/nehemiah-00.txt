The Living Voice. Meet the Book of Nehemiah.

A man stands in a king’s palace, holding a golden cup. It is his job to taste the king’s wine before the king drinks it, to make sure it is safe. He is trusted. He is comfortable. He lives in luxury, far from the troubles of his homeland. And then some visitors arrive with news from home. The walls of Jerusalem are still broken down. The gates are burned. And this comfortable man sits down, and weeps.

Welcome to the book of Nehemiah, on The Living Voice. Nehemiah has thirteen chapters, so on this walk it is thirteen steps, one chapter at a time.

We have just walked through the book of Ezra. The exiles came home, and rebuilt the temple. Then Ezra the scribe came, and taught them God’s law. But Jerusalem itself was still a ruin. Its walls were broken down, and its gates burned. In those days, a city without walls was open to every enemy and every robber. It was weak, and it was a shame to God’s people.

Nehemiah tells how the walls were rebuilt. And most of the book is told by Nehemiah himself, in his own words. He says I, and me, and my God. It is like reading his diary.

Nehemiah was not a priest or a prophet. He was a government official, the cupbearer to the king of Persia. But when he heard about Jerusalem, his heart broke. He prayed, and then he asked the king for permission to go and rebuild the walls. And the king said yes.

What follows is one of the great adventure stories of the Bible. The walls go up in record time, while enemies mock, threaten, plot and lie. The builders work with a tool in one hand and a weapon in the other. Then, when the walls are finished, the people gather, and Ezra reads God’s law aloud to them, from early in the day until midday. They weep, and then they celebrate, because the joy of the LORD is their strength.

Nehemiah is the last of the history books of the Old Testament, in the order things happened. After this, the Old Testament story falls silent for a long time, until an angel appears to an old priest in the temple, at the beginning of the Gospel of Luke.

Here are three things to watch for as we walk.

Watch for prayer. Nehemiah prays long prayers, with fasting and tears. But he also prays tiny, quick prayers, in the middle of conversations, in a single breath. Listen for both.

Watch for perseverance. The enemies try everything: mockery, threats, rumours, false letters, even a false prophet. And again and again, Nehemiah keeps going, and keeps the people building.

And watch for a little phrase that Nehemiah repeats. Remember me, O my God. He knew that God saw everything he did, even when no one else did.

A gentle word before we begin. At the end of the book, Nehemiah deals very sternly with people who had turned back to old sins. The Bible tells honestly what he did, but it does not say that every action of his was right. We will tell it gently.

Here is a question to carry with you. Nehemiah heard about the broken walls of Jerusalem, and it broke his heart. What breaks your heart, and how might God want you to respond?

Let us pray. Lord God, as I walk through the book of Nehemiah, teach me to pray, both long prayers and quick ones. Give me a heart that cares about what you care about. Help me to keep going when things are hard, and to remember that you see everything I do for you. Amen.
