The Living Voice. Nehemiah, chapter 3. Every One over Against His House.

Imagine a huge jigsaw puzzle, laid out in a big circle. Instead of one person trying to do it all, dozens of families each take a small section. A priest here. A goldsmith there. A man and his daughters over there. Each family works on the piece right in front of their own home. And slowly, piece by piece, the whole circle comes together.

In chapter two, the king noticed Nehemiah’s sad face. Nehemiah prayed a quick prayer to the God of heaven, and asked to be sent to rebuild Jerusalem. The king gave him letters and timber, according to the good hand of his God. Nehemiah inspected the broken walls secretly in the dark, and then said to the people, let us build up the wall. And they said, let us rise up and build.

Now, in chapter three, the work begins. It is a long list of names and places. But listen for how many different people take part. And listen for a few small details that tell us a great deal.

The list begins at the sheep gate, in the north east corner of the city, near the temple. And who is first? 'Then Eliashib the high priest rose up with his brethren the priests, and they builded the sheep gate'. The high priest himself, leading the way, with his own hands. And they dedicated it to God.

Then the list goes round the wall, like walking around the city. Next to them, the men of Jericho built. Next to them, Zaccur. Then the sons of Hassenaah built the fish gate, where fish were brought in from the sea of Galilee and the coast. They laid its beams, and set up its doors, its locks and its bars.

Next came Meremoth, and Meshullam, and Zadok. Next to them, the men of Tekoa, a small town to the south.

But there is a sad note about Tekoa.

Then the list goes on. Jehoiada and Meshullam repaired the old gate. Men from Gibeon and Mizpah built next to them. Then Uzziel, one of the goldsmiths. And Hananiah, one of the apothecaries, the perfume makers. Goldsmiths and perfume makers were people who worked with delicate, precious things. Now they were hauling heavy stones.

Rephaiah, the ruler of half of Jerusalem, built next. Then Jedaiah, opposite his own house.

And then, a lovely detail.

The valley gate was repaired by Hanun and the people of Zanoah. They rebuilt a long stretch of wall too, all the way to the gate at the far end, where the rubbish was taken out of the city. And that gate too was rebuilt, by a ruler from a nearby district.

Then came the fountain gate, and the wall of the pool of Siloah, by the king’s garden, and the stairs down from the city of David. Many years later, Jesus would send a blind man to wash in that very pool, the pool of Siloam, and he came back seeing.

Then came another Nehemiah, a ruler of a nearby town, who built as far as the tombs of David. Then the Levites. Then rulers from the town of Keilah.

And then, another lovely detail.

Further along, Meremoth, who had already repaired one section, repaired another. Some people did more than their share.

Then the priests, the men of the plain. Then Benjamin and Hashub, opposite their own house. Then Azariah, beside his own house. 'From above the horse gate repaired the priests, every one over against his house.' Over and over, the same pattern: each person repairing the part of the wall right in front of their own home.

That was wise. People work hard when they are protecting their own families. And no one had to travel far. Each one could see their own piece of wall, and take care of it.

The Tekoites, whose nobles would not help, repaired a second section. The ordinary people of Tekoa did double work, while their leaders did nothing.

The list ends where it began, back at the sheep gate. 'And between the going up of the corner unto the sheep gate repaired the goldsmiths and the merchants.' The whole circle, complete.

Priests and goldsmiths, perfume makers and merchants, rulers and ordinary families, men and daughters. People from Jerusalem, and from towns all around. Everyone had a part. And together, they rebuilt the wall.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 5. 'And next unto them the Tekoites repaired; but their nobles put not their necks to the work of their Lord.' The nobles were the important people, the wealthy ones. To put your neck to the work is like an ox putting its neck under the yoke to pull. The nobles of Tekoa thought they were too important for hard work. And God made sure it was written down. God notices who serves, and who stands back and watches.

Verse 12. 'And next unto him repaired Shallum the son of Halohesh, the ruler of the half part of Jerusalem, he and his daughters.' He and his daughters. In a long list of men, here are some young women, working on the wall alongside their father. The writer wanted us to know they were there. God’s work is not only for men, or only for adults. Everyone can have a part.

Verse 20. 'After him Baruch the son of Zabbai earnestly repaired the other piece, from the turning of the wall unto the door of the house of Eliashib the high priest.' Earnestly means with all his heart, with energy and zeal. Of all the builders, only Baruch is described this way. God saw not only what he did, but how he did it. Whatever work we do for God, we can do it earnestly, with all our heart.

Paul wrote, 'For as the body is one, and hath many members, and all the members of that one body, being many, are one body: so also is Christ.' And Solomon wrote, 'Whatsoever thy hand findeth to do, do it with thy might'.

What is the piece of wall right in front of your own house? Your family, your friends, your neighbours, your work? Start there. And whatever you do for God today, do it earnestly, with all your heart.

Here is today’s question. In Nehemiah’s list, everyone had a part to play. What part do you think God has given you to play among his people?

Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that you have a place for everyone in your work. Keep me from being like the nobles who would not bend their necks. Help me to serve earnestly, like Baruch, and to care for the part of the wall in front of my own home. And thank you that when we all work together, your work is done. Amen.
