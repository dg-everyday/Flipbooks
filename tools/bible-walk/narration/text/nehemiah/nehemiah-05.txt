The Living Voice. Nehemiah, chapter 5. Because of the Fear of God.

A mother stands in a crowd, crying. Her family has had to borrow money to buy food. To pay it back, they have sold their fields. And now, to pay what they still owe, her son and daughter have been taken as servants by the man they borrowed from. And the man who did this is not a foreign enemy. He is one of their own people, a rich neighbour.

In chapter four, Sanballat and Tobiah mocked the builders, and plotted to attack. But the people had a mind to work. Nehemiah set guards, and said, be not afraid, remember the Lord. They built with a tool in one hand and a weapon in the other, and said, our God shall fight for us.

Now, in chapter five, the danger comes from inside the city. Listen for what had gone wrong among God’s people. And listen for how Nehemiah leads by example.

While the wall is going up, a great cry rises from the people, and from their wives, against their own fellow Jews.

Some say, we have large families, and we need grain to eat, to stay alive. Others say, we have mortgaged our fields, our vineyards and our houses, to get grain because of the famine. Others say, we have had to borrow money to pay the king’s tax on our fields and vineyards.

And then the worst of it. We are the same as our brothers. Our children are just like their children. And yet we are having to sell our sons and daughters as servants. Some of our daughters are already servants, and we cannot do anything about it, because other people now own our fields and vineyards.

The rich were lending to the poor, and charging them interest. When the poor could not pay, the rich took their land. And when there was no land left, they took their children as servants. God’s law said that his people should not charge interest to their own poor brothers. But they were doing it anyway.

'And I was very angry when I heard their cry and these words.'

But Nehemiah does not act in a rush of anger. 'Then I consulted with myself'. He thought it through carefully. Then he goes to the nobles and the officials, and confronts them. You are charging interest to your own brothers!

He calls a great assembly. And he says, as far as we could, we have bought back our fellow Jews who were sold to foreigners. And now you are selling your own brothers, so that we have to buy them back again! And they are silent. They have nothing to say.

Then Nehemiah says something important.

And Nehemiah gives his own example. I too, and my brothers and my servants, have been lending them money and grain. Let us stop charging this interest. Give them back their fields, vineyards, olive groves and houses, today. And give back the interest you have charged them.

And the leaders say, we will give it back, and we will not ask for anything more from them. We will do as you say.

Nehemiah calls the priests, and makes the leaders take an oath to keep their promise. Then he shakes out the fold of his robe, and says, may God shake out of his house and his property every man who does not keep this promise. Shaken out, and emptied. And all the people say, Amen, and praise the LORD. And the people do what they promised.

Then Nehemiah tells us something about his own conduct as governor. For all the years he was governor of Judah, he and his family never took the food allowance that governors were entitled to. The governors before him had laid heavy burdens on the people, taking food, wine and silver from them. Even their servants bossed the people around.

And Nehemiah tells us why he was different.

Instead of taking, Nehemiah gave. He worked on the wall himself. He did not buy up any land for himself, when people were selling it cheap. All his servants worked on the wall too. And every day he fed a large number of people at his own table, Jewish leaders, and visitors from the nations around. It cost him a great deal. But he did not demand the governor’s allowance, 'because the bondage was heavy upon this people.'

And then Nehemiah prays.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 9. 'Also I said, It is not good that ye do: ought ye not to walk in the fear of our God because of the reproach of the heathen our enemies?' The enemies outside were watching. What would they think of a people who said they followed God, but treated their own poor like this? How God’s people treat one another, especially the poor, is a witness to the world. Our behaviour can bring honour to God’s name, or shame.

Verse 15. 'But the former governors that had been before me were chargeable unto the people, and had taken of them bread and wine, beside forty shekels of silver; yea, even their servants bare rule over the people: but so did not I, because of the fear of God.' But so did not I. Nehemiah had every right to take what the other governors took. But he chose not to. Why? Because of the fear of God. He knew he would answer to God, not just to the king. Real respect for God changes how we use power and money.

Verse 19. 'Think upon me, my God, for good, according to all that I have done for this people.' This is the first of Nehemiah’s little prayers, remember me. He did not do good to be praised by people. He did it for God, and he asked God to remember. God sees the good we do in secret, and he does not forget.

Micah wrote, 'what doth the LORD require of thee, but to do justly, and to love mercy, and to walk humbly with thy God?' And Jesus said, 'lend, hoping for nothing again; and your reward shall be great'.

Is there someone in need near you, whom you could help, without expecting anything back? And is there anywhere you are using your position or your money in a way that does not honour God? Ask him to show you.

Here is today’s question. Nehemiah gave up what he was entitled to, because of the fear of God. Is there anything you have a right to that you might give up, for the good of others?

Let us pray. Lord God, open my eyes to those who are struggling around me. Keep me from taking advantage of anyone. Help me to walk in the fear of God, so that my life brings honour to your name. Think upon me, my God, for good, and help me to do good, not to be seen, but for you. Amen.

Next on the walk, Nehemiah chapter six. The wall is nearly finished. The enemies try trickery, false letters and a hired prophet. But Nehemiah says, I am doing a great work, so that I cannot come down.
