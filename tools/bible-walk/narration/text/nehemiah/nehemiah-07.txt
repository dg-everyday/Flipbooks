The Living Voice. Nehemiah, chapter 7. He Feared God Above Many.

Imagine a brand new school, with strong walls, a shining gate, and lovely classrooms. Everything is ready. But on the first day, only a handful of children turn up. The corridors echo. Most of the rooms are empty. A beautiful building is not enough. It needs people. That is the problem Nehemiah faces now.

In chapter six, the enemies tried every trick to stop the wall. They invited Nehemiah to the plain of Ono, sent an open letter full of lies, and hired a false prophet to frighten him. But Nehemiah said, I am doing a great work, so that I cannot come down, and prayed, O God, strengthen my hands. The wall was finished in fifty two days, and even the enemies saw that it was the work of God.

Now, in chapter seven, the wall is done, but the work is not over. Listen for the kind of person Nehemiah chooses to lead the city. And listen for his next problem.

The wall is built, and now the doors are hung in the gates. Nehemiah appoints the gatekeepers, the singers and the Levites, to their work.

Then he chooses who will be in charge of Jerusalem. He puts his brother Hanani, who first brought him news of the broken walls, in charge. And with him, Hananiah, the commander of the fortress. And Nehemiah tells us why he chose Hananiah.

Then Nehemiah gives careful instructions. Do not open the gates of Jerusalem until the sun is hot, well after the day has begun. And close and bar the doors while the guards are still on duty. Appoint watchmen from among the people of Jerusalem, each one at his post, and each one guarding the part of the wall near his own house.

The walls were strong. But walls alone could not keep a city safe. They needed faithful people to guard them.

And then Nehemiah notices a big problem.

The city had strong walls, but hardly anyone lived inside them. Most of the houses had not been rebuilt. Most people lived in the towns and villages around. A great city with no one in it is not much of a city.

So what does Nehemiah do?

God gave Nehemiah the idea, to count the people, and to find out who belonged where. Like the wall itself, the people needed to be built up again.

And Nehemiah finds an old book, a record of the families who had first come back from Babylon with Zerubbabel and Jeshua, many years before. And he copies it out, word for word.

It is almost exactly the same list we heard in Ezra chapter two. The children of Parosh. The children of Shephatiah. The men of Bethlehem and Netophah. The men of Anathoth. The priests, the Levites, the singers, the gatekeepers and the temple servants. Those who could not prove their family line. The whole congregation, the same great number, besides their servants and singers. Their horses, mules, camels and donkeys.

Why would the Bible include the same long list twice? Because it mattered. It connected the people of Nehemiah’s time with those who had first come home. They were the same family, the same people of God, continuing the same story. The faith of the first generation was being carried on by the next.

And the list ends with gifts. Some of the leaders gave to the work. The governor gave gold, bowls and garments for the priests. The heads of families gave gold and silver. And the rest of the people gave gold, silver and garments too. Everyone giving, as they could.

Then the priests, the Levites, the gatekeepers, the singers, some of the people, the temple servants, and all Israel, settled in their towns.

And then the chapter ends, pointing forward. When the seventh month came, the people of Israel were in their towns. The seventh month was the time of some of Israel’s great feasts. And in the next chapter, the whole people will gather together, for something wonderful.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 2. 'That I gave my brother Hanani, and Hananiah the ruler of the palace, charge over Jerusalem: for he was a faithful man, and feared God above many.' When Nehemiah chose a leader, he did not look first for someone rich, or clever, or popular. He looked for someone faithful, who feared God more than most people did. Faithfulness and reverence for God are the most important qualities in anyone who leads. And they are qualities any of us can grow in.

Verse 4. 'Now the city was large and great: but the people were few therein, and the houses were not builded.' Strong walls, but few people. It is possible to have a fine building, and still be missing what matters most. God’s city is not made of stones. It is made of people. The walls were done. Now the people needed to be gathered and built up.

Verse 5. 'And my God put into mine heart to gather together the nobles, and the rulers, and the people, that they might be reckoned by genealogy. And I found a register of the genealogy of them which came up at the first, and found written therein'. My God put into mine heart. Nehemiah did not just come up with good ideas. He listened for what God was putting in his heart. And he looked back, to learn from those who had gone before. Good plans often come from listening to God, and remembering what he has already done.

Jesus said, 'He that is faithful in that which is least is faithful also in much'. And Paul wrote, 'Moreover it is required in stewards, that a man be found faithful.'

Ask God to make you someone who is faithful, and who fears him above many. And think of someone in your life, an older believer, whose faith you are carrying on. Thank God for them.

Here is today’s question. Nehemiah chose Hananiah because he was faithful, and feared God above many. What do you think it means to fear God above many, and how could you grow in that?

Let us pray. Lord God, make me a faithful person, who fears you above many. Thank you for those who walked with you before me, and passed on their faith. Help me to carry it on. Put into my heart what you want me to do, and help me to listen. And thank you that your city is built with people, not stones. Amen.
