The Living Voice. Nehemiah, chapter 10. We Will Not Forsake the House of Our God.

A long scroll is laid out on a table. One by one, the leaders of the people step forward, and press their seals into the wax at the bottom, a sign that they promise to keep what is written. The governor goes first. Then the priests. Then the Levites. Then the heads of the families. Name after name. And behind them stand all the people, ready to make the same promise.

In chapter nine, the people gathered with fasting and confession, and the Levites prayed a great prayer, telling the whole story of God’s faithfulness, from creation to that very day. Again and again the people had failed, but God was a God ready to pardon, gracious and merciful. And because of all this, they made a sure covenant, and wrote it down.

Now, in chapter ten, we hear what they promised. Listen for how practical their promises are. And listen for the last line of the chapter.

The chapter begins with the names of those who sealed the covenant. First, Nehemiah the governor. Then the priests, a long list of family names. Then the Levites. Then the leaders of the people. Many of the names we have heard before, in the lists of those who came home, and those who built the wall.

And then everyone else joins in.

They promise to walk in God’s law, given through Moses, and to obey all the commands of the LORD. And then they make some very specific promises.

First, they will not marry their sons and daughters to the peoples around them who worship other gods. They had learned that lesson painfully, in the days of Ezra.

Second, they will keep the sabbath. If the people of the land bring goods or grain to sell on the sabbath day, they will not buy from them. In God’s law, the sabbath was a day of rest, set apart for the LORD. They had been tempted to treat it like any other day of business.

Third, every seventh year, they will let the land rest, and cancel the debts of their fellow Jews. God’s law commanded that, so that the poor would not stay in debt for ever. Do you remember how the rich had been taking advantage of the poor, back in chapter five? This promise would protect them.

Then come promises about the house of God. Every year, each person will give a third of a shekel, a small amount of silver, to pay for the service of the temple: the bread of the presence, the regular offerings, the feasts, and everything else needed.

They also cast lots, to decide which families will bring wood for the altar at set times each year. Someone had to bring the firewood, to keep the fire burning on the altar, as the law commanded. It was not glamorous work. But they shared it out, so everyone had a turn.

And then they promise to bring the first of everything to God.

The firstborn of their sons and their animals, as written in the law. The first of their dough, and of every kind of fruit, wine and oil, they will bring to the priests. And a tenth of their harvest to the Levites, so that the Levites could live, and serve in the house of God. The Levites in turn would bring a tenth of what they received to the house of God.

All of this was very practical. It was about money, food, wood, harvests and time. Their faith was not just about feelings. It touched every part of their lives.

And then the chapter ends with one simple, powerful sentence.

In the days of Haggai, the people had let God’s house lie in ruins while they built their own houses. Now, they promised: never again.

But as we will see at the end of the book, keeping promises is harder than making them.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 29. 'They clave to their brethren, their nobles, and entered into a curse, and into an oath, to walk in God’s law, which was given by Moses the servant of God, and to observe and do all the commandments of the LORD our Lord, and his judgments and his statutes'. Clave means held on closely. The people did not just make private promises. They held on to each other, and promised together. Following God is easier, and stronger, when we do it together, encouraging one another.

Verse 35. 'And to bring the firstfruits of our ground, and the firstfruits of all fruit of all trees, year by year, unto the house of the LORD'. Firstfruits are the first part of the harvest, the very first fruit picked from the tree. They gave God the first, not the leftovers. Giving God the first of our time, our money and our energy shows that he comes first in our lives.

Verse 39. 'For the children of Israel and the children of Levi shall bring the offering of the corn, of the new wine, and the oil, unto the chambers, where are the vessels of the sanctuary, and the priests that minister, and the porters, and the singers: and we will not forsake the house of our God.' We will not forsake the house of our God. Forsake means abandon, neglect, leave behind. They promised to care for the place of worship, and for those who served in it. God wants his people to be faithful, not just on special days, but year after year.

Paul wrote, 'present your bodies a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable unto God, which is your reasonable service.' And Solomon wrote, 'Honour the LORD with thy substance, and with the firstfruits of all thine increase'.

Is there a practical way you could put God first this week? Your time, your money, your energy? Give him the first, not the leftovers. And think of someone who could join you in following God, so you can encourage each other.

Here is today’s question. The people’s promises were very practical: money, wood, harvests and time. What practical promise could you make to God, to show that he comes first?

Let us pray. Lord God, I want to put you first in every part of my life, not just in words, but in practical ways. Help me to give you the firstfruits of my time, my money and my energy. Help me to hold on to other believers, so we can follow you together. And help me not to forsake your house, or your people. Amen.
