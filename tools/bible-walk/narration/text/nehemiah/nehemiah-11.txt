The Living Voice. Nehemiah, chapter 11. Willingly Offered Themselves.

Imagine being asked to leave your farm, your fields, your village, and everything you know, to move into a city that is half empty, and still being rebuilt. A city that enemies would love to attack. Most people would say, no thank you, I am fine where I am. But some people stepped forward and said, I will go.

In chapter ten, the leaders and all the people sealed a covenant. They promised to walk in God’s law, to keep the sabbath, to release debts in the seventh year, to give their firstfruits and tithes, and to bring wood for the altar. And they said, we will not forsake the house of our God.

Now, in chapter eleven, Nehemiah deals with the problem we heard about in chapter seven: the city was large, but the people were few. Listen for how they solve it. And listen for those who willingly offered themselves.

The chapter begins.

The leaders were already living in Jerusalem. But the city needed more people. So the rest of the people cast lots, a bit like drawing straws, so that one family out of every ten would move into Jerusalem, and the other nine would stay in their towns.

And then the writer adds something lovely.

Not everyone waited for the lots. Some volunteered. And the people blessed them, and thanked God for them.

Why was it a sacrifice to live in Jerusalem? Because most people made their living from farming, and their farms were in the countryside. Moving to the city meant leaving their land. The city was still being rebuilt, and many houses were in ruins. And Jerusalem was the place enemies would attack first. Living there took courage, and it cost something.

Then the writer lists who lived in Jerusalem. Leaders from the tribe of Judah, from the family of Perez. Leaders from the tribe of Benjamin. Hundreds of valiant men, strong and brave, ready to defend the city.

Then the priests. One of them is described as the ruler of the house of God. And their brothers who did the work of the house, hundreds of them. Mighty men of valour among them.

Then the Levites, with their leaders who were in charge of the outside work of the house of God.

And then one man is mentioned for a special reason.

Then the gatekeepers, who kept watch at the gates. And the temple servants, who lived on the hill called Ophel, near the temple.

The singers were in charge of the music in the house of God. And the writer tells us that the king of Persia himself had ordered that the singers should be given a daily share of food, so they could give their full time to worship.

The rest of the people, priests and Levites, lived in the towns of Judah, each on his own land. And the chapter ends with a list of the towns and villages where they lived. Places we know from earlier books. Kirjath-arba, which is Hebron. Beer-sheba, where Abraham dug wells. Ziklag, where David once lived. Lachish. Adullam, where David hid in a cave. Bethel. Anathoth. Ramah. The people of Judah lived from Beer-sheba in the south, to the valley of Hinnom just outside Jerusalem.

It is another list of names and places. But it shows that God’s people were settling back into the land, filling it once more, in the very places where their ancestors had lived. The promises to Abraham, Moses and David were still alive. God had not forgotten a single town, or a single family.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 1. 'And the rulers of the people dwelt at Jerusalem: the rest of the people also cast lots, to bring one of ten to dwell in Jerusalem the holy city, and nine parts to dwell in other cities.' The holy city. Jerusalem was special, because God had chosen it for his name. It needed people to live in it, to guard it, and to keep its worship going. Every community of God’s people needs some who are willing to live at the centre, where the needs are greatest.

Verse 2. 'And the people blessed all the men, that willingly offered themselves to dwell at Jerusalem.' Willingly offered themselves. They were not forced. They volunteered. And the people blessed them. God loves those who offer themselves willingly. Many years later, Isaiah heard God ask, whom shall I send? And he answered, here am I, send me. That is the spirit of these volunteers.

Verse 17. 'And Mattaniah the son of Micha, the son of Zabdi, the son of Asaph, was the principal to begin the thanksgiving in prayer: and Bakbukiah the second among his brethren, and Abda the son of Shammua, the son of Galal, the son of Jeduthun.' Of all the jobs in the city, one man’s job was to lead the thanksgiving in prayer. Mattaniah came from the family of Asaph, who wrote many of the Psalms. Thanking God was so important that someone was given the job of leading it every day. Thanksgiving should be at the centre of our lives too.

Isaiah said, 'Here am I; send me.' And the psalmist wrote, 'Pray for the peace of Jerusalem'.

Is there somewhere God might be asking you to offer yourself willingly, a task no one else wants, a place where the need is great? Say, here am I. And today, try leading someone in thanksgiving, even just at a meal.

Here is today’s question. Some people willingly offered themselves to live in Jerusalem, even though it cost them. What might it look like for you to willingly offer yourself to God?

Let us pray. Lord God, thank you for those who willingly offer themselves for your work. Here am I. Make me willing to go where you want me to go, and to do what you want me to do. Fill my heart with thanksgiving, and help me to lead others in thanking you. Amen.
