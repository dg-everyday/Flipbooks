The Living Voice. Nehemiah, chapter 13. Remember Me, O My God, for Good.

A man comes home after a long time away. He walks into the house of God, expecting to find everything as he left it. But he stops in the doorway of one of the storerooms. Where the offerings for God should be kept, someone has moved in all their furniture. And not just anyone. It is one of the enemies who fought against the building of the walls. The man’s face goes red. He picks up the first piece of furniture, and throws it out into the street.

In chapter twelve, two great choirs marched around the top of the walls, singing, and met at the house of God. They offered great sacrifices, and rejoiced, for God had made them rejoice with great joy. The wives and children rejoiced too, and the joy of Jerusalem was heard even afar off.

Now we come to the last chapter of Nehemiah, and the last chapter of the history books of the Old Testament. Listen for the promises that had been broken. And listen for Nehemiah’s little prayers.

The chapter begins with the reading of God’s law. And the people find written there that the Ammonites and the Moabites should not be part of the assembly of God, because long ago they had refused to help Israel in the wilderness, and had hired Balaam to curse them. 'howbeit our God turned the curse into a blessing.' Do you remember Balaam? He tried to curse Israel, and every time, God made him bless them instead.

Then Nehemiah tells us what had happened while he was away. After his first years as governor, he had gone back to the king of Persia. And while he was gone, things went wrong.

Eliashib the priest, who was in charge of the storerooms in the house of God, was related to Tobiah, the very man who had mocked the wall and tried to frighten Nehemiah. And Eliashib had given Tobiah a large room in the courts of the house of God. The room where the offerings, the incense and the tithes for the Levites and singers were supposed to be kept.

When Nehemiah came back and found out, he was very upset. He threw all of Tobiah’s furniture out of the room. Then he ordered the rooms to be purified, and brought back the vessels of the house of God, with the offerings and the incense.

Then he discovered something else. The Levites had not been given their portions. The people had stopped bringing their tithes. So the Levites and singers had gone back to their own fields, to farm, just to survive. The house of God was empty.

And then Nehemiah prays.

Then Nehemiah sees people in Judah working on the sabbath, treading grapes and bringing in goods to sell, and merchants from Tyre selling fish.

Nehemiah confronts the leaders. What evil thing is this you are doing, breaking the sabbath? Is this not exactly what your fathers did, and why God brought all this trouble on us and on this city? So he orders the gates of Jerusalem to be shut for the sabbath, and sets guards at them. And again he prays, remember me, O my God, and spare me according to the greatness of your mercy.

And then the old sin again. Nehemiah sees Jews who had married women from Ashdod, Ammon and Moab, peoples who worshipped other gods. And half of their children could not even speak the language of Judah. They could not understand God’s law when it was read.

Nehemiah reacts with fierce anger. He rebukes them, and even strikes some of them, and pulls out their hair. The Bible tells us honestly what he did. It does not say that every action of his was right. His anger was real, but so was the danger. He makes them promise before God not to give their children in marriage to those who worship other gods.

And he reminds them. Did not Solomon, king of Israel, sin because of marriages like these? There was no king like him. God loved him, and made him king over all Israel. And yet even Solomon was led into sin by his foreign wives.

Even a grandson of the high priest had married the daughter of Sanballat, Nehemiah’s old enemy. So Nehemiah drove him away.

And the book ends with one last little prayer.

The people had made a covenant in chapter ten. And by chapter thirteen, they had broken almost every part of it. The house of God, the tithes, the sabbath, the marriages. Nehemiah, Ezra and all the reforms could not change the people’s hearts for good.

That is where the history of the Old Testament ends. Not with a perfect people, but with a people who kept failing, and a God who kept being faithful. And it leaves us waiting for something more. A new covenant, written on hearts, not on scrolls. A Saviour who would not only rebuild walls, but change hearts. A long silence follows. And then, in the temple, an angel appears to an old priest named Zacharias, and says, fear not.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 11. 'Then contended I with the rulers, and said, Why is the house of God forsaken? And I gathered them together, and set them in their place.' Forsaken. In chapter ten, the people had promised, we will not forsake the house of our God. Now it was forsaken. It is easy to make promises to God when we are full of joy. It is much harder to keep them day after day. Faithfulness needs to be renewed again and again.

Verse 14. 'Remember me, O my God, concerning this, and wipe not out my good deeds that I have done for the house of my God, and for the offices thereof.' Nehemiah was not boasting. He knew his work might be undone again by others. But he trusted that God would remember. Even when our good work seems to be forgotten or undone by others, God remembers. Nothing we do for him is ever wasted.

Verse 31. 'And for the wood offering, at times appointed, and for the firstfruits. Remember me, O my God, for good.' The very last words of the book. Remember me, O my God, for good. After all his work, all his battles, all his disappointments, Nehemiah ended by placing himself in God’s hands. And that is the best place for any of us to be.

Paul wrote, 'I have fought a good fight, I have finished my course, I have kept the faith'. And the psalmist prayed, 'Remember me, O LORD, with the favour that thou bearest unto thy people'.

Is there a promise you made to God that has slipped? Do not give up. Renew it today. And if you feel that the good you have done has been forgotten, bring it to God. He remembers.

Here is today’s question. The people broke their covenant within a few years. Why do you think it is so hard to keep our promises to God, and what can help us?

Let us pray. Lord God, thank you for walking with me through the book of Nehemiah. Thank you for his prayers, his courage and his faithfulness. I confess that I too find it hard to keep my promises to you. Thank you that you are faithful, even when I am not. Change my heart from the inside. And remember me, O my God, for good. Amen.
