The Living Voice. Meet the Book of Numbers.

A whole nation packs up its tents. Hundreds of thousands of people, with their children and their animals, line up tribe by tribe around a golden tent. Over the tent hangs a cloud. And when the cloud lifts and begins to move, they move with it, out into the wilderness. They are on their way to the land God promised them. It should take a few weeks. It will take a lifetime.

Welcome to the book of Numbers, on The Living Voice. Numbers has thirty six chapters, so on this walk it is thirty six steps, one chapter at a time.

At the end of Exodus, the glory of the LORD filled the tabernacle, and the cloud came down over it. The book of Numbers picks up the story right there, at Mount Sinai. God’s people have the law, and the tabernacle, and God’s presence in the middle of their camp. Now it is time to set out for the promised land.

Why is it called Numbers? Because twice in the book, Moses counts the people. Once at the beginning, at Sinai, and once near the end, on the edge of the promised land. Between those two counts, a whole generation passes away in the wilderness, and a new one grows up. In Hebrew, the book has a different name, taken from its first verse: in the wilderness. That is a good name too, because most of the book happens there.

It is a book of journeys, and of hard lessons. You will hear about the people complaining about food and water, again and again. You will hear about twelve spies sent into the promised land, and how ten of them came back afraid. You will hear about the years of wandering that followed. And you will hear some of the most surprising stories in the Bible: water from a rock, a bronze serpent on a pole, and a prophet whose donkey speaks to him.

Here are three things to watch for as we walk.

Watch for the cloud. When it moved, the people moved. When it stayed, they stayed. It is a picture of what it means to follow God, step by step, trusting him to lead.

Watch for grumbling. Again and again, the people complain against God and against Moses. And again and again, it costs them dearly. The New Testament says these things were written as examples for us, so that we would learn to trust God instead of complaining.

And watch for grace. Even when the people fail, God does not give up on them. He feeds them, gives them water, forgives them, and keeps his promise. The book ends with a new generation, standing on the edge of the land God promised.

Numbers also contains one of the most beautiful blessings in the Bible, which God gave Aaron to speak over the people. The LORD bless thee, and keep thee. We will hear it in chapter six.

And Jesus himself pointed back to this book. He said that just as Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, so he himself would be lifted up, so that whoever believes in him would have eternal life.

There are some hard chapters in Numbers, with judgment and war. We will tell them honestly and gently. And there are some long lists, of tribes and families and journeys. We will not read every name, but we will listen for what the lists are telling us: that God knew every one of his people, and led them every step of the way.

Here is a question to carry with you. The people of Israel followed the cloud wherever it led. What would it look like for you to follow God step by step, even when you cannot see far ahead?

Let us pray. Lord God, as I walk through the book of Numbers, teach me to follow you, wherever you lead. Keep me from grumbling, and help me to trust you in the wilderness places of my life. Thank you for your grace, which never gives up on your people. The LORD bless me, and keep me. Amen.
