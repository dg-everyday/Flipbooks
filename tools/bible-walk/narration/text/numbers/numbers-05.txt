The Living Voice. Numbers, chapter 5. Then They Shall Confess Their Sin.

You have wronged someone. Perhaps you took something that was not yours, or cheated them, and they never found out. Saying sorry to God feels easier than facing the person you hurt. But God says something surprising here. It is not enough to be sorry. You must go to the person, confess, give back what you took, and then add a little more.

In chapter four, God told the Levites exactly how to carry the tabernacle. The priests covered the holy things first, so the Kohathites could carry them safely. The Gershonites carried the curtains, and the Merarites carried the boards and pillars. Every one according to his service, and according to his burden.

Now, in chapter five, God speaks about keeping his camp clean and right. Listen for the reason he gives. And listen for what God asks of someone who has wronged another person.

The LORD speaks to Moses, and tells him to keep the camp clean.

People with certain diseases, or who had become ceremonially unclean by touching the dead, were to stay outside the camp for a time. The law of Moses explained how they could later be made clean, and come back in. These rules protected the health of a huge crowd living close together in the desert. But God gives a deeper reason too.

God was not far away. He lived right in the middle of their camp. And so the camp was to be a holy place, fit for a holy God.

And the children of Israel did so, as the LORD commanded.

Then God speaks about a different kind of uncleanness. Not of the body, but of the heart. The uncleanness of wrong done to others.

Notice that when we wrong another person, God says we have done a trespass against the LORD. Every wrong against someone made in God’s image is also a wrong against God himself.

And then God tells them what to do.

Confess, repay, and add a fifth. If you had cheated someone of a hundred coins, you would pay back the hundred, plus twenty more.

And if the person you wronged had died, and had no close relative to receive the payment, then it was to be given to the LORD, through the priest, along with an offering for atonement. The wrong still had to be put right, even if the person was gone.

God also says that the holy gifts people bring to the priest belong to the priest. The priests and Levites had no land of their own. They lived on what the people brought to God.

Then the chapter turns to a very different and difficult law. It is called the law of jealousies. It dealt with a husband who suspected his wife of being unfaithful to him, but had no proof.

In the ancient world, a jealous husband might take matters into his own hands, with terrible results for the woman. This law did something different. The husband was not allowed to punish his wife himself. Instead, the matter was brought before the LORD, at the tabernacle, through the priest, and left for God to judge. If she was innocent, God himself would clear her, and she would be free.

This law is hard for us to understand today, and Christians have understood it in different ways. Many have seen it as a protection for the innocent wife, taking the matter out of an angry husband’s hands, and putting it into God’s. What is clear is that God cared about faithfulness in marriage, and that he was the one who judged, not the jealous husband.

The chapter as a whole is about one thing: a camp where God lives must be a place of truth, faithfulness and right relationships. Hidden sin and secret wrongs could not stay hidden before a holy God.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 3. 'Both male and female shall ye put out, without the camp shall ye put them;' so that their camps stay clean, 'in the midst whereof I dwell.' In the midst whereof I dwell. That is the reason for everything in this chapter. God lived in the middle of his people. And so his people were to be holy. The New Testament says that God now lives in his people by his Spirit, so we too are called to be holy, because he is holy.

Verse 6. 'Speak unto the children of Israel, When a man or woman shall commit any sin that men commit, to do a trespass against the LORD, and that person be guilty;' Any sin that men commit. God is realistic. He knows that his people will sin. And notice, a man or woman. The same law applies to everyone. When we wrong others, we sin against the LORD. That is why making things right with others matters so much to God.

Verse 7. 'Then they shall confess their sin which they have done: and he shall recompense his trespass with the principal thereof, and add unto it the fifth part thereof, and give it unto him against whom he hath trespassed.' Confess, and recompense, which means repay. The principal is the original amount, and the fifth part is the extra. Real repentance is not just words. It puts things right, and goes a little further. Long after, when Zacchaeus met Jesus, he said, if I have taken any thing from any man by false accusation, I restore him fourfold.

Zacchaeus said to Jesus, 'if I have taken any thing from any man by false accusation, I restore him fourfold.' And Jesus said, 'first be reconciled to thy brother, and then come and offer thy gift.'

Is there someone you have wronged, and never made it right? Ask God today for the courage to go to them, confess, and put it right, and perhaps go a little further than you need to.

Here is today’s question. God asked his people not only to confess, but to repay and add a fifth. Why do you think putting things right with people matters so much to God?

Let us pray. Lord God, you dwell among your people, and you are holy. Show me any wrong I have done to others that I have not put right. Give me courage to confess, to repay, and to go further than I must. Thank you that you are a God of truth, who protects the innocent. Amen.
