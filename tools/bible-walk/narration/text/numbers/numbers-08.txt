The Living Voice. Numbers, chapter 8. The Seven Lamps Shall Give Light.

Inside the tabernacle, it is dark. There are no windows. The heavy curtains shut out the sun. But in the holy place, a priest lifts a flame, and lights a lamp, then another, and another, until seven flames are burning on a golden lampstand shaped like a tree in blossom. And the whole room fills with warm, golden light.

In chapter seven, the leaders of the twelve tribes brought their gifts, each prince on his day, the same gift written out in full twelve times. The Kohathites received no wagons, because they carried the holy things on their shoulders. And when Moses went into the tabernacle, he heard the voice of God from off the mercy seat.

Now, in chapter eight, the lamps are lit, and the Levites are set apart for their service. Listen for how the Levites are given to God. And listen for what happens to the Levites when they grow older.

The LORD tells Moses to speak to Aaron.

So Aaron lights the lamps, so that they shine in front of the lampstand, as the LORD commanded Moses.

The writer reminds us how the lampstand was made. From its base to its flowers, it was beaten out of a single piece of gold, made exactly according to the pattern God had shown Moses on the mountain.

In the dark holy place, the lampstand was the only light. And Aaron kept its lamps burning before the LORD. It was a picture of God’s light, shining among his people.

Then the LORD speaks to Moses about the Levites. Take the Levites from among the children of Israel, and cleanse them.

The Levites are sprinkled with the water of purifying. They shave their whole bodies, and wash their clothes, and so make themselves clean. Then they bring a young bull and an offering of flour mixed with oil, and another young bull for a sin offering.

Moses brings the Levites to the front of the tabernacle, and gathers the whole assembly of Israel.

Imagine that moment. The whole people of Israel coming forward, and laying their hands on the Levites. When someone laid their hands on an offering, they were saying, this stands for me. Now the whole nation lays its hands on the Levites, and gives them to God, in the place of all their firstborn sons.

Aaron offers the Levites before the LORD, as a wave offering from the children of Israel, so that they can do the LORD’s service. Then the Levites lay their hands on the heads of the bulls, and the bulls are offered, to make atonement for the Levites.

And God says why.

Then God explains again. The Levites are wholly given to me, instead of all the firstborn of Israel. For all the firstborn are mine, since the day I struck the firstborn in Egypt.

And then God says something lovely.

So Moses and Aaron and all the people do to the Levites exactly as the LORD commanded. The Levites are purified, and wash their clothes, and Aaron offers them before the LORD. And after that, the Levites go in to do their service at the tabernacle, as the LORD had commanded.

The chapter ends with a gentle rule about age. The Levites are to begin their work at the tabernacle at twenty five years old. And at fifty, they are to stop the heavy work of carrying and serving. But they do not simply retire and disappear. They are to help their brothers, keeping watch and assisting, but not doing the heavy lifting.

God cared about his servants’ bodies, and their strength. The heavy work was for the young. But the older Levites still had a place, helping and guiding those who came after them. There is a season for every kind of service.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 2. 'Speak unto Aaron, and say unto him, When thou lightest the lamps, the seven lamps shall give light over against the candlestick.' The seven lamps were to give light. That was their whole purpose. Jesus would one day say, I am the light of the world. And he would also say to his followers, ye are the light of the world. Let your light so shine before men. God’s people are meant to shine.

Verse 10. 'And thou shalt bring the Levites before the LORD: and the children of Israel shall put their hands upon the Levites:' The whole people laid their hands on the Levites. The Levites served on behalf of everyone. Their work was not separate from the people. It was the people’s offering to God. Those who serve God’s people are a gift from God’s people, and should be supported and honoured by them.

Verse 14. 'Thus shalt thou separate the Levites from among the children of Israel: and the Levites shall be mine.' The Levites shall be mine. To belong to God is the greatest privilege. The Levites gave up land and fields and ordinary work, to serve at God’s house. And God said, they are mine. The New Testament tells all believers, ye are not your own, for ye are bought with a price.

Jesus said, 'Ye are the light of the world. A city that is set on an hill cannot be hid.' And he said, 'Let your light so shine before men, that they may see your good works, and glorify your Father which is in heaven.'

How can you let your light shine today? A kind word, an act of help, an honest answer. And if you are older, think of someone younger you could encourage and guide, as the older Levites helped their brothers.

Here is today’s question. The older Levites stopped the heavy work, but still helped their brothers. How can people of every age serve God, even when their strength changes?

Let us pray. Lord God, thank you for your light, shining in the darkness. Let my life shine for you. Thank you that I belong to you, and that you have a place for me in your service at every stage of life. Help me to serve you faithfully, and to support those who serve your people. Amen.
