The Living Voice. Numbers, chapter 14. My Servant Caleb Hath Followed Me Fully.

A whole nation is weeping in the dark, in their tents, as if someone had died. They have heard about the giants, and the walls. And by the time the sun comes up, their tears have turned to anger. They want a new leader. They want to go back to Egypt. And when two brave men stand up and say, no, trust God, the crowd picks up stones.

In chapter thirteen, twelve spies explored the promised land, and came back carrying a huge cluster of grapes. The land floweth with milk and honey, they said. Nevertheless, the people are strong, and we were as grasshoppers. But Caleb stilled the people and said, let us go up at once, for we are well able to overcome it.

Now, in chapter fourteen, the people must decide. Listen for what Joshua and Caleb say. And listen for how Moses prays for the very people who rebelled.

The whole congregation raises its voice and cries, and the people weep through the dark hours.

All the children of Israel grumble against Moses and Aaron. If only we had died in Egypt, they say, or in this wilderness! Why is the LORD bringing us to this land, only for us to die in battle, and for our wives and children to be taken? Would it not be better to go back to Egypt?

And they say to each other, let us choose a leader, and go back to Egypt.

Back to Egypt. Back to slavery. After everything God had done, the plagues, the sea, the manna, the water, the law, the cloud. They were standing at the very edge of the promise, and they wanted to turn around.

Moses and Aaron fall on their faces before the whole assembly.

And Joshua and Caleb, two of the spies, tear their clothes in grief, and speak to the people. The land we explored is an exceedingly good land.

And they plead with them. Only do not rebel against the LORD. Do not fear the people of the land. They are like bread for us. Their protection has gone, and the LORD is with us. Do not be afraid of them.

But the whole congregation talks of throwing stones at them.

Then the glory of the LORD appears at the tabernacle, before all the people.

And the LORD says to Moses, how long will these people despise me? How long will they refuse to believe in me, after all the signs I have done among them? I will strike them, and make of you a greater nation than they.

And Moses prays. Not for himself, but for the people. If you do this, Moses says, the Egyptians will hear of it. They know you brought this people out with your power. And the nations will say that the LORD was not able to bring his people into the land he promised.

And then Moses reminds God of his own words, the words God spoke on Mount Sinai. The LORD is longsuffering, and of great mercy, forgiving iniquity and transgression. Pardon the iniquity of this people, according to the greatness of your mercy, as you have forgiven them from Egypt until now.

Moses could have had a nation of his own. Instead, he begged God to forgive the people who wanted to replace him.

And God answers.

But there will be consequences. As truly as I live, God says, all the earth shall be filled with the glory of the LORD. But none of the men who saw my glory and my miracles in Egypt and the wilderness, and still tested me again and again, will see the land I promised.

Except one.

God says the people will turn back into the wilderness. Every man counted from twenty years old and upward, who grumbled against God, will not enter the land, except Caleb and Joshua. But your little ones, God says, the children you said would be taken as prey, I will bring them in, and they will know the land you rejected.

God says they will wander in the wilderness, a year for every day the spies explored the land, until that generation has passed away.

The ten men who spread the bad report die before the LORD. But Joshua and Caleb live.

When Moses tells the people, they mourn bitterly. And early the next day they say, we have sinned. Now we will go up to the place the LORD promised.

But Moses says, why are you disobeying the LORD again? It will not succeed. Do not go up, for the LORD is not with you.

But they go anyway, without the ark, and without Moses. And the Amalekites and Canaanites come down, and defeat them.

First, God said go, and they would not. Then God said do not go, and they went. Obedience means following God’s timing, not our own.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 8. 'If the LORD delight in us, then he will bring us into this land, and give it us; a land which floweth with milk and honey.' If the LORD delight in us. Joshua and Caleb did not trust in their own strength. They trusted in God’s delight, his love for his people. If God is for us, he will bring us in. The question was never about the giants. It was about God.

Verse 20. 'And the LORD said, I have pardoned according to thy word:' I have pardoned. Because Moses prayed, God forgave. The people still faced the consequences of their unbelief. But God did not destroy them, and he did not abandon them. Forgiveness and consequences can go together. God forgives, and still lets us learn from what we have done.

Verse 24. 'But my servant Caleb, because he had another spirit with him, and hath followed me fully, him will I bring into the land whereinto he went; and his seed shall possess it.' Another spirit. A different attitude, a different heart. Caleb followed God fully, not halfway. And God promised him the very land he had walked through. Long after, in the book of Joshua, we heard old Caleb say, give me this mountain. God kept his promise.

The letter to the Hebrews says, 'So we see that they could not enter in because of unbelief.' And it says, 'To day if ye will hear his voice, harden not your hearts.'

Is there something God has asked you to do, and you have been holding back out of fear? Do not wait until the moment has passed. Follow him fully today. And pray for someone who is turning away from God, as Moses prayed for the people.

Here is today’s question. Caleb had another spirit, and followed God fully. What do you think it means to follow God fully, and not just halfway?

Let us pray. Lord God, you are longsuffering, and of great mercy, forgiving iniquity and transgression. Thank you for your patience with me. Give me another spirit, like Caleb’s, to follow you fully. Keep me from unbelief, and help me to obey you in your time, not mine. Amen.
