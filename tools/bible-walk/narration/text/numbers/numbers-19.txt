The Living Voice. Numbers, chapter 19. A Water of Separation, a Purification for Sin.

In a camp of hundreds of thousands of people, someone dies almost every day. And every time, the people who touch the body, or even enter the tent, become unclean. Cut off from worship. How can they ever be clean again? God gives them an answer that seems very strange: the ashes of a red cow.

In chapter eighteen, God told Aaron that the priests and Levites would guard the holy things, so the people could live near a holy God. The priesthood was a gift, and the Levites were a gift. And God said to Aaron, thou shalt have no inheritance in their land: I am thy part and thine inheritance.

Now, in chapter nineteen, God gives a law about cleansing. It is one of the most unusual laws in the Bible. Listen for what makes the heifer special. And listen for how the New Testament uses this picture.

The LORD speaks to Moses and Aaron.

A heifer is a young cow. This one must be red, without any spot or flaw, and must never have worked, never had a yoke on its neck.

The heifer is given to Eleazar the priest. And it is taken outside the camp, and offered there, in front of him.

Outside the camp. Most offerings were made at the altar, in the middle of the camp. But this one happened outside, away from the camp, the place of the unclean.

Eleazar takes some of its blood with his finger, and sprinkles it seven times toward the front of the tabernacle. Seven was the number of completeness in the Bible.

Then the heifer is burned completely, while Eleazar watches.

Cedar wood, hyssop and scarlet. These same three things were used to cleanse someone healed from a skin disease, in the book of Leviticus. Cedar, a wood that does not rot. Hyssop, a small plant used for sprinkling. And scarlet, the colour of blood. All of them pictures of cleansing and life.

Then the priest washes his clothes and bathes, and is unclean for the rest of that day. The person who burned the heifer does the same. Even those who handled the cleansing were made unclean by it, for a little while.

Then a clean man gathers up the ashes, and puts them in a clean place outside the camp.

And this law was not only for Israel. God says it is for the foreigner living among them too, a statute for ever.

Then God explains when the ashes are used. Anyone who touches a dead body will be unclean for seven days. On the third day and on the seventh day, they must be purified with the water of separation. Then they will be clean. But if they refuse to be cleansed, they make the tabernacle of the LORD unclean, and will be cut off from Israel.

When someone dies in a tent, everyone who comes into the tent, and everyone already in it, will be unclean for seven days. Even the open pots and jars without lids.

For an unclean person, some of the ashes of the heifer are put in a vessel, and fresh running water is poured over them. Then a clean person dips hyssop in the water, and sprinkles it on the tent, and the things in it, and the people who were there. On the third day and the seventh day, they are sprinkled. Then they wash their clothes, and bathe, and are clean by the end of the day.

Why so much concern with death? Because death was the great enemy, the result of sin coming into the world. God is the living God, the God of life. And death could not be brought carelessly into his presence. The ashes of the heifer, mixed with living water, were God’s way of washing away the uncleanness of death, so that his people could come back to him.

The New Testament writer of the letter to the Hebrews looked back at this law, and saw something greater. If the ashes of a heifer could make people outwardly clean, how much more can the blood of Christ, who offered himself without spot to God, make our consciences clean from dead works, to serve the living God. Jesus, like the heifer, was without blemish. And like the heifer, he suffered outside the gate of the city.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 2. 'This is the ordinance of the law which the LORD hath commanded, saying, Speak unto the children of Israel, that they bring thee a red heifer without spot, wherein is no blemish, and upon which never came yoke:' Without spot, without blemish. Only the perfect could cleanse the unclean. Peter wrote that we were redeemed with the precious blood of Christ, as of a lamb without blemish and without spot. Only the one who was perfect could make us clean.

Verse 6. 'And the priest shall take cedar wood, and hyssop, and scarlet, and cast it into the midst of the burning of the heifer.' Hyssop appears again and again in the Bible. It was used to put the blood on the doorposts at the first Passover. And David prayed, purge me with hyssop, and I shall be clean. And at the cross, when Jesus was thirsty, they lifted a sponge to his lips on hyssop. Cleansing, from beginning to end.

Verse 9. 'And a man that is clean shall gather up the ashes of the heifer, and lay them up without the camp in a clean place, and it shall be kept for the congregation of the children of Israel for a water of separation: it is a purification for sin.' The ashes were kept, ready to be used again and again, whenever anyone needed to be cleansed. God provided a way of cleansing that was always available. And in Christ, his cleansing is always available to us, whenever we come to him.

The letter to the Hebrews says, 'For if the blood of bulls and of goats, and the ashes of an heifer sprinkling the unclean, sanctifieth to the purifying of the flesh: How much more shall the blood of Christ, who through the eternal Spirit offered himself without spot to God, purge your conscience from dead works to serve the living God?' And David prayed, 'Purge me with hyssop, and I shall be clean'.

Is there something on your conscience that makes you feel unclean before God? Bring it to him today. The blood of Christ, offered without spot, can purge your conscience, and set you free to serve the living God.

Here is today’s question. The ashes of the heifer were always kept ready, for anyone who needed cleansing. How does it encourage you that God’s cleansing is always available?

Let us pray. Lord God, purge me with hyssop, and I shall be clean. Thank you for Jesus, who offered himself without spot, and suffered outside the gate, to make me clean. Wash my conscience from dead works, so that I can serve you, the living God. Amen.
