The Living Voice. Numbers, chapter 20. Speak Ye unto the Rock.

The people are desperate. There is no water. Children are crying, animals are dying, and the crowd is angry again. God tells Moses exactly what to do: take your staff, and speak to the rock, and water will come out. But Moses is tired, and grieving, and furious with these people. He raises his staff, and instead of speaking, he strikes the rock. Twice. And it costs him the promised land.

In chapter nineteen, God gave the law of the red heifer, without spot, whose ashes mixed with running water made a water of separation, a purification for sin. Anyone made unclean by death could be cleansed on the third and seventh days. And the letter to the Hebrews said, how much more shall the blood of Christ purge your conscience.

Now, in chapter twenty, we come to a chapter of loss. Listen for who dies. And listen for the moment Moses loses his temper, and what it costs him.

The whole congregation of Israel comes into the desert of Zin, and stays at Kadesh. And there Miriam dies, and is buried.

Miriam. The girl who watched over baby Moses in the reeds. The prophetess who led the women in song at the Red Sea, with her tambourine. Now she is gone. The writer says it in a single sentence, quietly. But Moses and Aaron must have grieved deeply for their sister.

And there is no water for the people. And they gather against Moses and Aaron. They argue with Moses. If only we had died when our brothers died before the LORD! Why have you brought us into this wilderness, to die here with our animals? Why did you bring us out of Egypt, to this terrible place? There is no grain, no figs, no vines, no pomegranates, and no water to drink.

The same old complaints. A new generation is growing up, but they are saying exactly what their parents said.

Moses and Aaron go to the door of the tabernacle, and fall on their faces. And the glory of the LORD appears to them.

And God gives Moses clear instructions.

Speak to the rock. Many years before, at the start of the journey, God had told Moses to strike a rock, and water came out. This time, God says, speak to it.

So Moses takes the staff from before the LORD, as God commanded. And Moses and Aaron gather the people in front of the rock. And Moses says, listen, you rebels! Must we bring water out of this rock for you?

Then Moses lifts his hand, and strikes the rock with his staff. Twice.

And water gushes out, plenty of it. The people drink, and their animals drink.

God was still faithful. The people got their water. But something had gone badly wrong.

The LORD speaks to Moses and Aaron.

What was Moses’ sin? He did not do what God said. God said speak, and Moses struck. He spoke in anger, calling the people rebels. And he said, must we bring water out of this rock? As if it were Moses and Aaron doing the miracle, not God. In that moment, Moses did not honour God as holy before the people.

It seems a harsh punishment for one moment of anger. But Moses was the leader of God’s people, and he had shown them a wrong picture of God. Leaders carry great responsibility. And the place was called Meribah, which means quarrelling.

Then Moses sends messengers to the king of Edom. Edom was descended from Esau, Jacob’s brother. So Moses says, your brother Israel asks you. You know all the hardship we have been through. Please let us pass through your land. We will not go through your fields or vineyards, or drink from your wells. We will stay on the king’s highway.

But Edom says, you shall not pass through, or I will come out against you with the sword. And Edom comes out with a large, strong army. So Israel turns away.

Then the people travel to mount Hor, near the border of Edom. And the LORD tells Moses and Aaron that Aaron is about to die. He will not enter the land, because of what happened at Meribah.

God says, take Aaron and his son Eleazar up the mountain. Take Aaron’s priestly garments off him, and put them on Eleazar. And Aaron will die there.

So Moses does as the LORD commanded. The three of them climb mount Hor, while all the people watch. Moses takes off Aaron’s garments, and puts them on Eleazar. And Aaron dies there, at the top of the mountain. And Moses and Eleazar come down.

Imagine Moses coming down that mountain. His sister gone. His brother gone. His own hope of entering the land gone. It is one of the saddest chapters in the journey.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 8. 'Take the rod, and gather thou the assembly together, thou, and Aaron thy brother, and speak ye unto the rock before their eyes; and it shall give forth his water, and thou shalt bring forth to them water out of the rock: so thou shalt give the congregation and their beasts drink.' Speak ye unto the rock. God wanted to show his power through a simple word. Paul later wrote that the rock that followed Israel was Christ. Jesus gives living water to all who ask him. We do not need to strike, or force. We need only ask.

Verse 12. 'And the LORD spake unto Moses and Aaron, Because ye believed me not, to sanctify me in the eyes of the children of Israel, therefore ye shall not bring this congregation into the land which I have given them.' To sanctify means to treat as holy. In his anger, Moses did not show the people that God is holy. Even the greatest leaders can fall in a moment of anger. And those who lead others are held to a high standard, because people see God through them.

Verse 29. 'And when all the congregation saw that Aaron was dead, they mourned for Aaron thirty days, even all the house of Israel.' The whole house of Israel mourned Aaron for thirty days. For all his failures, the golden calf, the complaint against Moses, Aaron had served God and the people faithfully for many years. And the people grieved for him. It is right to honour those who have served God, and to grieve when they are gone.

Paul wrote, 'for they drank of that spiritual Rock that followed them: and that Rock was Christ.' And James wrote, 'For the wrath of man worketh not the righteousness of God.'

Is there a situation where you are tempted to act in anger, instead of doing what God says? Stop, and ask God for patience today. And thank God for someone who has served him faithfully, perhaps someone who is now gone.

Here is today’s question. Moses struck the rock in anger instead of speaking to it, and it cost him dearly. Why do you think anger is so dangerous, especially for those who lead others?

Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that you are faithful, even when your people fail. Keep me from anger that dishonours you. Help me to speak and act in a way that shows others that you are holy. Comfort all who grieve today. And thank you for Jesus, the Rock who gives living water. Amen.
