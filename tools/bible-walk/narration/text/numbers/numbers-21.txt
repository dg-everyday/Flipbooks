The Living Voice. Numbers, chapter 21. When He Beheld the Serpent of Brass, He Lived.

People are lying on the ground all through the camp, bitten by snakes, sick and in pain. There is no medicine. No cure. And then a message spreads from tent to tent. In the middle of the camp, Moses has raised a pole, with a bronze snake on top. If you have been bitten, just look at it. That is all. Look, and you will live.

In chapter twenty, Miriam died, and the people had no water. God told Moses to speak to the rock, but Moses struck it twice in anger, and God said that Moses and Aaron would not enter the land. Edom refused to let Israel pass, and Aaron died on mount Hor, and all Israel mourned him thirty days.

Now, in chapter twenty one, the people complain once more, and face a deadly danger. Listen for what they must do to be healed. And listen for how Jesus himself used this story.

A Canaanite king named Arad attacks Israel, and takes some of them captive. Israel makes a vow to the LORD, and the LORD gives them victory, and the place is called Hormah.

Then the people set out from mount Hor, going the long way round, to avoid the land of Edom. And the people become very discouraged because of the long road.

They had been so close to the promised land. Now they were going backwards, around the edge of Edom, through the hot desert. It was hard and discouraging.

And the people speak against God and against Moses. Why have you brought us up out of Egypt to die in the wilderness? There is no bread, and no water. And we hate this miserable food.

This miserable food. They were talking about the manna, the bread from heaven, God’s daily gift.

And the LORD sends fiery serpents among the people. Their bite burns like fire. And many of the people of Israel die.

Then the people come to Moses.

This time, the people do not blame anyone else. They confess. And Moses, as always, prays for them.

And God answers, in a way no one would have expected.

So Moses makes a serpent of bronze, and puts it on a pole.

Why a snake? It was a picture of the very thing that was harming them. And God did not take the snakes away. He gave a way to be healed, even after being bitten. And the cure was simple. Just look. Not climb the pole, or pay a price, or do something difficult. Just look at what God had provided, and trust him.

Many years later, Jesus was talking quietly with a man named Nicodemus. And Jesus said, as Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, even so must the Son of man be lifted up: that whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have eternal life.

Jesus was speaking of his cross. Just as the bronze serpent was lifted up on a pole, Jesus would be lifted up on the cross. And just as the people looked at the serpent and lived, all who look to Jesus in faith receive eternal life. The very next verse in John is perhaps the most famous verse in the Bible: For God so loved the world, that he gave his only begotten Son.

After this, the people travel on, from place to place, north along the edge of Moab. And at a place called Beer, which means well, God says to Moses, gather the people, and I will give them water. And Israel sings a song. 'Spring up, O well; sing ye unto it'.

What a change. For once, instead of complaining about water, they sing.

Then Israel sends messengers to Sihon, the king of the Amorites, asking to pass through his land on the king’s highway, without touching his fields or wells. But Sihon refuses, and comes out to fight. And Israel defeats him, and takes his land and his cities, including Heshbon.

Then Og, the king of Bashan, comes out to fight them. And the LORD says to Moses, do not be afraid of him, for I have given him into your hand. And Israel defeats Og too, and takes his land.

Sihon and Og. Remember those names. Long after, in Joshua, Rahab of Jericho told the spies, we have heard what you did to Sihon and Og. These victories became famous throughout the land.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 7. 'Therefore the people came to Moses, and said, We have sinned, for we have spoken against the LORD, and against thee; pray unto the LORD, that he take away the serpents from us. And Moses prayed for the people.' We have sinned. At last, the people admit it, without excuses. And Moses prays for them, again. Confession is the first step to healing. When we stop blaming others and say, I have sinned, God opens the way back.

Verse 8. 'And the LORD said unto Moses, Make thee a fiery serpent, and set it upon a pole: and it shall come to pass, that every one that is bitten, when he looketh upon it, shall live.' Every one that is bitten. No one was too far gone. No one was left out. Whoever looked would live. The gospel is like that. Whoever believes in Jesus, whoever looks to him, will not perish. The invitation is for everyone.

Verse 9. 'And Moses made a serpent of brass, and put it upon a pole, and it came to pass, that if a serpent had bitten any man, when he beheld the serpent of brass, he lived.' When he beheld it, he lived. Healing came not by effort, but by looking in faith at what God had provided. That is how we are saved. Not by our own efforts, but by looking to Jesus, lifted up on the cross for us.

Jesus said, 'And as Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, even so must the Son of man be lifted up: That whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have eternal life.' And Isaiah heard God say, 'Look unto me, and be ye saved, all the ends of the earth'.

Is there something in your life that is poisoning you, a sin, a fear, a bitterness? Look to Jesus today. Bring it to the cross, and trust him. He is God’s answer, lifted up for you.

Here is today’s question. The people were healed simply by looking at the bronze serpent in faith. What does it mean for you to look to Jesus, lifted up on the cross?

Let us pray. Lord God, I have sinned, and I need your healing. Thank you that you gave your Son, lifted up on the cross, so that whoever believes in him should not perish. I look to Jesus now. Heal me, and give me life. And teach me to sing instead of complain. Amen.
