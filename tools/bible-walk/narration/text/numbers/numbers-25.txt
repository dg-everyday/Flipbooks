The Living Voice. Numbers, chapter 25. He Was Zealous for His God.

Balaam could not curse Israel. Every time he opened his mouth, God turned it into a blessing. But if God’s people could not be cursed from the outside, perhaps they could be led astray from the inside. And that is exactly what happens next. Not with an army, or a curse, but with an invitation to a feast.

In chapter twenty four, Balaam looked down on Israel’s camp and said, how goodly are thy tents, O Jacob. He blessed Israel a third time, and then saw far into the future: there shall come a Star out of Jacob, and a Sceptre shall rise out of Israel. Then Balaam went home.

Now we come to chapter twenty five. A gentle word first. This is a dark chapter, about unfaithfulness and judgment, and we will tell it briefly, without the details. If you are listening with children, you may want to listen to this one yourself first. Listen for how Israel is drawn away from God. And listen for the man who stands up for God.

Israel is camped at a place called Shittim, on the plains of Moab, just across the Jordan from the promised land. They are so close.

And the people begin to be unfaithful to God with the women of Moab.

This was a double unfaithfulness. The men of Israel were unfaithful in their relationships, and through those relationships, they were drawn into worshipping the false god of Moab, called Baal of Peor. They broke faith with God, the one who had rescued them and blessed them.

And Israel joins itself to Baal of Peor. And the anger of the LORD burns against Israel. A plague begins among the people.

Think how terrible this was. Only days before, the most famous prophet in the land had been unable to curse them. God had protected them from every enemy. And now, right at the edge of the promise, they turn away from God themselves.

Moses tells the judges of Israel to deal with those who had joined themselves to the false god. And the people gather at the door of the tabernacle, weeping, in sorrow over what has happened.

And then, while they are weeping, one of the men of Israel openly brings a woman from Midian into the camp, right in front of Moses and the whole congregation, as if nothing were wrong. He was a leader in the tribe of Simeon, and she was the daughter of a Midianite prince. It was a brazen, open defiance of God, while everyone else was mourning.

When Phinehas sees it, he rises up from among the congregation. Phinehas was the grandson of Aaron, the son of Eleazar the priest. He takes a spear, and follows the man, and puts an end to the defiance, and to both of them. And the plague stops.

This is a hard and fierce story. Phinehas acted swiftly in a terrible moment, when the whole nation was in danger. The Bible tells it plainly, and we do not need to dwell on it.

And the LORD speaks to Moses about Phinehas.

And God says, tell him, I give him my covenant of peace.

Why does God honour Phinehas? Because he cared about God’s honour, when others did not. While God’s people were being led away from him, Phinehas stood up for God.

Then God tells Moses to treat the Midianites as enemies, because they had deliberately led Israel astray, through their schemes at Peor.

Later in the book of Numbers, we will discover who was behind this plan. It was Balaam. He could not curse Israel. So instead, he advised Balak to lead them into sin, so that God himself would turn against them. The book of Revelation, at the end of the Bible, says that Balaam taught Balak to put a stumbling block before the children of Israel.

What an important lesson. The greatest danger to God’s people was not the curse of their enemies. It was their own unfaithfulness. No one could curse them from outside. But they could turn away from God from within.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 2. 'And they called the people unto the sacrifices of their gods: and the people did eat, and bowed down to their gods.' It started with an invitation. Come to our feast. Then they ate. Then they bowed down. Sin often begins with something that seems small, a friendship, an invitation, a meal. But step by step, it can lead us far from God. That is why we must guard our hearts, and be careful whose invitations we accept.

Verse 11. 'Phinehas, the son of Eleazar, the son of Aaron the priest, hath turned my wrath away from the children of Israel, while he was zealous for my sake among them, that I consumed not the children of Israel in my jealousy.' Zealous means full of passionate care. Phinehas cared deeply about God’s honour. And because of him, the plague stopped, and Israel was not destroyed. God’s jealousy here is not petty. It is the jealousy of a husband for a beloved wife who is being unfaithful. God loves his people, and wants their whole hearts.

Verse 13. 'And he shall have it, and his seed after him, even the covenant of an everlasting priesthood; because he was zealous for his God, and made an atonement for the children of Israel.' Because he was zealous for his God. God rewarded Phinehas with a covenant of peace, and a lasting priesthood for his family. Jesus, too, was zealous for God’s house. When he cleared the temple, his disciples remembered, the zeal of thine house hath eaten me up.

Paul wrote of these very events, 'Now all these things happened unto them for ensamples: and they are written for our admonition'. And when Jesus cleared the temple, 'his disciples remembered that it was written, The zeal of thine house hath eaten me up.'

Is there an invitation, a friendship or a habit in your life that is slowly drawing you away from God? Be honest with him about it today. And ask God to give you a heart that cares about his honour.

Here is today’s question. Balaam could not curse Israel, but Israel turned away from God from within. Why do you think the greatest dangers to our faith often come from inside, not outside?

Let us pray. Lord God, keep me faithful to you. Guard my heart from the invitations and habits that would draw me away. Give me a zeal for your honour, like Phinehas. Thank you for Jesus, who was zealous for your house, and who made atonement for us. Amen.
