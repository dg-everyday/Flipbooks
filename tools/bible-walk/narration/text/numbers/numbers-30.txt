The Living Voice. Numbers, chapter 30. He Shall Not Break His Word.

Have you ever made a promise without really thinking about it? I promise I will be there. I promise I will pay you back. I promise I will never do that again. And then life got in the way, and the promise was quietly forgotten. In Numbers chapter thirty, God tells his people that their promises matter. Especially the ones they make to him.

In chapter twenty nine, God set out the offerings for the seventh month: the day of blowing the trumpets, the solemn day when the people afflicted their souls, and the great feast of seven days, with seventy bulls offered in thanks. All this was beside the people’s own vows and freewill offerings.

Now, in chapter thirty, God speaks about those vows, the promises people made to him. Listen for how seriously God takes our words. And listen for how God protected people who made promises they could not keep.

Moses speaks to the heads of the tribes. This is what the LORD has commanded.

A vow was a solemn promise made to God. Perhaps a promise to give a gift, or to do something, or to go without something for a time, like the Nazarite vow in chapter six. People often made vows when they were praying for help: if you do this, Lord, I will do that.

And God says, if you make a vow, keep it.

Then God speaks about vows made by women in the family. In Israel at that time, a young woman usually lived in her father’s house, and then in her husband’s house. And the family shared its goods and its decisions together.

If a young woman living in her father’s house made a vow, and her father heard it and said nothing, the vow would stand. But if her father objected on the day he heard it, the vow would not stand.

And God adds something kind.

The same was true for a married woman. If her husband heard her vow and said nothing, it would stand. If he objected on the day he heard it, it would be cancelled, and the LORD would forgive her.

But a widow, or a woman who was divorced, was responsible for her own vows.

This law is part of the family life of ancient Israel, and Christians have understood how it applies in different ways. But notice what it did. It protected people from rash promises that could harm the whole family. A young woman might promise something in a moment of strong feeling, a promise that would cost her family dearly. The father or husband could step in, on that same day. And when he did, God released her, and forgave her.

But there was a limit. The man had to speak up on the day he heard the vow. He could not stay silent for a long time, and then cancel it later when it suited him. If he did, God says, the guilt would be on him, not on her.

Remember Jephthah, in the book of Judges? He made a rash vow, and it led to great sorrow. God’s law gave a way to step in before a rash promise did harm.

The heart of this chapter is simple. Words matter to God. What we promise, especially to him, we should do. And God is merciful to those who make promises they cannot keep, when someone with responsibility for them steps in.

Jesus taught something similar, but even deeper. He said, do not swear great oaths at all. Instead, let your yes mean yes, and your no mean no. People who always tell the truth do not need to make special promises. Their ordinary word is enough.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 2. 'If a man vow a vow unto the LORD, or swear an oath to bind his soul with a bond; he shall not break his word, he shall do according to all that proceedeth out of his mouth.' He shall not break his word. God is a promise keeper. He has never broken a single promise. And he wants his people to be like him. When we keep our word, we reflect the faithfulness of God. When we break it, we dishonour him.

Verse 5. 'But if her father disallow her in the day that he heareth; not any of her vows, or of her bonds wherewith she hath bound her soul, shall stand: and the LORD shall forgive her, because her father disallowed her.' The LORD shall forgive her. God did not hold her guilty for a vow she was not able to keep. God knows our situations. He is gracious to those who find themselves unable to do what they promised, through no fault of their own.

Verse 9. 'But every vow of a widow, and of her that is divorced, wherewith they have bound their souls, shall stand against her.' A widow or a divorced woman stood on her own before God. Her vows were her own, and she was responsible for them. God treated her as a full person, able to make and keep her own promises to him.

The Preacher wrote, 'When thou vowest a vow unto God, defer not to pay it; for he hath no pleasure in fools: pay that which thou hast vowed.' And Jesus said, 'But let your communication be, Yea, yea; Nay, nay'.

Is there a promise you have made, to God or to someone else, that you have not kept? Ask God for help to keep it today. And before you make any new promise, stop and think.

Here is today’s question. Jesus said, let your yes be yes, and your no be no. What would it look like for your everyday words to be so trustworthy that you never needed to make special promises?

Let us pray. Lord God, you have never broken a promise. Make me a person whose word can be trusted. Help me to keep the promises I have made, and to think carefully before I make new ones. Thank you for your mercy when I fail. Let my yes be yes, and my no be no. Amen.
