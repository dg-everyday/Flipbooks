The Living Voice. Numbers, chapter 31. There Lacketh Not One Man of Us.

Twelve thousand soldiers march out of the camp, a thousand from every tribe. With them goes a priest, carrying the holy things, and the silver trumpets. They are going to war against the people who led Israel into sin at Peor. And when they come back, their commanders count the men. And the number they find is astonishing.

In chapter thirty, God taught that words matter. If a man vow a vow unto the LORD, he shall not break his word. And God protected those who made rash promises, by letting a father or husband step in, and saying, the LORD shall forgive her.

Now we come to chapter thirty one. A gentle word first. This chapter tells of war, and of judgment on the Midianites, and it includes some of the hardest commands in the whole Bible. We will tell it briefly, and pass over the hardest details. If you are listening with children, you may want to listen to it yourself first. Listen for why this battle happened. And listen for what the soldiers bring back to God.

The LORD speaks to Moses. Bring judgment on the Midianites for what they did to the children of Israel. After that, you will be gathered to your people.

This was to be one of the last things Moses did before he died. The Midianites had deliberately led Israel into sin at Peor, in chapter twenty five, and a plague had followed. Now judgment would come on them.

Moses tells the people to send a thousand men from every tribe, twelve thousand in all. And he sends them out with Phinehas the priest, the same Phinehas who stood up for God at Peor. Phinehas carries the holy things and the trumpets.

Israel’s army fights against Midian, as the LORD commanded, and defeats them. Five kings of Midian fall. And Balaam, the son of Beor, falls among them too.

Balaam. The prophet who could not curse Israel, but who found another way to harm them. He had said, let me die the death of the righteous. But he did not die that way. He died among God’s enemies.

The soldiers bring back captives and animals and goods from Midian, to Moses and Eleazar and the leaders, who come out of the camp to meet them.

And Moses is angry with the commanders. And he tells them something we did not know before.

So it was Balaam who was behind the disaster at Peor. He could not curse Israel with words. So he taught the Midianites how to lead Israel into sin, so that God himself would turn against them.

Then the Bible records hard commands Moses gave about the captives. They are among the most difficult words in Scripture, and we will not describe them. These were judgments in a particular time, against a people who had deliberately set out to destroy Israel’s faith. Christians have thought hard about chapters like this, and have understood them in different ways. What we can say is that God takes sin, and those who lead others into sin, with great seriousness.

Then Moses tells the soldiers to stay outside the camp for seven days, and to purify themselves and everything they brought back, using fire and the water of separation. War, even at God’s command, brought death, and death made people unclean. They had to be cleansed before coming back into the camp where God lived.

Then God tells Moses how to divide the spoil. Half for the soldiers who fought. Half for the whole congregation who stayed behind. And from each half, a portion was to be given to the LORD, to the priests and the Levites.

Remember David, at Ziklag, who said that those who stayed with the supplies should share alike with those who fought? The same idea is here. The whole people shared in what God had given.

Then the officers come to Moses, and say something remarkable.

Not a single one. Twelve thousand men went out to battle, and every single one came back. God had protected them all.

And in thanks, the officers bring an offering to the LORD. Gold jewellery, chains, bracelets, rings and earrings, everything they had taken. They say, we have brought it to make atonement for our souls before the LORD.

Moses and Eleazar take the gold, a great amount of it, and bring it into the tabernacle.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 16. 'Behold, these caused the children of Israel, through the counsel of Balaam, to commit trespass against the LORD in the matter of Peor, and there was a plague among the congregation of the LORD.' The counsel of Balaam. Here we learn the truth about Balaam. He said all the right words, but his heart loved money, and he led God’s people into sin. The book of Revelation warns against holding the doctrine of Balaam. It is possible to say beautiful things about God, and still lead people away from him.

Verse 49. 'And they said unto Moses, Thy servants have taken the sum of the men of war which are under our charge, and there lacketh not one man of us.' There lacketh not one. Every soldier came home. God had kept every single one of them safe. The officers recognised it as God’s gift. Jesus said of those the Father had given him, I have lost none.

Verse 54. 'And Moses and Eleazar the priest took the gold of the captains of thousands and of hundreds, and brought it into the tabernacle of the congregation, for a memorial for the children of Israel before the LORD.' A memorial, something to remember. The gold was kept in the tabernacle as a reminder of how God had protected his people. When God brings us safely through something hard, it is good to give thanks, and to keep a reminder of his faithfulness.

Jesus said, 'Those that thou gavest me I have kept, and none of them is lost'. And the risen Jesus warned a church, 'thou hast there them that hold the doctrine of Balaam'.

Think of a time God brought you, or your family, safely through something dangerous or hard. Thank him for it today. And ask him to keep you from anyone, or anything, that would lead you away from him.

Here is today’s question. Balaam said beautiful words about God, but led Israel into sin. How can we be careful about whose advice and teaching we follow?

Let us pray. Lord God, this is a hard chapter, and I bring my questions to you. Thank you that you take sin seriously, and that you protect your people. Thank you that, like the soldiers, not one of yours is lost. Keep me from false teaching, and help me to give thanks for your faithfulness. Amen.
