The Living Voice. Numbers, chapter 33. Moses Wrote Their Goings Out.

Have you ever looked back over the places you have lived, or the journeys you have taken? Each name brings back a memory. A happy time. A hard time. A time when everything changed. In Numbers chapter thirty three, Moses does exactly that. He writes down every place Israel camped, from Egypt to the edge of the promised land. More than forty stops. And every one has a story.

In chapter thirty two, the tribes of Reuben and Gad asked to settle east of the Jordan. Moses asked, shall your brethren go to war, and shall ye sit here? They promised to go armed before their brothers, and Moses warned them, be sure your sin will find you out. And they said, as the LORD hath said, so will we do.

Now, in chapter thirty three, Moses looks back over the whole journey. Listen for the places you recognise. And listen for what God tells them to do when they enter the land.

The chapter begins with a title.

Then Moses tells us how the journey began.

The children of Israel set out from Rameses in Egypt, the day after the Passover. They went out boldly, with a high hand, in full view of all the Egyptians, while the Egyptians were burying their firstborn, whom the LORD had struck down. The LORD had brought judgment on the gods of Egypt too.

Then the list begins. And each name is a memory.

From Rameses to Succoth. From Succoth to Etham, at the edge of the wilderness. Then to Pi-hahiroth, by the sea. Then, the writer says simply, they passed through the midst of the sea.

The Red Sea. The greatest miracle of the whole journey, told in half a sentence. But every Israelite hearing this list would remember. The walls of water. The dry ground. The army of Egypt behind them.

Then three days into the wilderness, to Marah, where the water was bitter, and God made it sweet.

Then to Elim.

Then on through the wilderness of Sin, where God first sent the manna. To Rephidim, where there was no water for the people to drink, and water came from the rock. Then to the wilderness of Sinai, where God gave the law, and they built the tabernacle, and stayed almost a year.

Then the list goes on. Many places we have never heard of, places where they camped during the long years of wandering. Names like Rithmah, and Rimmon-parez, and Libnah, and Rissah. Each one a camp. Each one a day, or a month, or a year, of waiting for the cloud to move.

Then to Kadesh, in the wilderness of Zin. Then to mount Hor, at the edge of Edom, where Aaron the priest went up the mountain at the LORD’s command, and died there.

Then on, around Edom, past the place of the bronze serpent, to the border of Moab. And finally, to the plains of Moab, by the Jordan, across from Jericho. Where they are now.

The whole journey, written down, stop by stop. Why? Because God wanted his people to remember. Every name was a place where God had led them, fed them, protected them, or disciplined them. Even the places where they failed were part of the story of God’s faithfulness.

Then the LORD speaks to Moses on the plains of Moab. When you cross the Jordan into the land of Canaan, drive out the people of the land. Destroy their carved images, their metal idols, and their high places, where they worship their false gods.

And God gives a warning. But if you do not drive them out, those you allow to remain will be like splinters in your eyes, and thorns in your sides. They will trouble you in the land.

Remember the book of Judges? Again and again, we heard, neither did they drive them out. And just as God warned here, those peoples became thorns in Israel’s side, and their gods became a snare.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 2. 'And Moses wrote their goings out according to their journeys by the commandment of the LORD: and these are their journeys according to their goings out.' By the commandment of the LORD. God told Moses to write it all down. God wanted his people to have a record of their journey, so that later generations could look back and see his faithfulness. It is good for us, too, to remember the journey God has led us on, and to tell it to others.

Verse 9. 'And they removed from Marah, and came unto Elim: and in Elim were twelve fountains of water, and threescore and ten palm trees; and they pitched there.' Twelve springs and seventy palm trees, an oasis in the desert. Right after Marah, the place of bitter water, came Elim, a place of rest and refreshment. God does not only lead us through hard places. He also gives us places of rest along the way.

Verse 53. 'And ye shall dispossess the inhabitants of the land, and dwell therein: for I have given you the land to possess it.' I have given you the land. The land was God’s gift. All Israel had to do was go in and take hold of it. God had promised it to Abraham, long before, and now, at last, the promise was within reach.

A psalm says, 'He led them forth by the right way, that they might go to a city of habitation.' And Paul wrote, 'And we know that all things work together for good to them that love God'.

Look back over your own journey with God, the places and seasons of your life. Write down a few of them, the bitter places and the oases. And thank God for leading you through them all.

Here is today’s question. Moses wrote down every stop of the journey, the good and the bad. Looking back over your own life, where can you see God’s leading, even in the hard places?

Let us pray. Lord God, thank you for leading me on my journey, through bitter places and through places of rest. Thank you that you never left your people in the wilderness. Help me to remember your faithfulness, and to take hold of all that you have given me. Amen.
