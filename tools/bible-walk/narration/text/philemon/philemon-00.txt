The Living Voice. Meet the Letter to Philemon.

A young man stands at the door of a large house, his heart pounding. He has not been here for a long time. Once he lived and worked in this house, as a servant. Then one day he ran away. Now he has come back. In his hand is a letter. He knocks on the door, and waits. Will his master be angry? Will he be punished? Everything depends on what that letter says.

Welcome to the letter to Philemon, on The Living Voice. Philemon is the shortest of all Paul’s letters, just one chapter. On this walk, it is one step, after this introduction. You can read the whole letter in a few minutes. But it tells one of the most moving stories in the New Testament.

Philemon was a believer who lived in Colosse, the town we have just heard about in the letter to the Colossians. He was probably quite wealthy. He had a large house, big enough for the church to meet in, and he had servants. Paul calls him a dear friend and fellow worker. It seems Philemon had come to faith through Paul.

One of Philemon’s servants was named Onesimus. And Onesimus ran away. Perhaps he took something with him, money or goods, because Paul hints that he may have owed Philemon something. He fled, far from Colosse.

And then something remarkable happened. Somehow, Onesimus met Paul, who was in prison. And through Paul, Onesimus came to believe in Jesus. His whole life was changed. He became like a son to Paul, and a great help to him.

But Paul knew that Onesimus needed to put things right with Philemon. So he sent him back home, travelling with Tychicus, who was carrying the letter to the Colossians. In that letter, Paul mentions Onesimus, a faithful and beloved brother, who is one of you. And Paul sent with him this short, personal letter to Philemon, asking him to welcome Onesimus back.

We need to understand a little about those days. In the Roman world, a great many people were servants, or slaves, owned by masters. A runaway servant could be punished very severely. Onesimus was taking a big risk by going back. Paul does not write a law for the whole empire. Instead, he does something that, over time, would change how Christians saw one another. He asks Philemon to receive Onesimus, not as a servant, but as a brother. In Christ, master and servant are family.

Here are three things to watch for as we walk.

Watch for how Paul asks. He could have given Philemon an order. Instead, he appeals to him, out of love. Listen for how gentle and wise he is.

Watch for how the good news changes relationships. Onesimus was once a runaway. Now he is a brother. Philemon was once his master. Now he is called to welcome him as family. Jesus changes not just our hearts, but the way we treat each other.

And watch for what Paul offers to do. If Onesimus owes Philemon anything, Paul says, charge it to me. I will pay it. Many have seen in those words a picture of what Jesus does for us.

Here is a question to carry with you. Onesimus had to go back and face the person he had wronged. Is there someone you need to make things right with?

Let us pray. Lord God, as I walk through the letter to Philemon, show me how your good news changes the way we treat one another. Give me courage to put things right where I have done wrong. And give me a forgiving heart, to welcome others back. Amen.
