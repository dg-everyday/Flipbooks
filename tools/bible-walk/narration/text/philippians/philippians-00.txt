The Living Voice. Meet the Book of Philippians.

Two men sit in the darkest part of a prison. Their backs are sore from a beating. Their feet are locked fast in wooden stocks. Most people would be groaning, or crying, or complaining. But these two men are singing. 'And at midnight Paul and Silas prayed, and sang praises unto God: and the prisoners heard them.' That happened in a city called Philippi. And years later, Paul wrote a letter to the church in that city. It is the happiest letter in the Bible.

Welcome to the letter to the Philippians, on The Living Voice. Philippians has four chapters, so on this walk it is four steps, one chapter at a time. Like James, it is short enough to finish in less than a week.

We heard how the church in Philippi began, when we walked through the book of Acts. Paul had a vision of a man saying, 'Come over into Macedonia, and help us.' So Paul and his friends sailed across the sea, and came to Philippi, a proud Roman city. By a river, outside the city, they met a group of women who gathered to pray. One of them was Lydia, a seller of purple cloth, and the Lord opened her heart. She and her household believed, and she welcomed the missionaries into her home.

Then came trouble. Paul and Silas were beaten, and thrown into prison. And there, as they sang, God sent an earthquake. The doors flew open, and everyone’s chains came loose. The jailer woke up, saw the open doors, and was so afraid that he was ready to harm himself. But Paul cried out, 'Do thyself no harm: for we are all here.' The jailer fell trembling before them and asked, what must I do to be saved? And they told him, 'Believe on the Lord Jesus Christ, and thou shalt be saved, and thy house.'

So the church in Philippi began with a businesswoman, a jailer, and their families. Paul loved them dearly. They were the only church that kept sending him gifts to help him in his work.

Now, years later, Paul is a prisoner again, waiting to hear whether he will live or die. The church in Philippi has sent a friend named Epaphroditus to bring him a gift, and to look after him. Epaphroditus became very ill, and nearly died, but God had mercy on him. Now he is going home, and Paul sends this letter with him. It is a thank-you letter, and a love letter, and a letter full of joy.

Here are three things to watch for as we walk.

Watch for joy. The words joy and rejoice come again and again in this short letter. And remember where Paul is when he writes them: in chains. His joy does not come from easy days. It comes from knowing Jesus.

Watch for the mind of Christ. In chapter two, Paul writes some of the most beautiful words ever written about Jesus, who stepped down from heaven, and became a servant. And Paul says, let that same mind be in you.

And watch for contentment. Paul says he has learned to be content, whether he has plenty, or whether he is hungry. And he tells us the secret.

Philippians is a letter to read when you are worried, or discouraged, or when life seems small and hard. It shows that joy and peace can be found anywhere, even in a prison cell, because Jesus is there.

Here is a question to carry with you. Paul and Silas sang praises in prison, and Paul wrote a letter full of joy while he was in chains. What helps you to find joy in God when things are hard?

Let us pray. Lord God, as I walk through the letter to the Philippians, fill me with your joy. Thank you that your joy does not depend on easy days. Teach me the mind of Christ, and show me how to be content in you, whatever comes. Amen.
