The Living Voice. Meet the Book of Proverbs.

A father sits with his son on a stone bench by the city gate. People pass by. A lazy man yawns in the shade. A quarrelsome woman shouts at her neighbour. A businessman counts his coins with a crooked smile. An old man helps a stranger carry his load. And the father leans over and says, my son, watch them. Learn from them. Let me tell you what I have learned about life.

Welcome to the book of Proverbs, on The Living Voice. Proverbs has thirty one chapters, so on this walk it is thirty one steps, one chapter at a time. Many people read one chapter of Proverbs each day, through a month. You may like to do the same.

In first Kings, we heard that God gave Solomon wisdom and understanding exceeding much, and that he spoke three thousand proverbs. The book of Proverbs begins, the proverbs of Solomon the son of David, king of Israel. Many of its sayings come from Solomon. Others were collected later. One section was copied out by the men of Hezekiah, the good king of Judah we met in second Kings. And near the end are the words of two other wise men, Agur and king Lemuel.

What is a proverb? It is a short, wise saying, easy to remember. Like a seed, it is small, but it grows in your mind. Many have become everyday sayings. Pride goes before a fall. Spare the rod. A soft answer turns away wrath. They all come from this book.

The book has three parts. In chapters one to nine, a father talks to his son, urging him to choose wisdom. In chapters ten to twenty nine come hundreds of short sayings, about words and work, friends and money, families and kings. Many of them are not in any order. They are like a box of jewels, each one to be picked up and looked at. Then the last two chapters bring the words of Agur, the words of king Lemuel, and a beautiful poem about a virtuous woman.

One important thing to understand. A proverb tells us how life usually works, when God is honoured. For example, Proverbs says the diligent will prosper. That is generally true. But the Bible also shows us faithful people who suffered, like Job, and wicked people who seemed to do well for a time. A proverb is wise advice, not a promise that every single case will turn out that way.

Here are four things to watch for as we walk.

Watch for the fear of the LORD. The book says that the fear of the LORD is the beginning of knowledge. Fear here means deep respect and awe. True wisdom starts with honouring God.

Watch for two ways. Again and again, Proverbs sets two paths side by side: the wise and the foolish, the righteous and the wicked, the hardworking and the lazy. Every day, we choose which path to walk.

Watch for the tongue. Proverbs has a great deal to say about our words, how they can wound or heal, and when to speak and when to stay quiet.

And watch for work and money. Proverbs is very practical. It talks about honesty, generosity, debt and laziness, as if God cares about ordinary daily life. And he does.

A gentle word before we begin. In chapters five, six and seven, a father warns his son about being tempted to be unfaithful in marriage. We will tell those chapters gently. If you are listening with young children, you may want to listen to them yourself first.

Here is a question to carry with you. Proverbs is full of advice from a father to his son. What is one piece of wise advice someone gave you, that you have never forgotten?

Let us pray. Lord God, as I walk through the book of Proverbs, give me wisdom for ordinary days. Teach me to fear you, to choose the wise path, to guard my tongue, and to work and give honestly. Thank you that you care about every part of my life. Amen.
