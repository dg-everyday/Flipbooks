The Living Voice. Proverbs, chapter 7. Keep My Commandments, and Live.

A bird hops across the ground, pecking at seeds. It sees a little pile of grain, and hops over, delighted. It does not notice the thin net hidden under the leaves, or the string waiting to be pulled. It only sees the food. And by the time it understands, it is too late. Proverbs says that is what happens to a young man who does not listen to wisdom.

In chapter six, the father told his son to go to the ant, and learn from her hard work. He listed six things the LORD hates, yes, seven. He said, the commandment is a lamp, and the law is light. And he warned that unfaithfulness is like taking fire into your arms.

Now, in chapter seven, the father tells a story, to show his son how temptation works. We will tell it gently. Listen for what the young man did not have. And listen for the father’s advice at the end.

The father begins with a call to hold on to his words.

He says it in several ways. 'Bind them upon thy fingers, write them upon the table of thine heart.' Tie them on your fingers, so you see them every time you use your hands. Write them on your heart, so they become part of you.

'Say unto wisdom, Thou art my sister; and call understanding thy kinswoman'. Treat wisdom like a close member of your family, someone you love and spend time with. Why? So that wisdom will keep you from temptation, and from the one who flatters with smooth words.

And then the father tells a story. I was looking out through the window of my house, he says, and I saw a group of young people. Among them was a young man 'void of understanding', with no sense at all.

He was walking along the street, near the corner where a certain woman lived. And it was dark. He was going towards her house. Perhaps he told himself he was just taking a walk. But he knew where he was going.

And there she was, waiting for him. She was married, but her husband was away on a long journey. She was loud and restless, the father says. She did not stay at home. She was always out in the streets, waiting at every corner.

She caught hold of him, kissed him, and spoke boldly to him. She flattered him, and pretended she had been looking especially for him. She told him her husband was away, and would not be home for a long time. She tried to make the wrong thing sound exciting and safe.

'With her much fair speech she caused him to yield, with the flattering of her lips she forced him.' Fair speech means smooth, attractive words. The young man gave in.

And then the father describes what happened next, with sad and serious pictures. 'He goeth after her straightway'. He follows her, like an ox being led away, not knowing what is about to happen to it. Like a fool walking into a trap. 'as a bird hasteth to the snare, and knoweth not that it is for his life.' He thought he was going to have a good time. He did not understand that he was walking into something that would hurt him deeply.

Then the father turns to his children, and speaks to them directly. 'Hearken unto me now therefore, O ye children, and attend to the words of my mouth.'

And he gives his advice.

He reminds them that this kind of temptation has brought down many strong people. Its path leads down to death.

Notice where the young man’s trouble started. Not when the woman spoke to him. It started earlier, when he chose to walk down that street, near her corner, in the dark. He did not have wisdom as his sister. He had no sense. And he put himself in the place of temptation.

That is why the father keeps saying, hold on to my words. Write them on your heart. Wisdom does not only help us say no when temptation comes. It helps us stay away from the places where temptation waits.

This chapter is about faithfulness in marriage. But its lesson applies to every kind of temptation. Do not wander towards it. Do not listen to its flattery. Hold on to God’s Word, and stay on the right path.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 2. 'Keep my commandments, and live; and my law as the apple of thine eye.' The apple of the eye is the pupil, the dark centre of the eye, the part we protect most carefully. If anything comes near our eyes, we blink, or put up our hands, without even thinking. The father says, guard God’s commandments like that. Protect them like your most precious possession. Keeping them is the way to live.

Verse 3. 'Bind them upon thy fingers, write them upon the table of thine heart.' A table here means a tablet, like the stone tablets of the Ten Commandments. God’s Word should be close to us all the time: on our fingers, where we see it as we work, and on our hearts, where it shapes what we want. When God’s Word is written on our hearts, it is there to help us at the very moment we need it.

Verse 25. 'Let not thine heart decline to her ways, go not astray in her paths.' Decline means lean towards, turn aside. The young man’s heart leaned towards temptation before his feet went there. The father says, do not even let your heart lean that way. Temptation starts in the heart, long before it shows in our actions. So guard your heart, and do not let it drift towards what is wrong.

The psalmist asked, 'Wherewithal shall a young man cleanse his way? by taking heed thereto according to thy word.' And Paul wrote, 'God is faithful, who will not suffer you to be tempted above that ye are able; but will with the temptation also make a way to escape'.

Is there a place, a website, a friendship, a habit, where you know temptation is waiting? Do not wander towards it. Choose a different path. And write one verse of God’s Word on your heart today, by learning it.

Here is today’s question. The young man’s trouble began when he walked down the street near her corner. What are the streets in your life that it would be wise to avoid?

Let us pray. Lord God, help me to keep your commandments like the apple of my eye. Write your Word on my heart. Let wisdom be like a sister to me. Keep my heart from leaning towards temptation, and my feet from wandering towards it. And thank you that you are faithful, and always give me a way to escape. Amen.
