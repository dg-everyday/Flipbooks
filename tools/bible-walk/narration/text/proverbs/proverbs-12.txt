The Living Voice. Proverbs, chapter 12. The Tongue of the Wise Is Health.

Two people speak to a friend who is upset. The first says something sharp, and careless. Even years later, the friend still remembers those words, like a scar. The second says something gentle and kind. Even years later, the friend still remembers those words too, like a warm coat on a cold day. Words can wound, and words can heal.

In chapter eleven, we heard that a false balance is an abomination to the LORD, but a just weight is his delight. When pride cometh, then cometh shame. In the multitude of counsellors there is safety. And the liberal soul shall be made fat, and he that watereth shall be watered also himself.

Now, in chapter twelve, Solomon’s sayings go on. Listen for how much power our words have. And listen for a saying about kindness to animals.

The chapter begins with a blunt saying. 'Whoso loveth instruction loveth knowledge: but he that hateth reproof is brutish.' Brutish means like an animal without sense. If you love learning, you will welcome correction. If you hate being corrected, you are being foolish.

'A good man obtaineth favour of the LORD'. And, 'the root of the righteous shall not be moved.' Like a tree with deep roots, a good person stands firm in storms.

Then a saying about marriage. 'A virtuous woman is a crown to her husband'. A wife of strong character brings honour to her husband, like a king’s crown. We will hear much more about the virtuous woman at the end of the book.

Then a saying that might surprise you. 'A righteous man regardeth the life of his beast'. Regardeth means cares about. Good people are kind to their animals, feeding them, looking after them, and not treating them cruelly. God cares about the animals he made, and he wants us to care for them too.

'He that tilleth his land shall be satisfied with bread: but he that followeth vain persons is void of understanding.' Tilleth means ploughs and works. Steady work brings food. But chasing after empty, worthless schemes, and the people who promise them, is foolish.

Then come several sayings about listening and anger.

'A fool’s wrath is presently known'. Presently means immediately. A foolish person shows their anger right away, for everyone to see. 'but a prudent man covereth shame.' A wise person stays calm, and does not react to every insult.

Then many sayings about truth and lies. 'He that speaketh truth sheweth forth righteousness: but a false witness deceit.'

And then a striking picture.

'The lip of truth shall be established for ever: but a lying tongue is but for a moment.' A lie may seem to work for a while. But the truth lasts. Sooner or later, lies are found out.

'Deceit is in the heart of them that imagine evil: but to the counsellors of peace is joy.' Those who make peace between people have joy.

'Lying lips are abomination to the LORD: but they that deal truly are his delight.' God hates lies. He delights in people who are honest.

'A prudent man concealeth knowledge'. A wise person does not show off everything they know. But fools announce their foolishness to everyone.

'The hand of the diligent shall bear rule: but the slothful shall be under tribute.' Hardworking people end up leading. Lazy people end up working for others.

And then a beautiful saying about encouragement.

And then a saying that we need to understand carefully. 'There shall no evil happen to the just'. Remember what we heard at the start of the book. A proverb tells us how life usually works. It does not mean good people never suffer. Job was a good man, and he suffered terribly. Jesus was perfectly good, and he went to the cross. This saying means that, in the end, no real harm can come to those who belong to God. He keeps them.

And then a funny picture of a lazy hunter. 'The slothful man roasteth not that which he took in hunting'. He goes to all the trouble of catching an animal, and then is too lazy to cook it! Laziness wastes even the good things we already have.

The chapter ends with a wonderful promise. 'In the way of righteousness is life; and in the pathway thereof there is no death.'

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 15. 'The way of a fool is right in his own eyes: but he that hearkeneth unto counsel is wise.' A fool always thinks he is right. He does not need anyone’s advice. But a wise person listens, and is willing to learn from others. One of the easiest ways to spot a fool is to see whether they can admit they might be wrong.

Verse 18. 'There is that speaketh like the piercings of a sword: but the tongue of the wise is health.' Some people speak like stabbing with a sword. Their words cut deep, and leave wounds that can last for years. But the words of a wise person bring health, like medicine. Every time we speak, we choose: will my words wound, or heal?

Verse 25. 'Heaviness in the heart of man maketh it stoop: but a good word maketh it glad.' Heaviness means worry, sadness, anxiety. It weighs down the heart, and makes it stoop, like someone carrying a heavy load. But a good word, a kind and encouraging word, can make that heart glad again. You may never know how much a kind word means to someone who is carrying a heavy heart.

Paul wrote, 'Let your speech be alway with grace, seasoned with salt'. And Isaiah said, 'The Lord GOD hath given me the tongue of the learned, that I should know how to speak a word in season to him that is weary'.

Look out today for someone with a heavy heart, and give them a good word. Encourage them. And be kind to any animals in your care. God cares about them too.

Here is today’s question. Proverbs says a good word makes a heavy heart glad. Who has encouraged you with a good word, and who could you encourage today?

Let us pray. Lord God, my words have power to wound or to heal. Help me to speak words of health, not words that pierce like a sword. Keep me from lying lips, and help me to deal truly. Give me eyes to see the heavy hearts around me, and a good word to make them glad. Amen.
