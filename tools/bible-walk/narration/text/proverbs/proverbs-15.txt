The Living Voice. Proverbs, chapter 15. A Soft Answer Turneth Away Wrath.

Someone storms up to you, red in the face and shouting. Everything in you wants to shout back, even louder. But instead, you take a breath, and answer quietly and gently. And something surprising happens. The other person’s voice drops. Their shoulders relax. The anger drains away. A fire with no fuel goes out.

In chapter fourteen, we heard that there is a way which seemeth right unto a man, but the end thereof are the ways of death. In the fear of the LORD is strong confidence. And whoever oppresses the poor insults their Maker, but whoever is kind to the poor honours him.

Now, in chapter fifteen, Solomon’s sayings go on. Listen for the power of a gentle word. And listen for what really makes a home happy.

The chapter begins with one of the best known sayings in the whole Bible.

'The tongue of the wise useth knowledge aright: but the mouth of fools poureth out foolishness.' Wise people use what they know in the right way. Fools just pour out words, like water from a jug.

Then a saying about God.

'A wholesome tongue is a tree of life: but perverseness therein is a breach in the spirit.' Wholesome words, gentle, true and kind, give life. Twisted words break people’s spirits.

'A fool despiseth his father’s instruction: but he that regardeth reproof is prudent.'

Then a saying about worship. 'The sacrifice of the wicked is an abomination to the LORD: but the prayer of the upright is his delight.' God is not impressed by religious gifts from people who are living in wickedness. But the simple prayer of someone who loves him delights him. God cares about our hearts more than our offerings.

Then several sayings about happiness. 'A merry heart maketh a cheerful countenance'. Countenance means face. A happy heart shows on the face. 'but by sorrow of the heart the spirit is broken.'

'All the days of the afflicted are evil: but he that is of a merry heart hath a continual feast.' The afflicted here are those who see everything in a gloomy way. Every day seems bad to them. But someone with a cheerful heart has a feast every day, even when they do not have much. Our attitude makes a great difference.

Then two sayings that begin with the word better.

'Better is little with the fear of the LORD than great treasure and trouble therewith.' It is better to have a little, and honour God, than to have great wealth and all the worry and trouble that come with it.

And then, the second.

'A wrathful man stirreth up strife: but he that is slow to anger appeaseth strife.' An angry person starts fights. A patient person calms them down.

'The way of the slothful man is as an hedge of thorns'. For a lazy person, every task looks like a thorny hedge, too difficult to get through. 'but the way of the righteous is made plain.' For those who simply do what is right, the path is clear.

'Without counsel purposes are disappointed: but in the multitude of counsellors they are established.' Plans fail without advice, but succeed with many advisers. We heard something similar in chapter eleven.

Then a lovely saying about the right word at the right time. 'A man hath joy by the answer of his mouth: and a word spoken in due season, how good is it!' In due season means at just the right time. Have you ever had someone say exactly the right thing, just when you needed it? How good that is.

'The LORD will destroy the house of the proud: but he will establish the border of the widow.' God opposes the proud, but he protects the widow, the most vulnerable.

'The heart of the righteous studieth to answer'. Studieth means thinks carefully. A good person thinks before answering. 'but the mouth of the wicked poureth out evil things.'

'The LORD is far from the wicked: but he heareth the prayer of the righteous.' What a comfort. God hears our prayers.

And the chapter ends with this. 'The fear of the LORD is the instruction of wisdom; and before honour is humility.' The way up is down. Humility comes first, and then God gives honour.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 1. 'A soft answer turneth away wrath: but grievous words stir up anger.' A soft answer is a gentle, calm reply. Grievous words are harsh, hurtful words. When someone is angry with us, our natural reaction is to fight back. But harsh words are like throwing petrol on a fire. A gentle answer is like water. It takes strength to answer gently when someone is angry. But it can turn an argument into peace.

Verse 3. 'The eyes of the LORD are in every place, beholding the evil and the good.' Beholding means watching, seeing. There is nowhere God cannot see. He sees the evil that people do in secret, and he sees the good that no one else notices. That is a warning to those who think they can hide. And a comfort to those who do good unseen. God sees you.

Verse 17. 'Better is a dinner of herbs where love is, than a stalled ox and hatred therewith.' A dinner of herbs means a simple vegetable meal, what poor people ate. A stalled ox is a fattened animal, the finest meat, a feast for the rich. Proverbs says a simple meal with people who love each other is far better than a fancy feast where people hate each other. A happy home is not made by expensive things. It is made by love.

The letter to the Hebrews says, 'Neither is there any creature that is not manifest in his sight'. And Paul wrote, 'for I have learned, in whatsoever state I am, therewith to be content.'

If someone speaks to you angrily today, try a soft answer, and see what happens. And at your next meal with family or friends, be thankful for the love around the table, whatever is on the plates.

Here is today’s question. Proverbs says, a soft answer turneth away wrath. When have you seen a gentle word calm an angry situation, and what makes it hard to answer gently?

Let us pray. Lord God, your eyes are in every place, and you see all I do. Give me a soft answer when others are angry, and keep me from grievous words. Make my heart merry, and my home a place of love, even if it is simple. Thank you that you hear the prayer of the righteous. Teach me humility, for before honour is humility. Amen.
