The Living Voice. Proverbs, chapter 18. The Name of the LORD Is a Strong Tower.

In ancient cities, there was often a tall stone tower in the middle, with thick walls and a heavy door. When enemies attacked, the people ran inside, shut the door, and were safe. Arrows could not reach them. Spears bounced off the stones. Proverbs says God is like that tower. And anyone can run to him.

In chapter seventeen, we heard that the LORD trieth the hearts, like silver in the fining pot. A friend loveth at all times, and a brother is born for adversity. A merry heart doeth good like a medicine. And even a fool, when he holds his peace, is counted wise.

Now, in chapter eighteen, Solomon’s sayings go on. Listen for where to find safety. And listen for the great power of our words.

The chapter begins with someone who cuts himself off from others. A person who separates himself, Proverbs says, chases after his own desires, and argues against all good sense. We were not made to be alone. People who refuse to listen to anyone else end up foolish.

'A fool hath no delight in understanding, but that his heart may discover itself.' A fool is not interested in learning. He just wants to tell everyone his own opinions.

Then sayings about words. 'The words of a man’s mouth are as deep waters, and the wellspring of wisdom as a flowing brook.' Wise words are deep, and they keep flowing, like fresh water.

'A fool’s lips enter into contention, and his mouth calleth for strokes.' A fool’s mouth gets him into fights. 'A fool’s mouth is his destruction, and his lips are the snare of his soul.' His own words trap him.

'The words of a talebearer are as wounds, and they go down into the innermost parts of the belly.' A talebearer is a gossip. Gossip is like tasty food. People swallow it eagerly, and it goes deep inside them. But it wounds. It hurts the person being talked about, and it poisons the hearts of those who listen.

'He also that is slothful in his work is brother to him that is a great waster.' A lazy worker is as bad as someone who destroys things. Both waste what could have been good.

Then comes a beautiful picture of safety.

Then a contrast. 'The rich man’s wealth is his strong city, and as an high wall in his own conceit.' Conceit means imagination. A rich person thinks his money is a strong city that will protect him. But that is only in his imagination. Real safety is only in the name of the LORD.

'Before destruction the heart of man is haughty, and before honour is humility.' We have heard this before. Pride comes before a fall. Humility comes before honour.

Then a very practical saying. 'He that answereth a matter before he heareth it, it is folly and shame unto him.' Have you ever started answering before someone finished asking? Or made up your mind before you heard the whole story? Proverbs says that is foolish. Listen first, then answer.

'The spirit of a man will sustain his infirmity; but a wounded spirit who can bear?' A strong spirit can help us through sickness. But when someone’s spirit is crushed, who can bear it? Hurting spirits need gentle care.

'A man’s gift maketh room for him, and bringeth him before great men.' A gift can open doors, and gain a hearing.

Then another very practical saying. 'He that is first in his own cause seemeth just; but his neighbour cometh and searcheth him.' When you hear only one side of an argument, it always sounds right. But then the other person tells their side, and you realise it is not so simple. Always hear both sides.

Then a sad saying. 'A brother offended is harder to be won than a strong city'. When family members hurt each other, it can be harder to make peace than to capture a walled city. The quarrels are like the bars on a castle gate. That is why we should be so careful not to hurt those close to us.

And then the most powerful saying in the chapter, about the tongue.

'Whoso findeth a wife findeth a good thing, and obtaineth favour of the LORD.' A good marriage is a gift from God.

'The poor useth intreaties; but the rich answereth roughly.' Intreaties means humble requests. Poor people often have to beg politely, while rich people can answer harshly. God sees how we treat those with less power than us.

And the chapter ends with a saying about friendship.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 10. 'The name of the LORD is a strong tower: the righteous runneth into it, and is safe.' The name of the LORD means God himself, who he is, all his power, love and faithfulness. When trouble comes, we can run to him, like running into a strong tower, and be safe. Not running away from life, but running to God in prayer and trust. He is our refuge.

Verse 21. 'Death and life are in the power of the tongue: and they that love it shall eat the fruit thereof.' Our words have power. They can bring death: crushing hope, destroying friendships, breaking hearts. Or they can bring life: encouraging, comforting, building up. And those who love to talk will eat the fruit of what they say. Our words come back to us, for good or for harm. Choose words that give life.

Verse 24. 'A man that hath friends must shew himself friendly: and there is a friend that sticketh closer than a brother.' If you want friends, be friendly. Friendship takes effort, kindness and care. And there is a friend who sticks closer than a brother, one who never leaves, no matter what. Many Christians have seen in this verse a picture of Jesus, who calls us his friends, and promises, I will never leave thee.

David sang, 'The LORD is my rock, and my fortress, and my deliverer'. And James wrote, 'Out of the same mouth proceedeth blessing and cursing.'

When you feel afraid or under attack today, run to the strong tower. Pray. And watch your tongue: try to speak only words that bring life. Before you judge any argument, make sure you have heard both sides.

Here is today’s question. Proverbs says, death and life are in the power of the tongue. What kind of fruit have your words been producing lately?

Let us pray. Lord God, your name is a strong tower, and I run to you. Keep me safe. Help me to use my tongue to bring life, not death. Teach me to listen before I answer, and to hear both sides. Help me to be friendly, and thank you for Jesus, the friend who sticks closer than a brother. Amen.
