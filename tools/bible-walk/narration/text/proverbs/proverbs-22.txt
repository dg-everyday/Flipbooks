The Living Voice. Proverbs, chapter 22. Train Up a Child in the Way He Should Go.

A gardener plants a young tree, and ties it to a straight stick, so it will grow tall and upright. Every so often she checks it, and adjusts the tie. It takes years. But one day, the tree is strong and straight, and does not need the stick any more. If she had left it to grow however it liked, it might have grown crooked, and stayed that way.

In chapter twenty one, we heard that the king’s heart is in the hand of the LORD. To do justice is more acceptable to the LORD than sacrifice. And the horse is prepared against the day of battle, but safety is of the LORD.

Now, in chapter twenty two, Solomon’s collection comes to an end, and a new section begins: the words of the wise. Listen for what is better than riches. And listen for one of the most famous sayings about children.

The chapter begins with a saying about reputation.

'The rich and poor meet together: the LORD is the maker of them all.' More about this in a moment.

'A prudent man foreseeth the evil, and hideth himself: but the simple pass on, and are punished.' A wise person sees trouble coming, and gets out of the way. A simple person walks straight into it. Wisdom helps us see ahead.

'By humility and the fear of the LORD are riches, and honour, and life.' Humility and honouring God bring true riches.

Then comes the famous saying about children.

'The rich ruleth over the poor, and the borrower is servant to the lender.' When we borrow money, we become like a servant to the person we owe. Debt takes away our freedom. That is a wise warning about borrowing more than we need.

'He that soweth iniquity shall reap vanity'. Plant wrongdoing, and you will harvest emptiness.

'He that hath a bountiful eye shall be blessed; for he giveth of his bread to the poor.' A bountiful eye means a generous eye, an eye that notices people in need, and wants to help. Generous people are blessed.

'Cast out the scorner, and contention shall go out; yea, strife and reproach shall cease.' Sometimes one mocking troublemaker keeps a whole group arguing. When they leave, peace returns.

Then another funny excuse from the lazy man. The slothful man says, there is a lion outside! It might get me in the streets! He makes up ridiculous excuses, so that he does not have to go out and work.

'Foolishness is bound in the heart of a child; but the rod of correction shall drive it far from him.' Children are not born wise. They need loving correction to help them grow. As we heard before, Christians understand the rod in different ways. But the heart of it is clear: loving parents correct their children.

Then, at verse seventeen, a new section begins. These are called the words of the wise. 'Bow down thine ear, and hear the words of the wise, and apply thine heart unto my knowledge.' It is good, the writer says, to keep these words inside you, ready on your lips. Why? 'That thy trust may be in the LORD'. The aim of all this wisdom is that we would trust God.

Then come the first of these wise sayings. 'Rob not the poor, because he is poor: neither oppress the afflicted in the gate'. Do not take advantage of poor people just because they cannot defend themselves. The gate was where courts were held. Do not crush the helpless in court. 'For the LORD will plead their cause'. God himself will be their lawyer, and he will deal with those who wrong them.

'Make no friendship with an angry man; and with a furious man thou shalt not go: Lest thou learn his ways, and get a snare to thy soul.' If you spend a lot of time with a hot tempered person, you may become like them. Their anger rubs off.

'Be not thou one of them that strike hands, or of them that are sureties for debts.' Again, be careful about promising to pay other people’s debts. If you cannot pay, they might even take the bed from under you.

'Remove not the ancient landmark, which thy fathers have set.' Landmarks were stones that marked the edge of a field. Moving one secretly was a way of stealing land. Do not cheat your neighbours.

And the chapter ends with a saying about hard work. 'Seest thou a man diligent in his business? he shall stand before kings; he shall not stand before mean men.' Mean here means unimportant. Someone who is skilled and hardworking will be noticed, and will serve important people. Think of Joseph, or Daniel, or Nehemiah. Faithful work opens doors.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 1. 'A good name is rather to be chosen than great riches, and loving favour rather than silver and gold.' A good name means a good reputation, being known as honest, kind and trustworthy. Proverbs says that is worth more than great wealth. Money can be lost, spent or stolen. But a good name, built over many years, is precious. And it can be lost in a moment by one dishonest act. Guard your name.

Verse 2. 'The rich and poor meet together: the LORD is the maker of them all.' Rich people and poor people may live very different lives. But in God’s eyes, they meet on the same ground. He made them all. Neither is more important to him. This should keep the rich humble, and give the poor dignity. Every person we meet, whatever they have or do not have, was made by God.

Verse 6. 'Train up a child in the way he should go: and when he is old, he will not depart from it.' To train up means to guide, teach and shape. The way he should go means the right way, God’s way. When parents lovingly teach their children to know and follow God from an early age, it shapes them for life. This is a proverb, not a promise for every case. Some children wander for a time. But early training in God’s ways is a precious gift, and its roots go deep.

Paul wrote to fathers, 'bring them up in the nurture and admonition of the Lord.' And Solomon wrote elsewhere, 'A good name is better than precious ointment'.

Think about your good name. Is there anything you are doing that could harm it? And if you have children, or help to raise them, look for one small way to train them in God’s way today.

Here is today’s question. Proverbs says a good name is rather to be chosen than great riches. What do you want to be known for, and what can you do today to build that kind of name?

Let us pray. Lord God, help me to value a good name more than riches. Thank you that you made both rich and poor, and that every person matters to you. Help parents to train up their children in your way. Give me a generous eye for the poor, and keep me from friendships that would lead me into anger. Help me to work diligently, and to trust in you. Amen.
