The Living Voice. Proverbs, chapter 24. A Just Man Falleth Seven Times.

A little child is learning to walk. She pulls herself up, takes one wobbly step, and falls down. She gets up again. Two steps, and down she goes. Up again. Again and again she falls, and again and again she gets up. She never once thinks, I am a failure, I will never walk. She just keeps getting up. And one day, she walks.

In chapter twenty three, we heard, labour not to be rich, for riches make themselves wings and fly away. Buy the truth, and sell it not. And the father said, my son, give me thine heart. Then came a warning about wine, which bites like a serpent.

Now, in chapter twenty four, the words of the wise continue. Listen for what Proverbs says about falling down. And listen for a lesson from a walk past a lazy man’s field.

The chapter begins with a warning we have heard before. 'Be not thou envious against evil men, neither desire to be with them.' Their hearts plan destruction, and their lips talk about causing trouble. Do not be jealous of them.

Then a saying about building.

'A wise man is strong; yea, a man of knowledge increaseth strength.' Wisdom is a kind of strength. 'For by wise counsel thou shalt make thy war: and in multitude of counsellors there is safety.'

'Wisdom is too high for a fool: he openeth not his mouth in the gate.' The gate was where the leaders of the city met. A fool has nothing worth saying there.

Then a saying about hard times. 'If thou faint in the day of adversity, thy strength is small.' Adversity means trouble. Anyone can be strong when things are easy. Our real strength shows when times are hard.

Then a serious call. Rescue those who are being led away to death, Proverbs says. Hold back those who are stumbling towards destruction. 'If thou sayest, Behold, we knew it not; doth not he that pondereth the heart consider it?' Do not say, we did not know. God knows our hearts. He knows if we looked away. When people are in danger, God expects us to help.

Then a sweet picture. 'My son, eat thou honey, because it is good; and the honeycomb, which is sweet to thy taste: So shall the knowledge of wisdom be unto thy soul'. Wisdom is not a bitter medicine. It is sweet, like honey. When you find it, there is a future, and your hope will not be cut off.

Then a saying about falling down.

Then a surprising saying.

'Fret not thyself because of evil men, neither be thou envious at the wicked'. Fret means worry, get upset. Do not let wicked people make you anxious or jealous. Their light will go out.

'My son, fear thou the LORD and the king'. Honour God, and respect those in authority.

Then, at verse twenty three, a short new section begins. 'These things also belong to the wise.' More wise sayings.

'It is not good to have respect of persons in judgment.' Judges should be fair, and not favour rich or important people. Whoever tells a guilty person, you are innocent, will be cursed by the people. But those who stand up for what is right will be blessed.

'Every man shall kiss his lips that giveth a right answer.' An honest answer is as welcome as a kiss from a friend.

'Prepare thy work without, and make it fit for thyself in the field; and afterwards build thine house.' First get your fields ready, so you can earn a living. Then build your house. Do things in the right order. Plan ahead.

'Be not a witness against thy neighbour without cause'. Do not lie about your neighbour. 'Say not, I will do so to him as he hath done to me'. Do not take revenge.

And the chapter ends with a little story. 'I went by the field of the slothful, and by the vineyard of the man void of understanding; And, lo, it was all grown over with thorns, and nettles had covered the face thereof, and the stone wall thereof was broken down.'

The writer walked past a lazy man’s farm. And what did he see? Weeds and thorns everywhere. Stinging nettles over the ground. The stone wall around the field had fallen down. No one had looked after it.

'Then I saw, and considered it well: I looked upon it, and received instruction.' He stopped, and thought about what he saw, and learned a lesson. Laziness does not destroy things all at once. It just lets them slowly fall apart. 'Yet a little sleep, a little slumber, a little folding of the hands to sleep: So shall thy poverty come as one that travelleth'. We heard those same words in chapter six. Little by little, poverty creeps in.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 3. 'Through wisdom is an house builded; and by understanding it is established'. The next verse says that by knowledge its rooms are filled with precious and pleasant riches. Proverbs may be talking about a real house. But it is also talking about a home, a family, a life. Money can build walls. But it takes wisdom to build a home that lasts, and understanding to make it strong, and knowledge to fill it with what is truly precious.

Verse 16. 'For a just man falleth seven times, and riseth up again: but the wicked shall fall into mischief.' Seven times means again and again. Good people are not people who never fall. They fall many times. They make mistakes, and sin, and fail. But they get up again, because they trust God. What matters is not whether we fall, but whether we get up. And God helps us up.

Verse 17. 'Rejoice not when thine enemy falleth, and let not thine heart be glad when he stumbleth'. When someone who has hurt us gets into trouble, part of us may want to cheer. Proverbs says, do not. God does not want us to be glad about anyone’s fall, even our enemies’. Jesus went even further, and said, love your enemies. A heart that celebrates others’ pain is not a heart like God’s.

The prophet Micah said, 'when I fall, I shall arise'. And Jesus said, 'Love your enemies'.

Have you fallen recently? Do not stay down. Get up again, and ask God to help you. And if someone who has hurt you is going through a hard time, pray for them instead of being glad.

Here is today’s question. Proverbs says a just man falleth seven times, and riseth up again. What helps you to get back up when you have failed?

Let us pray. Lord God, I fall many times. Thank you that you help me get up again. Build my life and my home with your wisdom. Keep me from being glad when others fall, even my enemies. Help me to care for what you have given me, so that it does not fall into ruin like the lazy man’s field. And give me courage to help those who are in danger. Amen.
