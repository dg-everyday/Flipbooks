The Living Voice. Proverbs, chapter 28. The Righteous Are Bold as a Lion.

A boy has done something wrong, and he knows it. All day, he feels jumpy. Every time a door opens, he jumps. Every time someone calls his name, his heart pounds. Is it my mum? Has she found out? No one is even looking for him. But his guilt makes him afraid of everything. His sister, who has nothing to hide, walks around the house calmly, without a care.

In chapter twenty seven, we heard, boast not thyself of to morrow. Faithful are the wounds of a friend, but the kisses of an enemy are deceitful. And iron sharpeneth iron, so a man sharpens his friend.

Now, in chapter twenty eight, the sayings go on. Listen for the difference between a guilty conscience and a clear one. And listen for the best thing to do with our sins.

The chapter begins with a striking picture.

'For the transgression of a land many are the princes thereof'. When a country is full of wrongdoing, its rulers keep changing, one after another. Do you remember the northern kingdom of Israel, in the books of Kings, where king followed king so quickly? 'but by a man of understanding and knowledge the state thereof shall be prolonged.' A wise leader brings stability.

'A poor man that oppresseth the poor is like a sweeping rain which leaveth no food.' Rain should help crops grow. But a violent storm washes everything away. When someone who was once poor gains power and then crushes other poor people, it is like a destroying storm.

'They that forsake the law praise the wicked: but such as keep the law contend with them.' People who ignore God’s law end up admiring wicked people. Those who keep God’s law stand against them.

'Evil men understand not judgment: but they that seek the LORD understand all things.' Those who seek God gain understanding that others miss.

'Better is the poor that walketh in his uprightness, than he that is perverse in his ways, though he be rich.'

'He that by usury and unjust gain increaseth his substance, he shall gather it for him that will pity the poor.' Usury here means charging the poor unfair interest. Proverbs says that money gained unfairly will one day end up in the hands of someone who is kind to the poor. God turns things around.

Then a serious saying about prayer. 'He that turneth away his ear from hearing the law, even his prayer shall be abomination.' If we refuse to listen to God, we cannot expect him to listen to us. Prayer is a two way conversation.

'When righteous men do rejoice, there is great glory: but when the wicked rise, a man is hidden.' When good people are in charge, everyone celebrates. When wicked people rule, people hide.

Then comes a precious saying about sin.

'Happy is the man that feareth alway: but he that hardeneth his heart shall fall into mischief.' Feareth alway means always respects God and is careful not to sin. Those who harden their hearts against God will fall.

'As a roaring lion, and a ranging bear; so is a wicked ruler over the poor people.' A cruel ruler is as dangerous to the poor as a hungry lion or an angry bear.

'Whoso walketh uprightly shall be saved'. 'He that tilleth his land shall have plenty of bread'.

'A faithful man shall abound with blessings: but he that maketh haste to be rich shall not be innocent.' Someone who is faithful and patient will be blessed. Someone desperate to get rich quickly will be tempted to cut corners and do wrong.

'To have respect of persons is not good: for for a piece of bread that man will transgress.' Showing favouritism is wrong. People will do wrong even for something as small as a piece of bread.

'He that rebuketh a man afterwards shall find more favour than he that flattereth with the tongue.' At first, people may not like being corrected. But later, they will be grateful to the one who told them the truth, more than to the one who only flattered them.

'Whoso robbeth his father or his mother, and saith, It is no transgression; the same is the companion of a destroyer.' Taking from your parents and saying it is not wrong, because it is family, is still stealing.

'He that is of a proud heart stirreth up strife: but he that putteth his trust in the LORD shall be made fat.' Pride causes quarrels. Trust in God brings blessing.

'He that trusteth in his own heart is a fool: but whoso walketh wisely, he shall be delivered.' Our own hearts can deceive us. Trusting only our own feelings is foolish. We need God’s wisdom.

And then a saying about the poor.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 1. 'The wicked flee when no man pursueth: but the righteous are bold as a lion.' A guilty conscience makes people afraid of everything, even when no one is chasing them. But people who live rightly, with nothing to hide, can be bold and confident, like a lion. That is not because they are proud, but because they are at peace with God. A clear conscience is a wonderful gift.

Verse 13. 'He that covereth his sins shall not prosper: but whoso confesseth and forsaketh them shall have mercy.' To cover our sins means to hide them, deny them, or make excuses. That never works out well. But when we confess our sins to God, and forsake them, turning away from them, we find mercy. Confessing is not enough on its own. We also need to leave our sins behind. And God’s mercy is waiting for everyone who does.

Verse 27. 'He that giveth unto the poor shall not lack: but he that hideth his eyes shall have many a curse.' Hiding our eyes means pretending not to see the needy, looking the other way. Those who give to the poor will not lack what they need. But those who deliberately ignore the poor will find that it comes back on them. God sees whether we look, or look away.

When the rulers saw how Peter and John spoke, the Bible says, 'when they saw the boldness of Peter and John, and perceived that they were unlearned and ignorant men, they marvelled'. And the psalmist wrote, 'Blessed is he that considereth the poor'.

Is there a sin you have been covering up? Confess it to God today, and turn away from it. He will show you mercy. And today, do not hide your eyes from someone in need. Look, and help if you can.

Here is today’s question. Proverbs says, the wicked flee when no man pursueth, but the righteous are bold as a lion. How does having a clear conscience before God change the way we live?

Let us pray. Lord God, I do not want to cover my sins. I confess them to you now, and I want to turn away from them. Thank you for your mercy. Give me a clear conscience, so that I can be bold as a lion. Open my eyes to the poor, and keep me from looking away. Help me to trust you, not my own heart. Amen.
