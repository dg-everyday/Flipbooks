The Living Voice. Psalm 2. Kiss the Son.

A group of small children stand at the foot of a huge mountain, shaking their fists and shouting. Go away, mountain! We do not want you here! We are going to push you over! The mountain does not move. It does not even notice. Psalm two pictures the rulers of the world shouting against God like that. And God’s answer is surprising.

Before this, in Psalm one, we heard about two ways of life. The blessed person does not walk in the counsel of the ungodly, but delights in the law of the LORD, and is like a tree planted by the rivers of water. The ungodly are like chaff that the wind blows away. And the LORD knows the way of the righteous.

Now, in Psalm two, the second of our thirty favourite psalms, we move from one person to the whole world. Psalm one and Psalm two together are like the front gates of the book of Psalms. Listen for who the King is. And listen for the psalm’s last word, which echoes the first word of Psalm one.

The psalm begins with a question.

'Why do the heathen rage, and the people imagine a vain thing?' The heathen means the nations, the peoples of the world. Rage means to be in uproar, to plot and rebel. A vain thing means something empty, pointless, that will never work.

'The kings of the earth set themselves, and the rulers take counsel together, against the LORD, and against his anointed'. The anointed one is the king God has chosen. In Hebrew, the word is Messiah. In Greek, it is Christ. The rulers of the world plot together against God, and against his chosen King.

And what do they say? 'Let us break their bands asunder, and cast away their cords from us.' They see God’s rule as chains, ropes tying them down. Let us break free, they say. We will be our own masters.

Then the scene changes, from earth to heaven. And how does God react?

Then God speaks, in his anger, and terrifies them. 'Yet have I set my king upon my holy hill of Zion.' Zion is the hill where Jerusalem stood. You can plot all you like, God says. But I have already put my King on the throne.

And then the King himself speaks.

God says to the King, 'Ask of me, and I shall give thee the heathen for thine inheritance, and the uttermost parts of the earth for thy possession.' Every nation, to the ends of the earth, will belong to this King. And he will rule with complete authority, and no rebellion will stand against him.

Then the psalm turns to the kings and rulers of the earth, and gives them a warning, and an invitation. 'Be wise now therefore, O ye kings: be instructed, ye judges of the earth. Serve the LORD with fear, and rejoice with trembling.'

And then.

Who is this King? At first, the psalm may have been sung when a new king of David’s family was crowned in Jerusalem. But no king of Israel ever ruled to the ends of the earth. The psalm points beyond them, to a greater King.

The first Christians knew who he was. When they were threatened by the rulers in Jerusalem, they prayed, quoting this very psalm. Why did the heathen rage? Herod and Pontius Pilate and the people gathered together against God’s holy child Jesus. And when Jesus was baptised in the river Jordan, a voice from heaven said, thou art my beloved Son. The letter to the Hebrews says the words of this psalm, thou art my Son, were spoken to Jesus.

Jesus is the King. And Psalm two is still true today. People still rage against God, and want to break free from him. But God has already set his King on his throne. And the invitation still stands: kiss the Son, and put your trust in him.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 4. 'He that sitteth in the heavens shall laugh: the Lord shall have them in derision.' Derision means scorn, mockery. When the most powerful people on earth plot against God, he is not worried. He is not even standing up. He sits on his throne, and laughs. Their plans are like children shouting at a mountain. When the news is frightening, and it seems that evil is winning, remember this verse. God is still on his throne.

Verse 7. 'I will declare the decree: the LORD hath said unto me, Thou art my Son; this day have I begotten thee.' A decree is an official announcement. On the day a king was crowned, he became like a son to God. But this verse points to a unique Son. The New Testament applies it to Jesus, especially to his resurrection, when God declared to the whole world who Jesus really is. Jesus is not just a good teacher. He is God’s own Son, the King.

Verse 12. 'Kiss the Son, lest he be angry, and ye perish from the way, when his wrath is kindled but a little. Blessed are all they that put their trust in him.' To kiss the king’s hand or feet was a sign of loyalty and love. The psalm says, come to the Son, honour him, and bow before him. And then the last words: blessed are all they that put their trust in him. Psalm one began with blessed. Psalm two ends with blessed. The truly happy life is found in trusting the King.

The believers prayed, 'Why did the heathen rage, and the people imagine vain things?' And at Jesus’ baptism a voice from heaven said, 'Thou art my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased.'

When the world seems out of control, and powerful people seem to be against God, remember Psalm two. God sits in the heavens, and his King reigns. Bring your worries to him. And ask yourself, have I bowed before the Son, and put my trust in him?

Here is today’s question. People in Psalm two see God’s rule as chains they want to break. Why do you think people want to be free from God, and what does real freedom look like?

Let us pray. Lord God, you sit in the heavens, and nothing can overthrow your King. Thank you that Jesus is your Son, and the King of all the earth. I bow before him, and I put my trust in him. When the world rages, help me not to fear, but to remember that you are on your throne. Blessed are all they that put their trust in him. Amen.

Next on the walk, we jump ahead to Psalm nineteen, because our psalms are grouped by theme, and Psalm nineteen, like Psalm one, delights in God’s Word. The heavens declare the glory of God. And God’s Word is more precious than gold, and sweeter than honey.
