The Living Voice. Psalm 27. The LORD Is My Light and My Salvation.

A little girl wakes up in the dark, frightened. Every shadow looks like a monster. Every creak sounds like footsteps. She calls out, and her father comes and switches on the light. Suddenly, the monster is just a coat on a chair. The footsteps are just the house settling. When the light comes on, fear goes away. Psalm twenty seven begins by saying that the LORD is our light.

In Psalm forty six, the earth shook and the mountains were carried into the sea, but the psalm said, we will not fear. God is our refuge and strength, a very present help in trouble. The LORD of hosts is with us, the God of Jacob is our refuge. And God said, be still, and know that I am God.

Now, in Psalm twenty seven, David faces enemies all around him. Listen for the one thing David wants more than anything. And listen for how the psalm changes halfway through, from confidence to crying out.

Psalm twenty seven is a psalm of David. We do not know exactly when he wrote it. But David faced many enemies in his life. King Saul chased him through the wilderness for years. Foreign armies attacked him. Even his own son Absalom turned against him. David knew what it was to be afraid.

And yet the psalm begins with a bold declaration.

Then David remembers what happened when his enemies attacked. 'When the wicked, even mine enemies and my foes, came upon me to eat up my flesh, they stumbled and fell.' They came at him like wild animals. But they tripped and fell.

'Though an host should encamp against me, my heart shall not fear: though war should rise against me, in this will I be confident.' An host means an army. Even if a whole army camped around me, David says, I would not be afraid. I am confident in God.

Then David tells us the one thing he wants most of all.

And he knows why it matters. 'For in the time of trouble he shall hide me in his pavilion: in the secret of his tabernacle shall he hide me; he shall set me up upon a rock.' A pavilion is a tent, like a king’s tent. In trouble, God hides David in his own tent, his own presence. And he sets him high up on a rock, where enemies cannot reach him.

'And now shall mine head be lifted up above mine enemies round about me: therefore will I offer in his tabernacle sacrifices of joy; I will sing, yea, I will sing praises unto the LORD.'

And then, halfway through, the psalm changes. The confident song becomes a cry for help. 'Hear, O LORD, when I cry with my voice: have mercy also upon me, and answer me.'

This is one of the honest things about the psalms. David has just said he is not afraid. And now he is crying out to God. Faith and fear can live side by side. We can trust God, and still need to cry out to him.

'When thou saidst, Seek ye my face; my heart said unto thee, Thy face, LORD, will I seek.' To seek someone’s face means to want to be close to them, to look for them. God invited David to seek him. And David’s heart answered, yes, LORD, I will.

'Hide not thy face far from me; put not thy servant away in anger: thou hast been my help; leave me not, neither forsake me, O God of my salvation.'

Then a very tender verse. 'When my father and my mother forsake me, then the LORD will take me up.' Forsake means leave, abandon. Most parents love their children deeply. But even the people who love us most are human, and sometimes they may let us down, or not be there. David says, even if the people closest to me fail me, the LORD will take me up, and hold me. God’s love is even more faithful than the love of the best father or mother.

'Teach me thy way, O LORD, and lead me in a plain path, because of mine enemies.' A plain path is a smooth, level path, where it is easy to walk without stumbling.

Then David remembers how close he came to giving up. 'I had fainted, unless I had believed to see the goodness of the LORD in the land of the living.' I would have collapsed, David says, if I had not believed that I would see God’s goodness, here in this life.

And then the psalm ends with advice.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 1. 'The LORD is my light and my salvation; whom shall I fear? the LORD is the strength of my life; of whom shall I be afraid?' Light chases away darkness, and shows us the way. Salvation means rescue. Strength means a stronghold, a safe fortress. If God is all these things, David asks, what is there left to fear? Fear may still come. But when it does, we can ask David’s question. Whom shall I fear? And remember who God is.

Verse 4. 'One thing have I desired of the LORD, that will I seek after; that I may dwell in the house of the LORD all the days of my life, to behold the beauty of the LORD, and to enquire in his temple.' Of all the things David could have wanted, safety, victory, riches, his one great desire was to be close to God. To behold means to gaze at. The beauty of the LORD is his goodness, his glory, his love. David wanted to spend his whole life gazing at God, and learning from him. What is the one thing you desire most?

Verse 14. 'Wait on the LORD: be of good courage, and he shall strengthen thine heart: wait, I say, on the LORD.' Waiting is hard. We want help right now. But waiting on the LORD is not doing nothing. It means trusting him, looking to him, expecting him to act in his own good time. And while we wait, he strengthens our hearts. David says it twice, because it is so important. Wait on the LORD.

Jesus said, 'I am the light of the world: he that followeth me shall not walk in darkness, but shall have the light of life.' And Isaiah wrote, 'they that wait upon the LORD shall renew their strength'.

Is there something you are afraid of, or waiting for? Pray David’s question, whom shall I fear? And if you are waiting for an answer from God, ask him to strengthen your heart while you wait.

Here is today’s question. David says his one great desire is to dwell in the house of the LORD, and behold his beauty. If you could ask God for only one thing, what would it be, and why?

Let us pray. Lord, you are my light and my salvation; whom shall I fear? You are the strength of my life. The one thing I desire is to be close to you, and to see your beauty. When I am afraid, hear me when I cry. Even if others let me down, thank you that you will take me up. Teach me your way. And help me to wait on you, with courage. Amen.
