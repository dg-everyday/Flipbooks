The Living Voice. Psalm 32. Blessed Is He Whose Transgression Is Forgiven.

A boy breaks his mother’s favourite vase. He hides the pieces under his bed, and says nothing. But all day long, he cannot stop thinking about it. He does not feel like eating. He cannot enjoy playing. His stomach feels tight, and he cannot sleep. Finally, he goes to his mother, and tells her everything. She hugs him and says, I forgive you. And suddenly, the heavy weight is gone. Psalm thirty two is about that feeling.

In Psalm twenty two, we heard the cry Jesus prayed on the cross, my God, my God, why hast thou forsaken me. We heard of the pierced hands and feet, and the soldiers casting lots for his clothing. But then came the turn. God heard. And all the ends of the world shall turn unto the LORD.

Now we move to psalms of repentance and forgiveness. Psalm thirty two is David’s song about the joy of being forgiven. Listen for what happened to David when he hid his sin. And listen for what God promises to do for those he forgives.

Psalm thirty two is a psalm of David, called a Maschil, a psalm that teaches. David wants to teach us something he learned the hard way.

He begins with a word we have heard before. Blessed.

Then he says, 'Blessed is the man unto whom the LORD imputeth not iniquity, and in whose spirit there is no guile.' Imputeth means counts against, writes down in his record. God does not count the sin against him any more. And there is no guile, no deceit, no pretending, in his spirit. He is honest with God.

Notice the three words for sin in these verses. Transgression means crossing a line, rebelling. Sin means missing the mark, like an arrow that falls short of the target. Iniquity means something twisted, crooked. And there are three words for forgiveness. Forgiven means lifted off, carried away. Covered means hidden from sight. And not imputed means not counted.

Many centuries later, the apostle Paul quoted these words in his letter to the Romans, to show that we are made right with God by faith, not by our good works.

Then David tells us what happened when he tried to hide his sin.

'When I kept silence, my bones waxed old through my roaring all the day long.' When I kept silence means when I refused to confess, when I said nothing. Waxed old means wasted away. Roaring means groaning. Keeping his sin secret made David feel ill, worn out, miserable. 'For day and night thy hand was heavy upon me: my moisture is turned into the drought of summer. Selah.' God’s hand was pressing on him, not to hurt him, but to bring him back. He felt dried up, like a field in a hot, dry summer.

Hidden sin is like that. It eats away at us. It makes us heavy, tired and unhappy.

And then David did the one thing that could help.

David stopped hiding. He told God everything. And God forgave him. As soon as he confessed, God forgave.

'For this shall every one that is godly pray unto thee in a time when thou mayest be found'. David says, everyone should do the same. Come to God while there is time. 'surely in the floods of great waters they shall not come nigh unto him.' Even when troubles rise like a flood, they will not sweep away the one who has come to God.

Then David speaks to God with joy. 'Thou art my hiding place; thou shalt preserve me from trouble; thou shalt compass me about with songs of deliverance. Selah.' Before, David was hiding from God. Now, God is his hiding place. That is a wonderful change. Instead of hiding his sin, he hides in God. And God surrounds him with songs, songs of being rescued.

Then, it seems, God himself speaks, with a promise.

'Be ye not as the horse, or as the mule, which have no understanding: whose mouth must be held in with bit and bridle, lest they come near unto thee.' A horse or a mule has to be controlled with a metal bit in its mouth and straps around its head, or it will not go where you want it to. God says, do not be stubborn like that. Come willingly, and let me guide you gently.

'Many sorrows shall be to the wicked: but he that trusteth in the LORD, mercy shall compass him about.' Compass means surround. God’s mercy will surround the one who trusts him, on every side.

And the psalm ends with a call to joy. 'Be glad in the LORD, and rejoice, ye righteous: and shout for joy, all ye that are upright in heart.' The one who has been forgiven has every reason to shout for joy.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 1. 'Blessed is he whose transgression is forgiven, whose sin is covered.' The happiest person in the world is not the richest, or the most famous, or the most popular. It is the person whose sins are forgiven. Covered means hidden from God’s sight, as if a blanket has been laid over them. For Christians, it is Jesus’ death on the cross that covers our sin. Our sins are not just hidden. They are paid for, and taken away.

Verse 5. 'I acknowledged my sin unto thee, and mine iniquity have I not hid. I said, I will confess my transgressions unto the LORD; and thou forgavest the iniquity of my sin. Selah.' To acknowledge means to admit, to own up. To confess means to agree with God about what we have done, and say it out loud to him. Notice how quickly forgiveness came. I said, I will confess, and thou forgavest. God is not waiting to punish us. He is waiting to forgive us. All we have to do is stop hiding, and come.

Verse 8. 'I will instruct thee and teach thee in the way which thou shalt go: I will guide thee with mine eye.' To guide with the eye is a beautiful picture. Think of a parent who only has to look at their child, and the child knows what to do. No shouting, no pulling. Just a look. That is how close God wants to be to us. Close enough to guide us with a glance. When we are forgiven, God does not just wipe away the past. He walks with us into the future.

Paul wrote, 'Blessed are they whose iniquities are forgiven, and whose sins are covered.' And John wrote, 'If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.'

Is there anything you have been hiding from God? Something you have done wrong, and kept silent about? Do not carry that heavy weight any longer. Tell God about it honestly today, and receive his forgiveness. And if you have hurt someone, think about how you could put it right.

Here is today’s question. David says that when he kept silent about his sin, he felt heavy and dried up. Why do you think hiding our wrongs makes us feel so bad, and confessing them makes us feel free?

Let us pray. Lord God, thank you that blessed is the one whose sin is forgiven, and whose sin is covered. Help me never to hide my sin from you. I confess my wrongs to you now, and I thank you that you forgive. Thank you that you are my hiding place. Instruct me and teach me in the way I should go, and guide me with your eye. Amen.
