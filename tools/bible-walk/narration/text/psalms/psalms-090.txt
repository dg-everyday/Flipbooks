The Living Voice. Psalm 90. So Teach Us to Number Our Days.

An old man sits beside the tent door, looking out over the wilderness. His hair is white. His face is lined. He has led his people through the desert for many long years. And one by one, he has watched a whole generation grow old and die. As he looks at the mountains, he thinks about how short life is. And about the God who was there before the mountains were made. His name is Moses. And Psalm ninety is his prayer.

Before this, in Psalm thirty seven, an old David said, fret not thyself because of evildoers. Delight thyself also in the LORD.

Now, with more wisdom for life, we jump ahead to Psalm ninety, the twenty first of our thirty favourite psalms, which are grouped by theme, not in number order. It is the only psalm written by Moses, and it may be the oldest psalm of all. Listen for how big God is, compared with us. And listen for what Moses asks God to teach us.

Psalm ninety is called a prayer of Moses the man of God. We have walked through Moses’ story in the book of Exodus. The baby in the basket on the river Nile. The burning bush. The plagues on Egypt. The crossing of the Red Sea. The giving of the law at Mount Sinai. Then, because the people refused to trust God, they had to wander in the wilderness, until that whole generation had passed away. Moses watched it happen. And perhaps that is why this psalm is so full of thoughts about how short life is.

It begins with God.

A dwelling place is a home. Through all generations, from Abraham, Isaac and Jacob, all the way to Moses, God has been the true home of his people. They had no land of their own. They lived in tents, and wandered from place to place. But God was their home.

Then Moses compares God with people. 'Thou turnest man to destruction; and sayest, Return, ye children of men.' God tells people to return to the dust they were made from. Do you remember what God said to Adam? Dust thou art, and unto dust shalt thou return.

'For a thousand years in thy sight are but as yesterday when it is past, and as a watch in the night.' To us, a thousand years is an enormous amount of time. To God, it is like a day that has just gone by. Or like a few hours of a guard’s watch in the dark, gone before you know it. God is not bound by time as we are.

Then Moses uses a picture we have heard before. 'Thou carriest them away as with a flood; they are as a sleep: in the morning they are like grass which groweth up. In the morning it flourisheth, and groweth up; in the evening it is cut down, and withereth.' Like grass in the hot desert, green and fresh one moment, dry and cut down the next. That is how short our lives are.

Then Moses becomes very serious. 'Thou hast set our iniquities before thee, our secret sins in the light of thy countenance.' Countenance means face. Our sins, even the secret ones, are in the full light of God’s face. Nothing is hidden. And it is because of sin that life is short, and full of trouble. Moses knew that the generation in the wilderness had died because they rebelled against God.

'we spend our years as a tale that is told.' Our lives pass like a story told around the fire. It is told, and then it is over.

'The days of our years are threescore years and ten; and if by reason of strength they be fourscore years, yet is their strength labour and sorrow; for it is soon cut off, and we fly away.' Threescore years and ten means seventy years. Fourscore means eighty. Even a long life is full of hard work and sorrow, and it passes so quickly. We fly away, like a bird.

So what should we do, knowing that life is short? Moses prays.

Then Moses asks God to come back to his people, and show them kindness. 'Return, O LORD, how long? and let it repent thee concerning thy servants.' Let it repent thee means have pity, change your hand from judgment to mercy.

'O satisfy us early with thy mercy; that we may rejoice and be glad all our days.' If God fills us with his mercy at the start of our lives, we can be glad all through them.

'Make us glad according to the days wherein thou hast afflicted us'. As many hard days as we have had, give us that many glad ones too.

'Let thy work appear unto thy servants, and thy glory unto their children.' Moses wanted the next generation, the children who would go into the promised land, to see God’s glory.

And then the psalm ends with a prayer about our work.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 2. 'Before the mountains were brought forth, or ever thou hadst formed the earth and the world, even from everlasting to everlasting, thou art God.' The mountains seem so old, and so permanent. But God was there before them. From everlasting means with no beginning. To everlasting means with no end. God has always been, and always will be. Everything else changes. He never does. And because he is everlasting, he can be a home for every generation.

Verse 12. 'So teach us to number our days, that we may apply our hearts unto wisdom.' To number our days means to count them, to realise that they are limited. We do not have endless time. This is not meant to make us sad or afraid. It is meant to make us wise. When we know our days are numbered, we use them well. We love God, love others, and do what really matters, instead of wasting our lives on things that do not last.

Verse 17. 'And let the beauty of the LORD our God be upon us: and establish thou the work of our hands upon us; yea, the work of our hands establish thou it.' Establish means make it last, make it firm. Our lives are short, like grass. But when God blesses what we do, it can last. The good we do, the kindness we show, the people we help to know God, can last for ever. Moses asks twice, because he wants it so much. Lord, make our work count.

Peter wrote, 'one day is with the Lord as a thousand years, and a thousand years as one day.' And Jesus said, 'Lay up for yourselves treasures in heaven, where neither moth nor rust doth corrupt'.

Think about your days today. How could you use this one day wisely? Pray Moses’ prayer: teach me to number my days. And ask God to establish the work of your hands, to make what you do today count for him.

Here is today’s question. Moses asks God to teach us to number our days, that we may apply our hearts unto wisdom. If you remembered every day that life is short, what might you do differently?

Let us pray. LORD, you have been our dwelling place in all generations. From everlasting to everlasting, you are God. Our lives are short, like grass. So teach us to number our days, that we may apply our hearts unto wisdom. Satisfy us with your mercy, that we may rejoice and be glad all our days. Let your beauty be upon us, and establish the work of our hands. Amen.
