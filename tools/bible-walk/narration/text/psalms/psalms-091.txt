The Living Voice. Psalm 91. Under the Shadow of the Almighty.

Dark clouds gather over a farmyard. The wind picks up, and the first heavy drops of rain begin to fall. A mother hen gives a sharp cluck, and her little yellow chicks come running from every corner of the yard. She spreads out her wings, and they tuck themselves underneath, out of the wind and the rain. The storm rages. But under her wings, they are warm, and dry, and safe.

Before this, in Psalm one hundred and twenty one, pilgrims lifted up their eyes unto the hills. My help cometh from the LORD, which made heaven and earth. He that keepeth Israel shall neither slumber nor sleep.

Now, still trusting God, we go back to Psalm ninety one, the ninth of our thirty favourite psalms, which are grouped by theme, not in number order. Here we hear about God as a shelter and a hiding place. Listen for the pictures of protection. And listen at the end, because God himself begins to speak.

We do not know for certain who wrote Psalm ninety one. But it is one of the most comforting psalms in the whole Bible. People have prayed it in times of sickness, in war, in storms, and in every kind of danger.

It begins with a picture of a hiding place.

Then the writer says, 'I will say of the LORD, He is my refuge and my fortress: my God; in him will I trust.' A refuge is a safe place to run to. A fortress is a strong castle with high walls. The writer does not just know these things about God. He says them out loud. He is my refuge. My God.

Then he begins to speak to the listener, to you. 'Surely he shall deliver thee from the snare of the fowler, and from the noisome pestilence.' A fowler was a bird catcher, who set hidden traps, called snares, to catch birds. The snare of the fowler means hidden dangers, traps we cannot see. Noisome pestilence means a deadly disease. God can rescue us from dangers we see, and dangers we do not see.

And then a tender picture, like the mother hen.

Then the psalm lists all kinds of fears. 'Thou shalt not be afraid for the terror by night; nor for the arrow that flieth by day; Nor for the pestilence that walketh in darkness; nor for the destruction that wasteth at noonday.' Dangers in the dark, and dangers in broad daylight. Arrows in battle, and sickness spreading silently. Whatever the fear, God is greater.

'A thousand shall fall at thy side, and ten thousand at thy right hand; but it shall not come nigh thee.' Like a soldier in a battle where many fall all around, but he is kept safe.

'For he shall give his angels charge over thee, to keep thee in all thy ways. They shall bear thee up in their hands, lest thou dash thy foot against a stone.' Charge means orders. God commands his angels to watch over his people, and carry them, like a parent lifting a small child over a rough path.

And here is something important. These words were once quoted to Jesus, by the tempter. When Jesus was in the wilderness, the devil took him to the highest point of the temple in Jerusalem, and said, if you are the Son of God, throw yourself down. For it is written, he shall give his angels charge over thee. But Jesus replied, it is written again, thou shalt not tempt the Lord thy God. In other words, this psalm is not a promise we can use to do foolish, dangerous things, and expect God to rescue us. It is a promise for those who trust God and walk in his ways.

'Thou shalt tread upon the lion and adder: the young lion and the dragon shalt thou trample under feet.' An adder is a poisonous snake. The dragon here means a great serpent. Even the fiercest dangers will be under the feet of God’s people.

And then, at the end, the voice changes. God himself speaks. 'Because he hath set his love upon me, therefore will I deliver him: I will set him on high, because he hath known my name.' God says, because this person loves me, and knows me, I will rescue him, and lift him up to a safe, high place.

And then God makes a great promise.

'With long life will I satisfy him, and shew him my salvation.'

Does this psalm mean that nothing bad will ever happen to people who trust God? No. The Bible tells of many faithful people who suffered, and even died for their faith. Jesus himself suffered on the cross. But this psalm tells us that God is our shelter, and that nothing can touch us outside his care. Notice that God says, I will be with him in trouble. Not, he will never have trouble. And in the end, God will bring his people safely through every danger, into his eternal salvation.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 1. 'He that dwelleth in the secret place of the most High shall abide under the shadow of the Almighty.' Dwelleth means lives. Abide means stays. The secret place is a hidden, private place, close to God. The person who makes God their home, who stays close to him day by day, lives under his shadow, his protection. Like standing in the shade of a great rock in the desert. The closer we stay to God, the safer we are.

Verse 4. 'He shall cover thee with his feathers, and under his wings shalt thou trust: his truth shall be thy shield and buckler.' God is like a mother bird spreading her wings over her chicks. Jesus used the same picture when he wept over Jerusalem. How often would I have gathered thy children together, he said, even as a hen gathereth her chickens under her wings. A buckler is a small shield. God’s truth, his faithfulness, his promises, protect us like a shield.

Verse 15. 'He shall call upon me, and I will answer him: I will be with him in trouble; I will deliver him, and honour him.' Four promises. I will answer. I will be with him. I will deliver him. I will honour him. Notice especially, I will be with him in trouble. God does not promise we will never have trouble. But he promises he will be right there with us in it. We are never alone.

Jesus said over Jerusalem, 'how often would I have gathered thy children together, even as a hen gathereth her chickens under her wings'. And to the tempter he said, 'It is written again, Thou shalt not tempt the Lord thy God.'

What are you afraid of today? Name it to God. Then say, as the psalm says, he is my refuge and my fortress: my God; in him will I trust. And remember that whatever happens, God promises, I will be with him in trouble.

Here is today’s question. God says, I will be with him in trouble, not, he will never have trouble. Why do you think that difference matters?

Let us pray. Lord, you are my refuge and my fortress, my God, in whom I trust. Cover me with your feathers, and let me hide under your wings. When I am afraid, remind me that you are greater than every danger. Thank you that when I call upon you, you will answer, and that you will be with me in trouble. Help me to dwell in your secret place, close to you. Amen.

Next on the walk, still trusting God, we go back to Psalm forty six. Though the earth be removed, and the mountains be carried into the sea, God is our refuge and strength. Be still, and know that I am God.
