The Living Voice. Psalm 103. Forget Not All His Benefits.

A girl sits down with a pencil and a piece of paper. She is feeling grumpy. Nothing has gone right. But her grandmother has given her a challenge. Write down every good thing you can think of that God has given you. She starts slowly. My family. My home. Food. Then she keeps going. Friends. Sunshine. Being forgiven. Before long, she has filled the whole page, and turned it over. And she is not grumpy any more. Psalm one hundred and three is a list like that.

Before this, in Psalm thirty four, David escaped from danger, and sang, I will bless the LORD at all times. O taste and see that the LORD is good.

Now, with more thanksgiving, we jump ahead to Psalm one hundred and three, the twenty third of our thirty favourite psalms, which are grouped by theme, not in number order. Here David counts his blessings. Listen for the list of things God does for us. And listen for three beautiful pictures of how great God’s mercy is.

Psalm one hundred and three is a psalm of David. It does not ask God for anything at all. It is pure praise, from beginning to end.

It begins with David speaking to his own soul. 'Bless the LORD, O my soul: and all that is within me, bless his holy name.' All that is within me. Every part of me. My mind, my heart, my feelings, my will. All of me, praise God.

Then he tells his soul something else.

And then David lists the benefits, the good gifts God gives. 'Who forgiveth all thine iniquities; who healeth all thy diseases; Who redeemeth thy life from destruction; who crowneth thee with lovingkindness and tender mercies; Who satisfieth thy mouth with good things; so that thy youth is renewed like the eagle’s.'

Notice where the list begins. With forgiveness. That is the greatest gift of all. Then healing. Then rescue from destruction. Then a crown, not of gold, but of lovingkindness and tender mercies. And then good things to satisfy us, so that we feel young and strong again, like an eagle soaring high in the sky.

Then David remembers what God told Moses. 'He made known his ways unto Moses, his acts unto the children of Israel. The LORD is merciful and gracious, slow to anger, and plenteous in mercy.' Do you remember, in the book of Exodus, when Moses asked to see God’s glory? God passed by, and proclaimed his own name. The LORD, The LORD God, merciful and gracious, longsuffering, and abundant in goodness and truth. David echoes those words.

'He will not always chide: neither will he keep his anger for ever.' Chide means scold. God does not keep on scolding us, or stay angry for ever. 'He hath not dealt with us after our sins; nor rewarded us according to our iniquities.' God does not give us what we deserve. If he did, we would be in terrible trouble. Instead, he gives us mercy.

Then David uses three wonderful pictures to show how great God’s mercy is.

The first picture looks up. 'For as the heaven is high above the earth, so great is his mercy toward them that fear him.' How high is the sky? Go up and up, past the clouds, past the moon, past the stars, and you will never reach the top. That is how great God’s mercy is.

The second picture looks across, from east to west.

The third picture is a family.

Then David thinks about how short our lives are. 'As for man, his days are as grass: as a flower of the field, so he flourisheth. For the wind passeth over it, and it is gone'. We heard the same picture from Moses in Psalm ninety. A wild flower blooms beautifully, then a hot wind blows across the field, and it is gone.

But then comes a great word. But. 'But the mercy of the LORD is from everlasting to everlasting upon them that fear him, and his righteousness unto children’s children'. We are like grass, but God’s mercy lasts for ever. It goes on, from parents to children, to grandchildren, and on and on.

And the psalm ends with a great choir of praise, growing bigger and bigger. 'The LORD hath prepared his throne in the heavens; and his kingdom ruleth over all.' God is King over everything.

'Bless the LORD, ye his angels, that excel in strength, that do his commandments'. David calls on the mighty angels to praise God. 'Bless ye the LORD, all ye his hosts'. All the armies of heaven. 'Bless the LORD, all his works in all places of his dominion'. Everything God has made, everywhere.

And then, at the very end, David comes back to where he started, to himself. 'bless the LORD, O my soul.' The angels are praising God. All creation is praising God. And David says, I want to join in too.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 2. 'Bless the LORD, O my soul, and forget not all his benefits'. Benefits means good gifts, kindnesses. Why would David need to tell himself not to forget? Because we forget so easily. We remember our problems, and forget our blessings. We notice what we do not have, and forget what we do. Counting our blessings, one by one, turns grumbling into gratitude, and worry into worship.

Verse 12. 'As far as the east is from the west, so far hath he removed our transgressions from us.' If you travel north, eventually you reach the North Pole, and start going south. But if you travel east, you can keep going east for ever, and never start going west. East and west never meet. That is how far God removes our sins when he forgives us. Not a little way, where they can catch up with us. Completely. For ever.

Verse 13. 'Like as a father pitieth his children, so the LORD pitieth them that fear him.' Pitieth means has tender compassion on. Think of a loving father when his child falls and scrapes her knee. He does not shout at her. He picks her up, and comforts her. The next verse tells us why God is so gentle. 'For he knoweth our frame; he remembereth that we are dust.' God knows how weak we are. He made us. And he treats us with tender care.

God proclaimed to Moses, 'The LORD, The LORD God, merciful and gracious, longsuffering, and abundant in goodness and truth'. And Jesus said, 'If ye then, being evil, know how to give good gifts unto your children, how much more shall your Father which is in heaven give good things to them that ask him?'

Take a piece of paper today, and write down as many of God’s benefits as you can think of. Do not stop until you have at least ten. Then read your list back to God, and say, bless the LORD, O my soul.

Here is today’s question. David tells his soul, forget not all his benefits. Why do you think it is so easy to forget God’s blessings, and what could help you remember them?

Let us pray. Bless the LORD, O my soul, and all that is within me, bless his holy name. I will not forget all your benefits. Thank you for forgiving all my sins, and crowning me with lovingkindness and tender mercies. Thank you that you have removed my sins as far as the east is from the west. Thank you that, like a father, you pity your children, and remember that we are dust. Bless the LORD, O my soul. Amen.

Next on the walk, more thanksgiving, in Psalm one hundred and seven. Wanderers, prisoners, the sick and sailors in a storm all cry to the LORD in their trouble. And every time, he saves them.
