The Living Voice. Psalm 121. My Help Cometh from the LORD.

A family sets out on a long walk to Jerusalem, for one of the great feasts. The road winds up through the hills. It is hot, and steep, and dangerous. Robbers sometimes hide among the rocks. Wild animals live in the caves. The children are tired. And as they look up at the hills ahead, the father begins to sing. I will lift up mine eyes unto the hills. And the whole family joins in.

Before this, in Psalm twenty three, we heard, the LORD is my shepherd; I shall not want. He makes me lie down in green pastures, leads me beside the still waters, and restores my soul. Though I walk through the valley of the shadow of death, I will fear no evil, for thou art with me. And goodness and mercy shall follow me all the days of my life.

Now, still trusting God, we jump ahead to Psalm one hundred and twenty one, the eighth of our thirty favourite psalms, which are grouped by theme, not in number order. Here we join pilgrims on the road. Listen for where our help comes from. And listen for how often the psalm uses the words keep and preserve.

Psalm one hundred and twenty one is called a song of degrees. There are fifteen songs of degrees in the book of Psalms, one after another. Degrees means going up, ascending. Many think these were songs that pilgrims sang as they walked up to Jerusalem, which was built on a hill, for the great feasts. As families travelled together, they sang these songs along the way.

And this one begins with a question.

'I will lift up mine eyes unto the hills, from whence cometh my help.' The pilgrims look up at the hills. Perhaps they are thinking, those hills are dangerous, where will help come from if we are attacked? Or perhaps they are looking towards the hills of Jerusalem, where the temple stood. Either way, the question is, where does my help come from?

And the answer comes straight away.

Not from the hills. Not from anything in creation. But from the one who made the hills, and everything else. The God who made heaven and earth is the one who helps us.

Then the psalm makes a series of promises. And it seems as though someone else answers the pilgrim, perhaps a priest, or a fellow traveller, or the father to his children. You, they say.

'He will not suffer thy foot to be moved'. He will not let your foot slip. On a steep, rocky path, a slip could be very dangerous. But God will keep you steady.

'he that keepeth thee will not slumber.' Slumber means to doze, to fall asleep. Even the best human guard eventually gets tired, and nods off. But God never does.

'Behold, he that keepeth Israel shall neither slumber nor sleep.' God has been watching over his people for generations, through every danger, and he has never once fallen asleep on the job.

'The LORD is thy keeper: the LORD is thy shade upon thy right hand.' On a hot, dusty road under the burning sun, shade is precious. God is like a shady tree, or a cool shadow, right beside you, protecting you.

'The sun shall not smite thee by day, nor the moon by night.' Smite means strike, harm. The sun could cause sunstroke in the heat. And people in those days feared that the moon could bring harm too. At every hour, the psalm says, God protects you.

'The LORD shall preserve thee from all evil: he shall preserve thy soul.' Preserve means keep safe, guard. God watches over your whole life, your very soul.

And then the last promise, which stretches out for ever.

Count the words keep and preserve in this psalm. He that keepeth thee. He that keepeth Israel. The LORD is thy keeper. The LORD shall preserve thee. He shall preserve thy soul. The LORD shall preserve thy going out and thy coming in. Six times. This psalm is all about a God who keeps and guards his people.

That does not mean nothing bad will ever happen to God’s people. The Bible is full of faithful people who suffered. But it means that God is always watching, always caring, never asleep. And nothing can happen to us that is outside his loving care. In the end, he will bring us safely home.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 2. 'My help cometh from the LORD, which made heaven and earth.' When we need help, we often look to other things: money, friends, our own strength, clever plans. Those things may help a little. But real help comes from the LORD, who made heaven and earth. If he made everything, then nothing is too big for him. The Creator of the universe is the one who helps you.

Verse 3. 'He will not suffer thy foot to be moved: he that keepeth thee will not slumber.' Suffer here means allow. God will not let your foot slip. And he never sleeps. Think of a parent watching over a sick child all through the hours of darkness. God watches over us like that, all the time, without ever growing tired. You can sleep peacefully, because he is awake.

Verse 8. 'The LORD shall preserve thy going out and thy coming in from this time forth, and even for evermore.' Thy going out and thy coming in means everything you do: leaving home in the day, coming back later, starting things and finishing them, every journey, every task. God watches over all of it. And not just for today, but from this time forth, and even for evermore. His care never ends.

Jesus said, 'Are not two sparrows sold for a farthing? and one of them shall not fall on the ground without your Father.' And Isaiah wrote, 'the everlasting God, the LORD, the Creator of the ends of the earth, fainteth not, neither is weary'.

When you go out today, and when you come in, remember that God is watching over you. Pray this psalm as you leave home. And if you are worried about something, lift up your eyes, and remember that your help comes from the LORD, who made heaven and earth.

Here is today’s question. The psalm says God neither slumbers nor sleeps. How does it help you to know that God is always awake, always watching over you?

Let us pray. Lord God, I lift up my eyes. My help comes from you, who made heaven and earth. Thank you that you will not let my foot slip, and that you never slumber or sleep. Be my keeper and my shade. Preserve me from all evil, and keep my soul. Watch over my going out and my coming in, from this time forth, and for evermore. Amen.

Next on the walk, still trusting God, we go back to Psalm ninety one. He that dwelleth in the secret place of the most High shall abide under the shadow of the Almighty. Under his wings, we find refuge.
