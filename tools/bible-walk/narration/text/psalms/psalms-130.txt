The Living Voice. Psalm 130. Out of the Depths.

A city lies quiet in the dark. Up on the high walls, a watchman paces back and forth. He has been there for hours. He is cold, and tired, and his eyes ache. Every so often, he looks towards the east, hoping to see the first grey light on the hills. Because when the sun comes up, his long watch will be over. He waits, and waits. Psalm one hundred and thirty says that our souls should wait for God even more eagerly than that watchman waits for the light.

Before this, in Psalm fifty one, after the prophet Nathan came to him, David prayed, have mercy upon me, O God, according to thy lovingkindness. Wash me, and I shall be whiter than snow. Create in me a clean heart, O God. And he learned that a broken and a contrite heart, God will not despise.

Now we jump ahead to Psalm one hundred and thirty, the nineteenth of our thirty favourite psalms, which are grouped by theme, not in number order. It is the last of our psalms about forgiveness. Someone cries to God from the very bottom of their trouble. Listen for the question that has only one honest answer. And listen for the wonderful word but.

Psalm one hundred and thirty is another song of degrees, one of the songs that pilgrims sang as they went up to Jerusalem. We heard one earlier, Psalm one hundred and twenty one, I will lift up mine eyes unto the hills. This one is a much more serious song. It is short, only eight verses. But it is one of the deepest psalms in the Bible.

It begins with a cry.

Then he says, 'Lord, hear my voice: let thine ears be attentive to the voice of my supplications.' Attentive means listening carefully. Supplications means humble, earnest requests. Please, Lord, listen.

What are the depths he is crying from? The depths can mean the bottom of a deep pit, or the bottom of the sea. Do you remember Jonah? When he was thrown overboard, and swallowed by a great fish, he prayed from the depths of the sea. Out of the belly of hell cried I, he said, and thou heardest my voice. The depths are the lowest place you can be. When everything has gone wrong, and you feel you are sinking, with no way out.

And what has put this writer in the depths? It seems to be his own sin. Because the next thing he says is about iniquities.

Imagine if God kept a record book, and wrote down every single wrong thing we had ever done. Every unkind word. Every selfish thought. Every lie. Every time we disobeyed. And then, at the end, he read it all out, and judged us by it. Who could stand? Who could say, I am innocent? The honest answer is, no one. Not one of us.

That is the depths. Knowing that we cannot stand before God by ourselves.

And then the psalm’s most beautiful word. But.

This is the turning point of the whole psalm. God does not keep a record of our sins against us, when we come to him. There is forgiveness.

And then the psalm turns to waiting. 'I wait for the LORD, my soul doth wait, and in his word do I hope.' The writer has cried out. He knows God forgives. Now he waits for God, trusting in God’s word, his promises.

And how does he wait?

Then the writer turns from himself to all of God’s people. 'Let Israel hope in the LORD: for with the LORD there is mercy, and with him is plenteous redemption.' Plenteous means plenty, more than enough. Redemption means buying back, setting free. God’s redemption is not small or limited. There is plenty of it, enough for everyone who comes.

And the last verse makes a great promise. 'And he shall redeem Israel from all his iniquities.' Not some of them. All of them.

When the angel told Joseph about the baby Mary would have, he said, thou shalt call his name Jesus: for he shall save his people from their sins. The name Jesus means the LORD saves. Psalm one hundred and thirty looked forward to the day when God would redeem his people from all their iniquities. And Jesus came to do exactly that.

So the psalm moves from the depths, to forgiveness, to waiting, to hope. It is a journey many people have made. Perhaps you will make it too.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 3. 'If thou, LORD, shouldest mark iniquities, O Lord, who shall stand?' To mark means to keep track of, to write down and remember. If God kept score of every sin, no one could stand before him. Not the best person you know. Not the greatest saint. Not you, and not me. This verse is very honest. It tells us we cannot get to God by being good enough. We all need forgiveness.

Verse 4. 'But there is forgiveness with thee, that thou mayest be feared.' What a wonderful word, but. There is forgiveness with God. It is part of who he is. And feared here does not mean being terrified of God. It means honouring him, worshipping him, loving him with awe. When we discover that God forgives us, we do not take it lightly. It makes us love him, and want to follow him. Forgiveness does not make us careless. It makes us grateful.

Verse 6. 'My soul waiteth for the Lord more than they that watch for the morning: I say, more than they that watch for the morning.' The watchmen on the city walls stayed awake through the dark hours, watching for danger, and longing for the first light. They knew it would come. It always does. The writer says, I wait for God even more eagerly than that. And just as surely as the sun rises, God will come. He says it twice, because he means it.

Jonah prayed, 'out of the belly of hell cried I, and thou heardest my voice.' And the angel said, 'thou shalt call his name JESUS: for he shall save his people from their sins.'

If you feel that you are in the depths, cry out to God. He is listening. And if you know you have done wrong, remember the word but. There is forgiveness with God. Then wait for him, with hope.

Here is today’s question. The psalm asks, if God kept a record of every sin, who could stand? Why is it such good news that there is forgiveness with God?

Let us pray. Out of the depths I cry to you, O LORD. Lord, hear my voice. If you kept a record of my sins, I could not stand. But thank you that there is forgiveness with you. My soul waits for you, more than the watchman waits for the light. With you there is mercy, and plenteous redemption. Thank you for Jesus, who saves his people from their sins. Amen.
