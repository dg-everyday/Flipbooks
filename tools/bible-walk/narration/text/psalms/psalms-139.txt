The Living Voice. Psalm 139. Fearfully and Wonderfully Made.

Have you ever felt that nobody really understands you? That people only see the outside, and not what you are really thinking or feeling inside? Psalm one hundred and thirty nine says there is someone who knows you completely. Every thought. Every word. Every step. He knew you before you were born. And knowing everything about you, he still loves you.

Before this, in Psalm sixty two, David said, truly my soul waiteth upon God. He only is my rock and my salvation; I shall not be moved. He told his own soul to wait only upon God. And he invited everyone to trust in him at all times, and pour out your heart before him, for God is a refuge for us.

Now we jump ahead to Psalm one hundred and thirty nine, the thirteenth of our thirty favourite psalms, which are grouped by theme, not in number order. It is about the God who knows us completely, and it is one of the most personal psalms in the Bible. It has four parts. God knows me. God is with me everywhere. God made me. And, search me. Listen for each one.

Psalm one hundred and thirty nine is a psalm of David. And it begins with God knowing him.

Then David explains how much God knows. 'Thou knowest my downsitting and mine uprising, thou understandest my thought afar off.' My downsitting and mine uprising means every time I sit down, and every time I get up. Everything I do, all day long. And God understands my thoughts, even from far away.

'Thou compassest my path and my lying down, and art acquainted with all my ways.' Compassest means surround, keep watch over. Acquainted means familiar with. God knows all our habits, every path we take.

'For there is not a word in my tongue, but, lo, O LORD, thou knowest it altogether.' Before I even say a word, God already knows what I am going to say.

'Thou hast beset me behind and before, and laid thine hand upon me.' Beset means surrounded. God is behind me and in front of me, and his hand is on me, like a parent’s hand gently resting on a child’s shoulder.

And David is overwhelmed. 'Such knowledge is too wonderful for me; it is high, I cannot attain unto it.' I cannot understand it, David says. It is too big for me.

Then the second part. God is everywhere. 'Whither shall I go from thy spirit? or whither shall I flee from thy presence?' Whither means where. Where could I go to get away from God?

'If I ascend up into heaven, thou art there: if I make my bed in hell, behold, thou art there.' Hell here means the grave, the place of the dead, the deepest place David can imagine. From the highest place to the lowest place, God is there.

'If I take the wings of the morning, and dwell in the uttermost parts of the sea; Even there shall thy hand lead me, and thy right hand shall hold me.' This is a beautiful picture. As the sun rises in the east, its light flies across the sky to the far west, where the sea was for David. Even if I could fly as fast as light, to the farthest ocean, your hand would be there to lead me and hold me.

'If I say, Surely the darkness shall cover me; even the night shall be light about me.' Perhaps I could hide in the dark? No. 'the darkness and the light are both alike to thee.' To God, darkness is as bright as day.

This could sound frightening, if God were a policeman. But David does not feel trapped. He feels held. Wherever I go, God’s hand is holding me.

Then the third part. God made me. David says God formed him and covered him before he was born. And then.

'My substance was not hid from thee, when I was made in secret'. My substance means my body. Even before anyone else could see me, God could. 'and in thy book all my members were written'. Members means body parts. Before you were born, God planned every part of you, and every one of your days.

'How precious also are thy thoughts unto me, O God! how great is the sum of them! If I should count them, they are more in number than the sand'. God thinks about us more times than there are grains of sand on the seashore.

Near the end, David speaks some strong words about the wicked, who speak against God and take his name in vain. He feels grieved, and even angry, that people would hate the God he loves. David brings even those hard feelings to God. And then, instead of looking at others, he turns and looks at himself.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 1. 'O LORD, thou hast searched me, and known me.' To search means to look carefully, as you would search a room for something lost. God has looked into every corner of David’s life, and he knows him completely. That might feel scary. But think of it. God knows the worst things about you, and he still loves you. You never have to pretend with God. He already knows.

Verse 14. 'I will praise thee; for I am fearfully and wonderfully made: marvellous are thy works; and that my soul knoweth right well.' Fearfully means in an awesome way, that makes us gasp with wonder. Think of your eyes, your heartbeat, your brain, your fingerprints, which are different from everyone else’s in the whole world. You are not an accident. You were made by God, carefully and wonderfully. Whatever you look like, whatever others say, you are one of God’s marvellous works.

Verse 23. 'Search me, O God, and know my heart: try me, and know my thoughts'. At the start, David said, thou hast searched me. Now he asks, search me. He invites God to look deep inside, and show him anything that is wrong. And the next verse finishes the prayer, 'And see if there be any wicked way in me, and lead me in the way everlasting.' David does not just want God to find the wrong. He wants God to lead him in the right way, for ever.

Jesus said, 'But the very hairs of your head are all numbered.' And Jeremiah heard God say, 'Before I formed thee in the belly I knew thee'.

Look in a mirror today, and say, I am fearfully and wonderfully made. Thank God for one thing about the way he made you. And pray David’s prayer: search me, O God, and know my heart.

Here is today’s question. God knows everything about you, the good and the bad, and still loves you. How does that change the way you think about yourself?

Let us pray. O LORD, you have searched me, and known me. You know when I sit down and when I get up, and every word before I say it. Wherever I go, your hand holds me. Thank you that I am fearfully and wonderfully made. Search me, O God, and know my heart. See if there be any wicked way in me, and lead me in the way everlasting. Amen.
