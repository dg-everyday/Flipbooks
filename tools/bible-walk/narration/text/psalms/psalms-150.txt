The Living Voice. Psalm 150. Let Every Thing That Hath Breath Praise the LORD.

At the end of a great concert, the music builds and builds. First the trumpets ring out. Then the strings join in, the harps and the violins. The drums begin to beat. The cymbals crash. The whole orchestra is playing, as loud as it can, and the audience is on its feet, clapping and cheering. That is the grand finale, the big ending that brings everything together. Psalm one hundred and fifty is the grand finale of the whole book of Psalms.

In Psalm one hundred and forty five, David’s last psalm of praise, he said, great is the LORD, and greatly to be praised; and his greatness is unsearchable. The LORD is gracious, and full of compassion. The LORD upholdeth all that fall. And the LORD is nigh unto all them that call upon him in truth. Let all flesh bless his holy name.

Now we come to the last psalm of all, and the last episode of our walk through the Psalms. Listen for the word praise. How many times can you count it? And listen for who is invited to join in.

The book of Psalms is divided into five smaller books. And at the end of each one, there is a burst of praise, a blessing of God. Then, at the very end of the whole book, come five psalms, one hundred and forty six to one hundred and fifty, that each begin and end with the same words. Praise ye the LORD. In Hebrew, that is one word, Hallelujah.

And Psalm one hundred and fifty is the last and loudest of them all. It is only six verses long. But in those six verses, the word praise appears thirteen times. It is pure praise, nothing else.

The psalm answers four questions about praise. Where? Why? How? And who?

First, where should we praise God?

The sanctuary is God’s holy place, the temple, where his people gathered to worship. The firmament is the great sky above. So praise God on earth, in his house. And praise him in heaven, in the vast sky that shows his power. Praise him everywhere, from the temple to the stars.

Second, why should we praise God?

Third, how should we praise God? And here the psalm brings out the whole orchestra.

'Praise him with the sound of the trumpet: praise him with the psaltery and harp.' The trumpet was a ram’s horn, blown by the priests at great moments. The psaltery and harp were stringed instruments, plucked with the fingers.

'Praise him with the timbrel and dance: praise him with stringed instruments and organs.' A timbrel was a small hand drum, like a tambourine. Do you remember Miriam, Moses’ sister? After God brought Israel through the Red Sea, she took a timbrel in her hand, and all the women danced, and sang to the LORD. Organs here does not mean the large organs we know. It means pipes, or flutes, wind instruments that are blown.

'Praise him upon the loud cymbals: praise him upon the high sounding cymbals.' Cymbals are crashed together, loud and clanging.

When King David brought the ark of God up to Jerusalem, the first book of Chronicles tells us that he appointed musicians to play on psalteries and harps, and cymbals, and trumpets, and to praise the LORD. Every kind of instrument, all together, praising God.

And then, the fourth question. Who should praise God?

And the very last words of the whole book are, 'Praise ye the LORD.' Hallelujah.

Think back over our walk through the Psalms. We began with Psalm one, the tree planted by the rivers of water. We heard the heavens declare the glory of God, and God’s Word, a lamp unto our feet. We met the LORD our shepherd, our help, our refuge, our rock. We heard that God knows us completely, and that we are fearfully and wonderfully made.

Then we walked through the dark valleys. How long, O LORD? Why art thou cast down, O my soul? My God, my God, why hast thou forsaken me? We heard David confess his sins, and pray, create in me a clean heart, O God. We cried out of the depths, and found forgiveness.

And then we climbed up into praise. Taste and see that the LORD is good. Bless the LORD, O my soul. Let the redeemed of the LORD say so. A day in thy courts is better than a thousand. Make a joyful noise unto the LORD. Great is the LORD, and greatly to be praised.

The book of Psalms is like a journey through life. There are joys, and sorrows, and fears, and failures. But the journey ends here, with everything that has breath praising the LORD. And that is where the whole story of the Bible ends too. In the book of Revelation, John hears every creature in heaven and on earth, saying, blessing, and honour, and glory, and power, be unto him that sitteth upon the throne, and unto the Lamb for ever and ever.

Now, three verses to hold on to.

Verse 1. 'Praise ye the LORD. Praise God in his sanctuary: praise him in the firmament of his power.' Praise is not just for special gatherings. God is to be praised in his house, with his people. And he is to be praised under the open sky, wherever we are, because the whole universe shows his power. Wherever you are right now, you can praise God.

Verse 2. 'Praise him for his mighty acts: praise him according to his excellent greatness.' We praise God for what he does, his mighty acts: creating the world, rescuing his people, sending Jesus, raising him from the dead. And we praise him for who he is, his excellent greatness. According to means in a way that matches it. His greatness is enormous, so our praise should be as big and as wholehearted as we can make it.

Verse 6. 'Let every thing that hath breath praise the LORD. Praise ye the LORD.' Every thing that hath breath. That means every person, of every age and every nation. Even the animals and birds. If you are breathing, you are invited. And think about it. God gave us our breath. In Genesis, he breathed into Adam the breath of life. So the best way to use the breath he gives us is to praise him with it.

Miriam sang, 'Sing ye to the LORD, for he hath triumphed gloriously'. And John heard every creature saying, 'Blessing, and honour, and glory, and power, be unto him that sitteth upon the throne, and unto the Lamb for ever and ever.'

Use your breath to praise God today. Sing a song, play an instrument if you can, or simply say, praise the LORD, as you think of his mighty acts and his greatness. And think back over the psalms we have walked through. Which one will you carry with you?

Here is today’s question. The book of Psalms is full of every feeling, sorrow, fear, guilt and joy, but it ends with pure praise. Why do you think the book ends this way, and what does that teach us about life with God?

Let us pray. Praise ye the LORD! I praise you, Lord, for your mighty acts, and for your excellent greatness. Thank you for walking with me through these psalms, through the joys and the sorrows. Thank you that, in the end, every thing that hath breath will praise you. As long as I have breath, I will praise you. Hallelujah. Praise ye the LORD. Amen.
