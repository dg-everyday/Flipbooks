The Living Voice. Meet the Book of Ruth.

Three women stand on a dusty road, weeping. All three are widows. The oldest is going home to Bethlehem with nothing left. She begs the two younger women to go back to their own mothers. One of them kisses her goodbye. The other holds on, and will not let go. And with that one choice, a quiet love story begins that will lead, in the end, to a king.

Welcome to the book of Ruth, on The Living Voice. Ruth is one of the shortest books in the Bible. It has only four chapters, so on this walk it is four steps, one chapter at a time. You can finish the whole book in less than a week. That is why we call it a short win. And finishing a whole book of the Bible is a good thing to do.

Ruth begins with the words, in the days when the judges ruled. That was a dark time in Israel’s story. The book of Judges, just before Ruth, ends by saying that every man did that which was right in his own eyes. There were wars, and much wrongdoing. But Ruth shows us something else happening at the same time, in one small town. Ordinary people, being faithful and kind, in a field at harvest. And God quietly at work through them.

The book is named after Ruth, a young woman from Moab. Moab was a neighbouring nation, and the Moabites did not worship the LORD. Ruth was an outsider. Yet she chose to trust the God of Israel, and he welcomed her.

We do not know who wrote the book of Ruth. It ends with a family line that leads to King David, so it was written in David’s time, or after.

Here are three things to watch for as we walk.

Watch for kindness. In the language of the Old Testament there is a rich word for faithful, loyal love, the kind of love that keeps its promises. In the King James Bible it is often translated kindness, or mercy. Ruth shows this kind of love to Naomi. Boaz shows it to Ruth. And behind them both is the LORD, whose kindness never fails.

Watch for the word redeem. In Israel, when a family fell on hard times and lost its land, a close relative could step in and buy it back, to keep the family name alive. That relative was called a kinsman, or a redeemer. Listen for who will be the redeemer for Naomi and Ruth, and what it will cost him.

And watch for how the story moves from empty to full. It begins with famine, and three funerals, and a woman who says, I went out full, and the LORD hath brought me home again empty. It ends with a harvest, a wedding, and a baby in her arms.

Ruth is also part of a much bigger story. She became the great-grandmother of King David. And many years later, the Gospel of Matthew names her in the family line of Jesus. An outsider from Moab, welcomed into the family of the Saviour of the world.

Here is a question to carry with you. Ruth is a story about faithful, loyal love in a hard and dark time. Who in your life has shown you that kind of love?

Let us pray. Lord God, as I walk through the book of Ruth, open my eyes to see your kindness at work in ordinary days. Thank you that you welcome outsiders, and that you never stop caring for those who feel empty. Teach me to love others with a faithful love. Amen.
