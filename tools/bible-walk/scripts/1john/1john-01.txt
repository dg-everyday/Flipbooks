# 1 John 1: the Word of life heard, seen and handled (John 13, 20 recalled as the Gospel tells them), fellowship, joy full, God is light, walking in darkness, the blood cleanseth, if we say we have no sin, confess and he forgives, if we say we have not sinned. Keys 1:5, 1:7, 1:9.
TITLE: God Is Light

HOOK: Think of a room that has been shut up for years. The curtains are closed. It is dark, and it smells musty. You cannot see the dust, or the cobwebs, or the stains. Then someone throws open the curtains. Sunlight floods in. Suddenly you can see everything, all the mess you did not know was there. That might feel uncomfortable. But it is the only way the room can ever be made clean.

WHERE: In the introduction, we heard that this letter has long been linked with John, the disciple whom Jesus loved. He writes as an eyewitness, to people he calls his little children. False teachers had left the church and unsettled the believers, so John writes that they may know they have eternal life. And we heard three things to watch for: light, love, and the words, we know.

Now, in chapter one, John begins. Listen for how he insists that Jesus was real. And listen for what he says about our sin, and about God’s forgiveness.

STORY: John begins, not with a greeting, but with a rush of words, as if he cannot wait to tell us. 'That which was from the beginning, which we have heard, which we have seen with our eyes, which we have looked upon, and our hands have handled, of the Word of life'.

Heard. Seen. Looked upon. Handled. John piles up the words. Jesus was not a ghost, or an idea, or a legend. John heard his voice. He watched him heal the sick, and calm the storm. In the Gospel of John, we read that at the last supper John was leaning on Jesus’ breast. On the day Jesus rose, John ran to the empty tomb, and saw the grave clothes lying there, and believed. And he heard Jesus say to Thomas, 'Reach hither thy finger, and behold my hands'. Jesus was real. You could touch him.

Why does that matter? Because the false teachers were saying that Jesus only seemed to have a body. John says, no. We touched him. The eternal life that was with the Father came down, and was shown to us, in a real person.

And John tells us why he is writing. 'That which we have seen and heard declare we unto you, that ye also may have fellowship with us: and truly our fellowship is with the Father, and with his Son Jesus Christ.' Fellowship means sharing life together, being close. John wants his readers to share in what he has. And what he has is friendship with God himself.

'And these things write we unto you, that your joy may be full.' John is not writing to burden us with rules. He writes so that our joy can be full, like a cup filled to the brim.

Then John gives the message he heard from Jesus.

So what does that mean for us? If God is light, we cannot be close to him while we live in darkness. 'If we say that we have fellowship with him, and walk in darkness, we lie, and do not the truth'. You cannot say you are friends with God, and keep living in ways you know are wrong. That is like saying you are standing in the sunshine, while you are hiding in a dark cellar.

But there is good news.

Then John warns against fooling ourselves. 'If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us.' Some of the false teachers claimed that they had no sin at all. They were too spiritual for that. John says, anyone who says that is fooling himself.

When we refuse to admit our sin, we are not fooling God. We are only fooling ourselves.

So what do we do with our sin? John tells us, in one of the best loved verses in the Bible.

And John finishes the chapter with one more warning. 'If we say that we have not sinned, we make him a liar, and his word is not in us.' God says that all of us have sinned. If we say we have not, we are calling God a liar. But when we agree with God about our sin, the door to forgiveness swings wide open.

Notice the pattern in this chapter. If we say, three times. If we say we have fellowship, but walk in darkness. If we say we have no sin. If we say we have not sinned. Each time, it is about being honest. God is light, and he loves the truth. He does not want us to pretend. He wants us to come into the light, just as we are, so that he can make us clean.

KEY 1:5: 'This then is the message which we have heard of him, and declare unto you, that God is light, and in him is no darkness at all.' Light means God is completely good, completely pure, completely true. There is no shadow in him, no hidden evil, no tricks. Light also shows things as they really are. In God’s light, nothing is hidden. That might sound frightening. But it is wonderful news. It means we can always trust him. He will never lie to us, or lead us into darkness.

KEY 1:7: 'But if we walk in the light, as he is in the light, we have fellowship one with another, and the blood of Jesus Christ his Son cleanseth us from all sin.' Walking in the light does not mean we are already perfect. It means we live honestly before God, not hiding. And when we do, two wonderful things happen. We have fellowship with each other, real friendship with God’s people. And the blood of Jesus, his death on the cross for us, keeps on cleansing us from all sin. Not some sin. All sin.

KEY 1:9: 'If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.' To confess means to agree with God, to say honestly, I did this, and it was wrong. And when we do, God forgives. He is faithful, because he keeps his promise. And he is just, because Jesus has already paid for our sins on the cross. God does not forgive by pretending sin does not matter. He forgives because Jesus took it on himself. And he does not only forgive. He cleanses. He makes us clean.

THREAD: Jesus said, 'I am the light of the world: he that followeth me shall not walk in darkness, but shall have the light of life.' And Solomon wrote, 'He that covereth his sins shall not prosper: but whoso confesseth and forsaketh them shall have mercy.'

TODAY: Is there something you have been hiding from God, in the dark? Bring it into the light today. Tell him honestly. He is faithful and just to forgive you, and to make you clean. Then thank him for the blood of Jesus, which cleanses from all sin.

QUESTION: John says, if we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us. What makes it hard to be honest with God about our sin, and what does this verse promise?

PRAYER: Lord God, you are light, and in you is no darkness at all. I do not want to pretend with you. I confess my sins to you now. Thank you that you are faithful and just to forgive me, and to cleanse me from all unrighteousness, because of Jesus. Help me to walk in the light, as you are in the light. Amen.
