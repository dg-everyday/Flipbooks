# 1 John 2: that ye sin not, an advocate, the propitiation (for the whole world), keeping his commandments, walk as he walked, the old and new commandment, love and hate, light and darkness, little children / fathers / young men, love not the world (2:16 not quoted, "lust" avoided), the world passeth away, antichrists ("Christians have understood... in different ways"), they went out from us, the anointing, abide in him. Keys 2:1, 2:6, 2:17.
TITLE: An Advocate with the Father

HOOK: A boy stands before a judge in a courtroom. He knows he did wrong. He is frightened. He has no idea what to say. Then the door opens, and someone walks in and stands beside him. It is someone who knows the judge well, someone the judge loves. And that person says, I will speak for him. John says that is what Jesus does for us.

WHERE: In chapter one, John told us that he had heard, seen and touched the Word of life. Jesus was real. And the message John heard was this: God is light, and in him is no darkness at all. If we walk in the light, the blood of Jesus cleanseth us from all sin. And if we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us.

Now, in chapter two, John tells us what to do when we fail. Listen for who speaks for us. And listen for the difference between loving God and loving the world.

STORY: John begins tenderly. 'My little children, these things write I unto you, that ye sin not.' John does not want us to sin. He has just told us God forgives, but that is not permission to sin as much as we like. But he knows we will still stumble. So he gives us comfort.

Then John explains. 'And he is the propitiation for our sins: and not for ours only, but also for the sins of the whole world.' Propitiation is a big word. It means the sacrifice that takes away God’s anger against sin, and makes peace between us and God. Jesus is that sacrifice. He took our sin on himself on the cross. And not just for a few people. For the whole world.

How can we know that we really know God? 'And hereby we do know that we know him, if we keep his commandments.' Someone who says, I know God, but does not care what God says, is not telling the truth.

And John gives us a picture of what that looks like.

Then John talks about a commandment that is both old and new. It is old, because they heard it from the very beginning. And it is new, because in Jesus it shines in a new way. 'because the darkness is past, and the true light now shineth.' The commandment is to love one another.

'He that loveth his brother abideth in the light, and there is none occasion of stumbling in him.' But whoever hates his brother walks in darkness, blinded. Hatred makes us blind. Love lets us see clearly.

Then John writes to different groups in the church. To little children, because your sins are forgiven. To young men, because you are strong, and have overcome the wicked one. To fathers, because you have known him who is from the beginning. Every age has something to rejoice in.

Then comes a warning. 'Love not the world, neither the things that are in the world. If any man love the world, the love of the Father is not in him.'

The world here does not mean the beautiful world God made, or the people in it. God so loved the world, Jesus said. It means a way of living that leaves God out. And John names three parts of it. The cravings of our bodies for whatever feels good. The greedy wanting of our eyes, when we see something and must have it. And the pride of life, showing off what we have, and thinking we are better than others. Do you remember Eve in the garden? She saw that the fruit was good for food, and pleasant to the eyes, and desired to make one wise. The same three pulls, from the very beginning.

And none of it lasts.

Then John warns his readers about false teachers. He calls them antichrists. Christians have understood what the Bible says about an antichrist to come in different ways. But here John uses the word plainly, for anyone who denies that Jesus is the Christ. 'Who is a liar but he that denieth that Jesus is the Christ? He is antichrist, that denieth the Father and the Son.'

These teachers had once been part of the church. 'They went out from us, but they were not of us'. Their leaving showed that they had never truly belonged.

But John reassures the believers who stayed. You have an anointing from the Holy One, he says, meaning the Holy Spirit, who teaches them the truth. Hold on to what you heard from the beginning. 'And this is the promise that he hath promised us, even eternal life.'

And the chapter ends with a gentle call. 'And now, little children, abide in him; that, when he shall appear, we may have confidence, and not be ashamed before him at his coming.' Abide means stay, remain, make your home. Stay close to Jesus, so that when he comes, you can greet him with joy.

KEY 2:1: 'My little children, these things write I unto you, that ye sin not. And if any man sin, we have an advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the righteous'. An advocate is someone who speaks for you, like a lawyer in a courtroom, who stands beside you and pleads your case. When we sin, we do not have to face God alone. Jesus stands with us. He is righteous, completely good. And he does not plead that we are innocent. He pleads that he has already paid for our sin.

KEY 2:6: 'He that saith he abideth in him ought himself also so to walk, even as he walked.' If we say we belong to Jesus, we should live the way he lived. How did Jesus walk? He was kind to the poor and the outcast. He told the truth. He forgave his enemies. He served others, and obeyed his Father. Walking as he walked does not happen in a day. But every day, we can take one more step in his footsteps.

KEY 2:17: 'And the world passeth away, and the lust thereof: but he that doeth the will of God abideth for ever.' Lust here means strong desire. Everything this world offers is passing away. The newest phone, the fanciest clothes, the biggest house. Like a sandcastle on the beach, the tide will wash it all away. But whoever does the will of God will last for ever. Why build our lives on things that will not last, when we can build on what is eternal?

THREAD: The letter to the Hebrews says of Jesus, 'he ever liveth to make intercession for them.' And Jesus said, 'A new commandment I give unto you, That ye love one another; as I have loved you, that ye also love one another.'

TODAY: When you fail today, do not run from God. Remember that Jesus is your advocate, and come straight back to him. And notice what pulls at your heart. Is it something that will pass away, or something that will last for ever?

QUESTION: John says the world passes away, but whoever does the will of God abides for ever. What is one thing you spend time on that will last, and one thing that will not?

PRAYER: Lord Jesus, thank you that you are my advocate with the Father, and the sacrifice for my sins, and for the sins of the whole world. Help me to walk as you walked. Keep me from loving the things of this world more than you. And help me to abide in you, so that when you come, I can meet you with joy. Amen.
