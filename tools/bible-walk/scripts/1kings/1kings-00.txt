TITLE: Meet the First Book of Kings

HOOK: A young king lies asleep, after offering a thousand sacrifices. God speaks to him in a dream, and says, ask what I shall give thee. He could ask for anything. Gold. Long life. Victory over his enemies. But he asks for something else. A wise and understanding heart. God is so pleased that he gives him all the rest as well. That young king is Solomon. And his story begins the first book of Kings.

BODY: Welcome to the first book of Kings, on The Living Voice. First Kings has twenty two chapters, so on this walk it is twenty two steps, one chapter at a time.

We last heard the story of Israel at the end of the second book of Samuel. David was king, and God had promised him that his family would rule for ever. Since then, we have walked through two short letters. In James, we heard, if any of you lack wisdom, let him ask of God. And we heard that Elijah was a man like us, who prayed, and it did not rain. Now we will meet them both. Solomon, who asked God for wisdom. And Elijah, who prayed.

In the Hebrew Bible, first and second Kings were one long book. Together they tell the story of the kings of God’s people, from the last days of David, to the fall of Jerusalem. We do not know who wrote them. The writer often points to older records, like the book of the acts of Solomon, which have been lost.

The first half of first Kings belongs to Solomon. He asks for wisdom, and becomes the wisest man in the world. He builds the temple of the LORD in Jerusalem, a house for God’s name, covered in gold. Kings and queens come from far away to hear him. It is the golden age of Israel.

But then Solomon’s heart turns away from the LORD. And after he dies, the kingdom is torn in two. Ten tribes in the north become the kingdom of Israel. Two tribes in the south, with Jerusalem, become the kingdom of Judah. From then on, the book follows two lines of kings, side by side. And it can be hard to keep track of them all. So do not worry about remembering every name. Listen for the big story.

In the last part of the book, a prophet bursts onto the scene. Elijah, from the hills of Gilead. He stands up to the wicked king Ahab and his queen Jezebel. He calls down fire from heaven on Mount Carmel. And he hears God, not in the wind or the earthquake or the fire, but in a still small voice.

Here are three things to watch for as we walk.

Watch for the heart. Solomon asks for an understanding heart. God tells him to walk with a perfect heart, as David did. And later we read that his heart was turned away. First Kings shows that what happens in the heart shapes a whole life, and even a whole nation.

Watch for the prophets. God does not leave his people without a voice. Again and again, a prophet steps forward with a word from the LORD. Nathan, Ahijah, a man of God from Judah, Elijah, and Micaiah. Listen for how the kings respond.

And watch for how each king is measured. Again and again the book says, he did evil in the sight of the LORD, or, he did that which was right, as did David his father. David, for all his failures, becomes the measuring line, because his heart belonged to God.

A gentle word before we begin. Some chapters in this book tell of battles and judgments, and of kings who did terrible things. We will tell them gently, and we will not dwell on the details.

As we walk, remember that Jesus once said, 'a greater than Solomon is here.' Solomon was wise, and rich, and built a temple. But he failed. The book leaves us longing for a King who will never fail. And he has come.

QUESTION: Solomon asked God for a wise and understanding heart. If God said to you, ask what I shall give thee, what would you ask for, and why?

PRAYER: Lord God, as I walk through the first book of Kings, give me a wise and understanding heart. Keep my heart turned towards you all my life. Help me to listen when you speak, whether in great events or in a still small voice. And thank you for Jesus, the true King, who is greater than Solomon. Amen.
