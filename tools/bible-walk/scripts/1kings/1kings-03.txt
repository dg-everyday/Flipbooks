# 1 Kings 3: Pharaoh's daughter, the high places, Solomon loved the LORD, Gibeon, the dream (ask what I shall give thee), I am but a little child, an understanding heart, riches and honour also, the two women and the baby. Keys 3:5, 3:9, 3:28. The women not called harlots (3:16 not quoted); the baby died "while they slept"; "slay" (3:26-27) not quoted.
TITLE: An Understanding Heart

HOOK: Two women stand before the king, shouting at each other. One holds a baby in her arms. The other is crying. Each says the baby is hers. There are no witnesses, and no way to prove who is telling the truth. Everyone in the court watches the young king. What will he do? And then he says something that makes everyone gasp. Bring me a sword.

WHERE: In chapter two, David gave Solomon his last charge: be strong, and walk in the ways of the LORD. Then David died, and was buried in the city of David. Solomon dealt with Adonijah, Abiathar, Joab and Shimei, and the kingdom was established in his hand.

Now, in chapter three, God speaks to Solomon. Listen for what Solomon asks for. And listen for how God’s answer shows itself in one of the most famous stories in the Bible.

STORY: The chapter begins with Solomon making an alliance with Pharaoh, king of Egypt, by marrying Pharaoh’s daughter. It made his kingdom strong in the eyes of the world. But keep it in mind. Solomon’s marriages will matter later in the story.

The temple was not yet built, so the people offered their sacrifices at high places, hilltop altars around the land. 'And Solomon loved the LORD, walking in the statutes of David his father'. That is how Solomon began. He loved God.

Solomon goes to Gibeon, the most important of the high places, where the old tabernacle of Moses stood. And there he offers a thousand burnt offerings on the altar. A huge act of worship.

And then God speaks.

Solomon answers humbly. You showed great kindness to my father David, he says, because he walked before you in truth and righteousness. And you have kept that kindness, and given him a son to sit on his throne. 'And now, O LORD my God, thou hast made thy servant king instead of David my father: and I am but a little child: I know not how to go out or come in.' Solomon was not really a little child. But he felt like one. He did not know how to lead. And he is ruling over a great people, too many to count.

So he asks.

'And the speech pleased the Lord, that Solomon had asked this thing.' God tells him, because you did not ask for a long life, or riches, or the death of your enemies, but asked for understanding, I will give you a wise and understanding heart. No one before you was like you, and no one after you will be. 'And I have also given thee that which thou hast not asked, both riches, and honour'. And if you walk in my ways, as your father David did, I will give you a long life.

Solomon wakes up. It was a dream. But it was real. He goes to Jerusalem, stands before the ark of the covenant, offers sacrifices, and holds a feast for all his servants.

Then comes the test of his new wisdom. Two women come to the king. They live in the same house. Both had given birth to baby boys, three days apart. No one else was there. While they slept, one of the babies died. And the first woman says, this woman got up, took my son from beside me while I slept, and put her dead child in my arms. When I looked closely, it was not my son.

The other woman says, no, the living one is my son, and the dead one is yours. And they argue in front of the king.

Solomon listens. Then he says, 'Bring me a sword. And they brought a sword before the king.' And Solomon says, 'Divide the living child in two, and give half to the one, and half to the other.'

Of course, Solomon never meant for the child to be harmed. It was a test, to reveal the truth hidden in their hearts. And it worked. The real mother’s heart ached with love for her son, and she cried out, O my lord, give her the living child. Do not hurt him. But the other woman said, let it be neither mine nor yours. Divide it.

And the king answers. Give the baby to the first woman. Do not hurt him. 'she is the mother thereof.' The mother who would rather give her son away than see him harmed. That was the true mother.

KEY 3:5: 'In Gibeon the LORD appeared to Solomon in a dream by night: and God said, Ask what I shall give thee.' Imagine God saying that to you. Ask what I shall give thee. Anything. What we ask for shows what we truly love. Solomon could have asked for many things. But this question was not only a test for Solomon. It shows us the heart of God. He is a generous giver, who delights to give good gifts to his children.

KEY 3:9: 'Give therefore thy servant an understanding heart to judge thy people, that I may discern between good and bad: for who is able to judge this thy so great a people?' Solomon asked for an understanding heart, literally a listening heart. A heart that listens to God, and can tell right from wrong. He did not ask for wisdom so that he could look clever. He asked so that he could serve God’s people well. That is a prayer God loves to answer.

KEY 3:28: 'And all Israel heard of the judgment which the king had judged; and they feared the king: for they saw that the wisdom of God was in him, to do judgment.' Feared here means they stood in awe. The people saw it clearly. This was not just human cleverness. It was the wisdom of God at work in their king. When God gives wisdom, it shows. Not in clever words, but in good judgment, fairness, and care for people.

THREAD: James wrote, 'If any of you lack wisdom, let him ask of God, that giveth to all men liberally'. And Jesus said, 'But seek ye first the kingdom of God, and his righteousness; and all these things shall be added unto you.'

TODAY: Is there a decision you are facing, where you do not know what is right? Ask God for an understanding heart, as Solomon did. Then listen. God loves to give wisdom to those who ask.

QUESTION: Solomon said, I am but a little child: I know not how to go out or come in. Where do you feel too small for what is in front of you, and how could you ask God for help?

PRAYER: Lord God, thank you that you are a generous giver. Like Solomon, I feel too small for many things. Give me an understanding heart, a listening heart, so that I can tell good from bad, and serve others well. Help me to seek your kingdom first, and to trust you with everything else. Amen.
