# 1 Kings 7: Solomon's own house (thirteen years) and the house of the forest of Lebanon, the porch of judgment; Hiram the craftsman, the pillars Jachin and Boaz, the molten sea on twelve oxen, the ten bases and lavers, the golden furnishings, David's dedicated treasures. Keys 7:14, 7:21, 7:51.
TITLE: Jachin and Boaz

HOOK: In a field of clay by the river Jordan, a great fire roars. Men pour glowing, molten bronze into moulds dug deep in the ground. When it cools, they lift out two enormous pillars, taller than four men standing on each other’s shoulders. They are so wide that a man cannot reach around them. These pillars will stand at the doorway of the house of God. And each one will have a name.

WHERE: In chapter six, the house of the LORD was built of stone made ready, so that no hammer or axe was heard. It was lined with cedar and covered with gold. In the most holy place stood two golden cherubims, ready to watch over the ark. And God promised, I will dwell among the children of Israel.

Now, in chapter seven, Solomon builds his own palace, and a master craftsman makes everything the temple needs. Listen for two pillars with meaningful names. And listen for a great sea of bronze.

STORY: The chapter begins with a sentence that makes us stop and think. The temple had taken seven years to build. 'But Solomon was building his own house thirteen years, and he finished all his house.' Solomon spent almost twice as long on his own house as on God’s house. The writer does not say it was wrong. But he lets us notice it.

Solomon’s palace was a group of great buildings. One was called the house of the forest of Lebanon, because it had so many cedar pillars that walking through it was like walking through a forest of cedars. There was a porch of pillars, and a porch for the throne, called the porch of judgment, where Solomon sat to judge cases, just as he did for the two women. There was his own house, and a house for Pharaoh’s daughter. All were built with great, costly stones, cut and shaped with saws.

Then Solomon sends to Tyre for a craftsman, also named Hiram. Not the king this time, but a master worker in bronze.

And Hiram gets to work. First, he casts two great pillars of bronze. On top of each pillar he puts a crown, called a chapiter, decorated with chains like a net, with lilies, and with rows of bronze pomegranates, hundreds of them.

And he gives the pillars names.

Next, Hiram makes something called the molten sea. It was a huge round basin of cast bronze, ten cubits across, and five cubits deep. Its rim was shaped like the rim of a cup, like a lily flower. It held a great amount of water. And it stood on the backs of twelve bronze oxen, three facing north, three facing west, three facing south, and three facing east. The twelve oxen may remind us of the twelve tribes of Israel. The priests used the water in the sea to wash themselves before serving God.

Then Hiram makes ten bronze stands, each on four wheels like chariot wheels, decorated with lions, oxen, cherubims and palm trees. On each stand sat a bronze basin, called a laver. Five stood on one side of the house, and five on the other. These were for washing the offerings. And Hiram makes pots, shovels and bowls, all of bright, polished bronze.

There was so much bronze that Solomon never weighed it. 'And Solomon left all the vessels unweighed, because they were exceeding many: neither was the weight of the brass found out.'

Inside the house, Solomon makes the golden furnishings. There is a golden altar, where incense was burned, so that sweet smelling smoke rose up like prayers. There is a golden table for the shewbread, the bread of the presence, which was set out before God. And there are ten candlesticks of pure gold, five on each side, in front of the most holy place, with golden lamps and flowers. And bowls, and spoons, and even the hinges of the doors, all made of gold.

All of these things remind us of the tabernacle that Moses made in the wilderness, which we heard about in the book of Exodus. The same pattern, the same meaning, but now in a house of stone, cedar and gold.

And then, finally, Solomon brings in something special.

KEY 7:14: 'He was a widow’s son of the tribe of Naphtali, and his father was a man of Tyre, a worker in brass: and he was filled with wisdom, and understanding, and cunning to work all works in brass. And he came to king Solomon, and wrought all his work.' Cunning here means skill. Brass in the King James Bible usually means bronze. Notice, Hiram was filled with wisdom and understanding, the same words used for Solomon. God gives wisdom not only to kings and teachers, but to craftsmen and builders, to people who work with their hands. Skilled work, done well, can be a gift from God and a way of serving him.

KEY 7:21: 'And he set up the pillars in the porch of the temple: and he set up the right pillar, and called the name thereof Jachin: and he set up the left pillar, and he called the name thereof Boaz.' Jachin is usually understood to mean, he shall establish. Boaz means, in him is strength. And do you remember Boaz from the book of Ruth? He was Solomon’s great-great-grandfather. Every time someone walked into the temple, they passed between two reminders. God will establish. In God is strength. Our life, too, stands firm only when it is built on his strength.

KEY 7:51: 'So was ended all the work that king Solomon made for the house of the LORD. And Solomon brought in the things which David his father had dedicated; even the silver, and the gold, and the vessels, did he put among the treasures of the house of the LORD.' David was not allowed to build the temple. But he spent years gathering treasure for it, so that his son could finish the work. Sometimes we prepare for things we will never see. David sowed, and Solomon reaped. And both of them served God.

THREAD: In the wilderness, God said of Bezaleel, 'I have filled him with the spirit of God, in wisdom, and in understanding, and in knowledge, and in all manner of workmanship'. And Paul wrote, 'whatsoever ye do, do all to the glory of God.'

TODAY: Whatever work you do today, at home, at school, or at your job, do it well, as a gift to God. And think of someone who prepared the way for you, as David did for Solomon. Thank God for them.

QUESTION: The pillars said, he shall establish, and in him is strength. Where do you need to remember today that your strength comes from God?

PRAYER: Lord God, thank you that you give wisdom and skill to all kinds of people, for all kinds of work. Help me to do my work well, for your glory. Thank you that you establish your people, and that in you is strength. Build my life on you, and help me to prepare the way for those who come after me. Amen.
