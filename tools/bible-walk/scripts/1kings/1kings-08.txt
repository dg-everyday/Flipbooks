# 1 Kings 8: the ark brought in under the wings of the cherubims, only the two tables of stone, the cloud and the glory, Solomon blesses the people, the promise to David performed, Solomon's prayer (no God like thee, heaven cannot contain thee, hear and forgive; the neighbour, defeat, no rain, plague, the stranger, captivity), the blessing (not failed one word), the sacrifices and the feast, joyful and glad of heart. Keys 8:11, 8:27, 8:56. No sacrifice numbers.
TITLE: There Is No God Like Thee

HOOK: The priests carry the golden ark slowly into the house of the LORD, and set it down under the outstretched wings of the cherubims. They step back out into the holy place. And then it happens. A cloud begins to fill the house. Thicker and thicker, until the priests cannot see, and cannot stand to do their work. The glory of the LORD has come.

WHERE: In chapter seven, Solomon built his palace, and Hiram the craftsman made two great bronze pillars named Jachin and Boaz, he shall establish, and in him is strength. He made the bronze sea on twelve oxen, and Solomon made the golden furnishings. Then Solomon brought in the treasures David had dedicated.

Now, in chapter eight, the temple is dedicated to God. Listen for the cloud of glory. And listen to Solomon’s great prayer, and all the people he prays for.

STORY: Solomon gathers all the leaders of Israel to Jerusalem, at the great feast in the seventh month. The priests lift up the ark of the covenant, and carry it from the city of David up to the temple. The old tabernacle of Moses and all its holy vessels are brought up too. And all along the way, Solomon and the people offer so many sacrifices of sheep and oxen that no one could count them.

The priests carry the ark into the most holy place, and set it under the wings of the cherubims. 'There was nothing in the ark save the two tables of stone, which Moses put there at Horeb'. The two stone tablets of the Ten Commandments, written by the finger of God.

And then the cloud comes.

Then Solomon turns around, and blesses all the people standing there. 'Blessed be the LORD God of Israel, which spake with his mouth unto David my father, and hath with his hand fulfilled it'. God spoke with his mouth, and kept his word with his hand. It was in David’s heart to build this house, Solomon says. God told him, you did well that it was in your heart. But your son will build it. 'And the LORD hath performed his word that he spake'.

Then Solomon stands before the altar, in front of everyone, and spreads out his hands towards heaven. And later we learn that he kneels down as he prays. 'LORD God of Israel, there is no God like thee, in heaven above, or on earth beneath, who keepest covenant and mercy with thy servants that walk before thee with all their heart'.

And then Solomon asks a great question.

Even so, Solomon asks God to listen whenever people pray towards this house. 'and when thou hearest, forgive.' Those words come again and again in his prayer. Hear, and forgive.

Then Solomon prays for all kinds of situations. When someone wrongs a neighbour and swears an oath at the altar, judge fairly. When your people are defeated by enemies because they have sinned, and they turn back to you, hear and forgive. When the sky is shut up and there is no rain, because they have sinned, and they pray and turn from their sin, hear, and send rain. Keep that one in mind. Before long, we will hear about a time when there was no rain for years.

When there is famine or sickness, hear and forgive, 'for thou, even thou only, knowest the hearts of all the children of men'.

Then Solomon prays for people who are not even Israelites. 'Moreover concerning a stranger, that is not of thy people Israel, but cometh out of a far country for thy name’s sake'. When he prays towards this house, hear him, and do what he asks, 'that all people of the earth may know thy name'. God’s house was always meant to be a blessing for the whole world.

And last of all, Solomon looks far ahead, to a dark time. If your people sin, 'for there is no man that sinneth not', and they are carried away captive to a far land, and there they turn back to you and say, we have sinned, and they pray towards this land, and this city, and this house, then hear their prayer, and forgive them. Do you remember Daniel, far away in Babylon, praying with his windows open towards Jerusalem? That is exactly what he was doing.

When Solomon finishes praying, he rises from his knees, and blesses the people with a loud voice.

'The LORD our God be with us, as he was with our fathers: let him not leave us, nor forsake us'. And he charges them, 'Let your heart therefore be perfect with the LORD our God'.

Then come great sacrifices, and a feast for fourteen days. And at the end, the people go home 'joyful and glad of heart for all the goodness that the LORD had done for David his servant, and for Israel his people.'

KEY 8:11: 'So that the priests could not stand to minister because of the cloud: for the glory of the LORD had filled the house of the LORD.' This was the same cloud that led Israel through the wilderness, and filled the tabernacle in the days of Moses. The glory of God is his shining, holy presence. It was so great that the priests could not even stand. The temple was beautiful, but this was what made it truly holy. God himself had come to dwell there.

KEY 8:27: 'But will God indeed dwell on the earth? behold, the heaven and heaven of heavens cannot contain thee; how much less this house that I have builded?' Solomon knew that God is far too great to live in a building. The whole universe cannot hold him. And yet, in his kindness, he chose to put his name in that house, and to meet with his people there. Many years later, John wrote that God did dwell on the earth, in a person. The Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us.

KEY 8:56: 'Blessed be the LORD, that hath given rest unto his people Israel, according to all that he promised: there hath not failed one word of all his good promise, which he promised by the hand of Moses his servant.' Not one word. Joshua said the same thing at the end of his life. Every promise God made, to Abraham, to Moses and to David, he kept. You can trust every promise he has made to you too. Not one of them will fail.

THREAD: The prophet Isaiah heard God say, 'for mine house shall be called an house of prayer for all people.' And John wrote, 'If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins'.

TODAY: Solomon prayed, when thou hearest, forgive. Is there something you need to confess to God today? Bring it to him. He hears, and he forgives. And think of a promise of God that you can thank him for keeping.

QUESTION: Solomon prayed for his own people, for strangers from far away, and for those who would one day be far from home. Who could you pray for today, that you do not usually remember?

PRAYER: Lord God, there is no God like you, in heaven above or on earth beneath. The heaven of heavens cannot contain you, and yet you hear me when I pray. Thank you that not one word of your good promises has failed. When I pray, hear, and forgive. Turn my heart to you, and let all the earth know that you are God. Amen.
