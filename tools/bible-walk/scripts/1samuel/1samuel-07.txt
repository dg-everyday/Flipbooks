# 1 Samuel 7: the ark at Kirjath-jearim, Israel lamented after the LORD, return with all your hearts, put away the strange gods, Mizpeh, water poured out, we have sinned, cease not to cry, the LORD thundered, Eben-ezer, hitherto hath the LORD helped us, Samuel's circuit. Keys 7:3, 7:8, 7:12.
TITLE: Hitherto Hath the LORD Helped Us

HOOK: A man picks up a large stone, and sets it upright in a field between two towns. He gives it a name. And for thousands of years since, people all over the world have sung that name in their songs, and remembered what it means. It is not the stone that matters. It is what the stone says about God.

WHERE: In chapter six, the Philistines sent the ark home on a new cart, pulled by two cows who went straight to Beth-shemesh, lowing as they went, and turned not aside to the right hand or to the left. But some men looked into the ark, and many fell, and the people asked, who is able to stand before this holy LORD God? So the ark was taken to Kirjath-jearim.

Now, in chapter seven, Samuel calls the people back to God. Listen for what they must do to return. And listen for the name of the stone.

STORY: The men of Kirjath-jearim come and take the ark of the LORD up to the house of a man named Abinadab, on the hill. And they set apart his son Eleazar to look after it.

The ark stays there a long time. And all the house of Israel mourns, and longs for the LORD.

That is a turning point. For years, Israel had ignored God. Now, at last, they begin to miss him. They feel the emptiness of life without him.

And Samuel speaks to all Israel.

Strange gods. Ashtaroth was a goddess of the Canaanites. Even while they longed for the LORD, the people were still keeping these idols. Samuel tells them, you cannot have both. If you want to return to the LORD, put away everything that has taken his place.

And they do. The children of Israel put away the Baals and the Ashtaroth, and serve the LORD only.

Then Samuel says, gather all Israel at Mizpeh, and I will pray to the LORD for you.

So they gather at Mizpeh. They draw water and pour it out before the LORD, a sign of pouring out their hearts in sorrow. They fast that day. And they say, 'We have sinned against the LORD.' And Samuel judges the people there.

But then the Philistines hear that all Israel has gathered at Mizpeh. And the lords of the Philistines come up to attack. When Israel hears it, they are afraid.

This is often how it happens. Just when we turn back to God, trouble comes. The people had just repented. They had no army ready. They were fasting and praying, not preparing for war.

But this time, instead of grabbing the ark as a lucky charm, they turn to prayer.

So Samuel takes a young lamb, and offers it as a whole burnt offering to the LORD. And Samuel cries out to the LORD for Israel. And the LORD hears him.

While Samuel is still offering the sacrifice, the Philistines come near to attack. But the LORD thunders with a great thunder that day against the Philistines, and throws them into confusion. And they are defeated before Israel. The men of Israel go out from Mizpeh, and chase the Philistines.

Remember chapter four. Then, Israel trusted in the ark, and lost. Now, Israel trusts in the LORD, and the LORD fights for them. The difference was not the strength of the army. It was the state of their hearts.

Then Samuel takes a stone.

The name Eben-ezer means stone of help. And it was near the very place where Israel had been defeated, back in chapter four, by another place called Eben-ezer. The place of defeat has become a place of victory and thanks.

After this, the Philistines are subdued, and do not come into Israel’s land again for a long time. The hand of the LORD is against the Philistines all the days of Samuel. The towns the Philistines had taken are given back to Israel. And there is peace.

And Samuel judges Israel all the days of his life. Every year he travels in a circle, to Bethel, Gilgal and Mizpeh, judging Israel in each place. And then he goes home to Ramah, where his house is. And there he builds an altar to the LORD.

KEY 7:3: 'And Samuel spake unto all the house of Israel, saying, If ye do return unto the LORD with all your hearts, then put away the strange gods and Ashtaroth from among you, and prepare your hearts unto the LORD, and serve him only: and he will deliver you out of the hand of the Philistines.' With all your hearts. Serve him only. Returning to God is not just feeling sad, or longing for him. It means putting away whatever has taken his place, and giving him our whole heart. God does not want a share of our hearts. He wants all of them.

KEY 7:8: 'And the children of Israel said to Samuel, Cease not to cry unto the LORD our God for us, that he will save us out of the hand of the Philistines.' Cease not to cry. Do not stop praying for us. This time, the people knew where their help came from. They did not reach for the ark. They asked Samuel to pray. It is a precious thing to have people who pray for us. And it is a precious thing to be someone who prays for others.

KEY 7:12: 'Then Samuel took a stone, and set it between Mizpeh and Shen, and called the name of it Eben-ezer, saying, Hitherto hath the LORD helped us.' Hitherto means up to now, all the way to here. Samuel looked back over the long, hard years, the defeat, the loss of the ark, the years of sorrow. And he could say, up to now, the LORD has helped us. Every step of the way, God has been with us. And if he has helped us this far, he will help us the rest of the way.

THREAD: A psalm says, 'God is our refuge and strength, a very present help in trouble.' And Paul wrote, 'Having therefore obtained help of God, I continue unto this day'.

TODAY: Set up your own Eben-ezer today. Look back over your life, or over the past year, and write down the ways God has helped you. Then say, with Samuel, hitherto hath the LORD helped me.

QUESTION: Samuel looked back and said, hitherto hath the LORD helped us. Looking back over your own life, where can you see that God has helped you?

PRAYER: Lord God, hitherto you have helped me. Thank you for every step of the way you have been with me, even in the hard years. I put away anything that has taken your place in my heart, and I give you my whole heart. And I trust you to help me the rest of the way. Amen.
