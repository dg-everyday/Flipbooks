# 1 Samuel 11: Nahash besieges Jabesh-gilead and his cruel condition (told gently), seven days' respite, the people weep, Saul comes from the field, the Spirit of God, the yoke of oxen and the call, help by the time the sun is hot, the rescue (no slew), no man put to death, the kingdom renewed at Gilgal. Keys 11:6, 11:13, 11:15.
TITLE: To Day the LORD Hath Wrought Salvation

HOOK: The messengers arrive in town with terrible news, and everyone begins to weep. Just then, a farmer comes walking in from the fields behind his oxen. He has been anointed king of Israel. But no one has really treated him like one. He hears the weeping, and asks what is wrong. And when they tell him, something stirs inside him that changes everything.

WHERE: In chapter ten, Samuel anointed Saul, and God gave him another heart. Saul prophesied among the prophets, and the people asked, is Saul also among the prophets? At Mizpeh, the lot fell on Saul, but he had hidden himself among the stuff. The people shouted, God save the king. But some worthless men said, how shall this man save us? And Saul held his peace.

Now, in chapter eleven, Saul gets his first test as king. Listen for where his strength comes from. And listen for how he treats the men who mocked him.

STORY: Nahash, the king of the Ammonites, comes up and surrounds the town of Jabesh-gilead, on the east side of the Jordan. And the men of Jabesh say to Nahash, make a treaty with us, and we will serve you.

But Nahash gives them a cruel answer. I will make a treaty with you, he says, on one condition. That I put out the right eye of every one of you, and so bring shame on all Israel.

It was a cruel demand, meant to humiliate. A soldier with only his left eye could hardly fight, because his shield covered his left side.

The elders of Jabesh say, give us seven days, so that we can send messengers throughout Israel. If no one comes to save us, we will surrender to you.

Perhaps Nahash agreed because he did not believe anyone would come. Israel had no army, and its new king was a farmer.

The messengers come to Gibeah, Saul’s town, and tell the news. And all the people weep aloud.

Just then, Saul comes in from the field, behind his oxen. What is wrong with the people, he asks. Why are they weeping? And they tell him what the men of Jabesh have said.

Saul takes a pair of oxen, cuts them in pieces, and sends the pieces throughout Israel with messengers. Whoever does not come out to follow Saul and Samuel, he says, this is what will be done to his oxen.

It was a dramatic call to arms, one no one could ignore. And the fear of the LORD falls on the people, and they come out as one man.

Notice that Saul calls them to follow Saul and Samuel. He still honours the prophet, and the LORD behind him.

Saul gathers the army at Bezek. And they send a message to Jabesh. 'Thus shall ye say unto the men of Jabesh-gilead, To morrow, by that time the sun be hot, ye shall have help.'

The messengers go and tell the men of Jabesh, and they are glad. And the men of Jabesh say to Nahash, cleverly, 'To morrow we will come out unto you, and ye shall do with us all that seemeth good unto you.'

The next day, Saul divides his army into three groups. They break into the Ammonite camp before daybreak, and fight until the heat of the day. And the Ammonites are scattered, so completely that no two of them are left together.

Jabesh-gilead is saved.

Then the people say to Samuel, who was it that said, shall Saul reign over us? Bring those men, so that we can put them to death.

It is the moment for revenge. Saul could have punished the men who mocked him in the last chapter.

But Saul says no.

What a fine answer. Saul gives the glory to God, and shows mercy to his enemies. This is Saul at his very best.

Then Samuel says to the people, come, let us go to Gilgal, and renew the kingdom there.

And so the people go to Gilgal, the very place where Israel first camped after crossing the Jordan under Joshua.

The men of Jabesh-gilead would never forget what Saul did for them. Many years later, at the end of this book, they would show their love for him in return.

KEY 11:6: 'And the Spirit of God came upon Saul when he heard those tidings, and his anger was kindled greatly.' The Spirit of God came upon Saul, just as it had come upon Othniel, Gideon and Samson. And his anger was kindled. Not selfish anger, but righteous anger at cruelty and injustice. There is a right kind of anger, the kind that is stirred when the weak are oppressed, and moves us to act. That anger, guided by God’s Spirit, can do great good.

KEY 11:13: 'And Saul said, There shall not a man be put to death this day: for to day the LORD hath wrought salvation in Israel.' Wrought means worked. Saul gives God the credit. The LORD hath wrought salvation. And because God has been merciful, Saul will be merciful too. A day of God’s salvation is a day for mercy, not revenge. When we remember how much God has forgiven us, we can forgive others.

KEY 11:15: 'And all the people went to Gilgal; and there they made Saul king before the LORD in Gilgal; and there they sacrificed sacrifices of peace offerings before the LORD; and there Saul and all the men of Israel rejoiced greatly.' Before the LORD. Three times in this verse we hear the word there, and twice before the LORD. The kingdom is renewed in God’s presence, with worship and joy. This is how it should be. A king, and a people, rejoicing together before God. If only it had stayed this way.

THREAD: Isaiah wrote, 'learn to do well; seek judgment, relieve the oppressed'. And Paul wrote, 'Be not overcome of evil, but overcome evil with good.'

TODAY: Is there someone being treated unfairly, whom you could help today? Ask God to stir your heart, and give you the courage to act. And if someone has mocked or hurt you, choose mercy instead of revenge.

QUESTION: Saul could have punished the men who mocked him, but he said, to day the LORD hath wrought salvation. Why do you think remembering God’s mercy helps us to be merciful?

PRAYER: Lord God, stir my heart when I see others suffering unfairly, and give me courage to help. Thank you for your salvation. Because you have been merciful to me, help me to be merciful to others, even to those who have hurt me. Let me rejoice before you, today and every day. Amen.
