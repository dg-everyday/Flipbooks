# 1 Samuel 13: Saul's small army, Jonathan strikes the garrison, the Philistines gather like sand, the people hide and tremble, seven days at Gilgal, Samuel came not, Saul offers the burnt offering, what hast thou done, thou hast done foolishly, a man after his own heart, no smith in Israel. Keys 13:8, 13:13, 13:14.
TITLE: The LORD Hath Sought Him a Man After His Own Heart

HOOK: A king is waiting. Day after day, he watches the road for the old prophet who promised to come. Day after day, more of his soldiers slip away in fear. The enemy is gathering, more of them than anyone can count. And on the seventh day, the king can wait no longer. He decides to take matters into his own hands. And just as he finishes, the prophet arrives.

WHERE: In chapter twelve, Samuel gave his farewell. Whose ox have I taken, he asked, and the people said, none. He sent thunder and rain at wheat harvest, and the people were afraid. But Samuel said, fear not, the LORD will not forsake his people. God forbid that I should sin against the LORD in ceasing to pray for you. Only fear the LORD, and serve him with all your heart.

Now, in chapter thirteen, Saul faces his first great test as king. Listen for what Saul was told to do. And listen for what it costs him when he does not do it.

STORY: Saul chooses three thousand men as his army. Two thousand stay with him, and a thousand go with his son Jonathan. The rest are sent home.

Jonathan attacks a Philistine outpost at Geba. And the Philistines hear about it. Saul blows the trumpet throughout the land, and calls the people to join him at Gilgal.

But the Philistines gather a huge army to fight Israel. Chariots and horsemen, and soldiers as many as the sand on the seashore. They camp at Michmash.

When the men of Israel see how much danger they are in, they hide. In caves, in thickets, among the rocks, in holes in the ground and in pits. Some cross the Jordan and flee. And the people who stay with Saul at Gilgal follow him trembling.

Remember what Samuel told Saul back in chapter ten. Go down to Gilgal ahead of me, and wait seven days, until I come. Then I will offer the sacrifices, and show you what to do.

So Saul waits.

Imagine the pressure. The enemy is gathering. Your soldiers are running away. Every day you wait, you look weaker. And the prophet has not come.

So Saul says, bring me the burnt offering and the peace offerings. And he offers the burnt offering himself.

Saul was not a priest. He had no right to offer the sacrifice. And Samuel had clearly told him to wait.

As soon as he finishes offering the sacrifice, Samuel arrives. And Saul goes out to greet him, as if nothing were wrong.

Samuel says, 'What hast thou done?'

And Saul makes excuses. I saw that the people were scattering from me, and you did not come at the time you said, and the Philistines were gathering at Michmash. So I thought, the Philistines will attack me here at Gilgal, and I have not asked the LORD for help. 'I forced myself therefore, and offered a burnt offering.'

Listen to Saul’s excuses. The people. You. The Philistines. Everyone else is to blame. And I forced myself, as if he had no choice. But there was a choice. He could have waited a little longer, and trusted God.

And Samuel answers him.

Then Samuel leaves, and goes up to Gibeah. And Saul counts the men who are still with him. About six hundred.

The chapter ends with a glimpse of how weak Israel is. The Philistines send out raiding parties in three directions. And there is no blacksmith anywhere in Israel. The Philistines had made sure of that, so that the Hebrews could not make swords or spears. The Israelites had to go down to the Philistines even to sharpen their farm tools, their ploughs and axes and sickles. So on the day of battle, not one of the soldiers with Saul and Jonathan had a sword or a spear. Only Saul and Jonathan had them.

Six hundred frightened men, with almost no weapons, facing an army like the sand on the seashore. And a king who did not trust God enough to wait.

KEY 13:8: 'And he tarried seven days, according to the set time that Samuel had appointed: but Samuel came not to Gilgal; and the people were scattered from him.' Saul waited seven days, almost. He did most of what he was told. But he stopped just short. Waiting is one of the hardest things God asks of us. When God seems slow, we are tempted to rush ahead and fix things ourselves. But partial obedience is still disobedience.

KEY 13:13: 'And Samuel said to Saul, Thou hast done foolishly: thou hast not kept the commandment of the LORD thy God, which he commanded thee: for now would the LORD have established thy kingdom upon Israel for ever.' Thou hast done foolishly. Saul thought he was being wise and practical. But in God’s eyes, it was foolish. True wisdom is trusting and obeying God, even when it seems to make no sense. And notice what Saul missed. God would have established his kingdom for ever. Disobedience always costs more than we think.

KEY 13:14: 'But now thy kingdom shall not continue: the LORD hath sought him a man after his own heart, and the LORD hath commanded him to be captain over his people, because thou hast not kept that which the LORD commanded thee.' A man after his own heart. God was already looking for someone else, someone whose heart would be like God’s heart. Not a perfect man, but a man who loved God, and who would turn back to him when he failed. We will meet him soon. In the New Testament, Paul says this man was David.

THREAD: Isaiah wrote, 'but they that wait upon the LORD shall renew their strength'. And Paul said of David, 'I have found David the son of Jesse, a man after mine own heart, which shall fulfil all my will.'

TODAY: Is there something you are waiting on God for, and you are tempted to rush ahead and fix it yourself? Bring it to him today. Ask for patience to wait for his timing.

QUESTION: Saul blamed the people, Samuel and the Philistines for his choice. Why do you think it is so easy to make excuses instead of admitting we were wrong?

PRAYER: Lord God, forgive me for the times I have rushed ahead instead of waiting for you. Forgive me for making excuses. Give me patience to wait for your timing, and a heart that trusts you even when you seem slow. Make my heart a heart after your own. Amen.
