# 1 Samuel 15: the command against Amalek (told gently, why: Exodus attack), the Kenites spared, Saul spares Agag and the best, it repenteth me, Samuel cries all night, I have performed the commandment, the bleating of the sheep, little in thine own sight, to obey is better than sacrifice (avoids 15:23 "witchcraft"), I feared the people, the torn mantle, Agag (gently), Samuel mourned for Saul. Keys 15:11, 15:22, 15:24.
TITLE: To Obey Is Better Than Sacrifice

HOOK: A king stands proudly in front of the prophet, and says, I have done everything the LORD told me to do. And just at that moment, from somewhere behind him, comes the sound of sheep bleating, and cattle lowing. Sheep and cattle that should not be there. And the prophet says, then what is this noise I hear?

WHERE: In chapter fourteen, Jonathan and his armour bearer climbed up to a Philistine outpost, saying, there is no restraint to the LORD to save by many or by few. God sent panic through the Philistine army, and the LORD saved Israel that day. But Saul made a rash oath, and would have put Jonathan to death, if the people had not rescued him.

Now, in chapter fifteen, God gives Saul a clear command. This is a hard chapter, about war and judgment. Listen for what Saul does, and what he says he did. And listen for Samuel’s famous words about obedience.

STORY: Samuel comes to Saul with a message from the LORD.

God says, I remember what Amalek did to Israel, when they attacked them on the way up from Egypt. When Israel was weak and weary in the wilderness, the Amalekites had attacked from behind, picking off the weakest and the stragglers. And God had said, long before, that one day he would bring judgment on Amalek for it.

Now God tells Saul to carry out that judgment. Go and strike Amalek, and destroy everything they have. Spare nothing. Not even the animals. The whole of Amalek was to be given over to God in judgment, as Jericho had been. This is one of the hardest commands in the Bible to hear, and Christians have thought about it carefully. But the point of the chapter is clear. God gave a command, and Saul was to obey it fully.

So Saul gathers a great army. He warns the Kenites, who live among the Amalekites, to leave, because they had shown kindness to Israel when they came out of Egypt. So the Kenites go.

Then Saul defeats the Amalekites. But he spares Agag, the king of the Amalekites, and takes him alive. And Saul and the people spare the best of the sheep and the cattle, the fattened calves and the lambs, everything that is good. They destroy only what is worthless.

He kept what he wanted, and destroyed what he did not want.

Then the word of the LORD comes to Samuel.

Early the next day, Samuel goes to meet Saul. He is told that Saul has gone to Carmel, and set up a monument there in his own honour, and then gone on to Gilgal.

When Samuel finds him, Saul greets him brightly. Blessed be thou of the LORD. I have performed the commandment of the LORD.

And Samuel says, 'What meaneth then this bleating of the sheep in mine ears, and the lowing of the oxen which I hear?'

Saul says, the people brought them from the Amalekites. They spared the best of the sheep and cattle, to sacrifice to the LORD your God. And the rest we destroyed.

Notice Saul’s words. The people spared them. We destroyed the rest. When something is wrong, it is the people’s fault. When something is right, it is we. And notice, the LORD your God. Not my God.

Samuel says, stop. Let me tell you what the LORD said to me. When you were small in your own eyes, were you not made the head of the tribes of Israel? The LORD anointed you king. And he sent you on a mission. Why did you not obey the voice of the LORD?

But Saul insists. I did obey the LORD. I went on the mission. I brought back Agag. It was the people who took the sheep and cattle, to sacrifice to the LORD.

And Samuel answers with words that have echoed down the centuries.

Then Samuel says, because you have rejected the word of the LORD, he has rejected you from being king.

And at last Saul admits it.

Saul begs Samuel to come back with him, so that he can worship the LORD. Samuel refuses, and turns to leave. Saul grabs the edge of his robe, and it tears. And Samuel says, the LORD has torn the kingdom of Israel from you today, and given it to a neighbour of yours, who is better than you.

Saul says again, I have sinned. But honour me now before the elders and before Israel. Come back with me. So Samuel goes back, and Saul worships.

Even in his confession, Saul is worried about how he looks to the people.

Then Samuel calls for Agag, the king of the Amalekites, and Samuel himself carries out the judgment that Saul had left undone. And Agag dies at Gilgal.

Samuel goes home to Ramah, and Saul to Gibeah. And Samuel never goes to see Saul again until the day of Samuel’s death. But Samuel mourns for Saul. And the LORD is sorry that he made Saul king over Israel.

KEY 15:11: 'It repenteth me that I have set up Saul to be king: for he is turned back from following me, and hath not performed my commandments. And it grieved Samuel; and he cried unto the LORD all night.' It repenteth me means God was grieved. God is not cold. He feels sorrow when people turn away from him. And look at Samuel. He cried to the LORD for hours. Saul had replaced him as leader. And yet Samuel loved him, and grieved for him. That is the heart of a true servant of God.

KEY 15:22: 'And Samuel said, Hath the LORD as great delight in burnt offerings and sacrifices, as in obeying the voice of the LORD? Behold, to obey is better than sacrifice, and to hearken than the fat of rams.' To obey is better than sacrifice. Saul thought he could make up for disobedience with religious offerings. But God does not want our religious activity as a replacement for obedience. He wants our hearts, and our listening ears. No amount of worship can make up for refusing to do what God says.

KEY 15:24: 'And Saul said unto Samuel, I have sinned: for I have transgressed the commandment of the LORD, and thy words: because I feared the people, and obeyed their voice.' I feared the people. There is the root of Saul’s problem. He cared more about what the people thought than about what God thought. He wanted to be popular. And so he obeyed their voice instead of God’s. The fear of people is a trap. Only the fear of God sets us free.

THREAD: A proverb says, 'The fear of man bringeth a snare: but whoso putteth his trust in the LORD shall be safe.' And Jesus said, 'If ye love me, keep my commandments.'

TODAY: Is there an area where you are doing part of what God asks, but not all? Or where you are more worried about what people think than about what God thinks? Ask God today for courage to obey him fully.

QUESTION: Saul said, I feared the people, and obeyed their voice. Whose opinion do you find it hardest to stand against, and how can you put God’s voice first?

PRAYER: Lord God, forgive me for the times I have obeyed you only partly, and tried to make up for it in other ways. Forgive me for fearing people more than you. Give me a heart that listens to your voice, and obeys it fully. To obey is better than sacrifice. Amen.
