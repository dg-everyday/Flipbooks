# 1 Samuel 16: how long wilt thou mourn, the horn of oil, Jesse the Bethlehemite, Eliab, the LORD looketh on the heart, seven sons pass by, the youngest keeping the sheep, arise anoint him, the Spirit upon David, the Spirit departs from Saul and a troubling spirit, the cunning harp player, David in Saul's service. Avoids 16:2 "kill". Keys 16:7, 16:11, 16:13.
TITLE: The LORD Looketh on the Heart

HOOK: Seven young men stand in a line, the sons of a farmer from Bethlehem. The eldest is tall and strong and handsome. Surely he is the one. The old prophet looks him up and down, and reaches for his horn of oil. And God says, no. Not him. Not any of them. And far away on a hillside, the youngest son is out with the sheep, with no idea that his life is about to change for ever.

WHERE: In chapter fifteen, Saul spared the best of the Amalekites’ animals, and said he had obeyed. But Samuel heard the bleating of the sheep, and said, to obey is better than sacrifice. Saul admitted, I feared the people, and obeyed their voice. The LORD rejected Saul from being king. And Samuel mourned for Saul.

Now, in chapter sixteen, God chooses a new king. Listen for what Samuel looks at, and what God looks at. And listen for the name of the boy God chooses.

STORY: The LORD says to Samuel, how long will you mourn for Saul, since I have rejected him as king? Fill your horn with oil, and go. I am sending you to Jesse of Bethlehem, for I have chosen a king for myself among his sons.

Samuel is afraid. If Saul hears about it, my life will be in danger. And the LORD says, take a young cow with you, and say, I have come to offer a sacrifice to the LORD. Invite Jesse to the sacrifice, and I will show you what to do. You will anoint for me the one I show you.

So Samuel goes to Bethlehem. The elders of the town come trembling to meet him. Do you come in peace? And he says, in peace. I have come to sacrifice to the LORD. Get yourselves ready, and come with me to the sacrifice. And he makes Jesse and his sons ready, and invites them.

Bethlehem. Remember Ruth and Boaz? This is their town. And Jesse is their grandson.

When they arrive, Samuel sees Eliab, Jesse’s eldest son. And he thinks, surely this is the LORD’s anointed, standing right here before the LORD.

Eliab was tall and impressive, just as Saul had been. Samuel was making the same mistake the people had made.

But the LORD speaks to Samuel.

Then Jesse calls Abinadab, and he passes before Samuel. And Samuel says, the LORD has not chosen this one either. Then Shammah passes by. Not this one either.

Jesse makes seven of his sons pass before Samuel. And Samuel says, the LORD has not chosen any of these.

Then Samuel asks a question.

Jesse had not even bothered to call him. The youngest was only good for looking after sheep. No one imagined that God might choose him.

So Jesse sends for him, and brings him in. He is ruddy, with bright eyes, and good looking.

And the LORD says, 'Arise, anoint him: for this is he.'

Notice that the writer does tell us David was good looking. God did not choose him because of his looks, but God did not hold his looks against him either. God simply looked deeper.

Then comes the moment of anointing.

And now, for the first time, we hear his name. David.

And at the same time, something terrible happens to Saul. The Spirit of the LORD leaves Saul, and a troubling spirit from the LORD torments him. The Bible tells us plainly that God allowed this, as part of his judgment on Saul’s disobedience.

Saul’s servants say to him, a troubling spirit from God is tormenting you. Let us find a man who can play the harp well. When the troubling spirit comes upon you, he will play, and you will feel better.

Saul says, find me someone who plays well, and bring him to me.

And one of the young men says, I have seen a son of Jesse of Bethlehem who plays well. He is brave, a warrior, wise in speech, and good looking. And the LORD is with him.

So Saul sends messengers to Jesse. Send me your son David, who is with the sheep. And Jesse sends David, with a donkey loaded with bread, a skin of wine and a young goat, as a gift for the king.

David comes to Saul, and serves him. And Saul loves him very much, and makes him one of his armour bearers. Saul sends word to Jesse, let David stay in my service, for I am pleased with him.

And whenever the troubling spirit comes upon Saul, David takes his harp and plays. And Saul is refreshed, and feels better, and the troubling spirit leaves him.

Think how strange this is. The old king, rejected by God, is comforted by the music of the young man God has chosen to replace him. And neither of them knows it yet, except David.

KEY 16:7: 'But the LORD said unto Samuel, Look not on his countenance, or on the height of his stature; because I have refused him: for the LORD seeth not as man seeth; for man looketh on the outward appearance, but the LORD looketh on the heart.' This is one of the most important verses in the Bible. We judge by how people look, how tall, how strong, how impressive. But God sees what is inside. He sees the heart, the real person. That is a warning, because God sees what we hide. And it is a comfort, because God sees the good that others overlook.

KEY 16:11: 'And Samuel said unto Jesse, Are here all thy children? And he said, There remaineth yet the youngest, and, behold, he keepeth the sheep. And Samuel said unto Jesse, Send and fetch him: for we will not sit down till he come hither.' There remaineth yet the youngest. David was the one left out, the one no one thought to invite. But God had his eye on him all along. If you have ever felt overlooked or forgotten, take heart. God sees you, out in the fields, doing faithful work no one notices.

KEY 16:13: 'Then Samuel took the horn of oil, and anointed him in the midst of his brethren: and the Spirit of the LORD came upon David from that day forward. So Samuel rose up, and went to Ramah.' From that day forward. The Spirit came upon David, and stayed. David did not become king that day. It would be many years, and many hard trials, before he took the throne. But from that day, God was with him. God’s calling often comes long before we see it fulfilled.

THREAD: Paul wrote, 'But God hath chosen the foolish things of the world to confound the wise; and God hath chosen the weak things of the world to confound the things which are mighty'. And a psalm of David says, 'The LORD is my shepherd; I shall not want.'

TODAY: Think of someone in your life who is often overlooked. How might God see them? Show them kindness today. And remember that God sees your heart, not your outward appearance.

QUESTION: Man looketh on the outward appearance, but the LORD looketh on the heart. How does it change the way you see yourself, and others, to know that God looks on the heart?

PRAYER: Lord God, thank you that you look on the heart, and that you see those whom others overlook. Search my heart, and make it a heart after your own. Help me not to judge others by how they look, but to see them as you see them. Amen.
