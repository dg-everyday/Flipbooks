# 1 Samuel 19: Saul orders David's death, Jonathan speaks good of David, Saul swears and relents, war again, the javelin, Michal lets David down through a window, the image in the bed, David flees to Samuel at Naioth, Saul's messengers prophesy, Saul himself overcome (19:24 told without nakedness). Avoids 19:1, 19:5, 19:11. Keys 19:4, 19:12, 19:18.
TITLE: Michal Let David Down Through a Window

HOOK: Soldiers are watching a house, waiting for a man to come out at first light. Inside, a young wife is helping her husband climb out of a back window. He drops to the ground and runs into the dark. Then she goes back to the bedroom, puts a household statue in the bed, covers it with a blanket, and puts goat’s hair where his head should be. And waits for the soldiers to knock.

WHERE: In chapter eighteen, the soul of Jonathan was knit with the soul of David, and Jonathan gave David his robe and his sword. But when the women sang that David had done more than Saul, Saul eyed David from that day forward. He threw a spear at him, and tried to have him fall in battle. But David behaved himself wisely in all his ways, and the LORD was with him.

Now, in chapter nineteen, Saul’s jealousy becomes open hatred. Listen for the people who protect David. And listen for what happens when Saul himself goes after him.

STORY: Saul speaks to his son Jonathan, and to all his servants, and tells them to put David to death.

Saul no longer hides it. He wants David dead, and he says so openly. Even to Jonathan, David’s closest friend.

But Jonathan delights in David. He tells David, my father is trying to take your life. Be careful. Hide in a secret place in the field. I will go out and stand beside my father in that field, and speak to him about you. And I will tell you what I find out.

So Jonathan speaks to his father.

Jonathan reminds Saul. David risked his life when he faced the Philistine, and the LORD won a great victory for all Israel. You saw it, and you rejoiced. Why would you sin against an innocent man, without any reason?

And Saul listens to Jonathan. He swears, as the LORD lives, David shall not be put to death.

So Jonathan calls David, and tells him everything, and brings him back to Saul. And David serves Saul as before.

For a while, there is peace. Jonathan was a peacemaker, brave enough to stand up to his own father for what was right.

But then war breaks out again with the Philistines. David goes out and fights them, and wins a great victory, and they flee from him.

And once again a troubling spirit from the LORD comes upon Saul, as he sits in his house with his spear in his hand, while David plays the harp. Saul tries to pin David to the wall with the spear. But David slips away, and the spear sticks in the wall. And David runs, and escapes.

Saul’s promise lasted only until David’s next success. Jealousy is very hard to uproot.

Saul sends men to watch David’s house, to put him to death when he comes out. But Michal, David’s wife, warns him. If you do not escape now, she says, you will not live to see the light.

Michal takes a household idol, lays it in the bed, puts a pillow of goat’s hair at its head, and covers it with a cloth. When Saul’s messengers come for David, she says, he is sick.

Saul sends them back. Bring him to me in his bed, he says. When they go in, there is the idol in the bed, with goat’s hair at its head.

Saul is furious with Michal. Why did you deceive me, and let my enemy escape? And Michal lies again, saying David threatened her.

It is a strange detail that Michal had a household idol in her home. It shows us that even in David’s house, not everyone was wholly devoted to the LORD.

Meanwhile, David runs.

When Saul hears that David is at Naioth, he sends messengers to capture him. But when they arrive, they see a group of prophets prophesying, with Samuel standing as their leader. And the Spirit of God comes upon Saul’s messengers, and they also prophesy.

Saul sends more messengers, and they prophesy too. He sends a third group, and they prophesy also.

So Saul goes himself. And on the way, the Spirit of God comes upon him too, and he prophesies all the way to Naioth. He throws off his royal robes, and lies down before Samuel, overcome, for a whole day. And so the people say again, is Saul also among the prophets?

Think about what God did. Saul came with soldiers to capture David. And God simply stopped him, by his Spirit. Saul could not lay a finger on the one God had chosen. God protected David without a single sword being drawn.

KEY 19:4: 'And Jonathan spake good of David unto Saul his father, and said unto him, Let not the king sin against his servant, against David; because he hath not sinned against thee, and because his works have been to thee-ward very good:' Jonathan spake good of David. It took courage to defend David to an angry king, especially when that king was his own father. Jonathan risked his father’s anger to speak up for an innocent man. Speaking good of others, especially when they are being attacked, is a brave and godly thing to do.

KEY 19:12: 'So Michal let David down through a window: and he went, and fled, and escaped.' David, who faced the giant, the hero of Israel, escapes like a thief, down through a window. Sometimes even God’s chosen ones must run. Running from danger is not a lack of faith. God used Michal’s quick thinking to save David’s life. God protects his people in many different ways.

KEY 19:18: 'So David fled, and escaped, and came to Samuel to Ramah, and told him all that Saul had done to him. And he and Samuel went and dwelt in Naioth.' When David was in trouble, he went to Samuel, the old prophet, the man of God. He told him everything. When life falls apart, it is wise to go to God, and to wise, godly people who can pray with us and help us. David did not try to face it alone.

THREAD: A psalm of David, written when Saul sent men to watch his house, says, 'Deliver me from mine enemies, O my God: defend me from them that rise up against me.' And a proverb says, 'The name of the LORD is a strong tower: the righteous runneth into it, and is safe.'

TODAY: Is there someone being spoken against, whom you could speak good of today? Be a Jonathan for them. And if you are facing trouble yourself, do not face it alone. Go to God, and to someone wise who can pray with you.

QUESTION: When David was in danger, God protected him through Jonathan, through Michal, and through his Spirit. How have you seen God protect you, or others, through different people and ways?

PRAYER: Lord God, you are my strong tower, and my defence. Thank you for the people you have sent to protect and help me. Give me courage to speak good of others, even when it is hard. And when I am in trouble, help me to run to you, and not to face it alone. Amen.
