# 1 Samuel 20: David comes to Jonathan, but a step between me and death, the plan at the new moon feast, Jonathan's covenant with the house of David, the three arrows, David's empty place, Saul's rage at Jonathan (avoids 20:30), the javelin, the arrows beyond thee, the parting, go in peace. Keys 20:3, 20:17, 20:42.
TITLE: The LORD Be Between Me and Thee for Ever

HOOK: A young man stands in a field, with a bow in his hand and a boy beside him. He fires an arrow, far over the boy’s head. Then he shouts, is not the arrow beyond you? Hurry, do not stop! But hidden behind a stone, someone else is listening to every word. And those words will decide whether he lives or dies.

WHERE: In chapter nineteen, Saul ordered his servants to put David to death. Jonathan spake good of David, and Saul promised not to harm him. But when Saul threw his spear again, Michal let David down through a window, and he fled to Samuel. Saul came after him, but the Spirit of God came upon Saul, and he could not touch David.

Now, in chapter twenty, David comes to Jonathan for help. Listen for the plan the two friends make. And listen for the promise they make to each other, for themselves and for their families.

STORY: David runs from Naioth, and comes to Jonathan. What have I done, he asks. What is my crime? What is my sin against your father, that he is trying to take my life?

Jonathan says, it cannot be. You will not die. My father does nothing without telling me. Why would he hide this from me?

But David is sure.

Jonathan says, whatever you want me to do, I will do it for you.

So they make a plan. David says, there is a feast for the new moon, when I should be sitting at the king’s table. Let me hide in the field instead. If your father notices I am missing, tell him I asked to go home to Bethlehem for a family sacrifice. If he says, all right, then I am safe. But if he is very angry, then you will know he has decided to harm me.

Then they go out into the field together. And Jonathan makes a solemn promise before the LORD. If my father means you good, I will send and tell you. If he means you harm, I will tell you, and send you away in peace. And may the LORD be with you, as he has been with my father.

Then Jonathan asks something in return. Show me the kindness of the LORD while I live, he says. And even after I am gone, never cut off your kindness from my family, not even when the LORD has removed all your enemies from the earth.

Jonathan knows that David will one day be king, and asks him to protect his family.

And so Jonathan makes a covenant with the house of David.

Then Jonathan explains the signal. Hide by the stone Ezel. I will shoot three arrows to the side of it, as if I were shooting at a target. Then I will send a boy to find them. If I say, the arrows are on this side of you, then come, for you are safe. But if I say, the arrows are beyond you, then go, for the LORD has sent you away.

So David hides in the field. When the new moon comes, the king sits down to eat, in his usual place by the wall. Abner sits beside him. And David’s place is empty.

Saul says nothing that first day. He thinks something must have happened to make David unclean, unable to come to the feast.

But on the second day, David’s place is still empty. And Saul asks Jonathan, why has the son of Jesse not come to the meal?

Jonathan gives the answer they agreed. David asked me if he could go to Bethlehem, for a family sacrifice.

And Saul explodes with rage. He insults Jonathan and his mother. Do you not know, he says, that as long as the son of Jesse lives, you will never be king? Now send and bring him to me, for he must die.

Jonathan says, why should he die? What has he done?

And Saul hurls his spear at Jonathan, his own son. Now Jonathan knows for certain that his father is determined to put David to death. He leaves the table in fierce anger, and eats nothing that day. He is grieved for David, because his father has treated him so shamefully.

Saul’s jealousy has turned him against his own son.

Early the next day, Jonathan goes out to the field with a young boy. Run and find the arrows I shoot, he says. As the boy runs, Jonathan shoots an arrow beyond him. And he calls out, is not the arrow beyond you? Hurry, be quick, do not stop! The boy knows nothing. Only Jonathan and David understand.

As soon as the boy is gone, David comes out from his hiding place, and bows to the ground three times. And the two friends kiss each other, and weep together, David most of all.

And Jonathan says his last words of the chapter.

Then David leaves, and Jonathan goes back to the city.

KEY 20:3: 'And David sware moreover, and said, Thy father certainly knoweth that I have found grace in thine eyes; and he saith, Let not Jonathan know this, lest he be grieved: but truly as the LORD liveth, and as thy soul liveth, there is but a step between me and death.' There is but a step between me and death. David felt how fragile his life was. One wrong step, one thrown spear, and it would be over. Every one of us is only a step from death. That is not meant to make us afraid, but to make us wise, and to hold each day as a gift from God.

KEY 20:17: 'And Jonathan caused David to swear again, because he loved him: for he loved him as he loved his own soul.' Jonathan loved David as his own soul. Jesus said that we should love our neighbour as ourselves. Jonathan lived that out. He gave up his own claim to the throne, risked his father’s anger, and faced a spear, all for his friend. That is what real love looks like.

KEY 20:42: 'And Jonathan said to David, Go in peace, forasmuch as we have sworn both of us in the name of the LORD, saying, The LORD be between me and thee, and between my seed and thy seed for ever. And he arose and departed: and Jonathan went into the city.' The LORD be between me and thee. Their friendship was built on God. And it would reach beyond them to their children. Many years later, David would keep this promise, and show great kindness to Jonathan’s son. Friendships built on God last.

THREAD: Jesus said, 'Greater love hath no man than this, that a man lay down his life for his friends.' And a proverb says, 'there is a friend that sticketh closer than a brother.'

TODAY: Think of a friend God has given you. Thank God for them today, and tell them what they mean to you. And if you know someone who is struggling, be the friend who stands beside them.

QUESTION: Jonathan and David built their friendship on God. How does sharing faith in God make a friendship stronger?

PRAYER: Lord God, thank you for the gift of friendship. Thank you for friends who love me as their own soul. Help me to be that kind of friend to others. And thank you that, in Jesus, I have a friend who laid down his life for me, and who sticks closer than a brother. Amen.
