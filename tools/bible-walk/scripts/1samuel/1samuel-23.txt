# 1 Samuel 23: Keilah robbed, David enquires of the LORD twice, Keilah saved, Abiathar and the ephod, Saul thinks David is shut in, will the men of Keilah deliver me up, the wilderness of Ziph, Saul sought him every day, Jonathan strengthened his hand in God, the Ziphites betray him, the mountain of Maon, the Philistines invade, the rock of escape. Keys 23:2, 23:14, 23:16.
TITLE: Jonathan Strengthened His Hand in God

HOOK: A hunted man is hiding in a forest. The king and his army are searching every hill and valley for him, day after day. He is exhausted, and probably afraid. Then he hears footsteps. Someone is coming through the trees. It is not a soldier. It is his best friend, the king’s own son, who has risked everything to come and find him. And he has come with words of hope.

WHERE: In chapter twenty two, people in distress, in debt and discontented gathered to David in the cave of Adullam. But Doeg the Edomite told Saul that the priests of Nob had helped David, and Saul brought terrible judgment on the priests. Only Abiathar escaped, and David said, abide thou with me, fear not, with me thou shalt be in safeguard.

Now, in chapter twenty three, David keeps asking God what to do. Listen for how David makes his decisions. And listen for the visit that strengthens him in the wilderness.

STORY: David is told that the Philistines are attacking the town of Keilah, and robbing the threshing floors, where the grain is harvested.

David is on the run himself. He could have said, that is not my problem. But he does something else first.

David’s men are not keen. We are afraid here in Judah, they say. How much more if we go to Keilah against the Philistine army?

So David asks the LORD again. And the LORD answers, go down to Keilah, for I will give the Philistines into your hand.

So David and his men go to Keilah, and fight the Philistines, and save the people of the town.

David did not just ask once, and then do what he wanted. When his men were afraid, he went back to God and asked again. He made sure he was following God, not just his own feelings.

Abiathar the priest, who escaped from Nob, has come to David, bringing the ephod. Through the ephod, David can ask the LORD for guidance.

Saul hears that David is in Keilah. And Saul says, God has given him into my hands. He has trapped himself, by going into a town with gates and bars.

How sad. Saul uses God’s name to justify his hatred. He thinks God is on his side, when God has left him.

So Saul calls up his army to besiege Keilah.

David finds out, and says to Abiathar, bring the ephod. And David prays. O LORD God of Israel, I have heard that Saul plans to come to Keilah and destroy the town because of me. Will the men of Keilah hand me over to him? Will Saul come down? And the LORD says, he will come down.

David asks, will the men of Keilah hand me and my men over to Saul? And the LORD says, they will.

David had just saved them. And still they would hand him over, out of fear of Saul.

So David and his men, about six hundred now, leave Keilah, and go wherever they can. When Saul hears David has escaped, he gives up the plan.

Then David stays in the wilderness, in strongholds, in the hill country of Ziph.

David is in the wilderness of Ziph, in a forest, knowing that Saul is out to take his life.

And then Jonathan comes.

Jonathan says, do not be afraid. My father Saul will not find you. You will be king over Israel, and I will be next to you. Even my father Saul knows it.

And the two friends renew their covenant before the LORD. Then David stays in the forest, and Jonathan goes home.

This is the last time David and Jonathan will meet.

Then the people of Ziph go to Saul, and betray David. Is not David hiding among us, in the strongholds, on the hill of Hachilah? Come down, O king, and we will hand him over to you.

And Saul says, the LORD bless you, for you have had compassion on me. Again, Saul uses God’s name for his own purposes.

Saul and his men go after David. David hears of it, and goes down to a rock in the wilderness of Maon. Saul chases him there.

Saul is going along one side of the mountain, and David and his men are on the other side, hurrying to get away. Saul and his men are closing in around them, about to capture them.

And then a messenger comes running to Saul. Hurry and come! The Philistines have invaded the land!

So Saul stops chasing David, and goes to fight the Philistines. And the place is called the rock of escape.

Just in time. God’s timing was perfect. David was surrounded, with no way out. And God sent a messenger at exactly the right moment.

KEY 23:2: 'Therefore David enquired of the LORD, saying, Shall I go and smite these Philistines? And the LORD said unto David, Go, and smite the Philistines, and save Keilah.' David enquired of the LORD. This is the difference between David and Saul. Saul acted on his own ideas and feelings. David asked God first. Shall I go? And even when his men were afraid, he asked again. Before big decisions, and even small ones, ask God.

KEY 23:14: 'And David abode in the wilderness in strong holds, and remained in a mountain in the wilderness of Ziph. And Saul sought him every day, but God delivered him not into his hand.' Saul sought him every day. But God delivered him not into his hand. Every single day, the king of Israel, with all his soldiers, was hunting one man. And every single day, God kept him safe. No enemy is stronger than God’s protection.

KEY 23:16: 'And Jonathan Saul’s son arose, and went to David into the wood, and strengthened his hand in God.' Jonathan strengthened his hand in God. He did not just say, cheer up. He pointed David to God, and to God’s promises. You will be king. God has said so. That is the best kind of encouragement. Not just making someone feel better, but reminding them of who God is, and what he has promised.

THREAD: James wrote, 'If any of you lack wisdom, let him ask of God, that giveth to all men liberally'. And Paul wrote, 'Wherefore comfort yourselves together, and edify one another'.

TODAY: Is there a decision in front of you? Enquire of the LORD before you act. And think of someone who is going through a wilderness time. Go to them, like Jonathan, and strengthen their hand in God.

QUESTION: Jonathan strengthened David’s hand in God by reminding him of God’s promises. Who has done that for you, and who could you do it for?

PRAYER: Lord God, thank you that you guide those who ask you. Teach me to enquire of you before I act, and to ask again when I am unsure. Thank you that no enemy is stronger than your protection. Make me a friend like Jonathan, who strengthens others in you. Amen.
