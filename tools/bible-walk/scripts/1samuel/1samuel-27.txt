# 1 Samuel 27: David said in his heart, I shall now perish; flight to Achish of Gath with six hundred men; Saul gives up; Ziklag; raids on Israel's old enemies and the deception of Achish (told plainly, not praised, no details); Achish trusts David. Keys 27:1, 27:4, 27:12.
TITLE: David Said in His Heart

HOOK: A man has been running for years. He has escaped spears, armies, betrayals and traps. God has rescued him again and again. And then one day, he sits down, and says to himself, it is no use. One of these days, Saul is going to get me. And with that thought, he makes a decision that will lead him into some of the darkest days of his life.

WHERE: In chapter twenty six, David crept into Saul’s camp, and found the king asleep with his spear beside his head. Abishai wanted to strike, but David said, destroy him not, who can stretch forth his hand against the LORD’s anointed? He took the spear and the water jar, and called to Saul from a hilltop. And Saul said, I have played the fool, and have erred exceedingly.

Now, in chapter twenty seven, David makes a surprising choice. Listen for what David says in his heart. And notice who he does not ask.

STORY: David says in his heart.

Think about that. God had promised David the kingdom. Samuel had anointed him. Jonathan had told him, you will be king. Even Saul had admitted it. God had saved David again and again, from the lion and the bear, from Goliath, from Saul’s spear, from the cave, from Keilah, from the wilderness. And yet David says, I shall now perish.

And notice what is missing. In chapter twenty three, David enquired of the LORD again and again. Here, there is no prayer, no asking God. David talks to himself, not to God. And his own heart tells him to give up.

So David goes, with his six hundred men, to Achish the son of Maoch, the king of Gath.

Gath. Goliath’s city again. The last time David went to Gath, he had to pretend to be mad to escape. Now he goes back, with his whole band of men and their families, including his two wives, Ahinoam and Abigail.

And it works, in a way.

Then David says to Achish, if I have found favour with you, let me have a place in one of the country towns, where I can live. Why should your servant live in the royal city with you?

So Achish gives David the town of Ziklag. And the writer adds that Ziklag has belonged to the kings of Judah ever since.

David lives in the land of the Philistines for a year and four months.

During that time, David and his men go out and raid Israel’s old enemies in the south, the Geshurites, the Girzites and the Amalekites, peoples who had lived in the land since ancient times. They bring back sheep, cattle, donkeys, camels and clothing.

But when Achish asks, where did you raid today, David lies to him. He says, against the south of Judah, and against the peoples who live alongside Judah. He wants Achish to believe he is attacking his own people. And to make sure no one can tell Achish the truth, David leaves no one alive from the places he attacks, to carry news back to Gath.

The Bible tells us this plainly. It does not praise it. David was living a double life. Pretending to serve the Philistines, while really fighting their allies. Lying to the king who trusted him. And the cost of keeping that lie was terrible.

And Achish believes him.

This is a dark chapter in David’s life. The man after God’s own heart has stopped asking God for guidance, and is living by his own wits, among God’s enemies, keeping up a dangerous lie.

And yet God has not abandoned him. God was still at work, and the town of Ziklag would one day belong to the kings of Judah. In the next chapters, we will see how David’s choice comes back to trouble him, and how God brings him through.

KEY 27:1: 'And David said in his heart, I shall now perish one day by the hand of Saul: there is nothing better for me than that I should speedily escape into the land of the Philistines; and Saul shall despair of me, to seek me any more in any coast of Israel: so shall I escape out of his hand.' David said in his heart. Not, David enquired of the LORD. When we are tired and discouraged, it is easy to listen to our own fearful thoughts, instead of to God. Our hearts can tell us lies. That is why we need to keep bringing our fears to God, and listening to his promises.

KEY 27:4: 'And it was told Saul that David was fled to Gath: and he sought no more again for him.' David’s plan worked. Saul stopped chasing him. Sometimes a choice made without God seems to succeed. The immediate problem goes away. But that does not mean it was the right choice. Success is not always a sign of God’s approval.

KEY 27:12: 'And Achish believed David, saying, He hath made his people Israel utterly to abhor him; therefore he shall be my servant for ever.' Achish trusted David completely. And David was deceiving him. David had escaped Saul, but he had walked into a trap of his own making. Now he had to keep up the lie, and soon Achish would ask him to fight against Israel itself. One lie often leads to another, and then another, until we are trapped.

THREAD: Jeremiah wrote, 'The heart is deceitful above all things'. And a psalm says, 'Why art thou cast down, O my soul? and why art thou disquieted in me? hope thou in God'.

TODAY: Are you listening more to your own discouraged thoughts than to God? Speak to your soul today, as the psalm says: hope thou in God. Bring your fears to him, and listen for his promises.

QUESTION: David had seen God rescue him many times, yet he said, I shall now perish one day by the hand of Saul. Why do you think discouragement can make us forget what God has already done?

PRAYER: Lord God, forgive me for the times I have listened to my own fears instead of you. When I am tired and discouraged, remind me of all the ways you have rescued me. Keep me from lies and double lives. Help me to hope in you, and to keep asking you for guidance. Amen.
