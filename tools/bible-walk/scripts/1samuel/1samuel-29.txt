# 1 Samuel 29: the Philistines gather at Aphek, David in the rear with Achish, the princes object, Achish's praise of David, go in peace, as an angel of God, David's protest, sent back to Ziklag. Avoids 29:5. Keys 29:6, 29:9, 29:11.
TITLE: Return, and Go in Peace

HOOK: Thousands of Philistine soldiers are marching to war against Israel, rank after rank. And near the back of the army, marching with the king of Gath, is a band of six hundred Hebrews, led by the man who will one day be Israel’s king. He is about to be forced to fight his own people. And he has no way out.

WHERE: In chapter twenty eight, the Philistines gathered against Israel, and Achish told David he must go with him to battle. Saul, terrified, enquired of the LORD, but the LORD answered him not. So Saul went in disguise to a medium at Endor, and heard a word of judgment: the LORD hath rent the kingdom out of thine hand, and given it to David.

Now, in chapter twenty nine, we go back to David, marching with the Philistines. Listen for how David is rescued from the trap he made for himself. And notice who does the rescuing.

STORY: The Philistines gather all their armies at Aphek. And the Israelites camp by a spring in Jezreel.

The lords of the Philistines march past with their troops, in units of hundreds and thousands. And at the back, David and his men march with Achish.

Think about how David must have felt. He had lied his way into this position. Now he was marching to fight against Saul, and against Israel, his own people, against Jonathan, his dearest friend. Would he really fight them? Or would he turn on the Philistines in the middle of the battle? We are not told what David planned to do. Perhaps he did not know himself.

The commanders of the Philistines see David and his men, and ask, what are these Hebrews doing here?

And Achish defends him.

But the Philistine commanders are angry. Send this man back, they say, to the place you gave him. Do not let him go down with us into battle, or he will turn against us in the middle of the fight. What better way could he win back his master’s favour, than with the heads of our men? Is this not the David they sang about in their dances, the one who defeated tens of thousands?

The Philistine commanders were more suspicious than Achish, and in a way they were right. They did not trust a Hebrew hero to fight against his own people.

So Achish calls David, and speaks to him.

Achish goes on. So go back now, in peace, and do not displease the lords of the Philistines.

And David protests. But what have I done? What have you found against your servant, from the day I came to you until now, that I may not go and fight against the enemies of my lord the king?

What does David mean? My lord the king. Achish would hear it as, I want to fight for you, Achish. But was David secretly thinking of Saul, his real lord the king? The words are carefully unclear. Even now, David is keeping up his double life.

Achish answers.

Achish tells David to get up early with his men, and leave as soon as it is light.

And so it happens.

Think about how God rescued David. David did not pray his way out of this. He had not even asked God about going to Gath. But God, in his mercy, used the suspicion of the Philistine commanders to send David home. David did not have to fight Israel. He did not have to fight Jonathan. His hands would stay clean of his own people’s blood.

God is so faithful, even when we are not. David had got himself into this mess. And God got him out.

But David is about to discover that something terrible has happened while he was away. As we will see in the next chapter, getting sent home was not only a rescue. It was God putting David exactly where he needed to be.

KEY 29:6: 'Then Achish called David, and said unto him, Surely, as the LORD liveth, thou hast been upright, and thy going out and thy coming in with me in the host is good in my sight: for I have not found evil in thee since the day of thy coming unto me unto this day: nevertheless the lords favour thee not.' How strange to hear a Philistine king swear by the LORD, and praise David as upright. Achish had been deceived, and yet he spoke of David with kindness. It is a little sad. David had earned this man’s trust through lies. God was still at work, even through people who did not know him.

KEY 29:9: 'And Achish answered and said to David, I know that thou art good in my sight, as an angel of God: notwithstanding the princes of the Philistines have said, He shall not go up with us to the battle.' As an angel of God. Achish truly liked David. But the other commanders said no. Sometimes God uses the very people who oppose us to protect us. The rejection that seemed unfair was actually God’s rescue.

KEY 29:11: 'So David and his men rose up early to depart in the morning, to return into the land of the Philistines. And the Philistines went up to Jezreel.' Two roads part here. The Philistines go up to Jezreel, to the battle where Saul will fall. David goes back to Ziklag. God kept David away from that battle. He would not be there when Saul and Jonathan died. No one could ever say that David had a hand in it.

THREAD: Paul wrote, 'If we believe not, yet he abideth faithful: he cannot deny himself.' And a proverb says, 'The king’s heart is in the hand of the LORD, as the rivers of water: he turneth it whithersoever he will.'

TODAY: Is there a door that has closed for you, and you are disappointed? Ask God today whether that closed door might be his protection. Thank him that he is faithful, even when we are not.

QUESTION: David got himself into a trap, and God got him out through people who did not even know God. Have you ever seen God rescue you from a situation you made for yourself?

PRAYER: Lord God, thank you that you are faithful, even when I am not. Thank you for the times you have closed doors to protect me. Forgive me for the messes I have made by not asking you. Keep my hands clean, and lead me back to where you want me to be. Amen.
