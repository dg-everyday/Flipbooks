# 1 Samuel 31: the battle on mount Gilboa (no "slain/slew"), Jonathan and his brothers fall, Saul badly wounded takes his own life (told gently, avoids 31:4), the armourbearer, Israel flees, the Philistines dishonour Saul (gently), the men of Jabesh-gilead bring the bodies home and bury them, the fast. Looking back over the book and ahead to 2 Samuel. Keys 31:6, 31:11, 31:12.
TITLE: Saul Died, and His Three Sons

HOOK: On a mountainside, as the battle rages, a king stands wounded. His sons have fallen. His army is running. The enemy is closing in. The tallest, most impressive man in Israel, the king the people asked for, is about to reach the end of his story. And the end is as sad as any in the Bible.

WHERE: In chapter thirty, David found Ziklag burned and the families taken. His men talked of turning on him, but David encouraged himself in the LORD his God. He asked the LORD, and God said, pursue, and without fail recover all. And David recovered everything. He made a rule that those who stayed by the supplies should share alike with those who fought.

Now we come to the last chapter of the first book of Samuel. This is a sad chapter, and we will tell it gently. Listen for how Saul’s story ends. And listen for the men who remember him with kindness.

STORY: The Philistines fight against Israel. And the men of Israel flee before the Philistines, and many fall on mount Gilboa.

The Philistines press hard after Saul and his sons. And Jonathan falls, and his brothers Abinadab and Melchi-shua, the sons of Saul.

Jonathan. David’s dearest friend. The prince who gave David his robe and his sword. The man who strengthened David’s hand in God. He dies fighting beside his father, faithful to the end, even though his father had treated him so badly.

The battle goes hard against Saul. The archers find him, and he is badly wounded.

Saul asks his armour bearer to end his life, so that the Philistines will not capture him and mock him. But the armour bearer is too afraid, and will not do it. And so Saul, badly wounded and with no way out, takes his own life. When the armour bearer sees that Saul is dead, he dies with him.

It is a deeply sad ending. Saul began so well, humble and small in his own eyes, filled with God’s Spirit. But he turned away from God, little by little. He feared the people more than God. He grew jealous, and spent years hunting an innocent man. And in the end, he died in despair, on a mountain, with the enemy closing in.

When the Israelites on the other side of the valley, and across the Jordan, see that Saul and his sons are dead, they abandon their towns and flee. And the Philistines come and live in them.

The next day, the Philistines come to take what they can from the fallen. They find Saul and his three sons lying on mount Gilboa. They take Saul’s armour, and dishonour his body, and send news of their victory throughout their land, to the temples of their idols and to their people. They put his armour in the temple of their goddess. And they fasten his body to the wall of the town of Beth-shan.

It is a shameful end for the king of Israel. The very Philistines he was anointed to fight now parade his defeat before their gods.

But then, the writer tells us one more thing.

Do you remember Jabesh-gilead? In chapter eleven, at the very beginning of Saul’s reign, the Ammonites threatened the town with a cruel demand. And the Spirit of God came upon Saul, and he gathered all Israel, and rescued them. That was Saul at his very best. And the people of Jabesh-gilead never forgot it.

They bury the bones of Saul and his sons under a tree at Jabesh, and fast for seven days.

And so the first book of Samuel ends. It began with Hannah, praying for a son. It told of Samuel, who heard God’s voice. Of Saul, who looked like a king, but whose heart turned from God. And of David, the shepherd boy, whom God chose because he looked on the heart.

David is not yet king. He is in Ziklag, and does not yet know what has happened on mount Gilboa. But the way is now open. The second book of Samuel will tell how David becomes king, and what kind of king he will be.

KEY 31:6: 'So Saul died, and his three sons, and his armourbearer, and all his men, that same day together.' That same day together. Everything Samuel had told Saul came true. The book is honest about the cost of turning away from God. Saul’s disobedience did not only hurt himself. It brought sorrow on his family, his soldiers and his whole nation. Our choices affect the people around us, for good or for ill.

KEY 31:11: 'And when the inhabitants of Jabesh-gilead heard of that which the Philistines had done to Saul;' The people of Jabesh-gilead remembered what Saul had done for them, long ago, when he rescued their town. They did not forget kindness, even after all the years, even after Saul had fallen so far. Gratitude remembers. A single act of kindness can be remembered for a lifetime.

KEY 31:12: 'All the valiant men arose, and went all night, and took the body of Saul and the bodies of his sons from the wall of Beth-shan, and came to Jabesh, and burnt them there.' All the valiant men arose. They risked their lives, travelling through the dark into enemy territory, to give their king an honourable burial. It is a beautiful act of loyalty and love at the end of a sad story. Even when someone has failed badly, we can still honour the good they did.

THREAD: The Preacher wrote, 'Remember now thy Creator in the days of thy youth'. And Paul wrote, 'Wherefore let him that thinketh he standeth take heed lest he fall.'

TODAY: Saul’s story is a warning about drifting from God little by little. Is there an area of your life where you have been slowly drifting? Turn back to God today, while there is time. And remember someone who was kind to you long ago, and thank them if you can.

QUESTION: Saul began well, but ended badly, while the men of Jabesh-gilead remembered his kindness. What do you hope people will remember about you, and how can you live that way now?

PRAYER: Lord God, Saul’s story is a sad one. Keep me from drifting away from you, little by little. Help me to finish well, and not only to start well. Thank you for those who have been kind to me, and help me to remember them with gratitude. And thank you that your plans go on, even through sorrow. Amen.
