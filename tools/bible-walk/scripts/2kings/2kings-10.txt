# 2 Kings 10: Jehu's letters to Samaria, the leaders submit and the rest of Ahab's house is ended (10:6-8, 10:14 not told in detail, no counts or heads), nothing of the word of the LORD shall fall, Jehonadab (is thine heart right), see my zeal, the Baal assembly and its end (10:25 told in one line), the house of Baal destroyed, but the golden calves kept, the fourth generation, took no heed with all his heart, Hazael cuts Israel short. Hosea 1:4 cited (God later judged Jehu's bloodshed). Keys 10:10, 10:16, 10:31.
TITLE: See My Zeal for the LORD

HOOK: A man stands in the middle of a city square, and shouts to the crowd. Ahab served Baal a little. But I will serve him much! Call all the prophets of Baal, all his priests, all his worshippers. Not one must be missing. I am going to hold a great sacrifice for Baal. And the crowd is amazed. Has the new king become a Baal worshipper too? But he has a secret plan.

WHERE: In chapter nine, a young prophet anointed Jehu king over Israel. Jehu drove furiously to Jezreel, and in the field of Naboth, Ahab’s son Joram fell, just as God had said. Ahaziah of Judah died too. And Jezebel met her end in Jezreel, according to the word of the LORD by Elijah.

Now, in chapter ten, Jehu finishes the task God gave him, and more. This is another hard chapter, and we will tell it gently. Listen for Jehu’s zeal. And listen for what the chapter says about his heart.

STORY: Ahab still had many sons living in Samaria, the capital. So Jehu writes letters to the leaders of Samaria, and to those who were bringing up Ahab’s sons. You have chariots, horses, a strong city and weapons. Choose the best of your master’s sons, put him on the throne, and fight for your master’s house.

But the leaders are terrified. Two kings could not stand against him, they say. How can we? So they send a message to Jehu. We are your servants. We will do whatever you say. We will not make anyone king.

So Jehu commands them to bring an end to Ahab’s family, and they do. And Jehu stands before the people, and says something that sums up what has happened.

Then Jehu puts an end to all who were left of Ahab’s family and his officials in Jezreel. And on the way to Samaria, he also puts to death some relatives of the king of Judah, who were connected to Ahab’s house.

On the road, Jehu meets a man named Jehonadab, the son of Rechab, a man known for his faithfulness to the LORD. Is your heart right with mine, as mine is with yours? Jehu asks. Jehonadab says, it is. Then give me your hand, Jehu says, and he pulls him up into his chariot.

And Jehu says something revealing.

When Jehu arrives in Samaria, he finishes off what remained of Ahab’s house, according to the word the LORD spoke to Elijah.

Then comes Jehu’s secret plan. He gathers the people and announces, 'Ahab served Baal a little; but Jehu shall serve him much.' Call all the prophets, servants and priests of Baal. I am holding a great sacrifice for Baal. Anyone who does not come will not live. 'But Jehu did it in subtilty, to the intent that he might destroy the worshippers of Baal.' Subtilty means cunning, a trick.

So all the worshippers of Baal in Israel come. The temple of Baal is packed, from one end to the other. Jehu has special robes brought out for every worshipper, so that they can all be recognised. And he tells them, make sure there are no servants of the LORD here with you, only worshippers of Baal.

Then, when the sacrifices begin, Jehu’s guards go in, and the worshippers of Baal are put to death. They bring out the images from the temple of Baal and burn them. They break down the image of Baal, and tear down his temple, and turn it into a rubbish heap. 'Thus Jehu destroyed Baal out of Israel.'

So far, it sounds like a great victory for the LORD. Baal worship, which Ahab and Jezebel had brought into Israel, was wiped out.

But then the writer adds a sad word. 'Howbeit from the sins of Jeroboam the son of Nebat, who made Israel to sin, Jehu departed not from after them, to wit, the golden calves that were in Bethel, and that were in Dan.' Jehu got rid of Baal. But he kept Jeroboam’s golden calves. He destroyed the idols that belonged to his enemies, but kept the ones that were useful to him, to keep the people away from Jerusalem.

God does tell Jehu that because he did what was right in carrying out judgment on the house of Ahab, his sons will sit on the throne of Israel to the fourth generation.

But.

And many years later, the prophet Hosea brought a word from God, that he would bring judgment on the house of Jehu, for the bloodshed at Jezreel. Jehu had gone further than God had asked, and with a heart that was not right.

In those days, the writer says, God began to make Israel smaller. Hazael, king of Syria, just as Elisha had foreseen, took land from Israel all along the east side of the Jordan. After many years as king, Jehu died, and his son Jehoahaz reigned in his place.

KEY 10:10: 'Know now that there shall fall unto the earth nothing of the word of the LORD, which the LORD spake concerning the house of Ahab: for the LORD hath done that which he spake by his servant Elijah.' Nothing shall fall to the earth. Not one word God spoke through Elijah went unfulfilled. It may have taken years. But it all came true. This is a hard reminder, but also a comfort. If God’s warnings come true, so do all his promises.

KEY 10:16: 'And he said, Come with me, and see my zeal for the LORD. So they made him ride in his chariot.' Zeal means burning eagerness. But notice the words, see my zeal. Jehu wanted people to watch him, and admire how devoted he was. True zeal for God does not need an audience. Jesus warned against doing good works to be seen by others. When we serve God, we should want people to see him, not us.

KEY 10:31: 'But Jehu took no heed to walk in the law of the LORD God of Israel with all his heart: for he departed not from the sins of Jeroboam, which made Israel to sin.' With all his heart. That was what was missing. Jehu was energetic, bold and effective. He did some of what God wanted. But his heart was not fully given to God. It is possible to be busy for God, and even successful, without truly loving him. God wants all of our heart.

THREAD: The LORD told Hosea, 'for yet a little while, and I will avenge the blood of Jezreel upon the house of Jehu'. And Jesus said, 'This people draweth nigh unto me with their mouth, and honoureth me with their lips; but their heart is far from me.'

TODAY: Is there something in your life you have been keeping back from God, like Jehu’s golden calves, because it is useful to you? Ask God to show you. And ask him for a heart that wants to serve him, not to be seen, but out of love.

QUESTION: Jehu said, come and see my zeal for the LORD, but his heart was not fully given to God. How can you tell the difference between serving God to be seen, and serving him from the heart?

PRAYER: Lord God, thank you that not one of your words fails. Search my heart. Show me any golden calves I am holding on to. I do not want to serve you only with energy, or to be seen by others. I want to love you with all my heart. Make my heart whole, and true to you. Amen.
