# 2 Kings 11: Athaliah destroys the seed royal, Jehosheba hides the baby Joash and his nurse, hid in the house of the LORD (years only inside the quote), Jehoiada's covenant with the captains, David's spears and shields, the crowning (God save the king), Athaliah cries treason and is put to death (one line), the covenant that they should be the LORD'S people, the house of Baal broken down (Mattan's death not quoted), the boy king on the throne, the city in quiet. Keys 11:2, 11:12, 11:17.
TITLE: Hid in the House of the LORD

HOOK: A young woman slips quietly through the palace, holding a tiny baby close to her chest. Behind her, terrible things are happening. The queen has ordered that every child of the royal family must die. The young woman does not stop until she reaches a small storeroom, deep inside the temple of God. There, she hides the baby, and his nurse. If he is found, they will all be lost. But if he lives, the promise of God lives with him.

WHERE: In chapter ten, Jehu wiped out the house of Ahab, and destroyed the worship of Baal in Israel. He said, come, and see my zeal for the LORD. But he kept Jeroboam’s golden calves, and did not walk with God with all his heart.

Now, in chapter eleven, the story moves south, to Jerusalem. Ahab’s daughter is still alive there. Listen for how close David’s family comes to being wiped out. And listen for one brave woman and one faithful priest.

STORY: Do you remember Athaliah? She was the daughter of Ahab and Jezebel, who married into David’s family. Her son Ahaziah, king of Judah, had just died at the hands of Jehu.

'And when Athaliah the mother of Ahaziah saw that her son was dead, she arose and destroyed all the seed royal.' The seed royal means the royal family, her own grandchildren. She wanted the throne for herself, and she was willing to do anything to get it.

If she had succeeded, David’s family line would have ended. There would be no more sons of David to sit on the throne. And God’s promise, that a son of David would reign for ever, would have been broken.

But God had his people in the right place.

Jehosheba was married to Jehoiada, the high priest. So the baby was hidden in the temple. 'And he was with her hid in the house of the LORD six years. And Athaliah did reign over the land.' For years, the wicked queen ruled Judah, while the true king grew up secretly in the house of God.

Then, at the right time, Jehoiada the priest acts. He secretly calls together the captains of the royal guard, brings them into the temple, makes a covenant with them, and swears them to secrecy. And then he shows them the king’s son. Imagine their surprise. The boy everyone thought was dead was alive.

Jehoiada makes a careful plan. On the sabbath, when the guards change over, the soldiers will divide into groups. Some will guard the palace. Others will surround the young king in the temple, every man with his weapon in his hand, and stay with him wherever he goes. Jehoiada gives the captains the spears and shields that had once belonged to king David himself, kept in the temple. Fitting weapons to protect David’s heir.

The guards take their places, from one side of the temple to the other. And then comes the great moment.

When Athaliah hears the noise of the guards and the people, she comes into the temple to see what is happening. And there is the young king, standing by the pillar, where kings stood. The officers and trumpeters are around him, and all the people are rejoicing and blowing trumpets. Athaliah tears her clothes, and cries, treason, treason!

But Jehoiada commands the captains to take her out of the temple. He will not let her be put to death in the house of the LORD. They take her out by the horses’ entrance to the palace, and there she dies.

Then Jehoiada does something very important.

And then the people go to the temple of Baal, which Athaliah had brought to Jerusalem, and break it down. They smash its altars and its images to pieces. And Jehoiada appoints guards over the house of the LORD.

Finally, the captains, the guards and all the people bring the young king down from the temple, through the gate of the guard, to the palace. And he sits on the throne of the kings. 'And all the people of the land rejoiced, and the city was in quiet'.

The chapter ends with a simple line. 'Seven years old was Jehoash when he began to reign.' Jehoash is another form of the name Joash. A seven year old king. But he had a wise priest beside him, who loved God.

Think about how close it came. One baby, hidden in the temple. If Jehosheba had been afraid, if the nurse had been careless, if anyone had told Athaliah, David’s line would have ended. But God kept his promise, through ordinary, brave people.

Many years later, another wicked ruler, king Herod, tried to kill a baby born in the family of David. And God protected that child too. His name was Jesus.

KEY 11:2: 'But Jehosheba, the daughter of king Joram, sister of Ahaziah, took Joash the son of Ahaziah and stole him from among the king’s sons which were slain; and they hid him, even him and his nurse, in the bedchamber from Athaliah, so that he was not slain.' Jehosheba was a princess, and she risked everything. If Athaliah had found out, she would surely have been killed. Yet she acted bravely, at exactly the right moment. God often works through quiet courage, done in secret, that no one else sees. One brave act saved the line of David.

KEY 11:12: 'And he brought forth the king’s son, and put the crown upon him, and gave him the testimony; and they made him king, and anointed him; and they clapped their hands, and said, God save the king.' The testimony probably means a copy of God’s law. Moses had said that every king should have God’s law with him, and read it all the days of his life. So the young king was given a crown, and God’s Word. A good king needs both. And the people clapped and cheered. The rightful king had come out of hiding.

KEY 11:17: 'And Jehoiada made a covenant between the LORD and the king and the people that they should be the LORD’S people; between the king also and the people.' A covenant is a solemn promise. Jehoiada did not just put a new king on the throne. He called the king and the people back to God. They would be the LORD’s people again. Real change does not come from a new leader alone. It comes when hearts turn back to God.

THREAD: The angel told Joseph, 'Arise, and take the young child and his mother, and flee into Egypt'. And David wrote, 'For in the time of trouble he shall hide me in his pavilion: in the secret of his tabernacle shall he hide me'.

TODAY: Is there a quiet act of courage God is asking of you, something no one else may ever see? Ask him for the bravery of Jehosheba. And thank God that he keeps his promises, even when everything seems lost.

QUESTION: For years, the true king was hidden in the temple while a wicked queen ruled. When does it feel to you as if God’s plans are hidden, and how can you keep trusting him?

PRAYER: Lord God, thank you that no one can stop your promises. Thank you for brave people like Jehosheba and Jehoiada, who protected the line of David. Hide me in the secret of your presence when trouble comes. Give me quiet courage to do what is right. And make me one of your people, by your covenant, all my days. Amen.
