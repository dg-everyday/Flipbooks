# 2 Kings 17: Hoshea, Shalmaneser, Hoshea's conspiracy with Egypt, the siege and fall of Samaria (years only inside quotes), Israel carried to Assyria; why: other gods, high places, images under every green tree, yet the LORD testified by all the prophets, they hardened their necks, the two calves, Baal, terrible practices (17:17, 17:31 not quoted), removed out of his sight, Judah also; the new peoples in Samaria, the lions (told briefly), the priest at Bethel, they feared the LORD and served their own gods (the Samaritans); remember the covenant. Keys 17:13, 17:23, 17:33.
TITLE: So Was Israel Carried Away

HOOK: A long line of people trudges along a dusty road, heading north and east, away from home. Families, carrying what little they can. Soldiers march beside them with spears. Behind them, their city lies broken. Ahead of them lies a foreign land, with strange languages and strange gods. They will never come back. The northern kingdom of Israel has fallen.

WHERE: In chapter sixteen, Ahaz, king of Judah, refused God’s sign through Isaiah, and sent to the king of Assyria, saying, I am thy servant and thy son. He paid for Assyria’s help with treasure from the temple, and copied a foreign altar for the house of the LORD. Then Ahaz died, and his son Hezekiah became king.

Now, in chapter seventeen, the northern kingdom comes to its end. Listen for what happened. And listen carefully for why it happened.

STORY: Hoshea is king of Israel, the last king of the northern kingdom. He does evil, but not as badly as the kings before him.

Shalmaneser, the new king of Assyria, comes against him, and Hoshea becomes his servant, and pays him tribute every year. But then Hoshea makes a secret plan. He sends messengers to the king of Egypt, hoping for help, and stops paying Assyria.

The king of Assyria finds out. He arrests Hoshea and puts him in prison. Then the Assyrian army marches through the whole land, and surrounds Samaria. The siege lasts a long time. 'In the ninth year of Hoshea the king of Assyria took Samaria, and carried Israel away into Assyria'. He settles them in faraway places, by the river Gozan, and in the cities of the Medes.

Long after the kingdom split in two, the northern kingdom was gone.

And now the writer stops the story, to explain. Why did this happen? Was the LORD too weak to protect them? No. 'For so it was, that the children of Israel had sinned against the LORD their God, which had brought them up out of the land of Egypt'.

They worshipped other gods. They copied the ways of the nations God had driven out. They built high places in every town, from the smallest watchtower to the strongest city. They set up idols on every high hill and under every green tree. They served idols, even though God had said, you shall not do this.

But God did not let them go without warning.

'Notwithstanding they would not hear, but hardened their necks'. Like a stubborn animal that will not turn its head. They rejected God’s laws, and his covenant. 'and they followed vanity, and became vain'. Vanity means emptiness. They followed empty idols, and became empty themselves. We become like what we worship.

They made the two golden calves. They worshipped Baal, and the stars. They did terrible things that God hated, even to their own children. They gave themselves over to evil.

So the LORD was very angry with Israel, and removed them from his sight. Only the tribe of Judah was left. And the writer adds a sad warning. Judah too did not keep God’s commandments. What happened to Israel could happen to Judah.

It all went back to Jeroboam. He tore Israel away from David’s family, and made them sin a great sin with his golden calves. And they never turned away from it.

Then the king of Assyria brings people from other conquered lands, from Babylon and other cities, and settles them in Samaria instead of the Israelites. At first, these newcomers do not worship the LORD at all. And lions come among them, and attack some of them. So they send word to the king of Assyria: the people you have sent do not know how to worship the God of this land.

So the king sends back one of the priests of Israel, who settles in Bethel, and teaches them how to fear the LORD. But it does not go well. Every nation keeps making its own gods as well, and puts them in the shrines on the high places. Each group brings its own idols from home.

The writer reminds us of what God had told Israel. You shall not fear other gods, nor bow to them, nor serve them. But the LORD, who brought you out of Egypt with great power, him you shall fear, and him you shall worship. 'And the covenant that I have made with you ye shall not forget'. But they did not listen.

These mixed peoples became known as the Samaritans. And that is why, in the days of Jesus, Jews and Samaritans did not get along. When Jesus spoke with a Samaritan woman at a well, and told a story about a good Samaritan, he was reaching across a very old divide that began here.

KEY 17:13: 'Yet the LORD testified against Israel, and against Judah, by all the prophets, and by all the seers, saying, Turn ye from your evil ways, and keep my commandments and my statutes, according to all the law which I commanded your fathers, and which I sent to you by my servants the prophets.' Think of all the prophets we have met. Ahijah, Elijah, Elisha, Micaiah, Jonah, and many more. Again and again, God sent them, calling, turn ye. God did not give up quickly. He warned for generations. Judgment came only after a very long time of patience.

KEY 17:23: 'Until the LORD removed Israel out of his sight, as he had said by all his servants the prophets. So was Israel carried away out of their own land to Assyria unto this day.' As he had said. Moses had warned that if the people turned away, they would be scattered. Ahijah had said it to Jeroboam’s wife. God’s warnings are as sure as his promises. This is sad and serious. But it also tells us that God means what he says. And so we can trust his promises of mercy too.

KEY 17:33: 'They feared the LORD, and served their own gods, after the manner of the nations whom they carried away from thence.' They feared the LORD, and served their own gods. They tried to have both. A little bit of God, and a little bit of everything else. It sounds like a sensible mixture. But God does not want a share of our hearts. He wants all of it. A faith that mixes God with other gods is not true faith at all.

THREAD: The letter to the Hebrews says, 'To day if ye will hear his voice, harden not your hearts'. And Jesus said, 'No man can serve two masters'.

TODAY: Is God saying something to you that you have been ignoring? Do not harden your heart. Turn to him today. And ask yourself honestly: am I trying to serve God and something else at the same time?

QUESTION: The Samaritans feared the LORD, and served their own gods too. What other things compete with God for first place in people’s hearts today?

PRAYER: Lord God, thank you for your great patience, and for all the times you have called me to turn back to you. Forgive me for hardening my heart. I do not want to serve you and other gods too. Take all of my heart. And thank you that Jesus reached across every divide, even to the Samaritans, to bring people home to you. Amen.
