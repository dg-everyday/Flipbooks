# 2 Kings 19: Hezekiah tears his clothes and goes into the house of the LORD, sends to Isaiah, be not afraid, the letter, spread it before the LORD, Hezekiah's prayer (thou art the God even thou alone, bow down thine ear, they were no gods, that all the kingdoms may know), Isaiah's answer (I have heard; hook in thy nose; the remnant shall take root downward; I will defend this city), the angel of the LORD and the Assyrian camp (19:35 not quoted, no count, no time words), Sennacherib returns to Nineveh and falls by his sons' hands (told briefly). 19:21 not quoted. Keys 19:14, 19:19, 19:34.
TITLE: Thou Art the God, Even Thou Alone

HOOK: A king walks into the temple, holding a letter. It is full of threats and boasting. Your God cannot save you. We have destroyed every nation, and their gods with them. You are next. The king does not argue. He does not call his generals. He goes into the house of the LORD, and unrolls the letter, and spreads it out flat before God. As if to say, LORD, read this. And then he prays.

WHERE: In chapter eighteen, Hezekiah became king of Judah. He trusted in the LORD God of Israel like no king before or after him. He broke the bronze serpent that people had made into an idol. But the Assyrian army came, and their chief officer, the Rabshakeh, stood outside Jerusalem and mocked. What confidence is this wherein thou trustest? Who can save you now? And the people answered him not a word.

Now, in chapter nineteen, we see what Hezekiah does with the enemy’s threats. Listen to his prayer. And listen for how God answers.

STORY: When Hezekiah hears what the Rabshakeh said, he tears his clothes, puts on sackcloth, and goes into the house of the LORD. And he sends his officials, also wearing sackcloth, to the prophet Isaiah.

This is a day of trouble, they tell Isaiah. It is like a mother about to give birth, but with no strength to bring the baby into the world. Perhaps the LORD your God will hear the words of the Rabshakeh, who has mocked the living God. Please pray for the people who are left.

And Isaiah sends back God’s answer. 'Thus saith the LORD, Be not afraid of the words which thou hast heard, with which the servants of the king of Assyria have blasphemed me.' God says, I will cause the king of Assyria to hear a rumour, and he will return to his own land. And there he will fall.

The Rabshakeh goes back to his master, who is now fighting elsewhere. But Sennacherib hears that the king of Ethiopia is marching against him. So he sends a letter to Hezekiah, full of threats. Do not let your God deceive you. Have the gods of the other nations saved them? Where are their kings now? You will not escape.

And then Hezekiah does the most important thing in the chapter.

And Hezekiah prays.

'O LORD God of Israel, which dwellest between the cherubims, thou art the God, even thou alone, of all the kingdoms of the earth: thou hast made heaven and earth.' He begins, not with the problem, but with who God is. The God who made heaven and earth.

'LORD, bow down thine ear, and hear: open, LORD, thine eyes, and see: and hear the words of Sennacherib, which hath sent him to reproach the living God.' Listen, LORD. Look, LORD. See what they are saying about you.

It is true, Hezekiah prays, that the kings of Assyria have destroyed many nations, and thrown their gods into the fire. 'for they were no gods, but the work of men’s hands, wood and stone'. Of course those gods could not save anyone. They were not real. But you are.

And then he asks.

Then Isaiah sends a message to Hezekiah. 'Thus saith the LORD God of Israel, That which thou hast prayed to me against Sennacherib king of Assyria I have heard.'

And God speaks to the proud king of Assyria. Who have you mocked? Who have you shouted at? The Holy One of Israel! You boast that with your chariots you have climbed the highest mountains. But did you not know that I planned it all, long ago? You only did what I allowed. I know where you sit, and when you come and go, and how you rage against me. 'therefore I will put my hook in thy nose, and my bridle in thy lips, and I will turn thee back by the way by which thou camest.' Like a farmer leading a bull by a ring in its nose, God would lead the mighty king of Assyria home.

And God gives a promise to his people. 'And the remnant that is escaped of the house of Judah shall yet again take root downward, and bear fruit upward.' Like a tree cut down, that grows again from its roots.

And about the king of Assyria, God says, he will not come into this city, or shoot an arrow here, or lift a shield against it, or build a siege ramp. He will go back the way he came.

Then the angel of the LORD goes out, and strikes the Assyrian camp. And when the people of Jerusalem look out, the great army that surrounded them has been destroyed. Without one arrow fired by Hezekiah’s soldiers.

So Sennacherib, king of Assyria, breaks camp, and goes home to Nineveh. And some time later, as he is worshipping in the temple of his own god, his own sons strike him down. Exactly as God had said through Isaiah.

The gods of Assyria could not save their own king, even in their own temple. But the God of Israel saved Jerusalem, because his people prayed.

KEY 19:14: 'And Hezekiah received the letter of the hand of the messengers, and read it: and Hezekiah went up into the house of the LORD, and spread it before the LORD.' What a wonderful picture. Hezekiah did not hide the letter, or panic, or try to fix it himself. He spread it out before God. We can do the same with our troubles, our worries, our frightening news. Lay them out before God. Show him everything. He sees it all, and he cares.

KEY 19:19: 'Now therefore, O LORD our God, I beseech thee, save thou us out of his hand, that all the kingdoms of the earth may know that thou art the LORD God, even thou only.' Notice why Hezekiah asked to be saved. Not only so that his people would be safe, though he wanted that. But so that the whole world would know who the true God is. The best prayers care about God’s name and glory, not only our own comfort.

KEY 19:34: 'For I will defend this city, to save it, for mine own sake, and for my servant David’s sake.' For mine own sake, and for my servant David’s sake. Once again, God remembers his promise to David. He did not save Jerusalem because Judah was so good, but because of his own faithfulness and his promise. God’s promises are the strongest walls any city could have.

THREAD: The psalmist wrote, 'God is our refuge and strength, a very present help in trouble.' And Jesus said, 'for your Father knoweth what things ye have need of, before ye ask him.'

TODAY: Is there a letter, a message, a worry, or a threat that is troubling you? Do what Hezekiah did. Spread it out before the LORD. Tell him everything, and ask him to act, so that others may see who he is.

QUESTION: Hezekiah began his prayer with who God is, before he spoke about the problem. How might it change your prayers to start by remembering who God is?

PRAYER: O LORD, you are God, even you alone. You made heaven and earth. I spread my troubles out before you now. Bow down your ear, and hear. Open your eyes, and see. Save me, LORD, so that others may know that you are the LORD God, even you only. Thank you that you defend your people, for your own name’s sake. Amen.
