# 2 Kings 21: Manasseh a boy king (age, not years of reign, outside quotes), rebuilt the high places, altars for Baal and the host of heaven in the LORD's house (21:6 not quoted; one phrase), the graven image in the house of the name, Manasseh seduced them, the prophets' word (ears shall tingle, wipe Jerusalem as a dish), innocent blood, 2 Chronicles 33 noted (his repentance in Babylon); Amon, his servants' plot (brief), Josiah made king. Keys 21:7, 21:9, 21:13.
TITLE: Both His Ears Shall Tingle

HOOK: Imagine a father who spends his whole life building a beautiful garden. He pulls up every weed, plants good seeds, and waters them faithfully. Then he dies, and his son takes over. And the son tears it all up. He plants thorns and poison ivy where the flowers grew. Worse, he plants them on purpose. That is what happened in Judah after Hezekiah died.

WHERE: In chapter twenty, Hezekiah was sick unto death, and wept before the LORD. God said, I have heard thy prayer, I have seen thy tears, and made the shadow go backwards. But Hezekiah showed all his treasures to visitors from Babylon, and Isaiah said that one day it would all be carried away to Babylon. Then Hezekiah died, and his son Manasseh became king.

Now, in chapter twenty one, we meet the worst king Judah ever had. It is a dark chapter. Listen for what Manasseh did. And listen for the surprising end to his story, found in another book of the Bible.

STORY: Manasseh was only a boy of twelve when he became king. And he reigned longer than any other king of Judah. 'And he did that which was evil in the sight of the LORD, after the abominations of the heathen, whom the LORD cast out before the children of Israel.'

He undid everything his father had done. He rebuilt the high places Hezekiah had torn down. He set up altars for Baal, and made an image of Asherah, just like Ahab, the wicked king of Israel. He worshipped the stars. And he built altars for them right inside the courts of the house of the LORD.

He did the most terrible things that the nations had done, even to his own son. He turned to fortune tellers, magic, and those who claimed to speak with the dead. Everything God had forbidden, Manasseh did.

And then he did the worst thing of all.

God had promised that if his people obeyed him, they would never be moved out of their land. 'But they hearkened not'.

So God sent his prophets with a message. Because Manasseh has done these terrible things, worse than the nations who lived here before, and has led Judah into sin with his idols, God says, 'Behold, I am bringing such evil upon Jerusalem and Judah, that whosoever heareth of it, both his ears shall tingle.' The news of what would happen to Jerusalem would be so shocking that people’s ears would ring when they heard it.

And God uses a picture.

'Moreover Manasseh shed innocent blood very much, till he had filled Jerusalem from one end to another'. He was cruel, as well as idolatrous.

Then the writer says that Manasseh died, and was buried in the garden of his own house. And his son Amon became king.

But that is not the whole story. Another book of the Bible, the second book of Chronicles, tells us something astonishing. Later in his reign, the Assyrians captured Manasseh, put a hook in him, bound him in chains, and took him to Babylon. And there, in his great trouble, Manasseh humbled himself greatly before the God of his fathers, and prayed to him. And God heard him. God brought him back to Jerusalem, to his kingdom. 'Then Manasseh knew that the LORD he was God.' He took away the foreign gods, and the idol from the house of the LORD, and told Judah to serve the LORD.

The worst king in Judah’s history, who did more evil than anyone, was forgiven when he humbled himself. That shows how great God’s mercy is. If God could forgive Manasseh, there is no one he cannot forgive.

But the damage Manasseh had done to the nation could not easily be undone. The people had learned his wicked ways. And his son Amon followed in his father’s early footsteps, not his later ones. Amon served the idols his father had served, and forsook the LORD. He reigned only a short time. His own servants plotted against him, and took his life in his own house. Then the people of the land dealt with those who had plotted against him, and made his son Josiah king.

Josiah was only a boy of eight. And after the darkest chapter in Judah’s history, he would be like a light shining in the dark.

KEY 21:7: 'And he set a graven image of the grove that he had made in the house, of which the LORD said to David, and to Solomon his son, In this house, and in Jerusalem, which I have chosen out of all tribes of Israel, will I put my name for ever'. A graven image means a carved idol. Of all the places in the world, Manasseh put an idol in the very house where God had put his name. It was like saying to God, this is not your house any more. That is how far Judah had fallen.

KEY 21:9: 'But they hearkened not: and Manasseh seduced them to do more evil than did the nations whom the LORD destroyed before the children of Israel.' Seduced means led them astray, tempted them. A leader has great power to lead people towards God, or away from him. Manasseh led his whole nation into evil. Those who lead others carry a great responsibility. And all of us lead someone, by our example.

KEY 21:13: 'And I will stretch over Jerusalem the line of Samaria, and the plummet of the house of Ahab: and I will wipe Jerusalem as a man wipeth a dish, wiping it, and turning it upside down.' A line and a plummet were tools builders used to check whether a wall was straight. God would measure Jerusalem with the same measure he used for Samaria, and it would fail the same way. And like someone washing a plate, wiping it clean and turning it upside down to dry, God would empty Jerusalem. It is a hard picture. But it shows God takes sin seriously.

THREAD: Isaiah heard God say, 'though your sins be as scarlet, they shall be as white as snow'. And Paul wrote, 'Christ Jesus came into the world to save sinners; of whom I am chief.'

TODAY: Do you ever feel that you have done something too bad for God to forgive? Remember Manasseh. Humble yourself before God, and ask for his mercy. He hears. And think about who follows your example. Ask God to help you lead them towards him.

QUESTION: Manasseh did more evil than any king of Judah, yet when he humbled himself, God heard him. What does that tell you about God’s mercy?

PRAYER: Lord God, your mercy is greater than my sin. Thank you that you heard even Manasseh when he humbled himself. I humble myself before you now. Forgive me, and wash me whiter than snow. Help me to lead others towards you, by my words and by my life. Amen.
