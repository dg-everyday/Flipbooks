TITLE: Meet the Second Book of Samuel

HOOK: A shepherd boy has become a king. He has waited many years, running for his life, refusing to take the throne by force. Now, at last, the crown is his. He will dance before God with all his might. He will receive one of the greatest promises in the whole Bible. And then, at the height of his power, he will fall into a terrible sin. The second book of Samuel tells the story of David, the king. All of it, the glory and the shame.

BODY: Welcome to the second book of Samuel, on The Living Voice. Second Samuel has twenty four chapters, so on this walk it is twenty four steps, one chapter at a time.

At the end of the first book of Samuel, Saul and Jonathan fell in battle on mount Gilboa. David was in Ziklag, and did not yet know. The second book of Samuel picks up the story right there.

It is the story of David’s reign. First he becomes king over his own tribe of Judah, in the town of Hebron. Then, after years of struggle, he becomes king over all Israel. He captures the city of Jerusalem, and makes it his capital, and brings the ark of God there with great joy.

And then God makes David an astonishing promise. Thine house and thy kingdom shall be established for ever. God promises that a king from David’s family will reign for ever. That promise runs right through the rest of the Bible. And the New Testament begins by calling Jesus the son of David.

But the second book of Samuel is also painfully honest. At the height of his power, David sins terribly, taking another man’s wife and then arranging her husband’s death. God sends the prophet Nathan to confront him. And David does what Saul never truly did. He says, I have sinned against the LORD. He repents from his heart. And God forgives him.

Yet the consequences of his sin follow him. His family is torn apart by violence and rebellion. His own son Absalom tries to take his throne. David is driven from Jerusalem, weeping. And yet through it all, David turns again and again to God.

Here are three things to watch for as we walk.

Watch for the promise. In chapter seven, God promises David a house, a kingdom and a throne for ever. Listen for how that promise points beyond David, to a greater King.

Watch for the difference between David and Saul. Both sinned. But Saul made excuses, and David repented. A man after God’s own heart is not a man who never falls. He is a man who turns back to God when he does.

And watch for God’s mercy. Again and again, in David’s grief and failure, God is merciful. David himself says, near the end of the book, let us fall now into the hand of the LORD; for his mercies are great.

A gentle word before we begin. This book tells honestly of some dark events, including David’s sin, and a terrible crime within his family. We will tell those chapters gently, without dwelling on the details. If you are listening with children, you may want to listen to chapters eleven and thirteen yourself first.

QUESTION: David was called a man after God’s own heart, and yet he sinned terribly. What do you think it means to have a heart after God’s heart?

PRAYER: Lord God, as I walk through the second book of Samuel, teach me from David’s life, both his faith and his failures. Thank you for your promise of a King who will reign for ever. When I fall, give me a heart that turns back to you. And thank you that your mercies are great. Amen.
