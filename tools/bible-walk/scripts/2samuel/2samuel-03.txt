# 2 Samuel 3: long war, David waxed stronger, sons born in Hebron, Abner quarrels with Ish-bosheth (3:7 told without "concubine"), Abner turns to David, Michal returned and Phaltiel weeping, Abner speaks to the elders, the feast and Abner sent away in peace, Joab's revenge (gently), David's innocence and mourning, a prince and a great man fallen, I am this day weak. Keys 3:1, 3:18, 3:38.
TITLE: A Prince and a Great Man Fallen This Day

HOOK: A king walks behind a coffin, weeping aloud. The whole city follows him, weeping too. The man in the coffin was, until recently, his enemy. But he had just made peace. And now he has been struck down, not by an enemy, but by one of the king’s own men. And the king refuses to eat a single bite until the sun goes down.

WHERE: In chapter two, David asked the LORD, shall I go up? and was anointed king over Judah at Hebron. But Abner made Saul’s son Ish-bosheth king over the rest of Israel. At the pool of Gibeon, the two sides fought, and Abner struck down Joab’s brother Asahel, who would not stop chasing him. Then Abner called out, shall the sword devour for ever? And Joab blew the trumpet for peace.

Now, in chapter three, the long struggle between the two kingdoms continues. Listen for how David grows stronger. And listen for how David responds to an act he did not want.

STORY: There is a long war between the house of Saul and the house of David.

While David is in Hebron, sons are born to him, by several wives. His firstborn is Amnon. Another is Absalom, whose mother was a king’s daughter from Geshur. Another is Adonijah. Remember these names. They will come back in sorrowful ways later in the story.

Meanwhile, in the house of Saul, Abner is making himself stronger and stronger. Ish-bosheth accuses Abner of taking a woman who had belonged to Saul’s household, which was like claiming Saul’s throne.

Abner is furious. Am I a dog? he shouts. I have been loyal to the house of Saul, and kept you from falling into David’s hands, and you accuse me over this woman? May God deal with me if I do not do for David what the LORD has sworn to him, to transfer the kingdom from the house of Saul, and set up David’s throne over Israel and Judah, from Dan to Beer-sheba.

Ish-bosheth cannot say a word, because he is afraid of Abner.

Notice that Abner knew all along that God had promised the kingdom to David. He had been fighting against God’s plan. Now, in anger, he switches sides.

Abner sends messengers to David. Make an agreement with me, and I will bring all Israel over to you.

David says, good, I will make an agreement with you. But first, bring me my wife Michal, Saul’s daughter.

Remember Michal? She loved David, and helped him escape through a window. But while David was running, Saul gave her to another man. Now Ish-bosheth takes her from her husband, Phaltiel. And Phaltiel follows behind her, weeping, all the way to Bahurim, until Abner tells him, go home. And he goes.

It is a sad little scene. Kings and generals make their deals, and ordinary people are hurt.

Then Abner talks to the elders of Israel. For some time now, he says, you have wanted David as your king. Now do it.

Abner speaks to the tribe of Benjamin too, Saul’s own tribe. Then he comes to David at Hebron with twenty men. And David holds a feast for them.

Abner says, let me go and gather all Israel to my lord the king, so that they may make a covenant with you, and you may reign over everything your heart desires. And David sends Abner away, and he goes in peace.

But just then, Joab and David’s men come back from a raid. And they tell Joab, Abner came to the king, and the king has sent him away in peace.

Joab goes to David, angry. What have you done? Abner came to you. Why did you let him go? You know Abner. He came to deceive you, and to spy on everything you do.

Then Joab leaves, and sends messengers after Abner, and brings him back. David knows nothing about it. And when Abner comes back to Hebron, Joab takes him aside in the gateway, as if to speak with him privately. And there Joab strikes him down, to avenge his brother Asahel.

Joab took revenge, and broke the peace David had made. He may also have been afraid that Abner would take his place as commander.

When David hears what has happened, he says, I and my kingdom are innocent before the LORD for ever of the blood of Abner. Let it rest on the head of Joab.

And David says to Joab and all the people with him, tear your clothes, put on sackcloth, and mourn for Abner. And King David himself walks behind the coffin. They bury Abner in Hebron. And the king weeps aloud at his grave, and all the people weep.

David sings a short lament. Should Abner die as a fool dies? Your hands were not bound, your feet were not in chains. You fell as one falls before wicked men. And all the people weep again.

The people try to get David to eat, but he swears he will not taste food until the sun goes down. And all the people notice, and it pleases them. They understand that the king had no part in Abner’s death.

Then David says to his servants.

And David says, today I am weak, even though I am anointed king. These sons of Zeruiah, Joab and Abishai, are too hard for me. May the LORD repay the evildoer as his evil deserves.

KEY 3:1: 'Now there was long war between the house of Saul and the house of David: but David waxed stronger and stronger, and the house of Saul waxed weaker and weaker.' Waxed means grew. David did not seize the kingdom in one great battle. He grew stronger, step by step, over a long time, while Saul’s house grew weaker. God’s purposes often unfold slowly. Patience is part of faith.

KEY 3:18: 'Now then do it: for the LORD hath spoken of David, saying, By the hand of my servant David I will save my people Israel out of the hand of the Philistines, and out of the hand of all their enemies.' Even Abner knew what God had said. My servant David. God’s promise was known far and wide. People can know what God has said, and still resist it, as Abner did for years. Knowing God’s will is not enough. We have to follow it.

KEY 3:38: 'And the king said unto his servants, Know ye not that there is a prince and a great man fallen this day in Israel?' David honoured Abner, even though Abner had fought against him for years. He recognised a great man, and grieved for him. David’s tears were real. And they showed everyone that David did not want power by violence. A good leader honours even his former enemies.

THREAD: A proverb says, 'Say not thou, I will recompense evil; but wait on the LORD, and he shall save thee.' And Paul wrote, 'Recompense to no man evil for evil.'

TODAY: Is there a grudge you have been holding, like Joab held his grudge against Abner? Ask God today to help you let it go, before it leads to something worse.

QUESTION: Joab took revenge, and broke the peace David had made. Why do you think revenge so often makes things worse, instead of better?

PRAYER: Lord God, thank you that your purposes unfold, step by step, even through long struggles. Keep me from holding grudges and taking revenge. Help me, like David, to honour others, even those who once opposed me. And give me patience to wait for your timing. Amen.

NEXT: Next on the walk, Second Samuel chapter four. Two men think they will win David’s favour by striking down Saul’s son. But David is not pleased. And we meet Jonathan’s son, Mephibosheth, lame in both feet.
