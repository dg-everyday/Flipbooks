# 2 Samuel 5: all the tribes to Hebron, thy bone and thy flesh, thou shalt feed my people, anointed king over Israel, Jerusalem and the stronghold of Zion (the taunt about the blind and lame told gently), the city of David, the LORD God of hosts with him, Hiram's cedar, children born in Jerusalem, the Philistines at Rephaim, Baal-perazim, the sound in the mulberry trees. Keys 5:3, 5:10, 5:12.
TITLE: The LORD God of Hosts Was with Him

HOOK: High on a hill stands a fortress so strong that its defenders laugh at anyone who tries to attack it. Even the blind and the lame could keep you out of here, they shout down. The city has never been taken. Joshua could not take it. The tribes of Israel could not take it. But a new king has just been crowned. And he has set his eyes on that hill.

WHERE: In chapter four, Saul’s son Ish-bosheth was struck down by two of his own captains, who brought the news to David expecting a reward. But David said, as the LORD liveth, who hath redeemed my soul out of all adversity, and he punished them for their crime. And we met Mephibosheth, Jonathan’s son, lame in both feet.

Now, in chapter five, David becomes king over all Israel at last. Listen for why the tribes come to him. And listen for the secret of David’s success.

STORY: All the tribes of Israel come to David at Hebron. And they say, we are your own flesh and blood. Even when Saul was king, you were the one who led Israel out to battle and brought them home. And the LORD said to you, you will shepherd my people Israel, and you will be their ruler.

Shepherd my people. David had started out as a shepherd boy, looking after his father’s sheep. Now God makes him the shepherd of a whole nation.

And so it happens.

At last. The promise God made through Samuel, all those years before in Bethlehem, has come true. The shepherd boy, the youngest son no one thought to invite, is king over all Israel.

Then David and his men go to Jerusalem, to the Jebusites who live there. Remember, in the books of Joshua and Judges, we heard again and again that no one could drive the Jebusites out of Jerusalem. The city was a fortress on a hill, with steep slopes on three sides.

The Jebusites taunt David. You will never get in here. Even the blind and the lame could keep you out.

But David takes the stronghold of Zion. It seems his men got in through a water shaft, a tunnel that brought water into the city. And from then on, it is called the city of David.

David lives in the fortress, and builds up the city around it. Jerusalem was a wise choice for his capital. It stood between the northern and southern tribes, and had not belonged to any one tribe before. It could be a city for all Israel.

Hiram, king of the great trading city of Tyre, sends messengers to David, with cedar trees, carpenters and stonemasons, and they build David a palace.

More children are born to David in Jerusalem. Among them is a son named Nathan, and another named Solomon. Keep the name Solomon in mind.

But when the Philistines hear that David has been anointed king over all Israel, they come up to find him. They spread out in the valley of Rephaim.

And David asks the LORD, shall I go up against the Philistines? Will you give them into my hand? And the LORD says, go up, for I will certainly give them into your hand.

So David goes, and defeats them at a place he calls Baal-perazim, which means the Lord of breaking through. He says, the LORD has broken through my enemies before me, like a flood of water. The Philistines leave their idols behind, and David and his men burn them.

Remember chapter four of first Samuel? Long ago, the Philistines captured Israel’s ark. Now Israel captures the Philistines’ idols. And unlike the ark, the idols have no power at all.

The Philistines come again, and spread out in the same valley. And again David asks the LORD. This time, God gives a different answer. Do not go straight up. Go round behind them, and come at them from among the mulberry trees. When you hear the sound of marching in the tops of the trees, then move quickly, because the LORD will have gone out before you to strike the Philistine army.

So David does exactly as the LORD commands, and defeats the Philistines.

Notice that David asked God both times, even though he had just won. He did not assume that God’s answer would be the same. And God’s plan was different the second time. Following God means asking him fresh each time, not relying on what worked before.

KEY 5:3: 'So all the elders of Israel came to the king to Hebron; and king David made a league with them in Hebron before the LORD: and they anointed David king over Israel.' Before the LORD. David did not just make a political deal. He made a covenant with the people in God’s presence. He knew that he was not the true King. He was God’s servant, ruling God’s people under God’s authority. The best leaders know they answer to God.

KEY 5:10: 'And David went on, and grew great, and the LORD God of hosts was with him.' Here is the secret of David’s success. Not his skill, not his army, not his charm. The LORD God of hosts was with him. The LORD of hosts means the LORD of armies, the commander of heaven’s armies. With him on David’s side, no fortress could stand, and no enemy could win.

KEY 5:12: 'And David perceived that the LORD had established him king over Israel, and that he had exalted his kingdom for his people Israel’s sake.' For his people Israel’s sake. David understood that God had not made him king for David’s own sake, to enjoy power and riches. God had made him king to bless and serve the people. Every gift God gives us, every position, every ability, is given so that we can serve others.

THREAD: Jesus said, 'I am the good shepherd: the good shepherd giveth his life for the sheep.' And Peter wrote, 'As every man hath received the gift, even so minister the same one to another'.

TODAY: What gifts or position has God given you? Ask him today how you can use them to serve others. And before your next decision, even if you think you already know the answer, ask God fresh, as David did.

QUESTION: David understood that God had made him king for his people’s sake. How might your life change if you saw every gift God has given you as being for the sake of others?

PRAYER: Lord God of hosts, thank you that you were with David, and that you are with your people. Help me to use every gift you have given me to serve others, not myself. Teach me to ask you fresh each time, and not to rely on what worked before. Amen.
