# 2 Samuel 8: victories over the Philistines, Moab (gently, no measuring lines), Hadadezer and Syria, the LORD preserved David whithersoever he went, gifts dedicated to the LORD, Toi of Hamath, Edom, judgment and justice, David's officials. No counts. Keys 8:6, 8:11, 8:15.
TITLE: David Executed Judgment and Justice unto All His People

HOOK: A king comes home from war, his servants carrying shields of gold, vessels of silver and bronze, treasures from many nations. Other kings send gifts too, hoping to stay on his good side. He could keep it all for himself, and become the richest man in the world. Instead, he gives it to God.

WHERE: In chapter seven, David wanted to build a house for God. But God said, I took thee from the sheepcote. I will make thee an house. Thine house and thy kingdom shall be established for ever. And David sat before the LORD and said, who am I, O Lord GOD? There is none like thee.

Now, in chapter eight, God’s promise to give David rest from his enemies begins to come true. Listen for the words that are repeated twice. And listen for what kind of king David is to his own people.

STORY: After this, David defeats the Philistines, and brings them under his control.

The Philistines had troubled Israel since the days of the judges. They had captured the ark, defeated Saul, and ruled over the tribes. Now, at last, David breaks their power.

Then David defeats Moab, and the Moabites become his servants, and bring him tribute. The Bible tells us that this victory was a harsh one. It is one of the hard parts of this chapter, and we pass over it gently.

David also defeats Hadadezer, king of Zobah, to the north, as he goes to restore his control along the river Euphrates. David captures many chariots and horsemen and foot soldiers. He disables most of the chariot horses, keeping only enough for a hundred chariots.

Remember Joshua at the waters of Merom, when God told him to disable the horses and burn the chariots, so Israel would trust in God, not in horses? David keeps a few, but not many.

When the Syrians of Damascus come to help Hadadezer, David defeats them too. He puts garrisons in Syria, and the Syrians become his servants and bring tribute.

And then the writer says it.

David takes the gold shields carried by Hadadezer’s officers, and brings them to Jerusalem. And he takes a great amount of bronze from Hadadezer’s cities.

When Toi, king of Hamath, hears that David has defeated Hadadezer, he sends his son to greet and congratulate David, because Hadadezer had been at war with Toi. And he brings gifts of silver, gold and bronze.

And here is what David does with all this treasure.

David also defeats Edom, and puts garrisons throughout the land. And all the Edomites become his servants.

And again the writer says, 'And the LORD preserved David whithersoever he went.'

Twice in one chapter. God was keeping his promise. He was giving David rest from all his enemies, just as he had said.

But the most important verse in the chapter is not about battles at all.

A king could be a great warrior and still be a terrible ruler, cruel and unfair to his own people. But David used his power to bring justice. He did what was right, and he treated people fairly.

The chapter ends with a list of David’s officials. Joab, commander of the army. Jehoshaphat, the recorder, who kept the official records. Zadok and Ahimelech, the priests. Seraiah, the scribe. Benaiah, over the king’s bodyguard. And David’s sons, who served as royal advisers.

David had built a proper kingdom, with order and leadership. The days of the judges, when everyone did what was right in their own eyes, were over. Israel had a king who ruled with justice, and who gave the glory to God.

This is the high point of David’s reign. His enemies are defeated. His kingdom is strong. His people are treated fairly. And his heart is still turned toward God.

But we will soon see that the greatest danger to David is not his enemies. It is his own heart, in a moment of ease.

KEY 8:6: 'Then David put garrisons in Syria of Damascus: and the Syrians became servants to David, and brought gifts. And the LORD preserved David whithersoever he went.' To preserve means to keep safe. David won his battles, but the writer makes it clear who deserved the credit. The LORD preserved David. Wherever David went, God went with him, and kept him. It is the same promise God made to Joshua: the LORD thy God is with thee whithersoever thou goest.

KEY 8:11: 'Which also king David did dedicate unto the LORD, with the silver and gold that he had dedicated of all nations which he subdued;' David did not keep the treasure for himself. He dedicated it to the LORD. Many years later, his son Solomon would use these treasures to build the temple. David knew that his victories came from God, so the spoils belonged to God. When God blesses us, the right response is to give back to him.

KEY 8:15: 'And David reigned over all Israel; and David executed judgment and justice unto all his people.' Judgment and justice. To all his people, not just to the powerful, or to his friends. This is what God wants from every leader, and from every one of us. To do what is right, and to treat everyone fairly. A king after God’s own heart cares about justice, because God cares about justice.

THREAD: The prophet Micah said, 'what doth the LORD require of thee, but to do justly, and to love mercy, and to walk humbly with thy God?' And a psalm says, 'The LORD shall preserve thy going out and thy coming in from this time forth, and even for evermore.'

TODAY: Think about your own small area of influence, your home, your work, your friendships. How can you do what is right and fair there today? And think of something God has blessed you with that you could give back to him.

QUESTION: David was a great warrior, but the writer says the most important thing was that he executed judgment and justice. Why do you think justice matters so much to God?

PRAYER: Lord God, thank you that you preserve your people wherever they go. Thank you for every blessing you have given me. Help me to give back to you, and to do justly, love mercy and walk humbly with you. Make me fair and kind to everyone around me. Amen.
