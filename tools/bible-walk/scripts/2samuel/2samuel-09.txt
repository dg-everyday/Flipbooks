# 2 Samuel 9: is there yet any of the house of Saul, the kindness of God, Ziba, Lo-debar, Mephibosheth brought and falls on his face, fear not, eat bread at my table continually, a dead dog, as one of the king's sons, lame on both his feet. Keys 9:1, 9:7, 9:8.
TITLE: Thou Shalt Eat Bread at My Table Continually

HOOK: A young man is summoned to the palace. He cannot walk properly. He has been living in hiding, in a poor town far away, all his life. And he is the grandson of the king who tried to take the life of the man now on the throne. In those days, new kings often wiped out the families of the old ones. He comes before the king, and falls on his face, expecting the worst.

WHERE: In chapter eight, David defeated the Philistines and many other enemies, and the LORD preserved David whithersoever he went. He dedicated the treasures to the LORD. And David executed judgment and justice unto all his people.

Now, in chapter nine, with his kingdom secure, David remembers a promise. Listen for the question he asks. And listen for how the young man sees himself, and how the king sees him.

STORY: David asks a question.

Remember the promise. Years before, in a field, Jonathan had said to David, do not cut off your kindness from my house for ever. And David had sworn it. Now, with his enemies defeated and his throne secure, David remembers. He did not have to. Jonathan was gone. No one would have known. But David keeps his word.

There is a servant of Saul’s family named Ziba. They call him to David, and the king asks, are you Ziba? And he says, I am your servant.

And David asks.

The kindness of God. Not just ordinary kindness. David wants to show the kind of love that God shows, faithful, generous, undeserved.

Ziba says, there is still a son of Jonathan. He is lame in his feet.

David asks, where is he? And Ziba says, he is in the house of Machir, in Lo-debar.

Lo-debar was a remote place, on the far side of the Jordan. The name may mean something like no pasture, a barren place. That is where Mephibosheth had been living, out of sight, forgotten.

Remember Mephibosheth? He was five years old when news came that Saul and Jonathan had died. His nurse picked him up and ran, and in her hurry he fell, and was lame from then on.

So King David sends for him, and has him brought from Lo-debar.

Mephibosheth, the son of Jonathan, comes to David, and falls on his face and bows down. He must have been terrified. Perhaps he thought this was the end.

And David says his name. Mephibosheth.

And he answers, here is your servant.

Then David speaks.

Mephibosheth bows again.

Then the king calls Ziba, and says, I have given your master’s grandson everything that belonged to Saul and his family. You and your sons and your servants will farm the land for him, and bring in the harvest, so that he has food. But Mephibosheth himself will always eat at my table.

Ziba says, your servant will do everything my lord the king commands.

And David says it again. Mephibosheth will eat at my table, like one of the king’s sons.

Not as a guest. Not as a servant. Like one of the king’s own sons.

Mephibosheth has a young son of his own, named Micha. And all of Ziba’s household become servants to Mephibosheth.

So Mephibosheth lives in Jerusalem, and always eats at the king’s table. And the chapter ends with a simple detail: he was lame in both his feet.

Picture it. Every day, at the king’s great table, among the princes and the important people, sits a young man who cannot walk properly. And when he sits at the table, his feet are hidden underneath. All anyone sees is a son of the king.

Many Christians have seen in this story a beautiful picture of God’s grace. We are like Mephibosheth. Broken, unable to save ourselves, far from God, from a family that rebelled against the king. And God, for the sake of another, for the sake of Jesus, seeks us out, calls us by name, takes away our fear, and gives us a place at his table, as his own children.

KEY 9:1: 'And David said, Is there yet any that is left of the house of Saul, that I may shew him kindness for Jonathan’s sake?' For Jonathan’s sake. Mephibosheth did nothing to earn David’s kindness. He received it because of someone else, his father Jonathan, and the covenant Jonathan had made. That is exactly how God’s grace works for us. We are welcomed, not because of who we are, but for the sake of Jesus.

KEY 9:7: 'And David said unto him, Fear not: for I will surely shew thee kindness for Jonathan thy father’s sake, and will restore thee all the land of Saul thy father; and thou shalt eat bread at my table continually.' Fear not. The first thing David says is to take away his fear. Then he gives him back his family’s land. And then he gives him a place at the king’s table, continually, for the rest of his life. David’s kindness was far more than Mephibosheth could have hoped for. God’s grace is like that. It is always more than we expect.

KEY 9:8: 'And he bowed himself, and said, What is thy servant, that thou shouldest look upon such a dead dog as I am?' A dead dog. That is how Mephibosheth saw himself. Worthless. Broken. Forgotten. But that is not how the king saw him. The king saw a son. However little you think of yourself, God sees you differently. He looks on you with love, and invites you to his table.

THREAD: Paul wrote, 'But God commendeth his love toward us, in that, while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us.' And Jesus said, 'many shall come from the east and west, and shall sit down with Abraham, and Isaac, and Jacob, in the kingdom of heaven.'

TODAY: Is there someone who feels forgotten, broken or worthless, whom you could show the kindness of God to today? Seek them out, as David sought out Mephibosheth. And remember that you too have a place at the King’s table, because of Jesus.

QUESTION: Mephibosheth called himself a dead dog, but David treated him like one of the king’s sons. How does it change you to know that God sees you as his child, not as you see yourself?

PRAYER: Lord God, thank you for your kindness to me, for the sake of Jesus. I did nothing to earn it. Thank you that you sought me out, called me by name, and gave me a place at your table. Take away my fear. And help me to show your kindness to others who feel forgotten. Amen.
