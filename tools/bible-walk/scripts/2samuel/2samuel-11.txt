# 2 Samuel 11: told very gently, with a note for parents. David tarried at Jerusalem, the roof, Bathsheba wife of Uriah, David took her (no "lay with", no "adultery"), she is expecting a child, David sends for Uriah, Uriah will not go home while the ark and Israel are in tents (avoids 11:13 "drunk"), the letter, Uriah falls, Joab's message, the sword devoureth one as well as another, Bathsheba mourns, the thing displeased the LORD. Keys 11:1, 11:11, 11:27.
TITLE: But the Thing That David Had Done Displeased the LORD

HOOK: It is the time of year when kings go out to war. The army has marched off to battle. But the king has stayed at home. Restless, he gets up and walks on the flat roof of his palace. And there, from the roof, he sees something he should have looked away from. And in the space of a few moments, the man after God’s own heart begins a fall that will bring sorrow on him for the rest of his life.

WHERE: In chapter ten, David tried to show kindness to the new king of the Ammonites, but his messengers were shamed and sent home. War followed. Joab said, be of good courage, and let the LORD do that which seemeth him good. And Israel defeated the Ammonites and the Syrians.

Now we come to chapter eleven, one of the saddest chapters in the Bible. A gentle word first. This chapter tells of David’s great sin. We will tell it plainly but gently, without dwelling on the details. If you are listening with children, you may want to listen to this one yourself first. Listen for how one sin leads to another. And listen for the last words of the chapter.

STORY: The writer begins by telling us where David was, and where he was not.

One day, as the day is ending, David gets up from his bed and walks on the roof of the palace. And from the roof he sees a woman bathing. She is very beautiful.

David sends someone to find out who she is. And he is told, this is Bathsheba, the daughter of Eliam, the wife of Uriah the Hittite.

That should have been the end. She was another man’s wife. David knew God’s law: thou shalt not covet thy neighbour’s wife. But David did not stop.

David sends messengers, and takes her. And he sins with her. Then she goes back to her house.

Remember Achan, in Joshua chapter seven? I saw, I coveted, I took. David followed the very same steps.

Some time later, Bathsheba sends a message to David. I am expecting a child.

Now David tries to cover up his sin. He sends word to Joab, send me Uriah the Hittite. And Joab sends Uriah to David.

When Uriah arrives, David asks him how Joab is, how the soldiers are, and how the war is going. Then David says, go down to your house, and rest. And David sends a gift after him.

David hoped that Uriah would go home to his wife, and everyone would think the child was his.

But Uriah sleeps at the door of the palace, with the king’s servants, and does not go home.

When David hears this, he asks Uriah, you have just come from a long journey. Why did you not go home?

And Uriah gives an answer that should have broken David’s heart.

What a contrast. Uriah, a foreigner, a Hittite, shows more loyalty to God and to Israel than the king of Israel himself.

David keeps Uriah in Jerusalem another day. He invites him to eat and drink with him, hoping that Uriah will change his mind. But again Uriah sleeps among the servants, and does not go home.

So David does something even worse. He writes a letter to Joab, and sends it by the hand of Uriah himself. In the letter he says, put Uriah in the front line, where the fighting is fiercest, and then pull back from him, so that he will be struck down and die.

Uriah carries his own death sentence, without knowing it.

So Joab, while besieging the enemy city, puts Uriah where he knows the strongest enemy fighters are. The men of the city come out and fight, and some of David’s soldiers fall. And Uriah the Hittite dies too.

Joab sends a messenger to tell David about the battle. He knows David may be angry that they went so close to the city walls. So he tells the messenger, if the king is angry, just tell him, your servant Uriah the Hittite is dead also.

The messenger tells David. And David sends back a cold reply. Do not let this trouble you. The sword devours one as well as another. Keep attacking the city.

When Bathsheba hears that her husband Uriah is dead, she mourns for him. And when the time of mourning is over, David sends for her and brings her to his house, and she becomes his wife, and has a son.

It looked as if David had got away with it. No one knew. The child had a father, a husband, a home. The cover up had worked.

But the writer adds one last sentence.

KEY 11:1: 'And it came to pass, after the year was expired, at the time when kings go forth to battle, that David sent Joab, and his servants with him, and all Israel; and they destroyed the children of Ammon, and besieged Rabbah. But David tarried still at Jerusalem.' But David tarried still at Jerusalem. David was where he should not have been, doing nothing, at ease. Many falls begin not in a moment of great temptation, but in a time of idleness and comfort, when we let our guard down. Success can be more dangerous than struggle.

KEY 11:11: 'And Uriah said unto David, The ark, and Israel, and Judah, abide in tents; and my lord Joab, and the servants of my lord, are encamped in the open fields; shall I then go into mine house, to eat and to drink,' and to be with my wife? 'as thou livest, and as thy soul liveth, I will not do this thing.' Uriah was a man of honour. He would not enjoy comfort while the ark of God and his fellow soldiers were out in the field. His faithfulness shines out in this dark chapter. And it shows up David’s sin even more clearly. God sees the faithful, even when the powerful treat them unjustly.

KEY 11:27: 'And when the mourning was past, David sent and fetched her to his house, and she became his wife, and bare him a son. But the thing that David had done displeased the LORD.' But. One small word. David thought no one knew. But God knew. The cover up fooled everyone except the one who mattered most. We can hide our sin from people. We can never hide it from God. And that is not only a warning. It is also, as we will see, the beginning of hope.

THREAD: Moses told Israel, 'be sure your sin will find you out.' And a proverb says, 'He that covereth his sins shall not prosper: but whoso confesseth and forsaketh them shall have mercy.'

TODAY: Is there a place in your life where you are idle and unguarded, where temptation could creep in? Ask God today to keep you alert. And if there is something you are trying to hide, remember that God already knows. Bring it to him.

QUESTION: David went from one sin to another, trying to cover up the first. Why do you think trying to hide sin so often leads to even worse sin?

PRAYER: Lord God, this is a sad and sobering story. Keep me from idleness and from letting my guard down. Guard my eyes and my heart. Thank you that nothing is hidden from you. When I sin, help me not to cover it up, but to bring it into the light, to you. Amen.
