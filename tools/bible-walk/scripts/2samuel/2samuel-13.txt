# 2 Samuel 13: told very gently, with a note for parents. Amnon's crime against his sister Tamar named but not described (no "forced", "virgin"), Tamar's grief and ashes, Absalom takes her in, David very wroth but does nothing, Absalom's hatred, the sheepshearing feast, Amnon struck down (gently, avoids 13:28), the false report, Absalom flees to Geshur, David mourns and longs for Absalom. Keys 13:21, 13:37, 13:39.
TITLE: And David Mourned for His Son Every Day

HOOK: A young woman walks away from her brother’s house, weeping aloud. She has torn the beautiful robe that showed she was a king’s daughter. She has put ashes on her head. Her life has been shattered by the cruelty of someone who should have protected her. And her father, the king, hears about it. And does nothing.

WHERE: In chapter twelve, Nathan told David the story of the poor man’s lamb, and said, thou art the man. David said, I have sinned against the LORD, and was forgiven. But Nathan warned that the sword would never depart from David’s house. David’s child died, and then Solomon was born, loved by the LORD.

Now we come to chapter thirteen, one of the darkest chapters in the book. A gentle word first. This chapter tells of a terrible wrong done to a young woman named Tamar. We will not describe it. But if you are listening with children, you may want to listen to it yourself first. Listen for how sin spreads through David’s family. And listen for what David fails to do.

STORY: David’s son Absalom has a beautiful sister named Tamar. And another of David’s sons, Amnon, his eldest, becomes obsessed with her. Amnon was her half brother. He is so consumed by his desire that he makes himself ill.

Amnon has a friend, a cousin named Jonadab, who is very cunning. And Jonadab helps him make a wicked plan. Pretend to be sick, he says, and ask your father to send Tamar to make you some food.

So Amnon pretends to be ill. And when King David comes to see him, Amnon asks for Tamar to come and cook for him. And David sends Tamar to Amnon’s house.

Tamar goes, and kindly prepares food for her brother. But when she brings it to him, he sends everyone else out of the room. And Amnon commits a terrible crime against his sister. Tamar pleads with him not to do such a wicked thing. But he will not listen. He is stronger than she is, and he hurts her deeply.

And then, the writer tells us, Amnon’s desire turns to hatred. He hates her even more than he had wanted her. He has her thrown out of his house, and the door bolted behind her.

That is what sin does. What looked like love was only selfish desire. And when it was satisfied, it turned to hatred.

Tamar puts ashes on her head, and tears the special robe she wore as a king’s daughter. She puts her hand on her head, and goes away, weeping aloud.

Her brother Absalom finds her, and takes her into his house. And Tamar lives there, desolate, in her brother’s house.

Tamar did nothing wrong. She was kind, and she was wronged. The Bible does not hide her suffering. God saw her tears, and he sees the tears of everyone who has been wronged in this way.

And then comes one of the saddest verses in the chapter.

David was angry. But he did nothing. He did not punish Amnon, or protect Tamar, or bring justice. Perhaps he felt he could not, after his own sin with Bathsheba. Perhaps he loved Amnon too much. But his silence would cost his family dearly.

Absalom says nothing to Amnon, good or bad. But he hates Amnon for what he did to his sister. And he waits.

Two full years later, Absalom holds a sheep shearing feast, and invites all the king’s sons. He asks David to come, but David declines. So Absalom asks, then at least let my brother Amnon come. And David lets Amnon and all the king’s sons go.

At the feast, when Amnon is merry with wine, Absalom’s servants strike him down, as Absalom had commanded them. And all the other king’s sons jump on their mules and flee.

While they are still on the way, a rumour reaches David that Absalom has struck down all the king’s sons, and not one is left. David tears his clothes and lies on the ground, and all his servants stand by with their clothes torn.

But Jonadab, the cunning cousin, says, do not think they are all dead. Only Amnon is dead. Absalom has been planning this ever since the day Amnon wronged his sister.

Soon the king’s sons arrive, weeping aloud. And the king and all his servants weep bitterly too.

And Absalom runs away.

Absalom stays in Geshur for three years.

What a heartbreaking chapter. Nathan had said the sword would never depart from David’s house. Now it has begun. One son has wronged his sister. Another son has taken his brother’s life. And David, the great king, could not keep his own family safe.

KEY 13:21: 'But when king David heard of all these things, he was very wroth.' Very wroth means very angry. But anger is not enough. David did not act. He did not bring justice for his daughter. When leaders, or parents, see wrong and do nothing, it often leads to more wrong. Absalom took matters into his own hands because his father would not. Sometimes love means confronting sin, even in those we love most.

KEY 13:37: 'But Absalom fled, and went to Talmai, the son of Ammihud, king of Geshur. And David mourned for his son every day.' David mourned for his son every day. Which son? Perhaps Amnon, who was dead. Perhaps Absalom, who was gone. Perhaps both. David’s family was falling apart, and his heart was breaking. Sin always brings sorrow, not only to the one who sins, but to everyone around them.

KEY 13:39: 'And the soul of king David longed to go forth unto Absalom: for he was comforted concerning Amnon, seeing he was dead.' Even after all Absalom had done, David still loved him, and longed for him. It is a picture of a father’s love, which does not give up, even when a child goes terribly wrong. God’s love for us is like that too, and even greater.

THREAD: A psalm says, 'He healeth the broken in heart, and bindeth up their wounds.' And another psalm says, 'Defend the poor and fatherless: do justice to the afflicted and needy.'

TODAY: Pray today for all who have been hurt by someone who should have protected them. Ask God to heal them, and to bring justice. And if you see wrong being done, especially to someone vulnerable, ask God for courage to speak up and act.

QUESTION: David was very angry, but did nothing, and it led to more sorrow. Why is it sometimes so hard to confront wrongdoing, especially in our own families?

PRAYER: Lord God, this is a painful story. You see every person who has been wronged, and you heal the broken in heart. Comfort all who suffer as Tamar suffered. Give me courage to stand up for those who are hurt, and not to stay silent. And thank you that your love never gives up on us. Amen.
