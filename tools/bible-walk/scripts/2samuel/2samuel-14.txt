# 2 Samuel 14: Joab sees the king's heart, the wise woman of Tekoah and her story of two sons (gently), the king's promise, the people of God and the banished, water spilt on the ground, the hand of Joab, bring the young man again, let him not see my face, Absalom's beauty and hair, two years, Joab's field on fire, the king kissed Absalom. Avoids 14:6-7, 14:32. Keys 14:14, 14:24, 14:33.
TITLE: Yet Doth He Devise Means

HOOK: A woman dressed in mourning clothes throws herself at the king’s feet. Help me, O king, she cries. And she tells him a heartbreaking story about her two sons. The king listens, and gives his judgment. And then the woman says, but why will you not do the same for your own son? And the king realises that the story was really about him.

WHERE: In chapter thirteen, Amnon committed a terrible crime against his sister Tamar. David was very angry, but did nothing. Two years later, Absalom had Amnon struck down at a feast, and fled to Geshur. And David mourned for his son every day, and his soul longed to go forth unto Absalom.

Now, in chapter fourteen, Joab finds a way to bring Absalom home. Listen for the wise woman’s story. And listen for one of the most beautiful verses in the book, about how God brings back those who are far away.

STORY: Joab sees that the king’s heart longs for Absalom.

So Joab sends to the town of Tekoah, and brings a wise woman. Pretend to be in mourning, he tells her. Put on mourning clothes, and do not use any lotion. Act like a woman who has been grieving for a long time. Then go to the king, and say this. And Joab tells her what to say.

The woman goes to the king, and falls on her face. Help, O king! The king asks, what is the matter? She says, I am a widow, and my husband is dead. I had two sons. They quarrelled out in the field, with no one to separate them, and one struck the other, and he died. Now the whole family wants me to hand over my remaining son, so they can put him to death for his brother’s life. Then they will take away my last hope, and leave my husband with no name or descendant on the earth.

It was a story very like David’s own. One son had taken another son’s life. And the family was demanding justice that would leave the mother with nothing.

The king says, go home, and I will give orders about it. She presses him. At last the king swears, as the LORD lives, not a hair of your son’s head shall fall to the ground.

Then the woman says, please let me say one more thing. And she turns the story round. Why then have you planned such a thing against the people of God? In giving this decision, the king has judged himself, because he does not bring home his own banished son.

And then she says something beautiful.

The king says, do not hide anything from me. Is Joab behind all this? And the woman admits it. Yes, your servant Joab told me to do this, and put all these words in my mouth.

So the king says to Joab, very well, I will do it. Go and bring back the young man Absalom.

Joab falls on his face and thanks the king. Then Joab goes to Geshur, and brings Absalom back to Jerusalem.

But then the king says something sad.

Absalom was home, but not truly welcomed. He was back in Jerusalem, but still cut off from his father. It was a half reconciliation.

The writer tells us about Absalom. In all Israel, there was no one as praised for his good looks as Absalom. From the sole of his foot to the top of his head, there was no flaw in him. His hair was so thick and long that when he cut it once a year, it weighed a great deal.

Remember how Saul looked impressive, tall and handsome? Absalom is like that too. But God looks on the heart.

Absalom lives in Jerusalem for two full years, and never sees the king’s face.

Then Absalom sends for Joab, to ask him to go to the king. But Joab will not come. He sends a second time, and still Joab will not come. So Absalom tells his servants, Joab’s field is next to mine, and he has barley there. Go and set it on fire. And they do.

That gets Joab’s attention. He comes to Absalom and says, why have your servants set my field on fire? And Absalom says, I sent for you, so that you could ask the king why he brought me home. It would have been better to stay in Geshur. Let me see the king’s face.

Absalom gets what he wants by force. It is a sign of what kind of man he is.

So Joab goes to the king, and tells him. And the king calls for Absalom.

KEY 14:14: 'For we must needs die, and are as water spilt on the ground, which cannot be gathered up again; neither doth God respect any person: yet doth he devise means, that his banished be not expelled from him.' Our lives are short, like water spilt on the ground. But God devises means, makes a way, so that those who are banished, far from him, do not stay cut off for ever. This is the heart of the gospel. We have wandered far from God. But he has made a way to bring us home, through Jesus.

KEY 14:24: 'And the king said, Let him turn to his own house, and let him not see my face. So Absalom returned to his own house, and saw not the king’s face.' Let him not see my face. David brought Absalom home, but kept him at a distance. Half forgiveness is not real forgiveness. Absalom’s bitterness grew during those two years. Reconciliation needs more than being in the same city. It needs open arms.

KEY 14:33: 'So Joab came to the king, and told him: and when he had called for Absalom, he came to the king, and bowed himself on his face to the ground before the king: and the king kissed Absalom.' The king kissed Absalom. At last. It is a tender moment. But we will soon see that Absalom’s heart had not changed. A kiss on the outside does not always mean peace on the inside. And sadly, what comes next shows that Absalom only wanted to get close to his father’s throne.

THREAD: Jesus told of a father who saw his son coming home, 'and had compassion, and ran, and fell on his neck, and kissed him.' And Paul wrote that God 'hath reconciled us to himself by Jesus Christ'.

TODAY: Is there someone you have kept at a distance, half forgiven? Ask God today whether it is time to open your arms fully. And thank God that he devised a way to bring you home to him.

QUESTION: The wise woman said that God devises means, that his banished be not expelled from him. How has God made a way to bring you close to him?

PRAYER: Lord God, my life is like water spilt on the ground. But you have devised a way to bring me home to you, through Jesus. Thank you that you do not keep me at a distance, but welcome me fully. Help me to forgive others fully too, not halfway. Amen.
