# 2 Samuel 17: Ahithophel's counsel to strike at once, Hushai's counsel (the bear robbed of her whelps, gather all Israel), the LORD appointed to defeat the good counsel of Ahithophel, the message through the priests' sons, the well at Bahurim, David crosses the Jordan, Ahithophel's end (gently), Mahanaim, Shobi, Machir and Barzillai bring food. Keys 17:14, 17:22, 17:29.
TITLE: The LORD Had Appointed to Defeat the Counsel of Ahithophel

HOOK: In a council room in Jerusalem, two advisers stand before the new king, each giving a plan. One plan is brilliant: strike now, while the old king is tired and weak, and it will all be over quickly. The other plan sounds grand, but would take time. The new king must choose. He has no idea that the second adviser is secretly working for his father. Or that God has already decided the outcome.

WHERE: In chapter sixteen, Ziba brought food to David, and Shimei cursed him and threw stones. David said, let him alone, it may be that the LORD will look on mine affliction. In Jerusalem, Hushai pretended to serve Absalom. And Ahithophel gave Absalom shameful advice, and his counsel was treated as if it came from God.

Now, in chapter seventeen, the two counsellors give their advice. Listen for whose advice Absalom takes, and why. And listen for the kindness David receives in the wilderness.

STORY: Ahithophel says to Absalom, let me choose twelve thousand men, and set out after David straight away. I will come upon him while he is weary and discouraged, and throw him into a panic. All the people with him will run away, and I will strike down the king alone. Then I will bring all the people back to you, and there will be peace.

It was a clever plan. Strike quickly, while David was weak. Target only David. Avoid a long war. Absalom and all the elders of Israel like it.

But Absalom says, call Hushai too, and let us hear what he says.

So Hushai comes, and Absalom tells him Ahithophel’s plan. Shall we do it? If not, tell us what you think.

And Hushai says, the advice Ahithophel has given is not good this time.

Then Hushai paints a frightening picture. You know your father and his men. They are mighty warriors, and they are as fierce as a bear robbed of her cubs. Your father is an experienced soldier. He will not lodge with the troops. He is probably hiding in a cave or some other place right now. If some of your men fall in the first attack, everyone will hear that Absalom’s army has been defeated. And even the bravest men, with hearts like lions, will lose courage.

So here is my advice, says Hushai. Gather all Israel to you, from Dan to Beer-sheba, as many as the sand by the sea. And lead them into battle yourself. Then we will fall on David like dew on the ground, and not one of his men will be left. And if he goes into a city, all Israel will bring ropes and drag the city into the valley, until not a stone is left.

Hushai’s plan flattered Absalom’s pride. You yourself, leading a huge army. And it bought David time, which was exactly what David needed.

Absalom and all the men of Israel say, Hushai’s advice is better than Ahithophel’s.

And then the writer tells us why.

David had prayed, O LORD, turn the counsel of Ahithophel into foolishness. And God answered, through Hushai.

Hushai quickly tells the priests Zadok and Abiathar both plans, and says, send word to David at once. Do not stay in the wilderness plains. Cross over the Jordan quickly, or the king and everyone with him will be swallowed up.

The priests’ sons, Jonathan and Ahimaaz, are waiting just outside the city, so they will not be seen. A servant girl brings them the message. But a young man sees them, and tells Absalom. So they hurry away to a man’s house in Bahurim, which has a well in the courtyard, and climb down into it. The woman of the house spreads a cloth over the well, and scatters grain on it, so nothing can be seen.

Absalom’s servants come and ask, where are Ahimaaz and Jonathan? She says, they have crossed over the stream. The servants search, but find nothing, and go back to Jerusalem.

Then the two young men climb out of the well, and go and tell King David. Get up, and cross the water quickly, for this is what Ahithophel has advised against you.

So David gets up, with all his people.

When Ahithophel sees that his advice has not been followed, he saddles his donkey and goes home to his own town. He puts his affairs in order, and takes his own life. Perhaps he knew that Absalom’s rebellion would now fail, and that he would be held to account as a traitor. It is a sad end for the wisest counsellor in Israel.

David comes to Mahanaim. Absalom crosses the Jordan with all the men of Israel, and makes Amasa commander of his army in place of Joab.

And then, in the wilderness, David receives unexpected kindness.

Shobi from the Ammonites. Machir from Lo-debar, the very man who had cared for Mephibosheth. And Barzillai, an old and wealthy man from Gilead. They bring beds and bowls and pots, wheat and barley and flour, roasted grain, beans and lentils, honey and butter, sheep and cheese. For they say, the people are hungry and weary and thirsty in the wilderness.

KEY 17:14: 'And Absalom and all the men of Israel said, The counsel of Hushai the Archite is better than the counsel of Ahithophel. For the LORD had appointed to defeat the good counsel of Ahithophel, to the intent that the LORD might bring evil upon Absalom.' Behind all the plans and arguments, God was in control. Ahithophel’s advice really was good, from a human point of view. But God had decided to defeat it. No plan, however clever, can succeed against the LORD. And God was answering David’s prayer.

KEY 17:22: 'Then David arose, and all the people that were with him, and they passed over Jordan: by the morning light there lacked not one of them that was not gone over Jordan.' Not one was missing. Every single person crossed safely. God protected all of them. Long before, Israel crossed the Jordan into the land under Joshua. Now David crosses it the other way, fleeing. But God is with him just the same.

KEY 17:29: 'And honey, and butter, and sheep, and cheese of kine, for David, and for the people that were with him, to eat: for they said, The people is hungry, and weary, and thirsty, in the wilderness.' In David’s darkest hour, God sent friends with food. One was a foreigner. One had cared for Jonathan’s son. One was an old man. They saw that the people were hungry, weary and thirsty, and they did something about it. God often provides for us through the kindness of others.

THREAD: A proverb says, 'There is no wisdom nor understanding nor counsel against the LORD.' And a psalm of David says, 'Thou preparest a table before me in the presence of mine enemies'.

TODAY: Is there someone you know who is hungry, weary or thirsty, in body or in spirit? Be a Barzillai to them today. Bring them something they need. And trust that God is in control, even when others make plans against you.

QUESTION: God defeated Ahithophel’s clever plan through Hushai. How does it encourage you to know that no plan can succeed against the LORD?

PRAYER: Lord God, there is no wisdom or plan that can stand against you. Thank you that you hear the prayers of your people, and that you are in control even when others plot against us. Thank you for friends who bring help in the wilderness. Make me that kind of friend to others. Amen.
