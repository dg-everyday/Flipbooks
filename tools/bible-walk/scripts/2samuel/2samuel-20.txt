# 2 Samuel 20: Sheba blows the trumpet, we have no part in David, Judah clave unto their king, the ten women cared for (gently, no "concubines"), Amasa delays, Abishai and Joab pursue, Joab strikes down Amasa (gently, avoids 20:10, 20:12), the siege of Abel, the wise woman, a mother in Israel, the city spared (20:21-22 told without detail), David's officials. Keys 20:2, 20:16, 20:19.
TITLE: I Am One of Them That Are Peaceable and Faithful

HOOK: An army has surrounded a small city. Soldiers are battering at the walls, ready to break through. Inside, everyone is terrified. Then a woman’s voice calls out from the top of the wall. Listen! Listen! Tell Joab to come near, so I can speak with him. And with a few wise words, she saves her whole city.

WHERE: In chapter nineteen, Joab told David to stop weeping and speak to his people. On the way home, David forgave Shimei, and Mephibosheth said, let him take all, forasmuch as my lord the king is come again in peace. Old Barzillai went home, and sent his son with the king. But the men of Israel and the men of Judah quarrelled over the king.

Now, in chapter twenty, that quarrel leads to another rebellion. Listen for who stays loyal to David. And listen for the wise woman of Abel.

STORY: There happens to be a troublemaker there, a worthless man named Sheba, the son of Bichri, from the tribe of Benjamin. And he blows a trumpet, and shouts.

Notice that Sheba used the quarrel between Israel and Judah. The tribes had been arguing about who had more right to the king. And Sheba stepped into that argument, and turned it into rebellion.

So all the men of Israel desert David, and follow Sheba.

David comes back to his palace in Jerusalem. The ten women of his household, whom he had left to look after the palace, and whom Absalom had shamed, he provides for, and keeps them safe in a house of their own for the rest of their lives.

Then the king says to Amasa, call up the men of Judah, and be back here within three days. But Amasa takes longer than the time David gave him.

So David says to Abishai, Sheba is going to do us more harm than Absalom did. Take my men and chase him, before he finds fortified cities and escapes. So Joab’s men, and the king’s bodyguard, and all the mighty warriors go out from Jerusalem after Sheba.

At the great stone in Gibeon, Amasa comes to meet them. Joab greets him. Are you well, my brother? And Joab takes Amasa by the beard with his right hand, as if to kiss him. But Amasa does not notice the sword in Joab’s other hand. And Joab strikes him down.

It is the second time Joab has done this. First Abner, and now Amasa. Both times with a friendly greeting. Both times to protect his own position. Joab was a skilled and loyal soldier. But he was also ruthless, and he took matters into his own hands.

Then Joab and Abishai continue chasing Sheba. Sheba goes through all the tribes of Israel, to a town called Abel of Beth-maachah, far in the north. Joab’s army comes and besieges him there. They build a ramp against the city, and begin battering the wall to bring it down.

Then a wise woman calls out from the city.

When Joab comes near, she asks, are you Joab? He says, I am. She says, listen to the words of your servant. And he says, I am listening.

She says, in the old days, people used to say, ask advice at Abel, and that would settle the matter. Abel was known as a city of wisdom, where people came to settle disputes.

And then she speaks for her city.

Joab answers, far be it from me, far be it from me, to swallow up or destroy. That is not my purpose. A man named Sheba, from the hill country of Ephraim, has rebelled against King David. Hand him over, just him, and I will leave the city.

The woman says, he will be handed over. Then she goes to all the people in her wisdom. And they deal with Sheba, and hand him over to Joab. And Joab blows the trumpet, and his men leave the city, and go home. And Joab goes back to the king in Jerusalem.

One wise woman, speaking up at the right moment, saved her whole city from destruction. She did not have an army. She had courage and wisdom.

The chapter ends with a list of David’s officials, much like the list in chapter eight. Joab over the army. Benaiah over the bodyguard. Adoram over the forced labour. Jehoshaphat the recorder. Sheva the scribe. Zadok and Abiathar the priests.

But this list is different from the first. In chapter eight, David was at the height of his power, and executed judgment and justice. Now, the kingdom has been through rebellion and bloodshed. David is older, sadder, and wiser. And yet God has kept him on the throne, just as he promised.

KEY 20:2: 'So every man of Israel went up from after David, and followed Sheba the son of Bichri: but the men of Judah clave unto their king, from Jordan even to Jerusalem.' Clave means held on tightly. When almost everyone else deserted David, the men of Judah held on to their king. Loyalty is tested when things go wrong. It is easy to follow a king in victory. It is harder to cling to him when others are walking away.

KEY 20:16: 'Then cried a wise woman out of the city, Hear, hear; say, I pray you, unto Joab, Come near hither, that I may speak with thee.' A wise woman, whose name we are never told. She did not wait for someone else to act. She spoke up, boldly, to the commander of the army. Like Abigail, who stopped David from taking revenge, this woman stopped Joab from destroying a city. God often uses the courage of ordinary people at just the right moment.

KEY 20:19: 'I am one of them that are peaceable and faithful in Israel: thou seekest to destroy a city and a mother in Israel: why wilt thou swallow up the inheritance of the LORD?' Peaceable and faithful. That is how she described herself and her city. And she reminded Joab that the city was part of the inheritance of the LORD, God’s people, not an enemy. Sometimes all it takes to stop a conflict is someone willing to say, wait, we are on the same side.

THREAD: Jesus said, 'Blessed are the peacemakers: for they shall be called the children of God.' And the Preacher wrote, 'Wisdom is better than weapons of war'.

TODAY: Is there a conflict around you where a calm, wise word could make a difference? Ask God for wisdom and courage to speak up, like the woman of Abel. And think about how you can cling to Jesus, your King, even when others turn away.

QUESTION: The wise woman of Abel saved her city with a few wise words. When have you seen a calm, wise word stop a conflict from getting worse?

PRAYER: Lord God, make me peaceable and faithful. Give me wisdom to know when to speak, and courage to speak up for peace. Help me to cling to Jesus, my King, even when others walk away. And thank you that you keep your promises, through every storm. Amen.
