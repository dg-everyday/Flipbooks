# 2 Samuel 21: the famine, David enquires, Saul's broken oath to the Gibeonites (Joshua 9), the Gibeonites' demand (told gently), Mephibosheth spared for the oath, Rizpah's watch, the bones of Saul and Jonathan buried, God intreated for the land, David faint in battle, the light of Israel, the giants of Gath (no slew). Avoids 21:1, 21:6, 21:9, 21:17. Keys 21:7, 21:14, 21:22.
TITLE: Because of the LORD’s Oath

HOOK: On a rocky hillside, a grieving mother spreads a rough cloth of sackcloth on the ground, and sits down. She will not leave. All through the long weeks of harvest, through heat and through the dark, she stays there, guarding the bodies of her sons, chasing away the birds and the wild animals. Her name is Rizpah. And when the king hears what she has done, it moves him to act.

WHERE: In chapter twenty, Sheba blew a trumpet and said, we have no part in David. Israel followed him, but Judah clave unto their king. Joab pursued Sheba to the city of Abel, and a wise woman said, I am one of them that are peaceable and faithful in Israel, and her city was saved.

Now, in chapter twenty one, we come to the last part of the book. These last four chapters look back over David’s life. This chapter has hard parts, which we will tell gently. Listen for how an old broken promise brings trouble. And listen for how David keeps his own promise.

STORY: There is a famine in the days of David, year after year, for three years. And David seeks the face of the LORD.

That is the right thing to do. When trouble comes, ask God why.

And the LORD answers. The famine is because of Saul and his house, because he put the Gibeonites to death.

Remember the Gibeonites? In the book of Joshua, they tricked Israel into making a promise of peace. Israel had sworn by the LORD to let them live. And Israel kept that promise for many years. But Saul, in his zeal for Israel, had broken it, and tried to wipe out the Gibeonites. That broken oath, sworn in God’s name, had never been put right.

So David calls the Gibeonites, and asks, what shall I do for you? How can I make amends, so that you will bless the inheritance of the LORD?

The Gibeonites say, we do not want silver or gold from Saul’s family. Nor do we want anyone in Israel put to death for us, except from the family of the man who tried to destroy us. They ask that seven of Saul’s descendants be handed over to them.

This is hard to hear. In the ancient world, justice was often carried out on families, not just individuals. The Bible tells this honestly. And David agrees to it.

But there is one person David will not hand over.

Mephibosheth, Jonathan’s son, the young man who ate at David’s table. David protected him, because of his promise to Jonathan. Saul broke his oath to the Gibeonites. David kept his oath to Jonathan.

So seven of Saul’s descendants are handed over to the Gibeonites, and they are put to death on the hill before the LORD, at the beginning of the barley harvest. Two of them are sons of Rizpah, who had been in Saul’s household.

And then comes the scene that moves us most in this chapter. Rizpah takes sackcloth and spreads it out on the rock for herself. And from the beginning of the harvest until the rain falls from heaven, she stays there. She does not let the birds come near the bodies by day, or the wild animals in the dark.

A mother’s love, that would not leave her sons, even in death. She could not save them. But she could stay with them, and protect their bodies with dignity.

When David is told what Rizpah has done, he is moved. He goes and takes the bones of Saul and Jonathan from the men of Jabesh-gilead, who had rescued them from the Philistines long before. And he gathers the bones of the seven who were put to death.

The chapter then tells of some battles with the Philistines, near the end of David’s life. In one battle, David becomes exhausted, and a huge Philistine, one of the descendants of the giants, comes against him with a heavy spear and a new sword. But Abishai comes to David’s rescue, and strikes down the Philistine.

Then David’s men make him promise. You shall not go out to battle with us any more, so that the lamp of Israel is not put out.

David was getting old. The young man who faced Goliath alone now needed others to protect him. There is no shame in that. David’s men loved him, and called him the light of Israel.

There are more battles with the descendants of the giants of Gath. One is defeated by Sibbechai. Another by Elhanan, from Bethlehem. Another, a huge man with six fingers on each hand and six toes on each foot, who defied Israel, is defeated by David’s nephew Jonathan.

Long ago, a shepherd boy defeated one giant with a sling. Now, at the end of his life, the men David trained are defeating giants too. David’s faith had been passed on.

KEY 21:7: 'But the king spared Mephibosheth, the son of Jonathan the son of Saul, because of the LORD’S oath that was between them, between David and Jonathan the son of Saul.' Because of the LORD’s oath. Saul broke a promise made in God’s name, and it brought a famine. David kept a promise made in God’s name, and it saved Mephibosheth. Promises made before God matter. God remembers them, even when people forget.

KEY 21:14: 'And the bones of Saul and Jonathan his son buried they in the country of Benjamin in Zelah, in the sepulchre of Kish his father: and they performed all that the king commanded. And after that God was intreated for the land.' At last, Saul and Jonathan were brought home, and buried in the family tomb. After the wrong was put right, God was intreated, which means he heard their prayers, and ended the famine. Some wounds need to be faced and put right before healing can come.

KEY 21:22: 'These four were born to the giant in Gath, and fell by the hand of David, and by the hand of his servants.' By the hand of David, and by the hand of his servants. The victories over giants that began with David did not end with him. His men learned to trust God and face giants too. The best leaders pass on their faith, so that others can carry on the work when they are gone.

THREAD: The book of Ecclesiastes says, 'When thou vowest a vow unto God, defer not to pay it'. And Jesus said, 'Blessed are they that mourn: for they shall be comforted.'

TODAY: Is there a promise you made, to God or to someone else, that you have not kept? Ask God to help you put it right. And think of someone who is grieving. Sit with them, as Rizpah sat with her sons, and let them know they are not alone.

QUESTION: Saul broke his oath and it brought trouble, while David kept his oath to Jonathan. Why do you think God cares so much about the promises we make?

PRAYER: Lord God, you keep every promise you make. Help me to keep my promises too, and to put right any I have broken. Comfort all who mourn, as Rizpah mourned. And help me to pass on my faith to others, so that they too can face the giants in their lives. Amen.
