# Acts 3: the lame man at the Beautiful gate, Peter's second sermon. Keys 3:6, 3:16, 3:19.
TITLE: Such as I Have Give I Thee

HOOK: Every day, a man is carried to the gate of the temple and laid on the ground there to beg. He has never walked, not once in his life. He is more than forty years old. As two men walk past him toward the temple, he holds out his hand, hoping for a coin. One of them stops, looks straight at him, and says, look on us.

WHERE: In chapter two, the Holy Ghost came at Pentecost. Peter preached that God had raised Jesus and made him both Lord and Christ, and about three thousand people believed. The new believers continued steadfastly in the apostles’ teaching, in fellowship, in breaking bread and in prayers, and many wonders and signs were done by the apostles.

Now, in chapter three, Luke shows us one of those wonders up close. And it gives Peter another chance to tell a crowd about Jesus.

STORY: Peter and John go up together into the temple at the hour of prayer, the ninth hour. A certain man, lame from his mother’s womb, is being carried in. Every day he is laid at the gate of the temple called Beautiful, to ask for gifts from those who go in. When he sees Peter and John about to go in, he asks them for money.

Peter fixes his eyes on him, with John, and says, 'Look on us.' The man gives them his attention, expecting to receive something from them. Then Peter speaks. And he takes him by the right hand, and lifts him up. Immediately his feet and ankle bones receive strength. He leaps up, stands, and walks, and goes with them into the temple, 'walking, and leaping, and praising God.'

All the people see him walking and praising God. They know he is the man who sat begging at the Beautiful gate. And they are filled with wonder and amazement. As the healed man holds on to Peter and John, all the people run together to them, in the place called Solomon’s porch, a long covered walkway in the temple courts.

When Peter sees the crowd, he speaks to them. Men of Israel, why do you marvel at this? Why do you stare at us, as though by our own power or holiness we had made this man walk? The God of Abraham, of Isaac, and of Jacob, the God of our fathers, has glorified his Son Jesus. You handed him over, and denied him in the presence of Pilate, when Pilate had decided to let him go. 'But ye denied the Holy One and the Just, and desired a murderer to be granted unto you; And killed the Prince of life, whom God hath raised from the dead; whereof we are witnesses.'

Peter tells them that the name of Jesus, and faith in his name, has made this man strong. Then his voice softens. 'And now, brethren, I wot that through ignorance ye did it, as did also your rulers.' I wot means I know. You did not understand what you were doing. And God used even this to fulfil what he had foretold by all his prophets, that the Christ should suffer.

So Peter calls them to turn back to God. He tells them that God will send Jesus Christ again, who must remain in heaven until the times of restoring all things, which God has spoken of by the mouth of all his holy prophets since the world began.

Moses himself said, Peter tells them, that the Lord your God would raise up a prophet like him from among his brothers, and everyone must listen to that prophet. And all the prophets from Samuel onward spoke of these days. You are the children of the prophets, and of the covenant God made with Abraham, when he said, in thy seed shall all the kindreds of the earth be blessed. 'Unto you first God, having raised up his Son Jesus, sent him to bless you, in turning away every one of you from his iniquities.'

KEY 3:6: 'Then Peter said, Silver and gold have I none; but such as I have give I thee: In the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth rise up and walk.' Peter had no money to give. But he had something far greater: Jesus. The man asked for a coin to get him through one more day. He received legs to walk on for the rest of his life. We may think we have little to offer the people around us. But if we know Jesus, we have the best gift of all to share.

KEY 3:16: 'And his name through faith in his name hath made this man strong, whom ye see and know: yea, the faith which is by him hath given him this perfect soundness in the presence of you all.' Peter refuses to take any credit. It was not his power or his goodness. It was the name of Jesus. And notice where the faith came from: 'the faith which is by him'. Even our faith is a gift from Jesus. Perfect soundness means complete health. Jesus does not do half a work.

KEY 3:19: 'Repent ye therefore, and be converted, that your sins may be blotted out, when the times of refreshing shall come from the presence of the Lord;' To be converted means to turn around, to turn back to God. Blotted out means wiped away completely, like writing washed off a page. And then comes a beautiful promise: times of refreshing, from the presence of the Lord. Turning to God is not only about leaving sin behind. It is about being refreshed, like cool water on a hot and dusty day.

THREAD: The prophet Isaiah had written about the days when God would come to save his people: 'Then shall the lame man leap as an hart, and the tongue of the dumb sing'. A hart is a deer. At the Beautiful gate, a man who had never walked went leaping into the temple. Isaiah’s picture was coming true in front of everyone.

TODAY: Think about what you have to give. It may not be silver or gold. But you can give your time, your kindness, your prayers, and the name of Jesus. And if there is sin weighing on you, turn back to God today. He will blot it out, and refresh you.

QUESTION: Peter said, such as I have give I thee. What do you have that you could give to someone in need today?

PRAYER: Lord Jesus, thank you for your name, which made the lame man strong. I turn back to you; blot out my sins, and refresh me with your presence. Show me what I have to give, and use me to bring your name to someone in need. Amen.
