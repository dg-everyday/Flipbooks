# Acts 4: Peter and John before the council, no other name, the believers pray, all things common. Keys 4:12, 4:13, 4:31.
TITLE: Neither Is There Salvation in Any Other

HOOK: Two fishermen stand before the most powerful men in Jerusalem, the same council that condemned Jesus only weeks before. The rulers are learned and proud. The fishermen have had no training in the schools of the law. The rulers ask, by what power, or by what name, have you done this? And Peter, filled with the Holy Ghost, answers without fear.

WHERE: In chapter three, Peter and John healed a man lame from birth at the Beautiful gate of the temple, in the name of Jesus. The man went walking and leaping and praising God, and a great crowd gathered. Peter told them it was the name of Jesus that had made the man strong, and called them to repent and turn back to God.

Now, in chapter four, the leaders of the temple are angry. Opposition comes to the church for the first time. Watch how the apostles answer it, and how the believers pray.

STORY: While Peter and John are speaking to the people, the priests, the captain of the temple and the Sadducees come upon them. They are grieved that the apostles are teaching the people, and preaching through Jesus the resurrection from the dead. They arrest them and put them in custody until the next day, because the day is nearly over. But many of those who heard the word believe, and the number of the men grows to about five thousand.

The next day the rulers, elders and scribes gather in Jerusalem, with Annas the high priest, and Caiaphas, and others of the high priest’s family. They set Peter and John in the middle and ask them, 'By what power, or by what name, have ye done this?'

Then Peter, filled with the Holy Ghost, answers. Rulers of the people, and elders of Israel, if we are being examined today about a good deed done to a helpless man, and how he was made well, then let all of you and all the people of Israel know this. It is by the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, whom you crucified, whom God raised from the dead, that this man stands here before you whole. Peter tells them that Jesus is the stone which the builders set aside, which has become the head of the corner.

The council sees the boldness of Peter and John, and they marvel. And the healed man is standing right there beside them, so they can say nothing against it. They send the apostles out of the room, and talk among themselves. What shall we do with these men? Everyone in Jerusalem knows that a remarkable miracle has been done, and we cannot deny it. But to stop it spreading any further, let us threaten them, and tell them to speak to no one in this name.

So they call them back, and command them not to speak at all or teach in the name of Jesus. But Peter and John answer, 'Whether it be right in the sight of God to hearken unto you more than unto God, judge ye. For we cannot but speak the things which we have seen and heard.' The council threatens them further, and lets them go, finding no way to punish them, because all the people are glorifying God for what has happened.

Peter and John go back to their own company, and report everything the chief priests and elders have said. And when the believers hear it, they do not complain or panic. They lift up their voice to God with one accord, and pray. 'Lord, thou art God, which hast made heaven, and earth, and the sea, and all that in them is'. They remember the second psalm, which asks why the nations rage, and the kings of the earth set themselves against the Lord and against his Christ. It has happened, they say, just as you planned. Herod and Pontius Pilate, with the Gentiles and the people of Israel, gathered against your holy child Jesus.

And what do they ask for? Not safety, and not for their enemies to be punished. 'And now, Lord, behold their threatenings: and grant unto thy servants, that with all boldness they may speak thy word'.

After they pray, the answer comes at once.

The believers are of one heart and one soul. No one says that any of the things he owns is his own; they share everything. With great power the apostles give witness of the resurrection of the Lord Jesus, and great grace is upon them all. No one among them lacks anything, because those who own land or houses sell them, and bring the money, and lay it at the apostles’ feet, and it is shared to each as he has need. One of them is a Levite from Cyprus named Joseph, whom the apostles call Barnabas, which means the son of consolation, the son of encouragement. He sells a field, and brings the money, and lays it at the apostles’ feet.

KEY 4:12: 'Neither is there salvation in any other: for there is none other name under heaven given among men, whereby we must be saved.' Peter says this to the very men who condemned Jesus. He does not soften it to keep himself safe. There are many great names in history, many teachers and leaders. But only one name can save. Only Jesus died for our sins and rose again. This is not a closed door; it is an open one. The name that saves is offered to everyone.

KEY 4:13: 'Now when they saw the boldness of Peter and John, and perceived that they were unlearned and ignorant men, they marvelled; and they took knowledge of them, that they had been with Jesus.' Unlearned and ignorant here means they had no formal schooling, not that they were foolish. The council could not explain them, until they realised one thing: these men had been with Jesus. Time with Jesus changes people, and others notice. You do not need a title or a degree to speak for him.

KEY 4:31: 'And when they had prayed, the place was shaken where they were assembled together; and they were all filled with the Holy Ghost, and they spake the word of God with boldness.' They prayed for boldness, and God answered by filling them again with the Holy Ghost. Notice that they had already been filled at Pentecost. Being filled with the Spirit is not only a one time event. We can ask God again and again to fill us, especially when we are afraid.

THREAD: Peter quoted a psalm about a rejected stone: 'The stone which the builders refused is become the head stone of the corner.' Jesus had quoted the same psalm in the temple, near the end of his life. The leaders had rejected him. But God made him the cornerstone, the stone that holds the whole building together.

TODAY: Is there something you are afraid to say for Jesus? Pray the prayer of the first believers: Lord, grant me boldness to speak your word. Spend time with Jesus in his Word and in prayer, and let others see that you have been with him.

QUESTION: The council took knowledge of them, that they had been with Jesus. What would help people see that you have been with Jesus today?

PRAYER: Lord God, you made heaven and earth and the sea, and all that is in them. There is no other name by which we must be saved, but the name of Jesus. When I am afraid, fill me again with your Holy Spirit, and grant me boldness to speak your word. Amen.
