# Acts 5: Ananias and Sapphira, signs and wonders, the apostles freed, Gamaliel's counsel, rejoicing in suffering. Keys 5:4, 5:29, 5:41.
TITLE: We Ought to Obey God Rather than Men

HOOK: The apostles have been beaten. The council has whipped them and commanded them, once again, never to speak in the name of Jesus. Their backs are bleeding as they walk out of the council chamber. But they are not weeping or complaining. They are rejoicing. They are glad, because they have been counted worthy to suffer shame for his name.

WHERE: In chapter four, Peter and John were arrested and brought before the council, and declared that there is salvation in no other name than Jesus. The believers prayed for boldness, and were all filled with the Holy Ghost. They shared everything they had, and Barnabas sold a field and gave the money to the apostles.

Chapter five begins with a couple who want to look as generous as Barnabas, without really being so. It is a sobering chapter. It shows how seriously God takes honesty in his church, and how nothing can stop his word.

STORY: A man named Ananias, with Sapphira his wife, sells a piece of property. He keeps back part of the price, with his wife’s knowledge, and brings only a part and lays it at the apostles’ feet, as if it were the whole.

Peter says, 'Ananias, why hath Satan filled thine heart to lie to the Holy Ghost'? Then he tells him plainly that the land was his to keep, and the money was his to use. When Ananias hears these words, he falls down and dies. Great fear comes on all who hear it. The young men rise, wrap him up, carry him out and bury him.

About three hours later, his wife comes in, not knowing what has happened. Peter asks her, did you sell the land for so much? She says, yes, for so much. Peter says, 'How is it that ye have agreed together to tempt the Spirit of the Lord?' And she too falls down at his feet and dies. Great fear comes on the whole church, and on all who hear these things.

Many signs and wonders are done among the people by the hands of the apostles. The believers meet with one accord in Solomon’s porch. More and more believers are added to the Lord, crowds of both men and women. People even carry the sick out into the streets on beds and mats, so that at least the shadow of Peter passing by might fall on some of them. Crowds come from the cities around Jerusalem, bringing the sick and those troubled by unclean spirits, and they are healed, every one.

Then the high priest and the Sadducees are filled with jealousy. They arrest the apostles and put them in the common prison. But in the dark, an angel of the Lord opens the prison doors, brings them out, and says, 'Go, stand and speak in the temple to the people all the words of this life.' So at first light they enter the temple and teach.

The high priest calls the council together, and sends to the prison to have the apostles brought. But the officers come back and report, we found the prison shut securely, and the guards standing before the doors, but when we opened them, we found no one inside. Then someone comes and says, the men you put in prison are standing in the temple, teaching the people. The captain brings them in, but without violence, because they fear the people will stone them.

The high priest says, did we not strictly command you not to teach in this name? And look, you have filled Jerusalem with your teaching, and you want to bring this man’s blood upon us. Then Peter and the other apostles answer. And they speak again of Jesus: the God of our fathers raised up Jesus, whom you killed by hanging him on a tree. God has exalted him at his right hand to be a Prince and a Saviour, to give repentance and forgiveness of sins to Israel. 'And we are his witnesses of these things; and so is also the Holy Ghost'.

The council is furious, and wants to kill them. But a Pharisee named Gamaliel, a teacher of the law respected by all the people, stands up and has the apostles put outside for a little while. Then he speaks to the council. Be careful what you do with these men. Other leaders have risen up before, and gathered followers, and come to nothing. So leave these men alone. 'for if this counsel or this work be of men, it will come to nought: But if it be of God, ye cannot overthrow it; lest haply ye be found even to fight against God.'

The council agrees with him. They call the apostles in, beat them, command them not to speak in the name of Jesus, and let them go. And every day, in the temple and in every house, they do not stop teaching and preaching Jesus Christ.

KEY 5:4: 'Whiles it remained, was it not thine own? and after it was sold, was it not in thine own power? why hast thou conceived this thing in thine heart? thou hast not lied unto men, but unto God.' The sin of Ananias was not keeping some of the money. Peter says it was his to keep. The sin was pretending, lying to look more generous than he was. And Peter says that lying to the church is lying to God, because the Holy Ghost lives among his people. God wants honest hearts, not impressive appearances.

KEY 5:29: 'Then Peter and the other apostles answered and said, We ought to obey God rather than men.' The apostles were not rebels by nature. Elsewhere the Bible teaches us to respect those in authority. But when a human command forbids what God commands, God comes first. They had been told by Jesus himself to be his witnesses. No council could cancel that.

KEY 5:41: 'And they departed from the presence of the council, rejoicing that they were counted worthy to suffer shame for his name.' This is one of the most surprising verses in Acts. They rejoiced, not in spite of being beaten, but because they were counted worthy to share in the shame of Jesus. They remembered what he had said: blessed are you when men hate you for the Son of man’s sake; rejoice in that day. Suffering for Jesus is an honour.

THREAD: Gamaliel warned the council not to fight against God. Long before, a psalm had asked, 'Why do the heathen rage, and the people imagine a vain thing?' The believers had prayed that very psalm in chapter four. The rulers can rage and threaten, but what God has begun, no one can overthrow.

TODAY: Is there any part of your life where you are pretending, trying to look better than you are? Bring it honestly to God. And if following Jesus costs you something today, even a little shame, remember the apostles. It is an honour to bear his name.

QUESTION: The apostles said, we ought to obey God rather than men. Where do you need courage to put God first today?

PRAYER: Lord God, you see my heart. Make me honest before you and before others, with nothing hidden. Give me courage to obey you rather than people, and joy, even when following Jesus costs me something. Thank you that no one can overthrow your work. Amen.
