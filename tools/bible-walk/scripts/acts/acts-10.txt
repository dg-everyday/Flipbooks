# Acts 10: Cornelius, Peter's vision of the sheet, Peter at Caesarea, the Spirit falls on the Gentiles. Keys 10:15, 10:34, 10:43.
TITLE: God Is No Respecter of Persons

HOOK: Peter is praying on a flat rooftop by the sea, and he is hungry. While lunch is being prepared below, he falls into a trance. He sees heaven opened, and something like a great sheet let down by its four corners. It is full of all kinds of animals, the very creatures the law of Moses called unclean, the ones Peter has never eaten in his life. And a voice says, rise, Peter; kill, and eat.

WHERE: In chapter nine, Saul met the risen Jesus on the road to Damascus, and was chosen to carry the name of Jesus to the Gentiles. Then Peter healed Aeneas at Lydda, and raised Dorcas from the dead at Joppa, where he stayed with a man named Simon, a tanner.

So far, almost everyone who has believed in Jesus has been a Jew, or a Samaritan who shared part of their faith. The Gentiles are all the other nations. In chapter ten, God opens the door of his family to them. This is a turning point in the story of the church.

STORY: In Caesarea lives a man named Cornelius, a centurion, a Roman officer in charge of a hundred soldiers. He is a devout man, who fears God with all his household. He gives generously to the poor, and prays to God always.

About the ninth hour of the day, he sees an angel of God in a vision, coming in and saying, Cornelius. He stares at him in fear and says, 'What is it, Lord?' The angel says, 'Thy prayers and thine alms are come up for a memorial before God.' Alms are gifts to the poor. Now send men to Joppa, and call for Simon, whose surname is Peter. He is staying with Simon the tanner, by the sea. So Cornelius sends two of his servants and a devout soldier to Joppa.

The next day, as they come near the city, Peter goes up on the housetop to pray, about the sixth hour. He becomes very hungry. While the food is being made ready, he falls into a trance, and sees the sheet full of animals. A voice says, rise, Peter; kill, and eat. But Peter says, 'Not so, Lord; for I have never eaten any thing that is common or unclean.' And the voice speaks to him again. This happens three times, and then the sheet is taken back up into heaven.

While Peter is wondering what the vision means, the men from Cornelius are at the gate, asking for him. The Spirit says to Peter, three men are looking for you. Go with them, doubting nothing, for I have sent them. Peter goes down and says, I am the one you are looking for. They tell him about Cornelius, a just man who fears God and is well spoken of by all the Jews, and how an angel told him to send for Peter. So Peter invites them in to stay.

The next day Peter goes with them, and some believers from Joppa go too. When they arrive at Caesarea, Cornelius is waiting, and has called together his relatives and close friends. As Peter comes in, Cornelius falls down at his feet to worship him. But Peter lifts him up and says, 'Stand up; I myself also am a man.'

Peter goes in and finds many people gathered. He says, you know it is against our custom for a Jew to keep company with someone of another nation, or to visit him. 'but God hath shewed me that I should not call any man common or unclean.' Peter finally understands the vision. It was never really about food. It was about people.

Cornelius tells about his vision, and says, 'Now therefore are we all here present before God, to hear all things that are commanded thee of God.'

Then Peter opens his mouth and speaks. And he tells them about Jesus. God sent his word to Israel, preaching peace by Jesus Christ: 'he is Lord of all'. God anointed Jesus of Nazareth with the Holy Ghost and with power. He went about doing good, and healing all who were oppressed by the devil, for God was with him. We are witnesses of everything he did. They killed him by hanging him on a tree. But God raised him up on the third day, and showed him openly to witnesses chosen by God, who ate and drank with him after he rose from the dead. He commanded us to preach, and to testify that he is the one ordained by God to be the judge of the living and the dead.

While Peter is still speaking, the Holy Ghost falls on all who hear the word. The believers who came with Peter are astonished, because the gift of the Holy Ghost is poured out on the Gentiles too. They hear them speak with tongues, and magnify God. Then Peter says, 'Can any man forbid water, that these should not be baptized, which have received the Holy Ghost as well as we?' And he commands them to be baptized in the name of the Lord. Then they ask him to stay with them for some days.

KEY 10:15: 'And the voice spake unto him again the second time, What God hath cleansed, that call not thou common.' Common here means unclean, unfit for God. For centuries, Jews had kept apart from Gentiles, and Peter had grown up that way. But God was teaching him that the dividing wall was coming down. If God has made someone clean through Jesus, no one has the right to call them unclean. That includes people of every nation, and it includes you.

KEY 10:34: 'Then Peter opened his mouth, and said, Of a truth I perceive that God is no respecter of persons:' To be a respecter of persons means to show favouritism, to treat people differently because of who they are on the outside. God does not do that. Rich or poor, Jew or Gentile, any nation, any colour, any language. The next verse says that in every nation, whoever fears him and does what is right is accepted with him. God’s welcome is wide.

KEY 10:43: 'To him give all the prophets witness, that through his name whosoever believeth in him shall receive remission of sins.' Peter sums up the whole message in one sentence. All the prophets point to Jesus. Whosoever believes in him, anyone at all, receives remission of sins, which means forgiveness, sins sent away. Not by being born into the right family, or by keeping the right customs, but through faith in his name.

THREAD: Long before, God told Abraham, 'in thee shall all families of the earth be blessed.' Not one family or one nation, but all of them. And Jonah, the reluctant prophet, learned that God cared even for the people of Nineveh. In the house of Cornelius, God’s promise to Abraham reached a Roman soldier and his friends.

TODAY: Is there any group of people you find it hard to welcome, people you might quietly think of as outsiders? Ask God to show you how he sees them. And remember that you too were welcomed, not because of who you are, but through the name of Jesus.

QUESTION: Peter said, God is no respecter of persons. Who is someone different from you that you could show God’s welcome to today?

PRAYER: Lord God, thank you that you show no favouritism, and that whosoever believes in Jesus receives forgiveness. Forgive me for the times I have looked down on others. Open my heart, as you opened Peter’s, to welcome everyone you have made clean. Amen.
