# Acts 12: James killed, Peter in prison, the angel, Rhoda at the gate, Herod's death. Keys 12:5, 12:11, 12:24.
TITLE: But the Word of God Grew and Multiplied

HOOK: Peter is asleep in prison, chained between two soldiers, with guards at the door. In a few hours King Herod plans to bring him out and put him to death. Then a light shines in the cell, and someone strikes Peter on the side to wake him. It is an angel, and he says, get up quickly. And the chains fall off Peter’s hands.

WHERE: In chapter eleven, Peter explained to the believers in Jerusalem how God had given the Holy Ghost to the Gentiles, and they praised God. In Antioch, Barnabas and Saul taught a growing church, where the disciples were first called Christians, and they sent help to the believers in Judaea when famine came.

Now, in chapter twelve, the scene moves back to Jerusalem, and danger comes from the highest place of all, the king himself. This is the last chapter in Acts where Peter is the main character. After this, the story will follow Saul.

STORY: About that time, King Herod stretches out his hands to harm some of the church. This is Herod Agrippa, a grandson of the Herod who ruled when Jesus was born. He kills James, the brother of John, with the sword. James was one of the first disciples Jesus called, one of the three closest to him. And when Herod sees that this pleases the Jewish leaders, he arrests Peter too.

It is the time of the feast of unleavened bread, the Passover. Herod puts Peter in prison, guarded by four squads of four soldiers each. He plans to bring him out to the people after the feast.

On the very last stretch before Herod would bring him out, Peter is sleeping between two soldiers, bound with two chains, and guards before the door are keeping the prison. And the angel of the Lord comes, and a light shines in the prison. He strikes Peter on the side and raises him up, saying, 'Arise up quickly.' And the chains fall off his hands.

The angel says, get dressed, and put on your sandals. Wrap your cloak around you, and follow me. Peter follows him out, but he does not know that what is happening is real. He thinks he is seeing a vision. They pass the first guard, and the second, and come to the iron gate that leads into the city. It opens for them by itself. They go out, and walk along one street, and suddenly the angel is gone.

Then Peter comes to himself, and he understands what has happened.

He goes to the house of Mary, the mother of John Mark, where many believers are gathered, praying. Peter knocks at the door of the gate, and a servant girl named Rhoda comes to answer. When she recognises Peter’s voice, she is so glad that she does not open the gate. She runs in and tells everyone that Peter is standing outside.

They say to her, 'Thou art mad.' She keeps insisting that it is true. Then they say, 'It is his angel.' But Peter keeps on knocking. When they finally open the door and see him, they are astonished. He waves his hand to quieten them, and tells them how the Lord brought him out of prison. Tell these things to James, he says, meaning James the brother of Jesus, and to the believers. Then he leaves, and goes to another place.

As soon as it is day, there is no small stir among the soldiers about what has become of Peter. Herod searches for him and cannot find him. He questions the guards, and commands that they be put to death.

Some time later, Herod goes down to Caesarea. The people of Tyre and Sidon, who depend on his country for food, come to seek peace with him. On a set day, Herod puts on his royal robes, sits on his throne, and makes a speech to them. And the people shout, 'It is the voice of a god, and not of a man.' Immediately the angel of the Lord strikes him, because he does not give God the glory. He is eaten by worms, and dies.

And then Luke gives one of his short reports.

Barnabas and Saul return from Jerusalem, having delivered the famine gift, and they take with them John, whose surname is Mark.

KEY 12:5: 'Peter therefore was kept in prison: but prayer was made without ceasing of the church unto God for him.' Notice the word but. On one side, Herod’s prison, chains, soldiers, and gates of iron. On the other side, a group of believers praying. It does not look like a fair contest. But prayer was made without ceasing, which means earnestly, constantly, and with all their hearts. The church had no power to open the prison. But they knew the God who did.

KEY 12:11: 'And when Peter was come to himself, he said, Now I know of a surety, that the Lord hath sent his angel, and hath delivered me out of the hand of Herod, and from all the expectation of the people of the Jews.' Of a surety means for certain. It took a while for Peter to realise it was all real. Sometimes God answers so wonderfully that we can hardly believe it. Even the believers who were praying for Peter did not believe Rhoda at first. God is able to do more than we expect, even when we pray.

KEY 12:24: 'But the word of God grew and multiplied.' This short verse stands in sharp contrast to what comes just before it. Herod, who killed James and tried to kill Peter, the proud king who accepted praise as a god, is dead. And the word of God? It grew and multiplied. Kings come and go. Persecutors rise and fall. But the word of God keeps growing.

THREAD: A psalm says, 'The angel of the LORD encampeth round about them that fear him, and delivereth them.' God does not always rescue his people from death. James died, and he was just as faithful as Peter. But whether by rescue, or by taking them home, God keeps his people, and his word cannot be chained.

TODAY: Is there a situation in your life that looks like a locked prison, with no way out? Pray without ceasing, and ask others to pray with you. And when God answers, do not be like the believers who would not open the door. Expect him to act.

QUESTION: The church prayed without ceasing for Peter. Who or what will you pray for earnestly today, and keep on praying for?

PRAYER: Lord God, no chains or iron gates can stop you. Thank you that the word of God grows and multiplies, even when the powerful oppose it. Teach me to pray without ceasing, and to expect you to answer. Keep me humble, giving you the glory in all things. Amen.
