# Acts 18: Paul in Corinth with Aquila and Priscilla, the vision, Gallio, the return, Apollos taught. Keys 18:9, 18:10, 18:26.
TITLE: Be Not Afraid, but Speak, and Hold Not Thy Peace

HOOK: Paul arrives in Corinth alone, tired, and probably discouraged. He has been beaten in Philippi, driven out of Thessalonica and Berea, and mocked in Athens. Corinth is a busy port city, rich, crowded, and famous for its wickedness. Paul needs work to pay his way, so he goes looking for people who share his trade. He makes tents.

WHERE: In chapter seventeen, Paul preached in Thessalonica, where his enemies said the believers had turned the world upside down. In Berea, the people searched the Scriptures daily. And in Athens, Paul stood on Mars’ hill and told the philosophers about the unknown God, in whom we live, and move, and have our being.

Now, in chapter eighteen, Paul stays longer in one place than he has anywhere yet. Watch how God encourages him, and how a husband and wife become some of the most important helpers in the early church.

STORY: After these things, Paul leaves Athens and comes to Corinth. There he finds a Jewish man named Aquila, born in Pontus, recently come from Italy with his wife Priscilla, because the emperor Claudius has commanded all Jews to leave Rome. Paul goes to see them, and because they are tentmakers like him, he stays and works with them. And every sabbath he reasons in the synagogue, persuading both Jews and Greeks.

When Silas and Timothy arrive from Macedonia, Paul gives himself fully to preaching, testifying to the Jews that Jesus is the Christ. But when they oppose him and blaspheme, he shakes out his clothes and says, your blood be upon your own heads; I am clean. From now on I will go to the Gentiles.

So he moves next door, into the house of a man named Justus, who worships God. And Crispus, the chief ruler of the synagogue, believes on the Lord with all his house. Many of the Corinthians, hearing, believe and are baptized.

Then the Lord speaks to Paul in a vision. So Paul stays in Corinth a year and six months, teaching the word of God among them.

When Gallio is the Roman governor of the province of Achaia, the Jewish leaders rise up together against Paul and bring him to the judgment seat. This fellow, they say, persuades men to worship God contrary to the law. Paul is about to answer, but Gallio speaks first. If this were a matter of wrongdoing or wicked crime, he says, I would listen to you. But if it is a question of words and names and your own law, sort it out yourselves. I will be no judge of such matters. And he drives them away from the judgment seat. The Greeks then seize Sostenes, the new chief ruler of the synagogue, and beat him in front of the court. 'And Gallio cared for none of those things.' But his decision means that, for now, Rome will not stop the preaching of the gospel.

After staying a good while longer, Paul says goodbye to the believers, and sails for Syria, with Priscilla and Aquila. He leaves them at Ephesus, a great city across the sea, after reasoning in the synagogue there. They ask him to stay longer, but he says, I will return again unto you, if God will. He sails to Caesarea, greets the church in Jerusalem, and goes down to Antioch. After spending some time there, he sets out again, travelling through the regions of Galatia and Phrygia, strengthening all the disciples.

Meanwhile, a Jewish man named Apollos comes to Ephesus. He was born in Alexandria in Egypt, a great centre of learning. He is an eloquent man, mighty in the Scriptures. He has been taught the way of the Lord, and he speaks fervently, teaching accurately about Jesus. But he knows only the baptism of John. He has not yet heard the whole story.

When Apollos wants to cross over to Achaia, the believers write to the disciples there, encouraging them to welcome him. When he arrives, he is a great help to those who have believed through grace. 'For he mightily convinced the Jews, and that publickly, shewing by the scriptures that Jesus was Christ.'

KEY 18:9: 'Then spake the Lord to Paul in the night by a vision, Be not afraid, but speak, and hold not thy peace:' Even the great apostle Paul was sometimes afraid. Jesus did not scold him for it. He spoke to him gently, and told him to keep going. Hold not thy peace means do not go silent. If you have ever felt afraid to speak about Jesus, you are in good company. And the same Lord speaks to you: be not afraid, but speak.

KEY 18:10: 'For I am with thee, and no man shall set on thee to hurt thee: for I have much people in this city.' Here are the reasons Paul need not be afraid. First, I am with thee. That is the promise Jesus gave all his disciples. Second, no one would harm him there. And third, God had much people in that city, people who did not yet know Jesus, but who belonged to him. God saw the believers of Corinth before they believed.

KEY 18:26: 'And he began to speak boldly in the synagogue: whom when Aquila and Priscilla had heard, they took him unto them, and expounded unto him the way of God more perfectly.' Apollos was brilliant and bold, but he did not know everything. Aquila and Priscilla did not correct him in public, or embarrass him. They took him aside, into their home, and explained the way of God more fully. Expounded means explained. And Apollos was humble enough to learn from two tentmakers. That is a picture of how God’s family should help each other grow.

THREAD: Jesus’ words to Paul, I am with thee, echo through the whole Bible. God told Joshua, 'Be strong and of a good courage; be not afraid, neither be thou dismayed: for the LORD thy God is with thee whithersoever thou goest.' And Jesus promised all his disciples, 'lo, I am with you alway, even unto the end of the world.'

TODAY: Is fear keeping you quiet about Jesus? Hear his words to Paul: be not afraid, I am with thee. And think about Aquila and Priscilla. Is there someone you could gently help to understand God’s way more fully, or someone you could humbly learn from?

QUESTION: The Lord told Paul, be not afraid, but speak, for I am with thee. Where is God asking you to speak up for him?

PRAYER: Lord Jesus, thank you that you are with me, and that you see people who will belong to you before they know you. When I am afraid, help me not to go silent. Make me humble enough to learn, and gentle enough to teach, like Priscilla and Aquila. Amen.
