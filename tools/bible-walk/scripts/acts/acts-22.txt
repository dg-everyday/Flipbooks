# Acts 22: Paul's defence on the stairs, his conversion retold, the word Gentiles, the Roman citizen. Keys 22:10, 22:15, 22:16.
TITLE: Thou Shalt Be His Witness unto All Men

HOOK: Paul stands at the top of a stone staircase, his body bruised from a beating, chains on his wrists, and Roman soldiers all around him. Below him is a crowd that was trying to kill him only minutes ago. He lifts his hand. And when they hear him speak in their own Hebrew language, the crowd falls even more silent. Paul has one chance to speak. So he tells them his own story.

WHERE: In chapter twenty-one, Paul arrived in Jerusalem, despite the tears and warnings of his friends, who finally said, the will of the Lord be done. In the temple, a mob seized him and tried to kill him, and Roman soldiers rescued him and carried him up the steps of the fortress. Paul asked permission to speak to the people.

Now, in chapter twenty-two, Paul gives his defence. It is the second time Acts tells the story of Paul meeting Jesus on the road to Damascus. This time, we hear it in Paul’s own words.

STORY: 'Men, brethren, and fathers, hear ye my defence which I make now unto you.' When they hear him speak in Hebrew, they become even quieter.

I am a Jew, Paul says, born in Tarsus in Cilicia, but brought up in this city, at the feet of Gamaliel, taught strictly according to the law of our fathers. I was zealous toward God, just as all of you are today. I persecuted this Way to the death, binding and putting into prison both men and women. The high priest and all the elders can tell you so. I even received letters from them to the brothers in Damascus, and I went there to bring those who believed back to Jerusalem in chains, to be punished.

As I was on my way, and came near to Damascus, suddenly a great light from heaven shone around me. I fell to the ground, and heard a voice say to me, Saul, Saul, why persecutest thou me? I answered, who art thou, Lord? And he said to me, I am Jesus of Nazareth, whom thou persecutest. The men with me saw the light and were afraid, but they did not understand the voice of the one speaking to me.

Paul tells them what he asked, and what the Lord answered. Because of the brightness of that light, I could not see, and those with me led me by the hand into Damascus.

There was a man named Ananias, Paul says, a devout man according to the law, well spoken of by all the Jews who lived there. He came to me, stood by me, and said, Brother Saul, receive thy sight. And in that same hour I looked up at him. Then he said, the God of our fathers has chosen you, that you should know his will, and see that Just One, and hear the voice of his mouth. And he told Paul what that meant for him.

Later, when I came back to Jerusalem, Paul says, while I was praying in the temple, I fell into a trance, and saw the Lord saying to me, hurry, get out of Jerusalem quickly, for they will not receive your testimony about me. I said, Lord, they know that I imprisoned and beat those who believed in you, in every synagogue. And when the blood of your witness Stephen was shed, I was standing by, agreeing, and guarding the clothes of those who killed him. And the Lord said to me, 'Depart: for I will send thee far hence unto the Gentiles.'

The crowd has listened up to this word. But at the word Gentiles, they lift up their voices and shout, away with such a fellow from the earth, for it is not fit that he should live! They cry out, throw off their clothes, and fling dust into the air.

The commander orders Paul brought into the fortress, and examined by scourging, a brutal whipping, to find out why the crowd is shouting against him. As they tie him up with straps, Paul says to the centurion standing by, 'Is it lawful for you to scourge a man that is a Roman, and uncondemned?'

The centurion goes straight to the commander. Be careful what you do, he says. This man is a Roman. The commander comes and asks Paul, tell me, are you a Roman? Paul says, yes. The commander says, I bought this citizenship for a great sum of money. And Paul answers, 'But I was free born.' Those who were about to examine him step back at once. And the commander is afraid, because he has bound a Roman citizen.

The next day, wanting to know the real reason for the accusations, the commander releases him from his chains, and orders the chief priests and all their council to meet. And he brings Paul down, and sets him before them.

KEY 22:10: 'And I said, What shall I do, Lord? And the Lord said unto me, Arise, and go into Damascus; and there it shall be told thee of all things which are appointed for thee to do.' The first thing Paul said after meeting Jesus was, what shall I do, Lord? Not, why me, or what do I get, but, what do you want me to do? That is the question of a heart that has surrendered. And notice that Jesus did not tell him everything at once. Just the next step: go into Damascus. God often leads us one step at a time.

KEY 22:15: 'For thou shalt be his witness unto all men of what thou hast seen and heard.' A witness tells what he has seen and heard. Paul’s greatest argument was not a clever debate. It was his own story: what I was, what happened to me, and what Jesus has done. Every believer has a story like that. You may not have seen a light from heaven. But if you know Jesus, you have something to tell.

KEY 22:16: 'And now why tarriest thou? arise, and be baptized, and wash away thy sins, calling on the name of the Lord.' To tarry means to wait or delay. Ananias did not let Saul sit and wait. Arise. Do not put it off. Be baptized, and wash away your sins, calling on the name of the Lord. Notice the last words. It is the Lord who washes our sins away, when we call on his name.

THREAD: Paul was set apart to be a witness to all men. Long before, God told the prophet Jeremiah, 'Before I formed thee in the belly I knew thee; and before thou camest forth out of the womb I sanctified thee'. Paul later wrote that God had set him apart from his mother’s womb, and called him by his grace.

TODAY: Think about your own story with God. What were you like before you knew him? How did he find you? What has he done since? Practise telling it simply. And if there is something God has been asking you to do that you keep putting off, hear the words of Ananias: why tarriest thou?

QUESTION: Paul asked, What shall I do, Lord? What is the next step God is asking you to take today?

PRAYER: Lord Jesus, what shall I do? I give my life to you. Thank you that you met Paul on the road, and that you have met me too. Help me not to delay in obeying you, and make me your witness to all people of what I have seen and heard. Amen.
