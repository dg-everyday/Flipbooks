# Acts 23: Paul before the council, Pharisees and Sadducees, the Lord stands by Paul, the plot, Paul sent to Felix. Keys 23:1, 23:6, 23:11.
TITLE: Be of Good Cheer, Paul

HOOK: More than forty men have sworn a solemn oath. They will not eat or drink a single thing until they have killed Paul. They have a plan, and the chief priests have agreed to help. Paul is locked in a Roman fortress, and has no idea. But one young man has overheard everything. He is Paul’s nephew, the son of Paul’s sister. And he goes straight to the fortress.

WHERE: In chapter twenty-two, Paul stood on the steps of the fortress and told the angry crowd how Jesus met him on the road to Damascus, and sent him to the Gentiles. At that word, the crowd erupted. The Romans were about to whip him, until Paul said that he was a Roman citizen. The commander then brought him before the Jewish council, to find out what the charges really were.

Now, in chapter twenty-three, Paul stands before the same council that condemned Jesus, and Stephen. He is in real danger. Watch for the moment, in the middle of this chapter, when the Lord himself comes to him.

STORY: Paul looks steadily at the council. Then the high priest Ananias commands those standing near Paul to strike him on the mouth. Paul says to him, God shall smite thee, thou whited wall. Whited wall means a wall painted white to hide what is underneath, a hypocrite. Do you sit to judge me by the law, and command me to be struck against the law?

Those standing by say, do you insult God’s high priest? Paul answers, 'I wist not, brethren, that he was the high priest: for it is written, Thou shalt not speak evil of the ruler of thy people.' I wist not means I did not know. Paul quickly shows respect for the office, even if not for the man.

Then Paul notices that part of the council are Sadducees, and part are Pharisees. The Sadducees say there is no resurrection, no angel, and no spirit. The Pharisees believe in all of these. So Paul cries out in the council.

At once a quarrel breaks out between the Pharisees and the Sadducees, and the council is divided. There is a great uproar. Some of the scribes who belong to the Pharisees stand up and argue, we find no evil in this man. If a spirit or an angel has spoken to him, let us not fight against God. The quarrel becomes so violent that the commander fears Paul will be torn to pieces. He orders the soldiers to go down, take him by force from among them, and bring him back into the fortress.

And then the Lord comes to Paul.

The next day, some of the Jews band together, and bind themselves under a curse, saying they will neither eat nor drink until they have killed Paul. There are more than forty of them. They go to the chief priests and elders and say, ask the commander to bring Paul down to you again, as though you wanted to examine him more closely. We will be ready to kill him before he ever gets near.

But Paul’s sister’s son hears of the ambush. He goes into the fortress and tells Paul. Paul calls one of the centurions and says, take this young man to the commander; he has something to tell him. The commander takes the young man by the hand, leads him aside privately, and asks, what is it that you have to tell me? The young man tells him everything. Do not let them persuade you, he says. More than forty men are waiting for him, and they are ready now.

The commander sends the young man away, telling him, tell no one that you have told me these things. Then he calls two centurions, and orders them to make ready two hundred soldiers, seventy horsemen and two hundred spearmen, to set out under cover of darkness, and take Paul safely to Felix the governor at Caesarea.

He writes a letter to Felix. Claudius Lysias, to the most excellent governor Felix, greeting. This man was seized by the Jews, and would have been killed, when I came with soldiers and rescued him, having learned that he was a Roman. I found he was accused about questions of their law, but nothing worthy of death or chains. When I was told of a plot against him, I sent him at once to you.

The soldiers take Paul, as they were commanded, and bring him by the road to Antipatris. The next day the horsemen go on with him to Caesarea, deliver the letter to the governor, and present Paul to him. Felix reads the letter, asks what province Paul is from, and says, I will hear you when your accusers arrive. And he orders Paul to be kept in Herod’s palace.

KEY 23:1: 'And Paul, earnestly beholding the council, said, Men and brethren, I have lived in all good conscience before God until this day.' Your conscience is the inner voice that tells you whether you have done right or wrong. Paul could stand before his accusers and look them in the eye, because he had nothing to hide. He had once persecuted the church, but he had done it believing he served God, and since meeting Jesus he had lived to please him. A clear conscience gives courage.

KEY 23:6: 'But when Paul perceived that the one part were Sadducees, and the other Pharisees, he cried out in the council, Men and brethren, I am a Pharisee, the son of a Pharisee: of the hope and resurrection of the dead I am called in question.' Paul was wise as well as bold. But he was also telling the truth. The real issue was the resurrection. Paul preached that Jesus had risen from the dead, and that everyone who trusts him will rise too. That hope was at the heart of everything he did.

KEY 23:11: 'And the night following the Lord stood by him, and said, Be of good cheer, Paul: for as thou hast testified of me in Jerusalem, so must thou bear witness also at Rome.' Paul was alone in a fortress, beaten, accused, and surrounded by enemies. And the Lord stood by him. Not far off, but right beside him. Be of good cheer means take heart, be encouraged. And Jesus gave him a promise: you will bear witness in Rome. Paul’s enemies had their plans. But the Lord had his. And no plot of forty men could change that.

THREAD: A psalm says, 'He that keepeth thee will not slumber.' The forty men swore an oath, but the Lord used a young man’s ears and a Roman commander’s soldiers to keep his servant safe. Long before, Joseph told his brothers, 'ye thought evil against me; but God meant it unto good'.

TODAY: Are you feeling alone, or surrounded by trouble? Remember that the Lord stood by Paul, and he stands by you. Hear his words: be of good cheer. And notice Paul’s nephew. One young person, telling the truth at the right moment, saved a life. God can use you too.

QUESTION: The Lord stood by Paul and said, be of good cheer. Where do you most need to know that the Lord is standing beside you today?

PRAYER: Lord Jesus, thank you that you stand by your people, even in the darkest places. Help me to live with a clear conscience before you. Fill me with the hope of the resurrection, and when I am afraid, let me hear your voice: be of good cheer. Amen.
