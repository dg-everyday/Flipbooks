# Acts 24: Tertullus accuses Paul, Paul's defence before Felix, Felix trembles, Paul left in prison. Keys 24:14, 24:16, 24:25.
TITLE: A Conscience Void of Offence

HOOK: A Roman governor and his wife sit listening to a prisoner. They have sent for him because they are curious about this new faith in Christ. But as the prisoner speaks about doing what is right, about self-control, and about the judgment to come, the governor begins to tremble. Then he stops him. That is enough for now, he says. When I have a convenient time, I will call for you.

WHERE: In chapter twenty-three, Paul stood before the council in Jerusalem, and the Lord stood by him and said, be of good cheer, you must bear witness also at Rome. Paul’s nephew uncovered a plot by more than forty men to kill him, and the commander sent Paul under heavy guard to Felix, the governor, at Caesarea.

Now, in chapter twenty-four, Paul stands trial before Felix. This is the first of several trials in the last part of Acts. Watch how Paul defends himself, and how he turns his trial into a chance to speak about Jesus.

STORY: After five days, the high priest Ananias comes down to Caesarea with the elders, and with a speaker named Tertullus, a lawyer, who lays out their case against Paul before the governor.

Tertullus begins by flattering Felix. Seeing that by you we enjoy great quietness, and that very worthy deeds are done for this nation by your care, we accept it always, most noble Felix, with all thankfulness. Then he makes his charges. We have found this man a pest, a troublemaker, who stirs up riots among all the Jews throughout the world, and a ringleader of the sect of the Nazarenes. He even tried to profane the temple, so we seized him. The Jews who came with him agree, saying these things are so.

Then the governor nods to Paul to speak. Paul does not flatter him. He simply says, because I know that you have been a judge of this nation for many years, I gladly answer for myself.

It is only twelve days, Paul says, since I went up to Jerusalem to worship. They did not find me arguing with anyone in the temple, or stirring up the people, in the synagogues or in the city. And they cannot prove the things they accuse me of now.

But I do admit one thing to you. And I have the same hope in God that they themselves have, that there shall be a resurrection of the dead, both of the just and the unjust.

After many years, Paul says, I came to bring gifts to my nation, and offerings. While I was doing this, they found me purified in the temple, with no crowd and no uproar. It was some Jews from Asia who caused the trouble, and they ought to be here, if they have anything against me. Or let these men here say what wrong they found in me when I stood before the council, unless it is this one thing I cried out: 'Touching the resurrection of the dead I am called in question by you this day.'

Felix knows a good deal about the Way. So he puts them off, saying, when Lysias the commander comes down, I will decide your case. He orders a centurion to keep Paul in custody, but to give him some freedom, and to let his friends visit and care for him.

Some days later, Felix comes with his wife Drusilla, who is Jewish, and sends for Paul, and hears him speak about faith in Christ. Paul speaks to them plainly. And Felix trembles.

At the same time, Felix hopes that Paul will give him money to set him free. So he sends for Paul often, and talks with him. But he never decides the case. A long time passes, and Felix is replaced as governor by Porcius Festus. And wanting to do the Jewish leaders a favour, Felix leaves Paul in chains.

KEY 24:14: 'But this I confess unto thee, that after the way which they call heresy, so worship I the God of my fathers, believing all things which are written in the law and in the prophets:' Paul’s accusers called the followers of Jesus a sect, a breakaway group. Paul said no. He worshipped the same God as Abraham, Isaac and Jacob, and believed everything written in the Scriptures. Following Jesus was not leaving the faith of Israel. It was its fulfilment. The whole Old Testament points to Jesus.

KEY 24:16: 'And herein do I exercise myself, to have always a conscience void of offence toward God, and toward men.' Void of offence means clear, without guilt. Notice the word exercise. Paul worked at keeping a clear conscience, like an athlete training every day. And notice the two directions: toward God, and toward men. Our relationship with God and our relationships with people belong together. When we do wrong to either, we put it right quickly.

KEY 24:25: 'And as he reasoned of righteousness, temperance, and judgment to come Felix trembled, and answered, Go thy way for this time; when I have a convenient season, I will call for thee.' Righteousness means doing what is right. Temperance means self-control. Felix was a corrupt man, and Drusilla had left her first husband to marry him. Paul spoke to them about exactly what they needed to hear. Felix trembled, so he knew it was true. But he said, later, at a convenient season. As far as we know, that convenient season never came.

THREAD: Felix put off his answer. But the Bible warns against delay when God speaks. A psalm says, 'To day if ye will hear his voice, Harden not your heart'. And Paul himself wrote to the Corinthians, 'behold, now is the accepted time; behold, now is the day of salvation.'

TODAY: Is there anything God has been speaking to you about, that you keep putting off until a convenient time? Do not be like Felix. Answer him now. And take a moment to check your conscience. Is there anything to put right with God, or with someone else?

QUESTION: Felix said, when I have a convenient season, I will call for thee. What have you been putting off that God is asking you to deal with today?

PRAYER: Lord God, I do not want to put you off until a convenient time. Speak to me, and help me to answer you now. Search my heart, and help me to keep a clear conscience toward you and toward others. Thank you that all your Word points to Jesus. Amen.
