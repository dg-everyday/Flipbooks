# Acts 28: Malta and the viper, healing Publius' father, the road to Rome, Paul preaches in Rome. Keys 28:5, 28:15, 28:31.
TITLE: With All Confidence, No Man Forbidding Him

HOOK: Soaked, cold and exhausted, the shipwrecked travellers huddle on a beach as rain pours down. The people of the island have kindly built them a fire. Paul gathers a bundle of sticks and lays them on it. Out of the heat comes a snake, a viper, and it fastens itself onto his hand. The islanders gasp. They watch him closely, waiting for him to swell up, or suddenly fall down dead.

WHERE: In chapter twenty-seven, Paul sailed for Rome as a prisoner. A great storm raged for many days, and everyone gave up hope. But an angel stood by Paul and promised that no one would be lost. Paul said, I believe God. The ship was wrecked, and every one of the two hundred and seventy-six people on board reached land safely, some on boards and some on broken pieces of the ship.

Now we come to the last chapter of Acts. Paul has one more step to take: to Rome itself, as the Lord promised. And at the end, Luke leaves the story open.

STORY: Once they are safe, they learn that the island is called Melita, which we call Malta. The islanders show them unusual kindness. They build a fire and welcome everyone, because of the rain and the cold.

When the viper fastens onto Paul’s hand, the islanders say to each other, surely this man is a murderer. He escaped the sea, but justice will not let him live. But Paul shakes the creature off into the fire, and suffers no harm. They wait a long time for him to swell up or fall dead. When nothing happens, they change their minds, and say he is a god.

Nearby are the lands of the chief man of the island, named Publius, who welcomes them and looks after them kindly for three days. Publius’ father lies sick with a fever and a bloody flux, dysentery. Paul goes in to him, prays, lays his hands on him, and heals him. When this is done, the others on the island who have diseases come, and are healed too. They honour Paul and his companions with many gifts, and when they set sail, they give them everything they need.

After three months, they set sail in another ship from Alexandria, which has spent the winter at the island. They sail to Syracuse and Rhegium, and land at Puteoli in Italy. There they find believers, who invite them to stay for seven days. And then they set out for Rome.

The believers in Rome hear that Paul is coming, and they travel out to meet him, as far as Appii Forum and the Three Taverns, many miles from the city.

When they come to Rome, Paul is allowed to live by himself, with a soldier who guards him.

After three days, Paul calls together the leaders of the Jews in Rome. Brothers, he says, though I have done nothing against our people or the customs of our fathers, I was handed over as a prisoner from Jerusalem to the Romans. They examined me, and wanted to let me go, because there was no reason to put me to death. But when the Jewish leaders objected, I was forced to appeal to Caesar, not that I have any accusation against my own nation. 'because that for the hope of Israel I am bound with this chain.'

They answer, we have received no letters from Judaea about you, and none of the brothers who have come have said anything bad about you. But we want to hear what you think, for we know that everywhere people speak against this sect.

So they set a day, and many come to his lodging. All day long, Paul explains and testifies about the kingdom of God, persuading them about Jesus, from the law of Moses and from the prophets. Some believe what he says, and some do not.

As they leave, still disagreeing among themselves, Paul speaks one last word to them, from the prophet Isaiah. Go to this people, and say, hearing you shall hear, and shall not understand, and seeing you shall see, and not perceive. For the heart of this people has grown dull. 'Be it known therefore unto you, that the salvation of God is sent unto the Gentiles, and that they will hear it.'

Paul stays a long time in his own rented house, and welcomes all who come to him. And the last words of the book of Acts are these.

KEY 28:5: 'And he shook off the beast into the fire, and felt no harm.' The islanders first thought Paul was a murderer, then a god. He was neither. He was a servant of the living God, who had promised he would stand before Caesar. A storm could not stop that promise, and neither could a snake. Paul simply shook it off and went on. Sometimes we too need to shake off whatever has fastened on us, fear, or worry, or other people’s judgments, and keep going in God’s strength.

KEY 28:15: 'And from thence, when the brethren heard of us, they came to meet us as far as Appii Forum, and The three taverns: whom when Paul saw, he thanked God, and took courage.' Paul was a great apostle, but he was also a tired prisoner who had survived a shipwreck. When he saw these believers who had walked a long way to meet him, he thanked God and took courage. Never underestimate what it means to show up for someone. Your presence might be exactly the encouragement they need.

KEY 28:31: 'Preaching the kingdom of God, and teaching those things which concern the Lord Jesus Christ, with all confidence, no man forbidding him.' These are the last words of Acts. Paul is still a prisoner, still in chains, still waiting for trial. But the word of God is not chained. He preaches with all confidence, no man forbidding him. The book ends with the gospel going out freely in the capital of the world. And the story does not really end. It goes on, through every believer, to this very day.

THREAD: In chapter one, Jesus said, ye shall be witnesses unto me in Jerusalem, and in all Judaea, and in Samaria, and unto the uttermost part of the earth. Now the good news has reached Rome, the centre of the known world. And Isaiah’s words have come true: 'all the ends of the earth shall see the salvation of our God.'

TODAY: The book of Acts is unfinished, because you are part of the story. The same Holy Ghost who filled the apostles is given to every believer. Ask God where he wants you to be his witness today. And think of someone you could encourage, as the believers from Rome encouraged Paul.

QUESTION: Now that you have walked through the whole book of Acts, how will you carry the story on as Jesus’ witness, and who will you tell?

PRAYER: Lord Jesus, thank you that your word cannot be chained, and that your good news has reached the ends of the earth, and reached me. Fill me with your Holy Spirit, give me courage, and help me to speak of you with all confidence, wherever you send me. Amen.

NEXT: That is the end of our walk through the book of Acts. Well walked. The story of Acts is still being written, through everyone who follows Jesus. And Paul’s letters, written to many of the churches we have met on this walk, carry his voice on to us.
