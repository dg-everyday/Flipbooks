# 1 Kings 18 in The Big Picture: Elijah and the prophets of Baal on Mount Carmel. Keys 18:21, 18:37, 18:39.
TITLE: The LORD, He Is the God

HOOK: Hundreds of prophets are dancing around an altar on a mountaintop, shouting the name of their god. For hours they cry out. They leap. They cut themselves until the blood runs. And nothing happens. 'there was no voice, nor any that answered.' Then one lonely prophet steps forward, rebuilds a broken altar, soaks it with water, and prays one short prayer.

WHERE: In Proverbs chapter three, Solomon taught his son to trust in the LORD with all his heart. But in his old age Solomon’s own heart turned to the gods of his many foreign wives. After he died, the kingdom split in two. The ten tribes in the north were called Israel, and they had their own kings. The south, with Jerusalem and the temple, was called Judah, ruled by David’s family.

The kings of the north led the people further and further from God. The worst was King Ahab. He married Jezebel, a foreign princess who worshipped Baal, a god people believed brought rain and harvests. Ahab built a temple for Baal, and Jezebel killed the prophets of the LORD.

So God sent the prophet Elijah to Ahab with a message: there would be no rain, 'but according to my word.' For three years there was no rain, and a terrible famine came on the land. Baal, the so-called god of rain, could do nothing.

In The Big Picture, this is the kingdom turning away, and God refusing to let his people go without a fight.

STORY: In the third year, God says to Elijah, go and show yourself to Ahab, and I will send rain. When Ahab sees Elijah, he says, 'Art thou he that troubleth Israel?' Elijah answers that the trouble is not his. It is Ahab who has forsaken the commandments of the LORD and followed Baal.

So Elijah issues a challenge. Gather all Israel to Mount Carmel, along with four hundred and fifty prophets of Baal and four hundred more of the false goddess. When the people are gathered, Elijah speaks to them, and they answer him not a word.

Elijah sets out the test. Let the prophets of Baal take a bull and lay it on wood, with no fire under it. I will do the same. 'and the God that answereth by fire, let him be God.' And the people say, it is well spoken.

The prophets of Baal go first. They call on Baal for hours: 'O Baal, hear us.' They leap on the altar. Nothing. At midday, Elijah mocks them. 'Cry aloud: for he is a god; either he is talking, or he is pursuing, or he is in a journey, or peradventure he sleepeth, and must be awaked.' They cry louder, and cut themselves with knives, as was their custom. They rave on for hours more. But there is no voice, and no answer.

Then Elijah calls the people near. He repairs the broken altar of the LORD with twelve stones, one for each tribe of Israel. He digs a trench around it, lays out the wood and the bull. Then he tells them to fill four barrels of water and pour it over the sacrifice and the wood. Do it again, he says. And a third time. The water runs round about the altar, and fills the trench.

Then Elijah steps forward and prays. 'LORD God of Abraham, Isaac, and of Israel, let it be known this day that thou art God in Israel, and that I am thy servant'.

And the fire of the LORD falls. It burns up the sacrifice, the wood, the stones, the dust, and licks up the water in the trench. When the people see it, they fall on their faces. The prophets of Baal are seized, and put to death.

Then Elijah tells Ahab, go and eat, for there is a sound of abundance of rain. Elijah climbs to the top of Carmel, bows down with his face between his knees, and prays. Seven times he sends his servant to look out to sea. The seventh time the servant says, 'there ariseth a little cloud out of the sea, like a man’s hand.' Soon the sky is black with clouds and wind, and there is a great rain. The drought is over.

KEY 18:21: 'And Elijah came unto all the people, and said, How long halt ye between two opinions? if the LORD be God, follow him: but if Baal, then follow him. And the people answered him not a word.' To halt means to limp. The people were limping between two choices, trying to keep one foot with God and one with Baal. Elijah says you cannot do both. Choose. Their silence says everything. Many people still try to keep God and something else on the throne of their hearts at the same time.

KEY 18:37: 'Hear me, O LORD, hear me, that this people may know that thou art the LORD God, and that thou hast turned their heart back again.' Elijah’s prayer was short. There was no shouting and no show. And look at what he asked for. Not his own glory, but that the people would know God, and that their hearts would turn back. The goal of the fire was not destruction, but a people coming home to God.

KEY 18:39: 'And when all the people saw it, they fell on their faces: and they said, The LORD, he is the God; the LORD, he is the God.' The question Elijah asked was answered. The people who could not say a word now cannot stop saying it. The LORD is God. Not Baal, not any other power, not anything we can make or buy. Only he answers by fire.

THREAD: The New Testament says, 'Elias was a man subject to like passions as we are, and he prayed earnestly that it might not rain'. Elias is the Greek form of Elijah. He was an ordinary person, with the same feelings and weaknesses as us. And yet God answered his prayers. The prophet Malachi later promised that God would send Elijah before the great day of the LORD, and Jesus said that promise was fulfilled in John the Baptist, who prepared the way for him.

TODAY: Are you limping between two opinions? Is there something you are trying to hold on to alongside God? Make the choice today. And pray, even if your prayers feel small. The God who answered Elijah still hears.

QUESTION: Elijah asked, how long halt ye between two opinions? What is competing with God for first place in your heart, that you will give up today?

PRAYER: LORD God of Abraham, Isaac and Israel, you alone are God. Forgive me for limping between you and other things. Turn my heart back to you. Thank you that you hear the prayers of ordinary people. The LORD, he is the God. Amen.
