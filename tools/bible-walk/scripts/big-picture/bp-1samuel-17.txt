# 1 Samuel 17 in The Big Picture: David and Goliath. Keys 17:37, 17:45, 17:47.
TITLE: The Battle Is the LORD’s

HOOK: Two armies face each other across a valley. Every day, a giant walks out from one side, taller than any man, covered in bronze armour, with a spear like a weaver’s beam. And every day he shouts the same challenge across the valley: send me a man to fight. And every day the soldiers of Israel run back to their tents in fear. Until a shepherd boy arrives with bread and cheese for his brothers.

WHERE: In Ruth chapter one, a woman from Moab chose to follow the God of Israel, and she became the great-grandmother of David. After the time of the judges, the people of Israel asked God for a king, so that they could be like the other nations. God gave them Saul, who was tall and handsome. But Saul did not obey God, and God rejected him as king.

So God sent the prophet Samuel to Bethlehem, to the house of Jesse, to anoint a new king. Jesse’s older sons were strong and impressive, but God chose none of them. He told Samuel, 'for man looketh on the outward appearance, but the LORD looketh on the heart.' The one God chose was the youngest son, David, out in the fields keeping the sheep. Samuel anointed him with oil, and the Spirit of the LORD came upon him. But Saul was still on the throne, and David went back to his sheep.

In The Big Picture, this chapter shows the kind of king God was looking for: not the tallest or the strongest, but one who trusted the LORD.

STORY: The Philistines, Israel’s great enemy, gather their army on one side of the valley of Elah, and Saul and the army of Israel gather on the other. Out from the Philistine camp walks Goliath of Gath, a champion more than nine feet tall. He wears a helmet of brass and a coat of armour that weighs as much as a grown man. He cries out, 'I defy the armies of Israel this day; give me a man, that we may fight together.' Saul and all Israel are dismayed and greatly afraid. And Goliath comes out and shouts his challenge for forty days.

Jesse sends young David to the camp with food for his three oldest brothers. While he is there, Goliath comes out again, and David hears him. And David is shocked, not at the size of the giant, but at the soldiers’ fear. 'for who is this uncircumcised Philistine, that he should defy the armies of the living God?' Uncircumcised here means outside God’s covenant. David sees this as an insult to God himself.

His oldest brother Eliab is angry with him, and accuses him of pride. But David’s words reach King Saul, and Saul sends for him. David says, 'Let no man’s heart fail because of him; thy servant will go and fight with this Philistine.' Saul says, you are only a youth, and he has been a warrior since his youth.

David answers. When I kept my father’s sheep, a lion came, and a bear, and took a lamb from the flock. I went after them, and struck them, and rescued the lamb. Your servant killed both the lion and the bear. This Philistine will be like one of them, because he has defied the armies of the living God.

Saul puts his own armour on David, his helmet and his coat of mail and his sword. But David cannot even walk in it. He has never worn armour. So he takes it off. He takes his shepherd’s staff, chooses five smooth stones from the brook, puts them in his shepherd’s bag, takes his sling in his hand, and walks out to meet the giant.

Goliath sees a boy, ruddy and young, and he despises him. 'Am I a dog, that thou comest to me with staves?' He curses David by his gods, and says he will give his flesh to the birds. And David answers him.

Then Goliath comes toward him, and David runs toward the giant. He puts his hand in his bag, takes out a stone, and slings it. The stone sinks into Goliath’s forehead, and he falls on his face to the ground. David had no sword in his hand. He takes the giant’s own sword to finish the battle. When the Philistines see their champion dead, they flee, and the army of Israel chases them.

KEY 17:37: 'David said moreover, The LORD that delivered me out of the paw of the lion, and out of the paw of the bear, he will deliver me out of the hand of this Philistine. And Saul said unto David, Go, and the LORD be with thee.' David’s courage did not come from nowhere. It came from remembering what God had already done for him, alone in the fields, when no one was watching. The faith we build in small, hidden places is the faith that stands up in the big moments.

KEY 17:45: 'Then said David to the Philistine, Thou comest to me with a sword, and with a spear, and with a shield: but I come to thee in the name of the LORD of hosts, the God of the armies of Israel, whom thou hast defied.' Goliath trusted his weapons. David trusted a name. The LORD of hosts means the LORD of heaven’s armies. David was not brave because he thought he was strong. He was brave because he knew who was on his side.

KEY 17:47: 'And all this assembly shall know that the LORD saveth not with sword and spear: for the battle is the LORD’S, and he will give you into our hands.' This is the heart of the chapter. David wanted the whole world to know that victory comes from the LORD, not from size or weapons. We often think the question is, how big is my problem? David asked a different question: how big is my God?

THREAD: Long afterward, Paul wrote, 'But God hath chosen the foolish things of the world to confound the wise; and God hath chosen the weak things of the world to confound the things which are mighty'. And David’s victory points to a greater one. The whole army of Israel stood frozen with fear while one man went out alone to fight the enemy for them, and his victory became theirs. That is what David’s greater Son, Jesus, would do for his people against sin and death.

TODAY: What giant are you facing today? Before you look at its size, remember what God has already done for you, your own lion and bear. Then face it in the name of the LORD. The battle is his.

QUESTION: David remembered the lion and the bear before he faced the giant. What has God already done for you that will give you courage today?

PRAYER: LORD of hosts, you saved David, not with sword and spear, but by your own power. When I face giants, help me to remember what you have done, and to trust you more than I fear them. The battle is yours. Amen.
