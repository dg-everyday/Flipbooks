# 2 Samuel 7 in The Big Picture: God's covenant with David, a throne for ever. Keys 7:12, 7:16, 7:18.
TITLE: Thy Throne Shall Be Established for Ever

HOOK: A king is sitting in his new palace, made of fine cedar wood. His enemies are quiet, and his kingdom is at rest. And as he looks around his beautiful house, something bothers him. He lives in a palace, but the ark of God, the sign of God’s presence, is still kept in a tent. So the king decides to build God a house. And then God says, no, David. I am going to build you a house.

WHERE: In First Samuel chapter seventeen, young David defeated Goliath. Later, King Saul grew jealous of David and tried to kill him, and David lived for years as a fugitive. When Saul died in battle, David became king, first over Judah, and then over all Israel. He captured the city of Jerusalem and made it his capital. Then he brought the ark of God into the city, dancing before the LORD with all his might.

Now, in Second Samuel chapter seven, God makes one of the greatest promises in the whole Bible. In The Big Picture, God promised Abraham a family that would bless the world. Here he promises David a King who will reign for ever.

STORY: David says to the prophet Nathan, 'See now, I dwell in an house of cedar, but the ark of God dwelleth within curtains.' Nathan thinks it is a fine idea. Go, he says, do all that is in your heart, for the LORD is with you.

But then the word of the LORD comes to Nathan. Go and tell my servant David: 'Shalt thou build me an house for me to dwell in?' Since the day I brought Israel out of Egypt, God says, I have moved about in a tent. Did I ever ask anyone to build me a house of cedar?

Then God reminds David of all he has done. I took you from the sheepfold, from following the sheep, to be ruler over my people. I was with you wherever you went. I cut off your enemies. I have made you a great name, like the names of the great men of the earth. And I will give my people a place of their own, where they will be planted and not moved again.

And then the great surprise: 'the LORD telleth thee that he will make thee an house.' David wanted to build God a house of wood and stone. God will build David a house of people: a family line, a dynasty of kings.

God says, when you die, I will raise up one of your sons after you, and I will establish his kingdom. He will build a house for my name. 'I will be his father, and he shall be my son.' If he does wrong, I will correct him. But my mercy will not depart from him, as I took it from Saul. And then the words that echo through the rest of the Bible: your house, your kingdom, and your throne will be established for ever.

Nathan tells David everything. And David goes in and sits before the LORD, and prays.

He is overwhelmed. Who am I? he asks. You have already done so much for me, and now you speak of my house for a great while to come. 'Wherefore thou art great, O LORD God: for there is none like thee, neither is there any God beside thee'. He remembers how God redeemed Israel from Egypt to be his own people. And he ends with simple faith. Lord, you have spoken it; do as you have said, and let the house of your servant be blessed for ever.

KEY 7:12: 'And when thy days be fulfilled, and thou shalt sleep with thy fathers, I will set up thy seed after thee, which shall proceed out of thy bowels, and I will establish his kingdom.' To sleep with thy fathers is a gentle way of saying to die. Out of thy bowels means from your own body, your own descendant. God promises that a son of David will reign. In the near future that meant Solomon, who would build the temple. But the promise reaches much further than Solomon.

KEY 7:16: 'And thine house and thy kingdom shall be established for ever before thee: thy throne shall be established for ever.' For ever. No human kingdom lasts for ever. David’s family line of kings would eventually fall, and the throne in Jerusalem would sit empty. So how could God keep this promise? The prophets would keep asking that question. The answer would be a King from David’s family who would himself live for ever.

KEY 7:18: 'Then went king David in, and sat before the LORD, and he said, Who am I, O Lord GOD? and what is my house, that thou hast brought me hitherto?' David does not boast. He sits humbly before God, amazed at his grace. The shepherd boy from Bethlehem knows that everything he has is a gift. This is the right response to God’s promises: not pride, but wonder and thankfulness. Who am I, that God should care for me like this?

THREAD: Long after David, an angel came to a young woman named Mary, engaged to a man of David’s family, and told her she would have a son named Jesus. And the angel said, 'and the Lord God shall give unto him the throne of his father David: And he shall reign over the house of Jacob for ever; and of his kingdom there shall be no end.' The promise to David was kept in Jesus.

TODAY: Like David, you may want to do something great for God. That is good. But first, sit before him and remember what he has already done for you. Our service never comes first. God’s grace always does. Then pray David’s prayer: do as you have said.

QUESTION: David asked, who am I, that thou hast brought me hitherto? What has God brought you through, that you will thank him for today?

PRAYER: Lord GOD, who am I, that you should care for me? Thank you for your promise to David, kept in Jesus, the King whose kingdom has no end. Help me to sit before you in wonder, and to trust you to do as you have said. Amen.
