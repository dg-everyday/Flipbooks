# Exodus 3 in The Big Picture: the burning bush and the name of God. Keys 3:5, 3:7, 3:14.
TITLE: I AM THAT I AM

HOOK: A shepherd is leading his sheep across the desert, as he has done every day for forty years. Then something catches his eye. On the side of the mountain, a bush is on fire. That happens in the desert. But this bush keeps burning, and burning, and it does not burn up. The shepherd says to himself, 'I will now turn aside, and see this great sight, why the bush is not burnt.'

WHERE: In Genesis chapter forty-five, Joseph forgave his brothers, and Jacob’s whole family came down to Egypt to escape the famine. They were only seventy people. But in Egypt they grew, and grew, until they filled the land. Then a new king arose who did not know Joseph. He feared them, and made them slaves. He set taskmasters over them and made their lives bitter with hard labour. Then he ordered that every Hebrew baby boy be thrown into the river Nile.

One mother hid her baby in a little basket among the reeds of the river. Pharaoh’s own daughter found him, and raised him in the palace as her son. She named him Moses. When Moses grew up, he saw an Egyptian beating a Hebrew slave, and he killed the Egyptian. He fled into the desert of Midian, married, and became a shepherd. And the end of Exodus chapter two says that God heard the groaning of his people, and remembered his covenant with Abraham, Isaac and Jacob.

In The Big Picture, this is where the rescue begins.

STORY: Moses leads the flock of his father-in-law to Horeb, the mountain of God. There the angel of the LORD appears to him in a flame of fire out of a bush. Moses turns aside to look. And God calls to him out of the middle of the bush: 'Moses, Moses.' And Moses answers, 'Here am I.'

God tells him to come no closer, and to take off his sandals, because he is standing on holy ground. 'I am the God of thy father, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob.' And Moses hides his face, because he is afraid to look upon God.

Then God tells Moses why he has come. I have seen my people’s suffering. I have heard their cry. I know their sorrows. And I am come down to deliver them out of the hand of the Egyptians, and to bring them to a good and large land, 'a land flowing with milk and honey'. Then God says, 'Come now therefore, and I will send thee unto Pharaoh'.

Moses is stunned. 'Who am I, that I should go unto Pharaoh'? He is an old shepherd who once ran away from Egypt. God does not tell Moses how great he is. He says, 'Certainly I will be with thee'.

Moses tries again. When I go to the people of Israel and tell them the God of their fathers has sent me, they will ask me, what is his name? What shall I say? And God tells him. Then God adds, this is my name for ever, and this is how I am to be remembered through all generations.

God tells Moses what will happen. Pharaoh will not let them go, not at first. But God will stretch out his hand and strike Egypt with his wonders, and after that Pharaoh will let them go. And they will not leave empty-handed.

KEY 3:5: 'And he said, Draw not nigh hither: put off thy shoes from off thy feet, for the place whereon thou standest is holy ground.' The ground was not holy because of the dirt. It was holy because God was there. Moses learned the first lesson anyone must learn about God: he is holy, completely pure, completely other than us. We do not stroll up to God casually. But notice that the holy God also came close, called Moses by name, and spoke with him. He is holy, and he draws near.

KEY 3:7: 'And the LORD said, I have surely seen the affliction of my people which are in Egypt, and have heard their cry by reason of their taskmasters; for I know their sorrows;' For generations the people had cried out, and it may have seemed that God was silent. But he had seen, he had heard, and he knew. God is never unaware of his people’s suffering. And when it is time, he comes down to rescue.

KEY 3:14: 'And God said unto Moses, I AM THAT I AM: and he said, Thus shalt thou say unto the children of Israel, I AM hath sent me unto you.' God’s name means that he simply is. He was not made. He does not change. He depends on nothing. Everything else exists because of him, but he exists because he is God. When you see the word LORD in capital letters in the King James Bible, it stands for this name. The God who is, the God who was always there, is the God who sends Moses, and he is the God who is with you.

THREAD: Long afterward, Jesus was arguing with religious leaders who said he could not be greater than Abraham. And Jesus said, 'Before Abraham was, I am.' They picked up stones to throw at him, because they understood exactly what he meant. The God who spoke from the bush had come to speak face to face.

TODAY: Like Moses, you may feel that you are not enough for what God is asking of you. You are not. Neither was Moses. But God’s answer is the same: certainly I will be with thee. And if you are waiting in a hard place, remember: God sees, God hears, and God knows your sorrows.

QUESTION: Moses asked, who am I? and God answered, I will be with thee. What task feels too big for you, that you will trust God to be with you in today?

PRAYER: LORD, the great I AM, you are holy, and yet you come near. Thank you that you see my sorrows and hear my cry. When I feel too small for what you ask, remind me that you are with me. Amen.
