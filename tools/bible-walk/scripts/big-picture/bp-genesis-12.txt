# Genesis 12 in The Big Picture: the call of Abram and the promise. Keys 12:1, 12:3, 12:4.
TITLE: Get Thee Out of Thy Country

HOOK: An old man stands at the door of his house, with everything he owns loaded onto animals. His family is around him. His neighbours are watching. He is seventy-five years old, and he is leaving for good. Someone asks him where he is going. And he has no answer, except this: God has told me to go, and he will show me where.

WHERE: In Genesis chapter six, the earth was filled with violence, and Noah found grace. After the flood, the human family grew again, and so did its pride. In chapter eleven, people built a city and a tower reaching to heaven, saying, 'let us make us a name'. God scattered them across the earth, and their languages were confused.

Now, in chapter twelve, God does something new. Instead of judging the whole world, he chooses one man and his family, and through them he will bless the whole world. In The Big Picture, this is the promise. Everything from here to the last page of the Bible grows out of it.

STORY: The man’s name is Abram. Later God will rename him Abraham. His family came from Ur of the Chaldees, and they had settled in a place called Haran. His wife is Sarai, and she has no children.

Then the LORD speaks to Abram. Leave your country, your relatives, and your father’s house, and go to a land that I will show you. And God makes him a promise with seven parts. I will make of thee a great nation. I will bless thee. I will make thy name great. Thou shalt be a blessing. I will bless them that bless thee, and curse him that curseth thee. And in thee shall all families of the earth be blessed.

Notice that promise. The people at Babel tried to make a name for themselves. Here God says, I will make your name great. What they grabbed for, God gives as a gift.

So Abram goes. He takes Sarai, his nephew Lot, all their possessions and the people of their household, and they travel to the land of Canaan. They come to a place called Sichem, near the great tree of Moreh. Genesis notes that 'the Canaanite was then in the land.' The land is not empty, and Abram owns none of it.

There the LORD appears to Abram and says, 'Unto thy seed will I give this land'. Seed means children and descendants. Abram has no children yet. But he builds an altar to the LORD, who appeared to him. He moves on toward Bethel, pitches his tent, builds another altar, and calls upon the name of the LORD. Wherever Abram goes, he leaves an altar behind.

Then the story takes a surprising turn. A famine comes, and Abram goes down into Egypt to find food. On the way he grows afraid. Sarai is beautiful, and he thinks the Egyptians will kill him to take her. So he tells her to say she is his sister. Pharaoh’s officials see her, and she is taken into Pharaoh’s house. But God sends great plagues on Pharaoh’s house, and Pharaoh calls Abram and asks, 'What is this that thou hast done unto me?' He sends Abram away, with his wife and all he has.

The man of great faith at the start of the chapter is a man of fear by the end of it. The Bible does not hide the failures of its heroes. And yet God keeps his promise, and protects Abram and Sarai, even when Abram fails.

KEY 12:1: 'Now the LORD had said unto Abram, Get thee out of thy country, and from thy kindred, and from thy father’s house, unto a land that I will shew thee:' God asked Abram to leave everything familiar, and he did not tell him where he was going, only that he would show him. Following God often works like that. He does not give us the whole map. He gives us the next step, and asks us to trust him for the rest.

KEY 12:3: 'And I will bless them that bless thee, and curse him that curseth thee: and in thee shall all families of the earth be blessed.' This is one of the most important promises in the whole Bible. God chose one family, but not only for that family. He chose them so that through them blessing would reach every family on earth. From the very beginning, God’s plan was for the whole world. Keep listening as we walk, and you will see this promise come true.

KEY 12:4: 'So Abram departed, as the LORD had spoken unto him; and Lot went with him: and Abram was seventy and five years old when he departed out of Haran.' Abram was not young. He had a settled life and a comfortable place. But when God spoke, he went. It is never too late to begin following God, and no one is too old to step out in faith.

THREAD: The New Testament looks back on this moment: 'By faith Abraham, when he was called to go out into a place which he should after receive for an inheritance, obeyed; and he went out, not knowing whither he went.' And Paul says the promise that all nations would be blessed in Abraham was the gospel preached ahead of time. It found its fulfilment in Jesus, Abraham’s descendant, through whom people from every nation are blessed.

TODAY: Is God asking you to take a step without knowing the whole way? Take the next step he has shown you. And remember that you, too, are meant to be a blessing to the people around you. Ask God to show you one person to bless today.

QUESTION: Abram went out not knowing where he was going, only trusting the God who called him. What next step is God asking you to take today?

PRAYER: Lord God, you called Abram and kept your promise to him, even when he failed. Give me faith to follow you one step at a time, even when I cannot see the whole road. Make me a blessing to the people around me. Amen.
