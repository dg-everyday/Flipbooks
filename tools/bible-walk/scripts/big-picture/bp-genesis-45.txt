# Genesis 45 in The Big Picture: Joseph makes himself known; God's purpose in suffering. Keys 45:4, 45:5, 45:8.
TITLE: God Did Send Me Before You

HOOK: Eleven brothers stand trembling before the governor of Egypt. He is the most powerful man in the land after Pharaoh, and he holds their lives in his hands. Suddenly he orders everyone else out of the room. And then he begins to weep, so loudly that people outside hear him. And through his tears he says two words in their own language that turn their blood cold: 'I am Joseph'.

WHERE: In Genesis chapter twenty-two, God provided a ram in place of Isaac. Isaac grew up and had a son named Jacob, whom God renamed Israel. Jacob had twelve sons, and he loved one of them, Joseph, more than the others. He gave Joseph a special coat, and Joseph dreamed that his brothers would one day bow down to him.

His brothers hated him for it. One day, far from home, they sold Joseph to traders for twenty pieces of silver, and told their father a wild animal had killed him. Joseph was taken to Egypt as a slave. He was falsely accused and thrown into prison. But God was with him. At last Joseph explained Pharaoh’s dreams of seven years of plenty and seven years of famine, and Pharaoh made him ruler over all Egypt.

When the famine came, Joseph’s brothers travelled to Egypt to buy grain, and bowed before a governor they did not recognise. Joseph tested them, to see if they had changed. And when the youngest, Benjamin, was about to be made a slave, his brother Judah offered himself in Benjamin’s place. They had changed. Now, in chapter forty-five, Joseph can hold back no longer.

STORY: Joseph cannot stand it any more. He sends every Egyptian out of the room, and weeps aloud. 'I am Joseph; doth my father yet live?' His brothers cannot answer. They are terrified at the sight of him. The brother they sold is now their judge.

But Joseph says, 'Come near to me, I pray you.' They come near. And he tells them again who he is. Then, instead of punishing them, he comforts them. Do not be grieved or angry with yourselves. God sent me here, to save lives. The famine has lasted two years, he says, and there are five more to come. God sent me ahead of you to keep your family alive.

Then Joseph tells them to hurry home to their father Jacob. Tell him, God has made me lord of all Egypt. Come down to me, and do not wait. You will live in the land of Goshen, near me, with your children and your flocks, and I will take care of you through the famine.

Joseph falls on his brother Benjamin’s neck and weeps, and Benjamin weeps on his. He kisses all his brothers and weeps over them. And after that, Genesis says, his brothers talked with him.

When Pharaoh hears that Joseph’s brothers have come, he is glad. He sends wagons from Egypt to bring Jacob and all the families down. Joseph gives his brothers new clothes and provisions for the journey, and to Benjamin he gives silver and five changes of clothes. As they leave, he gives them a very brotherly piece of advice: 'See that ye fall not out by the way.' Do not quarrel on the road.

When they tell their father that Joseph is alive and rules Egypt, Jacob’s heart grows faint. He cannot believe it. But when he sees the wagons Joseph has sent, his spirit revives. 'It is enough; Joseph my son is yet alive: I will go and see him before I die.'

KEY 45:4: 'And Joseph said unto his brethren, Come near to me, I pray you. And they came near. And he said, I am Joseph your brother, whom ye sold into Egypt.' Joseph does not pretend the past did not happen. He names it plainly: you sold me. Real forgiveness is not saying that a wrong was not wrong. It is naming the wrong honestly, and then choosing not to make the other person pay for it. And notice his first words: come near. Forgiveness draws people close.

KEY 45:5: 'Now therefore be not grieved, nor angry with yourselves, that ye sold me hither: for God did send me before you to preserve life.' Joseph’s brothers meant to get rid of him. But Joseph saw a bigger hand at work. God took the worst thing they had done and used it to save their lives, and the lives of many others. God does not cause sin. But he is so great that he can weave even sin and suffering into his good plan.

KEY 45:8: 'So now it was not you that sent me hither, but God: and he hath made me a father to Pharaoh, and lord of all his house, and a ruler throughout all the land of Egypt.' Joseph had every reason to be bitter. Years of slavery and prison, taken from his father and his home. But he looked at his life and saw God’s purpose running through it. This is what let him forgive. When we see God’s hand in our story, we can let go of the need to get even.

THREAD: Years later, after their father died, the brothers feared Joseph would finally take revenge. And Joseph said, 'ye thought evil against me; but God meant it unto good'. Long afterward, Paul wrote, 'And we know that all things work together for good to them that love God, to them who are the called according to his purpose.' And in the greatest evil ever done, the cross, God brought the greatest good: the salvation of the world. Through Joseph, Jacob’s family came to Egypt. There they would grow into a nation, and there the next part of the story begins.

TODAY: Is there someone you need to forgive? Name the wrong honestly before God, and then choose to let him handle it. And if you are in a hard place right now, remember Joseph in the prison. God is still at work, even when you cannot see it.

QUESTION: Joseph saw God’s hand even in the wrong done to him. Where in your own story do you need to trust that God means it for good?

PRAYER: Lord God, thank you that nothing in my life is outside your hand. Help me to forgive those who have wronged me, as Joseph did, and to trust that you can bring good out of what others meant for evil. Draw me near, and teach me to draw others near. Amen.
