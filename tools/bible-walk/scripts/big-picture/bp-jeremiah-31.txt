# Jeremiah 31 in The Big Picture: everlasting love and the new covenant. Keys 31:3, 31:33, 31:34.
TITLE: A New Covenant

HOOK: A prophet has spent his life telling people bad news they did not want to hear. He has been mocked, beaten, put in the stocks, and thrown into a muddy pit and left to die. He has watched his warnings come true and seen his city burn. And now, out of all that sorrow, God gives him the most hopeful message he has ever spoken: 'Yea, I have loved thee with an everlasting love'.

WHERE: In Second Kings chapter twenty-five, Jerusalem fell to Babylon, the temple was burned, and the people were carried away out of their land. The prophet Jeremiah had warned them for many years that this would happen. He is often called the weeping prophet, because he grieved so deeply over his people.

But in the middle of his book, Jeremiah writes four chapters full of hope. They are sometimes called the book of comfort. Chapter thirty-one is at their heart. In The Big Picture, God is looking past the exile to something new: a new covenant that will change people from the inside.

STORY: God begins by reminding his scattered people that his love has not ended. He calls them by a tender name, the virgin of Israel, and promises that she will be built again. Once more she will take up her tambourines and go out dancing. Once more they will plant vineyards on the hills of Samaria and eat the fruit.

'For thus saith the LORD; Sing with gladness for Jacob'. God promises to bring his people back from the north country and from the ends of the earth: the blind and the lame, mothers with young children, a great company. They will come with weeping and with prayers, and God will lead them by rivers of water on a straight path where they will not stumble. 'He that scattered Israel will gather him, and keep him, as a shepherd doth his flock.' Their mourning will be turned to joy.

Then comes a sad and beautiful picture. 'A voice was heard in Ramah, lamentation, and bitter weeping'. It is Rachel, the mother of Israel long ago, weeping for her children who are gone, and refusing to be comforted. And God speaks to her: 'Refrain thy voice from weeping, and thine eyes from tears'. Your children will come again. 'And there is hope in thine end'.

God hears his people turning back to him. They say, like a young animal that would not be trained, I resisted you; turn me, and I shall be turned. And God answers like a loving father, remembering a dear son: my heart is troubled for him; I will surely have mercy upon him.

And then God makes the great promise of the chapter. The days are coming, he says, when I will make a new covenant with my people. Not like the covenant I made when I took them by the hand to bring them out of Egypt, which they broke. This new covenant will be different, from the inside out.

The chapter ends with God saying that sooner could the sun, the moon and the stars stop shining, than his people stop being his people before him.

KEY 31:3: 'The LORD hath appeared of old unto me, saying, Yea, I have loved thee with an everlasting love: therefore with lovingkindness have I drawn thee.' After everything, after all the idols and broken promises and judgment, God’s first word is love. Not a love that began recently or might soon run out, but an everlasting love. And lovingkindness is God’s faithful, covenant love that refuses to let go. God did not draw his people back because they deserved it. He drew them because he loved them.

KEY 31:33: 'But this shall be the covenant that I will make with the house of Israel; After those days, saith the LORD, I will put my law in their inward parts, and write it in their hearts; and will be their God, and they shall be my people.' The old covenant’s law was written on tablets of stone. The problem was never the law. The problem was the human heart, which could not keep it. So God promises to do what no one else could: to write his law on the heart itself, so that his people would want to do his will.

KEY 31:34: 'And they shall teach no more every man his neighbour, and every man his brother, saying, Know the LORD: for they shall all know me, from the least of them unto the greatest of them, saith the LORD; for I will forgive their iniquity, and I will remember their sin no more.' Under the new covenant, everyone can know God personally, from the least to the greatest. And it rests on forgiveness. God will remember their sin no more. He does not forget like we forget. He chooses never to hold it against us again.

THREAD: Shortly before he died, Jesus took a cup at supper with his disciples and said, 'This cup is the new testament in my blood, which is shed for you.' Testament here means covenant. The new covenant Jeremiah promised was sealed by the death of Jesus. Through him our sins are forgiven and remembered no more, and God’s Spirit writes his law on our hearts.

TODAY: Maybe you know God’s commands, but your heart does not want to keep them. Ask him to do what he promised: to write his law on your heart. And if you are carrying guilt, remember: God says he will remember your sin no more. You do not have to keep remembering it either.

QUESTION: God said, I have loved thee with an everlasting love. What would change today if you truly believed that God loves you like that?

PRAYER: LORD, thank you for your everlasting love that drew me back to you. Write your law on my heart, so that I want to do your will. Thank you for the new covenant in Jesus’ blood, and that you remember my sins no more. Amen.
