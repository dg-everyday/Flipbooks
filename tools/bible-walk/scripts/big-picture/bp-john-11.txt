# John 11 in The Big Picture: Lazarus raised; I am the resurrection. Keys 11:25, 11:35, 11:43.
TITLE: I Am the Resurrection, and the Life

HOOK: A man has been dead for four days. He has been wrapped in grave clothes and laid in a cave, and a great stone has been rolled across the entrance. His sisters are surrounded by mourners. And now Jesus is standing in front of the tomb, and he says something that makes everyone gasp: 'Take ye away the stone.'

WHERE: In Luke chapter fifteen, Jesus told stories about a lost sheep, a lost coin and a lost son. The Gospel of John tells his story in a different way. John shows us signs, miracles that point to who Jesus is: water turned into wine, the sick healed, five thousand fed, a man born blind given his sight. And John records Jesus’ great I am sayings: I am the bread of life, I am the light of the world, I am the good shepherd. Remember the burning bush, where God said his name was I AM.

By John chapter eleven, the religious leaders in Jerusalem are already trying to kill Jesus. In The Big Picture, this is the greatest sign before the cross: Jesus showing his power over death itself.

STORY: In the village of Bethany, near Jerusalem, lived three people Jesus loved: Martha, her sister Mary, and their brother Lazarus. Lazarus falls ill, and the sisters send a message to Jesus: 'Lord, behold, he whom thou lovest is sick.' Jesus says, 'This sickness is not unto death, but for the glory of God'. John tells us, 'Now Jesus loved Martha, and her sister, and Lazarus.' And then, strangely, Jesus stays where he is for two more days.

Then he tells his disciples, let us go back to Judaea. They are afraid; the people there just tried to stone you. Thomas says bravely, 'Let us also go, that we may die with him.' Jesus tells them, Lazarus sleepeth, but I go to wake him. They do not understand, so he says plainly, Lazarus is dead.

When Jesus arrives, Lazarus has been in the tomb four days. Martha hears he is coming and runs to meet him. 'Lord, if thou hadst been here, my brother had not died.' Jesus says, your brother will rise again. Martha says, I know he will rise in the resurrection at the last day. And Jesus answers her with the words at the centre of this chapter, and asks, 'Believest thou this?' And Martha says, 'Yea, Lord: I believe that thou art the Christ, the Son of God, which should come into the world.'

Then Mary comes, falls at his feet, weeping, and says the same words: Lord, if you had been here. Jesus sees her weeping, and the mourners weeping with her, and he groans in his spirit and is troubled. Where have you laid him? Come and see, they say.

And then comes the shortest verse in the Bible. The people say, 'Behold how he loved him!'

At the tomb, Jesus tells them to take away the stone. Martha protests: Lord, by now there will be a smell; he has been dead four days. Jesus says, 'Said I not unto thee, that, if thou wouldest believe, thou shouldest see the glory of God?' They roll the stone away. Jesus lifts up his eyes and prays: 'Father, I thank thee that thou hast heard me.' And then he cries with a loud voice.

And the dead man comes out, his hands and feet still bound with grave clothes, his face wrapped in a cloth. Jesus says, 'Loose him, and let him go.'

Many who saw it believed in Jesus. But some went to tell the Pharisees, and the leaders called a council. If we let him go on like this, they said, everyone will believe in him. The high priest Caiaphas said, 'it is expedient for us, that one man should die for the people, and that the whole nation perish not.' He meant it as cold politics. But John says that, without knowing it, he was prophesying that Jesus would die for the nation, and for all the scattered children of God. From that day, they planned to put him to death.

KEY 11:25: 'Jesus said unto her, I am the resurrection, and the life: he that believeth in me, though he were dead, yet shall he live:' Martha believed in a resurrection someday, at the end of time. Jesus says, the resurrection is not just an event; it is a person, and he is standing in front of you. Life comes from him. Those who believe in him will live, even though they die. Death is not the end for those who belong to Jesus.

KEY 11:35: 'Jesus wept.' Two words. Jesus knew he was about to raise Lazarus. And still he wept. He was not weeping because he had no hope. He wept because death is terrible, and because he loved his friends and felt their sorrow. God is not distant from our grief. He weeps with us. You can bring your tears to Jesus, and he understands.

KEY 11:43: 'And when he thus had spoken, he cried with a loud voice, Lazarus, come forth.' Someone once said that if Jesus had not called Lazarus by name, every grave would have opened. The voice that called light out of darkness in Genesis now calls a dead man out of his tomb. And one day, Jesus said, all who are in the graves will hear his voice.

THREAD: Long before, Isaiah promised that God would one day destroy death itself: 'He will swallow up death in victory; and the Lord GOD will wipe away tears from off all faces'. At the tomb of Lazarus, Jesus gave a preview. But raising Lazarus would cost him his own life. To swallow up death for ever, he would have to go into death himself.

TODAY: Is there a grief in your life, a place where you have said, Lord, if you had been here? Bring it to him. He weeps with you. And hear his question to Martha as if it were to you: I am the resurrection and the life. Believest thou this?

QUESTION: Jesus asked Martha, believest thou this? How will you answer him today?

PRAYER: Lord Jesus, you are the resurrection and the life. Thank you that you weep with me in my sorrow, and that death does not have the last word. Strengthen my faith to believe you, even at the grave. Call me out of everything that binds me, and let me live. Amen.
