# Joshua 6 in The Big Picture: the fall of Jericho, and Rahab saved. Keys 6:2, 6:20, 6:25.
TITLE: The Wall Fell Down Flat

HOOK: An army is marching around a city. But they are not attacking. No one shoots an arrow. No one climbs a ladder. They just walk, in complete silence, following priests who carry the ark of God and blow trumpets made of rams’ horns. On the walls above, the people of the city watch them, day after day, and wonder what kind of war this is.

WHERE: In Deuteronomy chapter six, Moses called the new generation to love the LORD with all their heart. Then Moses died, and God gave the people a new leader, Joshua. God said to him, 'Be strong and of a good courage; be not afraid, neither be thou dismayed: for the LORD thy God is with thee whithersoever thou goest.'

Joshua sent two spies to the city of Jericho. A woman named Rahab hid them on her roof, because she had heard what God had done at the Red Sea and believed that the LORD is God in heaven above and in earth beneath. The spies promised to save her family, if she tied a scarlet cord in her window. Then, as at the Red Sea, God stopped the waters of the river Jordan, and the whole nation crossed into the land on dry ground.

Now, in chapter six, they face their first city. In The Big Picture, this is God giving his people the land he promised to Abraham.

STORY: Jericho is shut up tight. 'none went out, and none came in.' Its walls are thick and high, and Israel has no machines of war to break them.

But God speaks to Joshua, and gives him a strange plan. March around the city once each day for six days. Seven priests are to go in front of the ark, blowing seven trumpets of rams’ horns. On the seventh day, march around the city seven times. When the priests make a long blast with the trumpets, all the people are to shout with a great shout, and the wall of the city will fall down flat.

Joshua gives the orders, and adds one more: 'Ye shall not shout, nor make any noise with your voice', until the day I tell you to shout.

So they do it. On the first day they march around the city once, and go back to camp. On the second day, the same. For six days they march, and the walls do not move.

On the seventh day, they rise early and march around the city seven times. On the seventh time, the priests blow the trumpets, and Joshua says to the people, 'Shout; for the LORD hath given you the city.'

They shout. And the wall falls down flat.

This is one of the hard parts of the Bible. Jericho was destroyed under God’s judgment, which the Bible presents as God’s verdict on peoples whose wickedness he had borne with for a long time. It records what God did at that moment in the story. It is never a pattern for us to follow in our own lives.

And in the middle of the judgment, there is mercy. Joshua sends the two spies to bring out Rahab, her father and mother, her brothers, and all her family, and to keep them safe outside the camp. They are saved, because Rahab believed and hid the messengers. 'So the LORD was with Joshua; and his fame was noised throughout all the country.'

KEY 6:2: 'And the LORD said unto Joshua, See, I have given into thine hand Jericho, and the king thereof, and the mighty men of valour.' Notice the words, I have given. Before a single step was taken, God spoke about the victory as already done. Faith is acting on what God has said, even before we see it happen. The walls still looked strong to everyone else. But God had already spoken.

KEY 6:20: 'So the people shouted when the priests blew with the trumpets: and it came to pass, when the people heard the sound of the trumpet, and the people shouted with a great shout, that the wall fell down flat, so that the people went up into the city, every man straight before him, and they took the city.' No army has ever conquered a city by walking and shouting. That was the point. The plan made no sense unless God was in it. Sometimes God asks us to do things his way, so that when the walls fall, everyone knows it was the LORD.

KEY 6:25: 'And Joshua saved Rahab the harlot alive, and her father’s household, and all that she had; and she dwelleth in Israel even unto this day; because she hid the messengers, which Joshua sent to spy out Jericho.' A harlot is a woman who sold herself for money. Rahab was an outsider, from an enemy city, with a broken past. And she is the one who is saved. Her faith brought her and her whole family into God’s people. And the story does not end there. Rahab became the great-great-grandmother of King David, and her name appears in the family line of Jesus.

THREAD: The New Testament remembers both parts of this story: 'By faith the walls of Jericho fell down, after they were compassed about seven days.' And in the very next verse: 'By faith the harlot Rahab perished not with them that believed not'. God’s promise to Abraham, that all families of the earth would be blessed, was already reaching outsiders like Rahab.

TODAY: What walls are you facing that look impossible? Do what God has told you, even if it seems small, and keep doing it, day after day. And remember Rahab. No one is too far outside, or too broken, for God to save and use.

QUESTION: God said, I have given, before the walls fell. What promise of God will you act on today, even before you see it?

PRAYER: LORD, you brought down the walls of Jericho, and you saved Rahab and her family. Give me faith to obey you even when your way seems strange, and to trust what you have said before I see it. Thank you that no one is too far gone for your mercy. Amen.
