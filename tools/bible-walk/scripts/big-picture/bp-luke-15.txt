# Luke 15 in The Big Picture: the lost sheep, the lost coin, the lost son. Keys 15:7, 15:20, 15:32.
TITLE: Was Lost, and Is Found

HOOK: A young man is sitting in a pigsty in a foreign country. He is starving. He looks at the pods the pigs are eating and wishes he could fill his stomach with them. Not long ago he had more money than he knew what to do with. He spent it all. Now no one gives him anything. And sitting in the mud, he suddenly remembers his father’s house.

WHERE: In Matthew chapter five, Jesus taught on the mountain: blessed are the poor in spirit, love your enemies. As his ministry went on, crowds followed him wherever he went, and some of the most eager were the people everyone else looked down on: tax collectors who cheated, and people known as sinners. Jesus welcomed them, and even ate with them.

The religious leaders were scandalised. Luke says, 'And the Pharisees and scribes murmured, saying, This man receiveth sinners, and eateth with them.' So Jesus told them three stories. In The Big Picture, these stories show the heart of God toward people who are lost.

STORY: First, a shepherd. Suppose a man has a hundred sheep and loses one of them. Does he not leave the ninety and nine and go after the one that is lost, until he finds it? And when he finds it, he lays it on his shoulders, rejoicing. He comes home and calls his friends and neighbours: 'Rejoice with me; for I have found my sheep which was lost.'

Second, a woman. Suppose a woman has ten silver coins and loses one. Does she not light a candle, sweep the house, and search carefully until she finds it? And when she finds it, she calls her friends: rejoice with me. In the same way, Jesus says, there is joy in the presence of the angels of God over one sinner who repents.

Then the third story, the longest. A man has two sons. The younger one says, 'Father, give me the portion of goods that falleth to me.' In those days, asking for your inheritance while your father was still alive was like saying, I wish you were dead. But the father divides his property between them.

The younger son gathers everything and goes to a far country, and there he wastes it all on wild living. When the money runs out, a great famine comes. He takes a job feeding pigs, the lowest work imaginable for a Jewish young man. He is so hungry he would eat the pigs’ food, and no one gives him anything.

Then, Jesus says, 'he came to himself'. He realises that even his father’s hired servants have bread enough and to spare, while he is starving. 'I will arise and go to my father'. I will tell him I have sinned against heaven and against him, and I am no longer worthy to be called his son. I will ask to be one of his hired servants.

So he gets up and goes home. And while he is still a long way off, his father sees him.

The son begins his speech. But the father hardly lets him finish. He calls to his servants: 'Bring forth the best robe, and put it on him; and put a ring on his hand, and shoes on his feet'. Kill the fatted calf, and let us eat and celebrate. 'For this my son was dead, and is alive again; he was lost, and is found.' And they begin to make merry.

But the story is not over. The older son is out working in the field. He comes near the house and hears music and dancing. A servant tells him his brother has come home and their father has thrown a feast. The older brother is angry, and refuses to go in.

So the father comes out to him too, and pleads with him. The older son says, all these years I have served you and never disobeyed you, and you never gave me even a young goat to celebrate with my friends. But as soon as this son of yours comes, who wasted your money, you kill the fatted calf for him. And the father says, 'Son, thou art ever with me, and all that I have is thine.'

The story ends there. We never find out whether the older brother went in.

KEY 15:7: 'I say unto you, that likewise joy shall be in heaven over one sinner that repenteth, more than over ninety and nine just persons, which need no repentance.' Heaven throws a party when one person turns back to God. The Pharisees were grumbling about sinners coming to Jesus. Heaven was celebrating. Jesus is asking them, and us, whose side are you on? When someone comes to God, do we grumble or rejoice?

KEY 15:20: 'And he arose, and came to his father. But when he was yet a great way off, his father saw him, and had compassion, and ran, and fell on his neck, and kissed him.' The father saw him a great way off, because he had been watching the road. In those days a respected older man would never run; it was undignified. This father ran. He did not wait for the apology. He threw his arms around his son and kissed him, mud and all. This is how God receives those who come home to him.

KEY 15:32: 'It was meet that we should make merry, and be glad: for this thy brother was dead, and is alive again; and was lost, and is found.' Meet means right and fitting. The father says thy brother, reminding the older son who this is. Both sons were lost: one in a far country, one at home, working hard but far from his father’s heart. The father went out to both. God’s grace is for the rebel, and for the proud, rule-keeping person too.

THREAD: Long before, God had said through the prophet Ezekiel, 'I will seek that which was lost, and bring again that which was driven away'. In Genesis chapter three, God came looking for Adam in the garden: where art thou? In Isaiah, we were all sheep who had gone astray. And Jesus later said of himself, 'For the Son of man is come to seek and to save that which was lost.' He is the shepherd who goes after the one.

TODAY: Are you like the younger son, far from home? Arise and go to your Father. He is watching the road. Or are you like the older brother, doing all the right things but resentful and cold? Your Father is coming out to you too. Go in and join the celebration.

QUESTION: The father ran to his son while he was still a great way off. Which son are you most like today, and what will you do about it?

PRAYER: Father, thank you that you are watching the road for me, and that you run to welcome me home. Forgive me for wandering, and forgive me for the times I have been proud and resentful. Give me your joy over every person who is found. Amen.
