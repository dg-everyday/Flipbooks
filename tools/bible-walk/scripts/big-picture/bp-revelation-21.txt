# Revelation 21 in The Big Picture: a new heaven and a new earth, God with his people. Keys 21:3, 21:4, 21:5.
TITLE: Behold, I Make All Things New

HOOK: An old man is alone on a rocky island in the sea, sent there because he would not stop speaking about Jesus. He is the last of the twelve apostles still alive. And there, God gives him a vision of the end of the story. He sees a city coming down out of heaven, shining like a jewel. And he hears a great voice say the words every tired, grieving heart has longed to hear.

WHERE: In Acts chapter two, God poured out his Spirit, and the good news began to spread from Jerusalem. In the years that followed, it went out through all Judaea and Samaria and across the Roman empire, just as Jesus said. Churches were planted, letters were written, and believers suffered for their faith.

The apostle John, who had walked with Jesus, was exiled to the island of Patmos, 'for the word of God, and for the testimony of Jesus Christ.' There he received the visions written in the book of Revelation: Jesus in glory, the Lamb who was slain and is worthy, and the long battle between good and evil, ending in God’s final judgment.

Revelation is full of pictures, and Christians understand many of them in different ways. But the hope of this chapter is shared by all who follow Jesus. In The Big Picture, this is the end of the story, and it brings us back to where we began.

STORY: John says, 'And I saw a new heaven and a new earth: for the first heaven and the first earth were passed away; and there was no more sea.' In the Bible the sea is often a picture of chaos and danger. In the new creation, there is no more of it.

He sees the holy city, the new Jerusalem, coming down from God out of heaven, prepared as a bride adorned for her husband. And he hears a great voice out of heaven.

Then the one who sits on the throne speaks. And he says, it is done. 'I am Alpha and Omega, the beginning and the end.' Alpha and Omega are the first and last letters of the Greek alphabet. 'I will give unto him that is athirst of the fountain of the water of life freely.' Freely means as a gift, without cost. Those who overcome will inherit all things, and God will be their God, and they will be his children. There is also a solemn warning: those who cling to evil and refuse God have no part in that city.

Then an angel carries John to a great high mountain and shows him the city. It shines with the glory of God, its light like a precious jewel, clear as crystal. It has a great high wall with twelve gates, and on the gates are the names of the twelve tribes of Israel. The wall has twelve foundations, and on them are the names of the twelve apostles of the Lamb. God’s people from both testaments are one people in one city. The twelve gates are twelve pearls, and the street of the city is pure gold, like transparent glass.

And then John notices something missing. 'And I saw no temple therein: for the Lord God Almighty and the Lamb are the temple of it.' There is no need for a building where people go to meet God, because God is everywhere present with his people. The city has no need of the sun or the moon, 'for the glory of God did lighten it, and the Lamb is the light thereof.' The nations walk in its light. Its gates are never shut. Nothing unclean will ever enter it, but only those whose names are written in the Lamb’s book of life.

KEY 21:3: 'And I heard a great voice out of heaven saying, Behold, the tabernacle of God is with men, and he will dwell with them, and they shall be his people, and God himself shall be with them, and be their God.' This is the thread that runs through the whole Bible. God walked with Adam in the garden. He lived in a tent among his people in the wilderness. He filled the temple. He came as a baby in a manger. He lives by his Spirit in his people. And at the end, he will dwell with his people face to face, for ever. The story is about God being with us.

KEY 21:4: 'And God shall wipe away all tears from their eyes; and there shall be no more death, neither sorrow, nor crying, neither shall there be any more pain: for the former things are passed away.' Notice who wipes the tears. Not an angel, but God himself. Every grief you have ever carried, every loss, every pain, every goodbye at a grave, God will wipe away with his own hand. Death came into the world in Genesis chapter three. Here it is gone for ever.

KEY 21:5: 'And he that sat upon the throne said, Behold, I make all things new. And he said unto me, Write: for these words are true and faithful.' God does not say, I make all new things, throwing the old world away. He says, I make all things new. He restores what was broken. And then he adds, write it down, because these words are true. You can stake your life on them.

THREAD: The Bible begins, 'In the beginning God created the heaven and the earth.' It ends with a new heaven and a new earth. In the first chapters, people are sent out of the garden and the way to the tree of life is guarded. In the last chapter, the tree of life grows beside the river in God’s city, and 'there shall be no more curse'. Everything lost in the fall is restored, and more. And the very last prayer of the Bible is, 'Even so, come, Lord Jesus.'

TODAY: Whatever you are carrying today, grief, pain, or fear, hold it up against this chapter. This is not the end of your story. The God who began all things will make all things new. Live today in that hope.

QUESTION: God says, behold, I make all things new. What would it mean for you to live today in the hope of that promise?

PRAYER: Lord God, Alpha and Omega, the beginning and the end, thank you that you will dwell with your people and wipe away every tear. Thank you that you make all things new. Give me the water of life, and keep me in hope until that day. Even so, come, Lord Jesus. Amen.
