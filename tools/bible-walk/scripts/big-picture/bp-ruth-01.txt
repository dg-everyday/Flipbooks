# Ruth 1 in The Big Picture: Naomi and Ruth, loyal love in the days of the judges. Keys 1:16, 1:20, 1:22.
TITLE: Whither Thou Goest

HOOK: Three widows are standing on a dusty road. The older woman has lost her husband and both her sons. She is going home, empty, to a town she left long ago. She turns to her two daughters-in-law and begs them to go back to their own mothers. One of them kisses her and walks away. The other one will not let go.

WHERE: In Joshua chapter six, the walls of Jericho fell, and Israel came into the promised land. After Joshua died, a sad time began, called the time of the judges. Again and again the people forgot God and worshipped idols. Enemies attacked them, they cried out to God, and God raised up leaders called judges, like Gideon and Samson, to rescue them. And then they forgot him again. The book of Judges ends with these words: 'every man did that which was right in his own eyes.'

The book of Ruth is set in those same dark days. But it is a quiet story, about one ordinary family, and about the kind of faithful love that God shows, and that his people are meant to show. In The Big Picture, it is also the story of how God prepared the way for a king.

STORY: There is a famine in the land. A man named Elimelech leaves Bethlehem with his wife Naomi and their two sons, and goes to live in the country of Moab, east of the Dead Sea. The people of Moab were not Israelites, and they did not worship the LORD.

Then Elimelech dies. Naomi’s two sons marry women of Moab, named Orpah and Ruth. They live there about ten years. And then both sons die too. Naomi is left with no husband and no sons, in a foreign land.

Then she hears that the LORD has visited his people in Israel and given them bread again. So Naomi sets out to go home, and her two daughters-in-law go with her.

On the road, Naomi stops. Go back, she tells them, each of you to your mother’s house. 'The LORD deal kindly with you, as ye have dealt with the dead, and with me.' She kisses them, and they weep. They say they will go with her. But Naomi insists. I have no more sons to give you as husbands. Go home. She says, 'the hand of the LORD is gone out against me.'

They weep again. Orpah kisses her mother-in-law goodbye and turns back. 'but Ruth clave unto her.' She holds on. Naomi says, look, your sister-in-law has gone back to her people and to her gods; go with her. And Ruth answers with some of the most beautiful words in the Bible.

When Naomi sees that Ruth is determined, she says no more. The two women walk on together to Bethlehem.

When they arrive, the whole town is stirred. The women ask, 'Is this Naomi?' Her name means pleasant. And she answers them out of her grief. 'I went out full, and the LORD hath brought me home again empty'.

And the chapter ends with a small detail that is actually a sign of hope: they came to Bethlehem at the beginning of the barley harvest.

KEY 1:16: 'And Ruth said, Intreat me not to leave thee, or to return from following after thee: for whither thou goest, I will go; and where thou lodgest, I will lodge: thy people shall be my people, and thy God my God:' Intreat means beg. Ruth had every reason to go home, where she might marry again and be safe. Instead she chose a grieving widow, a foreign land, and a hard future. And she chose Naomi’s God. This is more than family loyalty. It is faith. Ruth, an outsider from Moab, is choosing to belong to the LORD and his people.

KEY 1:20: 'And she said unto them, Call me not Naomi, call me Mara: for the Almighty hath dealt very bitterly with me.' Mara means bitter. Naomi is completely honest about her pain. She does not pretend. And the Bible does not hide her words or scold her for them. God can handle our honest grief. And look at what Naomi cannot yet see: she says she came home empty, but Ruth is standing right beside her.

KEY 1:22: 'So Naomi returned, and Ruth the Moabitess, her daughter in law, with her, which returned out of the country of Moab: and they came to Bethlehem in the beginning of barley harvest.' Bethlehem means house of bread. They left because there was no bread, and they come back just as the harvest begins. It is a hint that God has not forgotten them. In the chapters that follow, Ruth goes to gather grain in the field of a kind man named Boaz, a relative of Naomi’s family. Boaz protects her, and marries her, and redeems the family’s land.

THREAD: The book of Ruth ends with a family tree. Ruth and Boaz had a son named Obed, and 'he is the father of Jesse, the father of David.' Ruth the Moabite became the great-grandmother of King David. And in the very first chapter of the New Testament, in the family line of Jesus, we read, 'Booz begat Obed of Ruth'. The blessing promised to all families of the earth was already at work.

TODAY: Maybe you feel like Naomi, empty and bitter, and you have honest words for God. Tell him. And look around: who is the Ruth beside you that you have not noticed? Or maybe God is calling you to be a Ruth, to stay faithful to someone who is hurting.

QUESTION: Ruth said, whither thou goest, I will go. Who is someone you will stand beside in faithful love today?

PRAYER: Lord, in my emptiness, you have not forgotten me. Thank you for Ruth’s faith, and for the way you bring outsiders into your family. Make me faithful in love, like Ruth, and help me to see your kindness even when life feels bitter. Amen.
