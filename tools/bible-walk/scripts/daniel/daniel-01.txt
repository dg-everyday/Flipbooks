# Daniel 1: Jerusalem besieged, the young captives chosen, new names, Daniel purposes in his heart, the ten day test, pulse and water, God gives them wisdom, ten times better. Keys 1:12, 1:17, 1:19.
TITLE: Daniel Purposed in His Heart

HOOK: Imagine being taken from your home, your family and your country, and sent to a foreign palace. They feed you the finest food in the land, from the king’s own table. They give you the best education money can buy. They even give you a new name. Everything is designed to make you one of them. And you have to decide: how much of myself will I give away?

WHERE: In the introduction, we heard that Daniel was a young man of Judah, carried away to Babylon. We heard that on this walk we will take the first six chapters of Daniel, the stories of Daniel and his friends. And we heard that they ask a question: how do you stay faithful to God in a world that does not know him?

Now, in chapter one, the story begins. Listen for how Daniel makes his stand, not with anger or rebellion, but with wisdom and respect. And listen for how God honours those who honour him.

STORY: Nebuchadnezzar, king of Babylon, comes to Jerusalem and besieges it. And the Lord gives Jehoiakim, king of Judah, into his hand, along with some of the holy vessels from the house of God. Nebuchadnezzar carries the vessels away to the temple of his own god in Babylon.

Notice who is in charge. It was not just that Babylon was strong. The Lord gave Jerusalem into his hand. From the very first verses, the book tells us that God rules, even in defeat.

The king tells Ashpenaz, the chief of his court officials, to bring some of the young men of Israel, from the royal family and the nobles. Young men without any blemish, good looking, quick to learn, wise and well educated, fit to serve in the king’s palace. They are to be taught the language and the learning of the Chaldeans, the scholars of Babylon.

The king assigns them a daily portion of his own food, and of the wine he drinks. They are to be trained for three years, and then serve the king.

Among them are four young men of Judah: Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael and Azariah. The chief official gives them new names. Daniel becomes Belteshazzar. Hananiah becomes Shadrach. Mishael becomes Meshach. And Azariah becomes Abednego.

Their Hebrew names all honoured the God of Israel. Daniel means God is my judge. Hananiah means the LORD is gracious. Their new Babylonian names honoured the gods of Babylon. Babylon was trying to reshape these young men, inside and out.

But Daniel purposes in his heart that he will not make himself unclean with the king’s food, or with the wine he drinks. So he asks the chief official for permission not to eat it.

We are not told exactly why Daniel would not eat it. Perhaps the food was not allowed under the law of Moses. Perhaps it had been offered to Babylon’s idols. Perhaps eating from the king’s table meant sharing in his ways. Whatever the reason, Daniel drew a line. He would learn their language, and accept their names. But this was a step too far.

And God has given Daniel favour and kindness with the chief official. But the official is afraid. He says to Daniel, I fear my lord the king, who has appointed your food and your drink. What if he sees you looking worse than the other young men your age? Then you will put my head in danger with the king.

Daniel does not argue, or rebel. He goes to the steward the chief official has put in charge of the four friends, and makes a respectful offer.

The steward agrees, and tests them for ten days.

At the end of the ten days, the four young men look healthier and better nourished than all the young men who have eaten the king’s food. So the steward takes away their portion of the king’s food and wine, and gives them vegetables instead.

God honoured their faithfulness.

And when the three years of training are over, the chief official brings all the young men before King Nebuchadnezzar.

In every matter of wisdom and understanding that the king asks them about, he finds them ten times better than all the wise men in his whole kingdom.

And Daniel continues there, serving in the palace, until the first year of King Cyrus. Kings would come and go. Babylon itself would fall to Persia. But Daniel would still be there, faithful to the end.

KEY 1:12: 'Prove thy servants, I beseech thee, ten days; and let them give us pulse to eat, and water to drink.' Prove means test, and pulse means vegetables, or plain food grown from seeds. Notice how Daniel handled this. He did not make a scene. He did not put the steward in danger. He offered a gentle, reasonable test, and trusted God with the result. Daniel was firm about his convictions, but kind and respectful in how he held them. That is a good pattern for us when our faith is tested.

KEY 1:17: 'As for these four children, God gave them knowledge and skill in all learning and wisdom: and Daniel had understanding in all visions and dreams.' These young men worked hard and studied well. But the book tells us where their wisdom really came from. God gave it to them. They learned all that Babylon could teach, but they did not lose their faith in the process. God can give his people wisdom to do well in their studies and their work, even in a world that does not know him. And Daniel’s gift of understanding dreams will soon save many lives.

KEY 1:19: 'And the king communed with them; and among them all was found none like Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah: therefore stood they before the king.' The king himself tested them, and no one could match them. They had refused the king’s food, and yet they ended up standing before the king. God honours those who honour him. Daniel’s faithfulness in a small thing, what he ate, prepared him for much bigger tests that were still to come.

THREAD: God said through the prophet Samuel, 'for them that honour me I will honour'. And Paul wrote, 'And be not conformed to this world: but be ye transformed by the renewing of your mind'.

TODAY: Daniel purposed in his heart before the test came. Is there an area of your life where you need to decide, ahead of time, how you will stay faithful to God? Make that decision today. And ask God for the wisdom to hold your convictions with kindness and respect.

QUESTION: Daniel accepted a new name and a new language, but he drew a line at the king’s food. Where do you think you need to draw a line to stay faithful to God?

PRAYER: Lord God, like Daniel, I want to purpose in my heart to be faithful to you. Give me courage to stand firm in small things, and wisdom to do it with kindness and respect. Thank you that every good gift of knowledge and skill comes from you. Help me to honour you in everything. Amen.
