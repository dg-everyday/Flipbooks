# Daniel 5: Belshazzar's feast, the vessels of the temple, the fingers of a man's hand, the wise men fail, the queen remembers Daniel, let thy gifts be to thyself, MENE MENE TEKEL UPHARSIN, the kingdom given to the Medes and Persians. Keys 5:5, 5:17, 5:27.
TITLE: Thou Art Weighed in the Balances

HOOK: A thousand guests are feasting in a great hall. The wine is flowing. The king is laughing. He calls for the gold and silver cups taken from God’s temple in Jerusalem, and they drink from them, and praise their idols. Then the laughter stops. Across the room, on the white plaster of the wall, the fingers of a human hand appear from nowhere, and begin to write.

WHERE: In chapter four, King Nebuchadnezzar dreamed of a great tree that was cut down. Daniel warned him to turn from his pride. But he boasted, is not this great Babylon, that I have built? And he was driven out to live like an animal, until he lifted his eyes to heaven and praised the King of heaven, who is able to bring down those who walk in pride.

Now, in chapter five, many years have passed. Nebuchadnezzar is gone, and a new king, Belshazzar, is on the throne. Daniel is an old man now. Listen for how Belshazzar refuses to learn the lesson that Nebuchadnezzar learned. And listen for the writing on the wall.

STORY: King Belshazzar holds a great feast for a thousand of his lords, and drinks wine in front of them all.

Belshazzar was a descendant of Nebuchadnezzar, and the book calls Nebuchadnezzar his father, which in those days could also mean grandfather or forefather.

As he tastes the wine, Belshazzar commands that the gold and silver vessels be brought, the ones Nebuchadnezzar took from the temple in Jerusalem, so that the king and his lords and his wives may drink from them. So they bring the golden vessels taken from the house of God, and they drink from them. And they praise the gods of gold and silver, bronze and iron, wood and stone.

These were holy cups, set apart for the worship of the living God. Belshazzar is mocking the God of Israel.

Then it happens.

The king’s face turns pale. His thoughts terrify him. His legs give way, and his knees knock together.

The king cries out for the astrologers and wise men of Babylon. Whoever can read this writing, and tell me what it means, shall be clothed in purple, with a gold chain around his neck, and shall be the third ruler in the kingdom. But none of the king’s wise men can read the writing, or tell what it means. King Belshazzar is greatly troubled, and his lords are bewildered.

Then the queen, perhaps the king’s mother, hears the noise and comes into the banquet hall. She says, O king, live for ever. Do not be troubled. There is a man in your kingdom in whom is the spirit of the holy gods. In the days of your father, light and understanding and wisdom were found in him. He has an excellent spirit, and knowledge, and understanding, and can interpret dreams and explain riddles. Let Daniel be called, and he will tell you what it means.

So Daniel is brought in. The king says, are you that Daniel, one of the captives of Judah? I have heard of you. Now if you can read the writing and tell me its meaning, you shall be clothed in purple, with a gold chain around your neck, and be the third ruler in the kingdom.

And Daniel, now an old man, answers him.

Then Daniel speaks plainly to the king. O king, the most high God gave Nebuchadnezzar your father a kingdom, and majesty, and glory. But when his heart was lifted up in pride, he was brought down from his throne. He was driven from men, and lived with the wild animals, and ate grass like an ox, until he knew that the most high God rules in the kingdom of men.

And you, his son, O Belshazzar, have not humbled your heart, though you knew all this. You have lifted yourself up against the Lord of heaven. You have used the vessels of his house for your wine, and praised gods of silver and gold, which cannot see, or hear, or know anything. But the God in whose hand your very breath is, and who owns all your ways, you have not honoured.

So God sent the hand, and this writing was written. MENE, MENE, TEKEL, UPHARSIN.

And this is what it means. MENE: God has numbered thy kingdom, and finished it.

Then the second word.

And the last word, PERES. Thy kingdom is divided, and given to the Medes and Persians.

Belshazzar keeps his word. Daniel is clothed in purple, with a chain of gold around his neck, and proclaimed the third ruler in the kingdom.

But it is too late for Belshazzar. 'In that night' the king of the Chaldeans loses his life. And Darius the Mede takes the kingdom.

The golden empire of Babylon, the head of gold in Nebuchadnezzar’s dream, falls in a single day. Just as God said.

KEY 5:5: 'In the same hour came forth fingers of a man’s hand, and wrote over against the candlestick upon the plaister of the wall of the king’s palace: and the king saw the part of the hand that wrote.' In the same hour. While Belshazzar was mocking God with the holy vessels, God answered. The writing appeared right where the lamp shone brightest, so that no one could miss it. Belshazzar thought he could treat holy things with contempt, and nothing would happen. He was wrong. God is patient, but he is not mocked.

KEY 5:17: 'Then Daniel answered and said before the king, Let thy gifts be to thyself, and give thy rewards to another; yet I will read the writing unto the king, and make known to him the interpretation.' Keep your gifts. Daniel could not be bought. He had served kings for many years, and he did not need Belshazzar’s purple robe or gold chain. He would tell the truth, whatever it cost, and whether the king liked it or not. A faithful servant of God speaks the truth without looking for reward.

KEY 5:27: 'TEKEL; Thou art weighed in the balances, and art found wanting.' Weighed in the balances, like goods on a merchant’s scales. God had weighed Belshazzar’s life, and it came up short. It is a sobering thought, that God weighs every heart. And if we are honest, all of us are found wanting. That is why we need a Saviour. The good news of the Bible is that Jesus, who was never found wanting, gave his life for those who are.

THREAD: Paul wrote, 'Be not deceived; God is not mocked: for whatsoever a man soweth, that shall he also reap.' And Paul wrote, 'For all have sinned, and come short of the glory of God'.

TODAY: Belshazzar knew what had happened to Nebuchadnezzar, but he did not humble his heart. Is there a lesson God has been teaching you, that you have not yet taken to heart? Do not wait for the writing on the wall. Turn to him today.

QUESTION: Daniel told Belshazzar, the God in whose hand thy breath is, hast thou not glorified. How can you honour the God who holds your very breath in his hand?

PRAYER: Lord God, my breath is in your hand, and all my ways belong to you. Forgive me for the times I have treated holy things lightly, or ignored the lessons you have taught me. I know that when you weigh my heart, I am found wanting. Thank you for Jesus, who makes me right with you. Help me to honour you all my days. Amen.
