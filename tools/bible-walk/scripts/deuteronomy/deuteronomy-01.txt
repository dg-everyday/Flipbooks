# Deuteronomy 1: these be the words, on this side Jordan (no date spoken), eleven days' journey (in quote only if used), ye have dwelt long enough in this mount, the land set before you, I am not able to bear you alone, judges who fear no face, the spies and Eshcol recalled, the people murmur in their tents, the LORD goeth before you, bare thee as a man doth bear his son, ye did not believe, Caleb and Joshua, the failed attack. Keys 1:21, 1:30, 1:31.
TITLE: As a Man Doth Bear His Son

HOOK: A father is walking a long, hard road with his small son. The boy is tired. His legs give out. And so the father picks him up, and carries him, mile after mile, all the way home. The boy might not even remember it later. But his father does. In Deuteronomy chapter one, Moses tells the people: that is what God did for you in the wilderness.

WHERE: In the introduction, we heard that Deuteronomy is Moses’ farewell to the new generation, on the plains of Moab, before they cross the Jordan. We heard that the name means second law, the same law told again with love. And we heard to watch for three words: remember, heart, and choose.

Now, in chapter one, Moses begins by looking back. Listen for what he reminds them of. And listen for the picture he uses to describe God’s care.

STORY: These are the words Moses spoke to all Israel, on the east side of the Jordan, in the wilderness.

The writer adds a striking detail. From Mount Horeb, which is Sinai, to Kadesh-barnea, at the edge of the promised land, was only eleven days’ journey. Eleven days. And yet it had taken them a lifetime. Their unbelief had turned a short journey into a lifetime.

Near the end of those years, Moses begins to speak, after the defeat of Sihon and Og. And he starts at Mount Horeb.

The LORD our God spoke to us at Horeb, Moses says, and said, you have stayed long enough at this mountain. Turn, and set out, and go to the land of the Amorites. Look, I have set the land before you. Go in and take possession of the land the LORD promised to your fathers, Abraham, Isaac and Jacob.

Then Moses remembers how he could not carry the people alone. You had become as many as the stars of heaven, he says. How can I bear your burdens and your quarrels by myself? So he chose wise and respected men from each tribe, and made them leaders and judges.

And Moses remembers what he told the judges. Hear the cases between your brothers, and judge fairly, even between an Israelite and a foreigner. Do not show favouritism. Hear the small as well as the great. Do not be afraid of anyone, for the judgment is God’s.

Even when telling the story of the journey, Moses stops to talk about justice. Fairness to the small, and to the stranger, mattered deeply to God.

Then Moses remembers the journey through the great and terrible wilderness, to the edge of the land. And what he said to the people then.

But the people wanted to send spies first. So twelve men went, and came back with fruit from the valley of Eshcol, and said, it is a good land that the LORD our God is giving us.

But you would not go up, Moses says. You rebelled. You grumbled in your tents, and said, the LORD hated us, so he brought us out of Egypt to hand us over to the Amorites. Our brothers have made our hearts melt, saying the people are bigger and taller than we are, and the cities are walled up to heaven, and we saw the giants there.

Listen to that. The LORD hated us. After the Red Sea, after the manna, after everything, they said God hated them. Fear had twisted the way they saw God.

And Moses remembers what he said to them.

And Moses goes on.

Yet even then, Moses says, you did not believe the LORD your God, who went ahead of you on the way, in fire through the dark hours and in a cloud by day, to find you places to camp.

So the LORD heard, and was angry, and swore that not one of that generation would see the good land, except Caleb, because he wholly followed the LORD. And Moses says, the LORD was angry with me too, because of you, and said, you will not go in either. But Joshua, who stands before you, he will go in. Encourage him. And your little ones, who you said would become prey, they will go in, and possess it.

Then Moses remembers how the people tried to go in anyway, after God had said no, and were chased away by the Amorites, as if by a swarm of bees.

Why does Moses begin his farewell with this sad story? Because the new generation needed to learn from their parents’ mistake. They were standing at the very same edge, facing the very same choice. Would they trust God this time?

KEY 1:21: 'Behold, the LORD thy God hath set the land before thee: go up and possess it, as the LORD God of thy fathers hath said unto thee; fear not, neither be discouraged.' Fear not, neither be discouraged. God had already set the land before them. All they had to do was go up and take it. Fear and discouragement were the enemies, not the giants. God says the same to us when he calls us forward. Fear not. I have gone before you.

KEY 1:30: 'The LORD your God which goeth before you, he shall fight for you, according to all that he did for you in Egypt before your eyes;' He shall fight for you. Remember the Red Sea, where Moses said, the LORD shall fight for you, and ye shall hold your peace. The God who defeated Pharaoh would defeat the giants too. When we remember what God has already done, it gives us courage for what lies ahead.

KEY 1:31: 'And in the wilderness, where thou hast seen how that the LORD thy God bare thee, as a man doth bear his son, in all the way that ye went, until ye came into this place.' As a man doth bear his son. Through the whole wilderness, God had carried them, like a father carrying a tired child. That is how God cares for his people. Not from a distance, but close, carrying us when we cannot walk on our own.

THREAD: Isaiah wrote, 'Even to your old age I am he; and even to hoar hairs will I carry you'. And Paul wrote, 'If God be for us, who can be against us?'

TODAY: Look back over the past year of your life. Where can you see that God carried you, like a father carrying a child? Thank him for it today. And if you are facing something new and frightening, hear his words: fear not, neither be discouraged.

QUESTION: Moses said God carried his people through the wilderness as a father carries his son. When have you felt carried by God, through a time when you could not walk on your own?

PRAYER: Lord God, thank you for carrying me, like a father carries his child, through all the hard places of my life. Forgive me for the times I have doubted your love. When you call me forward, help me not to be afraid or discouraged, because you go before me, and you fight for me. Amen.
