# Deuteronomy 16: Israel's three feasts told as Israel's covenant calendar (no months or days applied today): Passover and unleavened bread, the bread of affliction, remember the day thou camest out; the feast of weeks with a freewill offering and rejoicing with all; the feast of tabernacles after harvest, thou shalt surely rejoice; appear before the LORD, give as thou art able; judges who wrest not judgment and take no gift, that which is altogether just. Keys 16:11, 16:17, 16:20.
TITLE: That Which Is Altogether Just Shalt Thou Follow

HOOK: Three times every year, roads all over the land fill with families. Parents, children, grandparents, servants, all travelling together to the place where God has put his name. They carry food and gifts. They are going to a feast. And when they get there, the poor and the stranger and the widow sit down and celebrate with them, as part of the family.

WHERE: In chapter fifteen, God told his people to cancel debts every seventh year, to open thine hand wide to the poor, and to give without a grudging heart. Servants were to be set free with generous gifts, because, Moses said, thou shalt remember that thou wast a bondman in the land of Egypt.

Now, in chapter sixteen, Moses describes Israel’s three great feasts. Listen for who is invited to rejoice. And listen for what God says about justice at the end.

STORY: Moses begins with the Passover.

Keep the Passover to the LORD your God, Moses says, for it was then that the LORD brought you out of Egypt, in the dark hours. Offer the Passover sacrifice at the place the LORD chooses.

And for seven days, Moses says, eat unleavened bread, flat bread made without yeast. Moses calls it the bread of affliction, because you came out of Egypt in a hurry. So that all the days of your life, you will remember the day you came out of the land of Egypt.

The flat bread was a taste of their rescue. Every year, they would remember how God brought them out so quickly there was no time for the bread to rise.

Then Moses describes the second feast. Count seven weeks from the time you begin to harvest the grain. Then keep the feast of weeks to the LORD your God, with a freewill offering, giving as the LORD has blessed you.

And then Moses says who is to celebrate.

Look at the list. Your son and daughter. Your servants. The Levite. The stranger. The fatherless. The widow. Everyone. No one was too poor, too foreign, or too unimportant to rejoice before the LORD.

Remember that you were a slave in Egypt, Moses says, and be careful to do these things.

Then the third feast. Keep the feast of tabernacles seven days, after you have gathered in your grain and your wine at the end of the harvest. And rejoice in your feast, you and your family, your servants, the Levite, the stranger, the fatherless and the widow.

Keep the feast for seven days, Moses says, because the LORD your God will bless you in all your harvest, and in all the work of your hands. And then he adds three wonderful words: thou shalt surely rejoice.

Not just, you may rejoice. You shall surely rejoice. Joy was part of God’s command.

Three times a year, Moses says, all your men shall appear before the LORD your God, at the place he chooses: at the feast of unleavened bread, the feast of weeks, and the feast of tabernacles. And they shall not appear before the LORD empty handed.

And then Moses explains how much to give.

Not a fixed amount for everyone. Each person gave in proportion to what God had given them. The rich gave more. The poor gave less. But everyone gave, and everyone rejoiced.

These were Israel’s feasts, part of God’s covenant with them. Christians read them in the light of Jesus. Paul wrote that Christ our passover is sacrificed for us. And at the feast of weeks, many years later, called Pentecost, God poured out his Holy Spirit on the first followers of Jesus.

Then the chapter turns to justice. Appoint judges and officers in all your towns, Moses says, and they shall judge the people fairly.

Do not twist justice, Moses says. Do not show favouritism. And do not take a bribe, for a bribe blinds the eyes of the wise, and twists the words of the righteous.

And then Moses says it with great force.

Altogether just. Justice and only justice. Not mostly fair. Completely fair.

Moses ends with a warning not to set up idols beside the altar of the LORD. God’s worship and God’s justice belong together.

KEY 16:11: 'And thou shalt rejoice before the LORD thy God, thou, and thy son, and thy daughter, and thy manservant, and thy maidservant, and the Levite that is within thy gates, and the stranger, and the fatherless, and the widow, that are among you, in the place which the LORD thy God hath chosen to place his name there.' Everyone at the table. God’s feasts were not just for the comfortable. They included the servant, the outsider, the orphan and the widow. True worship always makes room for the people others leave out.

KEY 16:17: 'Every man shall give as he is able, according to the blessing of the LORD thy God which he hath given thee.' As he is able. God does not ask everyone to give the same. He asks each of us to give in proportion to how he has blessed us. And he sees the heart, like the poor widow Jesus noticed, who gave two small coins, all she had.

KEY 16:20: 'That which is altogether just shalt thou follow, that thou mayest live, and inherit the land which the LORD thy God giveth thee.' Altogether just. God cares deeply about fairness, especially for those without power. Bribes, favouritism and twisted justice are offensive to him. His people are to pursue justice with all their strength.

THREAD: Paul wrote, 'For even Christ our passover is sacrificed for us'. And the prophet Amos said, 'But let judgment run down as waters, and righteousness as a mighty stream.'

TODAY: Is there someone who is usually left out, whom you could invite to share a meal or a celebration with you? Make room for them today. And give to God as you are able, according to how he has blessed you.

QUESTION: Israel’s feasts included the servant, the stranger, the fatherless and the widow. Who might God want you to include at your table?

PRAYER: Lord God, thank you that your feasts make room for everyone. Help me to rejoice before you, and to include those whom others leave out. Make me generous, giving as you have blessed me. And make me a person who loves justice, altogether just, as you are. Amen.
