# Deuteronomy 19: three cities of refuge in the land with roads prepared, the case of one who caused a death by accident (the axe head, told without slayer/killeth outside quotes; avoids 19:4, 19:6, 19:11-13), three more if the land grows, that innocent blood be not shed, the deliberate crime, remove not thy neighbour's landmark, two or three witnesses, the false witness punished, life for life (19:21) and Jesus' teaching (Matthew 5:38-39). Keys 19:10, 19:14, 19:15.
TITLE: At the Mouth of Two Witnesses

HOOK: A man is cutting wood in the forest with his neighbour. He swings his axe, and the iron head flies off the handle, and strikes his friend. His friend dies. It was a terrible accident. But in that world, a grieving relative might come after him, determined to take revenge. Where can he go? God has already prepared the answer.

WHERE: In chapter eighteen, Moses told the people not to seek the future through fortune tellers or mediums. Thou shalt be perfect with the LORD thy God. And he promised that God would raise up a Prophet like unto Moses, and put his words in his mouth. Christians believe that Prophet is Jesus.

Now, in chapter nineteen, Moses speaks about justice: cities of refuge, boundary stones, and witnesses. Listen for how God protects the innocent. And listen for how many witnesses God requires.

STORY: When the LORD your God has given you the land, Moses says, set apart three cities in the middle of it. Prepare roads to them, and divide the land into three parts, so that anyone who causes a death by accident can flee to one of them.

Roads were to be prepared, kept in good condition, so that someone fleeing would not be held up on the way. God made refuge easy to reach.

And Moses gives an example. Suppose a man goes into the forest with his neighbour to cut wood. As he swings his axe to cut down a tree, the head slips off the handle and strikes his neighbour, so that he dies. That man may flee to one of these cities and live.

Otherwise, Moses says, the relative of the one who died might chase him in hot anger, and catch him, because the road is long, and take his life, even though he did not deserve to die, because he did not hate his neighbour.

These three cities were for the land west of the Jordan. Moses had already set apart three on the east side, in chapter four. And Moses says that if God enlarges their land, as he promised, they should add three more.

And God gives the reason.

But if someone hates his neighbour, and lies in wait for him, and attacks him so that he dies, and then flees to one of these cities, that is a different matter. The elders of his own town were to bring him back, and justice would be done. The cities of refuge protected the innocent, not the guilty.

Then Moses gives a short but important rule.

Boundary stones marked the edges of each family’s land. Moving one secretly was a way of stealing your neighbour’s land, little by little. God’s gift of land to each family was to be respected.

And then Moses gives the rule about witnesses.

And Moses goes further. If a false witness rises up against someone, accusing them of wrong, both people are to stand before the LORD, before the priests and judges. The judges must investigate carefully. And if the witness is lying, then do to him what he intended to do to his brother. Whatever punishment he wanted for the innocent person would fall on him instead.

That would make anyone think twice before lying in court.

And Moses ends with words that people have argued about ever since: life for life, eye for eye, tooth for tooth, hand for hand, foot for foot.

These words were a rule for judges, not for personal revenge. They meant that the punishment should fit the crime. No more, and no less. In a world where revenge could spiral out of control, this law set a limit.

But Jesus took it further. He said, ye have heard that it hath been said, an eye for an eye, and a tooth for a tooth. But I say unto you, that ye resist not evil: but whosoever shall smite thee on thy right cheek, turn to him the other also. Jesus called his followers beyond fair punishment, to a love that does not seek revenge at all.

KEY 19:10: 'That innocent blood be not shed in thy land, which the LORD thy God giveth thee for an inheritance, and so blood be upon thee.' God cared deeply about innocent life. The cities of refuge existed so that no one would die who did not deserve to die. God’s people are to protect the innocent, and to make sure that anger and revenge do not lead to more tragedy.

KEY 19:14: 'Thou shalt not remove thy neighbour’s landmark, which they of old time have set in thine inheritance, which thou shalt inherit in the land that the LORD thy God giveth thee to possess it.' A small, quiet sin, moving a stone a few steps, could steal a neighbour’s inheritance. God cares about honesty in small things, as well as big ones. Faithfulness in little things matters to him.

KEY 19:15: 'One witness shall not rise up against a man for any iniquity, or for any sin, in any sin that he sinneth: at the mouth of two witnesses, or at the mouth of three witnesses, shall the matter be established.' Two or three witnesses. This rule protected people from false accusation and gossip. Jesus and Paul both used this principle. Before we believe something bad about someone, we should be careful to know the truth.

THREAD: Jesus said, 'But I say unto you, That ye resist not evil: but whosoever shall smite thee on thy right cheek, turn to him the other also.' And Paul wrote, 'In the mouth of two or three witnesses shall every word be established.'

TODAY: Before you believe or repeat something bad about someone, stop and ask: do I really know this is true? And if someone has wronged you, ask God for grace to respond with love, not revenge.

QUESTION: God required two or three witnesses before anyone could be condemned. Why do you think it is so important to be careful before believing something bad about someone?

PRAYER: Lord God, you are a God of justice, who protects the innocent. Keep me from believing and spreading rumours. Help me to be honest in small things as well as big ones. And teach me to follow Jesus, who calls me beyond revenge, to love. Amen.
