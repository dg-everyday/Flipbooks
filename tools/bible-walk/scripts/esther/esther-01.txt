# Esther 1: Ahasuerus's empire, the long display of glory, the feast in the palace garden, Vashti refuses, Memucan's counsel, the unchangeable law, letters to every province. Keys 1:1, 1:12, 1:19.
TITLE: Vashti the Queen Refused to Come

HOOK: Imagine a party that lasts six months. Gold everywhere. Marble floors in red and blue and white and black. Wine served in golden cups, no two alike. The most powerful king in the world is showing off everything he owns. And at the end, he decides to show off one more thing: his beautiful queen. But she says no.

WHERE: In the introduction, we heard that Esther is set in the empire of Persia, where many of the Jewish people were scattered after the exile. We heard that the name of God never appears in the book, and yet his hand is at work in every turn.

Now, in chapter one, the curtain rises on the palace of King Ahasuerus. Esther herself has not appeared yet. But listen carefully. Everything in this chapter is setting the stage. A queen will be removed, a law will be made that cannot be changed, and a place will open up beside the king.

STORY: In the days of Ahasuerus, the king reigns over a hundred and twenty seven provinces, from India to Ethiopia. His throne is in Shushan the palace.

In the third year of his reign, he holds a feast for all his princes and servants, the power of Persia and Media, the nobles and the rulers of the provinces. For a hundred and eighty days, about six months, he shows them the riches of his glorious kingdom, and the honour of his excellent majesty.

When those days are over, the king holds another feast, for all the people in Shushan the palace, both great and small. It lasts seven days, in the court of the garden of the king’s palace. The writer describes it in rich detail. White, green and blue hangings, fastened with cords of fine linen and purple to silver rings and marble pillars. Couches of gold and silver, on a floor of red and blue and white and black marble. Drinks served in golden cups, each one different, and royal wine in abundance.

At the same time, Vashti the queen holds a feast for the women in the royal house.

On the seventh day, when the king’s heart is merry with wine, he commands his seven chamberlains, his personal servants, to bring Vashti the queen before him, wearing her royal crown. He wants to show the people and the princes her beauty, for she is lovely to look at.

But Vashti refuses.

The king is furious. His anger burns within him.

So the king turns to his wise men, his advisers, who know the law. He asks, what shall we do to Queen Vashti, according to law, because she has not obeyed the command of the king?

One of the princes, named Memucan, answers. Vashti has not only wronged the king. She has wronged all the princes, and all the people in all the provinces. When the women of the empire hear that the queen refused the king, they will look down on their husbands too. There will be no end of contempt and anger.

So Memucan gives his advice.

The king likes this idea, and so do the princes. So the king sends letters to every province, each in its own writing, and to every people in its own language, declaring that every man should be the ruler in his own house.

It is almost comic. The most powerful man in the world cannot get his own wife to come to a party, so he sends a law to every corner of his empire about it. The writer of Esther often shows us how foolish proud people can be.

But beneath the surface, something important has happened. The queen’s place is now empty. And notice the law of the Medes and Persians. Once written and sealed by the king, it could never be changed. Not even the king himself could undo it. That detail will matter greatly later in this story, when a law far more dangerous than this one is written.

Where is God in this chapter? He is not mentioned. The chapter is all about the glory of an earthly king. But the Bible has already told us about another King, whose kingdom is far greater, and whose glory lasts for ever. And while Ahasuerus is busy showing off, that King is quietly preparing to protect his people.

KEY 1:1: 'Now it came to pass in the days of Ahasuerus, (this is Ahasuerus which reigned, from India even unto Ethiopia, over an hundred and seven and twenty provinces:)' The story begins with an empire. A vast kingdom, a hundred and twenty seven provinces. And somewhere in that great empire, scattered among all those provinces, are God’s people, a small minority, far from Jerusalem. They must have felt small and forgotten. But God had not forgotten them. No people are too scattered, and no place too far, for God to watch over them.

KEY 1:12: 'But the queen Vashti refused to come at the king’s commandment by his chamberlains: therefore was the king very wroth, and his anger burned in him.' We are not told why Vashti refused. Perhaps she did not want to be put on display before a crowd of men at a drinking feast. The king could rule a hundred and twenty seven provinces, but he could not rule his own temper. His anger burned. Uncontrolled anger leads to foolish decisions, and in this chapter it does exactly that.

KEY 1:19: 'If it please the king, let there go a royal commandment from him, and let it be written among the laws of the Persians and the Medes, that it be not altered, That Vashti come no more before king Ahasuerus; and let the king give her royal estate unto another that is better than she.' That it be not altered. The laws of the Persians and the Medes could never be changed. Remember that. And notice, the queen’s royal place will be given to another. The king does not know it yet, but that other woman is a young Jewish orphan, living in his own city.

THREAD: The book of Proverbs says, 'He that is slow to anger is better than the mighty; and he that ruleth his spirit than he that taketh a city.' And it says, 'The king’s heart is in the hand of the LORD, as the rivers of water: he turneth it whithersoever he will.'

TODAY: The king’s anger led him into a foolish decision. Is there an area of your life where anger has been ruling you? Today, before you speak or act in anger, pause and pray. Ask God to help you rule your own spirit.

QUESTION: God is not named in this chapter, but he was already preparing to protect his people. Where might God be quietly at work in your life right now, even though you cannot see it yet?

PRAYER: Lord God, you are the King of kings, and your glory is far greater than any earthly king. Thank you that you never forget your people, wherever they are scattered. Help me to rule my own spirit, and to trust that you are at work, even when I cannot see you. Amen.
