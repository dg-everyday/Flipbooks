# Exodus 4: Moses' excuses, the rod and the serpent, the hand, the water, slow of speech, Aaron, Moses returns to Egypt, the people believe. Keys 4:11, 4:12, 4:31.
TITLE: I Will Be with Thy Mouth

HOOK: A shepherd stands before the burning bush, arguing with God. They will not believe me, he says. They will not listen. God tells him to throw his shepherd’s staff on the ground. He does, and it becomes a snake, writhing in the dust. Moses runs from it. Then God says, pick it up by the tail.

WHERE: In chapter three, God appeared to Moses in a bush that burned but was not consumed. He said, I have surely seen the affliction of my people, and I am come down to deliver them. He told Moses to go to Pharaoh. Moses asked, who am I? And God said, certainly I will be with thee. Then God revealed his name: I AM THAT I AM.

Now, in chapter four, the conversation continues. Moses keeps finding reasons not to go. And God, patiently, answers each one. Then Moses sets out for Egypt, and something happens that shows how seriously God takes his covenant.

STORY: Moses answers, behold, they will not believe me, nor listen to my voice. For they will say, the LORD hath not appeared unto thee.

The LORD says to him, what is that in thine hand? And he says, a rod, his shepherd’s staff. God says, cast it on the ground. He throws it down, and it becomes a serpent, and Moses flees from before it. Then God says, put forth thine hand, and take it by the tail. Moses reaches out and catches it, and it becomes a rod in his hand. This is so that they may believe, God says, that the LORD God of their fathers has appeared to you.

Then God gives a second sign. He tells Moses to put his hand into his cloak. When he takes it out, his hand is white with a terrible skin disease, as white as snow. Put it back, God says. And when he takes it out again, it is healed, like the rest of his flesh. And God gives a third sign. If they still will not believe, take water from the river and pour it on the dry land, and it will become blood.

But Moses has another excuse. O my Lord, I am not eloquent, neither before, nor since you have spoken to your servant. 'but I am slow of speech, and of a slow tongue.'

And God answers with a question.

But even after all this, Moses says, O my Lord, send, I pray thee, by the hand of him whom thou wilt send. In other words, please, send someone else.

Now the anger of the LORD is kindled against Moses. But even in his anger, God is gracious. Is not Aaron the Levite thy brother? God says. I know that he can speak well. And behold, he is coming out to meet you, and when he sees you, he will be glad in his heart. You shall speak to him, and put words in his mouth. I will be with thy mouth, and with his mouth, and will teach you what you shall do. Aaron will be your spokesman to the people. And take this rod in your hand, with which you shall do the signs.

So Moses goes back to Jethro his father-in-law, and asks to return to his brothers in Egypt. Jethro says, go in peace. And the LORD says to Moses in Midian, go, return into Egypt, for all the men are dead who sought your life. Moses takes his wife and his sons, sets them on a donkey, and returns to Egypt. And he takes 'the rod of God in his hand.' The shepherd’s staff has become the rod of God.

God warns Moses that Pharaoh will harden his heart and refuse to let the people go. And God gives Moses a message for Pharaoh: Israel is my son, even my firstborn. Let my son go, that he may serve me. If you refuse, your own firstborn will die. This is a solemn warning of what will happen at the end.

On the journey, at a place where they stay, the LORD meets Moses, and is about to put him to death. Moses has not given his son the sign of God’s covenant, the sign God gave to Abraham. Zipporah acts quickly and gives their son the sign herself, and the LORD lets Moses go. It is a strange and serious moment. The man who will lead God’s covenant people must first keep God’s covenant in his own home.

The LORD tells Aaron to go into the wilderness to meet Moses. Aaron goes, and meets him at the mountain of God, and kisses him. Moses tells Aaron all the words of the LORD, and all the signs.

Then Moses and Aaron go and gather together all the elders of the children of Israel. Aaron speaks all the words the LORD had spoken to Moses, and does the signs in the sight of the people.

KEY 4:11: 'And the LORD said unto him, Who hath made man’s mouth? or who maketh the dumb, or deaf, or the seeing, or the blind? have not I the LORD?' Moses looked at his weakness, and said, I cannot. God reminded Moses who made his mouth in the first place. Our weaknesses do not surprise God. He made us, and he knows our limits. And he is not limited by them. When God calls us to something, he does not ask what we can do. He asks us to trust what he can do.

KEY 4:12: 'Now therefore go, and I will be with thy mouth, and teach thee what thou shalt say.' Go, and I will be with thy mouth. God did not take away Moses’ weakness. He promised to be with him in it. Many of God’s greatest servants felt inadequate. That is often the very reason God uses them, so that everyone can see that the power comes from him. Jesus made a similar promise to his disciples: it is not you that speak, but the Spirit of your Father which speaketh in you.

KEY 4:31: 'And the people believed: and when they heard that the LORD had visited the children of Israel, and that he had looked upon their affliction, then they bowed their heads and worshipped.' Moses had been sure the people would not believe him. But they did. All his fears had been for nothing. When the people heard that God had seen their suffering, they bowed their heads and worshipped. Many of the things we fear never happen. And the news that God sees and cares for us is still the best reason to worship.

THREAD: Paul wrote to the Corinthians that his speech was not with enticing words of man’s wisdom, but in demonstration of the Spirit and of power, 'That your faith should not stand in the wisdom of men, but in the power of God.' And the Lord told Paul, 'My grace is sufficient for thee: for my strength is made perfect in weakness.'

TODAY: What is in your hand? It may seem as ordinary as a shepherd’s staff. Offer it to God. And if you are making excuses about something God has asked you to do, hear his answer: I will be with thee.

QUESTION: God asked Moses, what is that in thine hand? What ordinary gift or ability do you have that you could offer to God?

PRAYER: Lord God, you made my mouth, my hands and my mind, and you know my weaknesses. Forgive me for my excuses. Take what is in my hand and use it for your glory. Be with my mouth, and teach me what to say. Amen.
