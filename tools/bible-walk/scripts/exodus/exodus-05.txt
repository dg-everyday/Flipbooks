# Exodus 5: Moses and Aaron before Pharaoh, who is the LORD, bricks without straw, the officers beaten, the people blame Moses, Moses complains to God. Keys 5:1, 5:2, 5:22.
TITLE: Who Is the LORD, That I Should Obey His Voice?

HOOK: Two old men walk into the throne room of the most powerful king in the world. They are shepherds, brothers, from a nation of slaves. They stand before Pharaoh and give him a message from their God: let my people go. The king laughs. And by the end of the day, the slaves’ lives are worse than ever.

WHERE: In chapter four, Moses made excuse after excuse, and God answered each one. He gave Moses signs, and his brother Aaron to speak for him. God said, I will be with thy mouth. Moses returned to Egypt, and when the elders of Israel heard that the LORD had seen their affliction, they bowed their heads and worshipped.

Now, in chapter five, Moses and Aaron go to Pharaoh for the first time. Everything seems ready. The people believe. God has promised to act. But the first step of obedience makes everything worse. It is a chapter for anyone who has obeyed God, and then found that things got harder.

STORY: Afterward, Moses and Aaron go in to Pharaoh, and give him God’s message.

But Pharaoh answers with scorn.

Moses and Aaron try again. The God of the Hebrews has met with us, they say. Let us go, we pray thee, three days’ journey into the desert, and sacrifice to the LORD our God, lest he fall upon us with pestilence, or with the sword.

The king of Egypt says, Moses and Aaron, why are you taking the people away from their work? Get back to your burdens. The people of the land are many, and you are making them rest from their labour.

That same day, Pharaoh gives a command to the taskmasters and the officers. Until now, the Egyptians gave the Israelites straw, which they mixed with clay to make bricks. Now Pharaoh says, you shall no more give the people straw to make brick. Let them go and gather straw for themselves. But the number of bricks they make must stay the same. Do not lessen it. For they are idle. That is why they cry, let us go and sacrifice to our God. Let more work be laid on the men, so that they have no time to listen to lying words.

The taskmasters go out and tell the people, thus saith Pharaoh, I will not give you straw. Go yourselves, get straw where you can find it. Yet not one bit of your work shall be lessened.

So the people are scattered throughout all the land of Egypt, to gather stubble, the dry stalks left in the fields, instead of straw. And the taskmasters hurry them, saying, fulfil your works, your daily tasks, as when there was straw.

The Hebrew officers, whom the taskmasters had set over the work, are beaten. They are asked, why have you not fulfilled your task in making brick, as before? Those are Pharaoh’s own taskmasters asking, and the officers are blamed for an impossible task.

The officers go to Pharaoh and cry out. Wherefore dealest thou thus with thy servants? There is no straw given to your servants, and they say to us, make brick. Your servants are beaten, but the fault is in your own people. But Pharaoh says, ye are idle, ye are idle. That is why you say, let us go and sacrifice to the LORD. Go now, and work. No straw shall be given you, yet you shall deliver the full number of bricks.

The officers see that they are in evil case, in serious trouble. As they come out from Pharaoh, they meet Moses and Aaron, who are standing in the way, waiting for them. And they turn on them. The LORD look upon you, and judge, they say. You have made us stink in the eyes of Pharaoh and his servants, and put a sword in their hand to kill us.

Just a few days before, these people bowed their heads and worshipped. Now they blame Moses for making their lives worse.

And Moses does the only thing he can do. He goes back to God.

Since I came to Pharaoh to speak in thy name, Moses says, he has done evil to this people, 'neither hast thou delivered thy people at all.'

It is a raw and honest prayer. And God will answer it in the next chapter.

KEY 5:1: 'And afterward Moses and Aaron went in, and told Pharaoh, Thus saith the LORD God of Israel, Let my people go, that they may hold a feast unto me in the wilderness.' Let my people go. Those four words would echo through Egypt again and again. Notice why God wanted them free. Not just to escape hard work, but to worship him. God sets his people free so that they can belong to him and serve him. Freedom in the Bible is not freedom to do whatever we like. It is freedom to love and worship God.

KEY 5:2: 'And Pharaoh said, Who is the LORD, that I should obey his voice to let Israel go? I know not the LORD, neither will I let Israel go.' Pharaoh asked, who is the LORD? It was a proud question. Pharaoh thought he himself was a god. He saw no reason to obey a God of slaves. But the rest of the story will answer his question. By the time God is finished, Pharaoh and all Egypt will know exactly who the LORD is. Many people still ask Pharaoh’s question. God still answers it.

KEY 5:22: 'And Moses returned unto the LORD, and said, Lord, wherefore hast thou so evil entreated this people? why is it that thou hast sent me?' Moses did not hide his confusion from God. He asked, why? Why did you send me, if this is what happens? The Bible is full of honest prayers like this one. God is not offended by our questions. He invites us to bring them to him. Moses was discouraged, but notice where he went. He returned unto the LORD. That is always the right place to take our questions.

THREAD: The psalms are full of honest questions: 'How long wilt thou forget me, O LORD? for ever?' And yet the same psalm ends, 'But I have trusted in thy mercy; my heart shall rejoice in thy salvation.' Jesus himself said, 'In the world ye shall have tribulation: but be of good cheer; I have overcome the world.'

TODAY: Have you obeyed God, and then found that things got harder, not easier? Do not give up. Take your questions to God, honestly, as Moses did. The story is not over yet. And remember that the purpose of God’s freedom is that you may worship him.

QUESTION: Moses obeyed God, and things got worse. When has obeying God been hard, and how did you keep trusting him?

PRAYER: Lord God, when I obey you and things get harder, help me not to give up. Thank you that I can bring you my honest questions. Set me free to worship and serve you. And help me, like Moses, always to return to you. Amen.
