# Exodus 12: the first Passover, the lamb without blemish, the blood on the doorposts, unleavened bread, the last plague, Israel goes out of Egypt. Keys 12:13, 12:26, 12:51.
TITLE: When I See the Blood, I Will Pass over You

HOOK: In thousands of homes across the land of Goshen, families stand around a table, dressed for a journey. Their sandals are on their feet. Their staffs are in their hands. On the doorframe of every house is a red mark, painted with a bundle of hyssop. They eat quickly, and wait. Outside, something is about to pass through the land.

WHERE: In chapter eleven, God announced one more plague. All the firstborn of Egypt would die, but against the children of Israel not even a dog would move his tongue, so that they would know how the LORD puts a difference between the Egyptians and Israel. Moses left Pharaoh in great anger.

Now, in chapter twelve, God shows his people how they will be kept safe. This is one of the most important chapters in the whole Bible. It tells of the first Passover. The Passover would be remembered by Israel for ever after. And the New Testament tells us that it points to Jesus.

STORY: The LORD speaks to Moses and Aaron in Egypt. This month shall be unto you the beginning of months, he says. God gives his people a new beginning, and a new calendar. Their history as a free people will start with this.

On the tenth day of the month, every household is to take a lamb. If a household is too small for a whole lamb, it is to share with its nearest neighbour. 'Your lamb shall be without blemish, a male of the first year'. They are to keep it until the fourteenth day of the month. Then the whole assembly of Israel is to sacrifice it.

They are to take some of the lamb’s blood, and strike it on the two side posts and on the upper door post of the houses where they eat it. They are to roast the lamb with fire, and eat it with unleavened bread, bread made without yeast, and with bitter herbs. Bread without yeast, because there is no time to let it rise. Bitter herbs, to remember the bitterness of slavery.

And they are to eat it ready to leave: dressed for travel, their shoes on their feet, and their staff in their hand. They shall eat it in haste. 'it is the LORD’S passover.'

God says that he will pass through the land of Egypt, and strike all the firstborn, and execute judgment against all the gods of Egypt. I am the LORD. And then God tells them what the blood will mean.

God commands that this day shall be a memorial. They are to keep it as a feast to the LORD throughout their generations. For seven days they shall eat unleavened bread.

Moses calls all the elders of Israel and tells them what to do. Take a lamb according to your families. Take a bunch of hyssop, dip it in the blood, and strike the lintel and the two side posts. And none of you shall go out of the door of his house. For the LORD will pass through to strike the Egyptians. When he sees the blood, the LORD will pass over the door, and will not let the destroyer come in to your houses.

And Moses tells them they will be asked a question by their children.

And the people bow the head and worship. The children of Israel go away, and do as the LORD had commanded Moses and Aaron.

Then it happens. 'And it came to pass, that at midnight the LORD smote all the firstborn in the land of Egypt'. From the firstborn of Pharaoh on his throne, to the firstborn of the captive in the dungeon. And Pharaoh rises up, he and all his servants and all the Egyptians, and there is a great cry in Egypt, 'for there was not a house where there was not one dead.'

Pharaoh calls for Moses and Aaron in the dark, and says, rise up, and get you forth from among my people, both you and the children of Israel. Go, serve the LORD, as you have said. Take your flocks and your herds, as you have said, and be gone. 'and bless me also.' The proud king who asked, who is the LORD, now asks for a blessing.

The Egyptians urge the people to leave quickly. So the people take their dough before it is leavened, and the Egyptians give them silver, gold and clothing, as God had promised.

The children of Israel journey from Rameses to Succoth, about six hundred thousand men on foot, besides children. A mixed multitude of other peoples goes with them, with flocks and herds and very much cattle.

The time that the children of Israel lived in Egypt was four hundred and thirty years, just as God told Abraham long before. And at the end of those years, on the very same day, all the armies of the LORD go out from the land of Egypt.

KEY 12:13: 'And the blood shall be to you for a token upon the houses where ye are: and when I see the blood, I will pass over you, and the plague shall not be upon you to destroy you, when I smite the land of Egypt.' The Israelites were not saved because they were better than the Egyptians. They were saved because of the blood of the lamb. God did not say, when I see your good works. He said, when I see the blood. Every household under the blood was safe. That is the heart of the gospel. We are saved not by what we have done, but by what the Lamb has done for us.

KEY 12:26: 'And it shall come to pass, when your children shall say unto you, What mean ye by this service?' God expected children to ask questions, and he wanted parents to answer them. The answer was simple: it is the LORD’S passover. He passed over our houses, and delivered us. Faith is meant to be passed down, from one generation to the next, by telling the story of what God has done.

KEY 12:51: 'And it came to pass the selfsame day, that the LORD did bring the children of Israel out of the land of Egypt by their armies.' After four hundred and thirty years, on the very day God had planned, they walked out free. The slaves left as an army. God keeps his promises exactly. What seemed impossible for centuries happened in a single day, when God’s time came.

THREAD: John the Baptist saw Jesus and said, 'Behold the Lamb of God, which taketh away the sin of the world.' And Paul wrote, 'For even Christ our passover is sacrificed for us'. Peter wrote that we are redeemed 'with the precious blood of Christ, as of a lamb without blemish and without spot'.

TODAY: Thank God for Jesus, the Lamb without blemish, whose blood covers all who trust in him. And think of a child, or a younger person in your life. Is there a way you could tell them what God has done for you?

QUESTION: God said, when I see the blood, I will pass over you. What does it mean to you that you are saved by what Jesus has done, not by what you have done?

PRAYER: Lord God, thank you for the Passover lamb, and for Jesus, the Lamb of God, who was sacrificed for me. Thank you that I am safe, not because of my goodness, but because of his blood. Help me to tell others, and especially the young, what you have done. Amen.
