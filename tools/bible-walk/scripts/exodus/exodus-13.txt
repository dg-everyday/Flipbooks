# Exodus 13: the firstborn set apart, remember this day, unleavened bread, tell thy son, not the way of the Philistines, Joseph's bones, the pillar of cloud and fire. Keys 13:3, 13:17, 13:21.
TITLE: The LORD Went Before Them

HOOK: A vast crowd of people walks out into the desert, with their flocks and herds and children. They have no maps, no roads, and no idea where they are going. But at the front of the crowd, rising up into the sky, there is a great column of cloud. When it moves, they move. And when darkness falls, the cloud begins to glow like fire.

WHERE: In chapter twelve, God gave his people the first Passover. They put the blood of a lamb on their doorposts, and God said, when I see the blood, I will pass over you. The firstborn of Egypt died, and Pharaoh drove Israel out. After four hundred and thirty years, on the selfsame day, the LORD brought his people out of Egypt.

Now, in chapter thirteen, Israel is free. But they are not yet safe, and they are not yet home. Before they go any further, God teaches them to remember. And then he leads them himself, in a way they can see.

STORY: The LORD speaks to Moses. Sanctify unto me all the firstborn, he says, among the children of Israel, both of man and of beast. It is mine. The firstborn of Israel had been spared at the Passover. Now they belong to God in a special way.

Then Moses speaks to the people.

This day you came out, in the month Abib, Moses says, the month of fresh green ears of grain. And when the LORD brings you into the land of the Canaanites, a land flowing with milk and honey, you shall keep this service in this month. Seven days you shall eat unleavened bread, and on the seventh day there shall be a feast to the LORD.

And Moses gives a command for parents. You shall shew thy son in that day, saying, this is done because of that which the LORD did unto me when I came forth out of Egypt. Notice the words, unto me. Every generation is to tell the story as their own. It shall be for a sign upon your hand, and for a memorial between your eyes, so that the LORD’S law may be in your mouth. 'for with a strong hand hath the LORD brought thee out of Egypt.'

Moses tells them that when they come into the land, the firstborn sons are to be redeemed, bought back, with an offering. And when your son asks you in time to come, what is this? you shall say to him, by strength of hand the LORD brought us out from Egypt, from the house of bondage. When Pharaoh would hardly let us go, the LORD struck all the firstborn in the land of Egypt. Therefore I sacrifice to the LORD, and every firstborn of my children I redeem.

Then the people set out. And God chooses their road carefully.

Instead, God leads the people around, through the way of the wilderness of the Red Sea. And the children of Israel go up out of the land of Egypt prepared for battle, in orderly ranks.

And Moses takes something important with him: the bones of Joseph. Long before, at the end of Genesis, Joseph had made the children of Israel swear an oath. 'God will surely visit you; and ye shall carry up my bones away hence with you.' Now, four hundred years later, they keep that promise. Joseph’s faith in God’s promise is finally being fulfilled.

They take their journey from Succoth, and camp in Etham, at the edge of the wilderness.

And then God gives them a guide.

'He took not away the pillar of the cloud by day, nor the pillar of fire by night, from before the people.' It never left them.

KEY 13:3: 'And Moses said unto the people, Remember this day, in which ye came out from Egypt, out of the house of bondage; for by strength of hand the LORD brought you out from this place: there shall no leavened bread be eaten.' Remember. It is one of the most important words in the Bible. God knew how quickly his people would forget. So he gave them feasts, signs and stories, to help them remember what he had done. We forget too. That is why it is good to look back, to tell the stories of God’s faithfulness, and to remind each other of what he has done.

KEY 13:17: 'God led them not through the way of the land of the Philistines, although that was near; for God said, Lest peradventure the people repent when they see war, and they return to Egypt:' The short road was not the best road. God knew that his people were not ready to face war. So he led them the long way. Sometimes God leads us on a longer road than we would choose. It may seem slow or confusing. But he knows what we are ready for, and he is protecting us from battles we cannot yet face.

KEY 13:21: 'And the LORD went before them by day in a pillar of a cloud, to lead them the way; and by night in a pillar of fire, to give them light; to go by day and night:' God did not send his people into the wilderness alone. He went before them. The cloud showed them the way, and the fire gave them light in the dark. They never had to guess which way to go. They just had to follow. God still goes before his people, and leads us by his Word and his Spirit.

THREAD: The psalm says, 'Thy word is a lamp unto my feet, and a light unto my path.' And Jesus said, 'My sheep hear my voice, and I know them, and they follow me'.

TODAY: Take time today to remember something God has done for you, and tell someone about it. And if God seems to be leading you on a longer road than you expected, trust that he knows the way, and that he goes before you.

QUESTION: God led Israel the long way, because he knew they were not ready for war. Where might God be leading you on a longer road for your own good?

PRAYER: Lord God, thank you that you go before me, as you went before Israel in the cloud and the fire. Help me to remember all that you have done for me. When the road is longer than I expected, help me to trust that you know the way, and to follow you. Amen.

NEXT: Next on the walk, Exodus chapter fourteen. Pharaoh changes his mind and chases after Israel. The people are trapped between the army and the sea. And Moses says, stand still, and see the salvation of the LORD.
