# Exodus 17: no water at Rephidim, is the LORD among us, the rock in Horeb, Amalek attacks, Joshua fights, Aaron and Hur hold up Moses' hands, Jehovah-nissi. Keys 17:6, 17:12, 17:15.
TITLE: Aaron and Hur Stayed Up His Hands

HOOK: On a hilltop, an old man stands with his arms raised high, holding a shepherd’s staff toward heaven. Below him, in the valley, a battle is raging. As long as his hands are up, his people are winning. But his arms are getting tired. Slowly, they begin to drop. And down in the valley, the battle turns.

WHERE: In chapter sixteen, the people grumbled that they were hungry, and God rained bread from heaven. They called it manna. Every day there was enough, and on the sixth day there was twice as much, so that they could rest on the seventh. God was teaching them to trust him one day at a time.

Now, in chapter seventeen, Israel faces two new tests. First, there is no water. Then, for the first time, they are attacked by an enemy. In both, God provides. And in the second, we learn something about how God’s people need each other.

STORY: The whole congregation of Israel journeys from the wilderness of Sin, as the LORD commands, and camps in Rephidim. And there is no water for the people to drink.

The people quarrel with Moses. They say, give us water that we may drink. Moses says, why do you quarrel with me? Why do you tempt the LORD? But the people thirst, and murmur against Moses. Wherefore is this that thou hast brought us up out of Egypt, to kill us and our children and our cattle with thirst?

Moses cries to the LORD. What shall I do unto this people? They be almost ready to stone me.

The LORD says to Moses, go on before the people, and take with you some of the elders of Israel. And take in your hand your rod, with which you struck the river. Then God tells him what will happen.

Moses calls the name of the place Massah, which means testing, and Meribah, which means quarrelling, because the children of Israel quarrelled, and because they tempted the LORD, saying, 'Is the LORD among us, or not?' After everything they had seen, the plagues, the sea, the manna, they still asked whether God was with them.

Then Amalek comes and fights with Israel in Rephidim. The Amalekites are descendants of Esau. Later, Moses will remind Israel that the Amalekites attacked from behind, picking off the weak and weary at the back of the line.

Moses says to Joshua, choose us out men, and go out, fight with Amalek. I will stand on the top of the hill with the rod of God in my hand. This is the first time we meet Joshua, who will one day lead Israel into the promised land.

So Joshua does as Moses said, and fights with Amalek. And Moses, Aaron and Hur go up to the top of the hill. When Moses holds up his hand, Israel prevails. When he lets down his hand, Amalek prevails.

Then Moses’ arms grow tired.

And Joshua defeats Amalek and his people with the edge of the sword.

The LORD says to Moses, write this for a memorial in a book, and rehearse it in the ears of Joshua. This is the first time in the Bible that God tells someone to write something down. God wanted his people to remember this victory, and how it was won.

Then Moses builds an altar.

Moses says that the LORD has sworn that he will have war with Amalek from generation to generation.

KEY 17:6: 'Behold, I will stand before thee there upon the rock in Horeb; and thou shalt smite the rock, and there shall come water out of it, that the people may drink. And Moses did so in the sight of the elders of Israel.' Out of a hard, dry rock came water, enough for a whole nation and all their animals. God provided in the most unlikely way, from the most unlikely place. And notice God’s words: I will stand before thee there. God was present at the rock. When we cannot see how God could possibly provide, he can bring water out of stone.

KEY 17:12: 'But Moses’ hands were heavy; and they took a stone, and put it under him, and he sat thereon; and Aaron and Hur stayed up his hands, the one on the one side, and the other on the other side; and his hands were steady until the going down of the sun.' Moses was a great leader, but he could not do it alone. His arms grew tired. So his friends gave him a stone to sit on, and held up his hands. The victory in the valley depended on the friends on the hill. We all need people who will hold up our hands when we are weary. And we can be that for others.

KEY 17:15: 'And Moses built an altar, and called the name of it Jehovah-nissi:' Jehovah-nissi means the LORD is my banner. In battle, soldiers rallied around a banner, a flag lifted high. Moses was saying that the victory did not belong to Joshua, or to the soldiers, or even to Moses with his raised hands. It belonged to the LORD. He is the banner we gather under. Every victory is his.

THREAD: Paul wrote that Israel 'drank of that spiritual Rock that followed them: and that Rock was Christ.' And he wrote, 'Bear ye one another’s burdens, and so fulfil the law of Christ.'

TODAY: Is there someone in your life whose hands are growing heavy, a leader, a friend, a family member? Be their Aaron or their Hur today. Pray for them, encourage them, help them. And if your own hands are heavy, do not be too proud to let others help you.

QUESTION: Aaron and Hur held up Moses’ hands when he grew weary. Who holds up your hands, and whose hands could you hold up?

PRAYER: Lord God, you brought water from the rock, and you are my banner in every battle. Thank you for the people who hold up my hands when I am weary. Show me who needs my help today, and give me a willing heart to stand beside them. Every victory belongs to you. Amen.
