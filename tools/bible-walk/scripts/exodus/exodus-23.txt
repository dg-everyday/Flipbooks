# Exodus 23: honest witnesses, not following a multitude to do evil, no favouritism in court either way, help your enemy's ox and ass (Jesus: love your enemies), no bribes (the gift blindeth the wise), the stranger again (ye know the heart of a stranger), the seventh year rest for the land so the poor may eat, the seventh day rest for animals and servants, three feasts (unleavened bread, harvest, ingathering) told as Israel's feasts not today's practice, the Angel sent before them (my name is in him), blessing, hornets, little by little I will drive them out, no covenant with their gods. Keys 23:2, 23:4, 23:20.
TITLE: Thou Shalt Not Follow a Multitude to Do Evil

HOOK: A crowd of children gathers around a new boy in the playground. One of them starts teasing him. Then another joins in. Soon they are all laughing at him. One girl stands at the edge of the crowd. She knows it is wrong. But everyone else is doing it. If she does not join in, they might turn on her. What should she do? Exodus chapter twenty three has a law for exactly that moment.

WHERE: In chapter twenty two, the laws continued. A thief had to restore more than he took, five oxen for an ox, and four sheep for a sheep. God commanded, thou shalt neither vex a stranger, nor oppress him: for ye were strangers in the land of Egypt. He would hear the cry of the widow and the fatherless. And a poor man’s cloak had to be returned before he slept, for God said, I am gracious.

Now, in chapter twenty three, we come to the last of these laws, and then a great promise. Listen for what God says about crowds. And listen for who God will send ahead of his people.

STORY: The chapter begins with laws about telling the truth, especially in court. 'Thou shalt not raise a false report: put not thine hand with the wicked to be an unrighteous witness.' A false report is a lie, a rumour about someone. Do not spread lies, or help the wicked by lying in court.

Then a law about crowds.

Then, 'Neither shalt thou countenance a poor man in his cause.' Countenance means favour. You might think God would say, always take the side of the poor. And God does care deeply for the poor. But in court, justice must be fair to everyone. A judge must not favour the poor just because they are poor, or the rich just because they are rich. A few verses later, God says, 'Thou shalt not wrest the judgment of thy poor in his cause.' Wrest means twist. Do not twist justice against the poor either. Justice must be equal for all.

Then a surprising law about enemies.

And there is more. 'If thou see the ass of him that hateth thee lying under his burden, and wouldest forbear to help him, thou shalt surely help with him.' Imagine the donkey of someone who hates you, collapsed under a heavy load. Forbear means hold back. But God says, help him. Help your enemy lift the load off his donkey.

'And thou shalt take no gift: for the gift blindeth the wise, and perverteth the words of the righteous.' A gift here means a bribe, money given to a judge to decide a case unfairly.

And again, God speaks about strangers. 'Also thou shalt not oppress a stranger: for ye know the heart of a stranger, seeing ye were strangers in the land of Egypt.' You know how it feels, God says. You know the heart of a stranger.

Then laws about rest. For six years, the people would sow their fields and gather the harvest. 'But the seventh year thou shalt let it rest and lie still; that the poor of thy people may eat'. Every seventh year, the land was to rest. Whatever grew by itself was left for the poor, and even for the wild animals. And every seventh day, the people were to rest from work, 'that thine ox and thine ass may rest, and the son of thy handmaid, and the stranger, may be refreshed.' Even the animals and the servants were to have rest. God cares about tired people, and tired animals too.

Then God tells Israel about three great feasts they were to keep each year. The feast of unleavened bread, to remember how God brought them out of Egypt in a hurry. The feast of harvest, when the first crops were gathered. And the feast of ingathering, at the end of the harvest. Three times a year, the people would come together to thank God for rescuing them, and for providing for them.

And then the chapter turns from laws to a promise.

'Beware of him, and obey his voice, provoke him not; for he will not pardon your transgressions: for my name is in him.' This Angel was very special. God’s own name was in him. Many Christians have seen in this Angel the LORD himself, going before his people.

'And ye shall serve the LORD your God, and he shall bless thy bread, and thy water; and I will take sickness away from the midst of thee.'

Then God explains how he will give them the land. Not all at once. 'I will not drive them out from before thee in one year; lest the land become desolate, and the beast of the field multiply against thee. By little and little I will drive them out from before thee, until thou be increased, and inherit the land.' Otherwise wild animals would take over the empty land.

And God warns them: make no covenant with the gods of the land. Do not let them live among you, 'lest they make thee sin against me'. Their gods would be a trap.

KEY 23:2: 'Thou shalt not follow a multitude to do evil; neither shalt thou speak in a cause to decline after many to wrest judgment'. A multitude is a crowd. It is very hard to stand alone when everyone else is doing wrong. But God says, do not follow the crowd. Just because everyone is doing something does not make it right. Do not go along with them to twist the truth. But God will stand with you.

KEY 23:4: 'If thou meet thine enemy’s ox or his ass going astray, thou shalt surely bring it back to him again.' Going astray means wandering off, lost. If you find the lost animal of someone who hates you, take it back to him. This was a law of kindness to enemies, long before Jesus came. And Jesus taught it even more clearly. Love your enemies, do good to them which hate you. Kindness can turn an enemy into a friend.

KEY 23:20: 'Behold, I send an Angel before thee, to keep thee in the way, and to bring thee into the place which I have prepared.' God did not just give his people laws, and leave them to find their own way. He went ahead of them. He would keep them on the right path, and bring them to the place he had prepared. Jesus said something similar to his disciples. I go to prepare a place for you. God goes ahead of his people still.

THREAD: Jesus said, 'Love your enemies, bless them that curse you, do good to them that hate you'. And he said, 'I go to prepare a place for you.'

TODAY: If you see a crowd doing something wrong, have the courage not to join in. And think of someone who is unkind to you. Is there one small kind thing you could do for them today?

QUESTION: God says, thou shalt not follow a multitude to do evil. Why is it so hard to stand apart from the crowd, and what could help you to do what is right?

PRAYER: Lord God, give me courage not to follow the crowd when they do wrong. Help me to tell the truth, and to be fair to everyone. Help me to be kind even to those who are unkind to me. Thank you that you go ahead of me, to keep me in the way, and to bring me to the place you have prepared. Amen.
