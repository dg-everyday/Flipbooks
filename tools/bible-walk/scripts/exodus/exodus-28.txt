# Exodus 28: Aaron and his sons chosen as priests (a priest stands between God and the people), holy garments for glory and for beauty, made by the wise hearted filled with wisdom; the ephod with two onyx stones on the shoulders (six names each, a memorial), the breastplate of judgment with twelve stones in four rows (names of the tribes on his heart), Urim and Thummim (way of seeking God's answer; not fully known), the blue robe with pomegranates and golden bells, the gold plate HOLINESS TO THE LORD on the mitre, coats and bonnets for his sons. 28:42-43 not quoted. Jesus our great high priest (Hebrews 4:14-16), names on his heart; 1 Peter 2:9 a royal priesthood. Keys 28:2, 28:29, 28:36.
TITLE: Holiness to the LORD

HOOK: A man walks slowly towards the tabernacle, dressed in the most beautiful clothes anyone has ever seen. On his chest, twelve precious stones glitter in the sunlight: red, green, blue, purple and gold. On his shoulders, two dark onyx stones. On his forehead, a plate of pure gold. And around the hem of his blue robe, tiny golden bells ring softly with every step he takes. He is Aaron, the high priest. And everything he wears has a meaning.

WHERE: In chapter twenty seven, God described the bronze altar for sacrifices, standing just inside the courtyard. The courtyard had a white linen fence, and only one gate, wide and beautiful. And the people were to bring pure olive oil for the light, to cause the lamp to burn always.

Now, in chapter twenty eight, God chooses the priests who will serve in the tabernacle, and describes their special clothes. Listen for where Aaron carries the names of the twelve tribes. And listen for the words written on the golden plate.

STORY: God says to Moses, take Aaron your brother, and his sons, to serve me as priests. A priest is someone who stands between God and the people. Aaron would be the high priest, the chief priest. And his four sons would serve with him.

Then God says the priests need special clothes.

Who would make these garments? 'And thou shalt speak unto all that are wise hearted, whom I have filled with the spirit of wisdom, that they may make Aaron’s garments'. Skilled craftsmen and women, whom God himself had filled with wisdom and skill.

There were six main garments. A breastplate, an ephod, a robe, an embroidered coat, a turban called a mitre, and a sash called a girdle.

The ephod was like a sleeveless jacket or apron, made of gold thread and fine linen, woven with blue, purple and scarlet. On each shoulder was an onyx stone, set in gold. And on these two stones were engraved the names of the twelve sons of Jacob, six on each stone. 'and Aaron shall bear their names before the LORD upon his two shoulders for a memorial.' A memorial means a reminder. Whenever Aaron went before God, he carried all God’s people on his shoulders.

Then the breastplate. It was a square pocket of woven cloth, worn over Aaron’s chest. On the front were twelve precious stones, set in gold, in four rows of three. Sardius, topaz, carbuncle. Emerald, sapphire, diamond. Ligure, agate, amethyst. Beryl, onyx, jasper. Each stone had the name of one of the twelve tribes engraved on it. Every tribe was precious to God.

And then.

Inside the breastplate were two mysterious objects, called the Urim and the Thummim. We do not know exactly what they were. They seem to have been a way the high priest could ask God for answers, when Israel needed to know what to do.

Under the ephod, Aaron wore a long robe, all of blue. Around the hem were pomegranates, made of blue, purple and scarlet thread, and between them, little bells of gold. A golden bell and a pomegranate, a golden bell and a pomegranate, all the way around. 'and his sound shall be heard when he goeth in unto the holy place before the LORD, and when he cometh out, that he die not.' The people outside could hear the bells, and know that their high priest was still alive, serving God in the holy place on their behalf.

Then the golden plate.

The plate was tied with a blue cord to the front of Aaron’s turban, right on his forehead. 'that they may be accepted before the LORD.' Aaron was not perfect. As we will soon see, he sinned badly. But when he wore the plate, he stood before God as a holy priest, so that the people’s gifts could be accepted.

And Aaron’s sons also had coats, sashes and caps, 'for glory and for beauty.' Then they would be anointed with oil, and set apart for God’s service.

All of this was a picture. A picture of someone greater than Aaron. The letter to the Hebrews tells us that Jesus is our great high priest. Aaron carried the names of the tribes on his shoulders and his heart. Jesus carries all his people, every one of them, on his shoulders and on his heart. Aaron had to offer sacrifices for his own sins. Jesus never sinned. Aaron grew old and died. Jesus lives for ever, and is always praying for us. Aaron’s plate said, holiness to the LORD. Jesus is truly holy, and he makes us holy.

And there is more. Peter wrote that all who believe in Jesus are a royal priesthood. We too can bring others to God in prayer, and show them God’s love.

KEY 28:2: 'And thou shalt make holy garments for Aaron thy brother for glory and for beauty.' God cares about beauty. He did not just want plain, useful clothes. He wanted garments of glory and beauty, because they showed something of how glorious and beautiful God is. Holy means set apart for God. These clothes were not for everyday wear. They were only for serving God. And the Bible says that God gives his people garments of salvation, and robes of righteousness.

KEY 28:29: 'And Aaron shall bear the names of the children of Israel in the breastplate of judgment upon his heart, when he goeth in unto the holy place, for a memorial before the LORD continually.' Upon his heart. Every time Aaron went in before God, he carried the names of all God’s people over his heart. Not one tribe was left out. Jesus, our great high priest, carries your name on his heart. He knows you. He loves you. And he brings you before his Father, always.

KEY 28:36: 'And thou shalt make a plate of pure gold, and grave upon it, like the engravings of a signet, HOLINESS TO THE LORD.' A signet was a ring or seal used to stamp a name. These words were engraved deeply, so they could never be rubbed off. Holiness to the LORD means belonging completely to God, set apart for him. The high priest wore these words on his forehead, for everyone to see. Our lives, too, are meant to say, holiness to the LORD. Everything we think, and do, set apart for God.

THREAD: The letter to the Hebrews says, 'Seeing then that we have a great high priest, that is passed into the heavens, Jesus the Son of God, let us hold fast our profession.' And Peter wrote, 'But ye are a chosen generation, a royal priesthood, an holy nation, a peculiar people'.

TODAY: Think of the names of people you care about. Carry them on your heart today, as Aaron did, by praying for each one by name. And thank Jesus that he carries your name on his heart.

QUESTION: Aaron carried the names of all the tribes on his shoulders and on his heart. How does it make you feel to know that Jesus, our great high priest, carries your name before God?

PRAYER: Lord Jesus, thank you that you are our great high priest. Thank you that you carry my name on your heart, and that you always pray for me. Make my life holy to you, set apart for you. Help me to carry others to you in prayer, as Aaron carried the names of the tribes. Amen.
