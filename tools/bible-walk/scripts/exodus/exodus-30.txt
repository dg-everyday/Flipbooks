# Exodus 30: the altar of incense (gold, before the vail, sweet incense with the lamps; morning and even only inside quotes; no strange incense; atonement on its horns once a year), incense a picture of prayer (Psalm 141:2, Revelation 5:8, Zacharias in Luke 1:9-10); the half shekel ransom when the people are numbered, rich not more and poor not less (everyone equal before God, all need atonement); the laver of brass between the altar and the tent, wash hands and feet (Jesus washing feet, John 13; 1 John 1:7, 9); the holy anointing oil (myrrh, cinnamon, calamus, cassia) not to be copied; the holy incense (frankincense) not for themselves. Keys 30:7, 30:15, 30:20.
TITLE: Sweet Incense Before the LORD

HOOK: Imagine the holy place inside the tabernacle. There are no windows. The golden lampstand flickers with seven little flames, lighting up the golden walls. And in front of the great veil stands a small golden altar. A priest steps forward with burning coals, and sprinkles sweet spices on them. A cloud of fragrant smoke rises up, filling the room with a beautiful scent, and drifting up towards the veil, towards the presence of God.

WHERE: In chapter twenty nine, Aaron and his sons were washed, dressed and anointed as priests. They laid their hands on the head of a bull, as it died in their place. Blood was put on their right ear, their right thumb and their right big toe. Two lambs were to be offered every day, continually. And God promised, I will dwell among the children of Israel, and will be their God.

Now, in chapter thirty, God describes the last few objects for the tabernacle. Listen for what the incense is a picture of. And listen for one offering where the rich and the poor give exactly the same.

STORY: The first thing God describes is an altar of incense.

It was a small altar, one cubit square and two cubits high, about as tall as a child’s waist. Like the bronze altar outside, it had horns on its four corners. But this one was covered with pure gold, and it stood inside the holy place, right in front of the veil, near the ark and the mercy seat.

Incense is a mixture of spices and gums which, when sprinkled on hot coals, gives off a sweet smelling smoke. Every time Aaron tended the lamps, at the beginning and end of each day, he was to burn incense on this altar. 'a perpetual incense before the LORD throughout your generations.' Perpetual means never ending.

'Ye shall offer no strange incense thereon'. Strange incense means any incense other than the one God commanded. God was to be worshipped his way.

And once a year, the high priest put the blood of the sin offering on the horns of this golden altar, to make atonement. Even the incense altar needed to be made clean.

What was the incense a picture of? The Bible tells us. In Psalm one hundred and forty one, David prays, let my prayer be set forth before thee as incense. And in the book of Revelation, John sees golden bowls full of incense, which are the prayers of saints. The incense, rising up to God, was a picture of prayer, rising up to him. Many years later, a priest named Zacharias was burning incense in the temple, while the people prayed outside. And an angel appeared to him, to tell him that his prayer had been heard.

Then God gives a law about money. Whenever the people were counted, every man of twenty years old and over was to give an offering of half a shekel, a small silver coin. It was called a ransom for his soul, and atonement money. The money would be used for the service of the tabernacle.

And here is the remarkable thing.

Then God describes a large basin of bronze, called a laver. It stood between the bronze altar and the tent, filled with water. 'For Aaron and his sons shall wash their hands and their feet thereat'.

Then God gives the recipe for a holy anointing oil. Pure myrrh, sweet cinnamon, sweet calamus, which is a fragrant reed, and cassia, which is like cinnamon bark, all mixed with olive oil by an expert perfume maker. Moses was to anoint the tabernacle with it, and the ark, and the table, the lampstand, the altars, the laver, and all the tools. Everything was set apart as most holy. And Aaron and his sons were anointed with it too.

But this oil was not to be copied, or used for anything else. 'it is holy, and it shall be holy unto you.' It belonged to God alone.

And finally, God gives the recipe for the holy incense. Sweet spices, mixed with pure frankincense, in equal amounts, beaten into a fine powder. It too was holy, and was not to be made for anyone’s own pleasure.

Do you remember the wise men who came to visit the baby Jesus? They brought him gold, frankincense and myrrh. Gold for a king. Frankincense, used in the holy incense, for one who would be our great high priest. And myrrh, used in the holy anointing oil, and also for burial, for one who would die for us.

So in this chapter we have three pictures. The golden altar, where incense rose like prayer. The half shekel, showing that everyone is equal before God. And the laver, where the priests washed. All of them point to how we can come to God: through prayer, as people equally in need of his mercy, and made clean by him.

KEY 30:7: 'And Aaron shall burn thereon sweet incense every morning: when he dresseth the lamps, he shall burn incense upon it.' Dresseth means trims, takes care of. The incense and the lamps went together. Light and prayer. Every day, at the start and at the end, prayer rose up before God. Just like the incense, our prayers rise up to God like a sweet smell. He loves to hear them. And we can pray at any time, not just at set times.

KEY 30:15: 'The rich shall not give more, and the poor shall not give less than half a shekel, when they give an offering unto the LORD, to make an atonement for your souls.' Every person was worth the same before God. The rich could not buy a better place with God by giving more. And the poor were not shut out. Everyone needed atonement in the same way. Paul wrote, for all have sinned, and come short of the glory of God. And the same Jesus died for rich and poor alike.

KEY 30:20: 'When they go into the tabernacle of the congregation, they shall wash with water, that they die not; or when they come near to the altar to minister'. The priests had already been washed all over when they were made priests. But every time they served, they washed their hands and feet again. Walking through the dusty desert, their feet got dirty. Working at the altar, their hands got dirty. When Jesus washed his disciples’ feet, he told Peter, he that is washed needeth not save to wash his feet. Once we trust Jesus, we are clean. But every day, we need to confess our sins, and be washed again.

THREAD: David prayed, 'Let my prayer be set forth before thee as incense'. And John wrote, 'If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.'

TODAY: Let your prayers rise up to God like incense today, at the beginning and the end of your day. And if you have done something wrong, confess it, and let God wash you clean.

QUESTION: In the half shekel offering, the rich gave no more and the poor gave no less. What does that teach us about how God sees every person?

PRAYER: Lord God, let my prayer be set forth before you as incense. Thank you that you hear every prayer. Thank you that rich and poor are equal before you, and that Jesus died for everyone. When I get dirty with sin, wash me, and make me clean again. And keep my heart full of prayer and praise. Amen.
