TITLE: Meet the Book of Ezra

HOOK: A father stands with his children at the edge of a great city, far from home. He points west, across the desert. Over there, he says, is the land God gave our family. Your grandparents were taken from it, a long time ago. Our city was burned, and our temple destroyed. But now the king has said we can go home. And we are going to build the house of God again.

BODY: Welcome to the book of Ezra, on The Living Voice. Ezra has ten chapters, so on this walk it is ten steps, one chapter at a time. And when we finish, we will walk straight on into the book of Nehemiah, which carries on the same story.

At the end of the second book of Kings, Jerusalem lay in ruins. The temple was burned, the walls broken down, and the people carried away to Babylon. But in the very last verses, a captive king of David’s family was lifted out of prison, and spoken to kindly. It was a small glimmer of hope.

The book of Ezra shows that hope growing. Babylon has fallen to the Persians, and the Persian king Cyrus makes an announcement. The Jews may go home, and rebuild the house of their God. Long before, the prophet Jeremiah had said that the exile would not last for ever. Now God was keeping his word.

The first part of the book tells of the first group who go home, led by a man named Zerubbabel, from David’s family, and Jeshua the priest. They build an altar, and lay the foundations of the temple. But enemies oppose them, and the work stops for years. Then two prophets, Haggai and Zechariah, stir the people to start again, and at last the temple is finished.

Then the story jumps forward many years. Do you remember Esther, the young Jewish queen of Persia, who saved her people? Her story happened in the gap between the sixth and seventh chapters of Ezra. After that, a man named Ezra comes to Jerusalem. He is a priest, and a scribe, a teacher of God’s law. And he brings God’s Word back to the centre of the people’s lives.

Here are three things to watch for as we walk.

Watch for how God moves the hearts of kings. Cyrus, Darius, Artaxerxes. These were the most powerful men on earth, and none of them worshipped the LORD. And yet again and again we read that God stirred up their spirits, or turned their hearts, to help his people.

Watch for the hand of God. Ezra loves the phrase, the good hand of my God upon me. Listen for it. It is his way of saying that every good thing that happened was God’s doing.

And watch for God’s Word. The people do not only rebuild a building. They rebuild their lives around what God has said. Ezra, we are told, prepared his heart to seek the law of the LORD, and to do it, and to teach it.

A gentle word before we begin. The last two chapters of Ezra tell of a hard and painful decision about marriages, and families. We will tell it gently and honestly, and explain why it happened.

QUESTION: The people of Ezra’s day came home to ruins, and began to rebuild. Is there anything in your life that God might want to help you rebuild?

PRAYER: Lord God, as I walk through the book of Ezra, show me your faithfulness. Thank you that you keep your promises, even after long years of waiting. Thank you that you can move the hearts of kings. Let your good hand be upon me, and help me to seek your Word, to do it, and to share it. Amen.
