# Ezra 2: the list of those who returned, every one unto his city, Zerubbabel and Jeshua (and other leaders), families counted, the men of Bethlehem, Anathoth and others, priests, Levites, singers, porters, Nethinims, Solomon's servants, those who could not shew their father's house, the priests whose register was not found (Urim and Thummim), the whole congregation (inside the quote), servants and singers, the animals, the freewill offerings, dwelt in their cities. Keys 2:1, 2:21, 2:68.
TITLE: Every One unto His City

HOOK: Imagine someone reading out a list at a great gathering. Names, and numbers. The family of Parosh. The family of Arah. The men of Bethlehem. On and on. Most of us would stop listening after a minute. But now imagine you are in the crowd, and you hear your own family’s name read out. Suddenly the list is not boring at all. It is about you. You belong.

WHERE: In chapter one, the LORD stirred up the spirit of Cyrus, king of Persia, to let the Jews go home and rebuild the house of God, just as Jeremiah had promised. Those whose spirit God had raised got ready to go, and Cyrus gave back the gold and silver vessels Nebuchadnezzar had taken from the temple.

Now, in chapter two, we hear who went. It is mostly a list of names and numbers. But listen carefully, because it tells us a great deal about God and his people.

STORY: The chapter begins.

These were the people who left Babylon and crossed the desert, a journey of many weeks, to a land most of them had never seen. They were led by Zerubbabel, a descendant of king David, and Jeshua, the high priest, and other leaders.

Then comes the list. First, families, named after their ancestors. The children of Parosh. The children of Shephatiah. The children of Arah. And for each family, a number: how many men went home. Some families sent thousands. Some sent fewer than a hundred.

Then come people named after their home towns. The men of Netophah. The men of Anathoth, where Jeremiah came from. The men of Bethel and Ai. And one town in particular.

Then the list turns to those who served in God’s house. First, the priests, from four families. Then the Levites, the helpers in the temple. Then the singers, the children of Asaph, who led the worship, just as their ancestors had done in the days of David. Then the porters, or gatekeepers. Then the Nethinims, temple servants who did the hard practical work. And the children of Solomon’s servants.

Notice how many different kinds of people there are. Leaders and labourers. Priests and singers. Doorkeepers and servants. Every one of them was needed. Every one was counted.

Then something sad. Some people came who could not prove which family they belonged to. Their records had been lost in the exile. And some priests could not find their names in the family registers. So they were not allowed to serve as priests, until a priest could ask God for an answer, with the Urim and Thummim, sacred lots used to seek God’s will. Belonging to God’s people mattered. And for priests, it mattered very much.

Then the writer adds up the total. 'The whole congregation together was forty and two thousand three hundred and threescore'. Besides their servants, and two hundred men and women singers. They even counted the animals: horses, mules, camels and donkeys. Hundreds of camels and thousands of donkeys, carrying everything they owned across the desert.

When they finally reach Jerusalem, they see the ruins of the temple. And some of the leaders do something beautiful.

They gave gold, silver, and garments for the priests. Then the priests, the Levites, the singers, the gatekeepers, the temple servants and all the people settled in their towns. Every family back in its own place.

The writer could simply have said, about fifty thousand people went home. But he did not. He wrote down families, towns and numbers, and kept the list. Because to God, his people are not just a crowd. They are families, with names. Each one matters.

KEY 2:1: 'Now these are the children of the province that went up out of the captivity, of those which had been carried away, whom Nebuchadnezzar the king of Babylon had carried away unto Babylon, and came again unto Jerusalem and Judah, every one unto his city'. Every one unto his city. Nebuchadnezzar had carried them away. But God brought them back, each one to their own home town. The exile had not erased who they were. God had kept track of every family, and every town. He knows where each of his people belongs.

KEY 2:21: 'The children of Bethlehem, an hundred twenty and three.' A small line in a long list. Just a hundred and twenty three men from the little town of Bethlehem. Ruth and Boaz had lived there. David had been born there. And here, a small group of Bethlehem families went home. Many years later, in that same little town, a baby would be born, of David’s family, in a manger. Small things in God’s hands can lead to wonderful things.

KEY 2:68: 'And some of the chief of the fathers, when they came to the house of the LORD which is at Jerusalem, offered freely for the house of God to set it up in his place'. The temple was still just a ruin. There was nothing to see yet. But some of the leaders gave freely, right away. They did not wait for the building to be finished before they gave. They gave to start it. Giving to God is an act of faith in what he is going to do.

THREAD: Isaiah heard God say, 'I have called thee by thy name; thou art mine.' And Jesus said, 'rejoice, because your names are written in heaven.'

TODAY: Remember today that God knows your name. You are not a number in a crowd to him. And think about your place among God’s people. Like the singers, the gatekeepers and the servants, how could you serve?

QUESTION: Every family and town was counted and named in Ezra’s list. How does it feel to know that God knows you by name?

PRAYER: Lord God, thank you that you know my name, and that I belong to you. Thank you that you kept track of every family through the long years of exile, and brought them home. Show me my place among your people, and help me to serve you gladly, and to give freely for your work. Amen.
