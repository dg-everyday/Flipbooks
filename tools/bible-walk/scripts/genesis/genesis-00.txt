TITLE: Meet the Book of Genesis

HOOK: Before there were nations, before there were kings, before there was a single word written down, there was God. The first book of the Bible begins with four words that reach back further than any history: in the beginning God. And it ends, fifty chapters later, with a family in Egypt, holding on to a promise.

BODY: Welcome to the book of Genesis, on The Living Voice. It has fifty chapters, so on this walk it is fifty steps, one chapter at a time.

The word Genesis means origin, or beginning. In Hebrew, the book is named after its first words, in the beginning. And that is what it is: the book of beginnings. Here we find the beginning of the world, of the human family, of sin and sorrow, and of God’s plan to put things right.

Who wrote it? Jewish and Christian tradition have long credited the first five books of the Bible to Moses, and Jesus himself spoke of the law of Moses. Genesis was given to God’s people so they would know who made them, why the world is broken, and why God chose one family to bless the whole earth.

The book falls into two great parts.

Chapters one to eleven tell the story of the whole human race. God creates the heaven and the earth, and makes man and woman in his image. Adam and Eve disobey God, and sin enters the world. Cain kills his brother Abel. The earth fills with violence, and God sends a great flood, but Noah finds grace in his eyes. Then people build a tower to make a name for themselves, and God scatters them across the earth.

Then, from chapter twelve to the end, the story narrows to one man and his family. God calls Abraham and makes him a promise: 'in thee shall all families of the earth be blessed.' The rest of the book follows that promise as it passes from Abraham to his son Isaac, to his grandson Jacob, and to Jacob’s twelve sons. The last part is the story of Joseph, sold by his brothers, raised up by God, and used to save many lives.

Here are four things to watch for as we walk.

Watch for the word blessing. God blesses what he makes. Sin brings a curse. But again and again, God speaks blessing over people who do not deserve it.

Watch for promises. God makes covenants, solemn promises, with Noah, with Abraham, with Isaac and with Jacob. And he always keeps his word, even when it seems impossible.

Watch for ordinary, flawed people. The heroes of Genesis lie, scheme, doubt and quarrel. Genesis does not hide their faults. It shows how patient God is with them.

And watch for the promised seed. In chapter three, God promises that the seed of the woman will one day crush the serpent’s head. The whole Bible follows that line, until it comes to Jesus.

Genesis ends with these words of Joseph to his brothers: 'ye thought evil against me; but God meant it unto good'. It is the story of the whole book. People fail, but God is never defeated.

QUESTION: Genesis is the book of beginnings. Where in your own life do you need God to make a new beginning?

PRAYER: Lord God, you were there in the beginning, before anything was made. As I walk through the book of Genesis, show me who you are, help me to trust your promises, and teach me that you can bring good out of every story. Amen.
