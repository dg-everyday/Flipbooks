# Genesis 1: creation in six days, man and woman in God's image, very good. Keys 1:1, 1:26, 1:31.
TITLE: And God Saw That It Was Good

HOOK: There is no light. There is no sound. There is no land, no sky, no living thing. There is only deep water, and darkness over it. Then a voice speaks into the dark, and everything changes. 'Let there be light: and there was light.'

WHERE: This is the first step of our walk through Genesis, and the first page of the Bible. In the introduction we heard that Genesis is the book of beginnings. Here everything begins.

Christians have understood the days of this chapter in different ways. But the chapter is perfectly clear about the things that matter most: who made everything, how he made it, and what he thinks of it. As you listen, notice the pattern that repeats like a song. God said. And it was so. And God saw that it was good.

STORY: The chapter opens before anything else exists. 'And the earth was without form, and void; and darkness was upon the face of the deep. And the Spirit of God moved upon the face of the waters.' The world is empty and shapeless. But God is already there.

On the first day, God speaks, and there is light. He sees that the light is good, and he divides the light from the darkness. 'And God called the light Day, and the darkness he called Night.'

On the second day, God makes the firmament, the wide expanse of the sky. He separates the waters below from the waters above, and he calls the firmament Heaven.

On the third day, God gathers the waters under the heaven into one place, and dry land appears. He calls the dry land Earth, and the waters Seas. Then he says, let the earth bring forth grass, and plants, and trees bearing fruit, each with its own seed in it. And the earth turns green. Notice the phrase after his kind. Every living thing is made to bring forth more of its own kind. God builds life into the world, so that it can go on and on.

On the fourth day, God sets lights in the sky: 'the greater light to rule the day, and the lesser light to rule the night: he made the stars also.' In the ancient world, many people worshipped the sun and the moon. Genesis calls them simply lights, made by God and put in their place, to give light on the earth and to mark out days and seasons and years.

On the fifth day, God fills the waters and the sky. Great whales and every creature that swims fill the seas, and birds fly across the open sky. For the first time, God blesses what he has made: be fruitful, and multiply.

On the sixth day, God makes the animals of the land: cattle, and creeping things, and wild beasts, each after his kind. And God sees that it is good.

And then something new happens. Until now, God has said, let there be, and let the earth bring forth. Now he speaks as though taking counsel: let us make man. Of all the creatures, only one is made in the image of God.

'So God created man in his own image, in the image of God created he him; male and female created he them.' God blesses them. He tells them to be fruitful, and fill the earth, and take care of it. He gives them every plant and every fruit tree for food, and green plants for the animals and the birds.

Then God looks at everything he has made. Six times in this chapter, God has seen that something was good. Now he looks at all of it together, and the verdict is greater still.

KEY 1:1: 'In the beginning God created the heaven and the earth.' These are the first words of the Bible, and they answer the biggest question anyone can ask: where did everything come from? The answer is not chance, and not a battle between gods. The answer is one God, who was there before the beginning. Nothing made itself. Everything that exists, from the farthest star to the breath in your lungs, was made by him. That means your life is not an accident. Begin where the Bible begins, with God.

KEY 1:26: 'And God said, Let us make man in our image, after our likeness: and let them have dominion over the fish of the sea, and over the fowl of the air, and over the cattle, and over all the earth,' Every human being carries the image of God. That is why every person matters, whatever their age, their nation, or their abilities. And we are given a task. Dominion does not mean spoiling the world. It means caring for it on God’s behalf, the way a good king cares for his land. We are stewards of a world that belongs to God.

KEY 1:31: 'And God saw every thing that he had made, and, behold, it was very good.' Not just good. Very good. Before there was any sin or sorrow, the world was exactly as God meant it to be. Whatever is broken in the world now, it was not made that way. And the whole Bible is the story of how God sets out to make his world very good again.

THREAD: The Gospel of John begins with the same words: 'In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God.' John tells us that 'All things were made by him', and that the Word became flesh in Jesus. The God who spoke light into the darkness came to his world as the light of the world.

TODAY: Look around you today, at the sky, at a tree, at another person. Each one was made by God. Thank him for something he has made. And when you meet someone, remember that they too are made in the image of God.

QUESTION: God made man and woman in his own image. How should knowing that change the way you see yourself, and the people around you?

PRAYER: Lord God, Creator of heaven and earth, you spoke, and it was so. Thank you for making me in your image, and for giving me life and breath. Help me to care for your world, to honour every person I meet, and to put you first in everything. Amen.
