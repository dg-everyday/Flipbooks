# Genesis 2: the seventh day, man formed from dust, the garden, the two trees, the woman. Keys 2:7, 2:15, 2:18.
TITLE: The Breath of Life

HOOK: A man lies on the ground, shaped from the dust of the earth. He is perfectly formed, but he does not move. He does not breathe. Then God bends down, and breathes into him. And the first man opens his eyes on a world that is brand new.

WHERE: In chapter one, God made the heaven and the earth in six days. He spoke, and light shone. He filled the sea, the sky and the land with living things. He made man and woman in his own image, and he saw that everything he had made was very good.

Now, in chapter two, the story slows down and comes closer. Chapter one showed us the whole world from far above. Chapter two takes us down into one garden, to meet the first man and the first woman up close. And instead of simply God, we now hear a new name: the LORD God. This is the personal name of the God who makes promises and keeps them.

STORY: The heavens and the earth are finished. On the seventh day, God ends his work, and he rests. 'And God blessed the seventh day, and sanctified it: because that in it he had rested from all his work which God created and made.' God does not rest because he is tired. He rests because the work is complete, and it is very good.

Then the chapter tells us how the LORD God made the man. There is no rain yet, and no one to work the ground. A mist goes up from the earth and waters it. And the LORD God forms man from the dust of the ground, and breathes life into him.

The LORD God plants a garden eastward in Eden, and there he puts the man he has formed. Out of the ground he makes every tree grow that is pleasant to look at and good for food. In the middle of the garden stand two special trees: the tree of life, and the tree of the knowledge of good and evil.

A river flows out of Eden to water the garden, and from there it divides into four rivers. One flows through a land of gold. Two of the names, the Hiddekel and the Euphrates, are rivers that still flow through the lands we now call Iraq.

The LORD God gives the man work to do, and a command to keep. 'Of every tree of the garden thou mayest freely eat: But of the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, thou shalt not eat of it: for in the day that thou eatest thereof thou shalt surely die.' Notice how generous God is. Every tree in the garden is open to the man, except one. One command, and a clear warning. God wants the man to trust him.

Then, for the first time, God says that something is not good.

So the LORD God forms every beast of the field and every bird of the air, and brings them to the man, to see what he will call them. Whatever the man calls each living creature, that is its name. Adam names the cattle, the birds and every beast. But among all the animals, there is not found a help meet for him. There is no one like him.

So the LORD God causes a deep sleep to fall upon Adam. He takes one of his ribs, and closes up the flesh, and from the rib he makes a woman, and brings her to the man. And Adam speaks the first words of joy recorded in the Bible: 'This is now bone of my bones, and flesh of my flesh: she shall be called Woman, because she was taken out of Man.'

The chapter explains that this is why a man leaves his father and his mother, and shall cleave unto his wife: 'and they shall be one flesh.' And the chapter ends with a picture of innocence. The man and his wife are together in the garden, with nothing to hide and nothing to be ashamed of.

KEY 2:7: 'And the LORD God formed man of the dust of the ground, and breathed into his nostrils the breath of life; and man became a living soul.' Two things are true about us. We are made of dust, so we are small and frail. And we carry the breath of God, so we are precious. Every breath you take today is a gift from the One who first breathed life into Adam. We are not just animals, and we are not gods. We are creatures who live by the breath of God.

KEY 2:15: 'And the LORD God took the man, and put him into the garden of Eden to dress it and to keep it.' Before there was any sin, there was work. Adam was given a garden to tend and to guard. Work is not a punishment. It is part of God’s good design. Whatever honest work you do, at home, at school, or in a job, you can do it as a gift to God, caring for the part of his world that he has placed in your hands.

KEY 2:18: 'And the LORD God said, It is not good that the man should be alone; I will make him an help meet for him.' In a perfect world, with God himself as his friend, the man still needed another person. We are made for each other. A help meet means a helper suited to him, a partner, his equal. The same word helper is used of God himself in the Psalms, so there is nothing small about it. God made us to live in love and friendship, not alone.

THREAD: When Jesus was asked about marriage, he went back to this chapter. He said, 'Have ye not read, that he which made them at the beginning made them male and female,' and 'they twain shall be one flesh'. And at the very end of the Bible, the tree of life appears again, in the city of God, 'and the leaves of the tree were for the healing of the nations.'

TODAY: Take a slow breath, and thank God for it. Then think about the people he has placed beside you, family, friends, neighbours. Do something today to show one of them that they are not alone.

QUESTION: God said, it is not good that the man should be alone. Who has God put in your life, and who might need you to be a friend to them?

PRAYER: Lord God, you formed me from the dust and breathed into me the breath of life. Thank you for my life, for my work, and for the people you have given me. Help me to care for what you have placed in my hands, and to love others as you have loved me. Amen.
