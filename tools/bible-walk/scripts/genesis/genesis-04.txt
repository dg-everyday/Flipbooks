# Genesis 4: Cain and Abel, sin at the door, the mark of Cain, Lamech, Seth, calling on the LORD. Keys 4:7, 4:9, 4:26.
TITLE: Am I My Brother’s Keeper?

HOOK: Two brothers stand before God, each with an offering. One is a farmer, the other a shepherd. God looks with favour on one offering, but not on the other. And in one brother’s heart, a dark feeling begins to grow. God sees it, and warns him. Will he listen?

WHERE: In chapter three, the serpent lied to the woman, and Adam and Eve ate from the forbidden tree. They hid from God, and he called out, where art thou? God judged their sin, but he also promised that the seed of the woman would one day crush the serpent’s head. Then he clothed them, and sent them out of the garden.

Now, in chapter four, we see what happens to the world outside Eden. Sin does not stay small. In one generation, disobedience becomes jealousy, and jealousy becomes murder. But we also see that God does not give up on people.

STORY: Adam and Eve have a son, and Eve names him Cain. She says, 'I have gotten a man from the LORD.' Then she bears his brother Abel. Abel becomes a keeper of sheep, and Cain a tiller of the ground.

In time, both brothers bring an offering to the LORD. Cain brings some of the fruit of the ground. Abel brings the firstlings of his flock, and the best portions. 'And the LORD had respect unto Abel and to his offering: But unto Cain and to his offering he had not respect.'

Genesis does not tell us everything about why. But later the Bible says that Abel offered by faith, and that Cain’s works were evil. The difference was not just in the gift. It was in the heart.

Cain is very angry, and his face falls. And God, in his mercy, speaks to Cain. He asks, why are you angry? Why has your face fallen? Then he gives a warning.

Cain does not listen. He talks with Abel his brother. And when they are in the field, Cain rises up against Abel, and kills him.

Then God asks a question, just as he asked Adam, where art thou?

God says, 'What hast thou done? the voice of thy brother’s blood crieth unto me from the ground.' Cain is cursed from the earth that received his brother. When he tills the ground, it will no longer yield its strength to him. He will be a fugitive and a vagabond on the earth.

Cain cries out, 'My punishment is greater than I can bear.' He is afraid that whoever finds him will kill him. And God shows mercy even to Cain. He sets a mark on him, so that no one who finds him will kill him. Cain goes out from the presence of the LORD, and lives in the land of Nod, east of Eden.

Cain has a son named Enoch, and builds a city. His family grows. Some of his descendants become the first to live in tents and keep cattle, the first musicians with the harp and the organ, and the first workers in brass and iron. But one of them, Lamech, is even worse than Cain. He takes two wives, and boasts to them that he has killed a young man who wounded him. If Cain will be avenged sevenfold, he says, Lamech will be avenged seventy and sevenfold. Sin is spreading, and it is getting proud.

But the chapter does not end there. Adam and Eve have another son, and call him Seth, because, Eve says, God has appointed me another seed instead of Abel. And when Seth has a son, people begin to turn back to God.

KEY 4:7: 'If thou doest well, shalt thou not be accepted? and if thou doest not well, sin lieth at the door. And unto thee shall be his desire, and thou shalt rule over him.' This is the first time the word sin appears in the Bible, and it is pictured like a wild animal, crouching at the door, waiting to pounce. God did not leave Cain without help. He warned him. You can still do well. You can master this. Anger and jealousy are not sin yet, but they open the door. God calls us to deal with them before they deal with us.

KEY 4:9: 'And the LORD said unto Cain, Where is Abel thy brother? And he said, I know not: Am I my brother’s keeper?' Cain answered God with a lie and a shrug. But the answer to his question is yes. We are responsible for one another. God made us to care for each other, not to turn away. Whenever we say, that is not my problem, about someone in need, we are speaking Cain’s words. God still asks each of us, where is your brother? Where is your sister?

KEY 4:26: 'And to Seth, to him also there was born a son; and he called his name Enos: then began men to call upon the name of the LORD.' After a chapter of jealousy, murder and boasting, there is a ray of hope. Through Seth, God keeps a line of people who pray. To call upon the name of the LORD means to worship him, to pray to him, and to depend on him. In a world that was going wrong, some people turned to God. That is always the way back.

THREAD: Jesus spoke of 'the blood of righteous Abel'. And the letter to the Hebrews says, 'By faith Abel offered unto God a more excellent sacrifice than Cain'. It also says that the blood of Jesus 'speaketh better things than that of Abel.' Abel’s blood cried out for justice. The blood of Jesus speaks of mercy and forgiveness.

TODAY: Is there anger or jealousy crouching at the door of your heart? Do not let it in. Bring it to God, and ask him to help you master it. And think of one person you could look out for today, as your brother’s or your sister’s keeper.

QUESTION: God warned Cain that sin was crouching at the door. What feelings do you need to bring to God before they grow into something worse?

PRAYER: Lord God, you see my heart, even the anger and jealousy I try to hide. Help me to listen when you warn me, and to rule over sin by your strength. Make me a keeper of my brothers and sisters, and help me always to call upon your name. Amen.
