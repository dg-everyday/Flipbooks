# Genesis 5: the generations from Adam to Noah, Enoch walked with God, Methuselah, Noah's name. Keys 5:22, 5:24, 5:29.
TITLE: Enoch Walked with God

HOOK: A list of names. Ten generations, one after another. Each man lives, has children, and then come the same three words, again and again, like the toll of a bell: and he died. And he died. And he died. Until suddenly, in the middle of the list, there is a man who does not die at all.

WHERE: In chapter four, outside the garden, sin spread quickly. Cain killed his brother Abel, and his descendant Lamech boasted of killing too. But Adam and Eve had another son, Seth, and in Seth’s family, people began to call upon the name of the LORD.

Now, in chapter five, Genesis follows Seth’s family line, all the way from Adam to Noah. It may sound like just a list of names. But listen closely. There is a story hidden in this list, and a hope.

STORY: The chapter begins by looking back to creation. 'This is the book of the generations of Adam. In the day that God created man, in the likeness of God made he him; Male and female created he them; and blessed them'. Even after the fall, the image of God has not been lost. Adam has a son in his own likeness, after his image, and calls him Seth.

Then the list begins. Each entry follows the same pattern. A man lives a certain number of years, and has a son. He lives on, and has other sons and daughters. And then we are told how long he lived in all, and those three heavy words: and he died.

Adam lives nine hundred and thirty years, and he died. Seth lives nine hundred and twelve years, and he died. Then Enos, the son in whose time men began to call upon the name of the LORD. Then Cainan, Mahalaleel and Jared. Each one lives an astonishingly long life. And each one dies.

God had warned Adam, in the day you eat of it, you shall surely die. Here we see that warning coming true, generation after generation. The long lives show the strength of the first people, so close to the beginning. Adam would have lived to see many of his great-great-grandchildren. But no one, however long they live, escapes death. Sin has brought death into God’s good world, just as he warned.

Then we come to the seventh name from Adam, a man named Enoch. And the pattern breaks.

Enoch lives sixty and five years, and has a son named Methuselah. And then, instead of saying, Enoch lived, Genesis says something new.

Enoch walks with God for three hundred years. And then, instead of the words and he died, we read something wonderful.

Enoch’s son, Methuselah, lives longer than anyone else in the Bible: nine hundred sixty and nine years. And he died. Methuselah has a son named Lamech. This is not the proud Lamech of Cain’s family in chapter four, but a different man, in Seth’s line. And this Lamech has a son, and gives him a name full of hope.

Noah is five hundred years old when he has three sons: Shem, Ham and Japheth. And with that, the list ends, waiting for the next chapter, and for the great story of Noah.

KEY 5:22: 'And Enoch walked with God after he begat Methuselah three hundred years, and begat sons and daughters:' Notice where Enoch walked with God. Not in a monastery or far away from people, but in ordinary family life, raising sons and daughters. To walk with someone means to go in the same direction, at the same pace, day after day, in friendship. Enoch did not walk with God for one great moment. He walked with him for three hundred years. Faith is a daily walk, in the middle of ordinary life.

KEY 5:24: 'And Enoch walked with God: and he was not; for God took him.' In a chapter that rings with and he died, here is a man who did not die. God simply took him. One old saying puts it like this: God and Enoch walked together so long, that one day God said, we are closer to my home than to yours, come home with me. In the middle of a list of death, God gives a glimpse of hope. Death does not have the last word for those who walk with God.

KEY 5:29: 'And he called his name Noah, saying, This same shall comfort us concerning our work and toil of our hands, because of the ground which the LORD hath cursed.' The name Noah sounds like the Hebrew word for rest or comfort. Lamech remembered the curse on the ground from chapter three, and the hard toil it brought. He longed for someone who would bring comfort. He did not know how God would use his son. But his hope was not misplaced. Through Noah, God would bring a new beginning.

THREAD: The letter to the Hebrews says, 'By faith Enoch was translated that he should not see death'. It adds that 'before his translation he had this testimony, that he pleased God.' And Jesus speaks to every tired and burdened heart, as Lamech once was: 'Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest.'

TODAY: You do not need to do something great to walk with God. Walk with him in the ordinary things today: your work, your family, your conversations. Talk to him as you go. Ask him to set the direction and the pace.

QUESTION: Enoch walked with God through ordinary family life, day after day. What would it look like for you to walk with God this week?

PRAYER: Lord God, thank you that you want to walk with me, as you walked with Enoch. Help me to go in your direction and keep in step with you, in the ordinary days as well as the great ones. Thank you that death does not have the last word for those who belong to you. Amen.
