# Genesis 7: into the ark, the LORD shut him in, the fountains of the deep, the waters prevail. Keys 7:1, 7:16, 7:23.
TITLE: The LORD Shut Him In

HOOK: The animals come. Pairs of them, from every direction: birds and cattle, creeping things and beasts, walking and flying toward the great ship. Noah and his family step inside. And then the door closes. Not Noah, but God himself shuts it. Seven days later, the sky breaks open.

WHERE: In chapter six, the earth was filled with violence, and God was grieved at his heart. But Noah found grace in the eyes of the LORD. God told him to build an ark, and promised to establish his covenant with him. And Noah did all that God commanded him.

Now, in chapter seven, the waiting is over. The flood comes. As you listen, notice how the chapter keeps repeating that Noah did as God commanded, and how carefully it counts the days. This is told as something that really happened, at a real time, to real people.

STORY: The ark is finished. Now God speaks to Noah again, and invites him in.

God tells Noah to take with him seven of every clean beast, the kinds later used for offerings, and two of the beasts that are not clean, male and female. And seven of the birds of the air, to keep their kind alive on the face of all the earth. For in seven days, God says, 'I will cause it to rain upon the earth forty days and forty nights'. And Noah does all that the LORD commanded him.

Noah is six hundred years old when the flood of waters comes upon the earth. He goes into the ark with his sons, his wife, and his sons’ wives, because of the waters of the flood. Of clean beasts and of beasts that are not clean, of birds and of everything that creeps upon the earth, they go in two by two, male and female, as God commanded Noah.

After seven days, the waters of the flood come. Genesis gives the exact day, in the six hundredth year of Noah’s life, in the second month, on the seventeenth day of the month. 'the same day were all the fountains of the great deep broken up, and the windows of heaven were opened.' Water bursts up from below, and pours down from above. It is as though the separating of the waters on the second day of creation is being undone.

The rain falls upon the earth 'forty days and forty nights.' On that very same day, Noah and his family enter the ark, with every beast after his kind, every bird of every sort, and all flesh in which is the breath of life. And then the LORD shuts the door.

The flood is upon the earth for forty days. The waters increase, and lift up the ark, and it rises above the earth. The waters prevail, and increase greatly, and the ark goes upon the face of the waters. Higher and higher the waters rise, until all the high hills under the whole heaven are covered. The waters rise fifteen cubits above the mountains.

All flesh that moves upon the earth dies: birds, cattle, beasts, every creeping thing, and every man. Everything on the dry land in whose nostrils is the breath of life dies.

The ark floats on alone, over a silent world.

And the waters prevail upon the earth for a hundred and fifty days.

KEY 7:1: 'And the LORD said unto Noah, Come thou and all thy house into the ark; for thee have I seen righteous before me in this generation.' Notice God’s word: come. Not go into the ark, but come. God was inviting Noah into a place where God himself would be with him. And the invitation was for his whole house, his whole family. God’s salvation was never meant for Noah alone. The door of the ark was open, and God called them in.

KEY 7:16: 'And they that went in, went in male and female of all flesh, as God had commanded him: and the LORD shut him in.' Noah did not have to close the door himself. God did it. Noah was safe, not because of how strong the ark was, or how tightly he could close it, but because God had shut him in. The same door that kept the waters out kept Noah safe within. When God holds you, nothing can wash you away.

KEY 7:23: 'And every living substance was destroyed which was upon the face of the ground, both man, and cattle, and the creeping things, and the fowl of the heaven; and they were destroyed from the earth: and Noah only remained alive, and they that were with him in the ark.' This is a sobering verse. God’s judgment on sin is real. But look at the end of the verse. Noah only remained alive, and they that were with him in the ark. There was one place of safety, and everyone inside it was saved. The flood shows us both the seriousness of sin and the certainty of God’s rescue.

THREAD: The apostle Peter compared the ark to salvation in Jesus. He wrote of 'the days of Noah, while the ark was a preparing, wherein few, that is, eight souls were saved by water.' And Jesus said, 'I am the door: by me if any man enter in, he shall be saved'. Just as there was one door into the ark, Jesus is the way to safety with God.

TODAY: What storms are rising around you? Remember that your safety does not depend on how strong you are, but on the God who shuts you in. Thank him that, in Jesus, you are held safe. And if you have never come in through the door, he is still saying, come.

QUESTION: God shut Noah into the ark and kept him safe through the flood. What does it mean to you that God holds you safe in the storms of life?

PRAYER: Lord God, thank you that you called Noah and his family to come into the ark, and that you shut him in and kept him safe. Thank you for Jesus, the door to salvation. Keep me safe in you, whatever storms may rise. Amen.
