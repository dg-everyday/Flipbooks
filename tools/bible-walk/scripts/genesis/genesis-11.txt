# Genesis 11: one language, the tower of Babel, confusion and scattering, Shem to Terah, Abram and Sarai. Keys 11:4, 11:9, 11:30.
TITLE: Let Us Make Us a Name

HOOK: On a wide, flat plain, thousands of people are hard at work. They are baking bricks in the fire and stacking them higher and higher. Their plan is bold: a city, and a tower that will reach into the heavens. Everyone speaks the same language, and nothing seems impossible. But they have left someone out of their plans.

WHERE: In chapter ten, we heard the table of nations. The sons of Noah, Shem, Ham and Japheth, became about seventy peoples, spreading out across the earth, each with its own land and language. We met Nimrod, a mighty hunter, whose kingdom began in Babel. And we heard of Peleg, in whose days the earth was divided.

Now, in chapter eleven, we go back to see how that division happened. It is the last of the great stories of the whole human race in Genesis. And at the end of the chapter, the story narrows to one family, and one man.

STORY: The whole earth has one language, and one speech. As people journey from the east, they find a plain in the land of Shinar, and settle there.

They say to one another, let us make brick, and burn them thoroughly. They have brick for stone, and slime, or tar, for mortar. In a land without much stone, this is a clever new technology. And they decide what to build with it.

Notice their words. Let us. Let us build. Let us make. They are speaking as though they were God, who said, let us make man. Notice too what they want: to make a name for themselves, and to stay together in one place. But God had told Noah’s sons to fill the earth. They want to do the opposite.

'And the LORD came down to see the city and the tower, which the children of men builded.' There is gentle humour here. The people build a tower to reach heaven, but it is so small, from heaven’s point of view, that God has to come down to see it.

The LORD says, behold, the people is one, and they have all one language, and this is only the beginning of what they will do. Nothing they imagine will be held back from them. So God says, 'Go to, let us go down, and there confound their language, that they may not understand one another’s speech.'

So the LORD scatters them abroad from there upon the face of all the earth, and they stop building the city.

Then the chapter changes, and gives another family line, the generations of Shem. Just like chapter five, it lists one generation after another. Shem, Arphaxad, Salah, Eber, Peleg, Reu, Serug, Nahor. But this time the words and he died are left out, and the ages grow shorter with each generation. The line is moving toward someone important.

Finally we come to Terah. Terah has three sons: Abram, Nahor and Haran. Haran dies in the land of his birth, Ur of the Chaldees, and leaves a son named Lot. Abram marries Sarai, and Nahor marries Milcah.

Then comes a short sentence, with a great sadness in it.

Terah takes his son Abram, his grandson Lot, and his daughter-in-law Sarai, and they set out from Ur of the Chaldees, to go into the land of Canaan. But they come to a place called Haran, and settle there. And Terah dies in Haran.

The chapter ends there, halfway. A family has set out for Canaan, but has stopped short. A couple has no children. The people of the earth are scattered. And we are waiting for God to speak.

KEY 11:4: 'And they said, Go to, let us build us a city and a tower, whose top may reach unto heaven; and let us make us a name, lest we be scattered abroad upon the face of the whole earth.' Building is not wrong. Cities are not wrong. The problem was the heart behind it. They wanted to reach heaven by their own effort, and to make their own name great, without God. That desire is still in every human heart: to be important, to be secure, to do it ourselves. But true greatness is never something we build. It is something God gives.

KEY 11:9: 'Therefore is the name of it called Babel; because the LORD did there confound the language of all the earth: and from thence did the LORD scatter them abroad upon the face of all the earth.' The name Babel sounds like the Hebrew word for confusion. The people wanted a famous name, and they got one, but it meant confusion. Yet even this judgment was mercy. By scattering them, God stopped their pride from growing unchecked, and sent them out to fill the earth, as he had commanded.

KEY 11:30: 'But Sarai was barren; she had no child.' After all the long lists of fathers and sons, the line seems to come to a dead end. A woman who cannot have children. In the ancient world, this was a deep sorrow and shame. But in the Bible, this is often exactly where God begins. When there is no way forward, God makes a way. The next chapter will show what God will do with this family that has no future.

THREAD: In the book of Acts, on the day of Pentecost, the Holy Ghost came upon the disciples, and people from many nations heard them speaking, 'every man heard them speak in his own language.' At Babel, languages divided people. In Jesus, God was gathering people of every language into one family.

TODAY: Is there a tower you are trying to build for yourself, a name, a reputation, a sense of security without God? Lay it down before him today. Ask him to make your life count for his name, not yours.

QUESTION: The builders of Babel wanted to make a name for themselves. Where are you tempted to make your own name great, instead of trusting God?

PRAYER: Lord God, forgive me for the times I have tried to build my own tower and make my own name great. Teach me humility, and help me to live for your name. Thank you that you begin your greatest work where there seems to be no way forward. Amen.
