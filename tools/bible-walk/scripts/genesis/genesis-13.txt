# Genesis 13: back to Bethel, strife between herdmen, Lot chooses the plain, God's promise, Mamre. Keys 13:8, 13:15, 13:18.
TITLE: Let There Be No Strife

HOOK: Two men stand on a hilltop, looking out over the land. One is old, the other younger. Their herdsmen have been quarrelling, and the land cannot hold them both. The older man could insist on his rights. Instead, he turns to the younger and says, you choose. Whatever you want, take it.

WHERE: In chapter twelve, God called Abram to leave his country and his father’s house, and promised, in thee shall all families of the earth be blessed. Abram went, with Sarai and his nephew Lot, into the land of Canaan, building altars as he went. But when famine came, he went down to Egypt, and was afraid, and said that Sarai was his sister. God protected Sarai, and Pharaoh sent them away.

Now, in chapter thirteen, Abram returns from Egypt. He comes back to the place where he first called on the name of the LORD. And he faces a choice that shows how much his faith has grown.

STORY: Abram goes up out of Egypt into the south of Canaan, with his wife, all that he has, and Lot with him. Abram is very rich in cattle, in silver, and in gold. He travels on from place to place, back to Bethel, to the place where his tent had been at the beginning, to the altar he made there. 'and there Abram called on the name of the LORD.' After his failure in Egypt, Abram goes back to where he started, and calls on God again.

Lot also has flocks, herds and tents. So much, in fact, that the land is not able to bear them both. Their possessions are too great for them to dwell together. And there is strife between the herdmen of Abram’s cattle and the herdmen of Lot’s cattle. And the Canaanites and the Perizzites are living in the land, watching.

Abram does not wait for things to get worse. He goes to Lot.

And then Abram makes a generous offer. Is not the whole land before thee? Separate thyself from me, he says. If you go to the left, I will go to the right. If you go to the right, I will go to the left. Abram is the elder. God gave the promise to him. He has every right to choose first. But he gives the choice away.

Lot lifts up his eyes, and looks at the plain of Jordan. It is well watered everywhere, like the garden of the LORD, like the land of Egypt. This was before the LORD destroyed Sodom and Gomorrah. Lot sees the green fields, the water, the easy life. So Lot chooses all the plain of Jordan, and journeys east. They separate from each other.

Abram stays in the land of Canaan. Lot lives in the cities of the plain, and pitches his tent toward Sodom. And then the chapter adds a warning: 'But the men of Sodom were wicked and sinners before the LORD exceedingly.' Lot chose with his eyes. He looked at what was good for his flocks, but not at what would be good for his soul.

After Lot has gone, the LORD speaks to Abram. Lift up now thine eyes, God says, and look from the place where you are: northward, southward, eastward and westward. Lot lifted up his eyes and chose for himself. Now God tells Abram to lift up his eyes, and see what God will give him.

God promises to make Abram’s seed as the dust of the earth. If a man can count the dust of the earth, then shall thy seed also be numbered. 'Arise, walk through the land in the length of it and in the breadth of it; for I will give it unto thee.'

So Abram moves his tent, and comes to live by the oaks of Mamre, near Hebron.

KEY 13:8: 'And Abram said unto Lot, Let there be no strife, I pray thee, between me and thee, and between my herdmen and thy herdmen; for we be brethren.' Abram cared more about peace than about getting his own way. He reminded Lot, we are family. Quarrels between people who belong together do great harm, and others are always watching. Abram did not wait for Lot to make the first move. He went to him. Sometimes the strongest thing we can do is to give up our rights for the sake of peace.

KEY 13:15: 'For all the land which thou seest, to thee will I give it, and to thy seed for ever.' Abram let Lot choose the best. And God gave Abram all of it. When we let go of grasping for ourselves, we are not the losers. Abram could afford to be generous, because he trusted that God would provide. Lot had what he could see. Abram had what God promised. The one who trusts God never ends up with less.

KEY 13:18: 'Then Abram removed his tent, and came and dwelt in the plain of Mamre, which is in Hebron, and built there an altar unto the LORD.' Once again, Abram moved his tent and built an altar. Lot pitched his tent toward Sodom. Abram pitched his tent by an altar. Where we pitch our tent shows what we love. Lot drifted toward the world. Abram stayed close to God.

THREAD: Jesus said, 'Blessed are the peacemakers: for they shall be called the children of God.' And the apostle Paul wrote, 'If it be possible, as much as lieth in you, live peaceably with all men.' Abram showed what that looks like, long before those words were written.

TODAY: Is there a quarrel or tension in your family, among your friends, or where you work? Be like Abram. Go to them first. Remind them that you belong together. And be willing to give up your rights for the sake of peace, trusting God to take care of you.

QUESTION: Abram let Lot choose first, trusting God to provide. Is there a situation where you need to let go of your rights for the sake of peace?

PRAYER: Lord God, thank you for Abram’s example of generosity and peace. Forgive me when I have grasped for the best for myself. Help me to be a peacemaker, and to trust that you will provide all I need. Keep my tent pitched close to your altar. Amen.
