# Genesis 14: the war of the kings, Lot taken, Abram's rescue, Melchizedek, the king of Sodom's offer. Keys 14:18, 14:20, 14:23.
TITLE: Blessed Be Abram of the Most High God

HOOK: A messenger arrives at Abram’s tent, out of breath. There has been a war. Four kings from the east have swept through the land, defeated five kings in the valley, and carried off people and possessions. And one of the captives is Abram’s nephew, Lot. Abram is a herdsman, not a king. What can one man do?

WHERE: In chapter thirteen, the herdmen of Abram and Lot quarrelled, and Abram said, let there be no strife between us, for we are brethren. He let Lot choose first, and Lot chose the well-watered plain, and pitched his tent toward Sodom. Then God told Abram to lift up his eyes, and promised him all the land he could see.

Now, in chapter fourteen, Lot’s choice catches up with him. This chapter is different from the others around it. It reads like a page from an ancient history of kings and battles. And at its centre is one of the most mysterious people in the whole Bible.

STORY: The chapter begins with the names of kings. Four kings from the east, led by Chedorlaomer king of Elam, have ruled over five kings of the cities of the plain, including the kings of Sodom and Gomorrah. For twelve years the five kings serve Chedorlaomer, and in the thirteenth year they rebel.

So the four kings come with their armies. They sweep south, defeating one people after another. Then the five kings of the plain go out and join battle with them in the vale of Siddim, a valley full of slimepits, pits of tar. The kings of Sodom and Gomorrah flee, and some fall into the pits, and the rest flee to the mountain.

The four kings take all the goods of Sodom and Gomorrah, and all their food, and go on their way. And they take Lot, Abram’s brother’s son, who lives in Sodom, and his goods.

One who escaped comes and tells Abram the Hebrew. This is the first time anyone in the Bible is called a Hebrew. Abram is living by the oaks of Mamre, and the men who own them are his allies.

When Abram hears that his kinsman has been taken captive, he acts at once. He arms his trained servants, men born in his own household, three hundred and eighteen of them, and pursues the kings as far as Dan, in the far north. He divides his men, attacks by surprise in the dark, defeats the enemy, and chases them as far as Hobah, north of Damascus.

He brings back all the goods, and Lot his kinsman and his goods, and the women also, and the people.

On his way home, two kings come out to meet him in the valley of Shaveh, the king’s dale. One is the king of Sodom. But before he can speak, someone else steps forward, a man we have never heard of before.

His name is Melchizedek. His name means king of righteousness, and he is king of Salem, which means peace. Many believe Salem is the city later called Jerusalem. And he is a priest of the most high God. He blesses Abram.

Abram gives Melchizedek a tenth of everything, a tithe of all the spoils. Abram recognises that this priest serves the true God, and that the victory came from him.

Then the king of Sodom speaks. Give me the persons, he says, and take the goods for thyself. It is a generous offer. By the customs of war, Abram could have kept it all. But Abram has already made a promise. He says to the king of Sodom, I have lift up mine hand unto the LORD, the most high God, the possessor of heaven and earth.

The only things Abram will accept are the food his young men have eaten, and the share that belongs to his allies, Aner, Eshcol and Mamre. Let them take their portion.

KEY 14:18: 'And Melchizedek king of Salem brought forth bread and wine: and he was the priest of the most high God.' Melchizedek appears without warning and disappears just as suddenly. We are not told where he came from or what became of him. He is both a king and a priest, long before there were any kings or priests in Israel. He came to strengthen Abram, weary from battle, with bread and wine. He is a picture of someone greater who was still to come.

KEY 14:20: 'And blessed be the most high God, which hath delivered thine enemies into thy hand. And he gave him tithes of all.' Melchizedek reminded Abram where the victory came from. Not from his three hundred and eighteen servants, and not from his own cleverness, but from God. And Abram responded with thanksgiving, giving a tenth of all he had won. When God gives us success, the right response is to give him the glory, and to give back to him with a grateful heart.

KEY 14:23: 'That I will not take from a thread even to a shoelatchet, and that I will not take any thing that is thine, lest thou shouldest say, I have made Abram rich:' Abram refused even a thread or a sandal strap from the king of Sodom. He did not want anyone to say that his wealth came from Sodom. He wanted everyone to know that his riches came from God. Lot had moved toward Sodom. Abram refused to be in its debt. Some offers look generous, but they come with strings attached.

THREAD: A psalm about the coming Messiah says, 'Thou art a priest for ever after the order of Melchizedek.' And the letter to the Hebrews explains that Jesus is that priest, 'made an high priest for ever after the order of Melchisedec.' He is our king of righteousness and king of peace.

TODAY: When God gives you success or help, remember to give him the glory. And if someone offers you something that would put you in the debt of what is wrong, have the courage of Abram to say no, and trust God to provide.

QUESTION: Abram gave thanks to God for the victory, and refused the king of Sodom’s offer. Is there anything you need to turn down, so that God gets the glory in your life?

PRAYER: Lord God most high, possessor of heaven and earth, every victory and every good thing comes from you. Thank you for Jesus, our priest for ever and our king of peace. Give me courage to stand by those I love, and wisdom to refuse what would turn my heart from you. Amen.
