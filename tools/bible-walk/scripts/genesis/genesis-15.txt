# Genesis 15: fear not, Abram; count the stars; believed the LORD; the covenant between the pieces. Keys 15:1, 15:5, 15:6.
TITLE: He Believed in the LORD

HOOK: An old man stands outside his tent, under a sky full of stars. He has no children. He has been waiting for years for God to keep his promise, and nothing has happened. Then God speaks, and tells him to look up. Count the stars, God says, if you can. So shall thy seed be.

WHERE: In chapter fourteen, four kings carried Lot away captive, and Abram rescued him with three hundred and eighteen trained servants. Melchizedek, king of Salem and priest of the most high God, came out with bread and wine and blessed him. And Abram refused to take even a thread from the king of Sodom, so that no one could say anyone but God had made him rich.

Now, in chapter fifteen, after the battle, Abram is alone with his questions. He has turned down great riches. He still has no son. And God comes to him with one of the most important conversations in the Bible.

STORY: After these things, the word of the LORD comes to Abram in a vision. God speaks before Abram says a word, and the first thing he says is, fear not.

Abram answers honestly. Lord GOD, what will you give me, seeing I go childless? The heir of my house is Eliezer of Damascus, my servant. Abram had been promised a great nation, but he has not even one son. Perhaps, he thinks, God means that his servant will inherit everything. Abram says, 'Behold, to me thou hast given no seed: and, lo, one born in my house is mine heir.'

God answers him plainly. This man shall not be thine heir; but one that comes out of your own body shall be your heir.

Then God brings Abram outside the tent, and shows him the sky.

And Abram believes God.

Then God reminds Abram who brought him out of Ur of the Chaldees, to give him this land. And Abram asks, 'Lord GOD, whereby shall I know that I shall inherit it?'

God answers by making a covenant in the way that people did in those days. He tells Abram to bring a heifer, a she goat and a ram, each three years old, and a turtledove and a young pigeon. Abram divides the animals in two, and lays the halves opposite each other, with a path between them. In ancient times, when two people made a solemn agreement, they would walk together between the pieces. It meant, may this happen to me if I break my word.

Birds of prey come down on the carcases, and Abram drives them away. Then, as the sun is going down, a deep sleep falls upon Abram, and a great and dreadful darkness.

God tells Abram what lies ahead. His descendants will be strangers in a land that is not theirs. They will serve its people, and be afflicted, for four hundred years. But God will judge that nation, and afterward they will come out with great substance. As for Abram, he will go to his fathers in peace, and be buried in a good old age. In the fourth generation, his descendants will come back to this land, because the iniquity of the Amorites is not yet full. God is patient, even with the people of Canaan. He will not judge them before their time.

Then something extraordinary happens. When the sun has gone down and it is dark, a smoking furnace and a burning lamp pass between the pieces. They are signs of the presence of God. And notice who walks between the pieces. Not Abram. Only God. Abram is asleep. God alone binds himself to the promise. It is as if God is saying, if this promise is broken, let it fall on me.

On that same day, the LORD makes a covenant with Abram, and says, 'Unto thy seed have I given this land, from the river of Egypt unto the great river, the river Euphrates'.

KEY 15:1: 'After these things the word of the LORD came unto Abram in a vision, saying, Fear not, Abram: I am thy shield, and thy exceeding great reward.' Abram had just won a battle, and might have feared revenge. He had just turned down great riches, and might have wondered if he had been foolish. God answered both fears. I am thy shield: I will protect you. I am thy exceeding great reward: I am worth more than anything you gave up. God does not only give us gifts. He gives us himself.

KEY 15:5: 'And he brought him forth abroad, and said, Look now toward heaven, and tell the stars, if thou be able to number them: and he said unto him, So shall thy seed be.' God took Abram outside, away from the small space of his tent and his worries, and showed him the sky. Abram was looking at what he did not have. God told him to look up, at what God could do. Sometimes we need to step outside our worries, and look at the greatness of the One who made the stars.

KEY 15:6: 'And he believed in the LORD; and he counted it to him for righteousness.' This is one of the most important verses in the Bible. Abram did nothing to earn God’s approval. He simply believed God’s promise. And God counted that faith as righteousness. He declared Abram right with him. That is how anyone is made right with God: not by our goodness, but by trusting what God has promised.

THREAD: The apostle Paul quoted this verse in his letter to the Romans: 'Abraham believed God, and it was counted unto him for righteousness.' He wrote that it was not written for Abraham’s sake alone, 'But for us also, to whom it shall be imputed, if we believe on him that raised up Jesus our Lord from the dead'.

TODAY: Is there a promise of God that you are struggling to believe, because you cannot see how it could come true? Step outside your worries, and look up. Tell God honestly what you are afraid of, as Abram did. And then choose to believe him.

QUESTION: Abram believed God, and God counted it to him for righteousness. What would it mean for you to trust God’s promise, rather than your own goodness, to make you right with him?

PRAYER: Lord God, you are my shield, and my exceeding great reward. When I am afraid, help me to look up and remember how great you are. Thank you that I am made right with you not by what I do, but by trusting in your promise, fulfilled in Jesus. Amen.
