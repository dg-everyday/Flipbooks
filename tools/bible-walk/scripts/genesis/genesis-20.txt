# Genesis 20: Abraham in Gerar, Sarah called his sister again, God warns Abimelech in a dream, Abraham prays. Keys 20:6, 20:11, 20:17.
TITLE: Surely the Fear of God Is Not in This Place

HOOK: A king lies asleep in his palace, and God speaks to him in a dream. The words are terrifying: you are a dead man. The king has done something wrong without knowing it. He has taken a woman into his house who belongs to another man. And that man is a prophet of God, who did not tell him the truth.

WHERE: In chapter nineteen, two angels brought Lot and his daughters out of Sodom, just before the LORD rained brimstone and fire on the cities of the plain. Lot’s wife looked back and became a pillar of salt. Abraham rose early and saw the smoke going up. God had remembered Abraham, and saved Lot.

Now, in chapter twenty, it is Abraham’s turn to fail. You may remember that back in chapter twelve, Abraham said in Egypt that Sarah was his sister. Now he does it again. Genesis does not hide the faults of its heroes. But it shows us something even more important: the faithfulness of God, even when his people are faithless.

STORY: Abraham journeys south, and lives for a while in Gerar, in the land of the Philistines. And there, once again, he says of Sarah his wife, she is my sister. So Abimelech, the king of Gerar, sends and takes Sarah into his household.

This is a dangerous moment. God has just promised that Sarah will bear Abraham a son, at a set time, the next year. Now the mother of the promised child is in the house of a foreign king. The whole promise seems to be at risk, because of Abraham’s fear.

But God steps in. He comes to Abimelech in a dream, and says, behold, thou art but a dead man, because of the woman you have taken, for she is a man’s wife.

Abimelech has not come near her. He answers God, Lord, will you destroy a righteous nation? Did he not say to me, she is my sister? And she herself said, he is my brother. In the integrity of my heart and the innocency of my hands have I done this.

And God answers him in the dream.

God tells Abimelech to restore the man’s wife, 'for he is a prophet, and he shall pray for thee, and thou shalt live'. But if he does not restore her, he and all that are his will surely die.

Abimelech rises early. He calls all his servants, and tells them everything, and the men are sore afraid. Then he calls Abraham, and asks him the same kind of question Pharaoh once asked. 'What hast thou done unto us? and what have I offended thee, that thou hast brought on me and on my kingdom a great sin?' You have done deeds to me that ought not to be done. What did you see, that you have done this thing?

And Abraham gives his reasons.

Abraham explains that Sarah really is his sister, the daughter of his father, but not the daughter of his mother, and she became his wife. And when God caused him to wander from his father’s house, he asked Sarah to show him this kindness: wherever we go, say of me, he is my brother.

It is a sad excuse. Abraham had planned this half-truth from the very beginning of his journey. He had trusted God enough to leave his homeland, but not enough to trust God to protect him and his wife.

Abimelech takes sheep and oxen, and menservants and womenservants, and gives them to Abraham, and restores Sarah to him. He says, behold, my land is before thee: dwell where it pleaseth thee. And to Sarah he says, he has given her brother a thousand pieces of silver, as a covering before everyone, so that she is honoured, not shamed.

Then the chapter ends with a surprising picture.

For the LORD had kept all the house of Abimelech from having children, because of Sarah. Now, when Abraham prays, God heals them.

KEY 20:6: 'And God said unto him in a dream, Yea, I know that thou didst this in the integrity of thy heart; for I also withheld thee from sinning against me: therefore suffered I thee not to touch her.' God knew Abimelech’s heart. And God had been at work, holding him back from sin, before Abimelech even knew there was danger. God protects us in ways we never see. How many times has he kept us from sin, or from harm, without our knowing? Notice also that God says, sinning against me. All sin is, in the end, against God.

KEY 20:11: 'And Abraham said, Because I thought, Surely the fear of God is not in this place'. Abraham assumed that the people of Gerar did not fear God. But he was wrong. Abimelech listened to God, and acted with more honesty than Abraham did. It is easy to look down on others, and assume the worst about them. Abraham’s fear made him think badly of others, and act badly himself. God was at work in Gerar long before Abraham arrived.

KEY 20:17: 'So Abraham prayed unto God: and God healed Abimelech, and his wife, and his maidservants; and they bare children.' This is the first time in the Bible that someone prays for healing, and God answers. And the one who prays is the man who caused the trouble. God still used Abraham, flawed as he was, to bring blessing to others. God’s calling on our lives does not depend on our perfection. It depends on his grace.

THREAD: The apostle Paul wrote, 'If we believe not, yet he abideth faithful: he cannot deny himself.' And a psalm says of Abraham and his family, 'He suffered no man to do them wrong: yea, he reproved kings for their sakes; Saying, Touch not mine anointed, and do my prophets no harm.'

TODAY: Is there an old fear or habit that you keep falling back into, even after God has helped you before? Bring it to him honestly. And be careful not to assume the worst about people you do not know. God may already be at work in them.

QUESTION: Abraham repeated an old mistake, but God stayed faithful and still used him to bless others. What old fear or habit do you need God to help you leave behind?

PRAYER: Lord God, thank you that you are faithful, even when I am faithless. Thank you for the times you have protected me without my knowing. Forgive me for my fears, and for thinking the worst of others. Use me, flawed as I am, to bring blessing to the people around me. Amen.
