# Genesis 25: Abraham's last years and death, Ishmael's sons, Isaac prays, the twins, Esau sells his birthright. Keys 25:21, 25:23, 25:34.
TITLE: Esau Despised His Birthright

HOOK: A young man comes in from the field, exhausted and starving. The smell of stew drifts from his brother’s cooking pot. Give me some of that red stuff, he says. I am faint. His brother looks at him, and names a price. Not money. Something far more precious. And the hungry man agrees.

WHERE: In chapter twenty-four, Abraham’s servant travelled to Mesopotamia to find a wife for Isaac. He prayed at a well, and Rebekah came, and watered his camels. When her family asked her, wilt thou go with this man, she said, I will go. Isaac took her into his mother’s tent, and loved her.

Now, in chapter twenty-five, the story passes from one generation to the next. Abraham dies, and the promise passes to Isaac. And then a new generation is born, twin brothers who are as different as two people can be. Their rivalry will shape the rest of Genesis.

STORY: Abraham takes another wife, named Keturah, and has more sons. But he gives everything he has to Isaac. To his other sons he gives gifts, and sends them away eastward while he is still alive, so that Isaac alone will be his heir.

Abraham lives a hundred and seventy-five years. Then he gives up the ghost, and dies in a good old age, an old man, and full of years, and is gathered to his people. And his sons Isaac and Ishmael bury him in the cave of Machpelah, with Sarah his wife. It is touching to see the two brothers, so long apart, standing together at their father’s grave. After Abraham’s death, God blesses Isaac.

Genesis then lists the twelve sons of Ishmael, princes of twelve tribes, just as God promised Hagar. God keeps his word even to those outside the line of promise. Ishmael lives a hundred and thirty-seven years, and dies.

Then the story turns to Isaac. Isaac is forty years old when he marries Rebekah. But like Sarah before her, Rebekah cannot have children. And Isaac prays.

Isaac is sixty years old when the twins are born, so they have waited twenty years. Once again, the promise comes only through a miracle.

The children struggle together inside her, and Rebekah asks, if it be so, why am I thus? And she goes to enquire of the LORD.

The first baby comes out red, and hairy all over like a hairy garment, and they call him Esau. Then his brother comes out, holding on to Esau’s heel, and they call him Jacob, which means one who takes by the heel, or one who supplants. Even at birth, Jacob is grasping for his brother’s place.

The boys grow. Esau becomes a cunning hunter, a man of the field. Jacob is a plain man, a quiet man, who stays among the tents. And the family is divided. Isaac loves Esau, because he enjoys the meat Esau hunts. But Rebekah loves Jacob.

One day Jacob is cooking a stew. Esau comes in from the field, and he is faint with hunger. He says to Jacob, feed me, I pray thee, with that same red pottage, for I am faint. And Jacob says, sell me this day thy birthright.

The birthright belonged to the firstborn son. It meant a double share of the inheritance, and leadership of the family after the father died. In Abraham’s family, it also meant carrying on the promise of God.

Esau says, behold, I am at the point to die: and what profit shall this birthright do to me? Jacob says, swear to me this day. And Esau swears, and sells his birthright to Jacob.

KEY 25:21: 'And Isaac intreated the LORD for his wife, because she was barren: and the LORD was intreated of him, and Rebekah his wife conceived.' Isaac did not take matters into his own hands, as his parents had done with Hagar. He prayed. And he kept praying, for twenty years. God answered, in his own time. When we face something we cannot change, the first and best thing we can do is to bring it to God, and keep bringing it.

KEY 25:23: 'And the LORD said unto her, Two nations… and the one people shall be stronger than the other people; and the elder shall serve the younger.' In the ancient world, the elder son was always first. But God turns the usual order upside down. He chose Isaac over Ishmael, and now he chooses Jacob over Esau, before either of them had done anything good or bad. God’s choice depends on his grace, not on who comes first or who deserves it.

KEY 25:34: 'Then Jacob gave Esau bread and pottage of lentiles; and he did eat and drink, and rose up, and went his way: thus Esau despised his birthright.' Notice the quick, short verbs. He ate, he drank, he rose up, he went his way. Esau did not even pause to think about what he had given up. He traded something eternal for something that would be gone in a moment. Jacob was wrong to take advantage of his brother. But Esau showed what he truly valued. He cared more about his stomach than about God’s promise.

THREAD: The letter to the Hebrews warns us not to be like Esau, 'who for one morsel of meat sold his birthright.' And Jesus asked, 'For what is a man profited, if he shall gain the whole world, and lose his own soul?'

TODAY: Is there something you want right now that could cost you something far more precious? Do not trade what lasts for what will soon be gone. Stop and think about what you truly value. And if you are waiting for an answer to prayer, keep asking, as Isaac did.

QUESTION: Esau traded his birthright for a bowl of stew. What is one thing you might be tempted to put ahead of what matters most to God?

PRAYER: Lord God, thank you for your grace, which does not depend on who is first or who deserves it. Help me to value what you value, and not to trade what lasts for what quickly passes. Teach me to pray and keep praying, and to trust your timing. Amen.
