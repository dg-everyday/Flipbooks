# Genesis 27: Isaac plans to bless Esau, Rebekah's plan, Jacob's deception, the blessing, Esau's cry, Jacob must flee. Keys 27:22, 27:28, 27:38.
TITLE: The Voice Is Jacob’s Voice

HOOK: A blind old man reaches out his trembling hands, and feels the arms of the young man in front of him. They are covered in rough, hairy goatskin. He sniffs the clothes. They smell of the fields. But the voice is wrong. The old man frowns. Something is not right. And yet he lifts his hands, and speaks the blessing.

WHERE: In chapter twenty-six, God renewed his promise to Isaac. Isaac dug well after well, and when others quarrelled with him, he moved on, until the LORD made room for him. And at the end of the chapter, Esau married two Hittite women, who were a grief to his parents.

Now, in chapter twenty-seven, the family conflict that started at the twins’ birth comes to a head. Every member of the family does something wrong in this chapter. And yet God’s purposes still go forward. Listen for how one deception leads to years of sorrow.

STORY: Isaac is old, and his eyes are so dim that he cannot see. He calls Esau, his elder son, and says, I do not know the day of my death. Take your bow, go out to the field, hunt some venison for me, and make me savoury meat, such as I love, so that I may bless you before I die.

Isaac knew that God had said the elder would serve the younger. But he still planned to give the blessing to Esau, his favourite. And he plans to do it in secret.

Rebekah hears it all. When Esau goes out to hunt, she calls Jacob. She tells him to bring two good young goats from the flock, and she will make the savoury meat his father loves. Jacob will take it to his father, and receive the blessing instead of Esau.

Jacob is not worried about whether it is right. He is worried about being caught. My brother Esau is a hairy man, he says, and I am a smooth man. My father may feel me, and I will seem to him as a deceiver, and I will bring a curse on myself, not a blessing. Rebekah says, upon me be thy curse, my son. Only obey my voice.

So Rebekah cooks the meat. She dresses Jacob in Esau’s best clothes, and puts the skins of the young goats on his hands and on the smooth part of his neck. And Jacob goes in to his father.

My father, he says. Isaac asks, who art thou, my son? And Jacob lies. I am Esau thy firstborn. Isaac asks, how is it that you have found it so quickly? And Jacob lies again, and even brings God into his lie. Because the LORD thy God brought it to me.

Isaac says, come near, I pray thee, that I may feel thee, my son.

Isaac asks once more, art thou my very son Esau? And Jacob says, I am. Isaac eats, and then says, come near now, and kiss me, my son. He smells the smell of Esau’s clothes, and blesses him.

He blesses Jacob with the richness of the land, and with power. Let people serve thee, and nations bow down to thee. Be lord over thy brethren. Cursed be every one that curseth thee, and blessed be he that blesseth thee.

Jacob has scarcely gone out, when Esau comes in from his hunting. He too makes savoury meat, and brings it to his father. Isaac asks, who art thou? I am thy son, thy firstborn Esau. And Isaac trembles very exceedingly. Who? Where is he that hath taken venison, and brought it me, and I have eaten, and have blessed him? 'yea, and he shall be blessed.'

When Esau hears his father’s words, he cries with a great and exceeding bitter cry. Bless me, even me also, O my father. Isaac says, thy brother came with subtilty, and hath taken away thy blessing. Esau says, is he not rightly named Jacob? He has supplanted me these two times. He took away my birthright, and now he has taken away my blessing.

Then Esau asks one more question.

Isaac gives Esau a lesser blessing. He will live by his sword, and serve his brother. But a time will come when he will break his brother’s yoke from off his neck.

Esau hates Jacob, and says in his heart, when the days of mourning for my father come, then I will kill my brother Jacob. Rebekah hears of it. She tells Jacob to flee to her brother Laban in Haran, and stay a few days, until his brother’s anger turns away. She says she will send for him. But as far as Genesis tells us, she never does. Rebekah never sees her favourite son again.

KEY 27:22: 'And Jacob went near unto Isaac his father; and he felt him, and said, The voice is Jacob’s voice, but the hands are the hands of Esau.' Isaac’s senses told him two different things. His ears said Jacob. His hands said Esau. And he chose to trust his hands, the thing he could touch. Jacob looked like his brother on the outside, but his voice gave away who he really was. We can dress up on the outside, but God hears the voice of the heart.

KEY 27:28: 'Therefore God give thee of the dew of heaven, and the fatness of the earth, and plenty of corn and wine:' This was the blessing Jacob stole. And God did intend to bless Jacob. He had said so before the twins were born. But Jacob did not need to lie to receive it. If he had waited, and trusted God, the blessing would have come in God’s way. Instead, he grabbed it by deception, and it cost him his home, his mother and many years of hardship.

KEY 27:38: 'And Esau said unto his father, Hast thou but one blessing, my father? bless me, even me also, O my father. And Esau lifted up his voice, and wept.' It is hard not to feel sorry for Esau. He had been tricked. But he had also despised his birthright, and married against his parents’ wishes. Now he wept for what he had once treated as nothing. Some things, once thrown away, cannot be easily taken back. Esau’s tears were real, but they came too late.

THREAD: The letter to the Hebrews says that Esau, 'when he would have inherited the blessing, he was rejected: for he found no place of repentance, though he sought it carefully with tears.' And Galatians warns, 'Be not deceived; God is not mocked: for whatsoever a man soweth, that shall he also reap.' Jacob deceived his father. In the chapters ahead, he will be deceived himself.

TODAY: Is there something you are trying to get by your own scheming, instead of trusting God? Stop, and wait for him. And if there is division in your family, ask God to help you be a peacemaker, not a part of the problem.

QUESTION: Jacob grabbed the blessing by deception instead of waiting for God. Where are you tempted to take a shortcut instead of trusting God’s timing?

PRAYER: Lord God, forgive me for the times I have tried to get my way by scheming, instead of trusting you. Make me honest in word and deed. Heal the divisions in families, including my own, and help me to wait for the blessings you have promised, in your way and your time. Amen.
