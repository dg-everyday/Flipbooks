# Genesis 28: Isaac sends Jacob to Haran with a blessing, the dream of the ladder at Bethel, God's promise, Jacob's vow. Keys 28:12, 28:15, 28:16.
TITLE: Surely the LORD Is in This Place

HOOK: A young man lies alone on the hard ground, far from home, with a stone for a pillow. He has deceived his father and cheated his brother, and now his brother wants to kill him. He has nothing but the clothes he wears and the staff in his hand. He falls asleep. And then heaven opens above him.

WHERE: In chapter twenty-seven, Jacob deceived his blind father Isaac, and took the blessing meant for Esau. Esau wept bitterly, and swore that he would kill his brother. So Rebekah told Jacob to flee to her brother Laban in Haran, far away to the north.

Now, in chapter twenty-eight, Jacob begins his long journey. He is running away from the mess he has made. And at the lowest point of his life, God meets him. Not because Jacob deserved it, but because God is gracious.

STORY: Isaac calls Jacob, and blesses him, and charges him not to take a wife from the daughters of Canaan. Go to Padan-aram, he says, to the house of Bethuel, your mother’s father, and take a wife from the daughters of Laban, your mother’s brother. And Isaac adds a blessing, this time knowingly. God Almighty bless thee, and make thee fruitful, and give thee the blessing of Abraham, to thee and to thy seed with thee.

Esau sees that his father has blessed Jacob and sent him away to find a wife, and that the Canaanite women displease his father. So Esau goes to Ishmael’s family and takes another wife, a daughter of Ishmael. He is still trying to please his father, but he does not quite understand what matters.

Jacob goes out from Beersheba, and travels toward Haran. He comes to a certain place, and stays there, because the sun has set. He takes one of the stones of that place for his pillow, and lies down to sleep.

Then he dreams.

And the LORD stands above it, and speaks. I am the LORD God of Abraham thy father, and the God of Isaac. The land on which you lie, to you will I give it, and to your seed. Your seed shall be as the dust of the earth, and you shall spread abroad to the west, and to the east, and to the north, and to the south. 'and in thee and in thy seed shall all the families of the earth be blessed.'

Notice what God does not say. He does not scold Jacob for his lies. He does not ask Jacob to promise anything first. He simply gives Jacob the same promise he gave Abraham and Isaac. And then he adds a personal promise, just for Jacob.

Jacob wakes from his sleep.

He is afraid, and says, 'How dreadful is this place! this is none other but the house of God, and this is the gate of heaven.'

Jacob rises early, and takes the stone he had used for a pillow, and sets it up as a pillar, and pours oil on top of it, to mark the place. He calls the name of that place Bethel, which means the house of God. Before, the city had been called Luz.

Then Jacob makes a vow. If God will be with me, he says, and keep me in this way that I go, and give me bread to eat and clothing to put on, so that I come again to my father’s house in peace, then shall the LORD be my God. And this stone shall be God’s house. And of all that you give me, I will surely give the tenth to you.

Jacob’s vow sounds like a bargain. If you do this, God, then I will do that. His faith is still young. He has only begun to know God. But God has already promised to do everything Jacob asks, and more. Over the long years ahead, God will patiently teach Jacob to trust him.

KEY 28:12: 'And he dreamed, and behold a ladder set up on the earth, and the top of it reached to heaven: and behold the angels of God ascending and descending on it.' The people of Babel tried to build a tower up to heaven. It did not work. But here, God sets up a ladder from heaven to earth. We cannot climb up to God by our own efforts. But God reaches down to us. Jacob, the runaway, the cheat, was shown that heaven was not closed to him. God had opened the way.

KEY 28:15: 'And, behold, I am with thee, and will keep thee in all places whither thou goest, and will bring thee again into this land; for I will not leave thee, until I have done that which I have spoken to thee of.' This is one of the great promises of the Bible. I am with thee. I will keep thee. I will bring thee again. I will not leave thee. Jacob was alone, but he was not alone. And God would keep every one of these promises over the next twenty years. If you belong to God, he says the same to you. He will not leave you until he has finished what he started.

KEY 28:16: 'And Jacob awaked out of his sleep, and he said, Surely the LORD is in this place; and I knew it not.' Jacob thought he was in the middle of nowhere. He thought he had left God behind at home. But God was right there, in that lonely place. God is often present in places where we least expect him: in our loneliness, our failures, and our fears. We just do not always know it. Ask God to open your eyes to his presence today.

THREAD: Jesus told Nathanael, 'Hereafter ye shall see heaven open, and the angels of God ascending and descending upon the Son of man.' Jesus himself is the ladder. He is the way between heaven and earth, the way back to God for every runaway.

TODAY: Are you in a hard or lonely place right now? Stop, and remember that God is there with you, even if you did not know it. Thank him for his promise: I am with thee, and will not leave thee.

QUESTION: Jacob said, surely the LORD is in this place; and I knew it not. Where might God be present in your life right now, in a place you did not expect?

PRAYER: Lord God, thank you that you reach down to me, when I could never climb up to you. Thank you for Jesus, the way between heaven and earth. Open my eyes to see that you are with me, even in lonely places, and help me to trust your promise that you will never leave me. Amen.
