# Genesis 31: God says return, Jacob flees from Laban, Rachel's stolen images, Laban's pursuit, Jacob's complaint, the covenant at Mizpah. Keys 31:3, 31:42, 31:49.
TITLE: The LORD Watch Between Me and Thee

HOOK: A long line of camels, sheep, goats and servants moves quickly across the hills. Jacob is running away again, this time with his whole family. Behind them, an angry man and his relatives are riding hard to catch up. In seven days they will overtake them. And no one knows that one of Jacob’s wives has hidden a secret in her camel’s saddle.

WHERE: In chapter thirty, Rachel and Leah competed for Jacob’s love, and eleven sons and a daughter were born. At last God remembered Rachel, and she gave birth to Joseph. Jacob asked to go home, but Laban begged him to stay, saying, the LORD hath blessed me for thy sake. And though Laban tried to cheat him, Jacob’s flocks grew great.

Now, in chapter thirty-one, twenty years after Jacob arrived in Haran, God tells him it is time to go home. It is a chapter full of tension and accusation. But it ends with a promise of peace, and a heap of stones on a hilltop.

STORY: Jacob hears Laban’s sons saying, Jacob has taken away all that was our father’s. And Jacob sees that Laban’s face is not toward him as before. Then the LORD speaks.

Jacob calls Rachel and Leah out to the field, where his flock is. He tells them, your father’s face is not toward me as before, but the God of my father has been with me. You know that I have served your father with all my strength. Your father has deceived me, and changed my wages ten times. But God did not let him hurt me. God has taken away your father’s cattle, and given them to me. Then Jacob tells them of a dream, in which the angel of God said, 'I am the God of Bethel, where thou anointedst the pillar, and where thou vowedst a vow unto me'. Now arise, and return to the land of your family.

Rachel and Leah agree. Their father has treated them like strangers, they say. He sold us, and has used up the money paid for us. All the riches God has taken from our father belong to us and to our children. Whatever God has said to you, do it.

So Jacob rises up, sets his sons and wives on camels, and leaves with all his cattle and goods, to go to Isaac his father in Canaan. Laban has gone to shear his sheep. And Rachel steals the images that belonged to her father, his household gods. Jacob does not tell Laban that he is leaving.

On the third day, Laban is told that Jacob has fled. He takes his relatives with him, and pursues Jacob for seven days, and catches up with him in the mountains of Gilead. But God comes to Laban in a dream, and says, take heed that thou speak not to Jacob either good or bad.

Laban confronts Jacob. What have you done, carrying away my daughters like captives taken with the sword? Why did you flee secretly, and not let me kiss my sons and daughters goodbye? It is in my power to do you harm, but the God of your father spoke to me in a dream. And then, wherefore hast thou stolen my gods?

Jacob does not know that Rachel has taken them. He says, whoever has your gods, let him not live. Search, and see if anything of yours is with me. Laban searches Jacob’s tent, and Leah’s tent, and the maids’ tents. Then he goes into Rachel’s tent. Rachel has put the images in the camel’s saddle, and she sits on them. She says to her father, let it not displease my lord that I cannot rise up before you, for I am unwell. He searches, but does not find them.

Then Jacob is angry, and twenty years of hurt come pouring out. What is my trespass? What is my sin, that you have hotly pursued me? You have searched all my things. What have you found? These twenty years I have been with you. Your sheep and goats have not lost their young. I have not eaten your rams. When wild beasts tore an animal, I bore the loss myself. 'in the day the drought consumed me, and the frost by night; and my sleep departed from mine eyes.' Fourteen years for your daughters, six years for your cattle, and you changed my wages ten times.

Laban has no answer for that. So he suggests a covenant between them. Jacob sets up a stone as a pillar, and their relatives make a heap of stones. Jacob calls it Galeed, the heap of witness.

They swear that neither will pass over this heap to harm the other. Laban calls on the God of Abraham to judge between them, and Jacob swears by the fear of his father Isaac. Then they eat bread together on the mountain. Early the next day, Laban rises up, kisses his grandchildren and his daughters, blesses them, and returns home.

KEY 31:3: 'And the LORD said unto Jacob, Return unto the land of thy fathers, and to thy kindred; and I will be with thee.' Twenty years before, God had promised Jacob at Bethel, I will bring thee again into this land. Now the time had come. Jacob did not leave because things were difficult with Laban, though they were. He left because God told him to. And God gave him the same promise again: I will be with thee. When God calls us to move, he goes with us.

KEY 31:42: 'Except the God of my father, the God of Abraham, and the fear of Isaac, had been with me, surely thou hadst sent me away now empty. God hath seen mine affliction and the labour of my hands,' Twenty years of hard work and unfair treatment. But God saw it all. Jacob had come to Haran with nothing but a staff. Now he left with a large family and great flocks, not because of his own cleverness, but because God was with him. God sees the hard work that no one else notices, and the unfairness we suffer.

KEY 31:49: 'And Mizpah; for he said, The LORD watch between me and thee, when we are absent one from another.' Mizpah means watchtower. Today people sometimes use these words as a loving farewell. But Laban meant them as a warning. Neither man trusted the other, so they asked God to watch. Yet even so, there is truth here. God sees what happens when we are apart from each other. And in the end, these two men parted in peace.

THREAD: A psalm says, 'Behold, he that keepeth Israel shall neither slumber nor sleep.' And Paul wrote, 'Recompense to no man evil for evil… If it be possible, as much as lieth in you, live peaceably with all men.'

TODAY: Has someone treated you unfairly? Remember that God sees your affliction and the labour of your hands. Bring it to him, and leave the judgment with him. And if there is someone you need to make peace with, take a step toward that today.

QUESTION: Jacob said, God hath seen mine affliction and the labour of my hands. Where do you need to trust that God sees what you are going through?

PRAYER: Lord God, thank you that you see my work and my troubles, even when no one else does. Thank you that when you call me to move, you go with me. Help me to leave judgment with you, and to live at peace with others as far as I am able. Amen.
