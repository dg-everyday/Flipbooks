# Genesis 34: Dinah wronged by Shechem, Hamor's offer, the brothers' deceit, Simeon and Levi's revenge, Jacob's rebuke. Keys 34:7, 34:13, 34:30. Told gently for all ages.
TITLE: Which Thing Ought Not to Be Done

HOOK: A young woman goes out to visit the girls of the land where her family has settled. She does not come home. The son of the local prince has taken her and wronged her, and now he wants to keep her. Her father hears the news, and says nothing. Her brothers hear it, and they are furious. What happens next is one of the darkest stories in Genesis.

WHERE: In chapter thirty-three, Jacob met his brother Esau. Esau ran to meet him, and embraced him, and they wept. The brothers parted in peace. Jacob came to the city of Shechem in Canaan, bought a field, and built an altar there, calling it El-elohe-Israel, God, the God of Israel.

Now, in chapter thirty-four, Jacob’s family has settled near Shechem. But instead of being a blessing to the people around them, they become caught up in sin, both the sin done to them and the sin they do in return. God is hardly mentioned in this chapter. That silence is part of its message. This is what happens when people act without asking God.

STORY: Dinah, the daughter of Leah and Jacob, goes out to see the daughters of the land. And Shechem, the son of Hamor the Hivite, the prince of the country, sees her. He takes her, and wrongs her, and dishonours her. The Bible names it plainly as a great wrong.

Afterward, Shechem says he loves Dinah, and speaks kindly to her. He asks his father Hamor, get me this damsel to wife. But he still keeps her in his house.

Jacob hears that his daughter has been dishonoured. His sons are out in the field with the cattle. And Jacob holds his peace until they come. He says nothing, and does nothing. Dinah is Leah’s daughter, and Jacob has always loved Rachel more. We are not told why he is silent. But his silence leaves room for his sons’ anger.

Hamor, Shechem’s father, comes out to talk with Jacob. Meanwhile, Jacob’s sons come in from the field when they hear it. They are grieved, and very angry.

Hamor speaks to them all. My son Shechem longs for your daughter. Please give her to him as his wife. And let us make marriages between our peoples. Give your daughters to us, and take our daughters for yourselves. Live among us, trade here, and get possessions in the land. Shechem adds, let me find grace in your eyes. Ask me as great a dowry and gift as you like, and I will give it. Only give me the girl as my wife.

Notice what is missing. No one apologises. No one admits that a terrible wrong has been done. Hamor and Shechem talk about trade and marriage, as if nothing had happened.

Then the sons of Jacob answer.

They say, we cannot give our sister to a man who does not carry the sign of God’s covenant. But we will agree on one condition. If every man among you takes the sign of the covenant, as we have, then we will give our daughters to you, and take your daughters, and become one people.

Hamor and Shechem agree. Shechem is eager, because he delights in Jacob’s daughter. They go to the gate of their city and persuade the men. These men are peaceable with us, they say. Let them live in the land. And then they add, shall not their cattle and their substance and every beast of theirs be ours? The men of the city agree, hoping to gain Jacob’s riches. So every man of the city takes the sign.

But on the third day, while the men are still in pain and weak, two of Dinah’s brothers, Simeon and Levi, take their swords. They come upon the city boldly, and kill every man there, including Hamor and Shechem. They take Dinah out of Shechem’s house, and go away. Then the other sons of Jacob come and plunder the city. They take the flocks, the herds, the donkeys, all the wealth, and carry off the women and children as captives.

Then Jacob finally speaks.

Simeon and Levi answer their father with a question. Should he deal with our sister as with one who meant nothing? They felt that justice had to be done, and no one else would do it. But what they did went far beyond justice.

KEY 34:7: 'which thing ought not to be done.' Genesis is clear: what was done to Dinah was a great wrong. It should never have happened. The Bible does not excuse it or pass over it. God cares deeply about those who are hurt and dishonoured. And he calls his people to protect the vulnerable and to speak up for them, not to stay silent as Jacob did.

KEY 34:13: 'And the sons of Jacob answered Shechem and Hamor his father deceitfully'. The brothers used the holy sign of God’s covenant as a trick, to make their enemies weak so they could attack them. They took something sacred and turned it into a weapon. Their anger at the wrong done to their sister was right. But anger that is not brought to God can lead us to do terrible things. Two wrongs never make a right.

KEY 34:30: 'And Jacob said to Simeon and Levi, Ye have troubled me'. Jacob finally spoke, but only about the danger to himself. He said nothing about Dinah, and nothing about the wrong his sons had done to a whole city. He was worried about what the neighbours would think, and whether they would attack. Years later, on his deathbed, Jacob would speak more clearly about Simeon and Levi’s anger and cruelty. But here, the father’s silence and the sons’ violence both failed Dinah.

THREAD: Paul wrote, 'Recompense to no man evil for evil.' And, 'Dearly beloved, avenge not yourselves, but rather give place unto wrath: for it is written, Vengeance is mine; I will repay, saith the Lord.' And Proverbs says, 'Open thy mouth for the dumb in the cause of all such as are appointed to destruction.'

TODAY: If someone has hurt you, or someone you love, bring your anger to God. Do not let it grow into revenge. And if you know someone who is being hurt, do not stay silent. Speak up, and help them find safety.

QUESTION: Jacob stayed silent, and his sons took revenge. When you see something wrong, how can you respond in a way that is neither silent nor cruel?

PRAYER: Lord God, you see every wrong, and you care for those who are hurt. Comfort everyone who has been dishonoured or harmed. Give me courage to speak up for those who need help, and keep me from taking revenge. Teach me to leave justice in your hands. Amen.
