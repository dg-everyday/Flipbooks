# Genesis 39: Joseph in Potiphar's house, the LORD was with him, the temptation, the false accusation, prison, favour with the keeper. Keys 39:2, 39:9, 39:21.
TITLE: The LORD Was with Joseph

HOOK: A young slave runs out of a house in Egypt, leaving his cloak behind in a woman’s hand. He has done nothing wrong. In fact, he has just refused to do something very wrong. But within hours, he will be accused of a crime, and thrown into the king’s prison. Doing the right thing has cost him everything.

WHERE: In chapter thirty-seven, Joseph’s brothers sold him to traders, who took him to Egypt. In chapter thirty-eight, the story turned aside to Judah, who wronged his daughter-in-law Tamar, and then confessed, she hath been more righteous than I.

Now, in chapter thirty-nine, we return to Joseph, a slave in a foreign land. He is far from home, and no one there knows his family or his God. Listen for one phrase that is repeated again and again in this chapter. It is the secret of Joseph’s whole life.

STORY: Joseph is brought down to Egypt. Potiphar, an officer of Pharaoh and captain of the guard, an Egyptian, buys him from the Ishmeelites.

Potiphar sees that the LORD is with Joseph, and that the LORD makes all that he does to prosper. Joseph finds grace in his sight. Potiphar makes him overseer of his house, and puts everything he has into Joseph’s hand. And from that time, the LORD blesses the Egyptian’s house for Joseph’s sake. The blessing of the LORD is upon all that he has, in the house and in the field. Potiphar leaves everything in Joseph’s care, and does not worry about anything except the food he eats.

Joseph is a goodly person, and well favoured, handsome. And after a while, his master’s wife takes notice of him. She tries to tempt him into sin with her. But Joseph refuses.

Joseph says to her, my master trusts me completely, and does not know what goes on in the house. He has committed everything he has to my hand.

She speaks to Joseph day by day, but he will not listen to her, or even be near her. Joseph does more than say no. He stays away from temptation.

Then one day, Joseph goes into the house to do his work, and none of the men of the house are there. She catches him by his garment, and tries again to tempt him. And Joseph leaves his garment in her hand, and flees, and gets out of the house.

When she sees that he has left his garment in her hand and fled, she calls the men of her house. See, she says, he has brought in a Hebrew to mock us. He came in to me, and I cried with a loud voice, and he left his garment with me, and fled. She keeps the garment beside her until her husband comes home, and tells him the same story. Your Hebrew servant, whom you brought to us, came in to mock me.

Once again, Joseph’s garment is used to tell a lie about him. His brothers used his coat to deceive his father. Now his master’s wife uses his cloak to deceive his master.

When Potiphar hears his wife’s words, his anger is kindled. He takes Joseph and puts him into the prison, the place where the king’s prisoners are kept. Joseph is not even given a chance to defend himself.

Potiphar could have had Joseph put to death. Perhaps he did not fully believe his wife. But Joseph is now a prisoner. He has gone from favourite son, to slave, to prisoner, and each time, it was not his fault.

But even in prison, Joseph is not alone.

The keeper of the prison commits all the prisoners into Joseph’s hand. Whatever is done there, Joseph is the one who does it. The keeper does not need to check anything Joseph is in charge of, 'because the LORD was with him, and that which he did, the LORD made it to prosper.'

KEY 39:2: 'And the LORD was with Joseph, and he was a prosperous man; and he was in the house of his master the Egyptian.' Notice where Joseph was when the LORD was with him. Not at home with his father, but in a foreign land, as a slave. God’s presence does not depend on our circumstances. Joseph had lost his family, his freedom and his future. But he had not lost God. Wherever you are today, if you belong to God, the LORD is with you there.

KEY 39:9: 'There is none greater in this house than I; neither hath he kept back any thing from me but thee, because thou art his wife: how then can I do this great wickedness, and sin against God?' Joseph was young, far from home, and no one would ever know. But Joseph knew that God would know. He saw that this sin would not only betray his master’s trust. It would be a sin against God. Joseph did not argue with temptation. He named it for what it was, great wickedness, and when it would not stop, he ran. Sometimes the bravest thing to do is to run away.

KEY 39:21: 'But the LORD was with Joseph, and shewed him mercy, and gave him favour in the sight of the keeper of the prison.' Doing right had landed Joseph in prison. It would have been easy to become bitter, and to give up on God. But even in the dungeon, the LORD was with Joseph. God did not take Joseph out of prison straight away. He met him in it. And Joseph kept working faithfully, even there. God’s mercy can reach us in the darkest places.

THREAD: Paul wrote, 'There hath no temptation taken you but such as is common to man: but God is faithful'. And the letter to the Hebrews says of Jesus, he 'was in all points tempted like as we are, yet without sin.' And God promises, 'I will never leave thee, nor forsake thee.'

TODAY: Is there a temptation you keep finding yourself near? Do not just resist it. Stay away from it, and if you need to, run. And if doing the right thing has cost you something, remember that the LORD is with you, even there.

QUESTION: Joseph asked, how then can I do this great wickedness, and sin against God? What helps you to say no to temptation, even when no one else would know?

PRAYER: Lord God, thank you that you were with Joseph in the house of a stranger and in a prison cell, and that you are with me wherever I am. Give me the courage to say no to sin, and the wisdom to run from temptation. When doing right costs me something, help me to keep trusting you. Amen.
