# Genesis 49: Jacob blesses his twelve sons, Reuben, Simeon and Levi, Judah and Shiloh, I have waited for thy salvation, Joseph the fruitful bough, Jacob's death. Keys 49:10, 49:18, 49:22.
TITLE: Unto Him Shall the Gathering of the People Be

HOOK: Twelve grown men stand around their father’s bed. He is very old, and he knows he is about to die. He gathers his strength, and speaks to each son in turn, looking far into the future. Some of his words are hard to hear. Some are full of hope. And in the middle of them is a promise about a King who is still to come.

WHERE: In chapter forty-eight, Jacob blessed Joseph’s two sons, Ephraim and Manasseh, crossing his hands to put the younger before the elder. He spoke of the God which fed me all my life long, and the Angel which redeemed me from all evil.

Now, in chapter forty-nine, Jacob calls all twelve of his sons together for his last words. These words are written as poetry. They describe what will happen to each son’s descendants, the twelve tribes of Israel. And they show us that our choices matter, and that God’s grace reaches further than our failures.

STORY: Jacob calls his sons, and says, gather yourselves together, that I may tell you that which shall befall you in the last days. Hear, ye sons of Jacob, and hearken unto Israel your father.

He begins with Reuben, his firstborn. Reuben, thou art my firstborn, my might, and the beginning of my strength. But you are unstable as water, and you shall not excel, because you dishonoured your father. Reuben had the place of the firstborn. But years before, he had sinned against his father, and that sin cost him his place of leadership.

Next are Simeon and Levi, brothers in anger. Jacob remembers what they did at Shechem, when they took a cruel revenge for their sister Dinah. 'Cursed be their anger, for it was fierce; and their wrath, for it was cruel'. He says they will be divided and scattered in Israel. And yet, even here, there is grace. In time, the tribe of Levi would be scattered among all Israel as priests and servants of God’s house. God turned a judgment into a calling.

Then Jacob comes to Judah, and his words change completely. 'Judah, thou art he whom thy brethren shall praise'. Your hand shall be on the neck of your enemies, and your father’s children shall bow down before you. Judah is a lion’s whelp, a young lion. Judah, the brother who suggested selling Joseph, but who later offered his own life for Benjamin, receives the greatest blessing of all. Then Jacob looks far into the future.

Then Jacob speaks of the other brothers, briefly, one by one. Zebulun will live by the shore of the sea, a haven for ships. Issachar is a strong donkey, crouching between two burdens. He will bow his shoulder to work. Dan shall judge his people, as one of the tribes of Israel. Dan shall be a serpent by the way, an adder in the path, that bites the horse’s heels so that its rider falls backward.

And then, suddenly, in the middle of all these words, Jacob stops and prays.

Then Jacob goes on. Gad, a troop shall overcome him, but he shall overcome at the last. Asher’s bread shall be rich, and he shall yield royal dainties. Naphtali is a hind let loose, a deer set free. He gives goodly words.

Then Jacob comes to Joseph, the son he loved, the son he thought he had lost.

The archers have sorely grieved him, and shot at him, and hated him. But his bow abode in strength, made strong by the hands of the mighty God of Jacob. From there comes the shepherd, the stone of Israel. Jacob calls down on Joseph the blessings of heaven above, of the deep beneath, and of many children. The blessings shall be on the head of Joseph, on the crown of the head of him that was separate from his brethren.

Last of all comes Benjamin. 'Benjamin shall ravin as a wolf: in the morning he shall devour the prey, and at night he shall divide the spoil.' His tribe will be fierce and brave in battle.

'All these are the twelve tribes of Israel'. And Jacob blesses every one of them, each with his own blessing.

Then Jacob gives his final charge. I am to be gathered unto my people. Bury me with my fathers in the cave that is in the field of Machpelah, which Abraham bought for a burying place. There they buried Abraham and Sarah his wife. There they buried Isaac and Rebekah his wife. And there I buried Leah.

When Jacob has finished commanding his sons, he gathers up his feet into the bed, and yields up the ghost, and is gathered unto his people.

KEY 49:10: 'The sceptre shall not depart from Judah, nor a lawgiver from between his feet, until Shiloh come; and unto him shall the gathering of the people be.' A sceptre is the staff a king holds. Jacob promised that kings would come from Judah, until one came to whom it truly belonged. Shiloh has been understood to mean he whose right it is, or the one who brings peace. From Judah came King David. And from David’s line came Jesus, the King to whom all the peoples of the earth will one day gather.

KEY 49:18: 'I have waited for thy salvation, O LORD.' In the middle of his blessings, Jacob paused and prayed. He had seen the sins and failures of his sons. He knew that their descendants would face troubles. And he lifted his eyes from his family to God. I have waited for thy salvation. His whole life had been a long wait for God to fulfil his promises. And at the end, he was still waiting, still trusting. That is the heart of faith.

KEY 49:22: 'Joseph is a fruitful bough, even a fruitful bough by a well; whose branches run over the wall:' Joseph had been hated, attacked, thrown away and forgotten. But he had become a fruitful tree, planted by water, whose branches spread over the wall to bless others. The source of his fruitfulness was not his own strength, but the mighty God of Jacob. Those who stay close to God, like a tree beside a well, bear fruit even in hard places.

THREAD: In the book of Revelation, John hears these words: 'behold, the Lion of the tribe of Juda, the Root of David, hath prevailed'. And the psalm describes the person who loves God’s word: 'he shall be like a tree planted by the rivers of water, that bringeth forth his fruit in his season'.

TODAY: Like Jacob, lift your eyes from your own troubles and failures, and say to God, I wait for your salvation. And ask him to make you like Joseph, a fruitful bough by a well, whose life spills over to bless others.

QUESTION: Jacob prayed, I have waited for thy salvation, O LORD. What are you waiting for God to do, and how can you keep trusting him while you wait?

PRAYER: Lord God, I have waited for your salvation. Thank you for Jesus, the Lion of the tribe of Judah, the King to whom all peoples will gather. Make me like a fruitful bough by a well, drawing my strength from you. And help me to keep trusting you to the very end. Amen.
