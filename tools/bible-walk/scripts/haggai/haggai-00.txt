TITLE: Meet the Book of Haggai

HOOK: A man stands in the streets of Jerusalem. All around him, people are busy. They are building their own homes, with fine wooden walls. They are working hard in their fields. But up on the hill, where God’s temple once stood, there is only a pile of stones and a foundation with weeds growing through it. The man lifts his voice and says, thus saith the LORD of hosts. And what he says next will get a whole city building again.

BODY: Welcome to the book of Haggai, on The Living Voice. Haggai is one of the shortest books in the whole Bible. It has only two chapters, so on this walk it is just two steps. That makes it another short win.

Haggai is also the first book of the prophets on our walk. So what is a prophet? A prophet is someone God chose to carry his message to his people. The prophet did not make up the message. Again and again, Haggai says, thus saith the LORD, or, came the word of the LORD by Haggai the prophet. Haggai was the messenger. The message was God’s.

You have met Haggai before. In Ezra chapter five, we heard that when the people were stuck, God sent two prophets, Haggai and Zechariah, and the people rose up and built. The book of Haggai lets us hear what Haggai actually said.

Here is where his story fits. The people of Judah had come home from Babylon, as Cyrus allowed. They built an altar, and laid the foundation of the temple, and they sang for joy. But then their enemies opposed them and frightened them, and the work stopped. The foundation sat there for years. And little by little, the people got used to it. They built their own houses. They worked their own land. And they said, it is not the right time to build the house of the LORD.

Then God spoke through Haggai.

The book of Haggai is four short messages. Haggai is careful to tell us the exact day each one came, and they all came within a few months. The first message wakes the people up. The second encourages them when they feel small. The third teaches them about being clean before God. And the fourth is a promise to their leader.

We meet two leaders in this book. Zerubbabel is the governor of Judah, from the family of King David. Joshua is the high priest. In the book of Ezra, his name is spelled Jeshua, but he is the same man.

Here are three things to watch for as we walk.

Watch for the words, consider your ways. God says them more than once. It means, stop and think about how you are living, and what is happening because of it.

Watch for the words, I am with you. When the people obey, the very next thing God tells them is that he is with them. And he says it again when they feel discouraged.

And watch for the house of the LORD. For Haggai, the question was simple. Will God’s house come first, or our own?

Haggai’s book is short, but it reaches a long way forward. Zerubbabel, the governor in this book, appears again in the very first chapter of the New Testament, in the family line of Jesus. And the letter to the Hebrews quotes Haggai’s promise that God will shake the heavens and the earth.

QUESTION: Haggai’s people put their own houses first and left God’s house in ruins. What things in your life tend to crowd God out?

PRAYER: Lord God, as I walk through the book of Haggai, help me to hear your Word and to do it. Show me where I have put my own things first. Stir up my heart, as you stirred up your people long ago, and thank you that you promise to be with me. Amen.
