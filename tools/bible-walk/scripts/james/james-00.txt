TITLE: Meet the Book of James

HOOK: A man stands in front of a mirror. He sees a smudge on his cheek, and his hair sticking up. Then he turns away, walks out of the door, and forgets all about it. He goes through the whole day looking just as he did before. James says that is what it is like to hear God’s Word and not do it. It is like looking in a mirror, and walking away unchanged.

BODY: Welcome to the letter of James, on The Living Voice. We have walked a long way through the story of Israel, from Joshua all the way to King David. Now we turn to something different. James is not a story. It is a letter. It has five chapters, so on this walk it is five steps, one chapter at a time. You can finish the whole letter in less than a week. It is the first of a few short letters about living out our faith, day by day.

Who was James? There are several men named James in the New Testament. Two of them were among the twelve apostles. But this letter is usually linked with another James, a leader of the church in Jerusalem. Paul calls him 'James the Lord’s brother'. While Jesus was teaching and healing, his own family did not understand him. The Gospel of John says, 'For neither did his brethren believe in him.' But after Jesus rose from the dead, Paul tells us, 'he was seen of James'. And James, who had once doubted, became one of the leaders of the church.

Notice how he begins his letter. Not, James, the brother of Jesus. He could have said that. Instead he writes, 'James, a servant of God and of the Lord Jesus Christ'. He does not boast. He calls himself a servant.

He writes 'to the twelve tribes which are scattered abroad'. These were believers who had been scattered far from home. Many of them were poor, and many were going through hard times. James writes to them like a caring pastor. Again and again he calls them, my brethren, my beloved brethren.

James is a very practical book. It is full of short, sharp sayings, and pictures from everyday life. A wave of the sea, tossed by the wind. A horse with a bit in its mouth. A great ship, turned by a small rudder. A tiny spark that sets a whole forest on fire. A farmer, waiting patiently for the rain. Many people say that James sounds a lot like the Sermon on the Mount. That is no surprise. Again and again, James echoes the teaching of Jesus.

Here are three things to watch for as we walk.

Watch for the word doers. James does not want us only to listen to God’s Word, or only to agree with it. He wants us to do it. A faith that never changes the way we live, he says, is not a living faith at all.

Watch for the tongue. James has more to say about our words than almost any other book of the Bible. What we say, how we say it, and what we say about others when they are not there.

And watch for the poor. James cares deeply about the poor, the widows and the orphans, and about the way the rich and powerful treat them. He wants God’s people to treat every person with the same love and respect, whether rich or poor.

Some people have wondered whether James and Paul disagree. Paul writes, 'For by grace are ye saved through faith'. James writes, 'faith without works is dead'. But they are looking at the same truth from two sides. In the very next verse after his words about grace, Paul says we are 'created in Christ Jesus unto good works'. And James shows us what that faith looks like when it is lived. Real faith in God will always show itself in what we do.

QUESTION: James says that hearing God’s Word without doing it is like looking in a mirror and forgetting what you saw. What is one thing you have heard from God’s Word that you want to start doing?

PRAYER: Lord God, as I walk through the letter of James, speak to me through your Word. Do not let me be a hearer only. Give me a faith that shows itself in what I say and what I do, and in the way I treat others. Amen.
