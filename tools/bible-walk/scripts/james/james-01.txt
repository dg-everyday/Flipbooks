# James 1: greeting, count it all joy, patience, ask for wisdom, the wave of the sea, the double minded, rich and poor like grass, the crown of life, temptation is not from God, every good gift, swift to hear, the mirror, pure religion. Keys 1:2, 1:5, 1:22. Temptation's source told without "lust" or "conceived".
TITLE: Count It All Joy

HOOK: A goldsmith holds a lump of rough gold over a fierce fire. The gold melts, and the dirt and dross rise to the top, and he skims them away. He does it again, and again. The fire does not destroy the gold. It makes it pure. When it is finished, he can see his own face shining in it. James says that is what hard times can do to our faith.

WHERE: In the introduction, we heard that James was the Lord’s brother, who once had not believed, but became a leader of the church in Jerusalem. He wrote to believers scattered far from home, many of them poor and going through hard times. And we heard three things to watch for: being doers of the word, the tongue, and care for the poor.

Now, in chapter one, James goes straight to one of the hardest questions there is. What do we do when life is hard? Listen for joy in a surprising place. And listen for a man and a mirror.

STORY: James begins with a short greeting. 'James, a servant of God and of the Lord Jesus Christ, to the twelve tribes which are scattered abroad, greeting.' And then, with no warm-up at all, he says something that surprises everyone who hears it for the first time.

Then he tells us why. 'Knowing this, that the trying of your faith worketh patience. But let patience have her perfect work, that ye may be perfect and entire, wanting nothing.' Trying means testing, like gold tested in the fire. Patience means steady endurance, the strength to keep going. And perfect here means grown up, mature. Trials are not wasted. God uses them to make us strong.

But how do we get through a trial? We need wisdom. And James has good news about wisdom.

There is one condition. 'But let him ask in faith, nothing wavering. For he that wavereth is like a wave of the sea driven with the wind and tossed.' Picture a wave, pushed this way by the wind, and then that way. James says a person who asks God for help, but does not really trust him, is like that. 'A double minded man is unstable in all his ways.'

Then James speaks to the poor and the rich. The believer who is poor can rejoice, because in God’s eyes he has been lifted up. The believer who is rich should rejoice in being brought low, because riches do not last. Like the flower of the grass, James says, the rich man shall pass away. The sun rises with its burning heat, and the flower withers and falls.

Then comes a promise for everyone who is going through a hard time. 'Blessed is the man that endureth temptation: for when he is tried, he shall receive the crown of life, which the Lord hath promised to them that love him.'

Now, James is careful here. The word temptation can mean a trial from outside, or a pull towards sin from inside. And James makes something very clear. When we are tempted to do wrong, we must never blame God. 'Let no man say when he is tempted, I am tempted of God: for God cannot be tempted with evil, neither tempteth he any man'. The pull towards sin comes from our own wrong desires. And if we follow them, they lead to sin, and sin leads to death.

So what does come from God? Only good. 'Every good gift and every perfect gift is from above, and cometh down from the Father of lights, with whom is no variableness, neither shadow of turning.' God made the lights in the sky, the sun, the moon and the stars. They move and change, and their shadows shift through the day. But God never changes. He is always good.

Then comes some very practical advice. 'Wherefore, my beloved brethren, let every man be swift to hear, slow to speak, slow to wrath: For the wrath of man worketh not the righteousness of God.' Quick to listen. Slow to speak. Slow to become angry.

So put away what is wrong, James says, and humbly receive the word God has planted in you. And then he says the words that sum up this whole letter.

He explains with a picture. 'For if any be a hearer of the word, and not a doer, he is like unto a man beholding his natural face in a glass: For he beholdeth himself, and goeth his way, and straightway forgetteth what manner of man he was.' A glass means a mirror. But the one who looks closely into God’s Word, and keeps on doing what it says, will be blessed in what he does.

And James ends the chapter by telling us what real religion looks like. It is not just words. Someone who seems religious, but cannot control his tongue, is fooling himself. 'Pure religion and undefiled before God and the Father is this, To visit the fatherless and widows in their affliction, and to keep himself unspotted from the world.' Caring for those in need, and keeping our lives clean. That is faith in action.

KEY 1:2: 'My brethren, count it all joy when ye fall into divers temptations'. Divers means many different kinds. Temptations here means trials and testings. Count it all joy. James does not say the trial itself is a joy. Pain is still pain. But he says, count it, reckon it, as joy, because of what God is doing through it. Like gold in the fire, our faith is being made stronger and purer. That is something we can rejoice in, even with tears in our eyes.

KEY 1:5: 'If any of you lack wisdom, let him ask of God, that giveth to all men liberally, and upbraideth not; and it shall be given him.' Liberally means generously. Upbraideth not means he does not scold. When you do not know what to do, you can ask God for wisdom, and he will not tell you off for asking. He will not say, you should know this already. He gives generously, to all who ask. That is a promise you can bring to him today.

KEY 1:22: 'But be ye doers of the word, and not hearers only, deceiving your own selves.' It is possible to hear God’s Word every day, and still not be changed by it. James says that if we only listen, and never do, we are fooling ourselves. The Bible is not just to be heard, or read, or even understood. It is to be lived.

THREAD: Jesus said, 'Therefore whosoever heareth these sayings of mine, and doeth them, I will liken him unto a wise man, which built his house upon a rock'. And Paul wrote, 'tribulation worketh patience; And patience, experience; and experience, hope'.

TODAY: Think of one thing you have heard from God’s Word recently. A command, a promise, or a word of advice. What would it look like to do it today? And if you are going through a hard time, ask God for wisdom. He will give it generously.

QUESTION: James says, count it all joy when trials come, because they make our faith stronger. How has God used a hard time in your life to help you grow?

PRAYER: Lord God, thank you that every good and perfect gift comes from you, and that you never change. When I go through hard times, help me to trust you, and to count it all joy. Give me wisdom when I do not know what to do. And help me to be a doer of your Word, and not a hearer only. Amen.
