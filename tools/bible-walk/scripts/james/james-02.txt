# James 2: no respect of persons, the gold ring and the vile raiment, God hath chosen the poor, the royal law, guilty of all, mercy rejoiceth against judgment, the cold and hungry brother, faith alone is dead, the devils believe, Abraham, Rahab. Keys 2:5, 2:18, 2:26. Rahab told without "harlot" (2:25 not quoted); 2:11 not quoted; 2:24 told neutrally.
TITLE: Faith Without Works Is Dead

HOOK: A doctor kneels beside a man lying very still. She feels for a pulse at his wrist. Nothing. She listens for a breath. Nothing. He looks like a man. He has hands, and feet, and a face. But there is no life in him. James says that a faith which does nothing is like that. It may look like faith from the outside. But there is no life in it.

WHERE: In chapter one, James told us to count it all joy when trials come, because the testing of our faith makes us strong. If we lack wisdom, we can ask God, who gives generously. And he said the words that sum up his whole letter: be ye doers of the word, and not hearers only.

Now, in chapter two, James shows us what that looks like. Listen for how we treat people who are different from us. And listen for one of the most talked about passages in the whole letter, about faith and works.

STORY: James begins with a warning. 'My brethren, have not the faith of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Lord of glory, with respect of persons.' Respect of persons means favouritism, treating some people better than others because of how they look, or what they have.

Then he paints a picture. Two visitors come into your meeting. One wears a gold ring and fine clothes. The other is poor, and dressed in shabby clothes. And you say to the rich man, please, sit here in a good place. But you say to the poor man, you stand over there, or sit on the floor by my feet. James asks, have you not become judges with evil thoughts?

Then he says, listen, my beloved brethren.

And it was often the rich, James reminds them, who were treating them badly, and dragging them into court.

There is a better way. 'If ye fulfil the royal law according to the scripture, Thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself, ye do well'. It is called the royal law because it comes from the King himself. Jesus said it was one of the two greatest commandments. And loving your neighbour means loving every neighbour, rich or poor.

James goes on. Favouritism is not a small thing. It is sin. 'For whosoever shall keep the whole law, and yet offend in one point, he is guilty of all.' The law of God is like a pane of glass. You only need to break it in one place, and it is broken.

So speak and act, James says, as people who will be judged by God. And then he adds a beautiful line. 'For he shall have judgment without mercy, that hath shewed no mercy; and mercy rejoiceth against judgment.' Those who show mercy will receive mercy. Mercy wins.

Now James comes to his great question. 'What doth it profit, my brethren, though a man say he hath faith, and have not works? can faith save him?'

He gives an example. Imagine a brother or sister with no clothes to keep them warm, and no food for the day. And one of you says to them, go in peace, keep warm, and eat well. But you give them nothing. What good is that? Kind words, with no help, are empty.

'Even so faith, if it hath not works, is dead, being alone.'

Then James imagines someone arguing with him. You have faith, he says, and I have works. James answers that you cannot really separate the two.

Then he says something sharp. 'Thou believest that there is one God; thou doest well: the devils also believe, and tremble.' Even the devils know that God is real. Believing that God exists is not the same as trusting him, and following him.

So James turns to two people from the Old Testament. First, Abraham. When God asked him to offer his son Isaac on the altar, Abraham obeyed. James says, 'Seest thou how faith wrought with his works, and by works was faith made perfect?' Abraham trusted God, and his trust showed itself in what he did. 'And the scripture was fulfilled which saith, Abraham believed God, and it was imputed unto him for righteousness: and he was called the Friend of God.' The Friend of God. What a name.

And then Rahab, the woman of Jericho, whom we met in the book of Joshua. She had heard what the LORD had done, and she believed. And her faith moved her to act. She welcomed the two spies, hid them, and sent them out another way to safety. An outsider, with a past, yet James puts her right beside Abraham. Faith is for everyone who will trust God, and show it.

Christians have talked about these verses for a long time, and explained them in different ways. But James’s main point is plain. A faith that does nothing is not living faith.

KEY 2:5: 'Hearken, my beloved brethren, Hath not God chosen the poor of this world rich in faith, and heirs of the kingdom which he hath promised to them that love him?' The world looks at the rich, and thinks they are the important ones. But God often chooses the poor, and makes them rich in faith. When we look down on someone because they are poor, we are looking down on someone God may have chosen. In God’s kingdom, a poor person who loves God is truly rich.

KEY 2:18: 'Yea, a man may say, Thou hast faith, and I have works: shew me thy faith without thy works, and I will shew thee my faith by my works.' Faith is something inside us. No one can see it directly. But they can see what it does. James says, show me. Faith is like the wind. You cannot see the wind itself, but you can see the leaves move. People will not see our faith, but they will see our kindness, our honesty, and our love.

KEY 2:26: 'For as the body without the spirit is dead, so faith without works is dead also.' A body without breath is not a living person. And faith without works is not a living faith. James is not saying that we can earn God’s love by doing good things. He is saying that when faith is real, it is alive. And living things grow, and move, and bear fruit.

THREAD: John wrote, 'My little children, let us not love in word, neither in tongue; but in deed and in truth.' And Jesus said of false teachers, 'Ye shall know them by their fruits.'

TODAY: Is there someone in need near you, someone you could help in a practical way? Not just with kind words, but with kind actions. And is there anyone you have treated as less important? Ask God to help you see every person as he sees them.

QUESTION: James says, show me thy faith by thy works. If someone watched your life this week, what would they see that shows you trust God?

PRAYER: Lord God, thank you that you chose me, not because I was rich or important, but because of your love. Forgive me for the times I have treated some people better than others. Give me a faith that is alive, a faith that shows itself in love, in mercy, and in helping those in need. Amen.
