# John 13: Jesus washes the disciples' feet, Peter's protest, an example, the betrayer revealed, Judas goes out, a new commandment, Peter's denial foretold. Keys 13:14, 13:34, 13:35.
TITLE: Love One Another, As I Have Loved You

HOOK: The disciples are reclining around a table for supper. Their feet are dusty from the roads. There is a basin of water by the door, and a towel, but no servant to wash their feet, and none of them offers. Then Jesus gets up. He takes off his outer robe, ties a towel around his waist, pours water into the basin, and kneels down in front of them.

WHERE: In chapter twelve, Mary poured costly ointment on Jesus’ feet. The crowds welcomed him into Jerusalem, crying, Hosanna. Some Greeks came, saying, Sir, we would see Jesus. And Jesus said that his hour had come, and that like a corn of wheat, he must fall into the ground and die.

Now, in chapter thirteen, the second half of John’s Gospel begins. For the next five chapters, Jesus is alone with his closest disciples, at the last supper before the cross. Here, he shows them his love in the humblest way, and gives them a new commandment.

STORY: Before the feast of the passover, Jesus knows that his hour has come, that he should depart out of this world unto the Father. 'having loved his own which were in the world, he loved them unto the end.'

Supper is in progress. The devil has already put into the heart of Judas Iscariot, Simon’s son, to betray him. Jesus knows that the Father has given all things into his hands, and that he came from God, and goes to God.

And knowing all that, what does Jesus do with all things in his hands? He rises from supper, lays aside his garments, takes a towel, and girds himself. He pours water into a basin, and begins to wash the disciples’ feet, and to wipe them with the towel. Washing feet was the work of the lowest servant.

He comes to Simon Peter. Peter says, Lord, dost thou wash my feet? Jesus answers, what I do thou knowest not now, but thou shalt know hereafter. Peter says, thou shalt never wash my feet. Jesus answers, if I wash thee not, thou hast no part with me. Then Peter, all or nothing as always, says, Lord, not my feet only, but also my hands and my head. Jesus says, he that is washed needeth not save to wash his feet, but is clean every whit. And ye are clean, but not all. For he knew who should betray him.

After he has washed their feet, and put his garments back on, he sits down again. Know ye what I have done to you? Ye call me Master and Lord, and ye say well, for so I am.

For I have given you an example, Jesus says, that ye should do as I have done to you. The servant is not greater than his lord. If ye know these things, happy are ye if ye do them.

Then Jesus is troubled in spirit, and says, verily, verily, I say unto you, that one of you shall betray me. The disciples look at one another, wondering who he means. The disciple whom Jesus loves is leaning close beside Jesus. Peter signals to him to ask who it is. He asks, Lord, who is it? Jesus answers, he it is, to whom I shall give a sop, a piece of bread, when I have dipped it. And he dips the bread, and gives it to Judas Iscariot. Offering a piece of bread like that was a gesture of friendship. Even now, Jesus is offering Judas his love.

After the bread, Satan enters into Judas. Jesus says to him, that thou doest, do quickly. No one at the table understands. Some think that, because Judas has the bag, Jesus is telling him to buy what they need for the feast, or to give something to the poor. Judas, having received the bread, goes immediately out. 'and it was night.'

When he has gone out, Jesus says, now is the Son of man glorified, and God is glorified in him. Little children, yet a little while I am with you. Whither I go, ye cannot come.

Then Jesus gives them a new commandment.

Simon Peter says, Lord, whither goest thou? Jesus answers, whither I go, thou canst not follow me now, but thou shalt follow me afterwards. Peter says, Lord, why cannot I follow thee now? I will lay down my life for thy sake. Jesus answers, wilt thou lay down thy life for my sake? Verily, verily, I say unto thee, the cock shall not crow, till thou hast denied me thrice.

KEY 13:14: 'If I then, your Lord and Master, have washed your feet; ye also ought to wash one another’s feet.' The Lord of all knelt down and did the dirtiest job in the house. If he was willing to serve like that, then no task is beneath any of his followers. Christians have understood this command in different ways. But all agree on its heart: humble, practical, loving service. Greatness in God’s kingdom is measured by how much we serve.

KEY 13:34: 'A new commandment I give unto you, That ye love one another; as I have loved you, that ye also love one another.' The command to love was not new. But the measure was new: as I have loved you. Jesus had just washed the feet of men who would run away, and of Peter, who would deny him, and even of Judas, who would betray him. That is the love we are called to show one another. Not love for those who deserve it, but love like his.

KEY 13:35: 'By this shall all men know that ye are my disciples, if ye have love one to another.' Jesus did not say that people would know his disciples by their knowledge, or their buildings, or their miracles. He said they would know them by their love. The love that Christians show to one another is meant to be a sign to the whole world, pointing to Jesus. When we fail to love, we hide him. When we love, we show him.

THREAD: Paul wrote that Jesus 'made himself of no reputation, and took upon him the form of a servant'. And John, who leaned on Jesus at that supper, wrote years later, 'Beloved, if God so loved us, we ought also to love one another.'

TODAY: Look for one humble, practical way to serve someone today, a job no one else wants to do. Do it quietly, as Jesus did. And think of a fellow believer you find hard to love. Ask Jesus to help you love them as he has loved you.

QUESTION: Jesus washed his disciples’ feet, even the feet of Judas. Who is Jesus asking you to serve or to love, even though it is hard?

PRAYER: Lord Jesus, you took a towel and washed your disciples’ feet. Thank you for loving me unto the end. Make me humble enough to serve others, and help me to love my brothers and sisters as you have loved me, so that the world may know that I am your disciple. Amen.
