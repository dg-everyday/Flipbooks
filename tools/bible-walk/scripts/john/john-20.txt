# John 20: the empty tomb, Peter and John run, Mary Magdalene meets the risen Jesus, Jesus appears to the disciples, peace be unto you, Thomas doubts and believes, why John wrote. Keys 20:16, 20:29, 20:31.
TITLE: That Ye Might Believe

HOOK: Early, while it is still dark, a woman walks alone to a garden tomb, carrying her grief. When she arrives, the great stone has been rolled away, and the tomb is empty. Someone has taken him, she thinks. She stands outside, weeping. A man asks her, why are you weeping? She thinks he is the gardener. Then he says one word: her name.

WHERE: In chapter nineteen, Jesus was crowned with thorns and crucified at Golgotha. From the cross he cared for his mother, and then said, it is finished, and gave up his spirit. Joseph of Arimathea and Nicodemus wrapped his body in linen with spices, and laid him in a new tomb in a garden.

Now, in chapter twenty, it is the third day. This is the chapter everything has been leading to. Listen for how the risen Jesus meets different people in different ways: a weeping woman, frightened disciples, and a doubting friend. And at the end, John tells us why he wrote his book.

STORY: On the third day, Mary Magdalene comes early, while it is yet dark, to the sepulchre, and sees the stone taken away from the tomb. She runs to Simon Peter, and to the other disciple, whom Jesus loved, and says, they have taken away the Lord out of the sepulchre, and we know not where they have laid him.

Peter and the other disciple run together to the tomb. The other disciple outruns Peter, and comes first to the sepulchre. Stooping down, he sees the linen clothes lying, but he does not go in. Then Simon Peter comes, and goes into the sepulchre, and sees the linen clothes lying, and the napkin that had been about his head, not lying with the linen clothes, but wrapped together in a place by itself. It does not look like a robbery. Robbers would not leave the grave clothes neatly behind. Then the other disciple goes in too, 'and he saw, and believed.' For as yet they knew not the scripture, that he must rise again from the dead. The disciples go away again to their own home.

But Mary stands outside the sepulchre, weeping. As she weeps, she stoops down and looks in, and sees two angels in white sitting, one at the head, and the other at the feet, where the body of Jesus had lain. They say to her, woman, why weepest thou? She says, because they have taken away my Lord, and I know not where they have laid him.

She turns around, and sees Jesus standing, and does not know that it is Jesus. Jesus says to her, woman, why weepest thou? Whom seekest thou? She, supposing him to be the gardener, says, sir, if thou have borne him hence, tell me where thou hast laid him, and I will take him away.

Then Jesus says one word.

Jesus says to her, touch me not, or, do not hold on to me, for I am not yet ascended to my Father. But go to my brethren, and say unto them, I ascend unto my Father, and your Father, and to my God, and your God. Mary Magdalene comes and tells the disciples that she has seen the Lord. She is the first witness of the resurrection.

That same day, the disciples are gathered behind shut doors, for fear of the leaders. Jesus comes and stands in the middle of them, and says, 'Peace be unto you.' He shows them his hands and his side. 'Then were the disciples glad, when they saw the Lord.'

Jesus says to them again, peace be unto you. As my Father hath sent me, even so send I you. And he breathes on them, and says, receive ye the Holy Ghost.

But Thomas, one of the twelve, is not with them when Jesus comes. The other disciples tell him, we have seen the Lord. But he says, except I shall see in his hands the print of the nails, and put my finger into the print of the nails, and thrust my hand into his side, I will not believe.

After eight days, the disciples are inside again, and Thomas with them. The doors are shut. Jesus comes, and stands in the middle, and says, peace be unto you. Then he says to Thomas, reach hither thy finger, and behold my hands. And reach hither thy hand, and thrust it into my side. And be not faithless, but believing. Jesus knew exactly what Thomas had said. And he came for him.

Thomas answers, 'My Lord and my God.' The disciple who doubted makes the clearest statement of faith in the whole Gospel.

Then Jesus speaks words meant for every believer since.

Many other signs truly did Jesus in the presence of his disciples, John says, which are not written in this book.

KEY 20:16: 'Jesus saith unto her, Mary. She turned herself, and saith unto him, Rabboni; which is to say, Master.' Mary did not recognise Jesus by his appearance, or by an argument. She recognised him when he spoke her name. Jesus said in chapter ten that the good shepherd calls his own sheep by name, and they know his voice. The risen Lord knows each of his people by name. He knows yours.

KEY 20:29: 'Jesus saith unto him, Thomas, because thou hast seen me, thou hast believed: blessed are they that have not seen, and yet have believed.' Thomas saw, and believed. But Jesus pronounced a special blessing on everyone who would believe without seeing him with their own eyes. That includes you, if you trust in him. You have not seen the nail prints, but you have heard the witness of those who did. And Jesus calls you blessed.

KEY 20:31: 'But these are written, that ye might believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God; and that believing ye might have life through his name.' This is why John wrote his whole Gospel. Every sign, every I AM saying, every conversation was written for this purpose. That you might believe. And that believing, you might have life. Not just facts about Jesus, but life through his name.

THREAD: Paul wrote that Christ 'rose again the third day according to the scriptures', and that he was seen by Peter, then by the twelve, then by more than five hundred at once. And Peter wrote, 'Whom having not seen, ye love; in whom, though now ye see him not, yet believing, ye rejoice with joy unspeakable'.

TODAY: Like Thomas, do you have doubts? Be honest with Jesus about them. He came for Thomas, and he will meet you too. And remember that he knows your name, as he knew Mary’s. Today, say to him, as Thomas did, my Lord and my God.

QUESTION: Jesus said, blessed are they that have not seen, and yet have believed. What helps you to believe in Jesus, even though you have not seen him?

PRAYER: Risen Lord Jesus, thank you that you are alive, and that you know me by name. Thank you that you met Mary in her grief and Thomas in his doubt. Meet me where I am. You are my Lord and my God. Help me to believe, and to have life through your name. Amen.
