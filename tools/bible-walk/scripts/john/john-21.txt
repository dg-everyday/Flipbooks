# John 21: fishing on the sea of Tiberias, nothing caught, cast the net on the right side, it is the Lord, breakfast by the fire, lovest thou me, feed my sheep, follow thou me, the end of John's book. Keys 21:6, 21:15, 21:17.
TITLE: Lovest Thou Me? Feed My Sheep

HOOK: Seven tired fishermen are rowing back to shore with empty nets. They have worked all through the dark and caught nothing. A man is standing on the beach. He calls out, children, have ye any meat? No, they answer. Cast the net on the right side of the ship, he says. They do, and the net is suddenly so full of fish that they cannot pull it in. And one of them says, it is the Lord.

WHERE: In chapter twenty, Mary Magdalene found the empty tomb, and the risen Jesus called her by name. He appeared to his disciples behind shut doors and said, peace be unto you. Thomas doubted, until Jesus showed him his hands and side, and Thomas said, my Lord and my God. And John wrote, these are written, that ye might believe.

Now we come to the last chapter of John. It is like an epilogue, a final scene by the sea where it all began. Peter, who denied Jesus three times, has not yet heard Jesus speak about his failure. In this chapter, Jesus restores him, and gives him a new calling.

STORY: After these things, Jesus shows himself again to the disciples at the sea of Tiberias, another name for the sea of Galilee. Simon Peter, Thomas, Nathanael, the sons of Zebedee, and two other disciples are together. Simon Peter says, I go a fishing. They say, we also go with thee. They go out, and enter into a ship immediately. 'and that night they caught nothing.'

As the day is breaking, Jesus stands on the shore, but the disciples do not know that it is Jesus. Jesus says to them, children, have ye any meat? They answer him, no.

Then he tells them what to do.

The disciple whom Jesus loves says to Peter, it is the Lord. When Simon Peter hears that it is the Lord, he wraps his fisher’s coat around him, and throws himself into the sea, and swims for the shore. Peter could not wait. The other disciples come in a little boat, dragging the net full of fishes, for they are not far from land, about a hundred metres.

As soon as they come to land, they see a fire of coals there, with fish laid on it, and bread. The last time Peter stood by a fire of coals was in the high priest’s courtyard, when he denied Jesus three times.

Jesus says, bring of the fish which ye have now caught. Simon Peter goes up, and draws the net to land, full of great fishes, a hundred and fifty and three. And for all there are so many, the net is not broken. Jesus says, come and dine. None of the disciples dare ask him, who art thou? knowing that it is the Lord. Jesus comes, takes bread, and gives it to them, and fish likewise. This is the third time Jesus shows himself to his disciples after he is risen from the dead.

When they have eaten, Jesus turns to Peter.

Jesus asks him again the second time, Simon, son of Jonas, lovest thou me? Peter says, yea, Lord; thou knowest that I love thee. Jesus says, feed my sheep.

And then a third time.

Three denials, by a fire. Three questions, by a fire. Jesus did not ignore Peter’s failure, but he did not condemn him either. He gave Peter the chance to say, three times, I love you. And three times, he gave him a task.

Then Jesus tells Peter what lies ahead. When you were young, you dressed yourself and went where you wanted. But when you are old, you will stretch out your hands, and another will dress you, and carry you where you would not. John explains that Jesus said this to signify by what death Peter would glorify God. And when Jesus has spoken this, he says to Peter, 'Follow me.'

Peter turns around, and sees the disciple whom Jesus loves following them. He asks, Lord, and what shall this man do? Jesus says, if I will that he tarry till I come, what is that to thee? 'follow thou me.' Peter did not need to compare his path with anyone else’s. He just needed to follow Jesus.

Then John signs his name, in a way. This is the disciple which testifieth of these things, and wrote these things: and we know that his testimony is true.

And John closes his book with these words. And there are also many other things which Jesus did, the which, if they should be written every one, I suppose that even the world itself could not contain the books that should be written. Amen.

KEY 21:6: 'And he said unto them, Cast the net on the right side of the ship, and ye shall find. They cast therefore, and now they were not able to draw it for the multitude of fishes.' These men were experienced fishermen. They had worked hard all through the dark, and caught nothing. Then one word from Jesus changed everything. Our own efforts, however skilled, are not enough on their own. When we listen to Jesus and obey, even in small things, he brings an abundance we could never produce ourselves.

KEY 21:15: 'So when they had dined, Jesus saith to Simon Peter, Simon, son of Jonas, lovest thou me more than these? He saith unto him, Yea, Lord; thou knowest that I love thee. He saith unto him, Feed my lambs.' Jesus did not ask Peter, are you sorry? or, will you promise never to fail again? He asked, do you love me? That is the question that matters most. And then he gave Peter a job. Feed my lambs. Jesus does not set aside those who have failed. He restores them, and sends them to care for others.

KEY 21:17: 'He saith unto him the third time, Simon, son of Jonas, lovest thou me? Peter was grieved because he said unto him the third time, Lovest thou me? And he said unto him, Lord, thou knowest all things; thou knowest that I love thee. Jesus saith unto him, Feed my sheep.' Peter was grieved. The third question touched the deepest wound. But Peter did not boast this time, as he once had, I will lay down my life for thy sake. He simply said, Lord, you know everything. You know my heart. And Jesus trusted him with his sheep. Peter went on to become a great shepherd of God’s people.

THREAD: Peter later wrote to church leaders, 'Feed the flock of God which is among you'. He wrote that 'when the chief Shepherd shall appear, ye shall receive a crown of glory that fadeth not away.' The man who once denied Jesus spent the rest of his life feeding his sheep.

TODAY: Have you failed Jesus in some way, and wondered whether he could still use you? Hear his question: lovest thou me? Answer him honestly. And then hear his call: follow me. Do not compare your path with anyone else’s. Just follow him.

QUESTION: We have walked through the whole Gospel of John. Jesus asked Peter, lovest thou me? How would you answer Jesus today, and how will you follow him?

PRAYER: Lord Jesus, you know all things, and you know that I love you, even when I fail you. Thank you for your forgiveness, and for giving me a new start. Help me to feed your sheep, to care for others, and to follow you, wherever you lead. Amen.

NEXT: That is the end of our walk through the Gospel of John. Well walked. John wrote these things, that ye might believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God, and that believing ye might have life through his name. May you walk on with him.
