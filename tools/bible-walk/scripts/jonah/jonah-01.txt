# Jonah 1: the call to Nineveh, Jonah flees to Tarshish, the great wind, the sleeping prophet, the lots, I fear the LORD, cast into the sea, the sailors fear the LORD, the great fish. Keys 1:3, 1:9, 1:17.
TITLE: Jonah Rose Up to Flee

HOOK: A ship is caught in a terrible storm. Waves crash over the deck. The sailors are throwing the cargo into the sea, and every man is crying out to his own god. But down below, in the bottom of the ship, one passenger is fast asleep. He is the only one on board who knows the true God. And he is the reason the storm has come.

WHERE: In the introduction, we heard that Jonah was a real prophet of Israel, and that God sent him to Nineveh, the great city of Israel’s enemies, Assyria. We heard that Jonah is almost all story, and that it is really about the heart of God.

Now, in chapter one, God gives Jonah a command, and Jonah runs the other way. Listen for how many times the story says that Jonah went down. And notice how the pagan sailors come to fear the LORD, while the prophet runs from him.

STORY: The word of the LORD comes to Jonah, the son of Amittai, saying, arise, go to Nineveh, that great city, and cry against it; for their wickedness is come up before me. God has seen the evil of Nineveh. He is sending his prophet to warn them.

But Jonah gets up, not to go to Nineveh, but to run away.

Tarshish was probably in the far west, across the Great Sea, perhaps as far as Spain. Nineveh was far to the east. Jonah is heading in exactly the opposite direction, as far as a ship can take him.

But the LORD sends out a great wind on the sea. A mighty storm rises, so that the ship is about to break apart.

The sailors are terrified. Every man cries out to his own god. They throw the cargo overboard, to make the ship lighter. But Jonah has gone down into the inside of the ship, and has lain down, and is fast asleep.

Down, down, down. Jonah went down to Joppa. He went down into the ship. He went down into the bottom of the ship. When we run from God, the way only goes down.

The captain comes to him, and says, what meanest thou, O sleeper? Get up! Call upon thy God! Perhaps your God will think about us, 'that we perish not.' The pagan captain is begging the prophet of God to pray.

The sailors say to each other, come, let us cast lots, to find out whose fault this trouble is. Casting lots was a bit like drawing straws. They cast lots, and the lot falls on Jonah.

Then they crowd around him with questions. Tell us, why has this trouble come upon us? What is your work? Where do you come from? What is your country? Who are your people?

And Jonah answers.

Then the men are terribly afraid. They say, why have you done this? For Jonah has told them that he is running from the presence of the LORD. The God who made the sea, and Jonah thought he could escape him by sailing on it!

They ask him, what shall we do to you, that the sea may be calm for us? For the sea is growing wilder and wilder.

Jonah says, 'Take me up, and cast me forth into the sea; so shall the sea be calm unto you: for I know that for my sake this great tempest is upon you.'

But the sailors do not want to do it. They row hard to bring the ship back to land. But they cannot, for the sea grows wilder and wilder against them.

So they cry out, not to their own gods this time, but to the LORD. We beseech thee, O LORD, we beseech thee, let us not perish for this man’s life, and do not hold us guilty of innocent blood. For thou, O LORD, hast done as it pleased thee.

Then they take Jonah, and throw him into the sea. 'and the sea ceased from her raging.' Instantly, the storm is over.

The sailors stand on the deck, staring at the calm water. 'Then the men feared the LORD exceedingly, and offered a sacrifice unto the LORD, and made vows.' These pagan men, who began the chapter crying to their own gods, end it worshipping the true God. Even while Jonah was running away from God’s mission, God was using him to bring people to himself.

And Jonah sinks beneath the waves. But God is not finished with him.

KEY 1:3: 'But Jonah rose up to flee unto Tarshish from the presence of the LORD, and went down to Joppa; and he found a ship going to Tarshish: so he paid the fare thereof, and went down into it, to go with them unto Tarshish from the presence of the LORD.' Twice the verse says it: from the presence of the LORD. Jonah knew, better than anyone on that ship, that God is everywhere. And still he ran. Running from God always costs something. Jonah paid the fare. But he would soon find that no ship sails far enough to leave God behind.

KEY 1:9: 'And he said unto them, I am an Hebrew; and I fear the LORD, the God of heaven, which hath made the sea and the dry land.' Jonah says all the right words. I fear the LORD, the God who made the sea. And yet he is running from that very God, across that very sea. It is possible to know all the right things about God, and say them, and still be running from him. The sailors heard Jonah’s words, and took them more seriously than he did.

KEY 1:17: 'Now the LORD had prepared a great fish to swallow up Jonah. And Jonah was in the belly of the fish three days and three nights.' Notice the word prepared. The fish was not an accident. God had it ready. What looked like the end for Jonah was really God’s rescue. Jonah deserved to drown, but God, in mercy, caught him. And Jesus would later say that Jonah’s three days in the fish were a sign of his own three days in the grave, before he rose again.

THREAD: A psalm says, 'Whither shall I go from thy spirit? or whither shall I flee from thy presence? If I ascend up into heaven, thou art there'. And Jesus said, 'For as Jonas was three days and three nights in the whale’s belly; so shall the Son of man be three days and three nights in the heart of the earth.'

TODAY: Is there something God has been asking you to do, and you have been going the other way? It might be a person to forgive, or a word to say, or a habit to leave behind. Stop running. Turn around, and talk to God about it today.

QUESTION: The sailors came to fear the LORD, even while Jonah was running away from him. How has God used you, or someone else, even at a time when you were not following him well?

PRAYER: Lord God, maker of the sea and the dry land, I cannot run from your presence. Forgive me for the times I have gone the other way. Thank you that even when I run, you come after me in mercy. Turn me around, and help me to obey you. Amen.
