TITLE: Meet the Book of Joshua

HOOK: A great leader has died. For a whole lifetime, Moses led the people of Israel out of slavery and through the wilderness. Now he is gone. A whole nation stands on the bank of a river in flood, looking across at the land God promised them long ago. And the job of leading them over falls to one man. His name is Joshua. And the first thing God says to him is, be strong.

BODY: Welcome to the book of Joshua, on The Living Voice. Joshua has twenty four chapters, so on this walk it is twenty four steps, one chapter at a time.

Joshua comes straight after the five books of Moses. Those books end with Moses on a mountain, looking out over the promised land, the land he would never enter himself. The book of Joshua picks up the story right there. It tells how the people of Israel crossed the river Jordan, came into the land of Canaan, and settled in it, tribe by tribe.

Joshua himself is not new to us. He was Moses’ helper from the time of the Exodus. He led Israel’s army when they fought Amalek in the wilderness. He went up the mountain with Moses when God gave the law. And he was one of the twelve spies sent into Canaan. Ten of them came back afraid. Only Joshua and Caleb said, the LORD is with us, we can go in.

His name is worth knowing. In Hebrew, Joshua means the LORD is salvation. It is the very same name as Jesus. Jesus is the Greek form of Joshua. Keep that in mind as we walk. Joshua led God’s people into the land of rest. And the New Testament says that Jesus leads his people into a far greater rest.

The book falls into three parts. In chapters one to twelve, Israel enters the land. You will hear about Rahab and the scarlet line, the walls of Jericho, and the day the sun stood still. In chapters thirteen to twenty one, the land is shared out among the tribes. In the last three chapters, Joshua says goodbye, and calls the people to choose whom they will serve.

Here are three things to watch for as we walk.

Watch for the words, be strong and of a good courage. God says them to Joshua again and again, and Joshua says them to the people. Courage in this book does not come from being strong in yourself. It comes from knowing that God is with you.

Watch for promises kept. God had promised this land to Abraham, long before. Near the end of the book, we will hear that not one good thing God promised has failed. All came to pass.

And watch for stones. Twelve stones from the river, a heap of stones in a valley, a great stone under an oak. Again and again, Israel sets up stones so that their children will ask, what do these mean? And the answer is always the same. Look what the LORD has done.

We should also be honest. Joshua has some hard chapters. It tells of battles, and of whole cities brought under God’s judgment. We will not hurry past those chapters, and we will not pretend they are easy. The Bible tells us that God waited a long time before that judgment came, and that it came because of the great wickedness of the land. Christians have thought hard about these chapters, in different ways. As we walk, we will listen carefully to what Scripture says, and to what it shows us about God.

And we will meet some surprising people along the way. A woman of Jericho who hid two spies, and trusted the God of Israel. A people who tricked their way into a promise. An old man who asked for a mountain. God’s story in this book is bigger than one nation, and full of grace.

QUESTION: God told Joshua again and again to be strong and of a good courage. What is something in your life right now that needs courage?

PRAYER: Lord God, as I walk through the book of Joshua, teach me to trust your promises. Thank you that you are with your people wherever they go. When I am afraid, make me strong and of a good courage, because you are with me. Amen.
