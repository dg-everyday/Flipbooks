# Joshua 2: two spies sent to Jericho, Rahab hides them under the flax, I know that the LORD hath given you the land, the oath, the cord through the window, the scarlet line. Told gently (no "harlot"). Keys 2:9, 2:11, 2:21.
TITLE: The LORD Your God, He Is God

HOOK: Soldiers are pounding on the door. The king has sent them. Two enemy spies have been seen entering this house, and the soldiers want them now. Up on the flat roof, hidden under piles of drying plant stalks, two men lie perfectly still. Their lives are in the hands of one woman. And she is about to make the most important choice of her life.

WHERE: In chapter one, God spoke to Joshua after the death of Moses. He said, every place that the sole of your foot shall tread upon, that have I given unto you. And three times he said, be strong and of a good courage, for the LORD thy God is with thee. The eastern tribes promised to cross over and fight beside their brothers.

Now, in chapter two, before the people cross, Joshua sends two spies to look at the land, and especially at the city of Jericho. Listen for what one woman in Jericho knows about the God of Israel, and for the sign she ties in her window.

STORY: Joshua the son of Nun sends two men out from the camp at Shittim, to spy secretly. Go and look at the land, he says, especially Jericho.

Jericho was a strong walled city, just across the Jordan.

The two men go, and they come to the house of a woman named Rahab, and they stay there. Her house is built right into the city wall.

But someone has seen them. Word reaches the king of Jericho. Men of Israel have come here, he is told, to search out the country. And the king sends a message to Rahab. Bring out the men who have come to your house, for they have come to spy out the whole land.

Rahab has already hidden them. She says, yes, men came to me, but I did not know where they were from. And when it was dark, about the time the gate was shut, they went out. I do not know where they went. Go after them quickly, and you will catch them.

But she had taken them up to the roof, and hidden them under stalks of flax, which she had laid out there to dry. Flax was a plant used to make linen.

So the king’s men set off toward the river crossings. And as soon as they leave, the city gate is shut behind them.

Rahab did not tell the truth to the king’s men. The Bible does not praise her for that. It praises her for her faith, and for protecting the men who came in the name of the LORD.

Before the spies lie down to sleep, Rahab comes up to the roof. And she tells them something astonishing.

She tells them that the people of Jericho have heard everything. How the LORD dried up the water of the Red Sea when Israel came out of Egypt. How Israel defeated the two kings of the Amorites on the other side of the Jordan.

And then she says the most important thing of all.

Then Rahab asks for a promise. I have shown you kindness, she says, so swear to me by the LORD that you will show kindness to my family. Save my father and my mother, my brothers and my sisters, and all that they have.

The men answer, 'Our life for yours, if ye utter not this our business.' If you keep our secret, we will treat you kindly and truly when the LORD gives us the land.

Then, because her house is on the wall, Rahab lets them down by a rope through her window. Go to the hills, she tells them. Hide there three days, until they come back. Then you can go on your way.

The spies give her careful instructions. When we come into the land, they say, tie this line of scarlet thread in the window you let us down through. Bring your father and mother, your brothers and all your family into your house. Anyone who goes out into the street will be responsible for himself. But everyone who stays inside with you will be safe.

Rahab says, 'According unto your words, so be it.' And she sends them away.

The spies hide in the hills for three days. The king’s men search the whole road, but find nothing. Then the two men come down, cross the river, and go back to Joshua. They tell him everything that happened. And their report is full of faith. 'Truly the LORD hath delivered into our hands all the land; for even all the inhabitants of the country do faint because of us.'

Long before, ten spies came back from this land terrified. These two come back full of faith, and a woman of Jericho helped them see it.

KEY 2:9: 'And she said unto the men, I know that the LORD hath given you the land, and that your terror is fallen upon us, and that all the inhabitants of the land faint because of you.' I know, Rahab says. Not I hope, or I guess. She had only heard the stories. And from those stories, she knew that the God of Israel was real, and that his promise would come true. Faith often begins with hearing what God has done.

KEY 2:11: 'And as soon as we had heard these things, our hearts did melt, neither did there remain any more courage in any man, because of you: for the LORD your God, he is God in heaven above, and in earth beneath.' Everyone in Jericho heard the same news. Everyone’s heart melted with fear. But only Rahab turned that fear into faith. The LORD your God, she says, he is God in heaven above, and in earth beneath. Not just the god of one people. The God of everything. And so she chose to trust him, and to side with his people.

KEY 2:21: 'And she said, According unto your words, so be it. And she sent them away, and they departed: and she bound the scarlet line in the window.' Notice that she ties the scarlet line straight away. She does not wait until she sees the army coming. She believes the promise now, and she acts on it now. Many Christians have seen in that scarlet line a picture of salvation, a sign of safety marked in red, like the blood on the doorposts at the first Passover. Everyone inside the house with the scarlet line would be saved.

THREAD: The letter to the Hebrews lists Rahab among the heroes of faith: 'By faith' she 'perished not with them that believed not, when she had received the spies with peace.' And the Gospel of Matthew names her in the family line of Jesus: 'And Salmon begat Booz of Rachab'. Booz is Boaz, the kind man we met in the book of Ruth.

TODAY: Rahab heard about God, and chose to trust him, even though she was an outsider. Is there someone in your life who has heard about God but does not yet know him? Pray for them today.

QUESTION: Rahab said, I know that the LORD hath given you the land, before she had seen it happen. What promise of God do you need to believe today, before you see it come true?

PRAYER: Lord God, you are God in heaven above, and in earth beneath. Thank you that you welcomed Rahab, an outsider, and made her part of your family. Thank you that you welcome me too. Give me a faith that believes your promises and acts on them, straight away. Amen.
