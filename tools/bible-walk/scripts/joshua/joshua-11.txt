# Joshua 11: Jabin of Hazor gathers the north, as the sand upon the sea shore, be not afraid, the waters of Merom, horses and chariots, Hazor, he left nothing undone, made war a long time, the land rested from war. Told gently. Keys 11:15, 11:18, 11:23.
TITLE: And the Land Rested from War

HOOK: The army stretches as far as the eye can see. Soldiers from a dozen kingdoms. Horses and chariots, more than anyone can count. The Bible says they were as many as the sand on the seashore. And they have all come together for one reason: to fight Israel. Israel has no chariots, and very few horses. What hope do they have?

WHERE: In chapter ten, five kings attacked Gibeon, and Joshua marched up to save them, keeping his promise. The LORD threw great stones from heaven, and Joshua prayed, sun, stand thou still upon Gibeon. And there was no day like that, before it or after it, that the LORD hearkened unto the voice of a man. The southern part of the land was taken, because the LORD fought for Israel.

Now, in chapter eleven, the kings of the north gather against Israel. Listen for what God tells Joshua to do with the horses and chariots. And listen for the words that end the chapter.

STORY: When Jabin, king of Hazor, hears about the battles in the south, he sends messages to all the kings of the north. The kings of the hills and the plains, the valleys and the coast. The Canaanites, the Amorites, the Hittites, the Perizzites, the Jebusites, and the Hivites at the foot of mount Hermon.

They all come out with their armies, a huge crowd of people, as many as the sand on the seashore, with very many horses and chariots. And they camp together at the waters of Merom, to fight against Israel.

Chariots were the most feared weapons of that time. A chariot was a fast war cart pulled by horses. Soldiers on foot could hardly stand against them.

But the LORD speaks to Joshua. 'Be not afraid because of them'. By this time the next day, God says, I will give them all into Israel’s hands. And then he gives an unusual command. Thou shalt hough their horses, and burn their chariots with fire. To hough means to cut the leg tendons, so the horses could never be used for war again.

Why would God tell Israel to destroy the very weapons that could make them strong? Because God did not want Israel to trust in horses and chariots. He wanted them to trust in him.

So Joshua and all his fighting men come against the northern kings suddenly, by the waters of Merom. And the LORD gives them into Israel’s hand. Israel chases them far to the north and to the east. And Joshua does just as the LORD told him. He disables their horses, and burns their chariots with fire.

Then Joshua turns back, and takes Hazor. Hazor had been the head of all those kingdoms, the greatest city of the north. Joshua burns it with fire. But the other cities, standing on their hills, Israel does not burn. They will become homes for Israel.

The writer tells us that these cities came under God’s judgment, as Moses had commanded. These are hard words to hear, and the writer does not hide them.

And he adds something else, which is hard to understand. 'For it was of the LORD to harden their hearts, that they should come against Israel in battle'. Only one city made peace with Israel, and that was Gibeon. All the others chose to fight. Like Pharaoh in Egypt, they set themselves against God, and God let them follow the path they had chosen. Gibeon shows us that another way was possible. Those who turned to the God of Israel found mercy.

Then Joshua comes against the Anakim, the tall and powerful people who lived in the hills, around Hebron and Debir. Long before, ten of the twelve spies had come back terrified of these very people. We were like grasshoppers next to them, they had said. Now Joshua drives them out of the hill country. Only a few remain, in Gaza, Gath and Ashdod. Keep an ear out for Gath. A giant from that city will meet a shepherd boy named David, many years later.

The chapter ends with the whole land taken, from the south to mount Hermon in the north, and all its kings defeated.

KEY 11:15: 'As the LORD commanded Moses his servant, so did Moses command Joshua, and so did Joshua; he left nothing undone of all that the LORD commanded Moses.' Listen to the chain of obedience. The LORD commanded Moses. Moses commanded Joshua. And Joshua did it. He left nothing undone. Joshua did not pick and choose which of God’s commands to keep. He did them all. That is what faithfulness looks like. Not doing some of what God says, but all of it.

KEY 11:18: 'Joshua made war a long time with all those kings.' This short verse tells us something important. The story has moved quickly, battle after battle. But it was not quick for Joshua. It took a long time. God’s promises came true, but not all at once. Joshua had to keep going, year after year. Faith is not only for one great moment. It is for the long road.

KEY 11:23: 'So Joshua took the whole land, according to all that the LORD said unto Moses; and Joshua gave it for an inheritance unto Israel according to their divisions by their tribes. And the land rested from war.' At last, the land rested from war. God had promised this land to Abraham, long before. Through all the years of slavery, and wandering, and battles, God had not forgotten. Now the promise is kept. And the next part of the book will tell how each tribe received its inheritance, its share of the gift.

THREAD: A psalm says, 'Some trust in chariots, and some in horses: but we will remember the name of the LORD our God.' And the prophet Zechariah heard God say, 'Not by might, nor by power, but by my spirit, saith the LORD of hosts.'

TODAY: What are the horses and chariots in your life, the things you are tempted to trust instead of God? Money, cleverness, or the people you know? Thank God for them, but put your trust in him today.

QUESTION: God told Joshua to destroy the chariots, so Israel would not trust in them. What things are you most tempted to rely on instead of God, and how can you turn your trust back to him?

PRAYER: Lord God, some trust in chariots, and some in horses, but I will remember your name. Forgive me for trusting in other things instead of you. Help me to obey all that you say, and to keep going on the long road of faith, until I find the rest you have promised. Amen.
