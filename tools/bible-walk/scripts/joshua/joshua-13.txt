# Joshua 13: Joshua old, very much land yet to be possessed, divide it by lot, the eastern tribes' land recalled, the Geshurites not driven out, Balaam's end (gently), Levi's inheritance is the LORD. Keys 13:1, 13:14, 13:33.
TITLE: The LORD God of Israel Was Their Inheritance

HOOK: An old man sits with a map spread out before him. His hair is white. His battles are over. Most of the people who left Egypt with him are long gone. He has done more than most people do in a lifetime. And then God speaks to him, and says, there is still so much more to do.

WHERE: In chapter twelve, the writer looked back over all the kings Israel had defeated. Sihon and Og on the east side of the Jordan, under Moses. And on the west side, under Joshua, the king of Jericho, one, the king of Ai, one, and on through the list, all the kings thirty and one. Every name was a promise kept.

Now, in chapter thirteen, a new part of the book begins. The battles are mostly over, and the land must be shared out among the tribes. Listen for what God says to Joshua about the land that remains. And listen for the one tribe that receives no land at all.

STORY: The chapter opens with words that might surprise us.

God names the land that is still to be taken. The country of the Philistines along the coast, with its five lords, in Gaza, Ashdod, Ashkelon, Gath and Ekron. The land of the Sidonians in the north. All of Lebanon, toward the sunrising, up to the foot of mount Hermon.

And then God makes a promise. I will drive them out from before the children of Israel. God has not finished. The work that Joshua could not complete, God will still do. 'only divide thou it by lot unto the Israelites for an inheritance, as I have commanded thee.'

So Joshua is not asked to do everything. He is asked to do his part. To divide the land by lot among the nine and a half tribes who will live west of the Jordan. God will take care of the rest.

A lot was a little like drawing straws, or throwing dice. Israel used lots so that the decision would be left to God, and not to the strongest or loudest tribe. A proverb says that the lot is cast into the lap, but the whole disposing thereof is of the LORD.

Then the writer looks back at the tribes who had already received their land, on the east side of the Jordan. Reuben, Gad and half the tribe of Manasseh. Back in the days of Moses, they had seen that this land was good for cattle, and they had asked to settle there. Moses agreed, as long as their fighting men crossed the Jordan to help their brothers. And they kept that promise, as we heard in chapters one and four.

The writer describes their land. Reuben’s land in the south, from the river Arnon, with the plain of Medeba and the cities of Heshbon and Dibon. Gad’s land in the middle, with the cities of Gilead. And the half tribe of Manasseh in the north, in Bashan, the old kingdom of Og.

In among the lists, there are two small details we should not miss.

The first is a warning. 'Nevertheless the children of Israel expelled not the Geshurites, nor the Maachathites: but the Geshurites and the Maachathites dwell among the Israelites until this day.' Some peoples were not driven out. It seems a small thing here. But we will hear this kind of sentence again and again in these chapters. And in the book of Judges, we will see the trouble that came from it.

The second detail is about Balaam. Balaam was the prophet who was hired by the king of Moab to curse Israel, back in the days of Moses. But every time he opened his mouth, God turned his curse into a blessing. Later, he led Israel into sin. And here we learn that Balaam, the son of Beor, fell among Israel’s enemies in battle. He had heard God’s voice, and blessed Israel with his lips, but his heart was never truly with God.

And then twice in this chapter, the writer tells us about one tribe that receives no land at all.

The tribe of Levi was the tribe of the priests, and of those who served at the tabernacle. Every other tribe would get fields and towns and hills. But Levi would get no share of the land.

That might seem unfair. But listen to what God gives them instead.

KEY 13:1: 'Now Joshua was old and stricken in years; and the LORD said unto him, Thou art old and stricken in years, and there remaineth yet very much land to be possessed.' God speaks honestly to Joshua. You are old. But God does not say, so you are finished. He says, there is still much to do. Old age is not the end of usefulness to God. And no one finishes everything in one lifetime. God gives each person their part, and then he carries on his work through others.

KEY 13:14: 'Only unto the tribe of Levi he gave none inheritance; the sacrifices of the LORD God of Israel made by fire are their inheritance, as he said unto them.' The Levites lived from the offerings the people brought to God. They did not plough fields or harvest crops in the same way. Their work was to serve God, and the people supported them. It meant that the Levites had to trust God every day for what they needed.

KEY 13:33: 'But unto the tribe of Levi Moses gave not any inheritance: the LORD God of Israel was their inheritance, as he said unto them.' This is one of the most beautiful verses in the book. The other tribes received land. Levi received the LORD himself. What could be better than that? Land can be lost, and fields can fail. But God never fails. And the apostle Peter calls all who belong to Christ a royal priesthood. So this promise speaks to us too. The LORD is our inheritance.

THREAD: A psalm says, 'The LORD is the portion of mine inheritance and of my cup: thou maintainest my lot.' And another psalm says, 'Whom have I in heaven but thee? and there is none upon earth that I desire beside thee.'

TODAY: If you lost everything you own, what would you still have? Take a moment today to thank God that he himself is your portion. And ask him to show you your part in his work, whatever your age.

QUESTION: God told Joshua, thou art old, and there remaineth yet very much land to be possessed. What do you think God might still want to do in your life, whatever your age or stage?

PRAYER: Lord God, you are my inheritance, and my portion for ever. Thank you that you are better than anything this world can give. Thank you that you still have work for your people to do, at every age. Show me my part, and help me to do it faithfully, and to leave the rest to you. Amen.
