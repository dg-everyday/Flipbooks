# Joshua 20: cities of refuge for one who caused a death by accident (told gently, no "slayer/killeth" outside quotes), declare his cause at the gate, until the death of the high priest, the six cities; refuge in God and in Christ. Keys 20:2, 20:4, 20:7.
TITLE: Appoint Out for You Cities of Refuge

HOOK: A man is running for his life down a dusty road. Behind him, someone is chasing him, angry and determined. The man did something terrible, but he did not mean to. It was an accident. Up ahead, he can see the walls of a city, and the elders sitting at the gate. If he can just reach that gate, he will be safe.

WHERE: In chapter nineteen, the last six lots were cast: Simeon within Judah, Zebulun, Issachar, Asher, Naphtali in Galilee, and Dan. When every tribe had its land, the people gave Joshua the city he asked for, Timnath-serah, and he built it and lived there.

Now, in chapter twenty, the land is shared out. But God is not finished. He wants to make sure that his people live with justice and mercy. Listen for what the cities of refuge are for. And listen for how long a person must stay there.

STORY: The LORD speaks to Joshua.

God had already given these instructions to Moses, long before. Now that the land is divided, it is time to put them into practice.

The cities were for a person who had caused someone’s death by accident. Someone who did not mean to do it, and who had no hatred in his heart.

The law of Moses gives an example. Two men go into the forest together to cut wood. One of them swings his axe, and the head flies off the handle, and strikes his neighbour, and the neighbour dies. It was a terrible accident. But it was an accident.

In those days, there was no police force, and no prison. When someone died, a close relative of the one who died had the duty of seeking justice. The Bible calls him the avenger of blood. In his grief and anger, he might not stop to ask whether it was an accident or not.

So God made a way of safety.

If the relative came after him, the elders would not hand him over, because he had acted unknowingly, and had not hated his neighbour.

Then there would be a fair hearing. The person would stand before the congregation for judgment. If it truly was an accident, he would be protected. God cared about the life that had been lost. But he also cared about the person who had not meant any harm. He did not want one tragedy to lead to another.

And there is one more detail, a strange one. The person had to stay in the city of refuge until the death of the high priest who was serving at that time. Then he could go home, to his own town and his own house.

Why the death of the high priest? The chapter does not explain. But many Christians have seen in it a picture. The death of the high priest brought freedom. And the New Testament calls Jesus our great high priest, whose death sets us free.

Then Israel chooses the cities.

And on the east side of the Jordan, they choose three more. Bezer in the wilderness, in the land of Reuben. Ramoth in Gilead, in the land of Gad. And Golan in Bashan, in the land of Manasseh.

Six cities in all, three on each side of the river. Spread out across the whole land, so that no one was ever too far from a place of refuge.

And the writer adds that these cities were not only for Israelites. They were also for the stranger, the foreigner living among them. God’s protection was open to everyone.

Think of the cities of refuge as a picture. All of us have done wrong. Sometimes we have hurt others without meaning to. Sometimes we meant it. And all of us need a place to run to, where we can be safe, and where there is mercy. The Bible says that God himself is our refuge. And in Jesus, the door of refuge is open to all who come.

KEY 20:2: 'Speak to the children of Israel, saying, Appoint out for you cities of refuge, whereof I spake unto you by the hand of Moses:' The land had just been divided, and the first thing God thinks about is mercy. Before the people settle down to farm and build, God makes sure there is a place of safety for those in trouble. God is a God of justice. But he is also a God of mercy, who provides a way of escape.

KEY 20:4: 'And when he that doth flee unto one of those cities shall stand at the entering of the gate of the city, and shall declare his cause in the ears of the elders of that city, they shall take him into the city unto them, and give him a place, that he may dwell among them.' The person had to run, and reach the gate, and tell the truth about what happened. And then the elders would take him in, and give him a place. Refuge is not only about escaping danger. It is about being welcomed. When we come to God honestly, he does not just protect us. He takes us in, and gives us a place among his people.

KEY 20:7: 'And they appointed Kedesh in Galilee in mount Naphtali, and Shechem in mount Ephraim, and Kirjath-arba, which is Hebron, in the mountain of Judah.' Notice that these cities were on mountains. They could be seen from far away. A person running for safety would be able to see where to go. And notice Hebron, Caleb’s city, the old city of the giants, now a place of refuge. God can turn places of fear into places of safety.

THREAD: A psalm says, 'God is our refuge and strength, a very present help in trouble.' And the letter to the Hebrews speaks of us 'who have fled for refuge to lay hold upon the hope set before us'.

TODAY: Are you carrying guilt for something you did, whether you meant it or not? Run to God today. Tell him honestly what happened. He is your refuge, and in him there is mercy. And think of someone who needs a safe place. How could you be a place of refuge for them?

QUESTION: God made sure that no one in Israel was ever too far from a city of refuge. What does it mean to you that God is your refuge, a very present help in trouble?

PRAYER: Lord God, you are my refuge and my strength, a very present help in trouble. Thank you that you are a God of justice and of mercy. Thank you that in Jesus, the door of refuge is open to all who come. I run to you now. Take me in, and give me a place with your people. Amen.

NEXT: Next on the walk, Joshua chapter twenty one. The Levites receive their cities, forty eight of them, scattered through the land. And the chapter ends with some of the most wonderful words in the book: all came to pass.
