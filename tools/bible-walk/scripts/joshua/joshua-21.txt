# Joshua 21: the Levites ask for cities, Kohath (Aaron's sons) Gershon and Merari by lot, Hebron to the priests and its fields to Caleb, forty eight cities scattered through the land, the LORD gave rest, there failed not ought of any good thing. Keys 21:43, 21:44, 21:45.
TITLE: There Failed Not Ought of Any Good Thing

HOOK: Imagine keeping a list of every promise someone ever made to you. Every single one. And then, at the end of many years, going down that list with a pencil, and ticking each one off. Kept. Kept. Kept. And reaching the bottom, and finding that not one was broken. That is how Joshua chapter twenty one ends.

WHERE: In chapter twenty, God told Israel to appoint cities of refuge, six of them, three on each side of the Jordan. A person who caused a death by accident could run there, declare his cause at the gate, and be taken in and kept safe. God showed that he is a God of justice, and of mercy.

Now, in chapter twenty one, the last group to receive a home is the tribe of Levi. Listen for how their cities are spread through the land. And listen for the three verses at the end, which sum up the whole first half of the book.

STORY: The heads of the families of the Levites come to Eleazar the priest, and to Joshua, and to the heads of the tribes, at Shiloh.

They remind the leaders of God’s word. 'The LORD commanded by the hand of Moses to give us cities to dwell in, with the suburbs thereof for our cattle.' The suburbs were the open fields around each city, where the Levites could keep their animals.

Remember that the Levites received no land of their own. The LORD was their inheritance. But they still needed somewhere to live. And God had not forgotten them.

So the children of Israel give the Levites cities from their own land, at the commandment of the LORD.

The Levites were divided into three great families, named after the three sons of Levi: Kohath, Gershon and Merari. And the cities are given out by lot, family by family.

The first lot comes out for the family of Kohath. Among them were the priests, the children of Aaron. They receive thirteen cities from Judah, Simeon and Benjamin, close to where the temple would one day stand in Jerusalem. The rest of the family of Kohath receive ten cities from Ephraim, Dan and half of Manasseh.

The family of Gershon receive thirteen cities in the north, from Issachar, Asher, Naphtali, and the half of Manasseh in Bashan.

And the family of Merari receive twelve cities, from Reuben, Gad and Zebulun.

Among the cities given to the priests is Hebron. Remember Hebron? It was the city Caleb asked for, in chapter fourteen. The writer explains. The city itself, with its open fields around it, was given to the priests. But the farmland and the villages of Hebron still belonged to Caleb. Caleb shared his inheritance with the priests of God.

The writer also mentions that some of these cities were cities of refuge, like Hebron, Shechem and Kedesh. So the Levites lived in the places of mercy.

When all the cities are counted, there are forty eight, with their fields.

Why scatter the Levites all over the land? Why not give them one area of their own? Because the Levites had a special job. They taught God’s law, and served God, and helped the people worship him. If they were spread out across the whole country, then every tribe would have Levites living nearby. In every part of the land, there would be people reminding Israel of God and his word.

There is a picture here for us too. God scatters his people through the world, in towns and cities, in homes and workplaces, so that everywhere, people can hear about him.

And then, at the end of the chapter, the writer steps back. The battles are over, the land is divided, every tribe and every family has its home. And he sums it all up, in three wonderful verses.

KEY 21:43: 'And the LORD gave unto Israel all the land which he sware to give unto their fathers; and they possessed it, and dwelt therein.' Long before, God had promised this land to Abraham, when he had no children and no home. He repeated the promise to Isaac, and to Jacob. Then came long years of slavery in Egypt, and wandering in the wilderness, when the promise must have seemed far away. But God did not forget. The LORD gave. They possessed it. They dwelt therein. God’s promises may take a long time, but they always come true.

KEY 21:44: 'And the LORD gave them rest round about, according to all that he sware unto their fathers: and there stood not a man of all their enemies before them; the LORD delivered all their enemies into their hand.' Rest. After slavery, after the wilderness, after years of war, God gave his people rest. The book of Joshua says plainly that it was the LORD who did it. Not Joshua’s cleverness, nor Israel’s strength. The LORD gave them rest. And the New Testament tells us that there is a still greater rest for God’s people, a rest we enter by trusting him.

KEY 21:45: 'There failed not ought of any good thing which the LORD had spoken unto the house of Israel; all came to pass.' Ought means anything. Not anything failed. Not one good thing. All came to pass. Joshua had seen the river stop, the walls fall, and the sun stand still. He had seen battles won and lost. And at the end of it all, he could say that not one of God’s promises had failed. That is true for us too. Every promise God has made, he will keep.

THREAD: Solomon would say, years later, 'there hath not failed one word of all his good promise, which he promised by the hand of Moses his servant.' And Paul wrote, 'For all the promises of God in him are yea, and in him Amen'.

TODAY: Think of a promise of God that means a lot to you. Write it down today, and thank God that he keeps his word. And if you are waiting for something, remember Joshua’s words: there failed not ought of any good thing. All came to pass.

QUESTION: The writer said that not one of God’s good promises had failed. Which promise of God do you most need to hold on to right now?

PRAYER: Lord God, there has not failed one word of all your good promises. Thank you that you keep every one. Thank you for the rest you give to your people. When I am waiting, help me to trust you. And scatter me like the Levites, to be a reminder of you wherever I live. Amen.
