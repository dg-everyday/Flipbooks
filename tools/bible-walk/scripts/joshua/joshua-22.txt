# Joshua 22: the eastern tribes sent home with a blessing, love the LORD and cleave unto him, the great altar by Jordan, Israel gathers for war, Phinehas and ten princes, the LORD God of gods he knoweth, a witness not a place of sacrifice, the altar Ed. Keys 22:5, 22:27, 22:34.
TITLE: It Shall Be a Witness Between Us

HOOK: A message spreads through the camp like wildfire. Our brothers across the river have built an altar. A huge one. They are turning away from God. Within hours, a whole nation is gathering for war, ready to march against their own family. But before a single sword is drawn, someone decides to go and ask a simple question first.

WHERE: In chapter twenty one, the Levites received forty eight cities, scattered through the land, so that every tribe had God’s servants living nearby. And the writer summed up the story so far. The LORD gave Israel all the land, and gave them rest round about. There failed not ought of any good thing which the LORD had spoken. All came to pass.

Now, in chapter twenty two, the eastern tribes finally go home. Listen for Joshua’s words as he sends them off. And listen for how a terrible misunderstanding is put right.

STORY: Joshua calls together the men of Reuben, Gad and half the tribe of Manasseh. These are the tribes whose land is on the east side of the Jordan. For years, their fighting men have been away from home, helping their brothers take the land on the west side. They promised to do it in chapter one, and they have kept their promise.

Joshua thanks them. You have kept everything Moses commanded you, he says, and obeyed my voice. You have not left your brothers, all these many days. Now the LORD has given your brothers rest, as he promised. So go home, to your tents and your land, on the other side of the Jordan.

But Joshua gives them one last charge.

Then Joshua blesses them, and sends them away. Go home with great riches, he says, with many cattle, with silver and gold, bronze and iron, and much clothing. And share what you have taken with your brothers at home.

So the men of the eastern tribes leave Shiloh, and set off for home. When they reach the Jordan, before they cross, they build an altar there by the river. A great altar, big enough to be seen from far away.

When the rest of Israel hears about it, they are horrified. God had said that there was to be only one altar for sacrifices, at the tabernacle. Building another altar looked like rebellion, like starting a new religion. And the whole congregation gathers at Shiloh, to go to war against them.

Why were they so worried? Because they remembered what happened before. Once, in the wilderness at Peor, Israel had turned to other gods, and a plague fell on the whole congregation. And they remembered Achan, whose sin brought defeat on everyone. If one part of Israel turned away from God, the whole nation could suffer.

But instead of attacking at once, they send a group to talk first. Phinehas, the son of Eleazar the priest, goes with ten leaders, one from each tribe.

They speak strongly. What is this unfaithfulness you have committed against the God of Israel? Have you turned away from following the LORD? If your land is unclean, come over and live with us, in the land where the LORD’s tabernacle stands. But do not rebel against the LORD, by building another altar.

And the men of the eastern tribes answer.

'The LORD God of gods, the LORD God of gods, he knoweth, and Israel he shall know;' God knows our hearts, they say. If we built this altar to turn away from the LORD, or to offer sacrifices on it, let the LORD himself judge us.

Then they explain. We were afraid of something. The Jordan is a border between us and you. And we were afraid that one day, your children might say to our children, you have nothing to do with the LORD. You live on the other side of the river. And your children might make our children stop following the LORD.

So they built the altar, not for sacrifices, but as a copy, a reminder, to show that they belonged to the same God.

When Phinehas and the leaders hear this, they are pleased. Phinehas says, now we know that the LORD is among us, because you have not done this wrong against him. And they go back and tell the rest of Israel. The people are glad, and bless God, and the talk of war is over.

What could have been a terrible war became a moment of joy, because someone asked a question before they attacked.

KEY 22:5: 'But take diligent heed to do the commandment and the law, which Moses the servant of the LORD charged you, to love the LORD your God, and to walk in all his ways, and to keep his commandments, and to cleave unto him, and to serve him with all your heart and with all your soul.' Listen to the words. Love the LORD. Walk in his ways. Keep his commandments. Cleave unto him, which means hold on tight, like glue. Serve him with all your heart and soul. Joshua knew that the eastern tribes would be far from Shiloh. So the most important thing to take home was not riches. It was a heart that loved God.

KEY 22:27: 'But that it may be a witness between us, and you, and our generations after us, that we might do the service of the LORD before him with our burnt offerings, and with our sacrifices, and with our peace offerings; that your children may not say to our children in time to come, Ye have no part in the LORD.' The eastern tribes were thinking about their grandchildren. They wanted to make sure that their families would always belong to God’s people. Like the twelve stones from the Jordan, this altar was built so that future generations would ask, and hear, and remember.

KEY 22:34: 'And the children of Reuben and the children of Gad called the altar Ed: for it shall be a witness between us that the LORD is God.' Ed means witness. The altar that almost started a war became a witness that the LORD is God. Misunderstandings can divide God’s people. But honest talking and careful listening can turn them into moments of unity.

THREAD: James wrote, 'let every man be swift to hear, slow to speak, slow to wrath'. And a psalm says, 'Behold, how good and how pleasant it is for brethren to dwell together in unity!'

TODAY: Is there someone you have been upset with, because of something you heard about them? Before you react, go and ask them. Listen carefully, and give them the chance to explain. You may find there is more to the story.

QUESTION: Israel nearly went to war over a misunderstanding, but they talked first. Why do you think it is so important to listen before we judge?

PRAYER: Lord God, thank you that you know every heart. Help me to love you, to walk in your ways, and to cleave to you with all my heart. Make me swift to hear and slow to anger. Keep your people united, and help us to be a witness together that the LORD is God. Amen.

NEXT: Next on the walk, Joshua chapter twenty three. Joshua is old, and he calls the leaders of Israel together for a farewell. Not one thing hath failed, he tells them. So take good heed, and love the LORD your God.
