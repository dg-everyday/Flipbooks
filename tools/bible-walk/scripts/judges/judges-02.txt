# Judges 2: the angel at Bochim, the people weep, Joshua's death recalled, another generation which knew not the LORD, Baal and Ashtaroth, the cycle: evil, enemies, groaning, judges, relapse; nations left to prove Israel. Avoids 2:17. Keys 2:10, 2:16, 2:18.
TITLE: Nevertheless the LORD Raised Up Judges

HOOK: A family has a treasure that has been passed down for generations. The grandparents knew its story, and why it mattered. The parents knew it too. But somewhere along the way, someone forgot to tell the children. And so the children grew up, and the treasure sat on a shelf, gathering dust, and they had no idea what it was worth. Then one day, they gave it away.

WHERE: In chapter one, the tribes went up to take the land after the death of Joshua. It started well, with the people asking the LORD, who shall go up first? But then came the long sad list: neither did Manasseh drive them out, neither did Ephraim, neither did Zebulun. When Israel was strong, they put the Canaanites to work, and did not drive them out.

Now, in chapter two, God speaks to his people about what they have done. And the writer steps back to show us the pattern of the whole book. Listen for the sad circle. And listen for the word, nevertheless.

STORY: An angel of the LORD comes up from Gilgal to a place called Bochim.

He speaks to the people. I brought you up out of Egypt, he says, and brought you into the land I promised to your fathers. And I said, I will never break my covenant with you. And you were not to make any treaty with the people of this land. You were to throw down their altars. But you have not obeyed my voice. Why have you done this?

So now, God says, I will not drive them out before you. They will be thorns in your sides, and their gods will be a snare to you.

When the people hear these words, they lift up their voices and weep. They call the place Bochim, which means weepers. And they offer sacrifices there to the LORD.

It is good that they wept. But as we will see, tears are not the same as change.

Then the writer goes back in time, to remind us of something. When Joshua sent the people away, each to his own land, they served the LORD. All the days of Joshua, and all the days of the elders who outlived him, who had seen the great works of the LORD. Then Joshua, the servant of the LORD, died, and they buried him in his land in the hills of Ephraim.

And then comes one of the saddest verses in the Bible.

What happened? Somewhere, the story stopped being told. The parents who crossed the Jordan, who saw the walls fall, did not pass it on. Or their children did not listen. Remember the stones from the Jordan, set up so that children would ask, what mean these stones? Somehow, the questions stopped being asked, or the answers stopped being given.

And so, the writer says, the children of Israel did evil in the sight of the LORD, and served the Baals. Baal was the chief god of the Canaanites, thought to bring rain and good harvests. Ashtaroth was a goddess worshipped alongside him. The people forsook the LORD God of their fathers, who brought them out of Egypt, and followed the gods of the people around them.

So the anger of the LORD is hot against Israel. He lets their enemies plunder them. Wherever they go, the hand of the LORD is against them, just as he had warned. And they are greatly distressed.

And then comes the word that changes everything.

But the people do not listen even to their judges. They quickly turn aside from the way their fathers walked.

And here is the circle. When a judge dies, the people go back, and become even worse than their fathers. They follow other gods, and bow down to them. They do not stop their own ways, or their stubbornness.

Sin. Trouble. Crying out. Rescue. Then sin again. Round and round. And each time, a little further down.

So God says that he will no longer drive out the nations that Joshua left. Instead, he will use them to test Israel, to see whether they will keep the way of the LORD, as their fathers did.

This is a sobering chapter. But notice the heart of God in it. He is angry at sin. And yet again and again, he hears their groaning, and raises up someone to save them. He does not give up on his people.

KEY 2:10: 'And also all that generation were gathered unto their fathers: and there arose another generation after them, which knew not the LORD, nor yet the works which he had done for Israel.' One generation. That is all it took. The people who had seen the Jordan stop and the walls of Jericho fall were gone. And their children did not know the LORD. Faith is not passed on automatically, like a family name. Each generation must be taught, and each person must come to know God for themselves.

KEY 2:16: 'Nevertheless the LORD raised up judges, which delivered them out of the hand of those that spoiled them.' Nevertheless. The people had forsaken God. They deserved to be left in their trouble. Nevertheless, God raised up judges. That one word is the hope of the whole book. Our sin is great. But God’s mercy is greater. Again and again, he steps in to save people who do not deserve it.

KEY 2:18: 'And when the LORD raised them up judges, then the LORD was with the judge, and delivered them out of the hand of their enemies all the days of the judge: for it repented the LORD because of their groanings by reason of them that oppressed them and vexed them.' It repented the LORD means that God was moved to pity. When his people groaned under their enemies, God’s heart was moved. The judges did not save Israel by their own power. The LORD was with the judge. God is not cold or distant. He hears the cries of those who suffer.

THREAD: A psalm says, 'One generation shall praise thy works to another, and shall declare thy mighty acts.' And Paul wrote, 'But where sin abounded, grace did much more abound'.

TODAY: Who could you tell about what God has done? A child, a friend, or someone younger in faith? Share one story of God’s faithfulness with them today, so that the story does not stop with you.

QUESTION: A whole generation grew up who knew not the LORD. What do you think it takes for faith to be passed on from one generation to the next?

PRAYER: Lord God, thank you that even when your people forget you, you do not forget them. Thank you for the word nevertheless, and for your mercy that is greater than our sin. Help me to know you for myself, and to pass on the story of your works to those who come after me. Amen.
