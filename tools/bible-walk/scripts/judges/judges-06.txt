# Judges 6: Midian like grasshoppers, the caves, the prophet's message, the angel under the oak at Ophrah, Gideon threshing in the winepress, thou mighty man of valour, the least in my father's house, the fire from the rock, Jehovah-shalom, the altar of Baal thrown down, Jerubbaal, the fleece. Keys 6:12, 6:15, 6:24.
TITLE: The LORD Is with Thee, Thou Mighty Man of Valour

HOOK: A young man is hiding at the bottom of a winepress, a stone pit dug into the ground. He is threshing wheat, beating the grain out of the stalks. But he is hiding, because if the enemy sees him, they will take everything. Then he looks up, and there is a stranger sitting under the oak tree. And the stranger calls him a mighty man of valour.

WHERE: In chapter five, Deborah and Barak sang a song of victory. They praised the LORD when the people willingly offered themselves. The stars in their courses fought against Sisera, and the river Kishon swept his chariots away. Let them that love him be as the sun when he goeth forth in his might.

Now, in chapter six, the circle turns again. Israel is in deep trouble. Listen for what God calls Gideon, and how Gideon sees himself. And listen for the fleece.

STORY: The children of Israel do evil in the sight of the LORD. And the LORD gives them into the hand of Midian.

The Midianites are cruel. Every time Israel plants crops, the Midianites come, with the Amalekites and the people of the east. They come with their cattle and their tents, as many as grasshoppers. They camp on the land, and destroy the harvest, all the way to Gaza. They leave nothing for Israel to eat.

The people of Israel are so afraid that they hide in caves and dens in the mountains. They become very poor. And they cry out to the LORD.

This time, before God sends a deliverer, he sends a prophet. And the prophet reminds them. I brought you out of Egypt. I rescued you from all who oppressed you. And I said, I am the LORD your God, do not fear the gods of the Amorites. 'but ye have not obeyed my voice.'

Then an angel of the LORD comes, and sits under an oak at Ophrah. And there is Gideon, the son of Joash, threshing wheat in the winepress, to hide it from the Midianites.

The angel appears to him.

A mighty man of valour? Gideon is hiding in a hole in the ground. But God sees not what Gideon is, but what Gideon will become.

Gideon is not convinced. Oh my Lord, he says, if the LORD is with us, why has all this happened to us? Where are all the miracles our fathers told us about? Did not the LORD bring us up from Egypt? But now the LORD has forsaken us.

It is an honest question. And God does not scold him for it. 'And the LORD looked upon him, and said, Go in this thy might, and thou shalt save Israel from the hand of the Midianites: have not I sent thee?'

But Gideon still has doubts.

And God gives the answer that matters most. 'Surely I will be with thee, and thou shalt smite the Midianites as one man.'

Gideon asks for a sign. He goes and prepares a young goat and unleavened bread, and brings it out to the oak. The angel tells him to put it on a rock. Then the angel touches it with the end of his staff, and fire rises up out of the rock, and burns it all. And the angel vanishes.

Gideon is terrified. Alas, O Lord GOD, I have seen an angel of the LORD face to face! But the LORD says to him, peace be unto thee, fear not, thou shalt not die.

So Gideon builds an altar there.

Soon after, God gives Gideon his first task. Not to fight Midian, but to deal with sin in his own family. Your father has an altar to Baal. Pull it down. Cut down the idol beside it. And build an altar to the LORD on top of the rock.

Gideon takes ten servants and does it. But he is afraid of his family and the men of the town, so he does it under cover of darkness.

When the town wakes up, they see the altar of Baal thrown down. Who has done this, they ask. Gideon, the son of Joash! Bring out your son, they tell Joash, so that he may die.

But Joash stands up for his son. Will you plead for Baal? If Baal is a god, let him plead for himself! So Gideon is given a new name, Jerubbaal, which means let Baal plead against him.

Then the Midianites and their allies gather in the valley of Jezreel. And the Spirit of the LORD comes upon Gideon. He blows a trumpet, and men come from his own clan, and from Manasseh, Asher, Zebulun and Naphtali.

And yet Gideon still wants to be sure. He says to God, I will put a fleece of wool on the threshing floor. If the dew is on the fleece only, and the ground around it is dry, then I will know that you will save Israel by my hand. And it is so. He wrings a whole bowl of water out of the fleece.

Then Gideon asks again, the other way round. Do not be angry with me. Let the fleece be dry, and the ground be wet with dew. And God does so.

God is patient with Gideon’s weak faith, and makes him strong.

KEY 6:12: 'And the angel of the LORD appeared unto him, and said unto him, The LORD is with thee, thou mighty man of valour.' Notice the order. First, the LORD is with thee. Then, thou mighty man of valour. Gideon would become mighty because the LORD was with him. God does not call the strong. He makes strong those he calls. And he sees in us what we cannot yet see in ourselves.

KEY 6:15: 'And he said unto him, Oh my Lord, wherewith shall I save Israel? behold, my family is poor in Manasseh, and I am the least in my father’s house.' The least of the least. Gideon thinks this disqualifies him. But in God’s eyes, it is no problem at all. God loves to use the least, so that everyone can see that the power is his. Feeling small is not a reason to say no to God.

KEY 6:24: 'Then Gideon built an altar there unto the LORD, and called it Jehovah-shalom: unto this day it is yet in Ophrah of the Abi-ezrites.' Jehovah-shalom means the LORD is peace. Gideon had been full of fear. Now, in the middle of war and danger, he names his altar after peace. God’s peace does not depend on everything around us being calm. It comes from knowing that he is with us.

THREAD: Paul heard God say, 'My grace is sufficient for thee: for my strength is made perfect in weakness.' And Jesus said, 'Peace I leave with you, my peace I give unto you'.

TODAY: How do you see yourself? Weak, small, the least? Hear God’s words to Gideon today: the LORD is with thee. And if there is an altar in your life, something that has taken God’s place, ask him for courage to pull it down.

QUESTION: God called Gideon a mighty man of valour while he was hiding in a winepress. What do you think God might see in you that you cannot yet see in yourself?

PRAYER: Lord God, thank you that you are with me, and that you see what I can become. Thank you for your patience with my doubts and questions. When I feel like the least, remind me that your strength is made perfect in weakness. You are my peace, Jehovah-shalom. Amen.
