# Judges 7: the people are too many, the fearful go home, the test at the water, the three hundred, the dream of the barley loaf, trumpets, pitchers and lamps, the sword of the LORD and of Gideon, the host flees (told gently). Keys 7:2, 7:7, 7:20.
TITLE: The Sword of the LORD, and of Gideon

HOOK: A general looks at his army. Thirty two thousand men. Across the valley, the enemy is as many as grasshoppers, with camels as countless as the sand. And then God says something no general wants to hear. You have too many soldiers. Send most of them home.

WHERE: In chapter six, an angel found Gideon threshing wheat in a winepress, and said, the LORD is with thee, thou mighty man of valour. Gideon said, I am the least in my father’s house. He built an altar called Jehovah-shalom, the LORD is peace, and pulled down his father’s altar to Baal. And God was patient with him, giving him the sign of the fleece, wet and then dry.

Now, in chapter seven, Gideon faces the Midianites. Listen for why God makes his army smaller. And listen for the strange weapons the three hundred carry into battle.

STORY: Early one day, Gideon and all his men camp by the well of Harod. The army of Midian is camped to the north of them, in the valley.

And the LORD speaks to Gideon.

So God tells Gideon, tell the people that anyone who is afraid may go home. And twenty two thousand go home. Ten thousand remain.

But God is not finished.

The people are still too many, God says. Bring them down to the water, and I will test them there.

So Gideon brings them down to the water. And God says, everyone who laps the water like a dog, put by himself. And everyone who kneels down to drink, put by himself. Three hundred men lap the water, bringing it to their mouths in their hands. All the rest kneel down to drink.

People have wondered why God chose the ones he did. The Bible does not say. The point is not how they drank. The point is that God made the army so small that no one could ever boast that they won by their own strength.

Then God says to Gideon.

So the rest go home, and Gideon keeps the three hundred, with their food and their trumpets. And the huge army of Midian lies below them in the valley.

Then God speaks to Gideon again. Get up, go down against the camp, for I have given it into your hand. And then, gently, God adds, but if you are afraid to go down, take your servant Phurah with you. Go and listen to what they are saying, and afterward your hands will be strong.

God knows Gideon is afraid. And he does not scold him. He gives him one more encouragement.

So Gideon and Phurah creep down to the edge of the enemy camp. The Midianites and Amalekites and the people of the east lie along the valley like grasshoppers, with camels as many as the sand on the seashore.

And Gideon hears a man telling his friend about a dream. I dreamed, he says, that a loaf of barley bread came tumbling into the camp of Midian. It hit a tent, and knocked it over, and the tent fell flat.

A barley loaf was the bread of poor people. Israel, poor and small, is the barley loaf.

And his friend answers, this can only be the sword of Gideon, the son of Joash, a man of Israel. God has given Midian and all the army into his hand.

When Gideon hears the dream and its meaning, he worships. Then he goes back to his camp, and says, arise, for the LORD has delivered into your hand the army of Midian.

He divides the three hundred into three groups. He gives every man a trumpet, and an empty clay jar with a lamp hidden inside. Watch me, he says, and do what I do. When I blow my trumpet, all of you blow your trumpets on every side of the camp, and shout.

Trumpets, jars and torches. No swords in their hands.

In the middle of the dark hours, just after the guards have changed, Gideon and his hundred men come to the edge of the camp. They blow their trumpets, and smash the jars.

Imagine the noise. Three hundred trumpets blasting. Three hundred jars shattering. Three hundred torches suddenly blazing in the dark, all around the camp. And three hundred voices shouting.

Every man stands in his place around the camp. And the whole army of Midian runs, crying out, and flees. The LORD sets every man’s sword against his neighbour, throughout the camp, in the confusion. And the army flees far away.

Then men from Naphtali, Asher and Manasseh join the chase. Gideon sends to the men of Ephraim, and they take the river crossings, and capture two princes of Midian, Oreb and Zeeb.

The LORD won the battle.

KEY 7:2: 'And the LORD said unto Gideon, The people that are with thee are too many for me to give the Midianites into their hands, lest Israel vaunt themselves against me, saying, Mine own hand hath saved me.' To vaunt means to boast. God knew that if Israel won with a big army, they would say, look how strong we are. So he made the army small, so that everyone would know that God had done it. God sometimes lets us be weak, so that we learn to trust him, and give him the glory.

KEY 7:7: 'And the LORD said unto Gideon, By the three hundred men that lapped will I save you, and deliver the Midianites into thine hand: and let all the other people go every man unto his place.' By the three hundred will I save you. Notice who does the saving. I will save you. Not Gideon, and not the three hundred. Few or many makes no difference to God. Long after, Saul’s son Jonathan would say the same thing: there is no restraint to the LORD to save by many or by few.

KEY 7:20: 'And the three companies blew the trumpets, and brake the pitchers, and held the lamps in their left hands, and the trumpets in their right hands to blow withal: and they cried, The sword of the LORD, and of Gideon.' Notice the order. The sword of the LORD comes first, and then Gideon. And notice that the light was hidden inside the jars until the jars were broken. Many Christians have seen a picture in this. Paul wrote that we have God’s treasure in earthen vessels, in jars of clay. Sometimes it is when we are broken that God’s light shines out most clearly.

THREAD: Paul wrote, 'But we have this treasure in earthen vessels, that the excellency of the power may be of God, and not of us.' And a psalm says, 'Not unto us, O LORD, not unto us, but unto thy name give glory'.

TODAY: Is there a situation where you feel too small, or too weak? Remember the three hundred. God does not need us to be strong. He needs us to trust him and obey. And when he works, give him the glory.

QUESTION: God made Gideon’s army smaller so Israel could not boast. Why do you think God sometimes allows us to feel weak or small?

PRAYER: Lord God, not unto us, but unto your name give glory. Thank you that you do not need many or strong people to do your work. Thank you for your patience with my fears. Let your light shine through me, even through my weakness and brokenness, so that everyone may see that the power is yours. Amen.
