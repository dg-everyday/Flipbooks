# Judges 12: Ephraim threatens Jephthah, I put my life in my hands, Gilead against Ephraim, the fords of Jordan, Shibboleth (told gently, no counts), Jephthah's end; Ibzan, Elon and Abdon. Keys 12:3, 12:5, 12:7.
TITLE: Say Now Shibboleth

HOOK: A man walks up to a river crossing, trying to get home. Soldiers stop him. Are you from the tribe of Ephraim, they ask. No, he says. Then say this word, they tell him. Shibboleth. He tries. But it comes out Sibboleth. Just one sound different. And with that one sound, he gives himself away.

WHERE: In chapter eleven, Jephthah, the son his brothers drove out, was called back to lead Gilead. He said, the LORD the Judge be judge this day. The Spirit of the LORD came upon him, and he won a great victory over the Ammonites. But he had made a rash vow, and his only daughter came out to meet him. And he said, I have opened my mouth unto the LORD, and I cannot go back.

Now, in chapter twelve, the trouble comes not from enemies, but from Israel’s own tribes. Listen for the quarrel with Ephraim, and for the word that decides who may cross the Jordan. And then listen for three quiet judges.

STORY: The men of Ephraim gather and cross the Jordan, and come to Jephthah, angry. Why did you go to fight the Ammonites without calling us to go with you? We will burn your house down with you inside it!

Does this sound familiar? In chapter eight, the men of Ephraim complained to Gideon in almost the same words. Gideon answered them gently, and their anger cooled. But Jephthah is a different kind of man.

Jephthah answers.

Jephthah had called them, he says. And they did not come. So he risked his own life, and God gave him the victory. Now they want to fight him for it.

Then Jephthah gathers the men of Gilead, and they fight against Ephraim. And Gilead defeats them. The quarrel had begun with an insult, when the men of Ephraim mocked the Gileadites, saying, you are just runaways from Ephraim and Manasseh.

Then the men of Gilead take the river crossings of the Jordan, the fords, so that the men of Ephraim cannot get home.

And they set a test.

The Ephraimites could not say the sound sh. When they tried to say Shibboleth, it came out Sibboleth. So the men of Gilead could tell at once who was from Ephraim. And those who failed the test were not allowed to cross, and many of the men of Ephraim fell there by the Jordan.

It is a heartbreaking scene. These were not foreign enemies. They were brothers, children of Jacob. And they were fighting each other at the river, over pride and insults. The family of Israel is beginning to tear itself apart.

What started this? Pride. Ephraim felt left out. They wanted the honour, without having shared the danger. And instead of a gentle answer, they met another angry man. Two proud sides, and no one willing to give way.

The word shibboleth has even come into the English language. It means a word or a custom that marks out who belongs to a group, and who does not. Sadly, God’s people have often used their own shibboleths to divide from one another.

Then the writer tells us about the end of Jephthah’s life.

Then come three more judges, each described in just a few words.

After Jephthah, Ibzan of Bethlehem judges Israel. He has thirty sons and thirty daughters. He sends his daughters to marry outside his clan, and brings in thirty wives for his sons from outside. He judges Israel for some years, then dies, and is buried at Bethlehem.

After him, Elon, from the tribe of Zebulun, judges Israel. Then he dies, and is buried in the land of Zebulun.

After him, Abdon, the son of Hillel, from Pirathon, judges Israel. He has forty sons and thirty grandsons, who ride on seventy young donkeys. Riding on donkeys was a sign of importance and wealth. Then Abdon dies, and is buried in the land of Ephraim.

Ibzan, Elon and Abdon. We know little about them. They seem to have been men of position, with large families. No battles are mentioned. And no great acts of faith either. They led for a while, and then they were gone.

We are now near the end of the cycle of judges. Only one more judge is left, the most famous of them all. And the book is getting darker.

KEY 12:3: 'And when I saw that ye delivered me not, I put my life in my hands, and passed over against the children of Ammon, and the LORD delivered them into my hand: wherefore then are ye come up unto me this day, to fight against me?' I put my life in my hands. Jephthah had taken the risk alone. And he knew where the victory came from: the LORD delivered them. It is easy to criticise from the side, as Ephraim did. It is much harder to step into danger. Those who stay away from the battle should be slow to complain about how it was fought.

KEY 12:5: 'And the Gileadites took the passages of Jordan before the Ephraimites: and it was so, that when those Ephraimites which were escaped said, Let me go over; that the men of Gilead said unto him, Art thou an Ephraimite? If he said, Nay;' Art thou an Ephraimite? The very question shows how divided Israel had become. Brothers had become strangers, checking each other’s accents at the river. When God’s people turn against one another, everyone loses. Jesus prayed that his followers would be one.

KEY 12:7: 'And Jephthah judged Israel six years. Then died Jephthah the Gileadite, and was buried in one of the cities of Gilead.' Jephthah’s rule is short, and his story ends quietly. Not even the name of his city is given. He had won a great victory. But he had also made a rash vow, and fought his own brothers. The judges were real people, with real faith and real failings. None of them was the perfect deliverer Israel truly needed.

THREAD: A proverb says, 'Only by pride cometh contention: but with the well advised is wisdom.' And Jesus prayed for all who would believe in him, 'That they all may be one'.

TODAY: Is there a disagreement in your family, your church or your friendships, where pride is getting in the way? Be the one who gives a gentle answer today. Ask God to help you make peace, not win the argument.

QUESTION: The quarrel between Gilead and Ephraim began with pride and insults. What helps you to stop a disagreement from growing into a bigger fight?

PRAYER: Lord God, forgive me for pride, and for the times I have wanted honour without sharing the risk. Forgive your people for the ways we have divided from one another. Give me a gentle answer when others are angry. Make your people one, as Jesus prayed. Amen.
