# Judges 14: Samson sees a Philistine woman at Timnath, get her for me, it was of the LORD, the young lion, honey in the carcase, the wedding feast and the riddle, out of the eater came forth meat, the wife pressed, the riddle told, Ashkelon (gently), his anger. Keys 14:4, 14:6, 14:14.
TITLE: Out of the Strong Came Forth Sweetness

HOOK: A young lion leaps out of the vineyards, roaring. And the young man in its path has no weapon. Nothing in his hands at all. Moments later, the lion lies dead, and the young man walks on, and tells no one. He is the strongest man in the world. But strength of body is not the same as strength of character.

WHERE: In chapter thirteen, an angel told a woman who had no children that she would have a son, a Nazarite to God from his birth. Her husband Manoah prayed, teach us what we shall do unto the child. The angel said, why askest thou after my name, seeing it is secret? He went up in the flame of the altar. The child was born, and called Samson, and the LORD blessed him.

Now, in chapter fourteen, Samson is grown. Listen for what Samson wants, and how he gets it. And listen for how God works, even through Samson’s own stubborn choices.

STORY: Samson goes down to the town of Timnath, and sees a young woman there, one of the daughters of the Philistines. He comes home and tells his father and mother. I have seen a woman in Timnath, of the Philistines. Now get her for me as my wife.

His parents are troubled. Is there no woman among our own relatives, or among all our people, that you must marry one of the Philistines? The Philistines did not worship the LORD.

But Samson says, get her for me, for she pleases me well.

Notice what drives Samson. I have seen. She pleases me. Samson lives by what he sees and wants. It is a pattern that will run through his whole life.

And yet the writer adds something surprising.

So Samson goes down to Timnath with his father and mother. And as they come to the vineyards, a young lion comes roaring at him.

And Samson tells no one. Not even his parents.

Remember that a Nazarite was not to touch anything dead. Watch what happens next.

Some time later, Samson goes back to marry the woman. And he turns aside to look at the body of the lion. There is a swarm of bees in it, and honey. And he scoops out the honey in his hands, and eats it as he walks. He gives some to his father and mother, and they eat. But he does not tell them he took the honey from the dead lion.

Samson has already begun to treat his calling carelessly. He touched the dead body, and hid it from his parents.

Then Samson holds a wedding feast, as young men did in those days. The Philistines bring thirty young men to be his companions. And Samson says, let me tell you a riddle. If you can solve it within the seven days of the feast, I will give you thirty linen garments and thirty sets of clothes. If you cannot, you must give them to me. And they say, tell us your riddle.

They cannot solve it. Of course they cannot. It is about something only Samson knows.

For three days they try, and fail. Then they turn on Samson’s bride. Get your husband to tell you the answer, they say, or we will burn you and your father’s house with fire. Did you invite us here to rob us?

So the bride weeps before Samson. You hate me, she says. You do not really love me. You have told my people a riddle, and you have not told me the answer.

Samson says, I have not even told my father and mother. Why should I tell you?

But she weeps before him all the rest of the feast. And on the seventh day, because she presses him so hard, he tells her. And she tells the young men.

So on the seventh day, before the sun goes down, the men of the city say to him, what is sweeter than honey? And what is stronger than a lion?

Samson knows he has been tricked. And he answers with a bitter insult about his bride.

Then the Spirit of the LORD comes upon him. He goes down to the Philistine city of Ashkelon, and strikes down thirty men there, and takes their clothes, and gives them to the men who solved the riddle. And in his anger, he goes home to his father’s house.

And Samson’s bride is given to his best man.

What a sad and tangled story. Samson wanted a wife because she pleased his eyes. He broke his Nazarite vow and hid it. He set a riddle out of pride. His bride betrayed him under threat. And he left in a rage. Nothing about this is a good example. And yet the Spirit of the LORD came upon him, and God began to use him against the Philistines who ruled over Israel.

God can work even through our mistakes. But that does not make our mistakes good. Samson’s choices would cost him dearly.

KEY 14:4: 'But his father and his mother knew not that it was of the LORD, that he sought an occasion against the Philistines: for at that time the Philistines had dominion over Israel.' This is a hard verse to understand. God did not approve of Samson’s selfish choice. But God was able to use it to bring about his purpose, to begin to free Israel from the Philistines. God is so great that he can weave even our wrong choices into his plan. But that is no excuse for making them.

KEY 14:6: 'And the Spirit of the LORD came mightily upon him, and he rent him as he would have rent a kid, and he had nothing in his hand: but he told not his father or his mother what he had done.' Samson’s strength did not come from big muscles. It came when the Spirit of the LORD came mightily upon him. His strength was a gift from God. And it was meant to be used for God’s purposes. The tragedy of Samson is a man with God’s gift, but not always God’s heart.

KEY 14:14: 'And he said unto them, Out of the eater came forth meat, and out of the strong came forth sweetness. And they could not in three days expound the riddle.' Meat here means food. Out of the lion came honey. Many Christians have seen a picture in Samson’s riddle. Out of something fierce and deadly, God can bring sweetness. Out of the strongest enemy, death itself, God brought life, through the death and rising of Jesus.

THREAD: A proverb says, 'He that ruleth his spirit' is better 'than he that taketh a city.' And Paul wrote, 'And we know that all things work together for good to them that love God'.

TODAY: Samson lived by what pleased his eyes. Is there something you want right now that you have not brought to God? Ask him today whether it pleases him, not just whether it pleases you.

QUESTION: Samson had great strength, but little self control. Which do you think is harder to have, and why does the Bible value self control so highly?

PRAYER: Lord God, thank you that you can bring sweetness out of bitterness, and life out of death. Forgive me for the times I have lived by what pleases my eyes, instead of what pleases you. Give me not only your gifts, but your heart, and teach me to rule my own spirit. Amen.
