# Judges 15: Samson's wife given away, the foxes and firebrands, the Philistines' revenge (gently), the rock Etam, the men of Judah bind him, the cords as burnt flax, the jawbone (avoids 15:15-16), thirst and En-hakkore (avoids 15:19 "drunk"). Keys 15:11, 15:14, 15:18.
TITLE: The Cords Became as Flax Burnt with Fire

HOOK: Three thousand men climb up to a cave in a high rock. They have come to arrest one man. But they are not his enemies. They are his own people. And they have come to tie him up and hand him over to the enemy, because they are more afraid of making trouble than of living as slaves.

WHERE: In chapter fourteen, Samson saw a Philistine woman and said, get her for me, for she pleaseth me well. The Spirit of the LORD came upon him, and he tore a lion apart with his bare hands. Out of the lion came honey, and Samson made a riddle: out of the strong came forth sweetness. But his bride was pressed into telling the answer, and the feast ended in anger. And his bride was given to another man.

Now, in chapter fifteen, the trouble between Samson and the Philistines grows. Listen for the circle of revenge. And listen for what Samson’s own people say to him.

STORY: Some time later, at the wheat harvest, Samson goes to visit his wife, taking a young goat as a gift. But her father will not let him in. I really thought you hated her, he says. So I gave her to your friend. Is not her younger sister more beautiful? Take her instead.

Samson is furious. This time, he says, I will be blameless when I do the Philistines harm.

He catches three hundred foxes. He ties them tail to tail in pairs, and puts a burning torch between each pair of tails. Then he lets them loose into the standing grain of the Philistines. The fire burns up the stacked grain and the standing grain, and the vineyards and the olive groves.

The Philistines ask, who has done this? And they are told, Samson, because his father in law gave his wife to another man. So the Philistines come and burn the woman and her father with fire.

Do you see the circle? Each side hurts the other, and each side says, they started it. Revenge leads to revenge, and the circle grows wider and more terrible.

Samson says, since you have done this, I will take revenge on you, and then I will stop. And he strikes them down with great force. Then he goes and lives in a cave in the rock of Etam.

But revenge does not stop so easily. The Philistines come up and camp in Judah, and spread out around a place called Lehi.

The men of Judah are alarmed. Why have you come up against us?

We have come to bind Samson, the Philistines say, and do to him as he has done to us.

So three thousand men of Judah go down to the rock of Etam. And they say to Samson.

Listen to what they say. The Philistines are rulers over us. They have accepted it. They do not want a deliverer. They want a quiet life, even if it means living under the enemy. Samson is causing trouble, and they want him gone.

And Samson answers simply, as they did to me, so I have done to them.

The men of Judah say, we have come to tie you up, and hand you over to the Philistines.

Samson says, swear to me that you will not attack me yourselves.

And they promise. We will only tie you up and hand you over. We will not harm you. So they bind him with two new ropes, and bring him up from the rock.

Imagine the scene. The Philistines see Samson coming, tied up and helpless. And they shout in triumph. They have him at last.

Then the Spirit of the LORD comes upon Samson.

He finds the fresh jawbone of a donkey lying on the ground, picks it up, and fights the Philistines with it, and many fall. Then Samson sings a little song of victory, a play on words, and throws the jawbone away. He calls the place Ramath-lehi, which means the hill of the jawbone.

But after the battle, Samson is desperately thirsty.

For the first time in his story, Samson prays. And God answers. God splits open a hollow place in the ground at Lehi, and water comes out. Samson drinks, and his strength comes back, and he revives. He calls the place En-hakkore, which means the spring of the one who called.

It is a small moment, but a beautiful one. The strongest man in the world, lying in the dust, unable to save himself, crying out to God. And God gives him water from the ground.

The chapter ends by telling us that Samson judged Israel for twenty years, in the days of the Philistines.

KEY 15:11: 'Then three thousand men of Judah went to the top of the rock Etam, and said to Samson, Knowest thou not that the Philistines are rulers over us? what is this that thou hast done unto us? And he said unto them, As they did unto me, so have I done unto them.' Two sad things are in this verse. Judah had given up. They had grown so used to slavery that they would rather hand over their own deliverer than fight for freedom. And Samson’s answer shows his own problem. As they did to me, so I did to them. He was fighting for personal revenge, not for God’s glory.

KEY 15:14: 'And when he came unto Lehi, the Philistines shouted against him: and the Spirit of the LORD came mightily upon him, and the cords that were upon his arms became as flax that was burnt with fire, and his bands loosed from off his hands.' Flax that was burnt with fire falls apart at a touch. That is what the new ropes became. No bonds can hold when the Spirit of God comes. Many people feel tied up by fear, or habit, or the past. But God’s Spirit is stronger than any rope.

KEY 15:18: 'And he was sore athirst, and called on the LORD, and said, Thou hast given this great deliverance into the hand of thy servant: and now shall I die for thirst,' and fall into the hands of my enemies? Here Samson admits two things. The great deliverance was God’s gift, not his own. And without God, he is helpless, even against thirst. This is Samson at his best: weak, thirsty, and calling on the LORD. Our greatest strength is not our own power. It is knowing that we need God.

THREAD: Paul wrote, 'Recompense to no man evil for evil.' And Jesus said, 'If any man thirst, let him come unto me, and drink.'

TODAY: Is there someone you want to pay back for something they did to you? Ask God to break the circle of revenge in your heart today. And if you are tired and dry, call on him, like Samson. He gives water to the thirsty.

QUESTION: The men of Judah would rather tie up their deliverer than stand up to the Philistines. Why do you think people sometimes prefer a comfortable slavery to the risk of freedom?

PRAYER: Lord God, thank you that no rope can hold when your Spirit comes. Break anything that binds me, fear, habit or sin. Forgive me for wanting revenge, and help me not to repay evil for evil. When I am weak and thirsty, I call on you. Give me living water. Amen.
