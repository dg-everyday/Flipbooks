# Judges 16: Gaza and the gates carried away (16:1 told without detail), Delilah and the lords' silver, the green withs, new ropes, the woven locks, all his heart, the razor, he wist not that the LORD was departed, eyes put out, grinding in prison, the hair began to grow, Dagon's feast, the pillars, remember me, his burial. Keys 16:20, 16:22, 16:28.
TITLE: He Wist Not That the LORD Was Departed

HOOK: A man wakes up and hears a familiar shout: the Philistines are upon you! He has heard it before, and always shaken himself free. So he stands up, ready to do it again. But this time, something is different. And he does not even know it.

WHERE: In chapter fifteen, Samson’s revenge on the Philistines grew, and so did theirs on him. The men of Judah bound him with new ropes and handed him over. But the Spirit of the LORD came upon him, and the ropes became like burnt flax. Then Samson called on the LORD, and God gave him water.

Now, in chapter sixteen, we reach the end of Samson’s story. Listen for how Samson gives away his secret, little by little. And listen for the hair, and for Samson’s last prayer.

STORY: Samson goes to Gaza, one of the great cities of the Philistines, and stays there. The men of Gaza hear that he has come, and surround the place, and lie in wait for him at the city gate all through the dark hours. When it is light, they say, we will put an end to him.

But in the middle of the dark hours Samson gets up, and takes hold of the doors of the city gate, and the two posts, and pulls them up, bar and all. He puts them on his shoulders, and carries them up to the top of a hill near Hebron.

Samson keeps walking into danger, and relying on his strength to get out.

Then Samson falls in love with a woman in the valley of Sorek. Her name is Delilah.

The lords of the Philistines come to Delilah. Find out what makes him so strong, they say, and how we can overpower him and tie him up. And each of us will give you eleven hundred pieces of silver.

So Delilah asks Samson. Please tell me where your great strength comes from, and how you could be tied up and made helpless.

Samson plays with her. If they tie me with seven fresh bowstrings, he says, I will be as weak as any other man. So the Philistines bring them, and she ties him up, while men wait in hiding in the room. Then she cries, the Philistines are upon you, Samson! And he snaps the bowstrings like thread.

Twice more Delilah tries. New ropes that have never been used, Samson says. And he breaks them like thread. Then, weave the seven locks of my hair into the cloth on a loom. And he wakes, and pulls the whole loom away.

Step by step, Samson is playing with fire, coming closer and closer to the truth.

Then Delilah says, how can you say, I love you, when your heart is not with me? You have mocked me three times. And she presses him day after day, until he is sick to death of it.

And Samson tells her all his heart. No razor has ever touched my head, he says, for I have been a Nazarite to God from my birth. If my head is shaved, my strength will leave me, and I will be like any other man.

Delilah knows that this time he has told her the truth. She sends for the lords of the Philistines, and they come, with the money in their hands.

She lets him fall asleep with his head on her knees. She calls a man, and he shaves off the seven locks of his head. And his strength leaves him.

Then she cries, the Philistines are upon you, Samson!

The Philistines seize him, and put out his eyes. They take him down to Gaza, and bind him with bronze chains. And they make him grind grain in the prison.

The man who lived by what pleased his eyes has lost his eyes. The man who walked away with the gates of Gaza is now a prisoner in Gaza.

But then comes one small sentence of hope.

The lords of the Philistines gather for a great festival to their god Dagon. Our god has given Samson our enemy into our hands, they sing. And when they are merry, they say, bring out Samson, so that he can entertain us. They bring him out of the prison, and make fun of him, and stand him between the pillars of the temple.

Samson says to the boy leading him by the hand, let me feel the pillars that hold up the house, so that I can lean on them. The house is full, with all the lords of the Philistines, and people watching from the roof.

And Samson prays.

He takes hold of the two middle pillars that hold up the house, one with his right hand, and the other with his left. Let me die with the Philistines, he says. And he pushes with all his might. And the house falls on the lords, and on all the people in it.

Samson’s family come down and take his body, and bury him between Zorah and Eshtaol, in the tomb of his father Manoah. The very place where, long before, the Spirit of the LORD began to move him.

KEY 16:20: 'And she said, The Philistines be upon thee, Samson. And he awoke out of his sleep, and said, I will go out as at other times before, and shake myself. And he wist not that the LORD was departed from him.' He wist not means he did not know. This is one of the saddest verses in the Bible. Samson had played with his calling for so long that he did not even notice when the LORD left him. Sin does that. It dulls us, slowly, until we cannot feel what we have lost.

KEY 16:22: 'Howbeit the hair of his head began to grow again after he was shaven.' Howbeit means but. Samson is blind, chained and grinding grain. And yet, his hair begins to grow again. It is a quiet sign that God has not finished with him. Even after our worst failures, God can begin again. Grace grows back.

KEY 16:28: 'And Samson called unto the LORD, and said, O Lord GOD, remember me, I pray thee, and strengthen me, I pray thee, only this once, O God, that I may be at once avenged of the Philistines for my two eyes.' Remember me. For the first time, Samson admits that his strength comes only from God. Even his prayer is mixed with a wish for revenge. And still God answers it. The letter to the Hebrews lists Samson among the people of faith. Not because he was good, but because, at the end, he trusted God.

THREAD: The letter to the Hebrews names Samson among those who 'out of weakness were made strong'. And a dying thief once said to Jesus, 'Lord, remember me when thou comest into thy kingdom.'

TODAY: Is there something you are playing with, step by step, like Samson with Delilah? And if you have already fallen, remember: the hair began to grow again. Call on God. He remembers you.

QUESTION: Samson did not know that the LORD had departed from him. What helps you to notice when you are drifting away from God, before it is too late?

PRAYER: Lord God, keep me from playing with sin, step by step. Keep my heart awake to you. Thank you that even after the worst failures, your grace grows back. O Lord, remember me, I pray thee, and strengthen me, and use me for your glory. Amen.
