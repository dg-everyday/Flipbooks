# Judges 17: Micah steals and returns his mother's silver, the idols made "for the LORD", the house of gods, his son as priest, no king and every man right in his own eyes, the wandering Levite hired, now know I that the LORD will do me good. Keys 17:6, 17:10, 17:13.
TITLE: Every Man Did That Which Was Right in His Own Eyes

HOOK: A man steals a large amount of silver from his own mother. Then, when he hears her cursing the thief, he gets scared, and gives it back. His mother is so pleased that she blesses him in the name of the LORD. And then, with the very same silver, they make an idol, and set it up in their house. And they think they are doing something good for God.

WHERE: In chapter sixteen, Samson told Delilah all his heart, and his hair was shaved, and he wist not that the LORD was departed from him. The Philistines put out his eyes. But his hair began to grow again, and in his last prayer he said, O Lord GOD, remember me. With that, the stories of the judges come to an end.

Now, in chapter seventeen, the book changes. There are no more judges, no more battles against enemies. Instead, the writer shows us what was happening inside Israel. Listen for the sentence that sums it all up. And listen for how Micah tries to make his own religion.

STORY: There is a man in the hill country of Ephraim named Micah.

He says to his mother, the eleven hundred pieces of silver that were taken from you, the ones you put a curse on, I heard you. Look, I have the silver. I took it.

His mother does not scold him. Instead she says, 'Blessed be thou of the LORD, my son.' Perhaps she wanted to cancel her own curse.

Micah gives the silver back. And his mother says, I had set this silver apart for the LORD, for my son, to make a carved image and a metal idol. So she takes two hundred pieces of the silver, and gives it to a metalworker, who makes an idol. And it is put in Micah’s house.

Think about how confused this is. God’s law said clearly, thou shalt not make unto thee any graven image. It was one of the Ten Commandments. And here is a mother dedicating silver to the LORD, to make an idol. They used the LORD’s name, but they had completely forgotten what the LORD had said.

Micah has a shrine, a house of gods. He makes an ephod, a priest’s garment, and household idols. And he makes one of his own sons his priest.

But God had chosen the family of Aaron, from the tribe of Levi, to be priests. Micah does not care. He has built his own little religion, in his own house, his own way.

And then the writer gives us the key to the whole last part of the book.

Then a young man comes along. He is a Levite, from Bethlehem in Judah. He has left his town to live wherever he can find a place. And on his journey, he comes to Micah’s house in the hill country of Ephraim.

Micah asks him, where do you come from? And the young man says, I am a Levite from Bethlehem, and I am going to live wherever I can find a place.

Remember what we heard in Joshua. The Levites were given cities to live in, scattered through the land, so they could teach God’s law in every tribe. But this Levite is wandering, looking for somewhere to settle. Something has gone wrong. Perhaps the people were no longer supporting the Levites. Perhaps this young man was just looking for a better offer.

And Micah sees his chance.

So the Levite goes in. He is happy to live with Micah, and becomes like one of his sons. Micah ordains him, and the young man becomes his priest, and lives in Micah’s house.

And Micah is delighted.

Let us be honest. It is easy to laugh at Micah. But are we so different? It is very tempting to build a faith that suits us. To keep the parts of God’s word we like, and leave out the rest. To use God’s name, while really doing what is right in our own eyes. Micah thought that having the right religious things, a shrine, an ephod, a Levite, would make God bless him. But God is not pleased by religious objects. He is pleased by hearts that love him and obey his word.

The next chapter will show what happened to Micah’s gods, and to his priest.

KEY 17:6: 'In those days there was no king in Israel, but every man did that which was right in his own eyes.' This sentence will come back again and again in the last chapters of Judges. It explains everything that follows. Without a king, and without obeying the LORD, their true King, everyone decided for themselves what was right. When there is no authority above us, we become our own gods. And the result is confusion, and worse. The book is preparing us for the coming of a king. And in the end, it points us to the true King, Jesus.

KEY 17:10: 'And Micah said unto him, Dwell with me, and be unto me a father and a priest, and I will give thee ten shekels of silver by the year, and a suit of apparel, and thy victuals. So the Levite went in.' Ten pieces of silver a year, clothes, and food. That was the price. Micah wanted a priest who would serve him. And the Levite wanted a comfortable job. Neither of them asked what God wanted. When faith becomes a business deal, something has gone badly wrong.

KEY 17:13: 'Then said Micah, Now know I that the LORD will do me good, seeing I have a Levite to my priest.' Now know I. Micah is sure God will bless him. But his whole religion breaks God’s law. He has idols in his house, a priest he hired himself, and a shrine God never asked for. It is possible to feel very certain, and still be very wrong. The question is not how sure we feel, but whether we are following God’s word.

THREAD: A proverb says, 'There is a way which seemeth right unto a man, but the end thereof are the ways of death.' And Jesus said, 'God is a Spirit: and they that worship him must worship him in spirit and in truth.'

TODAY: Are there parts of God’s word you have quietly decided to ignore, because they do not suit you? Ask God to show you today. And ask him to help you worship him his way, not your own.

QUESTION: Micah used the LORD’s name, but did what was right in his own eyes. How can we tell the difference between truly following God, and making a faith that suits ourselves?

PRAYER: Lord God, you are the true King. Forgive me for the times I have done what is right in my own eyes, and called it following you. Keep me from building a faith that suits me. Teach me to worship you in spirit and in truth, and to follow your word, all of it. Amen.
