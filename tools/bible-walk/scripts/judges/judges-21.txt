# Judges 21: the rash oath at Mizpeh, weeping at the house of God, why is one tribe lacking, Jabesh-gilead (told gently), the six hundred at the rock Rimmon, the daughters of Shiloh at the feast (gently, not praised), every man to his inheritance, no king in Israel; looking ahead to Ruth, Samuel, David and the true King. Avoids 21:11-12. Keys 21:3, 21:6, 21:25.
TITLE: In Those Days There Was No King in Israel

HOOK: A nation stands weeping in front of an altar. They have just won a terrible war. But they are not celebrating. They are grieving. Because the enemy they defeated was their own brother. And now one of the twelve tribes of Israel is almost gone. How did it come to this?

WHERE: In chapter twenty, all Israel gathered as one man at Mizpeh. Benjamin refused to hand over the wicked men of Gibeah, and Israel went to war against its own brother. After two defeats, the people wept and fasted before the LORD. And in the end, the tribe of Benjamin was almost destroyed. Only six hundred men escaped to the rock of Rimmon.

Now we come to the last chapter of Judges. Listen for the problem Israel creates for itself with a rash promise, and how they try to solve it. And listen for the last sentence of the book.

STORY: Before the war, at Mizpeh, the men of Israel had made a solemn oath. None of us will give our daughters to Benjamin to marry.

Now the war is over. And the people come to the house of God, and sit there before God, and lift up their voices, and weep bitterly.

Israel is grieving for Benjamin, their brother. One tribe is cut off from Israel, they say. How can the six hundred survivors have wives and families, when we have sworn by the LORD not to give them our daughters?

Remember Jephthah’s rash vow? Here again, God’s people have made a hasty promise in anger, and now it has trapped them. Instead of admitting that the oath was wrong, and seeking God’s mercy, they try to find a way around it.

They remember that they had made another oath. Anyone who did not come up to the gathering at Mizpeh would be put to death. And they find that no one came from the town of Jabesh-gilead, on the other side of the Jordan.

So the assembly sends soldiers against Jabesh-gilead. The town is destroyed. And four hundred young women who had never married are brought back to the camp at Shiloh.

This is very hard to hear. To solve the problem of one terrible war, they start another. To keep one rash oath, they use another rash oath. The writer does not praise it. He simply tells us what happened when everyone did what was right in their own eyes.

Then the assembly sends a message of peace to the six hundred men of Benjamin at the rock of Rimmon. And the men of Benjamin come back, and are given the young women from Jabesh-gilead as wives. But there are not enough.

So the leaders come up with another plan. There is a yearly feast of the LORD at Shiloh, they say. When the young women of Shiloh come out to dance, let the men of Benjamin hide in the vineyards. Then let each one take a wife from the dancers, and go home to the land of Benjamin. And if their fathers or brothers complain, we will ask them to be kind, and remind them that they did not break the oath, because they did not give their daughters. They were taken.

And so it happens. The men of Benjamin take wives from among the dancers, and go back to their land, and rebuild their towns, and live in them.

Think about how twisted this is. The leaders found a way to keep the words of their oath, while ignoring its spirit. And the young women of Shiloh were treated as if they had no say at all. This is not a happy ending. It is the opposite. It shows us what happens when people try to fix the damage of sin with more of their own clever plans, instead of turning humbly to God.

Then the children of Israel go home, every man to his tribe and his family, and to his own land.

And the book ends with one sentence.

We have seen the sad circle go round and round. Sin, trouble, crying out, rescue. And each time a little lower. We have met unlikely heroes, Deborah, Gideon, Samson, flawed people whom God still used. And we have seen what happens when a nation forgets God, and everyone becomes their own king.

But the story does not end here. Remember the book of Ruth. In these very same days, in Bethlehem, God was quietly at work through a faithful young woman, and a kind man named Boaz. Their great-grandson would be David, the king who would lead Israel back to God. And from David’s family would come the true King, Jesus, who never fails, and who rules with perfect justice and love.

KEY 21:3: 'And said, O LORD God of Israel, why is this come to pass in Israel, that there should be to day one tribe lacking in Israel?' Why has this happened? It is an honest cry of grief. But the answer was not hard to find. It came about because of their own choices: the sin of Gibeah, Benjamin’s refusal, and their own rash anger. Sometimes when we ask God why, the honest answer is that we brought it on ourselves. And even then, the right place to bring our grief is to him.

KEY 21:6: 'And the children of Israel repented them for Benjamin their brother, and said, There is one tribe cut off from Israel this day.' Benjamin their brother. After all the fighting, they still love their brother tribe. Families can be broken by sin, but love can remain. And God would not let Benjamin disappear. From this small, broken tribe would come Israel’s first king, and many centuries later, the apostle Paul.

KEY 21:25: 'In those days there was no king in Israel: every man did that which was right in his own eyes.' This is how the book of Judges ends. It is not just a statement about politics. It is a diagnosis of the human heart. When everyone is their own king, deciding for themselves what is right, things fall apart. The whole book cries out for a good king. And the rest of the Bible tells the story of the King God would send.

THREAD: Isaiah wrote, 'All we like sheep have gone astray; we have turned every one to his own way; and the LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all.' And the prophet Zechariah said, 'behold, thy King cometh unto thee: he is just, and having salvation'.

TODAY: Where in your life are you doing what is right in your own eyes, instead of letting God be your King? Bring that area to him today. Ask Jesus to rule over every part of your life.

QUESTION: The book of Judges ends, every man did that which was right in his own eyes. What do you think it looks like in our world today when people do what is right in their own eyes? And what would it look like to let God be King instead?

PRAYER: Lord God, thank you for walking with me through the book of Judges. Thank you that even in the darkest times, you were faithful, and that you heard your people when they cried. I do not want to be my own king. Rule over me, Lord Jesus, the true King who never fails. Amen.
