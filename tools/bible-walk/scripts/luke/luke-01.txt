# Luke 1: Zacharias, Gabriel's message to Mary, Mary and Elisabeth, John born. Keys 1:37, 1:38, 1:46.
TITLE: With God Nothing Shall Be Impossible

HOOK: An old priest is standing alone in the holy place of the temple, burning incense before God, while a crowd prays outside. He has done this all his life. But this time, beside the altar, stands an angel. And the angel tells him that the prayer he gave up on long ago has been heard.

WHERE: This is the first step of our walk through Luke. Luke begins like a careful historian, telling his friend Theophilus that he has 'perfect understanding of all things from the very first', and is writing them down in order.

We are in the land of Israel, in the days of Herod, king of Judaea, under the rule of Rome. For a long time God has seemed silent. No prophet has spoken for generations. In this chapter, the silence breaks, twice, in two very different places: once in the temple in Jerusalem, and once in a small village in Galilee.

STORY: A priest named Zacharias and his wife Elisabeth were both 'righteous before God'. But they had no child, because Elisabeth was barren, and now they were both old.

It is Zacharias’ turn to burn incense in the temple. While the people pray outside, the angel of the Lord appears beside the altar, and Zacharias is afraid. The angel says, 'Fear not, Zacharias: for thy prayer is heard'. Your wife Elisabeth will have a son, and you will call his name John. He will be great in the sight of the Lord, filled with the Holy Ghost from his birth. He will go before the Lord 'in the spirit and power of Elias', to make ready a people prepared for the Lord.

But Zacharias cannot believe it. 'Whereby shall I know this? for I am an old man'. The angel answers, 'I am Gabriel, that stand in the presence of God'. Because you did not believe, you will be unable to speak until it happens. Zacharias comes out of the temple, and he cannot say a word. Soon Elisabeth is expecting a child.

Six months later, God sends Gabriel to a town in Galilee called Nazareth, to a young woman named Mary, engaged to a man named Joseph, of the family of David. The angel says, 'Hail, thou that art highly favoured, the Lord is with thee'. Mary is troubled, and wonders what this greeting means. 'Fear not, Mary: for thou hast found favour with God.' You will have a son, and you will call his name JESUS. He will be great, and will be called the Son of the Highest. God will give him the throne of his father David, 'and of his kingdom there shall be no end.'

Mary asks, 'How shall this be, seeing I know not a man?' Gabriel answers that the Holy Ghost will come upon her, and the power of the Highest will overshadow her, so the holy child will be called the Son of God. And your relative Elisabeth, who was called barren, is now six months along with a son in her old age.

Mary hurries to the hill country to visit Elisabeth. As soon as Elisabeth hears Mary’s greeting, the baby leaps inside her, and she is filled with the Holy Ghost. She cries out, 'Blessed art thou among women, and blessed is the fruit of thy womb.' And Mary answers with a song of praise. God has put down the mighty from their seats, she sings, and lifted up the lowly. 'He hath filled the hungry with good things; and the rich he hath sent empty away.' He has remembered his mercy, as he promised to Abraham.

When Elisabeth’s son is born, the neighbours expect him to be named Zacharias, after his father. But Elisabeth says no, he will be called John. They ask his father, who writes on a tablet, 'His name is John.' At once his mouth is opened, and he praises God. Filled with the Holy Ghost, Zacharias prophesies: 'Blessed be the Lord God of Israel; for he hath visited and redeemed his people'. And to his little son he says, 'And thou, child, shalt be called the prophet of the Highest'. He will prepare the way, because God’s mercy is coming like the first light of a new day, 'To give light to them that sit in darkness'.

And the child grows, and becomes strong in spirit, and lives in the desert until the day he begins his work.

KEY 1:37: 'For with God nothing shall be impossible.' Gabriel said this about two women: one too old to have a child, and one who had never been with a man. Both were impossible. Neither was impossible for God. This verse is not a promise that God will do whatever we want. It is a promise that nothing can stop God from doing what he has said he will do.

KEY 1:38: 'And Mary said, Behold the handmaid of the Lord; be it unto me according to thy word. And the angel departed from her.' A handmaid is a servant. Mary did not understand everything, and she knew what people might say about an unmarried girl expecting a child. But she put herself in God’s hands. Compare her with Zacharias. He asked, how can I know? and doubted. Mary asked, how will this be? and trusted. Be it unto me according to thy word is one of the most beautiful prayers anyone can pray.

KEY 1:46: 'And Mary said, My soul doth magnify the Lord,' To magnify means to make great. When something is magnified, it does not get bigger in itself; we simply see how big it really is. Mary’s song makes God look as great as he truly is. And her song is full of God turning things upside down: the proud brought down, the humble lifted up, the hungry filled. That is the kind of God Luke will show us all through his book.

THREAD: The last words of the Old Testament, in the prophet Malachi, promised, 'Behold, I will send you Elijah the prophet before the coming of the great and dreadful day of the LORD'. Elias is the Greek form of the name Elijah. When Gabriel says John will come in the spirit and power of Elias, he is saying: the long silence is over. The promise at the end of the Old Testament is beginning to come true.

TODAY: Is there something in your life that looks impossible? Bring it to God, and remember Gabriel’s words. And when God shows you his will, even if you do not understand it all, pray Mary’s prayer: be it unto me according to thy word.

QUESTION: Mary said, be it unto me according to thy word. What part of your life will you put into God’s hands today?

PRAYER: Lord God, with you nothing shall be impossible. Like Mary, I am your servant; be it unto me according to your word. Let my soul magnify you, and help me to trust you where I cannot yet see the way. Amen.
