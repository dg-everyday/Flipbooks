# Luke 3: John the Baptist's preaching, the baptism of Jesus, the family line to Adam. Keys 3:6, 3:11, 3:22.
TITLE: All Flesh Shall See the Salvation of God

HOOK: Crowds are streaming out of the towns into the wilderness by the river Jordan. They are going to hear a wild preacher who tells them hard truths, and they cannot stop listening. Tax collectors come. Soldiers come. Ordinary families come. And every one of them asks him the same question: 'What shall we do then?'

WHERE: In chapter two, Jesus was born in Bethlehem, Simeon held him in the temple, and at twelve he amazed the teachers in his Father’s house. Then he grew up quietly in Nazareth.

Now Luke jumps forward many years. True to his careful style, he names the rulers of the day: Tiberius Caesar the emperor, Pontius Pilate the governor of Judaea, Herod ruling Galilee, and Annas and Caiaphas the high priests. And then he says, in the middle of all these powerful names, 'the word of God came unto John the son of Zacharias in the wilderness.' God’s word did not come to the emperor’s palace, or to the high priest’s house. It came to a man in the desert, the son of an old priest, just as Gabriel had promised.

STORY: John goes all through the country around the Jordan, preaching the baptism of repentance for the forgiveness of sins. Luke says this is what Isaiah wrote about: 'The voice of one crying in the wilderness, Prepare ye the way of the Lord, make his paths straight.' Every valley will be filled, every mountain brought low, the crooked made straight and the rough ways smooth.

John does not flatter the crowds. 'O generation of vipers, who hath warned you to flee from the wrath to come?' Show by the way you live that you have really turned to God, he says. Do not tell yourselves, we have Abraham as our father, as if that is enough. God could raise up children for Abraham from these stones. The axe is already at the root of the trees.

So the people ask, what shall we do? John answers simply. Whoever has two coats, give one to someone who has none, and whoever has food, do the same. Tax collectors ask, what shall we do? Take no more than you are supposed to. Soldiers ask, and what shall we do? 'Do violence to no man, neither accuse any falsely; and be content with your wages.'

The people begin to wonder whether John might be the Christ. But John says, I baptize you with water, but one mightier than I is coming, whose shoes I am not worthy to untie. 'he shall baptize you with the Holy Ghost and with fire'. He compares the coming one to a farmer at harvest, separating the wheat from the chaff.

John speaks just as boldly to the powerful. He rebukes Herod for taking his brother’s wife and for all his other evil deeds. And Herod adds one more evil: he shuts John up in prison.

Before that, Luke tells us, Jesus himself came to be baptized. And while he was praying, the heaven was opened, and the Holy Ghost came down upon him in a bodily shape like a dove, and a voice came from heaven.

Jesus is now about thirty years old. And Luke ends the chapter with his family line. It goes back through Joseph, through King David, through Abraham, through Noah, all the way to the very first man: 'Adam, which was the son of God.'

KEY 3:6: 'And all flesh shall see the salvation of God.' This is the end of the quotation from Isaiah, and only Luke includes this line. All flesh means everyone: every people, every nation, every kind of person. Luke has been showing this since the angels said, good tidings to all people. The salvation God is bringing is not only for one nation, but for the whole world.

KEY 3:11: 'He answereth and saith unto them, He that hath two coats, let him impart to him that hath none; and he that hath meat, let him do likewise.' Impart means share, and meat here means food. When people asked John what turning to God looks like, he did not give them a complicated ritual. He told them to be generous, honest and fair, right where they were. Real repentance shows up in ordinary things: our clothes, our food, our work, our wages.

KEY 3:22: 'And the Holy Ghost descended in a bodily shape like a dove upon him, and a voice came from heaven, which said, Thou art my beloved Son; in thee I am well pleased.' Here we see the Father speaking, the Son being baptized, and the Holy Ghost coming down. And notice that Jesus had not yet preached a sermon or done a miracle. The Father was well pleased with him simply because he was his beloved Son. In Jesus, God’s people are loved like that too.

THREAD: Matthew traces Jesus’ family line back to Abraham, the father of Israel. Luke goes further, all the way back to Adam, the father of the whole human race. In Genesis, 'God created man in his own image'. Luke is showing that Jesus came not only for one nation, but for every son and daughter of Adam, everyone made in the image of God.

TODAY: John’s message was simple: turn back to God, and show it in how you treat others. Ask yourself John’s question today: what shall I do? Maybe it is sharing something you have, being honest at work, or being content instead of grumbling.

QUESTION: The crowds asked John, what shall we do then? What is one practical way you will show today that you belong to God?

PRAYER: Lord God, prepare the way in my heart for Jesus. Make the crooked places straight. Thank you that in Jesus you call me beloved. Make me generous, honest and content, and let all flesh see your salvation. Amen.
