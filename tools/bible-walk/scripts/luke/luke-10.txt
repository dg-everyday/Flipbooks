# Luke 10: the seventy sent, the good Samaritan, Martha and Mary. Keys 10:27, 10:37, 10:42.
TITLE: Go, and Do Thou Likewise

HOOK: A man is lying half dead beside a lonely road. Robbers have stripped him, beaten him, and left him there. A priest comes down the road, sees him, and crosses to the other side. Then a Levite, who served in the temple, does the same. And then a third traveller comes, the last person anyone listening to Jesus would expect to stop.

WHERE: In chapter nine, Peter said Jesus was the Christ of God, three disciples saw his glory on the mountain, and Jesus set his face steadfastly toward Jerusalem. From here to chapter nineteen, Luke follows Jesus on that long road. Many of the parables found only in Luke are told along the way.

In chapter ten, Jesus sends out more workers, answers a lawyer’s question with one of the most famous stories ever told, and gently teaches a busy woman what matters most.

STORY: Jesus appoints seventy others and sends them out two by two, ahead of him, into every town he will visit. He tells them, 'The harvest truly is great, but the labourers are few: pray ye therefore the Lord of the harvest, that he would send forth labourers into his harvest.' I send you as lambs among wolves, he says. When you enter a house, say, peace be to this house. Heal the sick, and tell them, the kingdom of God has come near to you.

The seventy return full of joy: Lord, even the devils obey us in your name! Jesus says, do not rejoice that the spirits obey you, 'but rather rejoice, because your names are written in heaven.' In that hour Jesus himself rejoices in spirit, and thanks his Father for hiding these things from the wise and revealing them to little children.

Then a lawyer, an expert in the law of Moses, stands up to test him. 'Master, what shall I do to inherit eternal life?' Jesus asks, what is written in the law? The lawyer answers with the great commandments. 'Thou hast answered right', Jesus says: 'this do, and thou shalt live.' But the lawyer, wanting to justify himself, asks, 'And who is my neighbour?'

So Jesus tells a story. A man is going down from Jerusalem to Jericho, on a steep and lonely road, when robbers attack him and leave him half dead. A priest happens to come by, sees him, and passes by on the other side. A Levite does the same.

Then a Samaritan comes. In those days Jews and Samaritans were bitter enemies, and most Jews would not even speak to a Samaritan. But this Samaritan sees the man, and has compassion on him. He goes to him, binds up his wounds, pouring in oil and wine, sets him on his own animal, brings him to an inn, and takes care of him. The next day he gives the innkeeper two pence and says, take care of him, and whatever more you spend, I will repay you when I come back.

Then Jesus asks the lawyer a question that turns his question around. Not, who is my neighbour? but, which of these three was a neighbour to the man who fell among thieves? The lawyer cannot even bring himself to say the Samaritan. He says, the one who showed mercy. And Jesus answers him.

As Jesus and his disciples travel on, they come to a village where a woman named Martha welcomes him into her house. Her sister Mary sits at Jesus’ feet and listens to his word. But Martha is 'cumbered about much serving', distracted with all the work of hosting. She comes to Jesus and says, 'Lord, dost thou not care that my sister hath left me to serve alone? bid her therefore that she help me.'

And Jesus answers her gently. 'Martha, Martha, thou art careful and troubled about many things'. Careful here means anxious and full of worry.

KEY 10:27: 'And he answering said, Thou shalt love the Lord thy God with all thy heart, and with all thy soul, and with all thy strength, and with all thy mind; and thy neighbour as thyself.' The lawyer knew the right answer. Love God with everything, and love your neighbour as yourself. The problem was not knowing it. The problem was doing it. That is why he wanted to know exactly who counted as his neighbour, so that he would know who did not.

KEY 10:37: 'And he said, He that shewed mercy on him. Then said Jesus unto him, Go, and do thou likewise.' Jesus never tells the lawyer exactly who his neighbour is. He shows him what a neighbour does. A neighbour sees someone in need and has compassion, whoever they are, even an enemy. The question is not, who deserves my help? but, who can I help? And Jesus himself is the greatest good Samaritan, who came to us when we were wounded and helpless, and paid for our healing.

KEY 10:42: 'But one thing is needful: and Mary hath chosen that good part, which shall not be taken away from her.' Martha was doing a good thing. Serving Jesus is good. But she was so busy working for him that she had no time to be with him. Mary chose the one thing that matters most: sitting at Jesus’ feet and listening. What we receive from Jesus can never be taken away.

THREAD: The command to love your neighbour comes from the law of Moses. And the same chapter says this about foreigners: 'thou shalt love him as thyself; for ye were strangers in the land of Egypt'. God always meant his people’s love to reach beyond their own kind, to strangers and outsiders, because he himself had shown mercy to them when they were strangers and slaves.

TODAY: Is there someone on your road today, hurting, that you have been passing by on the other side? Go, and do likewise. And if you are busy and anxious like Martha, stop. Sit at Jesus’ feet for a while, even just a few minutes. That is the one thing needful.

QUESTION: Jesus said, go, and do thou likewise. Who is the person in need on your road today, and what will you do for them?

PRAYER: Lord Jesus, you are the good Samaritan who came to me when I was wounded and helpless. Give me eyes to see those who are hurting, and a heart of compassion to stop and help. And when I am anxious and busy, draw me back to sit at your feet. Amen.

NEXT: Next on the walk, Luke chapter eleven. A disciple asks Jesus, Lord, teach us to pray, and Jesus answers with a prayer, a story about a friend at the door, and a promise: ask, and it shall be given you.
