# Luke 13: repent or perish, the fig tree, the bent woman loosed, the strait gate, the lament over Jerusalem. Keys 13:3, 13:12, 13:34.
TITLE: Woman, Thou Art Loosed

HOOK: A woman has been bent over for eighteen years. She cannot straighten up at all. For eighteen years she has seen mostly the ground, the dust, and the feet of the people around her. One sabbath day she is in the synagogue where Jesus is teaching. And he sees her, and calls her to come to him.

WHERE: In chapter twelve, Jesus told the story of the rich fool, taught his followers not to worry but to seek the kingdom of God, and called them to be ready for his return. Now, in chapter thirteen, Jesus is still on the road toward Jerusalem. People bring him news of tragedies, and he uses it to call them to turn back to God. He heals on the sabbath and angers a synagogue ruler. And at the end, he speaks with sorrow about the city where he is going to die.

STORY: Some people tell Jesus about Galileans whom Pilate had killed while they were offering sacrifices. They seem to think these people must have been worse sinners than others. Jesus asks, do you think they were sinners above all the Galileans because they suffered this? 'I tell you, Nay'. And he mentions another tragedy: eighteen people who died when a tower in Siloam fell on them. Were they worse than everyone else in Jerusalem? No.

Then he tells a parable. A man has a fig tree planted in his vineyard. For three years he comes looking for fruit and finds none. Cut it down, he tells the gardener; why should it use up the ground? But the gardener answers, 'Lord, let it alone this year also, till I shall dig about it, and dung it'. If it bears fruit, good. If not, then cut it down.

On the sabbath, Jesus is teaching in a synagogue, and there is the woman who has been bent over for eighteen years. Jesus calls her to him, speaks to her, and lays his hands on her. 'and immediately she was made straight, and glorified God.'

But the ruler of the synagogue is indignant, because Jesus has healed on the sabbath. He says to the people, there are six days for work; come and be healed on those days, not on the sabbath. Jesus answers, you hypocrites! Does not each of you untie his ox or his donkey on the sabbath and lead it to water? 'And ought not this woman, being a daughter of Abraham, whom Satan hath bound, lo, these eighteen years, be loosed from this bond on the sabbath day?' His opponents are ashamed, and all the people rejoice at the glorious things he is doing.

Jesus says the kingdom of God is like a grain of mustard seed that grows into a tree where the birds rest, and like a little leaven that a woman hides in flour until the whole batch rises. It starts small and hidden, and it grows.

As Jesus travels through the towns and villages toward Jerusalem, someone asks, 'Lord, are there few that be saved?' Jesus does not answer how many. He answers how. 'Strive to enter in at the strait gate'. Strait means narrow. Many will try to enter and will not be able. The master of the house will rise and shut the door, and some will stand outside knocking, saying, we ate and drank with you, you taught in our streets. And he will say, I do not know you. But people will come from the east and the west, the north and the south, and sit down in the kingdom of God. 'And, behold, there are last which shall be first, and there are first which shall be last.'

Some Pharisees warn Jesus to leave, because Herod wants to kill him. Jesus calls Herod 'that fox', and says he will go on with his work until it is finished, for no prophet may die outside Jerusalem. And then, thinking of the city, Jesus grieves over it.

KEY 13:3: 'I tell you, Nay: but, except ye repent, ye shall all likewise perish.' When tragedy strikes others, it is easy to think it must be their fault. Jesus will not let us look at other people’s sins. He turns the question back to us. Every one of us needs to turn back to God. And the parable of the fig tree that follows shows God’s patience: he gives time, and more time, to bear fruit. But time is not endless.

KEY 13:12: 'And when Jesus saw her, he called her to him, and said unto her, Woman, thou art loosed from thine infirmity.' She did not ask for anything. Jesus saw her, called her, and set her free. Loosed means untied, released. For eighteen years she had been bound, and in a moment Jesus set her free. And when the ruler complained, Jesus called her a daughter of Abraham. She was not just a problem to be solved on the right day. She was a precious member of God’s family.

KEY 13:34: 'O Jerusalem, Jerusalem, which killest the prophets, and stonest them that are sent unto thee; how often would I have gathered thy children together, as a hen doth gather her brood under her wings, and ye would not!' Jesus is walking toward the city that will kill him, and he is not angry. He is heartbroken. He longs to gather them like a mother hen gathering her chicks under her wings to protect them. The saddest words are the last ones: and ye would not. Jesus never forces his love on anyone.

THREAD: The picture of shelter under wings is an old one. The psalmist wrote of God, 'He shall cover thee with his feathers, and under his wings shalt thou trust'. Jesus speaks of himself the way the psalms speak of God: as the one who longs to shelter his people. The only question is whether we will come.

TODAY: Is there something that has bent you over for a long time: a burden, a habit, a sorrow? Jesus sees you, and he can set you free. And remember his patience with the fig tree. He is giving you time today to turn to him and bear fruit.

QUESTION: Jesus longed to gather Jerusalem under his wings, but they would not. What will you bring to Jesus today, to shelter under his care?

PRAYER: Lord Jesus, you saw the bent woman and set her free. See me, and loose me from whatever binds me. Thank you for your patience with me. Gather me under your wings, and let me never be among those who would not. Amen.
