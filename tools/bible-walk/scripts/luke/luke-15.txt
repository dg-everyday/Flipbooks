# Luke 15: the lost sheep, the lost coin, the lost son and his brother. Keys 15:4, 15:18, 15:31.
TITLE: He Was Lost, and Is Found

HOOK: A young man is sitting in a pigsty in a foreign country. He is starving. He looks at the pods the pigs are eating and wishes he could fill his stomach with them. Not long ago he had more money than he knew what to do with. He spent it all. Now no one gives him anything. And sitting in the mud, he suddenly remembers his father’s house.

WHERE: In chapter fourteen, Jesus told the story of the great supper, where the invited guests made excuses and the poor and the outsiders were brought in to fill the house. Now, in chapter fifteen, we see that it was really happening. Luke says, 'Then drew near unto him all the publicans and sinners for to hear him.' And the Pharisees and scribes murmured: 'This man receiveth sinners, and eateth with them.'

So Jesus tells them three stories, each about something lost and found. This is the heart of the book of Luke, and perhaps the most loved chapter Jesus ever spoke.

STORY: First, a shepherd. Suppose a man has a hundred sheep and loses one of them. Does he not leave the ninety and nine in the wilderness and go after the one that is lost, until he finds it? And when he finds it, he lays it on his shoulders, rejoicing. He comes home and calls his friends and neighbours: 'Rejoice with me; for I have found my sheep which was lost.' In the same way, Jesus says, there will be more joy in heaven over one sinner who repents than over ninety-nine who think they need no repentance.

Second, a woman. Suppose a woman has ten silver coins and loses one. Does she not light a candle, sweep the house, and search carefully until she finds it? And when she finds it, she calls her friends: rejoice with me. 'there is joy in the presence of the angels of God over one sinner that repenteth.'

Then the third story, the longest. A man has two sons. The younger one says, 'Father, give me the portion of goods that falleth to me.' In those days, asking for your inheritance while your father was still alive was like saying, I wish you were dead. But the father divides his property between them.

The younger son gathers everything and goes to a far country, and there he wastes it all on wild living. When the money runs out, a great famine comes. He takes a job feeding pigs, the lowest work imaginable for a Jewish young man. He is so hungry he would eat the pigs’ food, and no one gives him anything.

Then, Jesus says, 'he came to himself'. He realises that even his father’s hired servants have bread enough and to spare, while he is starving. And he decides what he will do.

So he gets up and goes home. 'But when he was yet a great way off, his father saw him, and had compassion, and ran, and fell on his neck, and kissed him.' In those days a respected older man would never run. This father ran.

The son begins his speech. But the father hardly lets him finish. He calls to his servants: 'Bring forth the best robe, and put it on him; and put a ring on his hand, and shoes on his feet'. Kill the fatted calf, and let us eat and celebrate. 'For this my son was dead, and is alive again; he was lost, and is found.' And they begin to make merry.

But the story is not over. The older son is out working in the field. He comes near the house and hears music and dancing. A servant tells him his brother has come home and their father has thrown a feast. The older brother is angry, and refuses to go in.

So the father comes out to him too, and pleads with him. The older son says, all these years I have served you and never disobeyed you, and you never gave me even a young goat to celebrate with my friends. But as soon as this son of yours comes, who wasted your money, you kill the fatted calf for him. And the father answers him tenderly. It was right to celebrate, he says, for this thy brother was dead, and is alive again.

The story ends there. We never find out whether the older brother went in.

KEY 15:4: 'What man of you, having an hundred sheep, if he lose one of them, doth not leave the ninety and nine in the wilderness, and go after that which is lost, until he find it?' Notice three things. The shepherd goes after the one. He keeps looking until he finds it. And when he finds it, he does not scold or drive it home; he carries it on his shoulders, rejoicing. That is how God feels about one lost person. Each one matters to him.

KEY 15:18: 'I will arise and go to my father, and will say unto him, Father, I have sinned against heaven, and before thee,' This is what repentance looks like. The son stops blaming others, admits his sin honestly, and turns around and heads home. He expected to come back as a servant. He did not yet know how much his father loved him. When we come back to God, we are always welcomed better than we expect.

KEY 15:31: 'And he said unto him, Son, thou art ever with me, and all that I have is thine.' The older brother was lost too, though he never left home. He worked hard, but like a servant, not a son, and he resented his father’s grace. The father did not reject him. He went out to him, called him son, and reminded him that everything was already his. God’s grace is for the rebel who ran away, and for the proud and bitter one who stayed.

THREAD: Long before, God said through the prophet Ezekiel, 'I will seek that which was lost, and bring again that which was driven away'. And later in this book, Jesus will meet a tax collector named Zacchaeus and say, 'For the Son of man is come to seek and to save that which was lost.' The shepherd, the woman and the father are all pictures of God himself, seeking the lost.

TODAY: Which son are you most like today: far away, or close by but cold inside? Either way, the Father is waiting, and running, and pleading. Come home, or come in. And when someone else comes back to God, join the celebration.

QUESTION: The younger son said, I will arise and go to my father. Is there something you need to bring home to your Father today?

PRAYER: Father, thank you that you seek the lost, and that you ran to meet me while I was still a great way off. Forgive me for wandering, and for the times I have been proud and resentful. Thank you that all you have is mine in Jesus. Help me rejoice over every one who is found. Amen.
