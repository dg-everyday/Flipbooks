# Luke 17: forgiveness and faith, unprofitable servants, the ten lepers, the coming of the kingdom. Keys 17:4, 17:5, 17:15.
TITLE: Where Are the Nine?

HOOK: Ten men stand at a distance, calling out across the road. They are lepers, and the law says they must keep away from everyone else. 'Jesus, Master, have mercy on us.' Jesus tells them to go and show themselves to the priests. As they go, their skin is healed. Ten men, made clean in a moment. But only one of them turns around and comes running back.

WHERE: In chapter sixteen, Jesus warned that no one can serve both God and money, and told of the rich man who ignored the beggar Lazarus at his gate. Now, in chapter seventeen, Jesus is still travelling toward Jerusalem, passing between Samaria and Galilee. He teaches his disciples about forgiveness, faith and humble service. Then comes the story of the ten lepers. And at the end, he speaks about the coming of his kingdom.

STORY: Jesus tells his disciples that it is impossible that things which cause people to stumble will never come. 'but woe unto him, through whom they come!' It would be better for him to be thrown into the sea with a millstone around his neck than to cause one of these little ones to stumble. 'Take heed to yourselves'.

If your brother sins against you, rebuke him, and if he repents, forgive him. And then Jesus raises the bar higher than anyone expected.

The apostles hear this and say to the Lord, increase our faith. Jesus answers that even a tiny faith, as small as a grain of mustard seed, could say to a sycamine tree, be pulled up by the roots and planted in the sea, and it would obey. The point is not the size of our faith, but the greatness of the God we trust.

Then Jesus tells a short parable about a servant who comes in from ploughing or looking after the sheep. Does his master thank him for doing what he was told? No. 'So likewise ye, when ye shall have done all those things which are commanded you, say, We are unprofitable servants: we have done that which was our duty to do.' We never put God in our debt. Everything we do for him is simply what we owe.

Then, as Jesus enters a village, ten lepers meet him, standing far off. They lift up their voices and cry for mercy. When he sees them, he says, 'Go shew yourselves unto the priests.' In those days, a leper who was healed had to be examined by a priest before he could return to normal life. And as they go, they are cleansed.

One of them, when he sees that he is healed, turns back. He comes praising God with a loud voice, and falls down on his face at Jesus’ feet, giving him thanks. 'and he was a Samaritan.' A foreigner, an outsider.

Jesus asks, 'Were there not ten cleansed? but where are the nine?' Were none found to return and give glory to God, except this stranger? And he says to him, 'Arise, go thy way: thy faith hath made thee whole.' All ten were healed in body. The one who came back received something more.

The Pharisees ask when the kingdom of God will come. Jesus says it does not come with signs you can watch for, so that people say, look here, or look there. 'for, behold, the kingdom of God is within you.'

Then he tells his disciples about the day when the Son of man will be revealed. It will come suddenly, like lightning. As in the days of Noah, people were eating and drinking, marrying and being given in marriage, until the flood came. As in the days of Lot, they were buying and selling, planting and building, until fire fell on Sodom. So it will be. Do not turn back for your belongings. 'Remember Lot’s wife.' Whoever tries to save his life will lose it, and whoever loses his life will preserve it.

KEY 17:4: 'And if he trespass against thee seven times in a day, and seven times in a day turn again to thee, saying, I repent; thou shalt forgive him.' To trespass means to sin against someone. Seven times in one day! It seems impossible. But that is the point. Forgiveness in God’s family has no limit, because God’s forgiveness toward us has no limit. We do not keep count of how many times we have forgiven, any more than God keeps count of how many times he has forgiven us.

KEY 17:5: 'And the apostles said unto the Lord, Increase our faith.' When Jesus told them to forgive seven times in a day, the apostles knew they could not do it on their own. Their answer was a prayer. That is the right response to every hard command of Jesus. Not, I cannot, but, Lord, increase my faith. Help me to trust you enough to obey.

KEY 17:15: 'And one of them, when he saw that he was healed, turned back, and with a loud voice glorified God,' Ten were healed, and nine kept walking. Perhaps they meant to say thank you later. Perhaps they were too busy, too excited, or took it for granted. Only one turned back. Thankfulness does not happen by itself. We have to choose to turn back and give God the glory.

THREAD: Long before, David sang, 'Bless the LORD, O my soul, and forget not all his benefits'. Benefits means the good things God has done. Nine of the ten lepers forgot. The one who remembered, and came back, was a Samaritan, an outsider. Once again in Luke, it is the outsider who shows the right heart toward God.

TODAY: Think of three things God has done for you recently, big or small. Turn back today and thank him for them, out loud if you can. And is there someone you need to forgive again? Ask God to increase your faith.

QUESTION: Jesus asked, were there not ten cleansed? but where are the nine? What will you turn back and thank God for today?

PRAYER: Lord Jesus, you have done so much for me, and so often I forget to thank you. Like the one leper, I turn back to glorify you. Increase my faith, so that I can forgive as you have forgiven me, again and again. Amen.
