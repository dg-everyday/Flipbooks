# Luke 18: the persistent widow, the Pharisee and the publican, little children, the rich ruler, the blind man at Jericho. Keys 18:1, 18:13, 18:27.
TITLE: God Be Merciful to Me a Sinner

HOOK: Two men walk up to the temple to pray. One is a Pharisee, respected and religious, who stands up tall and thanks God that he is not like other people. The other is a tax collector, despised by everyone. He stands far back, will not even lift his eyes to heaven, and beats his chest. He prays just seven words. And Jesus says that only one of these two men went home right with God.

WHERE: In chapter seventeen, Jesus taught his disciples to forgive again and again, and ten lepers were healed, though only one came back to give thanks. Now, in chapter eighteen, Jesus is drawing near to Jerusalem. He teaches about prayer with two stories, welcomes little children, meets a rich young ruler, and tells his disciples for the third time what will happen to him. And at the end of the chapter, he reaches Jericho, the last town before Jerusalem.

STORY: Jesus tells a parable so that his disciples will keep praying and not give up. In a certain city there was a judge who did not fear God or care about people. A widow kept coming to him, saying, 'Avenge me of mine adversary.' Give me justice. For a while he refused. But at last he said to himself, though I do not fear God or care about people, this widow keeps bothering me. I will give her justice, so she does not wear me out with her coming. And Jesus says, if even an unjust judge gives in, will not God, who loves his people, bring justice to those who cry to him? He will, and quickly. Then Jesus asks, 'when the Son of man cometh, shall he find faith on the earth?'

Then he tells a parable to some who trusted in themselves that they were righteous, and looked down on others. The Pharisee stood and prayed about himself: 'God, I thank thee, that I am not as other men are', thieves, cheats, adulterers, or even like this tax collector. I fast twice a week; I give a tenth of all I get. But the tax collector stood far off and prayed. 'I tell you, this man went down to his house justified rather than the other'. Justified means made right with God. 'for every one that exalteth himself shall be abased; and he that humbleth himself shall be exalted.'

People bring even babies to Jesus, so that he will touch them. The disciples rebuke them. But Jesus calls the children to him: 'Suffer little children to come unto me, and forbid them not: for of such is the kingdom of God.' Whoever does not receive the kingdom like a little child will never enter it.

A certain ruler asks him, 'Good Master, what shall I do to inherit eternal life?' Jesus reminds him of the commandments. The ruler says he has kept them all since he was young. Jesus says, 'Yet lackest thou one thing: sell all that thou hast, and distribute unto the poor, and thou shalt have treasure in heaven: and come, follow me.' When the ruler hears this, he is very sorrowful, for he is very rich. Jesus says how hard it is for the rich to enter the kingdom of God. It is easier for a camel to go through the eye of a needle. Those who hear it ask, 'Who then can be saved?' And Jesus answers them.

Peter says, we have left everything to follow you. Jesus promises that no one who has left home or family for the kingdom of God will fail to receive much more in this life, and in the world to come, life everlasting.

Then Jesus takes the twelve aside. We are going up to Jerusalem, he says, and everything written by the prophets about the Son of man will be accomplished. He will be mocked, mistreated, spat on, scourged and killed, and the third day he will rise again. But they understand none of these things.

As Jesus comes near Jericho, a blind man sits by the road begging. He hears the crowd and asks what is happening. They tell him Jesus of Nazareth is passing by. He cries out, 'Jesus, thou Son of David, have mercy on me.' People tell him to be quiet. He cries out all the more. Jesus stops, and has him brought near. 'What wilt thou that I shall do unto thee?' 'Lord, that I may receive my sight.' 'Receive thy sight: thy faith hath saved thee.' Immediately he can see, and he follows Jesus, glorifying God. And all the people who see it give praise to God.

KEY 18:1: 'And he spake a parable unto them to this end, that men ought always to pray, and not to faint;' To faint here means to lose heart and give up. Luke tells us the point of the story before it begins. Keep praying. Do not stop when the answer is slow. God is not like the unjust judge, who had to be worn down. He is a loving Father. But he does want us to keep coming to him, and to trust him while we wait.

KEY 18:13: 'And the publican, standing afar off, would not lift up so much as his eyes unto heaven, but smote upon his breast, saying, God be merciful to me a sinner.' A publican was a tax collector. He did not list his good deeds or compare himself with anyone. He simply asked for mercy. God be merciful to me a sinner. It may be the shortest and truest prayer in the Bible. Anyone can pray it, and God never turns away a person who prays it honestly.

KEY 18:27: 'And he said, The things which are impossible with men are possible with God.' The disciples were shocked. If a good, rich, commandment-keeping man could not get into the kingdom, who could? Jesus’ answer: no one, by their own effort. Salvation is impossible with men. But it is possible with God. And right after this, at Jericho, a blind beggar who had nothing to offer received his sight and followed Jesus.

THREAD: Long before, David prayed after his great sin, 'The sacrifices of God are a broken spirit: a broken and a contrite heart, O God, thou wilt not despise.' Contrite means truly sorry. The tax collector came with exactly that kind of heart, and God did not despise him. The Pharisee came with a list of his achievements, and went home unchanged.

TODAY: Is there something you have stopped praying about because the answer has been slow? Start again. And pray the tax collector’s prayer today, slowly and honestly: God be merciful to me a sinner.

QUESTION: The tax collector prayed, God be merciful to me a sinner. What do you need God’s mercy for today?

PRAYER: God, be merciful to me a sinner. I have nothing to boast of before you. Thank you that what is impossible with men is possible with you. Help me to keep praying and not lose heart, and to come to you like a little child. Amen.
