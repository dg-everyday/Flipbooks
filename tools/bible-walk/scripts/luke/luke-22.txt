# Luke 22: Judas, the Passover, the greatest is the servant, Peter warned, the Mount of Olives, the arrest, Peter's denial. Keys 22:32, 22:42, 22:61.
TITLE: Not My Will, but Thine, Be Done

HOOK: In a courtyard, by a fire in the dark, Peter is warming his hands. Three times, people look at him and say, you were with Jesus. Three times he says, I do not know him. And as he speaks the third time, a rooster crows. At that very moment, Jesus, standing nearby under guard, turns and looks straight at Peter.

WHERE: In chapter twenty-one, Jesus praised the poor widow and taught his disciples to watch and pray, and to lift up their heads when trouble comes. Now, in chapter twenty-two, the feast of the Passover draws near, the feast when Israel remembered how God set them free from Egypt, and how the blood of the lamb protected them. The chief priests are looking for a way to kill Jesus without causing a riot. This is a long chapter, and it moves quickly from a supper table, to a garden, to a courtyard. Watch the faithfulness of Jesus, and the failure of his friends.

STORY: Satan enters into Judas Iscariot, one of the twelve. He goes to the chief priests and captains, and they are glad, and agree to give him money. From then on, Judas looks for a chance to betray Jesus when no crowd is around.

On the day when the Passover lamb must be killed, Jesus sends Peter and John to prepare the meal. A man carrying a pitcher of water will meet you, he says. Follow him, and he will show you a large upper room, ready. They find it just as he said.

When the hour comes, Jesus sits down with the apostles and says, 'With desire I have desired to eat this passover with you before I suffer'. He takes bread, gives thanks, breaks it, and gives it to them: 'This is my body which is given for you: this do in remembrance of me.' After supper he takes the cup: 'This cup is the new testament in my blood, which is shed for you.' Testament here means covenant, the new covenant God promised through Jeremiah. Then Jesus says that the one who will betray him is at the table with him.

Even at that table, the disciples argue about which of them is the greatest. Jesus tells them, among the nations, kings lord it over people. But it will not be so with you. 'I am among you as he that serveth.'

Then Jesus speaks to Peter by his old name. 'Simon, Simon, behold, Satan hath desired to have you, that he may sift you as wheat'. Peter says, Lord, I am ready to go with you to prison and to death. And Jesus tells him that before the rooster crows that day, he will deny three times that he knows him.

They go out to the Mount of Olives, as Jesus often did. He tells them, 'Pray that ye enter not into temptation.' He goes a stone’s throw away, kneels down, and prays. An angel from heaven appears to strengthen him. And being in agony, he prays more earnestly, and his sweat falls like great drops of blood to the ground. Luke the doctor is the only Gospel writer who tells us this. When he gets up and comes to his disciples, he finds them asleep, worn out with sorrow.

While he is still speaking, a crowd comes, led by Judas, who comes near to kiss him. Jesus says, 'Judas, betrayest thou the Son of man with a kiss?' One of the disciples strikes the servant of the high priest and cuts off his right ear. Jesus says, 'Suffer ye thus far', and he touches the man’s ear and heals him. Even while being arrested, Jesus heals his enemy. Then he says to the priests and captains, 'this is your hour, and the power of darkness.'

They take him to the high priest’s house. Peter follows at a distance, and sits by a fire in the courtyard. A servant girl looks at him closely: this man was with him. Peter says, 'Woman, I know him not.' A little later another says, you are one of them. 'Man, I am not.' About an hour after, someone else insists, surely this man was with him, for he is from Galilee. Peter says, man, I do not know what you are saying. And immediately, while he is still speaking, the rooster crows.

Then Peter remembers the word of the Lord. And he goes out, and weeps bitterly.

The men guarding Jesus mock him and beat him. They blindfold him and say, prophesy, who hit you? When the council of elders meets, they ask him, 'Art thou the Christ? tell us.' And at last, 'Art thou then the Son of God?' He answers, 'Ye say that I am.' And they say, we need no more witnesses; we have heard it from his own mouth.

KEY 22:32: 'But I have prayed for thee, that thy faith fail not: and when thou art converted, strengthen thy brethren.' Jesus knew Peter would fall. And before it happened, he prayed for him. Notice the word when, not if. When thou art converted, when you turn back. Jesus already planned for Peter’s restoration, and gave him work to do afterward: strengthen your brothers. Jesus prays for you too, even when you fall.

KEY 22:42: 'Saying, Father, if thou be willing, remove this cup from me: nevertheless not my will, but thine, be done.' The cup was the suffering ahead, and the weight of the world’s sin. Jesus was honest about his anguish. He asked if there was another way. And then he chose his Father’s will. This is the prayer that saved us. And it is the prayer we can pray in our own hardest moments: not my will, but thine, be done.

KEY 22:61: 'And the Lord turned, and looked upon Peter. And Peter remembered the word of the Lord, how he had said unto him, Before the cock crow, thou shalt deny me thrice.' Only Luke tells us about this look. What was in Jesus’ eyes? Not anger, but sorrow and love. That look broke Peter’s heart, and he went out and wept. When we fail Jesus, he does not turn away from us. He looks at us with love, and that love calls us back.

THREAD: At the table Jesus said, 'this that is written must yet be accomplished in me, And he was reckoned among the transgressors'. He was quoting Isaiah’s song of the suffering servant, who 'was numbered with the transgressors; and he bare the sin of many'. Jesus knew exactly what was written about him, and walked into it willingly.

TODAY: Is there a cup in front of you, something hard you would rather avoid? Be honest with God about it, and then pray Jesus’ prayer. And if you have failed him, like Peter, do not run away. He has prayed for you, and he looks at you with love.

QUESTION: Jesus prayed, not my will, but thine, be done. Where in your life will you pray that prayer today?

PRAYER: Father, not my will, but thine, be done. Thank you, Lord Jesus, that you drank the cup for me, that you pray for me when I am weak, and that you look on me with love when I fail. Strengthen my faith, and use me to strengthen others. Amen.
