# Mark 2: the roof opened, Levi called, new wine, the sabbath. Keys 2:5, 2:17, 2:27.
TITLE: Thy Sins Be Forgiven Thee

HOOK: Four men are carrying their friend on a mat. He cannot walk. They have heard that Jesus is in a house in Capernaum, but when they get there, the crowd is so thick that no one can even reach the door. Most people would turn around and go home. These four climb up onto the roof, and they start to dig. And while Jesus is preaching inside, pieces of the roof begin to fall, and light pours in from above.

WHERE: In chapter one, John the Baptist prepared the way, Jesus called four fishermen to follow him, and he touched and healed a man with leprosy. News about him spread so fast that he could hardly go into a town. Now, after some days, he is back in Capernaum, the fishing town by the lake where Simon and Andrew live.

In this chapter something new begins. The crowds love Jesus, but the religious teachers start asking questions. Listen for the word why. Again and again in this chapter, someone asks Jesus why.

STORY: The house is full. People are packed inside and crowded around the door, and Jesus is preaching the word to them. Then the four friends arrive with a man 'sick of the palsy', which means he was paralysed. They cannot get near, so they go up on the flat roof, break it open, and let down the bed with their friend lying on it, right in front of Jesus.

Jesus looks up at the four faces in the hole in the roof. Mark says, 'When Jesus saw their faith'. Then he looks at the man on the mat and says something no one expected: 'Son, thy sins be forgiven thee.'

Some scribes are sitting there, teachers of the law. They say nothing out loud, but in their hearts they are thinking, 'Why doth this man thus speak blasphemies? who can forgive sins but God only?' Jesus knows what they are thinking. He asks them which is easier: to say, thy sins be forgiven, or to say, arise and walk? Anyone can say the first, because no one can see whether it happened. So Jesus does the second, so they will know the first is true. 'But that ye may know that the Son of man hath power on earth to forgive sins', he turns to the man and says, 'Arise, and take up thy bed, and go thy way into thine house.' And the man stands up, picks up his bed, and walks out in front of them all. Everyone is amazed. 'We never saw it on this fashion,' they say. We have never seen anything like this.

Then Jesus walks by the lake and sees a man named Levi sitting at his tax booth. In those days tax collectors worked for the Romans, many of them cheated, and people hated them. Jesus says two words: 'Follow me.' And Levi gets up and follows him.

Soon Jesus is eating at Levi’s house, and the table is full of tax collectors and sinners. The scribes and Pharisees see it and ask his disciples, 'How is it that he eateth and drinketh with publicans and sinners?' Publicans means tax collectors. Jesus hears them, and he answers with a saying about doctors.

Then people ask why his disciples do not fast, going without food to pray, as the followers of John and of the Pharisees do. Jesus says guests do not fast at a wedding while the bridegroom is with them. But the days will come when the bridegroom will be taken away. Jesus is the bridegroom, and already he is hinting that he will be taken away. Then he says no one sews a new patch on an old coat, and no one puts new wine into old wineskins, or they will burst. 'But new wine must be put into new bottles.' What Jesus brings is new. It will not fit inside old ways.

Last, on the sabbath, the day of rest, the disciples walk through a field of grain and pick some heads of it to eat. The Pharisees say, 'why do they on the sabbath day that which is not lawful?' Jesus reminds them that David once ate the holy bread from the house of God when he and his men were hungry. Then he gives them a saying to think about.

KEY 2:5: 'When Jesus saw their faith, he said unto the sick of the palsy, Son, thy sins be forgiven thee.' Notice whose faith Jesus saw: their faith. The faith of four friends who would not give up, who carried, and climbed, and dug. Faith is not only what we believe. Sometimes faith has sore arms and dusty hands. And notice what Jesus gives first. The man came for his legs, but Jesus started with his heart. Our deepest need is not always the one others can see. It is to be made right with God. Jesus gave this man both, and he called him son.

KEY 2:17: 'They that are whole have no need of the physician, but they that are sick: I came not to call the righteous, but sinners to repentance.' The Pharisees thought Jesus was in the wrong company. Jesus said he was exactly where a doctor should be: beside the sick. No one is too far gone to sit at his table. But this verse also carries a warning. Only those who know they are sick go to the doctor. People who think they are already good enough feel no need of him. The first step toward Jesus is to be honest and say, Lord, I need you.

KEY 2:27: 'And he said unto them, The sabbath was made for man, and not man for the sabbath:' God gave the day of rest as a gift, to bless people, not to weigh them down. The Pharisees had added so many rules that the gift had become a burden, and they cared more about their rules than about hungry men. Then Jesus goes further. He says, 'the Son of man is Lord also of the sabbath.' The one who gave the day of rest is standing in that field with them. True rest is found in him.

THREAD: The scribes asked, who can forgive sins but God only? They were right about that. Long before, God had said through the prophet Isaiah, 'I, even I, am he that blotteth out thy transgressions for mine own sake, and will not remember thy sins.' Only God can forgive sins. So when Jesus forgives this man, and then proves it by making him walk, Mark wants us to ask once more the question of his whole book: who is this?

TODAY: Think about the four friends. Is there someone you could carry to Jesus? You might carry them by praying for them by name, by inviting them, or by helping them through a hard week. And if you are the one who cannot walk right now, let others carry you. Let them pray for you.

QUESTION: The four friends would not give up until they brought their friend to Jesus. Who is one person you will carry to Jesus in prayer today?

PRAYER: Lord Jesus, thank you that you came for the sick, and not only for those who think they are well. Forgive my sins, as you forgave the man on the mat. Give me friends who carry me, and make me a friend who carries others to you. Amen.
