# Mark 5: Legion, the woman who touched his garment, Jairus' daughter raised. Keys 5:19, 5:34, 5:36.
TITLE: Be Not Afraid, Only Believe

HOOK: A father falls at the feet of Jesus. His little daughter is dying. Jesus sets off with him at once. But on the way, in the middle of a pushing crowd, Jesus stops, and asks a strange question: who touched my clothes? Precious minutes pass. And then the messengers arrive from the father’s house with the words every parent fears: 'Thy daughter is dead'.

WHERE: In chapter four, Jesus calmed a storm on the lake, and his friends asked, what manner of man is this? Now they reach the other side, the country of the Gadarenes, on the eastern shore, where many of the people were not Jews.

In this chapter Jesus meets three people no one else could help: a man with a legion of devils, a woman who had been sick for twelve years, and a girl of twelve who dies. Watch how Jesus is Lord over evil spirits, over sickness, and even over death.

STORY: As soon as Jesus steps out of the boat, a man comes running from the tombs. He lives among the graves. People had tried to bind him with chains, but he tore them apart. Mark says, 'And always, night and day, he was in the mountains, and in the tombs, crying, and cutting himself with stones.' When he sees Jesus, he runs and falls down before him, and the spirit cries out, 'What have I to do with thee, Jesus, thou Son of the most high God?' Jesus asks, 'What is thy name?' He answers, 'My name is Legion: for we are many.' In those days a legion was a Roman army of thousands of soldiers.

Nearby, a great herd of pigs is feeding, about two thousand of them. The spirits beg to be sent into the pigs, and Jesus lets them go. The whole herd rushes down the steep bank into the sea. The men who were feeding the pigs run and tell the town, and people come out to see. They find the wild man 'sitting, and clothed, and in his right mind: and they were afraid.' And they beg Jesus to leave.

As Jesus gets into the boat, the healed man begs to go with him. But Jesus sends him home with a task, and he goes all through the ten towns called Decapolis, telling what Jesus has done for him.

Back across the lake, a ruler of the synagogue named Jairus falls at Jesus’ feet. 'My little daughter lieth at the point of death', he says, and begs him to come and lay his hands on her. Jesus goes with him, and the crowd presses all around.

In that crowd is a woman who has been bleeding for twelve years. She has spent everything she had on doctors, and only grown worse. In those days, under the law, her sickness also made her unclean, cut off from others. She thinks, 'If I may touch but his clothes, I shall be whole.' She reaches through the crowd, touches his garment, and straightway she feels in her body that she is healed.

Jesus stops. 'Who touched my clothes?' His disciples are puzzled. The whole crowd is pressing against you, and you ask who touched you? But Jesus keeps looking around. At last the woman comes, fearing and trembling. She falls down before him and tells him all the truth. And Jesus speaks to her gently.

While he is still speaking, men arrive from the house of Jairus. Your daughter is dead, they say. Why trouble the Master any further? Jesus hears it, and turns straight to the father.

At the house there is loud weeping and wailing. Jesus says, 'the damsel is not dead, but sleepeth.' And they laugh at him. He puts them all out. He takes only the father and mother, and Peter, James and John, into the room where she is lying. He takes her by the hand and says, 'Talitha cumi'. Little girl, I say unto thee, arise. Straightway the girl gets up and walks. Everyone is astonished. And Jesus tells them to give her something to eat.

KEY 5:19: 'Howbeit Jesus suffered him not, but saith unto him, Go home to thy friends, and tell them how great things the Lord hath done for thee, and hath had compassion on thee.' Suffered him not means he did not let him. That seems strange. The man wanted to follow Jesus, and Jesus sent him home. But the people of that country had just asked Jesus to leave. So Jesus left a witness behind. Your first mission field may be your own home and your own friends. You do not need to be a preacher. You only need to tell how great things the Lord has done for you.

KEY 5:34: 'And he said unto her, Daughter, thy faith hath made thee whole; go in peace, and be whole of thy plague.' She was already healed, so why did Jesus stop and look for her? Because he wanted to heal more than her body. In front of the whole crowd, the woman no one would touch is called daughter. She came hiding, and she goes in peace. Jesus does not want us to slip away with a blessing. He wants to meet us face to face.

KEY 5:36: 'As soon as Jesus heard the word that was spoken, he saith unto the ruler of the synagogue, Be not afraid, only believe.' Jesus speaks to Jairus before fear can take hold of him. Only believe: keep trusting me, even now, when the news is the worst it can be. The father’s faith was small and shaken, but it was enough to keep walking beside Jesus to the house. And at the end of that road, death itself obeyed.

THREAD: A psalm describes people bound in darkness and chains, who cried to the LORD in their trouble. And it says, 'He brought them out of darkness and the shadow of death, and brake their bands in sunder.' In this chapter we see it happen. A man’s chains are broken, a woman’s long trouble ends, and a little girl is brought back from the shadow of death.

TODAY: Maybe you feel like the man among the tombs, or the woman in the crowd, or the father who has heard bad news. Bring your trouble to Jesus, plainly and honestly. And then, like the man sent home, tell someone what the Lord has done for you.

QUESTION: Jesus told the man to go home and tell how great things the Lord had done for him. Who will you tell today about something God has done for you?

PRAYER: Lord Jesus, you are stronger than every chain, every sickness, and even death. When the news is bad, help me to hear your voice: be not afraid, only believe. Thank you for your compassion on me. Amen.
