# Mark 12: the wicked husbandmen, Caesar's coin, the resurrection, the greatest commandment, the widow's mites. Keys 12:17, 12:30, 12:43.
TITLE: Thou Shalt Love the Lord Thy God

HOOK: Rich people are dropping large gifts into the temple treasury, and the coins ring loudly as they fall. Then a poor widow comes. Quietly she drops in two tiny copper coins, the smallest money there was. Nobody notices her. Except Jesus. He calls his disciples over and tells them that she has given more than anyone else there.

WHERE: In chapter eleven, Jesus rode into Jerusalem while the crowds shouted Hosanna, and he drove the buyers and sellers out of the temple. The leaders asked him, by what authority doest thou these things?

Now, in chapter twelve, Jesus is still teaching in the temple, and one group after another comes to trap him with a hard question. The Pharisees and Herodians, the Sadducees, and a scribe. But no one can catch him. By the end, no one dares to ask him anything more. And then Jesus notices the widow.

STORY: Jesus begins with a parable. A man plants a vineyard, rents it out to farmers, and goes away. At harvest time he sends a servant to collect some of the fruit. The farmers beat him and send him away with nothing. He sends another, and another. They beat some and kill others. At last he has one son left, his wellbeloved. He sends him too, saying, 'They will reverence my son.' But the farmers say, 'This is the heir; come, let us kill him, and the inheritance shall be ours.' They kill him and throw him out of the vineyard.

What will the owner do? He will come and destroy them, and give the vineyard to others. Then Jesus quotes a psalm: 'The stone which the builders rejected is become the head of the corner'. The leaders know he has told this story against them. They want to arrest him, but they are afraid of the people.

Next, Pharisees and Herodians come with flattering words, and then their trap: 'Is it lawful to give tribute to Caesar, or not?' If Jesus says yes, the people will hate him. If he says no, the Romans will arrest him. Jesus asks for a coin. 'Whose is this image and superscription?' Caesar’s, they say. And Jesus gives his answer, and they marvel at him.

Then come the Sadducees, who say there is no resurrection. They tell a story about a woman who married seven brothers, one after another, as each one died. In the resurrection, they ask, whose wife will she be? Jesus says, 'Do ye not therefore err, because ye know not the scriptures, neither the power of God?' In the resurrection people do not marry; they are like the angels. And God said to Moses at the bush, I am the God of Abraham, Isaac and Jacob, long after they had died. 'He is not the God of the dead, but the God of the living'.

Then a scribe, who has heard Jesus answer well, asks a real question: 'Which is the first commandment of all?' Jesus answers. And the scribe agrees: to love God and our neighbour 'is more than all whole burnt offerings and sacrifices.' Jesus tells him, 'Thou art not far from the kingdom of God.' And after that no one dares ask him any more questions.

Then Jesus asks a question of his own. The scribes say the Christ is the Son of David. But in a psalm, David calls him Lord: 'The Lord said to my Lord, Sit thou on my right hand'. How can he be David’s son and David’s Lord? The common people hear him gladly. And he warns them about scribes who love long robes, greetings in the market, and the best seats, while they take the houses of widows and pray long prayers for show.

And then he sits down opposite the treasury, and watches the people giving, and sees the poor widow with her two mites.

KEY 12:17: 'And Jesus answering said unto them, Render to Caesar the things that are Caesar’s, and to God the things that are God’s. And they marvelled at him.' The coin had Caesar’s image on it, so give it back to Caesar. Pay what you owe to those who rule. But there is something deeper here. What carries God’s image? We do. The Bible says God made people in his own image. So give God what bears his image: yourself. Caesar may have your coins. God wants you.

KEY 12:30: 'And thou shalt love the Lord thy God with all thy heart, and with all thy soul, and with all thy mind, and with all thy strength: this is the first commandment.' Jesus did not choose a small rule. He chose the one that holds all the others together: love God with everything you have. Heart, soul, mind and strength: your feelings, your life, your thinking, your doing. And then he added a second, which goes with it: 'Thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself.' Love for God always overflows into love for people.

KEY 12:43: 'And he called unto him his disciples, and saith unto them, Verily I say unto you, That this poor widow hath cast more in, than all they which have cast into the treasury:' How can two tiny coins be more? Because the rich gave out of what they had left over. She gave all she had to live on. God does not measure our gifts by their size, but by what they cost us and the love behind them. The widow did with her money what the first commandment asks: she loved God with everything.

THREAD: The verse Jesus quoted about the rejected stone comes from a psalm that says, 'The stone which the builders refused is become the head stone of the corner.' The leaders of Israel were the builders. They were about to throw away the stone God had chosen, just as the farmers threw out the son. But God would make that rejected stone the most important stone of all. Jesus was telling them what was about to happen to him, and what God would do after.

TODAY: The widow gave all she had, and no one but Jesus noticed. He notices you too. Your small, hidden acts of love count with him. Think about the first commandment. Is there part of you, your heart, your mind, your time, your money, that you are holding back from God?

QUESTION: Jesus said, love the Lord thy God with all thy heart, soul, mind and strength. Which part of your life will you give to God more fully today?

PRAYER: Lord my God, you are one Lord, and you deserve all of me. Help me to love you with all my heart, soul, mind and strength, and to love my neighbour as myself. Like the widow, let me give you all, even when no one else sees. Amen.

NEXT: Next on the walk, Mark chapter thirteen. As they leave the temple, Jesus tells his disciples that its great stones will all be thrown down, and on the Mount of Olives he teaches them to watch and be ready for his return.
