# Matthew 5: the Sermon on the Mount begins; the Beatitudes; salt and light; not to destroy but to fulfil; righteousness exceeding the scribes; anger and reconciliation; the heart (5:27-32 lust, adultery and divorce told in one gentle line, not quoted; eye and hand as a picture, not quoted); plain yea and nay; the other cheek and the second mile; love your enemies. Differs from bp-matthew-05 (keys 5:3, 5:14, 5:44). Keys 5:8, 5:16, 5:48.
TITLE: Blessed Are the Pure in Heart

HOOK: Who are the blessed people in this world? Most of us would say the rich, the strong, the popular, the ones who always win. Then Jesus sits down on a hillside, looks at the crowd, and turns all of that upside down. Blessed are the poor. Blessed are those who cry. Blessed are the gentle. And the people listening have never heard anything like it.

WHERE: In chapter four, Jesus was tempted in the wilderness and answered the devil with God’s Word. Then he began to preach, repent, for the kingdom of heaven is at hand. He called four fishermen to follow him, and great crowds came from everywhere.

Now, in chapter five, Jesus begins his most famous teaching, the Sermon on the Mount. It runs through chapters five, six and seven. Listen for the kind of people Jesus calls blessed. And listen for how he takes God’s law deeper, all the way into the heart.

STORY: When Jesus sees the crowds, he goes up on a mountain and sits down, as teachers did in those days. His disciples gather around him, and he begins to teach.

He starts with blessings. Blessed means truly happy, deeply well, under God’s favour. 'Blessed are the poor in spirit: for theirs is the kingdom of heaven.' Those who know they need God. 'Blessed are they that mourn: for they shall be comforted.' 'Blessed are the meek: for they shall inherit the earth.' Meek means gentle, not pushing yourself forward. 'Blessed are they which do hunger and thirst after righteousness: for they shall be filled.' Those who long to do what is right. 'Blessed are the merciful: for they shall obtain mercy.'

And then comes another blessing.

'Blessed are the peacemakers: for they shall be called the children of God.' And blessed are those who are treated badly because they follow Jesus, for theirs is the kingdom of heaven.

Then Jesus tells his followers who they are. 'Ye are the salt of the earth'. Salt gave food flavour and kept it from going bad. 'Ye are the light of the world.' Nobody lights a lamp and then hides it under a basket. They put it on a stand, so it gives light to everyone in the house.

And Jesus tells them what to do with their light.

Next, Jesus speaks about God’s law. 'Think not that I am come to destroy the law, or the prophets: I am not come to destroy, but to fulfil.' Jesus did not come to throw away the Old Testament. He came to bring it to its full meaning.

Then he says something startling. Unless your righteousness is greater than that of the scribes and Pharisees, you will not enter the kingdom of heaven. The Pharisees were very careful to keep the rules. How could anyone be more righteous than them? Jesus shows what he means. God cares not only about what we do, but about what is in our hearts.

You have heard, do not kill. But I say, whoever is angry with his brother without a cause, or calls him a fool, is in danger of judgment. If you are bringing a gift to God, and you remember that someone has something against you, leave your gift. 'first be reconciled to thy brother, and then come and offer thy gift.'

In the same way, Jesus says that sin begins with the wrong desires of the heart, long before anything is done. He speaks of marriage as a promise to be kept, not thrown away. He uses strong pictures to show how seriously we should turn away from sin.

Do not make big oaths to prove you are telling the truth. Just let your yes be yes, and your no be no.

You have heard, an eye for an eye. But I say, if someone strikes you on one cheek, turn the other also. If someone forces you to carry his load one mile, go with him two. Give to the one who asks.

You have heard, love your neighbour and hate your enemy. 'But I say unto you, Love your enemies, bless them that curse you, do good to them that hate you, and pray for them which despitefully use you, and persecute you'. Then you will be like your Father in heaven, who sends sunshine and rain on good and bad people alike. Even tax collectors love those who love them back.

And Jesus ends this part of his sermon with a call that sounds impossible.

KEY 5:8: 'Blessed are the pure in heart: for they shall see God.' Pure in heart means having a heart that is clean and undivided, that truly wants God, and does not pretend. Many people look good on the outside. But God looks at the heart. And the promise is the greatest of all. They shall see God. No one can make their own heart pure. But David prayed, create in me a clean heart, O God. That is a prayer God loves to answer.

KEY 5:16: 'Let your light so shine before men, that they may see your good works, and glorify your Father which is in heaven.' Our light is not for showing off. When people see us being kind, honest and forgiving, the goal is that they will praise God, not us. A lamp does not shine for itself. It shines so others can see. Every small good thing done in Jesus’ name lets a little more of his light into the world.

KEY 5:48: 'Be ye therefore perfect, even as your Father which is in heaven is perfect.' Perfect here means complete, whole, grown up into the likeness of our Father. Jesus has just said that God loves even his enemies. So to be perfect like him means to love like him, without leaving anyone out. None of us can do this on our own. That is why the sermon began with the poor in spirit, who know they need God. Jesus sets the goal high, and then gives us his help to grow towards it.

THREAD: David prayed, 'Create in me a clean heart, O God; and renew a right spirit within me.' And the prophet Micah asked, 'what doth the LORD require of thee, but to do justly, and to love mercy, and to walk humbly with thy God?'

TODAY: Which of the blessings in this chapter speaks to you most? Maybe you are mourning, and need God’s comfort. Maybe you need to make peace with someone. Choose one thing Jesus said today, and ask him to help you live it.

QUESTION: Jesus said, love your enemies, and pray for those who treat you badly. Who is one person you find hard to love, that you could pray for this week?

PRAYER: Lord Jesus, thank you that your kingdom belongs to the poor in spirit, who know they need you. Create in me a clean heart. Help me to make peace, to love even my enemies, and to let your light shine, so that others may praise our Father in heaven. Amen.
