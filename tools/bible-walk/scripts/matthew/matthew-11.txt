# Matthew 11: John in prison asks, art thou he that should come (Isaiah 35 and 61 signs); blessed is he not offended; more than a prophet (Malachi 3:1), Elijah; children in the market (piped and mourned); woes on the cities (11:23 "hell" not quoted, told briefly); revealed unto babes; the Father and the Son; come unto me, my yoke is easy. 11:12 skipped. Keys 11:5, 11:28, 11:29.
TITLE: Come unto Me, All Ye That Labour

HOOK: A man sits in a dark prison cell. Not long ago, he stood by the river and pointed at Jesus, saying, he is the one. Crowds listened to him. Now he is locked away, and he is beginning to wonder. Was I right? Is Jesus really the one? So he sends his friends with a question. And Jesus does not scold him. He sends back an answer.

WHERE: In chapter ten, Jesus chose twelve apostles and sent them out to preach and heal. He told them, freely ye have received, freely give. He warned them of hard times, but said, fear not, for you are of more value than many sparrows.

Now, in chapter eleven, John the Baptist has doubts, some cities will not listen, and Jesus speaks a beautiful invitation. Listen for how Jesus answers someone who doubts. And listen for who Jesus invites to come to him.

STORY: John the Baptist was in prison. King Herod had put him there, as we will hear later. In prison, John hears about the things Jesus is doing. So he sends two of his disciples to ask, 'Art thou he that should come, or do we look for another?'

John had expected the Messiah to come with fire and judgment. Instead, Jesus was healing and teaching quietly. And John was still in prison. It is not hard to understand why he wondered.

Jesus does not answer yes or no. He says, go and tell John what you hear and see.

Isaiah had promised that when God came to save, the blind would see, the deaf would hear, and the lame would leap. John knew those words. Jesus was saying, look at what is happening. The promises are coming true. 'And blessed is he, whosoever shall not be offended in me.' Blessed is the one who does not stumble because I am not what he expected.

When John’s disciples leave, Jesus speaks to the crowd about John. What did you go out into the wilderness to see? A reed blowing in the wind? A man in soft, fine clothes? No, those are in kings’ palaces. A prophet? Yes, and more than a prophet. Jesus says John was the messenger Malachi promised, sent to prepare the way. Among all people ever born, there has been no one greater than John the Baptist. Yet the least in the kingdom of heaven is greater than he. And Jesus says that John is the Elijah who was to come.

Jesus says this generation is like children playing in the market, who complain to their friends, we played happy music for you, and you would not dance. We played sad music, and you would not cry. John came living simply, fasting, and people said, he has a devil. Jesus came eating and drinking with people, and they said, look, he is a glutton, 'a friend of publicans and sinners.' Some people will find fault with anything, rather than listen.

Then Jesus speaks sadly about the towns where he had done most of his mighty works, like Chorazin, Bethsaida and Capernaum, because they did not repent. They had seen so much, and still turned away.

Then Jesus prays aloud. 'I thank thee, O Father, Lord of heaven and earth, because thou hast hid these things from the wise and prudent, and hast revealed them unto babes.' The proud and clever did not understand. But humble, childlike people did.

Jesus says, all things have been given to me by my Father. No one truly knows the Son except the Father, and no one truly knows the Father except the Son, and those to whom the Son chooses to show him.

And then Jesus turns to the crowd, and gives an invitation.

He goes on with a picture.

'For my yoke is easy, and my burden is light.'

KEY 11:5: 'The blind receive their sight, and the lame walk, the lepers are cleansed, and the deaf hear, the dead are raised up, and the poor have the gospel preached to them.' Jesus answered John’s doubt with evidence. He did not say, stop doubting. He said, look at what I am doing, and remember what the prophets promised. When we have doubts, we can do what John did. Bring our questions honestly to Jesus. And look again at what he has done.

KEY 11:28: 'Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest.' Labour means working hard until you are tired. Heavy laden means carrying a heavy load. Many people in Jesus’ day were tired out by trying to keep hundreds of religious rules. Others were worn out by hard work, sorrow or guilt. Jesus does not say, try harder. He says, come to me. And he promises rest. Not just a break, but rest for the soul.

KEY 11:29: 'Take my yoke upon you, and learn of me; for I am meek and lowly in heart: and ye shall find rest unto your souls.' A yoke was a wooden frame that joined two oxen together, so they could pull a plough side by side. Jesus invites us to be joined to him, and walk beside him, learning from him. And he tells us what he is like. Meek and lowly, which means gentle and humble. He is not a harsh master. He is the kindest teacher there could ever be.

THREAD: Isaiah wrote, 'Then the eyes of the blind shall be opened, and the ears of the deaf shall be unstopped.' And Jeremiah heard God say, 'ask for the old paths, where is the good way, and walk therein, and ye shall find rest for your souls.'

TODAY: Are you tired, or carrying something heavy today? Jesus says, come to me. Tell him what you are carrying. And if you have doubts, do what John did. Bring your question to Jesus, and look again at what he has done.

QUESTION: Jesus said, come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden. What is one heavy thing you could bring to him today?

PRAYER: Lord Jesus, thank you that you are gentle and humble in heart. Thank you that I can bring you my doubts and my tiredness. I come to you now, with everything I am carrying. Teach me to walk beside you, and give me rest for my soul. Amen.
