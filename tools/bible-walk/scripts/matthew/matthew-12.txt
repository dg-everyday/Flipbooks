# Matthew 12: plucking corn on the sabbath (David and the shewbread; one greater than the temple; mercy not sacrifice; Lord of the sabbath; sabbath told as Scripture tells it, no weekday applied today); the withered hand; the Pharisees plot; the gentle servant (Isaiah 42:1-4; bruised reed); Beelzebub, the divided kingdom, the strong man; blasphemy against the Holy Ghost ("different ways" and a pastoral word); the tree and its fruit, words; the sign of Jonas ("three days" only; 12:39 "adulterous" not quoted); greater than Solomon; the empty house; his mother and his brethren (no comment on who they were). Keys 12:7, 12:20, 12:41.
TITLE: A Bruised Reed Shall He Not Break

HOOK: A reed is a tall, thin plant that grows by the water. If one gets bent and cracked, most people would just snap it off and throw it away. A lamp wick that is only smoking, with no flame left, most people would pinch out. But Matthew says that Jesus is not like that. He does not throw away the cracked and the nearly burnt out. And in this chapter, we see why that matters.

WHERE: In chapter eleven, John the Baptist sent a question from prison, art thou he that should come? Jesus answered by pointing to what he was doing. Then he gave a loving invitation: come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest.

Now, in chapter twelve, the Pharisees grow angry with Jesus, and begin to plot against him. Listen for what Jesus says about mercy. And listen for the sign he promises.

STORY: One sabbath day, Jesus and his disciples walk through a field of grain. The disciples are hungry, so they pick some heads of grain and eat them. The Pharisees say, look, your disciples are doing what is not lawful on the sabbath. The sabbath was the day of rest God gave Israel. The Pharisees had made many extra rules about it, and picking grain counted as work.

Jesus answers, have you not read what David did when he was hungry? He went into the house of God and ate the holy bread that only the priests could eat. And the priests in the temple work on the sabbath, and are blameless. 'But I say unto you, That in this place is one greater than the temple.'

Then he quotes the prophet Hosea to them, as he did before.

'For the Son of man is Lord even of the sabbath day.' The one who made the day of rest knows what it is for.

Jesus goes into the synagogue, and there is a man with a withered, shrivelled hand. The Pharisees ask, to trap him, is it lawful to heal on the sabbath? Jesus says, if one of your sheep falls into a pit on the sabbath, will you not lift it out? How much more valuable is a man than a sheep! 'Wherefore it is lawful to do well on the sabbath days.' Then he says to the man, 'Stretch forth thine hand.' The man stretches it out, and it is made whole, like the other.

Then the Pharisees go out and plot how they might destroy him. Jesus knows, and goes away quietly. Great crowds follow him, and he heals them all, but tells them not to make him known.

Matthew says this fulfilled Isaiah’s words about God’s chosen servant. 'Behold my servant, whom I have chosen; my beloved, in whom my soul is well pleased'. He will not quarrel or shout in the streets.

And then comes a beautiful promise.

People bring a man who is blind and cannot speak, troubled by a devil. Jesus heals him. The crowds ask, 'Is not this the son of David?' But the Pharisees say, he casts out devils by Beelzebub, the prince of devils.

Jesus answers, a kingdom divided against itself cannot stand. If Satan casts out Satan, how can his kingdom stand? 'But if I cast out devils by the Spirit of God, then the kingdom of God is come unto you.' No one can break into a strong man’s house and take his things unless he first ties up the strong man. Jesus was showing that he was stronger than the devil.

Then Jesus warns that every sin can be forgiven, but speaking against the Holy Ghost will not be forgiven. Christians have understood this warning in different ways. Many have understood it as hardening your heart so completely against God that you call his good work evil, as these Pharisees were doing. And many have said that someone who worries they have done this is showing a heart still tender towards God.

A tree is known by its fruit, Jesus says. 'for out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaketh.' Our words show what is in our hearts.

Some scribes and Pharisees say, Master, we want to see a sign from you. Jesus says, the only sign this generation will be given is the sign of the prophet Jonah. As Jonah was three days in the belly of the great fish, so the Son of man will be three days in the heart of the earth. Jesus was speaking of his death and resurrection.

And then he speaks of Nineveh.

The queen of the south, the queen of Sheba, came from far away to hear Solomon’s wisdom. 'and, behold, a greater than Solomon is here.'

Then his mother and his brothers come, wanting to speak with him. Jesus points to his disciples and says, 'Behold my mother and my brethren!' Whoever does the will of my Father in heaven is my brother, and sister, and mother.

KEY 12:7: 'But if ye had known what this meaneth, I will have mercy, and not sacrifice, ye would not have condemned the guiltless.' This is the second time in Matthew that Jesus quotes these words from Hosea. The Pharisees knew the rules very well, but they had missed the heart of God. They cared more about the rule than about hungry men. Jesus says, if you understood God’s mercy, you would not have condemned people who did nothing wrong. Rules matter. But mercy matters more.

KEY 12:20: 'A bruised reed shall he not break, and smoking flax shall he not quench, till he send forth judgment unto victory.' A bruised reed is a person who is hurt, cracked and weak. Smoking flax is a lamp wick whose flame has almost gone out, like someone whose faith is very small. Jesus does not snap off the weak, or pinch out the small flame. He is gentle with them, and helps them grow strong again. If you feel bruised or almost burnt out, Jesus is gentle with you.

KEY 12:41: 'The men of Nineveh shall rise in judgment with this generation, and shall condemn it: because they repented at the preaching of Jonas; and, behold, a greater than Jonas is here.' You heard Jonah’s story on this walk. The people of Nineveh were enemies of Israel, yet when Jonah preached, they turned to God. Jonah was a reluctant prophet who did no miracles there. Jesus is far greater than Jonah. If Nineveh repented at Jonah’s word, how much more should people listen to Jesus?

THREAD: Isaiah wrote of God’s servant, 'A bruised reed shall he not break, and the smoking flax shall he not quench'. And Jonah prayed, 'Salvation is of the LORD.'

TODAY: Do you feel like a bruised reed today, hurt or weak? Jesus is gentle with you. Or do you know someone who is struggling? Be gentle with them, as Jesus is. And remember that Jesus wants mercy more than show.

QUESTION: Jesus said, out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaketh. What do your words this week show about what is in your heart?

PRAYER: Lord Jesus, thank you that you do not break the bruised reed or put out the smoking flax. Thank you for your gentleness when I am weak. Teach me mercy, more than rules. Fill my heart with good things, so that my words will be good. Amen.
