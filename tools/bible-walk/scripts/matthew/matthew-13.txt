# Matthew 13: the third great teaching, parables by the sea; the sower and its meaning; why parables (Isaiah 6:9-10), blessed are your eyes; the wheat and the tares (explained, told without dwelling on the furnace); the mustard seed; the leaven; Psalm 78:2; the treasure hid in a field; the pearl of great price; the net; things new and old; rejected at Nazareth ("the carpenter's son"; 13:55-56 brethren not named). Keys 13:23, 13:31, 13:44.
TITLE: Treasure Hid in a Field

HOOK: A man is digging in a field that is not his own. Suddenly his spade hits something hard. He brushes away the dirt, and finds a box full of treasure. Quickly, he covers it up again. Then he runs home, sells everything he owns, every last thing, and buys that field. His neighbours think he has gone mad. But he is smiling all the way. Jesus said, the kingdom of heaven is like that.

WHERE: In chapter twelve, the Pharisees argued with Jesus about the sabbath, and plotted against him. Matthew showed us that Jesus is the gentle servant, who does not break a bruised reed. Jesus said that one greater than Jonah and greater than Solomon was here.

Now, in chapter thirteen, comes the third great teaching in Matthew. Jesus sits in a boat and tells parables, short stories from everyday life with a deeper meaning. Listen for what each story says about the kingdom of heaven. And listen for what kind of ground you want to be.

STORY: The same day, Jesus goes out and sits by the sea. Such a great crowd gathers that he gets into a boat and sits down, while the people stand on the shore. And he teaches them in parables.

A sower went out to sow seed. As he scattered it, some fell on the path, and the birds came and ate it. Some fell on rocky ground, with only a little soil. It sprang up quickly, but when the sun came out, it withered, because it had no root. Some fell among thorns, and the thorns grew up and choked it. But some fell on good ground, and brought forth fruit, a hundred times, sixty times, or thirty times what was sown. 'Who hath ears to hear, let him hear.'

The disciples ask, why do you speak to them in parables? Jesus says, the secrets of the kingdom have been given to you. Many people look but do not see, and hear but do not understand, as Isaiah said. Parables open the truth to those who want to know, and hide it from those who do not care. 'But blessed are your eyes, for they see: and your ears, for they hear.' Many prophets longed to see what you are seeing.

Then Jesus explains the sower. The seed is the word of the kingdom. The path is someone who hears it but does not understand, and the wicked one snatches it away. The rocky ground is someone who receives it with joy, but has no root, so when trouble comes, he gives up. The thorns are someone who hears, but the worries of this world and the love of riches choke the word.

And then he explains the good ground.

Next, Jesus tells of a man who sowed good seed in his field. But while everyone slept, an enemy came and sowed weeds among the wheat. When both came up, the servants asked, shall we pull up the weeds? No, said the owner, or you will pull up the wheat too. Let both grow together until the harvest. Then the weeds will be gathered and burned, and the wheat gathered into my barn. Later, Jesus explains that the field is the world, the enemy is the devil, and the harvest is the end of the age. For now, good and evil grow side by side. But one day God will put everything right. 'Then shall the righteous shine forth as the sun in the kingdom of their Father.'

Then Jesus tells a story about a tiny seed.

'The kingdom of heaven is like unto leaven, which a woman took, and hid in three measures of meal, till the whole was leavened.' Leaven is yeast. A little yeast, hidden in a big bowl of flour, slowly spreads through all of it, and makes the whole loaf rise. The kingdom works quietly, from the inside, changing everything it touches.

Matthew says Jesus spoke in parables to fulfil the psalm, 'I will open my mouth in parables'.

Then Jesus tells two short stories about finding something precious.

'Again, the kingdom of heaven is like unto a merchant man, seeking goodly pearls: Who, when he had found one pearl of great price, went and sold all that he had, and bought it.'

The kingdom is also like a net thrown into the sea, which gathers every kind of fish. When it is full, the fishermen sort the good from the bad. So it will be at the end of the age.

After these parables, Jesus goes to his home town, Nazareth. He teaches in their synagogue, and they are astonished. Where did he get this wisdom and these mighty works? 'Is not this the carpenter’s son?' They know his family. And they are offended. Jesus says, 'A prophet is not without honour, save in his own country, and in his own house.' And he did not do many mighty works there, because of their unbelief.

KEY 13:23: 'But he that received seed into the good ground is he that heareth the word, and understandeth it; which also beareth fruit, and bringeth forth, some an hundredfold, some sixty, some thirty.' The same seed fell on all four kinds of ground. The difference was the soil. Good ground hears the word, understands it, and lets it grow. Not everyone bears the same amount of fruit, and that is all right. What matters is that the word takes root. Every time you listen to God’s Word, you can ask him to make your heart good ground.

KEY 13:31: 'Another parable put he forth unto them, saying, The kingdom of heaven is like to a grain of mustard seed, which a man took, and sowed in his field:' A mustard seed is tiny, smaller than most seeds a farmer would sow. But it grows into a plant so big that birds come and rest in its branches. The kingdom began with one man from Nazareth and twelve ordinary followers. Today it has spread to every nation. Never despise small beginnings. God loves to grow great things from little seeds.

KEY 13:44: 'Again, the kingdom of heaven is like unto treasure hid in a field; the which when a man hath found, he hideth, and for joy thereof goeth and selleth all that he hath, and buyeth that field.' Notice the words, for joy. The man did not sell everything with a long face, as if it were a great sacrifice. He was glad, because he knew what he had found was worth far more. Knowing Jesus is like that. Whatever it costs to follow him, it is worth more than everything else put together.

THREAD: Isaiah heard God say, 'So shall my word be that goeth forth out of my mouth: it shall not return unto me void'. And Paul wrote, 'I count all things but loss for the excellency of the knowledge of Christ Jesus my Lord'.

TODAY: What kind of ground is your heart today? Are worries or busyness choking God’s Word? Ask God to make your heart good ground. And think about the treasure. Is there anything you are holding on to that matters more to you than Jesus?

QUESTION: The man sold everything for joy, to buy the field with the treasure. What makes Jesus worth more than anything else to you?

PRAYER: Lord Jesus, make my heart good ground for your Word, so that it takes root and bears fruit. Thank you that your kingdom grows from small beginnings. Help me to treasure you above everything, and to follow you with joy. Amen.
