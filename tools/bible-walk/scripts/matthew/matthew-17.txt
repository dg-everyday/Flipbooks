# Matthew 17: the transfiguration (Moses and Elias, three tabernacles, the bright cloud, hear ye him, Jesus only); Elias is come already (John); the boy with seizures ("lunatick" explained) and the disciples' failure; faith as a grain of mustard seed; the second prediction (17:23 not quoted: the DB line has a stray break); the temple tax and the coin in the fish. Keys 17:5, 17:8, 17:20.
TITLE: Hear Ye Him

HOOK: Three fishermen climb a high mountain with Jesus. At the top, they stop to rest. And then, before their eyes, Jesus begins to change. His face starts to shine like the sun. His clothes become as white as light. Two men appear beside him, men who lived hundreds of years before. And the fishermen are so amazed and afraid that they hardly know what to say.

WHERE: In chapter sixteen, Peter said to Jesus, thou art the Christ, the Son of the living God. Then Jesus began to tell his disciples that he must suffer, be killed, and be raised again the third day. He said that anyone who follows him must deny himself and take up his cross.

Now, in chapter seventeen, Jesus takes three disciples up a mountain, and shows them his glory. Then they come down to a crowd and a suffering boy. Listen for what the voice from heaven says. And listen for what Jesus says about faith.

STORY: Six days later, Jesus takes Peter, James and John up a high mountain by themselves. And there he 'was transfigured before them: and his face did shine as the sun, and his raiment was white as the light.' Transfigured means completely changed in appearance. For a moment, the disciples saw the glory that Jesus had always had, shining through.

And then Moses and Elijah appear, talking with him. Moses, who gave the law. Elijah, the great prophet. The law and the prophets, standing with Jesus.

Peter speaks up. 'Lord, it is good for us to be here'. If you want, I will make three shelters here, one for you, one for Moses, and one for Elijah. Peter wanted to hold on to the moment, and perhaps to honour all three men equally.

But while he is still speaking, a bright cloud covers them, and a voice speaks out of the cloud.

When the disciples hear it, they fall on their faces, terrified. But Jesus comes and touches them. 'Arise, and be not afraid.'

And when they look up, everything has changed again.

As they come down the mountain, Jesus tells them not to tell anyone what they saw, until he has risen from the dead. They ask, why do the scribes say Elijah must come first? Jesus says, Elijah has already come, and they did not recognise him. And the disciples understand he means John the Baptist.

When they reach the crowd at the bottom of the mountain, a man comes and kneels before Jesus. Lord, have mercy on my son. He has terrible seizures, and suffers badly. He often falls into the fire, and into the water. I brought him to your disciples, and they could not heal him.

Jesus is grieved by their lack of faith. 'bring him hither to me.' Jesus rebukes the devil, it comes out of the boy, and the boy is healed from that very hour.

Later, the disciples ask Jesus privately, why could we not cast it out?

And Jesus tells them.

While they are in Galilee, Jesus tells them again that the Son of man will be betrayed, killed, and raised on the third day. And the disciples are deeply sad.

When they come to Capernaum, the men who collect the temple tax ask Peter, does your master not pay the tax? Peter says, yes. When Peter comes into the house, Jesus speaks first. Simon, do kings collect taxes from their own sons, or from others? From others, says Peter. Then the sons are free, Jesus says. Jesus was the Son of God, so he did not owe a tax for his Father’s house. But so as not to offend anyone, he tells Peter, go to the sea and cast a hook. Take the first fish that comes up. Open its mouth, and you will find a coin. Pay it for me and for you. Jesus not only knew there was a coin in a fish. He chose to pay what he did not owe, to keep the peace.

KEY 17:5: 'While he yet spake, behold, a bright cloud overshadowed them: and behold a voice out of the cloud, which said, This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased; hear ye him.' The Father said the same words he spoke at Jesus’ baptism, and then added three more. Hear ye him. Not Moses, not Elijah, but my Son. Peter wanted to make three shelters, as if all three were equal. God said, listen to Jesus. The law and the prophets pointed to him, and now he is here.

KEY 17:8: 'And when they had lifted up their eyes, they saw no man, save Jesus only.' Moses and Elijah were gone. The cloud was gone. The shining glory was gone. Only Jesus was left, the same Jesus who walked with them every day. The disciples would have to go back down the mountain, into ordinary life, and trouble, and a crowd. But they would not go alone. Jesus only. He is enough.

KEY 17:20: 'And Jesus said unto them, Because of your unbelief: for verily I say unto you, If ye have faith as a grain of mustard seed, ye shall say unto this mountain, Remove hence to yonder place; and it shall remove; and nothing shall be impossible unto you.' A mustard seed is tiny. Jesus does not say we need huge faith. He says even a tiny faith, if it is real faith in God, can do great things. The power is not in how big our faith is, but in how great our God is. A small faith in a great God can move mountains.

THREAD: Moses told the people, 'The LORD thy God will raise up unto thee a Prophet from the midst of thee, of thy brethren, like unto me; unto him ye shall hearken'. And Peter later wrote of that day, 'And this voice which came from heaven we heard, when we were with him in the holy mount.'

TODAY: The Father said, hear ye him. How can you listen to Jesus today? Read or hear his words, and think about one thing he said. And if your faith feels small, remember the mustard seed. Bring your small faith to a great God.

QUESTION: After the shining glory faded, the disciples saw Jesus only. Why do you think it matters that Jesus is enough, even in ordinary days?

PRAYER: Lord Jesus, you are the beloved Son of God, full of glory. Help me to hear you, and to listen to you above every other voice. When my faith feels small, remind me that you are great. And in every ordinary day, help me to see you, Jesus only. Amen.
