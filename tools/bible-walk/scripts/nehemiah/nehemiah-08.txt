# Nehemiah 8: the people gather as one man at the water gate and ask Ezra to bring the book, Ezra reads from early until midday (morning and midday only inside quotes), the pulpit of wood, all the people stood, Amen Amen, the Levites caused them to understand, they read distinctly and gave the sense, the people wept, this day is holy, eat the fat drink the sweet and send portions, the joy of the LORD is your strength, great mirth because they understood, the booths (the feast of the seventh month), very great gladness, reading day by day. Keys 8:6, 8:8, 8:10.
TITLE: The Joy of the LORD Is Your Strength

HOOK: A huge crowd stands in an open square, men, women and children, packed together. At the front, an old man climbs up onto a tall wooden platform, and unrolls a scroll. As he opens it, the whole crowd rises to its feet. He reads aloud, hour after hour. And the people listen, hardly moving. Some begin to cry. They are hearing the words of God.

WHERE: In chapter seven, the wall was finished and the gates were hung. Nehemiah put Hanani and Hananiah in charge of Jerusalem, because Hananiah was a faithful man, and feared God above many. But the city was large, and the people were few. So Nehemiah found the old list of those who first came home, and gathered the people.

Now, in chapter eight, God’s people gather to hear God’s Word. Listen for how they respond. And listen for why their tears turn into joy.

STORY: The seventh month comes, and all the people gather together, as one man, in the open square in front of the water gate. And this time, it is the people themselves who ask. They tell Ezra the scribe to bring out the book of the law of Moses.

Ezra the priest, whom we met in the book of Ezra, brings the law before the whole assembly, men and women, and all the children old enough to understand. 'And he read therein before the street that was before the water gate from the morning until midday, before the men and the women, and those that could understand; and the ears of all the people were attentive unto the book of the law.' For hours, they stood and listened.

Ezra stands on a high wooden platform, built specially for this day, with leaders standing on either side of him. And when Ezra opens the book, where everyone can see it, all the people stand up.

Then Ezra blesses the LORD, the great God.

Then the Levites move among the people, helping them understand the law. The people stand in their places, and the Levites explain.

Many of the people had grown up in exile, speaking the language of Babylon and Persia. The law was written in Hebrew. So the Levites helped to translate and explain it, so that everyone could understand what God was saying.

And as the people hear and understand God’s law, they begin to weep. They realise how far they and their fathers have fallen short of what God commanded.

But Nehemiah, Ezra and the Levites say to them, 'This day is holy unto the LORD your God; mourn not, nor weep.' This is not a day for sorrow.

And then Nehemiah says the most famous words in the book.

The Levites calm the people. Be quiet, this day is holy. Do not grieve.

'And all the people went their way to eat, and to drink, and to send portions, and to make great mirth, because they had understood the words that were declared unto them.' They rejoiced because they understood. God’s Word had first made them weep, and then made them glad.

The next day, the leaders of the families, the priests and the Levites gather around Ezra, to study the law more closely. And they find something written there. God had commanded that in the seventh month, the people should live in booths, shelters made of branches, during the feast. It reminded them of how God had cared for their ancestors in the wilderness, when they lived in tents.

So they send word throughout Jerusalem and the towns. Go out to the hills, and bring branches from olive trees, pine trees, myrtles, palms and other leafy trees, to make booths, as it is written.

And the people do. They build booths on the flat roofs of their houses, in their courtyards, in the courts of the house of God, and in the open squares. The whole city is covered in little shelters made of branches.

And the writer says something remarkable. Since the days of Joshua, they had not kept this feast in this way. 'And there was very great gladness.'

And every single day of the feast, Ezra reads aloud from the book of the law of God. For seven days they keep the feast, and on the eighth day, a solemn gathering, just as God’s law said.

KEY 8:6: 'And Ezra blessed the LORD, the great God. And all the people answered, Amen, Amen, with lifting up their hands: and they bowed their heads, and worshipped the LORD with their faces to the ground.' Amen means, so be it, it is true. When they heard God’s Word, the people did not just listen politely. They worshipped. They lifted their hands, and then bowed down to the ground. God’s Word is not just information. It leads us to worship.

KEY 8:8: 'So they read in the book in the law of God distinctly, and gave the sense, and caused them to understand the reading.' Distinctly means clearly. Gave the sense means explained the meaning. It is not enough just to hear the Bible read. We need to understand it. That is why good teachers matter, and why it is good to ask questions, and why we explain the old words as we walk. God wants us to understand his Word.

KEY 8:10: 'Then he said unto them, Go your way, eat the fat, and drink the sweet, and send portions unto them for whom nothing is prepared: for this day is holy unto our Lord: neither be ye sorry; for the joy of the LORD is your strength.' The joy of the LORD is your strength. The people had been right to be sorry for their sins. But God did not want them to stay in sorrow. The joy that comes from knowing God, his love and forgiveness, is what makes us strong. And notice, send portions to those who have nothing. Joy always shares.

THREAD: The psalmist wrote, 'The entrance of thy words giveth light; it giveth understanding unto the simple.' And the two friends on the road to Emmaus said, 'Did not our heart burn within us, while he talked with us by the way, and while he opened to us the scriptures?'

TODAY: Read a small part of God’s Word today, and if anything is unclear, ask someone, or look it up, so you can understand it. Then let it lead you to worship. And share your joy with someone who has nothing prepared.

QUESTION: The people wept when they heard God’s law, and then rejoiced because they understood it. How has God’s Word both humbled you and given you joy?

PRAYER: Lord God, thank you for your Word. Open my ears to listen, and my mind to understand it. When it shows me my sin, let me be sorry, and then let me find joy in your forgiveness. Let the joy of the LORD be my strength today. And help me to share that joy with others. Amen.
