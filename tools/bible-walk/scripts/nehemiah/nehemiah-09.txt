# Nehemiah 9: the assembly with fasting and confession, reading and worship, the Levites' prayer retelling the whole story (creation, Abram, Egypt and the Red sea, the pillar of cloud and fire (inside the quote), Sinai, manna and water, the fathers' pride, the golden calf, yet manifold mercies, the wilderness, the land, the judges' cycle, the prophets rejected (9:26 not quoted), many years thou didst forbear, gracious and merciful, thou art just, we are servants in the land), a sure covenant. Keys 9:6, 9:17, 9:31. A recap of the walk so far.
TITLE: Thou Art a God Ready to Pardon

HOOK: Imagine sitting down with someone you love, and telling the whole story of your friendship. Do you remember when we first met? Do you remember when you helped me through that hard time? Do you remember when I let you down, and you forgave me? That is what the people of Israel do in this chapter. They tell God the whole story of their life with him. And it is a story of his amazing faithfulness, and their many failures.

WHERE: In chapter eight, all the people gathered as one, and Ezra read God’s law to them from early in the day until midday. The Levites helped them understand it. The people wept, but Nehemiah said, the joy of the LORD is your strength. And they kept the feast of booths, with very great gladness.

Now, in chapter nine, the people gather again, to confess. Listen to the great prayer of the Levites. It tells the story of almost every book we have walked through. And listen for how often it says, but thou.

STORY: Later that same month, the people of Israel gather again. This time, they are fasting, wearing sackcloth, with dust on their heads, signs of deep sorrow. They separate themselves from those who worship other gods, and stand and confess their own sins, and the sins of their fathers.

For a quarter of the day, they listen to the reading of God’s law. For another quarter, they confess their sins and worship the LORD.

Then the Levites stand up and cry out to God. Stand up, and bless the LORD your God for ever and ever! And they pray a great prayer, telling the whole story.

It begins with creation.

Then Abraham. You chose Abram, and brought him out of Ur, and gave him the name Abraham. You found his heart faithful, and made a covenant to give his family the land. And you kept your word, for you are righteous.

Then Egypt. You saw the suffering of our fathers in Egypt. You heard their cry by the Red Sea. You did signs and wonders against Pharaoh. You divided the sea, and they walked through on dry land.

Then the wilderness. 'Moreover thou leddest them in the day by a cloudy pillar; and in the night by a pillar of fire, to give them light in the way wherein they should go.' You came down on Mount Sinai, and spoke with them from heaven, and gave them good laws. You gave them bread from heaven when they were hungry, and water from the rock when they were thirsty.

And then the turn. 'But they and our fathers dealt proudly, and hardened their necks, and hearkened not to thy commandments'.

Even the golden calf. Yet in your great mercy, you did not leave them in the wilderness. The pillar of cloud and the pillar of fire still led them. You gave your good Spirit to teach them. For forty years you provided for them, and they lacked nothing. Their clothes did not wear out, and their feet did not swell.

Then the land, the book of Joshua. You gave them kingdoms. You multiplied their children like the stars in the sky. You brought them into the land, with its cities and houses, its wells and vineyards and fruit trees. And they ate, and were full, and enjoyed your great goodness.

Then the book of Judges, and the sad circle. Nevertheless, they were disobedient. They turned their backs on your law. They rejected your prophets. So you handed them over to their enemies. But when they cried out to you, you heard from heaven, and in your great mercy you gave them saviours. Then, when they had rest, they did evil again. And again you rescued them, many times, according to your mercies.

Then the kings and the prophets. For many years you were patient with them, and warned them by your Spirit through your prophets. But they would not listen. So you gave them into the hands of the nations.

But still.

Now, our God, the great and mighty and awesome God, who keeps covenant and mercy, do not think lightly of all the trouble that has come upon us, upon our kings, our princes, our priests, our prophets, and all your people, from the days of the Assyrian kings until now.

And they end with honesty. You have been just in everything that has happened to us. You have been faithful, and we have done wrong. Here we are, servants today, in the very land you gave our fathers. Its rich harvest goes to the kings you have set over us, because of our sins. We are in great distress.

And because of all this, they make a solemn promise, a covenant, and write it down. And the leaders, the Levites and the priests put their seals on it.

KEY 9:6: 'Thou, even thou, art LORD alone; thou hast made heaven, the heaven of heavens, with all their host, the earth, and all things that are therein, the seas, and all that is therein, and thou preservest them all; and the host of heaven worshippeth thee.' The prayer starts at the very beginning: God the Creator. Before they talked about their sins, or their problems, they remembered who God is. He made everything, and he keeps everything going. Even the angels worship him. Every prayer is better when it begins with who God is.

KEY 9:17: 'And refused to obey, neither were mindful of thy wonders that thou didst among them; but hardened their necks, and in their rebellion appointed a captain to return to their bondage: but thou art a God ready to pardon, gracious and merciful, slow to anger, and of great kindness, and forsookest them not.' But thou. The people failed, again and again. But God is ready to pardon. Not reluctant. Ready. Gracious, merciful, slow to anger, full of kindness. This is the heart of the whole Bible. Our failures are many, but God’s readiness to forgive is greater.

KEY 9:31: 'Nevertheless for thy great mercies’ sake thou didst not utterly consume them, nor forsake them; for thou art a gracious and merciful God.' Nevertheless. After all the sin, after all the warnings, after the exile, God did not utterly destroy them, or abandon them. Why? Not because they deserved it, but for his great mercies’ sake. God’s mercy is the reason any of us are still standing.

THREAD: The psalmist wrote, 'The LORD is merciful and gracious, slow to anger, and plenteous in mercy.' And Paul wrote, 'For whatsoever things were written aforetime were written for our learning'.

TODAY: Take a few minutes today to tell God your own story, your life with him. Remember the times he helped you. Be honest about where you have failed. And thank him that he is a God ready to pardon.

QUESTION: The prayer tells of Israel’s failures again and again, and each time says, but thou. Where in your own story can you see God’s faithfulness, even when you failed?

PRAYER: Lord God, you alone are LORD. You made heaven and earth, and you keep them all. Thank you for your faithfulness through all the generations, and in my own life. I confess that I have often failed. But you are a God ready to pardon, gracious and merciful, slow to anger and of great kindness. Thank you that you have not forsaken me. Amen.
