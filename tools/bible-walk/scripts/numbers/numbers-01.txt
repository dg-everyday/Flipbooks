# Numbers 1: the census at Sinai (no dates spoken), a leader from every tribe, the names (Nahshon of Judah), every man from twenty years old able to go to war, the totals, the Levites not numbered but appointed over the tabernacle, they pitch round about it, as the LORD commanded. Keys 1:2, 1:53, 1:54.
TITLE: Every One Head of the House of His Fathers

HOOK: Imagine a teacher on the first day of school, standing at the front with a register, calling out every name. Not just counting heads, but reading each name, one by one. Here. Here. Here. Every child matters. Every child is known. Numbers chapter one is a bit like that. God is about to call the roll of his people.

WHERE: In the introduction, we heard that Numbers tells the journey of Israel from Mount Sinai to the edge of the promised land. We heard that the book is named after two counts of the people, one at the beginning and one at the end, with a whole generation passing away in between. And we heard that we should watch for the cloud, for grumbling, and for grace.

Now, in chapter one, the first count begins. Listen for who is counted, and why. And listen for the one tribe that is left out of the count.

STORY: The LORD speaks to Moses in the wilderness of Sinai, in the tabernacle of the congregation, a little more than a year after Israel came out of Egypt.

And God gives Moses a task.

By their polls means one by one, head by head. And God says who is to be counted. Every man from twenty years old and upward, who is able to go out to war. Israel is about to march toward the land of Canaan. They need to know how many fighting men they have, tribe by tribe.

Moses is not to do this alone. With him and Aaron, there will be one man from every tribe, the head of his father’s house. And God names them. Elizur from Reuben. Shelumiel from Simeon. Nahshon, the son of Amminadab, from Judah. And on through all the tribes, from Issachar to Naphtali.

Keep an ear out for Nahshon of Judah. Long after, in the book of Ruth, we heard his name in the family line that leads to King David. And the Gospel of Matthew names him in the family line of Jesus.

So Moses and Aaron gather the whole congregation together. And the people declare their family trees, by their families and by their fathers’ houses. 'As the LORD commanded Moses, so he numbered them in the wilderness of Sinai.'

Then the writer goes through the tribes one by one. Reuben, Simeon, Gad, Judah, Issachar, Zebulun, Ephraim, Manasseh, Benjamin, Dan, Asher and Naphtali. For each tribe, the same careful words: by their generations, after their families, by the house of their fathers, every male from twenty years old and upward, all who were able to go to war. And then the number.

Judah is the largest tribe, with more than seventy thousand men. And when all the tribes are added together, the total is more than six hundred thousand fighting men.

Think about that. When Jacob went down into Egypt, his whole family was about seventy people. Now, generations later, there are more than six hundred thousand men able to fight, besides the women and children. God had promised Abraham that his descendants would be as many as the stars. And here is the promise, being kept.

But one tribe is not counted with the others.

The LORD says to Moses, do not count the tribe of Levi, or include them in the total with the rest of Israel. Instead, put the Levites in charge of the tabernacle, and all its things. They will carry it, and look after it, and camp all around it. When it is time to move, the Levites will take the tabernacle down. When it is time to stop, the Levites will set it up again. And no one else is to come near it.

The Levites were not to go out to war with the other tribes. Their work was to serve God, and to care for his holy dwelling place.

And the chapter ends with words we heard again and again at the end of Exodus.

Why would the Bible include a chapter like this, full of names and numbers? Because it shows us that God knows his people. Not as a crowd, but one by one, by name, family by family. In the wilderness, it would be easy to feel lost, just one person among hundreds of thousands. But God counted every one. No one was forgotten.

KEY 1:2: 'Take ye the sum of all the congregation of the children of Israel, after their families, by the house of their fathers, with the number of their names, every male by their polls;' With the number of their names. God did not just want a total. He wanted names. Every person in Israel belonged to a family, and a tribe, and a people. And every one of them was known. Jesus said that the very hairs of our head are all numbered. God knows you by name.

KEY 1:53: 'But the Levites shall pitch round about the tabernacle of testimony, that there be no wrath upon the congregation of the children of Israel: and the Levites shall keep the charge of the tabernacle of testimony.' The Levites camped in a ring around God’s house, between the tabernacle and the rest of the camp. God is holy, and his presence in the middle of the camp was a wonderful gift, and also a serious one. The Levites guarded it, and served it, so that the people could live safely near a holy God.

KEY 1:54: 'And the children of Israel did according to all that the LORD commanded Moses, so did they.' At the start of the journey, the people obey completely. This is a good beginning. As we walk on through Numbers, we will see that it does not always stay this way. But for now, God’s people are ready, counted and ordered, waiting to follow him.

THREAD: Jesus said, 'But the very hairs of your head are all numbered.' And God said through Isaiah, 'I have called thee by thy name; thou art mine.'

TODAY: Do you ever feel lost in the crowd, unnoticed? Remember today that God knows you by name. Thank him for that. And think of someone who might feel forgotten. Call them by name, and let them know they matter.

QUESTION: God counted his people one by one, with the number of their names. What difference does it make to you that God knows you personally, by name?

PRAYER: Lord God, thank you that you know me by name, and that I am never just a face in the crowd to you. Thank you for keeping your promises, generation after generation. Help me to obey you, as your people did at the start of their journey, and to keep following you to the end. Amen.
