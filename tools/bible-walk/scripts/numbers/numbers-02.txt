# Numbers 2: the camp arranged by standards, every man by his own standard, far off about the tabernacle, Judah east toward the sunrising (in quote), Reuben south, Ephraim west, Dan north, the tabernacle and Levites in the midst, the order of marching, so they pitched by their standards. Keys 2:2, 2:17, 2:34.
TITLE: Every Man by His Own Standard

HOOK: Picture a great camp, seen from a mountain high above. Thousands upon thousands of tents, spread out across the desert. But they are not scattered randomly. They are laid out in perfect order, in four great groups, one on each side, with banners flying above them. And right in the middle, with every tent facing toward it, stands the tabernacle of God.

WHERE: In chapter one, Moses counted the people at Sinai, tribe by tribe, with the number of their names, every man from twenty years old who could go to war. There were more than six hundred thousand. But the Levites were not counted with the others. They were to camp around the tabernacle and care for it. And the children of Israel did according to all that the LORD commanded Moses.

Now, in chapter two, God arranges the camp. Listen for where each tribe is placed. And listen for what is at the very centre.

STORY: The LORD speaks to Moses and Aaron.

A standard was a banner, a flag on a pole, carried high so that everyone could see where their tribe belonged. Every family would know where to pitch its tents, and where to gather when it was time to move.

Then God places the tribes, three on each side of the tabernacle.

On the east side, the side where the sun rises, is the camp of Judah, with Issachar and Zebulun beside it. Judah’s captain is Nahshon, the son of Amminadab. These three tribes together number more than one hundred and eighty thousand. And they will set out first, leading the way.

The tribe of Judah leads. Remember that Jacob, long before, had blessed Judah with a special promise, that the sceptre would not depart from Judah. From Judah would come King David, and from David’s line, Jesus. Even here in the wilderness, Judah goes first.

On the south side is the camp of Reuben, with Simeon and Gad. They will set out second.

Then, in the middle of the marching order, comes the tabernacle.

On the west side is the camp of Ephraim, with Manasseh and Benjamin, the families of Rachel’s sons, Joseph and Benjamin. They will set out third.

And on the north side is the camp of Dan, with Asher and Naphtali. They will set out last, at the rear.

Then the writer gives the total again, more than six hundred thousand men. And he reminds us that the Levites are not counted among them, as the LORD commanded Moses.

Now imagine the camp from above. Twelve tribes, in four groups of three, east, south, west and north. In the middle, the Levites, in a ring around the tabernacle. And at the very centre, the tabernacle itself, with the cloud of God’s presence over it.

Everything in the camp faced the tabernacle. Every tent, every family, every tribe. God was at the centre of their life, literally. Wherever you lived in the camp, if you came out of your tent and looked toward the middle, you would see the place where God dwelt.

And when they moved, the order was the same. Judah in front, then Reuben, then the tabernacle carried by the Levites, then Ephraim, then Dan at the back. God was always in the middle of his people, whether they were camping or on the move.

There is something beautiful about this order. It was not chaos, with everyone pushing for the best spot. Every tribe had its place, given by God. No one had to fight for position. And no tribe was further from the centre than any other. All of them were gathered around God.

KEY 2:2: 'Every man of the children of Israel shall pitch by his own standard, with the ensign of their father’s house: far off about the tabernacle of the congregation shall they pitch.' An ensign is a sign or banner, like the standard. Every man knew where he belonged, under his family’s banner. God’s people are not a crowd of strangers. We belong to one another, and we each have a place. And notice, far off about the tabernacle. Not too close, because God is holy. But all around it, facing it.

KEY 2:17: 'Then the tabernacle of the congregation shall set forward with the camp of the Levites in the midst of the camp: as they encamp, so shall they set forward, every man in his place by their standards.' In the midst of the camp. When Israel marched, God’s house was in the very middle, with tribes in front and tribes behind. God was not far ahead, out of reach, or left behind. He was right in the middle of his people, on every step of the journey.

KEY 2:34: 'And the children of Israel did according to all that the LORD commanded Moses: so they pitched by their standards, and so they set forward, every one after their families, according to the house of their fathers.' Once again, the people obey. They camp where God tells them, and march when God tells them, in the order God gives them. Following God is not only about the big decisions. It is about letting him order the everyday shape of our lives.

THREAD: Jesus said, 'For where two or three are gathered together in my name, there am I in the midst of them.' And Paul wrote, 'Let all things be done decently and in order.'

TODAY: What is at the centre of your life? Your work, your family, your phone, your plans? Ask God today to be at the centre, with everything else arranged around him. And thank him that you have a place among his people.

QUESTION: Every tent in the camp faced the tabernacle, with God at the centre. What would it look like for God to be at the centre of your everyday life?

PRAYER: Lord God, be at the centre of my life, with everything else arranged around you. Thank you that I have a place among your people, and that I do not have to fight for it. Thank you that you are in the midst of your people, whether we are resting or on the move. Amen.
