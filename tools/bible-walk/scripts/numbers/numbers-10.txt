# Numbers 10: two silver trumpets (calling, journeying, alarm in war, days of gladness), the cloud taken up and Israel leaves Sinai (no date spoken), the order of march by standards, the Levites carrying the tabernacle, Hobab invited (thou mayest be to us instead of eyes), the ark goes before them to search out a resting place, rise up LORD, return O LORD. Keys 10:29, 10:33, 10:35.
TITLE: Rise Up, LORD

HOOK: For almost a year, the people of Israel have camped at the foot of Mount Sinai, where God gave them his law. They have built the tabernacle. They have been counted and arranged. And then one day, the silver trumpets sound, the cloud rises from the tabernacle, and begins to move. And for the first time, the whole nation sets out together, toward the promised land.

WHERE: In chapter nine, Israel kept the Passover in the wilderness, and God made a way for those who could not keep it at the first time. And the cloud covered the tabernacle, with fire in the dark. At the commandment of the LORD they journeyed, and at the commandment of the LORD they rested.

Now, in chapter ten, the journey from Sinai finally begins. Listen for the silver trumpets. And listen for the prayer Moses prays every time the ark sets out.

STORY: The LORD says to Moses, make two trumpets of silver, each from a single piece.

These were not trumpets for music. They were for giving signals across a huge camp. When both trumpets were blown, the whole assembly was to gather at the door of the tabernacle. When only one was blown, the leaders were to gather. When an alarm was sounded, a special call, the camps on the east side were to set out. At a second alarm, the camps on the south. And so on.

The priests, the sons of Aaron, were to blow the trumpets. And God made a promise. When you go to war in your land against an enemy who oppresses you, sound an alarm with the trumpets, and you will be remembered before the LORD your God, and saved from your enemies.

The trumpets were also to be blown on days of gladness, at Israel’s appointed feasts, over their offerings, as a reminder before God.

The trumpets were a way of crying out to God, and a way of rejoicing before him. In danger and in joy, God wanted his people to remember him.

Then the day comes. The cloud lifts from the tabernacle. And the children of Israel set out from the wilderness of Sinai, and the cloud settles in the wilderness of Paran.

They set out exactly as God had arranged. First, the standard of Judah, with Issachar and Zebulun. Then the tabernacle is taken down, and the families of Gershon and Merari set out, carrying it. Then the standard of Reuben, with Simeon and Gad. Then the Kohathites, carrying the holy things, so that the tabernacle would be set up again before they arrived. Then the standard of Ephraim, with Manasseh and Benjamin. And last of all, the standard of Dan, with Asher and Naphtali, as the rear guard.

Everything happened just as God had planned in chapter two. The long preparation was over. Now they were on the move.

Then Moses speaks to Hobab, his brother in law, from the family of Midian.

Hobab says, I will not go. I will go back to my own land and my own family.

But Moses asks again. Please do not leave us. You know where we should camp in the wilderness. 'thou mayest be to us instead of eyes.' And if you come with us, whatever good the LORD does for us, we will do for you.

Moses had the cloud to guide them. But he also valued the wisdom of a man who knew the desert. God often guides us through the experience and help of other people, as well as directly. And later in the Bible, we find Hobab’s family living among the people of Judah. It seems he came.

So they set out from the mountain of the LORD, and travel three days.

And whenever the ark set out, Moses prayed.

And whenever it came to rest, Moses prayed again. 'Return, O LORD, unto the many thousands of Israel.'

Every journey began and ended with prayer. Rise up, LORD, at the start. Return, O LORD, at the end. Moses knew that the most important thing about the journey was not the road, or the destination, but God’s presence with them.

KEY 10:29: 'And Moses said unto Hobab, the son of Raguel the Midianite, Moses’ father in law, We are journeying unto the place of which the LORD said, I will give it you: come thou with us, and we will do thee good: for the LORD hath spoken good concerning Israel.' Come thou with us. Moses invited an outsider to join God’s people, and share in God’s blessing. For the LORD hath spoken good concerning Israel. God’s people should always be inviting others to come along, and share the good things God has promised.

KEY 10:33: 'And they departed from the mount of the LORD three days’ journey: and the ark of the covenant of the LORD went before them in the three days’ journey, to search out a resting place for them.' The ark went before them, to search out a resting place. God went ahead of his people, finding the place where they would rest. That is how God leads. He does not send us into the unknown alone. He goes ahead, and prepares the way. Jesus said, I go to prepare a place for you.

KEY 10:35: 'And it came to pass, when the ark set forward, that Moses said, Rise up, LORD, and let thine enemies be scattered; and let them that hate thee flee before thee.' Rise up, LORD. Moses began every journey by asking God to lead, and to go before them against every danger. The people of God sang these words for centuries afterwards. It is a good prayer to begin any day, any task, or any journey with.

THREAD: A psalm begins with Moses’ prayer: 'Let God arise, let his enemies be scattered'. And Jesus said, 'I go to prepare a place for you.'

TODAY: Begin your day, or your next task, with Moses’ prayer: rise up, LORD, and go before me. And end it with his second prayer: return, O LORD, and stay with me. And think of someone you could invite to come along with you in following God.

QUESTION: Moses prayed at the start and at the end of every journey. What difference would it make to begin and end each day with prayer?

PRAYER: Rise up, LORD, and go before me today. Scatter whatever stands against me. Search out a resting place for me, as you did for your people. And at the end of the day, return, O LORD, and stay with me. Thank you that you go ahead of your people, and prepare the way. Amen.

NEXT: Next on the walk, Numbers chapter eleven. Only three days into the journey, the people begin to complain. They are tired of manna, and long for the food of Egypt. And Moses cries out, I am not able to bear all this people alone.
