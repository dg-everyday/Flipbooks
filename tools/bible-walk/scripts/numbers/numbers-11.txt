# Numbers 11: complaining and the fire at Taberah, the mixed multitude's craving, we remember the fish of Egypt, the manna described, Moses' burden (avoids 11:15 "kill"), seventy elders and the spirit shared, Eldad and Medad, would God all the LORD's people were prophets, is the LORD's hand waxed short, the quails, the graves of craving (gently). Keys 11:14, 11:23, 11:29.
TITLE: Is the LORD’s Hand Waxed Short?

HOOK: Every day, food falls from the sky. Fresh, sweet, enough for everyone, just lying on the ground, waiting to be gathered. It is a miracle that happens every single day. And the people are sick of it. They sit at the doors of their tents and weep, remembering the fish and cucumbers and melons and onions they used to eat in Egypt, where they were slaves.

WHERE: In chapter ten, Moses made two silver trumpets, and at last the cloud lifted from Sinai. Israel set out in order, tribe by tribe, and Moses invited Hobab to come with them. The ark went before them to search out a resting place. And whenever it set out, Moses prayed, rise up, LORD.

Now, in chapter eleven, only a few days into the journey, the trouble begins. Listen for what the people complain about. And listen for how God helps Moses carry the load.

STORY: The people complain, and it displeases the LORD. And the fire of the LORD burns at the edges of the camp. The people cry out to Moses. Moses prays to the LORD, and the fire dies down. And they call the place Taberah, which means burning.

It is a short and sharp warning, at the very start. Complaining against God is serious.

But the complaining does not stop. Some people among them, a mixed crowd who had come out of Egypt with Israel, begin to crave other food. And soon the children of Israel are weeping too. Who will give us meat to eat? We remember the fish we ate in Egypt for nothing. The cucumbers, the melons, the leeks, the onions and the garlic. But now we have nothing but this manna.

For nothing? They had been slaves in Egypt. They had cried out to God because of their suffering. Now, in their memory, Egypt has become a place of free food and happy times. Complaining often makes the past look better than it was, and the present look worse.

The writer describes the manna. It was like coriander seed, pale like a precious gum. The people gathered it, and ground it, and baked it into cakes. It tasted like cakes made with fresh oil. It fell with the dew on the camp every day. It was good food, given freely, every day, by God.

Moses hears the people weeping, every family at the door of its tent. And the LORD is very angry. And Moses is upset too. And Moses pours out his heart to God.

Why have you treated your servant so badly? Why have you put the burden of all these people on me? Did I give birth to them, that you should tell me to carry them in my arms like a baby, all the way to the land you promised? Where am I supposed to get meat for all these people? They keep weeping to me, give us meat to eat!

And then Moses says what is really on his heart.

Moses was exhausted. He was trying to carry everything on his own. And he told God honestly how he felt.

And God answers with kindness. Gather seventy of the elders of Israel, men you know to be leaders, and bring them to the tabernacle. I will come down and speak with you there. And I will take some of the Spirit that is on you, and put it on them. And they will carry the burden of the people with you, so that you do not have to carry it alone.

God does not scold Moses for being tired. He shares the load.

And God says, tell the people to get ready, because they will eat meat. Not for one day, or two, or ten, or twenty. But for a whole month, until they are sick of it. Because they have rejected the LORD who is among them, and wept, saying, why did we ever leave Egypt?

Moses is astonished. There are six hundred thousand men here. Would there be enough even if we used all our flocks and herds, or caught all the fish in the sea?

And God answers.

Moses gathers the seventy elders around the tabernacle. And the LORD comes down in the cloud, and puts some of the Spirit that was on Moses upon the seventy. And when the Spirit rests on them, they prophesy.

But two men, Eldad and Medad, had stayed in the camp. Their names were on the list, but they had not gone out to the tabernacle. And the Spirit rests on them too, and they prophesy in the camp.

A young man runs to tell Moses. And Joshua, Moses’ young helper, says, my lord Moses, stop them!

And Moses answers.

Then a wind from the LORD brings quails from the sea, and they fall all around the camp. The people gather them all day, all through the dark hours, and all the next day. But while the meat is still in their mouths, the LORD’s anger burns against them, and a plague strikes the people. And the place is called the graves of craving, because there they buried the people who had craved.

They got what they wanted. But their hearts had turned from God, and what they demanded did them no good.

KEY 11:14: 'I am not able to bear all this people alone, because it is too heavy for me.' Even Moses, the great leader, reached the end of his strength. He was honest with God about it. God never meant Moses to carry everything alone. And he does not mean us to either. When the load is too heavy, tell God, as Moses did. He shares the burden.

KEY 11:23: 'And the LORD said unto Moses, Is the LORD’S hand waxed short? thou shalt see now whether my word shall come to pass unto thee or not.' Waxed short means grown too short, too weak. Moses could not see how God could feed so many. And God asked him, is my arm too short? Is anything too hard for me? Whenever a problem seems too big, this is the question to remember.

KEY 11:29: 'And Moses said unto him, Enviest thou for my sake? would God that all the LORD’S people were prophets, and that the LORD would put his spirit upon them!' Joshua was jealous for Moses’ honour. But Moses was not jealous at all. He wished that all God’s people had God’s Spirit. Many years later, at Pentecost, Peter said that this is exactly what God had done: poured out his Spirit on all his people, young and old.

THREAD: Paul wrote, 'Do all things without murmurings and disputings'. And Peter said, quoting the prophet Joel, 'I will pour out of my Spirit upon all flesh: and your sons and your daughters shall prophesy'.

TODAY: Is there something God gives you every day that you have started to take for granted, or even complain about? Thank him for it today. And if you are carrying too much alone, tell God honestly, and ask him to send people to share the load.

QUESTION: The people grew tired of the manna God gave them every day. What gifts of God are you most likely to take for granted?

PRAYER: Lord God, forgive me for complaining, and for taking your daily gifts for granted. Thank you for the bread you give me every day. When my load is too heavy, help me to tell you honestly, and to accept help from others. Your hand is never too short. Amen.
