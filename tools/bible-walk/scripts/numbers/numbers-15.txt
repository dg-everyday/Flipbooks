# Numbers 15: laws for "when ye be come into the land" (a promise after chapter 14), offerings with flour, oil and wine, one law for the stranger, the first of the dough, sins of ignorance forgiven, presumptuous sin, the man gathering sticks on the sabbath (told briefly, as Scripture tells it, no day applied today, no "stoned"), fringes with a ribband of blue to remember (avoids 15:39). Keys 15:16, 15:40, 15:41.
TITLE: That Ye May Remember, and Do All My Commandments

HOOK: Imagine being told, you will never get there. You will spend the rest of your life wandering, and only your children will see the promised land. And then, straight away, God starts giving you instructions for what to do when you arrive in that land. It sounds strange. But it is one of the kindest things God could have done.

WHERE: In chapter fourteen, the people wept and rebelled, wanting to go back to Egypt. Joshua and Caleb said, if the LORD delight in us, he will bring us in. Moses prayed, and God said, I have pardoned. But that generation would wander in the wilderness, and only their children, with Caleb and Joshua, would enter the land.

Now, in chapter fifteen, God speaks to his people right after that terrible day. Listen for the first words God says. And listen for the blue thread he asks them to wear.

STORY: The LORD speaks to Moses. Speak to the children of Israel, and say to them, when you come into the land I am giving you to live in.

When you come into the land. After the rebellion of chapter fourteen, these words were full of hope. God had not cancelled his promise. The people would enter the land. Not that generation, but their children would. God was already planning for it.

Then God explains how they are to bring offerings when they are settled in the land. With every burnt offering, they are to bring a grain offering of fine flour mixed with oil, and a drink offering of wine. A little for a lamb, more for a ram, more again for a bull.

Flour, oil and wine. These were the fruits of the land, the crops they would grow. God was telling them, one day you will have fields and vineyards of your own, and you will bring their produce to me.

And God says the same law applies to foreigners living among them.

Then God says, when you eat the bread of the land, offer the first of your dough to the LORD. The very first of every batch, given back to God, as a way of saying, this all comes from you.

Then God speaks about sin. If the whole congregation sins without meaning to, through ignorance, they are to bring an offering, and the priest will make atonement, and they will be forgiven. And if one person sins without meaning to, they are to bring an offering too, and they will be forgiven. The same law applies to the Israelite and to the foreigner.

But the person who sins deliberately, with a high hand, in open defiance of God, is a different matter. That person despises the word of the LORD, and will be cut off from the people.

There is a difference between stumbling into sin, and deliberately rebelling against God. God provided forgiveness for those who sinned in weakness or ignorance. But open defiance of God was treated very seriously.

Then the writer tells a short, solemn story. While the people were in the wilderness, a man was found gathering sticks on the sabbath day, the day of rest God had given to Israel as a sign of his covenant. He was brought to Moses and Aaron, and held until they knew what to do. And the LORD said that the man must be put to death. And the people did as the LORD commanded.

It is hard to hear. It seems this man was openly defying the covenant God had just given, right after the rebellion of chapter fourteen. The sabbath was Israel’s special sign of belonging to God. The Bible tells this as it happened, in that time, to show how seriously God treated deliberate rebellion against his covenant.

Then the chapter ends with something gentle. The LORD tells Moses, tell the children of Israel to make tassels on the corners of their garments, and to put a cord of blue on each tassel.

And God explains why.

Blue was the colour of the most holy things in the tabernacle. Every time an Israelite looked down at the corner of their clothing, they would see a little thread of blue, and remember. Remember God’s commands. Remember that they belonged to him.

Many years later, in the Gospels, a woman who had been ill for years reached out and touched the border of Jesus’ garment, and was healed. Jesus, too, wore these reminders of God’s word.

And the chapter ends with God’s own name.

KEY 15:16: 'One law and one manner shall be for you, and for the stranger that sojourneth with you.' One law for everyone. The foreigner who joined God’s people was not a second class member. They worshipped the same God, in the same way, and received the same forgiveness. God’s people have always been open to all who come to him.

KEY 15:40: 'That ye may remember, and do all my commandments, and be holy unto your God.' Remember, do, and be holy. God knows how easily we forget. So he gave his people a simple, everyday reminder, right on their clothes. We need reminders too. A verse on the wall, a prayer before meals, a habit that turns our hearts back to God through the day.

KEY 15:41: 'I am the LORD your God, which brought you out of the land of Egypt, to be your God: I am the LORD your God.' The chapter ends with God saying his name twice. I am the LORD your God. After all their failures, he is still their God. He brought them out of Egypt, not just to free them, but to be their God. That relationship was the heart of everything.

THREAD: A psalm says, 'Thy word have I hid in mine heart, that I might not sin against thee.' And Peter wrote, 'as he which hath called you is holy, so be ye holy in all manner of conversation'.

TODAY: What reminders of God do you have in your everyday life? Choose one small thing today to help you remember God’s word, a verse on your phone, a note by your bed, or a prayer at a set moment.

QUESTION: God gave his people a blue thread so they would remember his commandments. What everyday reminders help you to remember God?

PRAYER: Lord God, you are the LORD my God. Thank you that even after failure, your promises still stand. Thank you that there is one law and one welcome for all who come to you. Help me to remember your word, to do it, and to be holy, because you are holy. Amen.
