# Numbers 16: Korah, Dathan, Abiram and On with two hundred and fifty princes, ye take too much upon you, Moses falls on his face, the test of the censers, seemeth it but a small thing, Dathan and Abiram refuse to come, the glory appears, the God of the spirits of all flesh, depart from the tents, the earth opens (told plainly, briefly), fire on the two hundred and fifty, the censers made plates for the altar, the people murmur again (avoids 16:41), Aaron stands between the dead and the living (no count). Keys 16:5, 16:22, 16:48.
TITLE: He Stood Between the Dead and the Living

HOOK: A plague is sweeping through the camp. People are falling sick and dying. And an old man, the high priest, is running. In his hands he carries a censer, a pan of burning coals with sweet incense rising from it. He runs right into the middle of the people, and stands still. On one side of him, the dead. On the other side, the living. And where he stands, the plague stops.

WHERE: In chapter fifteen, God gave laws for when ye be come into the land, a promise that the next generation would enter. There was one law for the stranger and for the Israelite, forgiveness for sins of ignorance, and a blue thread on every garment, that ye may remember, and do all my commandments.

Now, in chapter sixteen, there is a rebellion against Moses and Aaron. This is a serious and frightening chapter. Listen for what Korah wants. And listen for how Moses and Aaron respond, even to those who opposed them.

STORY: A Levite named Korah, from the family of Kohath, joins with three men from the tribe of Reuben, Dathan, Abiram and On. And they gather two hundred and fifty leaders of the congregation, well known men. And they confront Moses and Aaron.

You take too much upon yourselves, they say. All the congregation is holy, every one of them, and the LORD is among them. Why then do you lift yourselves up above the LORD’s assembly?

It sounds fair. Everyone is holy, so why should Moses and Aaron be special? But underneath was jealousy, and a desire for power.

When Moses hears it, he falls on his face. Then he speaks to Korah and his company.

Moses tells them, take censers, and put fire and incense in them before the LORD. And the man the LORD chooses will be the holy one. You take too much upon yourselves, you sons of Levi.

Then Moses speaks to Korah and the Levites. Is it a small thing to you that the God of Israel has set you apart from the rest of the people, to bring you near to himself, to serve at the tabernacle? He has brought you near to him, and now you want the priesthood too?

Korah already had a wonderful calling, serving at God’s house. But he was not content. He wanted more.

Moses sends for Dathan and Abiram, but they refuse to come. They accuse Moses of bringing them out of a land of milk and honey, Egypt, to let them die in the wilderness, and of not giving them the land he promised. We will not come up, they say.

The next day, Korah and the two hundred and fifty men stand at the door of the tabernacle with their censers, with Moses and Aaron. And Korah gathers the whole congregation against them. And the glory of the LORD appears to everyone.

The LORD says to Moses and Aaron, separate yourselves from this congregation, so that I may consume them in a moment.

And Moses and Aaron fall on their faces again, and pray.

God answers. Tell the people to move away from the tents of Korah, Dathan and Abiram. So Moses warns the people, get away from the tents of these wicked men, and touch nothing of theirs. And the people move back. Dathan and Abiram stand at the doors of their tents, with their families.

Moses says, this is how you will know that the LORD has sent me, and that I have not done these things on my own. If the LORD does a new thing, and the earth opens and swallows them, then you will know that these men have despised the LORD.

As soon as he finishes speaking, the ground splits open beneath them, and swallows them, and their households, and all their goods. And they go down into the pit, and the earth closes over them. All Israel runs away in terror. And fire comes out from the LORD, and consumes the two hundred and fifty men who offered incense.

This is one of the most fearful judgments in the whole journey. It shows how serious it is to rebel against the leaders God has chosen, and to grasp at holy things God has not given.

God tells Eleazar the priest to take the bronze censers, and hammer them into plates to cover the altar, as a reminder to Israel that only the family of Aaron may offer incense before the LORD.

But the very next day, the whole congregation grumbles against Moses and Aaron, blaming them for what happened. And the glory of the LORD appears again. And God says, get away from this congregation, so I may consume them.

And again Moses and Aaron fall on their faces. And Moses says to Aaron, quick, take a censer, put fire in it from the altar, and incense, and go to the congregation, and make atonement for them, because wrath has gone out from the LORD. The plague has begun.

And Aaron runs.

And Aaron goes back to Moses at the door of the tabernacle, and the plague is over.

KEY 16:5: 'And he spake unto Korah and unto all his company, saying, Even to morrow the LORD will shew who are his, and who is holy; and will cause him to come near unto him: even him whom he hath chosen will he cause to come near unto him.' Moses did not fight for his own position. He left it to God. The LORD will show who are his. When people challenge us, it is wise to let God defend us, rather than fighting for our own importance.

KEY 16:22: 'And they fell upon their faces, and said, O God, the God of the spirits of all flesh, shall one man sin, and wilt thou be wroth with all the congregation?' Moses and Aaron prayed for the very people who had gathered against them. The God of the spirits of all flesh means the God who made every living person, who knows every heart. They asked God to judge the guilty, but to spare the rest. Even under attack, they interceded.

KEY 16:48: 'And he stood between the dead and the living; and the plague was stayed.' Aaron put himself in the middle, between death and life, with the incense of atonement. And the plague stopped. It is a powerful picture of what Jesus, our great high priest, has done. He stands between us and death, and through his sacrifice, we live.

THREAD: The letter to the Hebrews says of Jesus, 'Wherefore he is able also to save them to the uttermost that come unto God by him, seeing he ever liveth to make intercession for them.' And Paul wrote, 'godliness with contentment is great gain.'

TODAY: Is there a calling God has given you that you have been treating as a small thing, while wanting something else? Thank God today for the place he has given you. And pray for someone who is in danger, standing in the gap for them as Aaron did.

QUESTION: Korah was not content with his calling, and wanted more. Why do you think discontent and jealousy are so dangerous?

PRAYER: Lord God, thank you for the place you have given me. Keep me content, and free from jealousy and grasping. Thank you for Jesus, who stands between the dead and the living, and who always lives to pray for us. Help me to pray for others, even those who oppose me. Amen.
