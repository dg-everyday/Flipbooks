# Numbers 17: twelve rods with the princes' names, Aaron's name on Levi's rod, laid before the testimony, the rod whom I choose shall blossom, the next day Aaron's rod budded, bloomed and yielded almonds, the rods returned, Aaron's rod kept as a token (Hebrews 9:4), the people's fear: we perish. Avoids "morrow"-type time words outside quotes. Keys 17:5, 17:8, 17:10.
TITLE: The Rod of Aaron Was Budded

HOOK: Twelve dry sticks are laid side by side in the dark, in the holiest place of all. They are dead wood, cut from trees long ago, used as walking staffs. Each one has a name carved on it. Overnight, nothing should happen to them. But when Moses goes in the next day, one of those dead sticks is covered in buds, and flowers, and ripe almonds.

WHERE: In chapter sixteen, Korah and two hundred and fifty leaders rose up against Moses and Aaron, saying, ye take too much upon you. The ground opened, and fire came out from the LORD. When the people grumbled again, and a plague began, Aaron ran with his censer, and stood between the dead and the living, and the plague was stayed.

Now, in chapter seventeen, God settles once and for all whom he has chosen to serve as priest. Listen for how he does it. And listen for what is kept as a reminder.

STORY: After all the rebellion and grumbling, God gives a sign.

The LORD tells Moses, take a rod from each of the twelve tribes, one from the leader of each tribe. Write each man’s name on his rod. And on the rod of Levi, write Aaron’s name.

A rod was a staff, a walking stick, often carried by a leader as a sign of his authority. Each tribe’s leader would hand over his rod, with his name on it.

Then lay them in the tabernacle, in front of the ark of the testimony, where I meet with you.

And God makes a promise.

So Moses speaks to the people, and each of the leaders gives him a rod, twelve in all. And Aaron’s rod is among them. Moses lays the rods before the LORD in the tabernacle.

Imagine the leaders waiting. Each one wondering, will it be mine? Korah and his followers had claimed that anyone could serve as priest. Now God himself would decide.

The next day, Moses goes into the tabernacle.

Not just a few buds. Buds, and blossoms, and ripe almonds, all at once, on a piece of dead wood. Life where there had been no life.

The almond tree was the first tree to blossom in spring in that land. Its name in Hebrew sounds like the word for watching, or waking. It was a sign of new life, of waking up after winter.

Moses brings out all the rods from before the LORD, and shows them to all the people. Every leader looks, and takes back his own rod. Eleven of them are still just dry sticks. Only Aaron’s rod has blossomed.

No one could argue with that. God had chosen Aaron.

And then God gives a command.

So Moses does exactly as the LORD commanded. Aaron’s blossoming rod is put back in front of the ark, and kept there. The letter to the Hebrews, in the New Testament, tells us that inside the ark of the covenant were the tablets of the law, a golden pot of manna, and Aaron’s rod that budded.

But the chapter ends with the people afraid. They say to Moses, look, we are dying. We are perishing, we are all perishing. Anyone who comes near the tabernacle of the LORD will die. Are we all going to be destroyed?

After the judgment on Korah, and the plague, the people were terrified of coming near God. They swung from rebellion to fear. They needed to know how a holy God and sinful people could live together. And in the next chapter, God gives them the answer: the priests and Levites would stand between them and God, and keep them safe.

The blossoming rod was a sign of God’s choice. But it was also a sign of something more. Out of dead wood, God brought life. Many Christians have seen in it a picture of resurrection. Jesus, our great high priest, was laid in a tomb, dead. And on the third day, God raised him to life. God’s chosen priest is the one who lives again.

KEY 17:5: 'And it shall come to pass, that the man’s rod, whom I shall choose, shall blossom: and I will make to cease from me the murmurings of the children of Israel, whereby they murmur against you.' Whom I shall choose. The priesthood was not something anyone could grab. It was God’s choice. And God gave the sign so that the grumbling would stop. God is patient with our doubts, and sometimes he gives clear signs to settle our hearts.

KEY 17:8: 'And it came to pass, that on the morrow Moses went into the tabernacle of witness; and, behold, the rod of Aaron for the house of Levi was budded, and brought forth buds, and bloomed blossoms, and yielded almonds.' Budded, blossomed, and yielded almonds. All three stages of growth, overnight, from dead wood. Only God can bring life out of death. This is the God who raised Jesus from the dead, and who brings dead hearts to life.

KEY 17:10: 'And the LORD said unto Moses, Bring Aaron’s rod again before the testimony, to be kept for a token against the rebels; and thou shalt quite take away their murmurings from me, that they die not.' A token is a sign, a reminder. The rod was kept so that future generations would remember who God had chosen, and stop grumbling, that they die not. God gave the reminder not to shame them, but to save them.

THREAD: The letter to the Hebrews describes the ark, 'wherein was the golden pot that had manna, and Aaron’s rod that budded, and the tables of the covenant'. And Paul wrote that Jesus was 'declared to be the Son of God with power, according to the spirit of holiness, by the resurrection from the dead'.

TODAY: Is there an area of your life that feels dead, dry and hopeless? Bring it to God today. He is the God who makes dead wood blossom. Ask him to bring new life.

QUESTION: God made a dead stick bud, blossom and bear fruit overnight. Where do you most need God to bring new life today?

PRAYER: Lord God, you bring life out of death. Thank you for Aaron’s rod that budded, and for Jesus, who rose from the dead. Take away my grumbling and my doubts. Where my life feels dry and dead, make it blossom and bear fruit for you. Amen.
