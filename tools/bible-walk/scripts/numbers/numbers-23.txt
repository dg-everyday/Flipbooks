# Numbers 23: seven altars, the LORD put a word in Balaam's mouth, how shall I curse whom God hath not cursed, the people shall dwell alone, who can count the dust of Jacob, let me die the death of the righteous, Balak's anger, Pisgah, God is not a man that he should lie, I have received commandment to bless, the LORD his God is with him, what hath God wrought (avoids 23:24), neither curse nor bless, Peor. Keys 23:8, 23:19, 23:20.
TITLE: God Is Not a Man, That He Should Lie

HOOK: A king has paid a fortune for a curse. He has built seven altars, and offered expensive sacrifices. He stands on a mountaintop beside the most famous prophet in the land, looking down at the camp of his enemies. And he waits for the prophet to open his mouth and pronounce their doom. And what comes out is a blessing.

WHERE: In chapter twenty two, Balak, king of Moab, sent for Balaam to curse Israel. God said, thou shalt not curse the people: for they are blessed. On the way, Balaam’s donkey saw the angel of the LORD, and the LORD made the donkey speak. Then the LORD opened Balaam’s eyes, and the angel said, speak only the word that I tell you.

Now, in chapter twenty three, Balaam tries to curse Israel. Listen for what comes out of his mouth. And listen for what Balaam says about God.

STORY: Balaam says to Balak, build me seven altars here, and prepare seven bulls and seven rams. And Balak does it, and they offer a bull and a ram on each altar.

Then Balaam says, stand beside your offering, and I will go. Perhaps the LORD will come to meet me. And whatever he shows me, I will tell you. And he goes to a high place alone.

And God meets Balaam, and puts a word in his mouth. Go back to Balak, and say this.

Balaam goes back. Balak is standing beside his offering, with all the princes of Moab. And Balaam speaks.

Balak brought me from the mountains of the east, saying, come, curse Jacob for me, and denounce Israel.

And then Balaam says.

From the top of the rocks I see him, and from the hills I watch him. Look, a people who live apart, not counted among the nations. Who can count the dust of Jacob? Who can number even a fourth of Israel? 'Let me die the death of the righteous, and let my last end be like his!'

Balaam had been hired to curse. Instead, he wished he could share Israel’s blessing.

Balak is furious. What have you done to me? I brought you here to curse my enemies, and you have blessed them!

And Balaam answers, must I not be careful to speak what the LORD puts in my mouth?

So Balak tries again. Come with me to another place, where you will see only part of them, and curse them from there. Perhaps he thought that if Balaam could not see the whole camp, he might be able to curse the part he saw.

He takes Balaam to the top of Pisgah, and builds seven more altars, and offers a bull and a ram on each. And again Balaam goes to meet the LORD, and again God puts a word in his mouth.

When Balaam comes back, Balak asks eagerly, what has the LORD said?

And Balaam speaks.

And Balaam goes on.

God has not seen wickedness in Jacob, nor trouble in Israel. The LORD their God is with them, and the shout of a king is among them. God brought them out of Egypt. There is no spell that can work against Jacob, and no fortune telling against Israel. People will say of Israel, 'What hath God wrought!'

What hath God wrought, what has God done! Many years later, those very words were sent as the first message by telegraph, the first time people sent words across great distances by wire.

Balak is desperate now. Then do not curse them at all, he says, but do not bless them at all either!

Balaam says, did I not tell you that I must do everything the LORD says?

But Balak still will not give up. Come, I will take you to another place. Perhaps it will please God to let you curse them from there. And he takes Balaam to the top of Peor, overlooking the wilderness. And builds seven more altars.

Three times now, a different mountain, a different view, the same expensive sacrifices. Balak thought that if he just tried hard enough, from the right angle, he could change God’s mind. But God does not change.

KEY 23:8: 'How shall I curse, whom God hath not cursed? or how shall I defy, whom the LORD hath not defied?' How shall I curse? Balaam could not do it. No prophet, no king, no power on earth could curse those whom God had blessed. Paul wrote the same truth in the New Testament: if God be for us, who can be against us?

KEY 23:19: 'God is not a man, that he should lie; neither the son of man, that he should repent: hath he said, and shall he not do it? or hath he spoken, and shall he not make it good?' People change their minds. They break promises, and go back on their word. But God is not like that. When God says something, he does it. When he speaks, he makes it good. This is one of the greatest comforts in the Bible. Every promise God has made, he will keep.

KEY 23:20: 'Behold, I have received commandment to bless: and he hath blessed; and I cannot reverse it.' I cannot reverse it. Once God has blessed, no one can undo it. Balak’s money could not do it. Balaam’s skill could not do it. The blessing God gives to his people is secure, because it rests on God himself, not on us.

THREAD: Paul wrote, 'If God be for us, who can be against us?' And the letter to the Hebrews says that 'it was impossible for God to lie'.

TODAY: Is there a promise of God that you are struggling to believe? Remember today: God is not a man, that he should lie. Hath he said, and shall he not do it? Hold on to his promise.

QUESTION: Balak kept moving to new places, hoping God would change his mind. Why is it such a comfort that God does not change, and does not go back on his word?

PRAYER: Lord God, you are not a man, that you should lie. Thank you that every promise you have made, you will keep. Thank you that no one can curse those you have blessed. When I doubt, help me to remember: what you have said, you will do. Amen.
