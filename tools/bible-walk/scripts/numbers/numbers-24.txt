# Numbers 24: Balaam no longer seeks enchantments, sees Israel in tents by tribes, the spirit of God, how goodly are thy tents, gardens by the river, blessed is he that blesseth thee (Genesis 12:3), Balak smites his hands, a house full of silver and gold, I shall see him but not now, a Star out of Jacob and a Sceptre out of Israel (Matthew 2, Revelation 22:16), the oracles on the nations (briefly, avoids 24:8, 24:17b-24 detail), Balaam returns. Keys 24:5, 24:13, 24:17.
TITLE: There Shall Come a Star out of Jacob

HOOK: A man stands on a mountaintop, looking down at a great camp spread out across the plain below. Tents, thousands of them, arranged in perfect order around a golden tent in the middle. And as he looks, the Spirit of God comes upon him, and he sees much further than the camp. He sees far into the future. And there, far away in the distance, he sees a Star rising.

WHERE: In chapter twenty three, Balaam tried to curse Israel from one mountain, then another. But every time, God put a blessing in his mouth. How shall I curse, whom God hath not cursed? God is not a man, that he should lie. I have received commandment to bless, and I cannot reverse it.

Now, in chapter twenty four, Balaam blesses Israel a third time, and then a fourth. Listen for how he describes Israel’s tents. And listen for the Star he sees.

STORY: When Balaam sees that it pleases the LORD to bless Israel, he does not go looking for omens as he did before. He simply turns his face toward the wilderness.

And Balaam lifts up his eyes, and sees Israel camped below, tribe by tribe. And the Spirit of God comes upon him. And he speaks.

This is the word of Balaam the son of Beor, the word of the man whose eyes are open, who hears the words of God, and sees the vision of the Almighty.

And he says.

He goes on. Like valleys they stretch out, like gardens beside a river, like trees the LORD has planted, like cedars beside the waters. Water will flow from their buckets, and their seed will be in many waters. Their king will be greater than Agag, and their kingdom will be exalted. God brought them out of Egypt, and they are strong.

And then Balaam says words that God had spoken long before, to Abraham. 'Blessed is he that blesseth thee, and cursed is he that curseth thee.'

Remember, God said to Abraham, I will bless them that bless thee, and curse him that curseth thee. Balak had wanted to curse Israel. Now he was hearing that whoever curses Israel would be cursed himself.

Balak is furious. He strikes his hands together in rage. I called you to curse my enemies, and you have blessed them three times! Now get out of here, and go home. I was going to honour you greatly, but the LORD has kept you from honour.

And Balaam answers.

Then Balaam says, I am going home. But before I go, let me tell you what this people will do to your people in the days to come.

And Balaam speaks again, about something far in the future.

I shall see him, but not now. I shall behold him, but not near.

Balaam sees a king. Not one who would come soon, but one far in the future. A Star, shining in the darkness. A Sceptre, the staff of a ruler. A king who would rise out of Israel, and have dominion over his enemies.

Then Balaam looks at the nations around, Amalek, the Kenites, and others, and speaks of their future too. And then he goes home, and Balak goes his way.

Who was this Star? Some saw it as a picture of King David, who did indeed defeat Moab and Edom. But many Christians have seen it as pointing further still, to Jesus. When Jesus was born, wise men from the east, perhaps from the very region Balaam came from, saw a star, and came looking for the one born King of the Jews. And at the very end of the Bible, Jesus says, 'I am the root and the offspring of David, and the bright and morning star.'

Think about it. God used a hired prophet, a man whose heart was drawn to money, to speak one of the great prophecies of the coming King. God can speak his truth through anyone.

KEY 24:5: 'How goodly are thy tents, O Jacob, and thy tabernacles, O Israel!' Goodly means beautiful, pleasing. These were not palaces. They were tents, in the wilderness. And yet they were beautiful in God’s eyes, because they were the dwellings of his people, gathered around his presence. God sees his people with delight, even in the wilderness places of life.

KEY 24:13: 'If Balak would give me his house full of silver and gold, I cannot go beyond the commandment of the LORD, to do either good or bad of mine own mind; but what the LORD saith, that will I speak?' Not of mine own mind. Balaam said the right thing. He spoke only what God gave him. But sadly, as we will see later, his heart did not stay true. It is possible to speak God’s words, and still not love God. Our hearts matter, not just our words.

KEY 24:17: 'I shall see him, but not now: I shall behold him, but not nigh: there shall come a Star out of Jacob, and a Sceptre shall rise out of Israel, and shall smite the corners of Moab, and destroy all the children of Sheth.' A Star out of Jacob. A Sceptre out of Israel. Hundreds of years before he came, God was already promising a King. Jesus is that bright Star. He is the King of kings, whose kingdom will never end.

THREAD: Wise men came asking, 'Where is he that is born King of the Jews? for we have seen his star in the east, and are come to worship him.' And Jesus said, 'I am the root and the offspring of David, and the bright and morning star.'

TODAY: Look up at the stars when it is dark, if you can, or picture them. And thank God for Jesus, the bright Star, the King he promised long ago. Worship him today, as the wise men did.

QUESTION: God promised a Star out of Jacob, hundreds of years before Jesus was born. How does it strengthen your faith to see God’s promises fulfilled across so many years?

PRAYER: Lord Jesus, you are the bright Star out of Jacob, the King God promised long ago. Thank you that God’s promises always come true. Thank you that your people are beautiful in your sight, even in the wilderness. Help me to speak your truth, and to love you with all my heart. Amen.
