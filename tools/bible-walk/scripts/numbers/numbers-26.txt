# Numbers 26: the second census in the plains of Moab after the plague, tribe by tribe (Korah's sons died not, Zelophehad's daughters named, Serah), the total similar to Sinai, the land divided by lot and by size, the Levites counted (Amram, Jochebed, Aaron, Moses, Miriam), not a man left of the first count save Caleb and Joshua. Keys 26:53, 26:64, 26:65.
TITLE: Save Caleb the Son of Jephunneh, and Joshua the Son of Nun

HOOK: Imagine a school photograph, taken on the first day. Rows and rows of faces. Now imagine another photograph, taken many years later, of the same school. The building is the same. The number of people is almost the same. But every single face has changed. Except two. That is what happens in Numbers chapter twenty six.

WHERE: In chapter twenty five, Israel was drawn into the worship of Baal at Peor, through the women of Moab. A plague came, and Phinehas, zealous for his God, stood up for God’s honour, and the plague was stayed. God gave Phinehas a covenant of peace. And we learned that Balaam had been behind the scheme.

Now, in chapter twenty six, after the plague, God tells Moses to count the people again. Listen for how the second count compares with the first. And listen for the two names that appear in both.

STORY: After the plague, the LORD speaks to Moses and to Eleazar, the son of Aaron the priest.

Notice the change. At the first count, in chapter one, it was Moses and Aaron. Now Aaron is gone, and his son Eleazar stands beside Moses. A new generation is taking over.

God says, count the whole congregation of Israel, from twenty years old and upward, by their families, all who are able to go to war. So Moses and Eleazar count them, on the plains of Moab, by the Jordan, across from Jericho.

Then the writer goes through the tribes, just as in chapter one. But this time, the families within each tribe are named too.

Reuben, with his families. And the writer reminds us that among them were Dathan and Abiram, who rebelled with Korah, and were swallowed by the earth. 'Notwithstanding the children of Korah died not.' Korah’s sons were spared. Later, the sons of Korah would become singers in the temple, and some of the psalms are written by them.

Then Simeon, Gad, Judah, Issachar, Zebulun. Then the sons of Joseph, Manasseh and Ephraim. In the tribe of Manasseh, the writer names a man called Zelophehad, who had no sons, only daughters: Mahlah, Noah, Hoglah, Milcah and Tirzah. Keep those names in mind. We will hear from these five sisters in the next chapter.

Then Benjamin, Dan, Asher, and Naphtali. In the tribe of Asher, the writer even mentions a daughter named Serah.

Some tribes have grown, and some have shrunk. Simeon is much smaller than before. Manasseh has grown. But when all the numbers are added together, the total is almost exactly the same as at Sinai, more than six hundred thousand.

All those years in the wilderness, and the nation had not grown. But it had not disappeared either. God had kept his people alive, even through their wanderings and failures.

Then God tells Moses how the land will be shared. To the larger tribes, give a larger share. To the smaller ones, a smaller share. And it will be divided by lot, according to the names of the tribes.

Then the Levites are counted, family by family. And the writer pauses to name one family in particular. Amram, of the tribe of Levi, married Jochebed, and they had three children: Aaron, Moses, and their sister Miriam. And Aaron’s sons were Nadab, Abihu, Eleazar and Ithamar.

The Levites were counted separately again, because they would receive no land.

And then the chapter ends with the most important verses of all.

All of them. The whole generation who came out of Egypt as adults, who saw the Red Sea part, who heard God speak at Sinai, who refused to enter the land at Kadesh. Every one of them gone, in the wilderness, just as God had said.

Except two.

Caleb and Joshua. The only two spies who said, we are well able to overcome it. The only two who trusted God when everyone else was afraid. And they are still alive, standing with the new generation, on the edge of the land.

KEY 26:53: 'Unto these the land shall be divided for an inheritance according to the number of names.' Unto these. Not to the generation that refused, but to their children. The promise God had made did not fail. It passed to the next generation. God’s purposes do not depend on one generation’s faithfulness. When one generation fails, God raises up another.

KEY 26:64: 'But among these there was not a man of them whom Moses and Aaron the priest numbered, when they numbered the children of Israel in the wilderness of Sinai.' Not a man. Every name from the first count was gone. God’s word had come true, exactly as he said. His warnings are as sure as his promises. That is sobering. But it also means we can trust everything he says.

KEY 26:65: 'For the LORD had said of them, They shall surely die in the wilderness. And there was not left a man of them, save Caleb the son of Jephunneh, and Joshua the son of Nun.' Save Caleb and Joshua. Faithfulness matters. The two men who trusted God when it was hardest outlived their whole generation, and lived to see the promise. God remembers those who follow him fully.

THREAD: The letter to the Hebrews says, 'Let us therefore fear, lest, a promise being left us of entering into his rest, any of you should seem to come short of it.' And a psalm says, 'The LORD knoweth the days of the upright: and their inheritance shall be for ever.'

TODAY: Think about the generation coming after you, children, grandchildren, younger believers. Pray for them by name today, that they will trust God and follow him fully, like Caleb and Joshua.

QUESTION: Only Caleb and Joshua were left from the first count. What do you think it was about them that made the difference?

PRAYER: Lord God, thank you that your promises pass from one generation to the next, and never fail. Make me faithful, like Caleb and Joshua, trusting you when others are afraid. Bless the generation coming after me, and help them to follow you fully. Amen.
