# Numbers 34: when ye come into the land of Canaan, the borders south (wilderness of Zin, Kadesh-barnea, river of Egypt), west (the great sea), north (mount Hor, Hamath), east (sea of Chinnereth, Jordan, the salt sea), the nine and a half tribes by lot, Reuben, Gad and half Manasseh already settled, Eleazar and Joshua with a prince from each tribe (Caleb named first). Keys 34:2, 34:17, 34:29.
TITLE: This Shall Be Your Land

HOOK: Imagine someone giving you a gift, and before you even open it, they show you exactly where its edges are. This part is yours. All the way to here. And here. And here. That is what God does in Numbers chapter thirty four. Israel is still camped on the wrong side of the river. But God takes them around the edges of the land, as if to say: look. All of this is yours.

WHERE: In chapter thirty three, Moses wrote down every stop of the journey, from Rameses through the sea, to Marah and the twelve springs of Elim, to Sinai, Kadesh and mount Hor, and on to the plains of Moab. And God said, I have given you the land to possess it.

Now, in chapter thirty four, God marks out the borders of the land. Listen for how far it reaches. And listen for the names of the men who will share it out.

STORY: The LORD speaks to Moses.

When ye come. Not if. When. After forty years of wandering, God speaks with complete certainty. You will come into the land. This is what it will look like.

Then God traces the borders, side by side.

The south border begins at the wilderness of Zin, by the edge of Edom. It runs from the southern end of the Salt Sea, past Kadesh-barnea, the very place where the spies had brought back their report, and where Israel had refused to go in. Now it is part of the border of the land they will receive. It runs on to the river of Egypt, and ends at the sea.

The west border is simple. The great sea, the Mediterranean, and its coast.

The north border runs from the great sea to mount Hor, a different mountain from the one where Aaron died, in the far north. Then on toward the entrance of Hamath, and further north still.

The east border runs down from the north, to the shore of the sea of Chinnereth. That is the lake later called the Sea of Galilee. Then down along the river Jordan, to the Salt Sea.

This shall be your land, God says, with its borders all around.

Then Moses tells the people, this is the land you will inherit by lot, which the LORD has commanded to give to the nine and a half tribes. Reuben, Gad, and half of Manasseh had already received their land, on the east side of the Jordan.

Then God names the men who will divide the land. Eleazar the priest, and Joshua the son of Nun.

Not Moses. Moses would not cross the Jordan. The work of dividing the land would fall to the next generation of leaders.

And God says, take one leader from each tribe to help divide the land. And he names them, one by one.

The very first name on the list, for the tribe of Judah, is Caleb the son of Jephunneh.

Caleb. The faithful spy, who said, let us go up at once, for we are well able to overcome it. Long ago, he had walked through this very land. Now he would help share it out. And as we heard in the book of Joshua, old Caleb would ask for the mountain of Hebron, and receive it.

Then the other leaders are named, from Simeon, Benjamin, Dan, Manasseh, Ephraim, Zebulun, Issachar, Asher and Naphtali. Names we never hear again, but each one trusted to lead his tribe into its inheritance.

Think about how this chapter must have felt to the people of Israel. For their whole lives, they had lived in tents, moving from place to place. Now, God was drawing the outline of their home. Rivers and mountains, seas and valleys. A real place, with real edges, given by God.

The New Testament tells us that Abraham lived in tents in this very land, looking for a city which hath foundations, whose builder and maker is God. Israel was about to receive a land. But all God’s people look forward to an even better home, a heavenly one, that God has prepared for them.

KEY 34:2: 'Command the children of Israel, and say unto them, When ye come into the land of Canaan; (this is the land that shall fall unto you for an inheritance, even the land of Canaan with the coasts thereof:)' When ye come. God’s promises are not maybes. After all the failures, all the years of wandering, God still spoke with certainty. When we doubt God’s promises, it helps to remember that he speaks of them as already decided.

KEY 34:17: 'These are the names of the men which shall divide the land unto you: Eleazar the priest, and Joshua the son of Nun.' A priest and a leader, side by side. Eleazar would seek God’s guidance, and Joshua would lead the people. God’s work needs both: those who seek God’s face, and those who lead his people forward.

KEY 34:29: 'These are they whom the LORD commanded to divide the inheritance unto the children of Israel in the land of Canaan.' Whom the LORD commanded. Every leader was chosen by God, by name. The dividing of the land would not be a scramble for the best parts. It would be done fairly, by God’s appointed people, by lot, before the LORD.

THREAD: The letter to the Hebrews says of Abraham, 'For he looked for a city which hath foundations, whose builder and maker is God.' And Jesus said, 'In my Father’s house are many mansions'.

TODAY: Think about the home God has promised all who belong to him, the city whose builder and maker is God. Thank him today that your future with him is certain. Not if, but when.

QUESTION: God spoke of the land with certainty: when ye come. Which of God’s promises do you most need to hear spoken with that kind of certainty today?

PRAYER: Lord God, thank you that your promises are certain. Thank you for the land you gave your people, and for the home you have prepared for all who belong to you. Help me to trust you, and to look forward to the city whose builder and maker is God. Amen.
