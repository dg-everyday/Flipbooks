# Numbers 35: cities for the Levites with their pasture, forty and eight in all, six cities of refuge (three each side of Jordan) for one who caused a death by accident, for Israel and the stranger, the difference between hatred and accident (told gently, no slayer/murderer/killeth outside quotes; avoids 35:11-12, 35:15-21, 35:27, 35:30-31), the congregation judges, until the death of the high priest, more than one witness, defile not the land wherein I dwell (Joshua 20, Hebrews 6:18). Keys 35:2, 35:7, 35:34.
TITLE: For I the LORD Dwell Among the Children of Israel

HOOK: A man is running for his life. Behind him, someone is chasing him, angry and determined. The man did something terrible, but he never meant to. It was an accident. Up ahead, on a hill, he sees the walls of a city. If he can just reach the gate, he will be safe. This is a city God himself set apart, for exactly this moment.

WHERE: In chapter thirty four, God marked out the borders of the land, from the wilderness of Zin to the great sea, and from Hamath to the Jordan and the Salt Sea. When ye come into the land, God said, this shall be your land. And he named Eleazar and Joshua, and a leader from each tribe, with Caleb first, to divide it.

Now, in chapter thirty five, God provides homes for the Levites, and places of safety for anyone in danger. Listen for how many cities there are. And listen for the reason God gives at the end.

STORY: The LORD speaks to Moses on the plains of Moab.

The Levites would have no land of their own. But they still needed somewhere to live, and somewhere for their animals to graze. So God tells the other tribes to give them towns, with fields around them. The tribes with more land would give more towns, and those with less would give fewer.

How many towns? Six of them would be cities of refuge, and forty two more besides.

Forty eight towns, spread all through the land. So in every tribe, there would be Levites living nearby, people whose lives were devoted to serving God, and teaching his law.

Then God speaks about the six cities of refuge.

When you come into the land, God says, choose cities to be places of refuge. They are for anyone who causes someone’s death by accident, without meaning to. They can flee there, and be safe from the relative of the one who died, who might want revenge, until the congregation can judge the case fairly.

Three cities on the east side of the Jordan, and three in the land of Canaan. Spread out, so that no one would be too far from safety. And they were not only for Israelites. The foreigner and the traveller living among them could flee there too.

Then God explains the difference between an accident and a deliberate crime. If someone attacks another person in hatred, or lies in wait for them, and they die, that is a deliberate crime, and it must be punished. But if it happens suddenly, without any hatred, by accident, without intending harm, then the congregation must protect that person, and send them back to the city of refuge.

God cared about justice. A life had been lost, and that mattered greatly. But he also cared about the person who had not meant any harm. He did not want one death to lead to another.

The person who fled would stay in the city of refuge until the death of the high priest. Then they could go home, in safety.

And God adds an important rule. No one could be condemned on the word of only one witness. There had to be more than one. God protected people from false accusations.

Remember, in the book of Joshua, chapter twenty, the people set apart these six cities, just as God commanded here. Kedesh, Shechem and Hebron on the west. Bezer, Ramoth and Golan on the east.

And then God gives the deepest reason for all of this.

The land was not just a place to live. It was the place where God himself lived, among his people. So it was to be a place of justice and mercy, where life was precious, and where blood was not shed carelessly.

Many Christians have seen the cities of refuge as a picture of Jesus. We have all done wrong, and we need somewhere to run. The letter to the Hebrews speaks of us as those who have fled for refuge, to lay hold of the hope set before us. And just as the death of the high priest set the refugee free, the death of Jesus, our great high priest, sets us free.

KEY 35:2: 'Command the children of Israel, that they give unto the Levites of the inheritance of their possession cities to dwell in; and ye shall give also unto the Levites suburbs for the cities round about them.' The people were to give from their own inheritance, to provide for those who served God. Everyone shared in caring for the Levites. God’s people have always been called to support those who give their lives to serving him.

KEY 35:7: 'So all the cities which ye shall give to the Levites shall be forty and eight cities: them shall ye give with their suburbs.' Forty eight cities, scattered through every tribe. God wanted his servants spread throughout the land, so that no part of Israel would be without people who knew and taught his word. God still scatters his people, so that his light can shine in every place.

KEY 35:34: Keep the land holy, God says, the land 'wherein I dwell: for I the LORD dwell among the children of Israel.' I the LORD dwell among the children of Israel. That was the heart of everything. The God who filled the tabernacle with his glory, who led them by the cloud, would live among them in the land. And so they were to keep it holy, full of justice and mercy.

THREAD: The letter to the Hebrews speaks of us as those 'who have fled for refuge to lay hold upon the hope set before us'. And a psalm says, 'God is our refuge and strength, a very present help in trouble.'

TODAY: Run to God today, your refuge, with whatever weighs on your conscience. And think about how you could be a place of safety and kindness for someone who is struggling.

QUESTION: God provided cities of refuge so that no one was too far from safety. What does it mean to you that God is your refuge?

PRAYER: Lord God, you are my refuge and strength, a very present help in trouble. Thank you that you care about justice and mercy, and that you dwell among your people. Thank you for Jesus, our great high priest, whose death sets us free. I run to you now. Amen.
