# Numbers 36: the heads of Gilead's families ask about Zelophehad's daughters' land if they marry outside the tribe (the jubilee), the tribe of the sons of Joseph hath said well, let them marry to whom they think best within their father's tribe, so did the daughters of Zelophehad (Mahlah, Tirzah, Hoglah, Milcah, Noah), the inheritance stays; the closing verse; looking back over Numbers and ahead to Deuteronomy. Keys 36:5, 36:10, 36:13.
TITLE: Even as the LORD Commanded Moses, So Did the Daughters of Zelophehad

HOOK: A family gathers to talk about something important. Not a battle, or a miracle, but land, and marriage, and making sure everything is fair. It might seem a quiet, ordinary way to end a book full of fiery serpents, talking donkeys and water from rocks. But the book of Numbers ends with five sisters doing exactly what God asked. And that is a beautiful way to end.

WHERE: In chapter thirty five, God gave the Levites forty eight cities, and set apart six cities of refuge, so that anyone who caused a death by accident could flee there and be safe. And God said, keep the land holy, 'for I the LORD dwell among the children of Israel.'

Now we come to the last chapter of Numbers. Listen for the question the family asks. And listen for how the daughters of Zelophehad respond.

STORY: The leaders of the families of Gilead, from the tribe of Manasseh, come to Moses and the leaders of Israel.

Remember the daughters of Zelophehad? In chapter twenty seven, these five sisters asked for their father’s land, because he had no sons. And God said, they speak right, and gave them their inheritance.

Now their relatives come with a question. The LORD commanded you to give our brother Zelophehad’s land to his daughters. But what if they marry men from another tribe? Then their land will go with them to that tribe, and our tribe will lose it. And at the year of jubilee, when land returned to its families, it would be counted as belonging to the other tribe for ever.

It was a fair question. God had promised each tribe its own land. If land passed from tribe to tribe through marriage, over time the tribes’ inheritances would be broken up and lost.

And Moses gives the answer, from the LORD.

And God gives the solution.

Let them marry whoever they choose. The sisters were free to choose their husbands. But they should marry within their father’s tribe, so that the land stays with the tribe of Manasseh.

And God makes it a rule for all Israel. Every daughter who inherits land should marry within her father’s tribe, so that every tribe keeps its own inheritance, and the land does not move from one tribe to another.

Think about how carefully God cared for this one family. First, he made sure the sisters were not left out, and received their father’s land. Then, he made sure that their tribe did not lose its share. God cared about the sisters, and about their family, and about the whole tribe. No one was forgotten.

And then the five sisters respond.

Mahlah, Tirzah, Hoglah, Milcah and Noah, the daughters of Zelophehad, marry their cousins, sons of their father’s brothers, in the families of Manasseh. And their inheritance stays in their father’s tribe.

These five sisters had been brave enough to ask for what was right. And now they were humble enough to obey God’s answer. Courage and obedience, together.

And then the book ends with a single verse.

And so the book of Numbers comes to a close. Think about how far we have come. The book began at Mount Sinai, with God counting his people, every one by name, and arranging them around his tabernacle. We heard the beautiful blessing: the LORD bless thee, and keep thee. We followed the cloud into the wilderness.

And then we saw the grumbling, again and again. The quail. The twelve spies, and the people who would not go in. The years of wandering. Korah’s rebellion. Moses striking the rock. The fiery serpents, and the bronze serpent lifted up. Balaam, who could not curse God’s people. And the sad failure at Peor.

But through it all, God never gave up on his people. He fed them, gave them water, forgave them, and kept his promise. A whole generation passed away in the wilderness. But a new generation stands now on the plains of Moab, looking across the Jordan at Jericho, at the land God promised to Abraham, long before.

And the next book, Deuteronomy, is Moses’ last words to this new generation, before they cross over.

KEY 36:5: 'And Moses commanded the children of Israel according to the word of the LORD, saying, The tribe of the sons of Joseph hath said well.' Hath said well. Just as God said the daughters spoke right, now he says their relatives spoke well too. God listens to his people’s questions. He did not mind being asked. And his answers were fair to everyone. We can bring our honest questions to God.

KEY 36:10: 'Even as the LORD commanded Moses, so did the daughters of Zelophehad:' What a way to end a book full of disobedience. Again and again in Numbers, the people grumbled and rebelled. But here, at the end, five sisters simply do what God says. Even as the LORD commanded, so they did. It is a picture of what God longs for: a people who trust him, and obey.

KEY 36:13: 'These are the commandments and the judgments, which the LORD commanded by the hand of Moses unto the children of Israel in the plains of Moab by Jordan near Jericho.' The book ends on the plains of Moab, by the Jordan, near Jericho. So close. The promise is right there, across the river. God has brought his people through the whole wilderness, and set them at the edge of the land. What God begins, he finishes.

THREAD: Paul wrote, 'Being confident of this very thing, that he which hath begun a good work in you will perform it until the day of Jesus Christ'. And he wrote, 'Now all these things happened unto them for ensamples: and they are written for our admonition'.

TODAY: Is there something God has clearly asked you to do? Do it today, simply and gladly, like the daughters of Zelophehad. And thank God that he finishes what he begins.

QUESTION: Numbers is full of grumbling and rebellion, but it ends with five sisters obeying God. What does this whole book teach you about God, and about following him?

PRAYER: Lord God, thank you for walking with me through the book of Numbers. Thank you that you never gave up on your people in the wilderness, and that you never give up on me. Forgive my grumbling. Help me to trust you, and to obey you, even as you command. And bring me safely home. Amen.
