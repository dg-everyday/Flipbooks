# Philippians 1: greeting, thanks and joy in prayer, he which hath begun a good work, Paul's prayer that love abound, chains that spread the gospel, preachers with mixed motives, to live is Christ, a strait betwixt two, conversation worthy of the gospel, striving together. Keys 1:6, 1:21, 1:27. 1:8 not quoted ("bowels").
TITLE: To Live Is Christ

HOOK: A guard sits beside a prisoner, chained to him, hour after hour. The prisoner is not like the others. He does not curse or complain. He prays. He writes letters. He talks about a man named Jesus, who died and rose again. And when the guard’s shift is over, another guard takes his place, and hears it all over again. Before long, the whole guard of the palace knows why this man is in chains.

WHERE: In the introduction, we heard how the church in Philippi began: with Lydia by the river, and a jailer who asked, what must I do to be saved? We heard that Paul is in prison again, and that the Philippians have sent Epaphroditus with a gift. And we heard three things to watch for: joy, the mind of Christ, and contentment.

Now, in chapter one, Paul opens his letter. Listen for how often he talks about joy, even in chains. And listen for how he can face either life or death without fear.

STORY: Paul begins, 'Paul and Timotheus, the servants of Jesus Christ, to all the saints in Christ Jesus which are at Philippi'. Timotheus is Timothy, Paul’s young helper, who was with him when the church began. And saints here means all God’s people, every believer.

Then Paul tells them how he feels about them. 'I thank my God upon every remembrance of you, Always in every prayer of mine for you all making request with joy'. Every time I think of you, I thank God. Every time I pray for you, I pray with joy. Because they have been partners with him in the gospel from the very first day.

And Paul is sure of something.

Paul says he holds them in his heart. Whether he is in chains or defending the gospel, they share in God’s grace with him. And he longs to see them.

Then he tells them what he prays for them. 'And this I pray, that your love may abound yet more and more in knowledge and in all judgment'. Not just more love, but wise love. Love that knows what is truly best, so that they may live pure lives, full of the good fruit that comes through Jesus Christ.

Then Paul gives them news. They might be worried about him. But he says, 'I would ye should understand, brethren, that the things which happened unto me have fallen out rather unto the furtherance of the gospel'. Furtherance means moving forward. Being in prison has not stopped the good news. It has spread it. The whole palace guard has heard why Paul is in chains. And many believers have become braver, and are speaking God’s word without fear.

It is true, Paul says, that some are preaching Christ for the wrong reasons, out of jealousy and rivalry, hoping to make things harder for him. Others preach out of love. And Paul’s answer is remarkable. 'What then? notwithstanding, every way, whether in pretence, or in truth, Christ is preached; and I therein do rejoice, yea, and will rejoice.' Paul does not care about his own reputation. As long as people hear about Jesus, he is glad.

Then Paul faces the biggest question of all. Will he live, or will he die? He does not know what the court will decide. But he is not afraid. His one hope is that Christ will be honoured in his body, whether by life, or by death.

And then he writes these famous words.

Paul is torn between the two. 'For I am in a strait betwixt two, having a desire to depart, and to be with Christ; which is far better'. A strait is a narrow place, a hard choice. To depart means to die and go to be with Jesus. That, he says, is far better. But staying alive means he can keep helping the Philippians. So he is confident that he will stay, for their sake, and help them grow in their faith and joy.

And then, whatever happens to him, he has one request for them.

He tells them to stand firm, together, and not to be frightened by those who oppose them. And he says something surprising. 'For unto you it is given in the behalf of Christ, not only to believe on him, but also to suffer for his sake'. Suffering for Jesus is not a sign that God has forgotten them. It is part of following him, and they are walking the same road Paul walks.

KEY 1:6: 'Being confident of this very thing, that he which hath begun a good work in you will perform it until the day of Jesus Christ'. God started something good in the Philippians, the day Lydia’s heart was opened by the river. And Paul is sure that God will finish what he started. God never leaves a job half done. If he has begun a good work in you, he will carry it on, through good days and hard ones, until the day Jesus returns.

KEY 1:21: 'For to me to live is Christ, and to die is gain.' For Paul, life meant Jesus. Knowing him, serving him, telling others about him. And death? Death would only bring him closer to Jesus. So he had nothing to lose. If Jesus is your life, then even death cannot take what matters most away from you. It can only bring you home.

KEY 1:27: 'Only let your conversation be as it becometh the gospel of Christ: that whether I come and see you, or else be absent, I may hear of your affairs, that ye stand fast in one spirit, with one mind striving together for the faith of the gospel'. Conversation here means the way you live. Becometh means fits, or matches. Live in a way that matches the good news. Whether Paul is there to see them or not, he wants them to stand together, like a team, working side by side for the gospel. A life that matches the gospel is a life lived in unity with God’s people.

THREAD: Paul wrote to another church, 'And we know that all things work together for good to them that love God'. And he said we are 'willing rather to be absent from the body, and to be present with the Lord.'

TODAY: Is there something hard in your life right now that God might use to spread the good news, or to make you stronger? Ask him to help you see it the way Paul did. And thank God for someone who has been a partner with you in faith.

QUESTION: Paul said, for to me to live is Christ. If you finished that sentence honestly, for me to live is, what would you say? And what would you like to say?

PRAYER: Lord God, thank you that you have begun a good work in me, and that you will finish it. Help me to live in a way that matches the gospel, and to stand together with your people. Like Paul, let me be able to say, for to me to live is Christ, and to die is gain. Amen.
