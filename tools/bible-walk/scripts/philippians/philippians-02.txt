# Philippians 2: be of one mind, esteem others better, look on the things of others, the mind of Christ (the hymn), every knee shall bow, work out your salvation / God worketh in you, without murmurings, shine as lights, poured out, Timothy, Epaphroditus nigh unto death. Keys 2:3, 2:8, 2:13. 2:1 not quoted ("bowels").
TITLE: Let This Mind Be in You

HOOK: A king takes off his crown, and lays it down. He takes off his royal robes, and puts on the plain clothes of a servant. He walks out of the palace, and into the poorest streets of the city. He washes the feet of tired travellers, and feeds the hungry, and sits with the sick. No one recognises him. That is only a picture. But Paul tells us that something far greater than this really happened.

WHERE: In chapter one, Paul wrote from prison, full of joy. He thanked God for the Philippians, and was sure that he which hath begun a good work in them would finish it. His chains had spread the gospel through the palace. And he said, for to me to live is Christ, and to die is gain.

Now, in chapter two, Paul asks the Philippians to live in humble unity. Listen for one of the greatest passages ever written about Jesus. And listen for two friends whom Paul loved.

STORY: Paul begins with a tender appeal. If you have found any comfort in Christ, any comfort in his love, any fellowship in the Spirit, then make my joy complete. 'Fulfil ye my joy, that ye be likeminded, having the same love, being of one accord, of one mind.'

How can God’s people live like that? Paul tells them.

And he goes on. 'Look not every man on his own things, but every man also on the things of others.' Do not only think about what you need. Think about what others need too.

Then Paul points them to the greatest example of all. 'Let this mind be in you, which was also in Christ Jesus'. And what follows sounds like a song, or a poem. Many think the first Christians may have sung it.

'Who, being in the form of God, thought it not robbery to be equal with God: But made himself of no reputation, and took upon him the form of a servant, and was made in the likeness of men'. Jesus was God. He shared all the glory of heaven. But he did not hold on to it. He emptied himself, and became a servant. The Creator became one of his own creatures.

And he went further still. Further down than anyone has ever gone.

But that is not the end of the song. Because Jesus went so low, God lifted him high. 'Wherefore God also hath highly exalted him, and given him a name which is above every name: That at the name of Jesus every knee should bow, of things in heaven, and things in earth, and things under the earth; And that every tongue should confess that Jesus Christ is Lord, to the glory of God the Father.' One day, everyone will bow before Jesus. Every person, in every place, will say, Jesus Christ is Lord.

So, Paul says, keep on obeying, just as you always have. 'work out your own salvation with fear and trembling.' Work it out does not mean work for it, or earn it. It means live it out. Let what God has done inside you show on the outside. With fear and trembling means with deep respect for God, taking it seriously.

And then Paul tells them how it is possible.

Then he gives some very practical advice. 'Do all things without murmurings and disputings'. Murmurings means grumbling. Disputings means arguing. Do everything without complaining or quarrelling. Why? 'That ye may be blameless and harmless, the sons of God, without rebuke, in the midst of a crooked and perverse nation, among whom ye shine as lights in the world'. In a dark world, God’s children shine like stars in the sky, just by living differently. And a cheerful, thankful person who does not grumble stands out.

Paul says that even if his life is poured out like an offering, he is glad, and he rejoices with them. And they should rejoice with him.

Then Paul talks about two friends. First, Timothy. Paul hopes to send him soon. 'For I have no man likeminded, who will naturally care for your state.' Everyone else, Paul says, looks out for themselves. But Timothy has served with Paul like a son with his father. He truly cares.

And then Epaphroditus, the man the Philippians had sent to help Paul. Paul calls him 'my brother, and companion in labour, and fellow soldier'. He had been very sick, close to death. 'but God had mercy on him; and not on him only, but on me also, lest I should have sorrow upon sorrow.' Now Epaphroditus is homesick, and worried because his friends heard that he was ill. So Paul is sending him home. Welcome him, Paul says, with joy, and honour people like him. He risked his life for the work of Christ.

Timothy and Epaphroditus are pictures of the mind of Christ. They put other people first.

KEY 2:3: 'Let nothing be done through strife or vainglory; but in lowliness of mind let each esteem other better than themselves.' Strife means rivalry. Vainglory means showing off, wanting to look important. Lowliness of mind means humility. Esteem means to value. Do not do things to beat others, or to make yourself look good. Instead, humbly treat other people as more important than yourself. That is not thinking badly of yourself. It is thinking of yourself less, and of others more.

KEY 2:8: 'And being found in fashion as a man, he humbled himself, and became obedient unto death, even the death of the cross.' Jesus did not only become a man. He became a servant. And he did not only serve. He obeyed his Father all the way to death. And not just any death, but the death of the cross, the most shameful death there was, kept for slaves and criminals. He went all the way down, for us. When we find it hard to put others first, we look at the cross.

KEY 2:13: 'For it is God which worketh in you both to will and to do of his good pleasure.' Here is the secret. We are told to work out our salvation. But God is the one working in us. He gives us the wanting, and he gives us the doing. When you want to do what is right, that desire is God at work in you. And he gives the strength to do it. We do not live the Christian life alone. God is working inside us, every day.

THREAD: Jesus said, 'For even the Son of man came not to be ministered unto, but to minister, and to give his life a ransom for many.' And he said to his followers, 'Ye are the light of the world.'

TODAY: Look for one chance today to put someone else first. Let someone go ahead of you, help without being asked, or listen instead of talking. And try going a whole day without grumbling. Notice how it changes you.

QUESTION: Jesus humbled himself, and became a servant. Where in your life is it hardest for you to put others first, and what would it look like to have the mind of Christ there?

PRAYER: Lord Jesus, thank you that you left the glory of heaven, became a servant, and died on the cross for me. Let your mind be in me. Take away my pride and my grumbling. Help me to put others first, and to shine like a light in this world. And thank you that you are working in me, to will and to do your good pleasure. Amen.
