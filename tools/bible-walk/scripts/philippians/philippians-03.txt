# Philippians 3: rejoice in the Lord, a warning about those who trusted in rules and ceremonies (circumcision verses 3:2-5 retold, not quoted), Paul's record, gain counted loss, righteousness by faith, to know him, not already perfect, forgetting what is behind, press toward the mark, follow good examples, enemies of the cross, our conversation is in heaven, the changed body. Keys 3:7, 3:10, 3:14. 3:8 not quoted ("dung"); 3:19 not quoted.
TITLE: I Press Toward the Mark

HOOK: A runner comes round the last bend of a long race. Her legs are aching, and her chest is burning. She does not look back to see who is behind her. She does not think about the bad start she made. Her eyes are fixed on one thing, the finish line ahead. She leans forward, and pushes with everything she has. Paul says the Christian life is like that race.

WHERE: In chapter two, Paul told us to have the mind of Christ. Jesus, who was equal with God, made himself a servant, and became obedient unto death, even the death of the cross. And God has given him the name which is above every name. Paul told us to do all things without grumbling, and to shine as lights in the world.

Now, in chapter three, Paul tells his own story. Listen for what he once was proud of, and what he thinks of it now. And listen for a runner, straining toward the finish.

STORY: Paul begins, 'Finally, my brethren, rejoice in the Lord.' Joy again. And then he gives a warning.

Some teachers were going round the churches, saying that believers from other nations must keep all the old Jewish rules and ceremonies to be right with God. Faith in Jesus was not enough, they said. You had to add your own good record too. Paul says, beware of them. True worship is not about outward ceremonies. It is to 'worship God in the spirit, and rejoice in Christ Jesus, and have no confidence in the flesh.' The flesh here means our own human efforts.

Then Paul says, if anyone could be proud of their own record, it was me. And he lists it. He was born into God’s people, Israel, from the tribe of Benjamin, a true Hebrew. He kept the law as a Pharisee, the strictest group of all. He was so zealous that he persecuted the church. And as far as keeping the rules went, no one could find fault with him.

And then he tells us what he thinks of all that now.

And he goes further. He counts everything as loss, compared with the joy of knowing Christ Jesus his Lord. For Christ, he has lost everything, and he counts it all as rubbish, so that he may gain Christ, and 'be found in him, not having mine own righteousness, which is of the law, but that which is through the faith of Christ, the righteousness which is of God by faith'. Paul’s own goodness could never make him right with God. Only the goodness that God gives, through faith in Jesus.

What does Paul want most? He tells us.

But Paul does not pretend he has arrived. 'Not as though I had already attained, either were already perfect: but I follow after'. He has not reached the finish yet. He is still growing, still running. Jesus took hold of him on the road to Damascus, and now Paul wants to take hold of everything Jesus has for him.

'Brethren, I count not myself to have apprehended: but this one thing I do, forgetting those things which are behind, and reaching forth unto those things which are before'. Forgetting what is behind. For Paul, that meant his old pride, and also his old shame. He had once hurt God’s people. But he did not let his past hold him back. He kept looking forward.

And then comes the finish line.

Paul tells the Philippians to follow his example, and the example of others who live this way. Because sadly, not everyone does. With tears, Paul says that many live as enemies of the cross of Christ. They live for themselves, for their appetites and for the things of this world, and they are proud of things they should be ashamed of.

But that is not who we are. 'For our conversation is in heaven; from whence also we look for the Saviour, the Lord Jesus Christ'. Conversation here means citizenship. Philippi was a Roman colony, and its people were proud to be citizens of Rome, even though they lived far away from it. Paul says, you belong to a greater city. Your true home is in heaven. And from there, Jesus will come. He will change our weak bodies to be like his glorious body, by the same power that rules over all things.

KEY 3:7: 'But what things were gain to me, those I counted loss for Christ.' Paul pictures an accountant with two columns, profit and loss. All the things he used to put in the profit column, his family, his religion, his good record, he moved over to the loss column. Not because they were evil, but because compared with knowing Jesus, they were worth nothing. When you have found the greatest treasure, everything else looks small.

KEY 3:10: 'That I may know him, and the power of his resurrection, and the fellowship of his sufferings, being made conformable unto his death'. Paul had known about Jesus for years. But he wanted to know him more, as a friend. To know the power that raised Jesus from the dead, working in his own life. And even to share in his sufferings, becoming more like him. Knowing Jesus is not something we finish. It is a friendship that grows deeper for the rest of our lives.

KEY 3:14: 'I press toward the mark for the prize of the high calling of God in Christ Jesus.' The mark is the finish line, the goal. Press means to strain, to push hard. Paul is like the runner who will not slow down until he crosses the line. And the prize is the upward call of God, to be with Jesus for ever. The Christian life is not a stroll. It is a race. But it is a race with a wonderful prize waiting at the end.

THREAD: The letter to the Hebrews says, 'let us run with patience the race that is set before us, Looking unto Jesus the author and finisher of our faith'. And Jesus said, 'For where your treasure is, there will your heart be also.'

TODAY: Is there something in your past, a failure or a regret, that keeps holding you back? Bring it to God, and leave it with him. And is there anything you are holding on to more tightly than Jesus? Ask him to help you count it as loss, so that you can know him better.

QUESTION: Paul said, this one thing I do: forgetting what is behind, I press toward the mark. What do you need to leave behind, and what will help you keep running toward Jesus?

PRAYER: Lord Jesus, I want to know you, and the power of your resurrection. Thank you that I am made right with God, not by my own goodness, but through faith in you. Help me to forget what is behind, and to press on toward the prize. Thank you that my true home is in heaven, and that one day you will come for me. Amen.

NEXT: Next on the walk, Philippians chapter four, the last chapter of the letter. Rejoice in the Lord alway. Be careful for nothing. Think on these things. And the secret of being content in every situation.
