# Philippians 4: stand fast, Euodias and Syntyche, rejoice alway, moderation, be careful for nothing, the peace of God, think on these things, the God of peace, the gift flourished again, content in whatsoever state, I can do all things (in context), they alone gave, my God shall supply, Caesar's household; looking back over Philippians. Keys 4:7, 4:13, 4:19.
TITLE: The Peace of God

HOOK: A guard stands at the gate of a city, hour after hour, watching. No enemy can slip past him. No one gets in without his say. The people inside can sleep soundly, because they know the gate is guarded. Paul says that God’s peace can stand guard like that, over our hearts and our minds.

WHERE: In chapter three, Paul told his own story. Everything he had once been proud of, he counted as loss, for the excellency of knowing Christ. He longed to know him, and the power of his resurrection. And like a runner, forgetting what is behind, he pressed toward the mark. Our citizenship, he said, is in heaven.

Now we come to the last chapter of the letter. Listen for joy, and peace, and a cure for worry. And listen for the secret of being content.

STORY: Paul begins with great tenderness. 'Therefore, my brethren dearly beloved and longed for, my joy and crown, so stand fast in the Lord, my dearly beloved.'

Then he names two women in the church, Euodias and Syntyche. They had worked hard beside Paul to spread the gospel. But now they disagree, and Paul begs them to be of the same mind in the Lord. He asks a faithful friend in the church to help them make peace. Even good, faithful people can fall out. And Paul does not take sides. He reminds them that their names are written together in the book of life.

Then, from his prison, Paul writes, 'Rejoice in the Lord alway: and again I say, Rejoice.' Alway means always. Not only when things go well. Always. And in case they missed it, he says it twice.

'Let your moderation be known unto all men. The Lord is at hand.' Moderation here means gentleness. Let everyone see that you are gentle and reasonable. The Lord is near.

Then comes Paul’s cure for worry. 'Be careful for nothing; but in every thing by prayer and supplication with thanksgiving let your requests be made known unto God.' In the King James Bible, careful means full of care, anxious. Do not be anxious about anything. Instead, pray about everything. Supplication means asking humbly. And notice, with thanksgiving. Thank God while you ask him.

And when we do, here is the promise.

Then Paul tells them what to fill their minds with. 'Finally, brethren, whatsoever things are true, whatsoever things are honest, whatsoever things are just, whatsoever things are pure, whatsoever things are lovely, whatsoever things are of good report; if there be any virtue, and if there be any praise, think on these things.' What we think about shapes who we become. So fill your mind with what is good.

And then, Paul says, put into practice what you have learned and seen in me, 'and the God of peace shall be with you.' First the peace of God. Now the God of peace himself.

Then Paul thanks them for their gift. He rejoiced greatly that they were able to help him again. But he wants them to understand something. 'Not that I speak in respect of want: for I have learned, in whatsoever state I am, therewith to be content.' Content means satisfied, at peace with what you have. And notice, Paul says, I have learned it. It did not come naturally. 'I know both how to be abased, and I know how to abound'. Abased means brought low, having little. Abound means having plenty. Paul has known full stomachs and empty ones, comfort and hardship. And he has learned the secret of being content in all of it.

What is the secret? Paul tells us in one of the most famous verses in the Bible.

Still, Paul says, it was kind of you to share in my troubles. He reminds them that when the gospel first came to them, they were the only church that gave to support him. Even when he moved on to the next city, they sent help again and again. He is not looking for more gifts, he says. He wants them to be blessed for their giving. Their gift was like a sweet smelling offering to God, a sacrifice that pleased him.

And then he gives them a promise.

Paul ends with greetings. All God’s people here send you greetings, he says, 'chiefly they that are of Caesar’s household.' Caesar was the emperor of Rome. Even some in the emperor’s household had become believers. Paul’s chains had carried the gospel right into the palace. 'The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you all. Amen.'

And that is the letter to the Philippians. Written in chains, and full of joy. Joy in prayer, joy in Christ, joy in serving others, joy in pressing on, and joy that does not depend on what we have.

KEY 4:7: 'And the peace of God, which passeth all understanding, shall keep your hearts and minds through Christ Jesus.' Passeth all understanding means it is greater than we can explain. Our problems may not disappear. But God’s peace comes in, and stands guard over our hearts and minds, like a soldier at the gate. Worry tries to get in, and God’s peace keeps it out. That is God’s promise to everyone who brings their cares to him in prayer.

KEY 4:13: 'I can do all things through Christ which strengtheneth me.' Many people know this verse. But notice where Paul says it. He is talking about being content, whether he has plenty or nothing. It does not mean we can do anything we want, like win every game. It means that in every situation God puts us in, rich or poor, sick or well, free or in chains, Christ gives us the strength to face it. The secret is not in us. It is in him.

KEY 4:19: 'But my God shall supply all your need according to his riches in glory by Christ Jesus.' The Philippians had given generously to meet Paul’s needs. Now Paul says, my God will meet all of yours. Not all our wants, but all our needs. And notice how much he gives: according to his riches in glory. Not a little, from a small purse, but from the endless riches of heaven. We can never out give God.

THREAD: Isaiah wrote, 'Thou wilt keep him in perfect peace, whose mind is stayed on thee: because he trusteth in thee.' And Peter wrote, 'Casting all your care upon him; for he careth for you.'

TODAY: What are you worried about today? Write it down, or say it out loud, and turn it into a prayer. Ask God for what you need, and thank him for something too. Then choose one thing that is true, or lovely, or good, and think on it.

QUESTION: Paul learned to be content in every situation, because Christ gave him strength. What is one worry you can turn into a prayer with thanksgiving today?

PRAYER: Lord God, thank you for walking with me through the letter to the Philippians. Thank you that I can rejoice in you always. I bring you my worries now, with thanksgiving. Let your peace, which passes all understanding, guard my heart and mind. Teach me to be content, and remind me that I can do all things through Christ who strengthens me. Amen.
