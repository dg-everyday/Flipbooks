# Proverbs 6: surety for a friend (co-signing a debt) and how to get free, go to the ant thou sluggard, a little sleep a little slumber, the naughty person who sows discord, six things the LORD hates yea seven, keep thy father's commandment and thy mother's law, the commandment is a lamp, kept from the evil woman (6:25-35 told gently as faithfulness in marriage; 6:26, 6:29, 6:32 not quoted), can a man take fire in his bosom. Keys 6:6, 6:16, 6:23.
TITLE: Go to the Ant, Thou Sluggard

HOOK: Look down at the ground on a warm day, and you may see a line of tiny ants, marching along. Each one is carrying something: a crumb, a seed, a piece of leaf, bigger than itself. No one is shouting orders at them. No one is checking their work. They just keep going, back and forth, storing food for the winter. Proverbs says, if you want to learn about hard work, watch the ant.

WHERE: In chapter five, a father spoke to his son about faithfulness in marriage. Temptation is sweet as honey at first, but bitter as wormwood at the end. Drink waters out of thine own cistern, he said, and rejoice with the wife of thy youth. For the ways of man are before the eyes of the LORD.

Now, in chapter six, the father gives advice on several topics. Listen for a little teacher in the dust. And listen for seven things that God hates.

STORY: The father begins with money. 'My son, if thou be surety for thy friend, if thou hast stricken thy hand with a stranger, Thou art snared with the words of thy mouth'. To be surety means to promise to pay someone else’s debt if they cannot. To strike hands was like shaking hands on a deal. The father says, if you have promised to pay for someone else’s loan, you are trapped by your own words.

What should you do? Go at once, humble yourself, and ask to be released. Do not sleep until you have dealt with it. Get free, like a deer escaping from a hunter, or a bird from a trap. Proverbs warns again and again about being careless with debts and promises.

Then comes the famous little teacher.

The ant has no boss, no supervisor, no ruler telling her what to do. And yet she gathers her food in the summer, and stores it up at harvest time. She works hard, and plans ahead.

Then the father speaks to the sluggard, the lazy person. 'How long wilt thou sleep, O sluggard? when wilt thou arise out of thy sleep?' And he imitates the lazy person’s excuses. 'Yet a little sleep, a little slumber, a little folding of the hands to sleep'. Just five more minutes. Just a little longer. And then the warning. 'So shall thy poverty come as one that travelleth, and thy want as an armed man.' Poverty creeps up on the lazy like a thief, and attacks like an armed robber.

Then the father describes a troublemaker. A worthless person, he says, who goes around with a twisted mouth. He winks his eyes, signals with his feet, and points with his fingers, sending secret messages. His heart is full of plots, and he is always stirring up trouble, and sowing discord, setting people against each other. But disaster will come upon him suddenly.

And that leads to a famous list.

'A proud look, a lying tongue, and hands that shed innocent blood, An heart that deviseth wicked imaginations, feet that be swift in running to mischief, A false witness that speaketh lies, and he that soweth discord among brethren.'

Notice how the list moves through the body. Eyes, tongue, hands, heart, feet. And it ends with the troublemaker the father has just described, the one who sows discord among brothers. God hates it when people tear families and friendships apart.

Then the father returns to the teaching of father and mother. 'My son, keep thy father’s commandment, and forsake not the law of thy mother'. Keep it always on your heart, and around your neck. When you walk, it will guide you. When you sleep, it will watch over you. And when you wake up, it will talk with you.

And then a beautiful verse.

Once again, the father warns his son about the temptation to be unfaithful in marriage. Do not let someone’s beauty capture your heart, or their glances draw you in, he says. And then he asks two questions. 'Can a man take fire in his bosom, and his clothes not be burned? Can one go upon hot coals, and his feet not be burned?' Of course not. If you play with fire, you get burned. Unfaithfulness in marriage brings wounds and shame that are very hard to wipe away, and anger and hurt that are very hard to heal.

So the chapter covers money, work, troublemakers, the things God hates, and faithfulness. Wisdom touches every part of life.

KEY 6:6: 'Go to the ant, thou sluggard; consider her ways, and be wise'. A sluggard is a lazy person. Consider means study, think about. God made even the tiny ant to teach us something. The ant works without being told, prepares for the future, and keeps going. Laziness is not a small thing. It wastes the time and gifts God gives us. Wise people work hard, even when no one is watching.

KEY 6:16: 'These six things doth the LORD hate: yea, seven are an abomination unto him'. An abomination is something God finds disgusting, deeply wrong. Six things, yes, seven, is a way of saying, here is a list, and the last one is the worst. Pride, lying, violence, wicked plans, eagerness to do wrong, false witness, and stirring up trouble between brothers. God loves people, and so he hates the things that destroy people and relationships.

KEY 6:23: 'For the commandment is a lamp; and the law is light; and reproofs of instruction are the way of life'. A lamp shows you where to put your feet in the dark. God’s commands are like that. They show us the way. And even reproofs, corrections, which do not always feel nice, are the way of life. When someone corrects us in love, they are helping us find the right path.

THREAD: Paul wrote, 'if any would not work, neither should he eat.' And he wrote, 'And whatsoever ye do, do it heartily, as to the Lord, and not unto men'.

TODAY: Is there a task you have been putting off, saying, just a little longer? Go to the ant, and get started today. And check your own life against the seven things God hates. Is there anything you need to turn away from?

QUESTION: The ant works hard without anyone telling her to. What helps you to work hard and do your best, even when no one is watching?

PRAYER: Lord God, thank you for the little ant, and what she teaches me. Forgive me for laziness, and for putting things off. Keep me from the things you hate: pride, lies, wicked plans, and stirring up trouble. Let your commandments be a lamp to my feet, and help me to accept correction as the way of life. Amen.

NEXT: Next on the walk, Proverbs chapter seven. A father looks out of his window, and sees a young man walking towards trouble. Keep my commandments, and live, he says. Write them on the table of your heart.
