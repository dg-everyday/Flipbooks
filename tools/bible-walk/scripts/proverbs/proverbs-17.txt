# Proverbs 17: better a dry morsel with quietness, the fining pot and the furnace (the LORD trieth the hearts), whoso mocketh the poor, children's children the crown of old men, he that covereth a transgression seeketh love, a reproof into a wise man, a bear robbed of her whelps rather than a fool, rewarding evil for good, the beginning of strife as letting out water, a friend loveth at all times, a merry heart doeth good like a medicine, the eyes of a fool in the ends of the earth, he that hath knowledge spareth his words, even a fool when he holdeth his peace is counted wise. Keys 17:3, 17:17, 17:22.
TITLE: A Friend Loveth at All Times

HOOK: When everything is going well, you have lots of friends. People want to sit with you, play with you, be seen with you. But then something goes wrong. You get sick, or into trouble, or just have a really bad time. And suddenly, most of those friends disappear. But one stays. One is still there, beside you. Proverbs says, that is a true friend.

WHERE: In chapter sixteen, we heard, commit thy works unto the LORD. A man’s heart deviseth his way, but the LORD directeth his steps. And pride goeth before destruction, and an haughty spirit before a fall.

Now, in chapter seventeen, Solomon’s sayings go on. Listen for what Proverbs says about friendship. And listen for some sayings about laughter, anger and silence.

STORY: The chapter begins with another better saying. 'Better is a dry morsel, and quietness therewith, than an house full of sacrifices with strife.' A dry morsel is a piece of plain, dry bread. An house full of sacrifices means a house full of feasting, because meat from the sacrifices was often eaten at feasts. It is better to eat dry bread in peace than to have a great feast with arguing and fighting. A peaceful home is worth more than a full table.

Then a picture from a metalworker’s workshop.

'Whoso mocketh the poor reproacheth his Maker'. We heard something like this in chapter fourteen. Making fun of the poor is like making fun of God, who made them.

Then a lovely saying about families. 'Children’s children are the crown of old men; and the glory of children are their fathers.' Grandchildren make grandparents proud, like a crown. And children are proud of good parents. Families can bring honour to each other across the generations.

Then a saying about forgiveness and gossip. 'He that covereth a transgression seeketh love; but he that repeateth a matter separateth very friends.' Someone who forgives an offence, and does not keep talking about it, builds love. But someone who keeps bringing it up, and telling others, separates even close friends.

'A reproof entereth more into a wise man than an hundred stripes into a fool.' A wise person learns more from one gentle correction than a fool learns from a hundred beatings.

Then a funny but serious picture. 'Let a bear robbed of her whelps meet a man, rather than a fool in his folly.' Whelps are cubs. A mother bear whose cubs have been taken is one of the most dangerous animals on earth. But Proverbs says it would be better to meet her than to meet a fool in the middle of his foolishness! Foolishness can do even more damage.

'Whoso rewardeth evil for good, evil shall not depart from his house.' Someone who repays kindness with harm brings trouble on their own home.

Then a wise picture about arguments. 'The beginning of strife is as when one letteth out water: therefore leave off contention, before it be meddled with.' Imagine a small crack in a dam. At first, just a trickle of water. But it grows and grows, until the whole dam bursts. Arguments are like that. Stop them while they are still small.

Then comes one of the most loved sayings about friendship.

'A man void of understanding striketh hands, and becometh surety in the presence of his friend.' Proverbs warns again about promising to pay other people’s debts.

'He loveth transgression that loveth strife'. Someone who loves arguing loves sin.

Then sayings about the joy and grief children bring their parents. A foolish son brings sorrow to his father, and bitterness to his mother.

And then a famous saying about joy.

'Wisdom is before him that hath understanding; but the eyes of a fool are in the ends of the earth.' A wise person sees what is right in front of them. A fool’s eyes are always wandering, looking far away, never focused.

And the chapter ends with two sayings about silence. 'He that hath knowledge spareth his words: and a man of understanding is of an excellent spirit.' Excellent spirit here means a calm and cool spirit. 'Even a fool, when he holdeth his peace, is counted wise: and he that shutteth his lips is esteemed a man of understanding.' Even a foolish person seems wise, if they keep quiet! Sometimes the wisest thing we can say is nothing.

KEY 17:3: 'The fining pot is for silver, and the furnace for gold: but the LORD trieth the hearts.' A fining pot is a pot for melting silver. The heat brings impurities to the top, so they can be removed. A furnace does the same for gold. God tests our hearts in a similar way. Hard times can be like the fire, bringing out what is really inside us. And God uses those times to make us purer and stronger, like refined gold.

KEY 17:17: 'A friend loveth at all times, and a brother is born for adversity.' Adversity means hard times, trouble. A true friend does not only love when things are easy. They love at all times, in good days and bad. And a brother, a true family member, is there especially for the hard times. Jesus is the greatest friend of all. He loves at all times, and he never leaves us.

KEY 17:22: 'A merry heart doeth good like a medicine: but a broken spirit drieth the bones.' A merry heart is a cheerful, joyful heart. It is good for us, like medicine. Laughter and joy really do help our bodies and minds. But a broken, crushed spirit can make us weak and dry inside. God wants his people to have joyful hearts. And when someone’s spirit is broken, we can help bring them joy again.

THREAD: The Bible says of David and Jonathan, 'the soul of Jonathan was knit with the soul of David'. And Paul wrote, 'Rejoice with them that do rejoice, and weep with them that weep.'

TODAY: Think of someone who is going through a hard time. Be the kind of friend who loves at all times. Send a message, pay a visit, or simply be there. And if there is a small argument starting in your life, stop it before the dam bursts.

QUESTION: Proverbs says, a friend loveth at all times. Who has been that kind of friend to you, and for whom could you be that kind of friend?

PRAYER: Lord God, thank you for the friends who have loved me at all times. Help me to be a true friend, who stays when others leave. Test my heart, and make it pure, like gold in the furnace. Give me a merry heart, and help me to know when to keep quiet. And thank you for Jesus, the friend who never leaves me. Amen.
