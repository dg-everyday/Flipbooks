# Proverbs 19: better the poor in integrity, he that hasteth with his feet sinneth, his heart fretteth against the LORD, wealth maketh many friends, a false witness shall not be unpunished, the poor forsaken by friends, he that getteth wisdom loveth his own soul, the discretion of a man deferreth his anger, the king's wrath and favour, a continual dropping, a prudent wife is from the LORD, slothfulness casteth into deep sleep, he that hath pity upon the poor lendeth unto the LORD, chasten thy son (19:18, brief), hear counsel, the counsel of the LORD shall stand, a poor man better than a liar, the slothful man's hand in the dish (bosom), cease to hear instruction that causeth to err. Keys 19:11, 19:17, 19:21.
TITLE: He That Hath Pity upon the Poor Lendeth unto the LORD

HOOK: Imagine lending some money to the richest, most trustworthy person in the world. Someone who has never broken a promise, and who always pays back more than they borrowed. You would not worry about it at all. Proverbs says that when we help a poor person, it is like lending to God himself. And God always repays.

WHERE: In chapter eighteen, we heard that the name of the LORD is a strong tower, and the righteous run into it and are safe. Death and life are in the power of the tongue. And there is a friend that sticketh closer than a brother.

Now, in chapter nineteen, Solomon’s sayings go on. Listen for what Proverbs says about helping the poor. And listen for who really has the last word about our plans.

STORY: The chapter begins with another better saying. 'Better is the poor that walketh in his integrity, than he that is perverse in his lips, and is a fool.' It is better to be poor and honest than to be a lying fool.

'Also, that the soul be without knowledge, it is not good; and he that hasteth with his feet sinneth.' Rushing into things without thinking leads us into trouble. Enthusiasm is good, but it needs wisdom.

Then a very honest saying. 'The foolishness of man perverteth his way: and his heart fretteth against the LORD.' People make foolish choices, which ruin their lives, and then they blame God and get angry with him. How often we blame God for troubles we brought on ourselves.

Then sayings about friends and money. 'Wealth maketh many friends; but the poor is separated from his neighbour.' Rich people attract lots of friends, though many are only interested in their money. Poor people often find themselves alone. Even their relatives avoid them, Proverbs says, and their friends keep their distance. Proverbs sees how unfair people can be. And it wants us to be different.

'A false witness shall not be unpunished, and he that speaketh lies shall not escape.' Lies catch up with liars. This saying appears twice in this chapter, because it matters.

'He that getteth wisdom loveth his own soul'. Getting wisdom is one of the best ways to love yourself.

Then comes a saying about anger and forgiveness.

'The king’s wrath is as the roaring of a lion; but his favour is as dew upon the grass.' An angry king is frightening, like a lion. But a kind king is refreshing, like dew on the grass.

Then sayings about home. 'A foolish son is the calamity of his father: and the contentions of a wife are a continual dropping.' Contentions are quarrels. A continual dropping is like a leaking roof, drip, drip, drip, all day long. Constant arguing at home, from anyone, wears everyone down. 'House and riches are the inheritance of fathers and a prudent wife is from the LORD.' A house can be inherited from parents. But a wise husband or wife is a gift from God.

'Slothfulness casteth into a deep sleep; and an idle soul shall suffer hunger.' Laziness makes people sleepy and dull, and leaves them hungry.

Then comes a beautiful promise about the poor.

'Chasten thy son while there is hope'. Correct your children while they are young, while there is still time to shape them. As we heard earlier, loving correction is a sign of love, not hatred.

'Hear counsel, and receive instruction, that thou mayest be wise in thy latter end.' Listen to advice now, and you will be wise when you are old.

Then a verse about God’s purposes.

'The desire of a man is his kindness: and a poor man is better than a liar.' What we should most want is to be kind. And it is better to be poor than to be a liar.

'The fear of the LORD tendeth to life: and he that hath it shall abide satisfied'. Honouring God leads to life and deep contentment.

Then a very funny picture of a lazy man. 'A slothful man hideth his hand in his bosom, and will not so much as bring it to his mouth again.' Bosom here means the dish. Picture a man so lazy that he puts his hand in the bowl of food, and then cannot be bothered to lift it to his mouth! Laziness can get so bad that people will not even do the things that are good for them.

And near the end, a warning to the father’s son. 'Cease, my son, to hear the instruction that causeth to err from the words of knowledge.' Stop listening to teaching that leads you away from the truth. Be careful who you listen to.

KEY 19:11: 'The discretion of a man deferreth his anger; and it is his glory to pass over a transgression.' Discretion means good sense. Deferreth means delays, holds back. A wise person does not explode with anger. They slow down. And it is their glory, their beauty, to overlook an offence, to let it go. Some people think it is weak to forgive. Proverbs says it is glorious. It is like God himself, who is slow to anger and quick to forgive.

KEY 19:17: 'He that hath pity upon the poor lendeth unto the LORD; and that which he hath given will he pay him again.' When we help someone poor, God takes it personally. It is as if we have given him a loan. And God promises to repay. Not always with money, but always with blessing. No kindness to the poor is ever wasted. God sees it, and he remembers.

KEY 19:21: 'There are many devices in a man’s heart; nevertheless the counsel of the LORD, that shall stand.' Devices means plans. We make many plans. Some work out, and some do not. But God’s purpose always stands. Nothing can stop what he has decided. That is a great comfort. Even when our plans fall apart, God’s good plan for his people never fails.

THREAD: The psalmist wrote, 'The counsel of the LORD standeth for ever'. And Paul wrote, 'Forbearing one another, and forgiving one another'.

TODAY: Is there an offence you could overlook today, letting it go instead of holding on to it? It is your glory. And look for a way to show pity on someone poor this week. You will be lending to the LORD.

QUESTION: Proverbs says it is a glory to pass over a transgression. Why do you think forgiving can be harder than staying angry, and why does God call it glorious?

PRAYER: Lord God, thank you that your purpose always stands, even when my plans fail. Give me good sense, to hold back my anger, and the grace to overlook offences. Give me a heart of pity for the poor. And thank you that you are slow to anger and quick to forgive, and that every kindness done for you is remembered. Amen.
