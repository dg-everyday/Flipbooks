# Proverbs 21: the king's heart in the hand of the LORD, every way right in his own eyes but the LORD pondereth the hearts, justice more acceptable than sacrifice, a proud heart, the diligent and the hasty, treasures by a lying tongue, better a corner of the housetop (and the wilderness) than a quarrelsome house, he that stoppeth his ears at the cry of the poor, joy to the just to do judgment, he that loveth pleasure, treasure in the dwelling of the wise, he that followeth after righteousness and mercy, a wise man scaleth the city of the mighty, whoso keepeth his mouth, the slothful's desire (21:25 not quoted), the righteous giveth and spareth not, no wisdom against the LORD, the horse prepared but safety is of the LORD. Keys 21:1, 21:3, 21:31.
TITLE: The King’s Heart Is in the Hand of the LORD

HOOK: A farmer digs little channels in his field, to bring water from the river to his crops. With a few strokes of his spade, he can turn the water this way, or that way, wherever he wants it to go. The water does not choose. The farmer does. Proverbs says that even the most powerful king in the world is like that water, in the hands of God.

WHERE: In chapter twenty, we heard that wine is a mocker. Who can say, I have made my heart clean? Say not, I will repay evil, but wait on the LORD. And the spirit of man is the candle of the LORD, searching all the inward parts.

Now, in chapter twenty one, Solomon’s sayings go on. Listen for who is really in control, even of kings. And listen for what God wants more than sacrifices.

STORY: The chapter begins with a great statement about God’s power.

'Every way of a man is right in his own eyes: but the LORD pondereth the hearts.' We heard this in chapter sixteen. We always think we are right. But God looks deeper, at our hearts.

Then a verse about what God really wants.

'An high look, and a proud heart, and the plowing of the wicked, is sin.' An high look means looking down on others. Pride, even in ordinary work, is sin.

'The thoughts of the diligent tend only to plenteousness; but of every one that is hasty only to want.' Careful, hardworking people end up with plenty. People who rush into things end up with nothing.

'The getting of treasures by a lying tongue is a vanity tossed to and fro of them that seek death.' Riches gained by lying are like a puff of smoke, blown about, and gone.

Then a funny saying, which appears twice in this chapter. 'It is better to dwell in a corner of the housetop, than with a brawling woman in a wide house.' In those days, houses had flat roofs. Living alone in a cramped corner up on the roof, in the wind and rain, would be better than living in a big house full of arguing. And again, 'It is better to dwell in the wilderness, than with a contentious and an angry woman.' Of course, the same is true of a quarrelsome man. The point is that constant quarrelling makes a home miserable, however big it is. Peace is worth more than space.

'The soul of the wicked desireth evil: his neighbour findeth no favour in his eyes.' A wicked person does not care about his neighbours at all.

Then a very serious warning. 'Whoso stoppeth his ears at the cry of the poor, he also shall cry himself, but shall not be heard.' If we refuse to listen when the poor cry for help, one day we may cry out, and find no one listening to us.

'It is joy to the just to do judgment: but destruction shall be to the workers of iniquity.' Good people enjoy doing what is right. It is not a burden to them. It is a joy.

'He that loveth pleasure shall be a poor man: he that loveth wine and oil shall not be rich.' Someone who lives only for fun and luxury will end up poor.

'There is treasure to be desired and oil in the dwelling of the wise; but a foolish man spendeth it up.' Wise people save. Foolish people spend everything they get.

Then a beautiful promise. 'He that followeth after righteousness and mercy findeth life, righteousness, and honour.' Those who chase after doing right and being kind find much more than they were looking for.

'A wise man scaleth the city of the mighty, and casteth down the strength of the confidence thereof.' Wisdom is more powerful than strength. A wise person can capture a city that strong soldiers trusted in.

'Whoso keepeth his mouth and his tongue keepeth his soul from troubles.' Once again, guarding our words protects us.

Then a contrast between the lazy and the generous. The lazy person wants things all day long, but his hands refuse to work. 'but the righteous giveth and spareth not.' The righteous person gives generously, and does not hold back.

'The sacrifice of the wicked is abomination'. God does not want gifts from people who are living wickedly, and who think they can buy his favour.

'There is no wisdom nor understanding nor counsel against the LORD.' No one can outsmart God. No plan can succeed against him.

And the chapter ends with a wonderful saying about trust.

KEY 21:1: 'The king’s heart is in the hand of the LORD, as the rivers of water: he turneth it whithersoever he will.' Whithersoever means wherever. Even the most powerful ruler on earth is not out of God’s control. God can turn a king’s heart as easily as a farmer turns a stream. We saw this with Cyrus, who sent the exiles home, and with Artaxerxes, who helped Nehemiah. When we worry about leaders and governments, we can pray. Their hearts are in God’s hands.

KEY 21:3: 'To do justice and judgment is more acceptable to the LORD than sacrifice.' Justice and judgment mean doing what is right and fair. Some people thought that if they offered enough sacrifices, God would not mind how they lived. But God wants obedient hearts more than religious offerings. He wants us to treat people fairly and live rightly. Our worship means nothing if our lives do not match it.

KEY 21:31: 'The horse is prepared against the day of battle: but safety is of the LORD.' Safety here means victory, deliverance. An army gets its horses ready for battle. That is sensible. Proverbs does not say, do not prepare. But in the end, victory comes from the LORD. We should work hard and prepare well. But we should never trust in our preparation more than in God. He gives the victory.

THREAD: Hosea heard God say, 'For I desired mercy, and not sacrifice'. And Samuel told Saul, 'Behold, to obey is better than sacrifice'.

TODAY: Is there a leader you are worried about? Pray for them today. Their heart is in God’s hands. And think about whether your everyday life matches your worship. Is there something fair or right you need to do?

QUESTION: Proverbs says, the horse is prepared against the day of battle, but safety is of the LORD. How can you prepare well for something, while still trusting God rather than your preparation?

PRAYER: Lord God, the hearts of kings are in your hand, and nothing is out of your control. Help me to do what is just and right, not just to go through religious motions. Open my ears to the cry of the poor. Help me to prepare well, and to work hard, but to trust you for the victory. Amen.
