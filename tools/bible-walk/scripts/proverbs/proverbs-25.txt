# Proverbs 25: proverbs of Solomon copied out by the men of Hezekiah (2 Kings), the glory of God to conceal and the honour of kings to search out, dross from the silver, put not forth thyself before the king (Luke 14:10), go not forth hastily to strive, debate thy cause with thy neighbour, a word fitly spoken, a wise reprover, a faithful messenger like the cold of snow in harvest, clouds and wind without rain, a soft tongue breaketh the bone, honey in moderation (25:16 not quoted), withdraw thy foot from thy neighbour's house, false witness like a maul, a broken tooth, singing songs to an heavy heart, feed thine enemy (coals of fire; Romans 12:20), cold waters and good news, no rule over his own spirit. Keys 25:11, 25:21, 25:28.
TITLE: A Word Fitly Spoken

HOOK: Picture a beautiful silver bowl, with delicate carvings all around it. And inside, a pile of golden apples, gleaming in the light. It is lovely to look at. Everything fits together perfectly. Proverbs says that is what it is like when someone says exactly the right thing, at exactly the right time.

WHERE: In chapter twenty four, we heard that through wisdom is an house builded. A just man falleth seven times, and riseth up again. Rejoice not when thine enemy falleth. And we walked past a lazy man’s field, overgrown with thorns, with its wall broken down.

Now, in chapter twenty five, a new section of the book begins. Listen for who collected these proverbs. And listen for what to do when your enemy is hungry.

STORY: The chapter begins with a heading. 'These are also proverbs of Solomon, which the men of Hezekiah king of Judah copied out.' Do you remember king Hezekiah, from second Kings? He trusted the LORD like no king before him. And here we learn that his scribes collected and copied out more of Solomon’s proverbs. Hundreds of years after Solomon, Hezekiah’s men treasured his wise sayings, and made sure they were not lost.

The first sayings are about kings. 'It is the glory of God to conceal a thing: but the honour of kings is to search out a matter.' God does not tell us everything. There are mysteries only he knows. But it is the job of good rulers to search out the truth, and get to the bottom of things.

'Take away the dross from the silver, and there shall come forth a vessel for the finer.' Dross is the rubbish that rises when silver is melted. When it is removed, a craftsman can make something beautiful. In the same way, remove wicked advisers from a king, and his rule will be good.

Then some advice about humility. 'Put not forth thyself in the presence of the king, and stand not in the place of great men: For better it is that it be said unto thee, Come up hither; than that thou shouldest be put lower'. Do not push yourself forward into the most important seat. It is far better to be invited up than to be sent down in front of everyone. Jesus told a parable with exactly the same lesson.

'Go not forth hastily to strive'. Do not rush to start a quarrel, or to take someone to court. You may end up embarrassed. 'Debate thy cause with thy neighbour himself'. If you have a problem with someone, talk to them directly, and do not spread their secrets to others.

Then comes a beautiful saying about words.

'As an earring of gold, and an ornament of fine gold, so is a wise reprover upon an obedient ear.' When a wise person corrects someone who is willing to listen, it is like putting on beautiful gold jewellery.

'As the cold of snow in the time of harvest, so is a faithful messenger to them that send him: for he refresheth the soul of his masters.' Imagine working in a hot field at harvest, and being given something icy cold. That is how refreshing a reliable person is.

'Whoso boasteth himself of a false gift is like clouds and wind without rain.' Someone who promises gifts and never gives them is like clouds that look like rain, in a dry land, but bring nothing.

'By long forbearing is a prince persuaded, and a soft tongue breaketh the bone.' Patience can persuade even a ruler. And gentle words can break through the hardest resistance.

'Hast thou found honey? eat so much as is sufficient for thee'. Enjoy good things, but do not overdo them. Too much of a good thing makes you sick.

'Withdraw thy foot from thy neighbour’s house; lest he be weary of thee, and so hate thee.' Do not visit your neighbours too often, or stay too long. Even good friends need space.

'A man that beareth false witness against his neighbour is a maul, and a sword, and a sharp arrow.' A maul is a heavy hammer. Lies about someone are as harmful as weapons.

'Confidence in an unfaithful man in time of trouble is like a broken tooth, and a foot out of joint.' Relying on someone untrustworthy when you need them is painful, like biting on a broken tooth.

'As he that taketh away a garment in cold weather, and as vinegar upon nitre, so is he that singeth songs to an heavy heart.' Singing cheerful songs to someone who is grieving is like taking away their coat on a cold day. Be sensitive to how people feel.

Then comes a surprising command.

'As cold waters to a thirsty soul, so is good news from a far country.' How refreshing it is to hear good news from someone far away.

'It is not good to eat much honey: so for men to search their own glory is not glory.' Chasing after praise for ourselves is not glorious.

And the chapter ends with a picture of a city.

KEY 25:11: 'A word fitly spoken is like apples of gold in pictures of silver.' Fitly spoken means the right word at the right time. Pictures of silver means carved silver settings. A well chosen word, spoken at just the right moment, is beautiful and precious, like golden apples in a silver bowl. Think of a time someone said exactly what you needed to hear. We can be that for others.

KEY 25:21: 'If thine enemy be hungry, give him bread to eat; and if he be thirsty, give him water to drink'. The next verse says that by doing this, you will heap coals of fire upon his head, and the LORD will reward you. That means your kindness may make him burn with shame, and perhaps change his heart. Paul quoted these very words in his letter to the Romans. This is the way of Jesus: not revenge, but kindness, even to enemies.

KEY 25:28: 'He that hath no rule over his own spirit is like a city that is broken down, and without walls.' In those days, a city without walls was helpless. Any enemy could walk right in. Someone who cannot control their temper, their appetite, or their words is like that. They are defenceless. Every temptation can walk right in. Self control is like a strong wall, protecting our lives.

THREAD: Jesus said, 'when thou art bidden, go and sit down in the lowest room'. And Paul wrote, 'Be not overcome of evil, but overcome evil with good.'

TODAY: Look for a chance to speak a word fitly today, the right word at the right moment. And is there someone who has treated you badly? Think of one practical kindness you could show them.

QUESTION: Proverbs says, if thine enemy be hungry, give him bread. Why do you think kindness can be more powerful than revenge?

PRAYER: Lord God, give me words fitly spoken, like apples of gold. Teach me humility, so that I do not push myself forward. Help me to be faithful and reliable, refreshing to others. Give me the strength to be kind to my enemies. And help me to rule my own spirit, so that my life is like a city with strong walls. Amen.
