# Proverbs 27: boast not thyself of to morrow (inside the quote), let another man praise thee, a fool's wrath heavier than stone and sand, who can stand before envy, open rebuke better than secret love, faithful are the wounds of a friend, the full and the hungry soul, a bird that wandereth from her nest, the sweetness of a friend's counsel, thy father's friend forsake not (a neighbour near better than a brother far off), a prudent man foreseeth the evil, the loud blessing (27:14 paraphrased), a continual dropping, iron sharpeneth iron, whoso keepeth the fig tree, as in water face answereth to face, the eyes of man never satisfied (27:20 not quoted), praise as a fining pot, know the state of thy flocks (riches are not for ever). Keys 27:1, 27:6, 27:17.
TITLE: Iron Sharpeneth Iron

HOOK: A man sits on a step, sharpening a knife. He takes a long iron rod and draws the blade across it, again and again, at an angle. Sparks fly. There is a scraping sound. It looks almost as if the two pieces of metal are fighting. But when he finishes, the knife is sharp enough to cut through anything. That is what a good friend does for us, Proverbs says.

WHERE: In chapter twenty six, we heard groups of sayings about fools, lazy people and gossips. There is more hope of a fool than of someone wise in his own eyes. Where there is no wood, the fire goes out, and where there is no gossip, the quarrel stops. And whoever digs a pit will fall into it.

Now, in chapter twenty seven, the sayings go on. Listen for a warning about making plans. And listen for several sayings about friendship.

STORY: The chapter begins with a famous warning.

'Let another man praise thee, and not thine own mouth; a stranger, and not thine own lips.' Do not go around praising yourself. If you are good at something, let others say so.

'A stone is heavy, and the sand weighty; but a fool’s wrath is heavier than them both.' Carrying a heavy stone or a bag of sand is hard work. But putting up with a fool’s anger is even harder.

'Wrath is cruel, and anger is outrageous; but who is able to stand before envy?' Anger is bad. But jealousy can be even more destructive.

Then a saying about honesty. 'Open rebuke is better than secret love.' It is better to have a friend who tells you the truth to your face, even when it hurts, than one who loves you secretly but never says anything.

And then the saying about wounds.

'The full soul loatheth an honeycomb; but to the hungry soul every bitter thing is sweet.' When you are full, even honey is unappealing. But when you are really hungry, even bitter food tastes good. Being thankful often depends on remembering what it is like to have little.

'As a bird that wandereth from her nest, so is a man that wandereth from his place.' A bird that leaves her nest leaves her eggs and chicks in danger. People who abandon their homes and responsibilities can cause great harm.

'Ointment and perfume rejoice the heart: so doth the sweetness of a man’s friend by hearty counsel.' Good advice from a friend who truly cares is as pleasant as sweet perfume.

'Thine own friend, and thy father’s friend, forsake not'. Do not abandon old family friends. 'for better is a neighbour that is near than a brother far off.' In an emergency, a neighbour nearby can help more than a relative who lives far away. Look after your neighbours, and let them look after you.

'My son, be wise, and make my heart glad'. A wise child brings joy to a parent.

'A prudent man foreseeth the evil, and hideth himself'. We heard this in chapter twenty two.

Then a funny saying. Someone who blesses a friend too loudly, at the wrong time, early before the friend is even awake, will be thought of as cursing him, not blessing him! Kindness needs good timing and good sense.

Then the saying about iron.

'Whoso keepeth the fig tree shall eat the fruit thereof: so he that waiteth on his master shall be honoured.' Someone who carefully tends a fig tree gets to eat its fruit. Someone who serves faithfully is honoured.

'As in water face answereth to face, so the heart of man to man.' When you look into still water, you see your own face reflected. In the same way, our hearts reflect who we really are. Or, our hearts show us what other people are like too, because we are all alike inside.

'As the fining pot for silver, and the furnace for gold; so is a man to his praise.' Silver and gold are tested by fire. People are tested by praise. How do we handle it when people praise us? Does it make us proud, or humble and thankful?

And the chapter ends with a little poem about a farmer. 'Be thou diligent to know the state of thy flocks, and look well to thy herds. For riches are not for ever'. Take care of what you have. Know how your animals are doing. Money and power do not last for ever, but if you look after your flocks, there will be grass in the fields, wool from the lambs for clothing, goats to sell, and enough milk for your family and your servants. Faithful, careful work provides for what we need.

KEY 27:1: 'Boast not thyself of to morrow; for thou knowest not what a day may bring forth.' To morrow means the next day. Do not brag about what you will do in the future, as if you are in control. None of us knows what the next day will bring. James said the same thing: you ought to say, if the Lord will. It is wise to plan. But we hold our plans humbly, trusting God with every day to come.

KEY 27:6: 'Faithful are the wounds of a friend; but the kisses of an enemy are deceitful.' Sometimes a true friend will say something that hurts. They will tell us when we are wrong, or warn us about a mistake. Those words may sting, like a wound. But they are faithful, because they come from love. An enemy may flatter us with kisses, but they cannot be trusted. Value friends who care enough to tell you the truth.

KEY 27:17: 'Iron sharpeneth iron; so a man sharpeneth the countenance of his friend.' Countenance here means face, but also the whole person. When two pieces of iron rub together, they both become sharper. Good friends do that for each other. They challenge each other, encourage each other, and help each other grow. Sometimes it feels like friction. But it makes us better. We need friends like that, and we can be friends like that.

THREAD: The letter to the Hebrews says, 'And let us consider one another to provoke unto love and to good works'. And James wrote, 'For what is your life? It is even a vapour'.

TODAY: Is there a friend who sharpens you, who encourages and challenges you to grow? Thank them today. And if a friend has told you something hard but true recently, thank God for their faithfulness, instead of being upset.

QUESTION: Proverbs says, iron sharpeneth iron, so a man sharpeneth his friend. Who sharpens you, and whom could you help to sharpen?

PRAYER: Lord God, I do not know what the days ahead will bring, but you do. Help me to hold my plans humbly. Thank you for friends who tell me the truth in love, even when it hurts. Help me to be a friend who sharpens others, who encourages and challenges them to grow. And help me to care faithfully for what you have given me. Amen.
