# Proverbs 31: the words of king Lemuel that his mother taught him (31:2-3 and 31:6-7 not quoted), not for kings to drink wine lest they forget the law, open thy mouth for the dumb and plead the cause of the poor; the virtuous woman (an acrostic, each line beginning with the next Hebrew letter): her price above rubies, her husband trusts her, she works willingly, like the merchants' ships, she rises early (time words only inside quotes), buys a field and plants a vineyard, strength, her hands to the poor, not afraid of the snow, her husband known in the gates, strength and honour her clothing, the law of kindness, her children call her blessed, favour is deceitful and beauty is vain but a woman that feareth the LORD; looking back over Proverbs; on to Ephesians. Keys 31:9, 31:10, 31:30.
TITLE: Who Can Find a Virtuous Woman?

HOOK: A woman is up before anyone else in the house, while it is still dark. She lights the lamp, prepares food for her family, and plans the day ahead. Later, she is buying a field, planting a vineyard, making cloth to sell, and giving food to a poor family down the street. Her children love her. Her husband trusts her completely. And when she speaks, people listen, because her words are wise and kind.

WHERE: In chapter thirty, we heard the words of Agur, a humble wise man. Every word of God is pure, he said, and he is a shield to those who trust him. He prayed, give me neither poverty nor riches. And he learned wisdom from four little creatures: the ants, the conies, the locusts and the spider.

Now we come to the last chapter of Proverbs. Listen for a mother’s advice to her son, the king. And listen to the poem that ends the whole book.

STORY: The chapter begins, 'The words of king Lemuel, the prophecy that his mother taught him.' We do not know who Lemuel was. But we know he had a wise mother, who taught him well. And here are her words.

She warns him about the things that destroy kings. 'It is not for kings, O Lemuel, it is not for kings to drink wine; nor for princes strong drink: Lest they drink, and forget the law, and pervert the judgment of any of the afflicted.' A king must keep a clear head, she says. If he lets strong drink control him, he will forget God’s law, and make unfair decisions that hurt the people who most need his help.

And then she gives him his most important task.

So the book of Proverbs, which began with a father teaching his son, ends with a mother teaching hers.

And then comes the famous poem. In the Hebrew language, this poem is an acrostic. Each line begins with the next letter of the Hebrew alphabet, from the first letter to the last. It is like an alphabet of a good and wise woman.

It begins with a question.

'The heart of her husband doth safely trust in her'. He can rely on her completely. 'She will do him good and not evil all the days of her life.'

Then the poem describes all that she does. 'She seeketh wool, and flax, and worketh willingly with her hands.' Flax is used to make linen cloth. She works gladly, not grudgingly. 'She is like the merchants’ ships; she bringeth her food from afar.' She goes to great effort to provide good things for her family.

'She riseth also while it is yet night, and giveth meat to her household'. She gets up early to feed everyone, including those who work for her.

'She considereth a field, and buyeth it: with the fruit of her hands she planteth a vineyard.' She is a businesswoman. She thinks carefully, buys land, and plants a vineyard with money she has earned.

'She girdeth her loins with strength, and strengtheneth her arms.' Girdeth her loins means she gets ready for hard work. She is strong.

And then a lovely line. 'She stretcheth out her hand to the poor; yea, she reacheth forth her hands to the needy.' For all her hard work and success, she never forgets the poor. She is generous.

'She is not afraid of the snow for her household: for all her household are clothed with scarlet.' When winter comes, she is not worried, because she has prepared warm clothes for her whole family.

'Strength and honour are her clothing; and she shall rejoice in time to come.' Her real clothing is not her fine linen, but her strength and dignity. And she can laugh at the future, without fear, because she is ready.

'She openeth her mouth with wisdom; and in her tongue is the law of kindness.' After a whole book about words, here is someone whose words are wise and kind.

'She looketh well to the ways of her household, and eateth not the bread of idleness.' After a whole book about laziness, here is someone who is never idle.

'Her children arise up, and call her blessed; her husband also, and he praiseth her. Many daughters have done virtuously, but thou excellest them all.'

And then, the most important line.

The poem ends, 'Give her of the fruit of her hands; and let her own works praise her in the gates.' Let her be honoured for everything she has done.

Notice how this woman brings together everything Proverbs has taught. She is hardworking, wise in her words, generous to the poor, careful with money, trustworthy, and above all, she fears the LORD. She is wisdom, lived out in an ordinary life.

And so the book of Proverbs ends where it began. It began, the fear of the LORD is the beginning of knowledge. It ends, a woman that feareth the LORD, she shall be praised.

KEY 31:9: 'Open thy mouth, judge righteously, and plead the cause of the poor and needy.' The verse before says, open thy mouth for the dumb, which means those who cannot speak for themselves. Lemuel’s mother told him, use your power to speak up for those who have no voice. That is the job of every good leader. And it is something each of us can do too. When we see someone treated unfairly who cannot defend themselves, we can speak up for them.

KEY 31:10: 'Who can find a virtuous woman? for her price is far above rubies.' Virtuous means of strong character, excellent, capable. Do you remember how Proverbs said that wisdom is more precious than rubies? Now it says the same about this woman. She is wisdom made real. In the book of Ruth, Boaz called Ruth a virtuous woman, using the very same words. Strong character is worth more than any jewel.

KEY 31:30: 'Favour is deceitful, and beauty is vain: but a woman that feareth the LORD, she shall be praised.' Favour here means charm. Charm can fool people, and beauty fades with time. They do not last. But someone who fears the LORD has something that lasts for ever. This is true for women and for men. What matters most is not how we look, or how charming we are, but whether we honour God.

THREAD: Peter wrote of 'the ornament of a meek and quiet spirit, which is in the sight of God of great price.' And Isaiah heard God say, 'relieve the oppressed, judge the fatherless, plead for the widow.'

TODAY: Think of a woman in your life who shows strength, kindness and faith. Thank her today, and tell her what you admire about her. And look for someone who has no voice, and ask how you could speak up for them.

QUESTION: Proverbs says, favour is deceitful, and beauty is vain, but a woman that feareth the LORD shall be praised. Why do you think the fear of the LORD matters more than charm or beauty?

PRAYER: Lord God, thank you for walking with me through the book of Proverbs. Thank you for all its wisdom, for every part of life. Help me to open my mouth for those who have no voice. Thank you for the women of strength and faith in my life. Help me to value what lasts, not what fades. And let me fear you, for that is the beginning and the end of wisdom. Amen.
