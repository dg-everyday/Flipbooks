# Psalm 8: O LORD our Lord how excellent is thy name (the frame), out of the mouth of babes (Jesus quotes it, Matthew 21:16), when I consider thy heavens (the moon and the stars), what is man that thou art mindful of him, a little lower than the angels, crowned with glory and honour, dominion over the works of thy hands (sheep, oxen, beasts, fowl, fish; caring for creation as God's stewards), Hebrews 2 applies it to Jesus. Keys 8:1, 8:4, 8:5.
TITLE: How Excellent Is Thy Name

HOOK: A small boy lies on his back in a field, looking up at the stars. There are so many, he cannot count them. And the moon is so big and bright. He feels very small. Tiny, in fact. Why would the God who made all of this even notice me? he wonders. And then he remembers something wonderful. He does notice. He cares. Psalm eight is about that very thought.

WHERE: Before this, in Psalm one hundred and nineteen, we heard the longest psalm, a love song to God’s Word, in twenty two sections, one for each letter of the Hebrew alphabet. Thy word is a lamp unto my feet, and a light unto my path. And the psalmist said, thy word have I hid in mine heart, that I might not sin against thee.

Now we move to psalms about God the Creator. Our thirty favourite psalms are grouped by theme, not in number order, so we go back to Psalm eight, the fifth of the thirty. Listen for how small we are, compared with the universe. And listen for how great we are, in God’s eyes.

STORY: The psalm begins and ends with the same words, like a frame around a picture.

O LORD our Lord. Notice the two words. LORD, in capital letters, is God’s personal name, the name he told Moses at the burning bush, I AM. And Lord, in small letters, means master, ruler. So David is saying, O God, who has told us your name, you are our Master. How excellent, how majestic, is your name in all the earth!

And God has set his glory above the heavens. His greatness is higher than the sky.

Then a surprising verse. 'Out of the mouth of babes and sucklings hast thou ordained strength because of thine enemies, that thou mightest still the enemy and the avenger.' Babes and sucklings means babies and little children. God uses the praise of little children to silence his enemies. When proud people mock God, the simple praise of a child is stronger than all their arguments.

Do you remember when Jesus rode into Jerusalem, and the children in the temple shouted, Hosanna to the Son of David? The religious leaders were annoyed. But Jesus quoted this very psalm. Have ye never read, out of the mouth of babes and sucklings thou hast perfected praise?

Then David looks up at the sky, at the moon and the stars.

'When I consider thy heavens, the work of thy fingers, the moon and the stars, which thou hast ordained'. Consider means look carefully, think about. The work of thy fingers. Think of that. The universe, with all its billions of stars, is like a delicate piece of embroidery, made by God’s fingers. Ordained means set in place.

And as David looks at the vast sky, he asks a question.

And then the answer, which is even more amazing than the question.

'Thou madest him to have dominion over the works of thy hands; thou hast put all things under his feet'. Dominion means rule, responsibility. God put people in charge of his world. 'All sheep and oxen, yea, and the beasts of the field; The fowl of the air, and the fish of the sea, and whatsoever passeth through the paths of the seas.' Farm animals, wild animals, birds, fish, everything that swims in the oceans.

Do you remember the first chapter of Genesis? God made man in his own image, and told them to have dominion over the fish of the sea, and the fowl of the air, and every living thing. Psalm eight sings about that.

Having dominion does not mean we can do whatever we like with God’s world. It means we are to care for it, like a gardener looking after someone else’s garden. We are stewards, looking after what belongs to God.

And then the psalm ends as it began. 'O LORD our Lord, how excellent is thy name in all the earth!' The psalm starts and finishes with God. Even when we think about how great people are, it is all to his glory.

There is one more wonderful thing about this psalm. The letter to the Hebrews quotes it, and says that we do not yet see everything put under people’s feet. The world is broken, and we often fail to care for it. But we see Jesus. He was made a little lower than the angels, when he became a man. He suffered death for everyone. And now he is crowned with glory and honour. In Jesus, the true man, Psalm eight comes perfectly true.

KEY 8:1: 'O LORD our Lord, how excellent is thy name in all the earth! who hast set thy glory above the heavens.' Excellent here means majestic, glorious, wonderful. God’s name stands for who he is. And who he is fills the whole earth. Wherever you go, on every continent, in every ocean, his glory is there. And his glory is even higher than the heavens. He is greater than the universe he made.

KEY 8:4: 'What is man, that thou art mindful of him? and the son of man, that thou visitest him?' Mindful means thinking about, remembering. Visitest means coming to help, caring for. Compared with the stars, people are tiny. Like a grain of sand on a beach. And yet God thinks about us. He remembers us. He cares for us. The God who made the stars knows your name. That is one of the most amazing truths in the whole Bible.

KEY 8:5: 'For thou hast made him a little lower than the angels, and hast crowned him with glory and honour.' A little lower than the angels. People are not animals, and not accidents. We are made in God’s image, crowned like royalty with glory and honour. Every person you meet, of every age and every nation, is someone God has crowned. That is why every human life is precious.

THREAD: Jesus said, 'Have ye never read, Out of the mouth of babes and sucklings thou hast perfected praise?' And the letter to the Hebrews says, 'But we see Jesus, who was made a little lower than the angels for the suffering of death, crowned with glory and honour'.

TODAY: Look at the sky today, or the stars if you can, and let it make you feel small, and then remember how much God cares for you. And think of one way you could care for God’s creation today, the animals, the plants, or the world around you.

QUESTION: David asks, what is man, that thou art mindful of him? How does it make you feel to know that the God who made the stars thinks about you?

PRAYER: O LORD our Lord, how excellent is your name in all the earth! When I look at the heavens, the work of your fingers, the moon and the stars, I feel so small. And yet you are mindful of me, and you care for me. Thank you that you made us a little lower than the angels, and crowned us with glory and honour. Help me to care for your world. And thank you for Jesus, crowned with glory and honour. Amen.
