# Psalm 13: David; a lament (a sad song), six verses in three steps: complaint (how long four times), request (consider and hear me, lighten mine eyes), trust (but I have trusted in thy mercy, I will sing); David's long wait (anointed as a boy, hunted by Saul for years); it is all right to ask God how long; the turn in sad psalms. Keys 13:1, 13:3, 13:5.
TITLE: How Long, O LORD?

HOOK: Have you ever waited and waited for something, and it never seemed to come? A sick friend who does not get better. A problem at home that goes on and on. A prayer that seems to have no answer. After a while, you might begin to wonder, has God forgotten me? Does he even care? If you have ever felt like that, you are not alone. King David felt it too. And he wrote a song about it.

WHERE: In Psalm one hundred and thirty nine, David said, O LORD, thou hast searched me, and known me. Wherever he went, God’s hand would lead him and hold him. He praised God, for I am fearfully and wonderfully made. And he prayed, search me, O God, and know my heart, and lead me in the way everlasting.

Now we begin a group of psalms for sorrow and hard times. The first is Psalm thirteen, one of the shortest psalms, only six verses long. Listen for a question David asks four times. And listen for the surprising way the psalm ends.

STORY: Psalm thirteen is a psalm of David. It is what we call a lament. A lament is a sad song, a song of complaint, where someone pours out their sorrow to God. There are more laments in the book of Psalms than any other kind of psalm. That tells us something. God wants to hear our sorrows, not just our praises.

We do not know exactly when David wrote this psalm. But think about his life. As a young shepherd boy, he was anointed by the prophet Samuel to be the next king of Israel. He must have been so excited. But then, instead of becoming king, he spent years running for his life. King Saul was jealous, and hunted him through the wilderness, from cave to cave. Year after year went by, and still David was not king. Still he was in danger. Perhaps it was in times like that that David prayed this psalm.

The psalm has three steps, like climbing out of a deep hole. The first step is complaint. The second step is a request. And the third step is trust.

The first step, complaint, begins with a question.

David asks how long, four times. 'How long shall I take counsel in my soul, having sorrow in my heart daily? how long shall mine enemy be exalted over me?' To take counsel in my soul means to keep thinking and worrying, going round and round in my mind, trying to work things out by myself. Sorrow in my heart daily. Every single day, sadness. And his enemy seems to be winning.

Notice that David is completely honest. He does not pretend to be fine. He does not say, I know I should not feel like this. He tells God exactly how he feels. He even says, it feels like you have forgotten me. It feels like you are hiding from me.

Is it all right to talk to God like that? Yes. This psalm is in the Bible. God put it there, to show us that we can bring our hardest feelings to him. Saying, how long, O LORD, is not a lack of faith. It is a kind of faith. David is still talking to God. He has not given up on him.

Then comes the second step, the request.

'Lest mine enemy say, I have prevailed against him; and those that trouble me rejoice when I am moved.' Prevailed means won. If I fall, David says, my enemies will celebrate. Please do not let that happen.

And then, the third step. Something changes.

The little word but changes everything. Nothing in David’s situation has changed. His enemies are still there. He is still waiting. But David remembers who God is. He remembers God’s mercy, his faithful love that never lets go. And he chooses to trust it.

'I will sing unto the LORD, because he hath dealt bountifully with me.' Bountifully means generously, with great kindness. David looks back, and remembers all the good things God has already done for him. And he begins to sing.

The psalm begins with, how long wilt thou forget me? And it ends with, I will sing unto the LORD. In just six verses, David climbs from the bottom of the hole to the top. Not because his problems disappeared. But because he took them to God, and remembered God’s love.

We will see this pattern again and again in the psalms. They start in darkness, and end in trust or praise. It is the turn we heard about in the introduction. Something happens when we pray honestly. We do not always get our answer straight away. But we remember who God is. And that changes us.

And did David’s wait end? Yes. After many years, Saul died, and David became king, just as God had promised. God had not forgotten him at all. He was working all along, even when David could not see it.

KEY 13:1: 'How long wilt thou forget me, O LORD? for ever? how long wilt thou hide thy face from me?' Can God really forget anyone? No. Isaiah tells us that God says, I will not forget thee. But sometimes it feels as though he has. And David is not afraid to say so. When you feel forgotten by God, you can pray these very words. God is big enough to hear our honest questions. He would rather we bring them to him than turn away from him.

KEY 13:3: 'Consider and hear me, O LORD my God: lighten mine eyes, lest I sleep the sleep of death'. Consider means look at me, pay attention. Lighten mine eyes means give light to my eyes, bring life back into them. When people are very sad, or very ill, their eyes become dull and tired. David asks God to put the sparkle back. Notice that even in his sorrow, he says, O LORD my God. He still knows God is his.

KEY 13:5: 'But I have trusted in thy mercy; my heart shall rejoice in thy salvation.' Mercy here is God’s steadfast, faithful love, his love that keeps its promises and never gives up. When our feelings tell us God has forgotten us, we can choose to trust his mercy instead. Our feelings go up and down. God’s love does not. And David is so sure of God’s salvation, his rescue, that he rejoices before it has even come.

THREAD: God said through Isaiah, 'Can a woman forget her sucking child, that she should not have compassion on the son of her womb? yea, they may forget, yet will I not forget thee.' And Peter wrote, 'Casting all your care upon him; for he careth for you.'

TODAY: Is there something you have been waiting a long time for? Tell God honestly how you feel about it, even if that means asking, how long? Then, like David, remember one good thing God has already done for you, and thank him for it.

QUESTION: David begins by asking, how long wilt thou forget me? and ends by singing. What do you think helped him to change, even though his situation had not changed yet?

PRAYER: O LORD my God, sometimes it feels as though you have forgotten me, and I wonder how long I must wait. Thank you that I can tell you exactly how I feel. Consider and hear me. Lighten my eyes. And help me to trust in your mercy, even when I cannot see what you are doing. I will sing unto you, because you have dealt bountifully with me. Amen.
