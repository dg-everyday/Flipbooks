# Psalm 19: two books, creation and Scripture; the heavens declare the glory of God, day unto day and night unto night (inside quotes), no speech nor language, a tabernacle for the sun (as a bridegroom, as a strong man to run a race), nothing hid from the heat thereof; the law of the LORD perfect, converting the soul (six names for God's Word and what each does), more to be desired than gold, sweeter than honey, by them thy servant warned, who can understand his errors, cleanse thou me from secret faults, keep back from presumptuous sins, let the words of my mouth. Keys 19:1, 19:10, 19:14.
TITLE: The Heavens Declare the Glory of God

HOOK: Go outside when the sky is clear and dark, far from city lights, and look up. The sky is full of stars, thousands upon thousands of them, stretching across the heavens. No one says a word. And yet, standing there, you feel that the sky is telling you something. Someone made all this. Someone great and wonderful. Psalm nineteen says the sky is preaching a sermon, every single day.

WHERE: Before this, in Psalm two, we heard the nations rage against the LORD and against his anointed King. But he that sitteth in the heavens laughs. God has set his King on Zion, and said, thou art my Son. And blessed are all they that put their trust in him.

Now we jump ahead to Psalm nineteen, the third of our thirty favourite psalms. They are grouped by theme, not in number order, and this group is about the two ways of life, and God’s Word. Here David writes about two ways God speaks to us. Listen for what the sky says without words. And listen for what God’s Word does for us.

STORY: The psalm opens with one of the most famous lines in the Bible, about the heavens and the firmament. We will hear it in a moment.

The firmament is the sky, the great dome above us. God’s handywork means the work of his hands. Just as a beautiful painting tells you something about the artist, the sky tells us about God: his power, his greatness, his beauty.

'Day unto day uttereth speech, and night unto night sheweth knowledge.' Uttereth speech means pours out words, like a fountain. Every single day, the sky pours out its message. And when the stars come out, it shows us more.

'There is no speech nor language, where their voice is not heard.' The sky does not use words. And yet its message goes out to every nation, in every language. Wherever you live, whatever language you speak, you can look up and see the glory of God.

'Their line is gone out through all the earth, and their words to the end of the world. In them hath he set a tabernacle for the sun'. A tabernacle is a tent. David pictures the sky as a great tent, where the sun lives.

And then he describes the sun with two lovely pictures. It comes out each day 'as a bridegroom coming out of his chamber', full of joy, dressed in his best, on his wedding day. And it 'rejoiceth as a strong man to run a race.' Like an athlete who loves to run, the sun races across the sky, from one end to the other. 'and there is nothing hid from the heat thereof.' Nothing escapes its warmth and light.

Then, suddenly, David changes the subject. He moves from the sun in the sky, to God’s Word. Just as nothing is hidden from the sun, nothing is hidden from God’s Word.

And here David uses six different names for God’s Word, and tells us what each one does. 'The law of the LORD is perfect, converting the soul'. Converting means turning around, bringing back to life. 'the testimony of the LORD is sure, making wise the simple.' Sure means reliable, trustworthy. It makes simple people wise. 'The statutes of the LORD are right, rejoicing the heart'. God’s instructions are right, and they make us glad. 'the commandment of the LORD is pure, enlightening the eyes.' It helps us see clearly. 'The fear of the LORD is clean, enduring for ever'. 'the judgments of the LORD are true and righteous altogether.'

Perfect, sure, right, pure, clean, true. And it restores the soul, makes wise, rejoices the heart, enlightens the eyes, and lasts for ever.

And then David tells us how precious it is.

'Moreover by them is thy servant warned: and in keeping of them there is great reward.' God’s Word warns us of danger, like a lighthouse warning ships away from the rocks. And keeping it brings great reward.

And then, as David thinks about God’s perfect Word, he becomes aware of his own faults. 'Who can understand his errors? cleanse thou me from secret faults.' Errors means mistakes, sins we commit without realising. Secret faults are sins hidden even from ourselves. We do not even know all our own sins. Only God does. So David asks God to clean him, even from the ones he cannot see.

'Keep back thy servant also from presumptuous sins; let them not have dominion over me'. Presumptuous sins are sins we commit on purpose, knowing they are wrong. Dominion means control. Do not let them rule over me.

And the psalm ends with one of the most loved prayers in the Bible.

So the psalm moves from the sky, to the Scriptures, to the heart. God speaks through creation, and through his Word. And when we hear him, we want our own lives, our words and our thoughts, to please him.

KEY 19:1: 'The heavens declare the glory of God; and the firmament sheweth his handywork.' Declare means announce, proclaim. Every rising sun, every star, every cloud is announcing how great God is. Many people never read the Bible. But everyone can see the sky. God has given the whole world a picture book of his glory. When you look up today, let the sky remind you to praise the One who made it.

KEY 19:10: 'More to be desired are they than gold, yea, than much fine gold: sweeter also than honey and the honeycomb.' God’s Word is more valuable than the most valuable thing we can think of, gold. And more delightful than the sweetest thing, honey straight from the honeycomb. Do we see the Bible like that? As treasure, and as something sweet? The more we read it, the more precious and sweet it becomes.

KEY 19:14: 'Let the words of my mouth, and the meditation of my heart, be acceptable in thy sight, O LORD, my strength, and my redeemer.' The words of my mouth, what I say out loud. The meditation of my heart, what I think inside. David asks that both would please God. And he calls God his strength and his redeemer, the one who rescues him and buys him back. This is a wonderful prayer to pray every day, before we speak, and before we think.

THREAD: Paul wrote that God’s eternal power can be seen, 'being understood by the things that are made'. And he told Timothy, 'All scripture is given by inspiration of God, and is profitable for doctrine'.

TODAY: Take a moment today to look up at the sky, and let it remind you of God’s glory. Then read a little of God’s Word, as treasure, and taste its sweetness. And pray David’s prayer before you speak: let the words of my mouth be acceptable in thy sight.

QUESTION: Psalm nineteen says God speaks through the heavens, and through his Word. Which of these helps you to know God more, and why?

PRAYER: O LORD, the heavens declare your glory, and the sky shows the work of your hands. Thank you for your Word, which is perfect, sure, right and pure, more precious than gold and sweeter than honey. Cleanse me from secret faults, and keep me from deliberate sin. Let the words of my mouth, and the meditation of my heart, be acceptable in your sight, O LORD, my strength and my redeemer. Amen.
