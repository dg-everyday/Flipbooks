# Psalm 23 (book plan; differs from bp-psalms-23, keys 23:1, 23:4, 23:6, title The LORD Is My Shepherd): David the shepherd boy, the LORD is my shepherd I shall not want, green pastures and still waters, he restoreth my soul, paths of righteousness for his name's sake, the valley of the shadow of death (thy rod and staff), a table before mine enemies, anointest my head with oil, my cup runneth over, goodness and mercy shall follow me, the house of the LORD for ever; Jesus the good shepherd (John 10). Keys 23:2, 23:3, 23:5.
TITLE: He Restoreth My Soul

HOOK: A tired, hot little lamb has wandered too far, and is lost and frightened. Then she hears a familiar voice, calling her name. The shepherd has come looking for her. He lifts her gently in his arms, carries her back to the flock, and sets her down in soft green grass, beside a quiet stream. She drinks the cool water, and lies down to rest. She is safe. Her shepherd is here.

WHERE: In Psalm one hundred and four, we walked through God’s creation: the mountains and seas, the birds singing among the branches, the wild goats on the rocks, and the great sea creatures playing in the waves. O LORD, how manifold are thy works, the psalm said. Thou sendest forth thy spirit, and renewest the face of the earth. I will sing unto the LORD as long as I live.

Now we begin a group of psalms about trusting God. And we start with the most famous psalm of all, Psalm twenty three. Listen for what the shepherd does for his sheep. And listen for the moment when the psalm changes.

STORY: The psalm is a psalm of David. And David knew all about sheep. As a boy, he was a shepherd, looking after his father’s flock on the hills around Bethlehem. He led them to grass and water. He protected them from lions and bears. Now, as a grown man, he thinks about God, and says, God is like that for me. I am the sheep, and he is my shepherd.

'The LORD is my shepherd; I shall not want.' Notice the little word, my. Not just a shepherd, or Israel’s shepherd, but my shepherd. And because he is my shepherd, I shall not want. I will not lack anything I truly need.

Then David describes what the shepherd does.

He makes me lie down, and leads me. A good shepherd knows where to find the best grass, and the safest water. He leads his sheep there, one step at a time.

And then.

He leads me on the right paths. Not because I deserve it, but for his name’s sake, because that is who he is.

And then the psalm goes down into a dark valley. 'Yea, though I walk through the valley of the shadow of death, I will fear no evil: for thou art with me; thy rod and thy staff they comfort me.'

Listen carefully, because here something changes. Until now, David has been talking about God. He leads me. He restoreth me. But now, in the darkest place, David starts talking to God. For thou art with me. In the dark valley, God is not far away, being talked about. He is right beside us, close enough to speak to.

The valley of the shadow of death is any deep, dark, frightening place. It might be sickness, or grief, or loneliness, or fear. The path through the hills went down into deep, shadowy gorges, where wild animals hid. But the sheep was not afraid, because the shepherd was there.

And the rod and staff? A shepherd carried a rod, a heavy club, to fight off wild animals. And a staff, a long stick with a hook, to guide the sheep and pull them out of danger. Both are a comfort. One protects, and the other guides.

Then the picture changes. The shepherd becomes a host, welcoming a guest to a feast.

In those days, a good host would put sweet smelling oil on a guest’s head, to refresh them and honour them. And he would keep filling their cup, until it ran over.

And then the psalm ends with a great promise. 'Surely goodness and mercy shall follow me all the days of my life: and I will dwell in the house of the LORD for ever.' Follow here means chase after, pursue. Wherever I go, God’s goodness and his faithful love will be running after me, every single day. And at the end, I will live in God’s house for ever.

Many years later, Jesus said, I am the good shepherd. The good shepherd giveth his life for the sheep. David’s shepherd was the LORD. And the LORD came, in Jesus, to be our shepherd. He did more than any shepherd ever did. He laid down his life for his sheep. And he rose again, to lead us home.

KEY 23:2: 'He maketh me to lie down in green pastures: he leadeth me beside the still waters.' Sheep will not lie down if they are afraid, or hungry, or bothered by insects, or quarrelling with each other. Only a good shepherd can make them feel safe enough to rest. And sheep are afraid of fast flowing water. So the shepherd leads them to still, quiet waters, where they can drink in peace. God knows what we need, and he gives us rest.

KEY 23:3: 'He restoreth my soul: he leadeth me in the paths of righteousness for his name’s sake.' Restoreth means brings back, makes new. When we are tired, discouraged, or have wandered away, the Shepherd brings us back and gives us new life. And he leads us in the right paths. Not because we always choose well, but for his name’s sake. He guides us because he is a good shepherd, and he keeps his promises.

KEY 23:5: 'Thou preparest a table before me in the presence of mine enemies: thou anointest my head with oil; my cup runneth over.' Even when enemies are all around, God sets out a feast for us. He does not wait until all our troubles are gone. He blesses us right in the middle of them. And his blessings are not small. My cup runneth over. More than enough, spilling over the top. God is generous beyond measure.

THREAD: Jesus said, 'I am the good shepherd: the good shepherd giveth his life for the sheep.' And Isaiah wrote, 'He shall feed his flock like a shepherd: he shall gather the lambs with his arm, and carry them in his bosom'.

TODAY: Are you walking through a dark valley right now? Talk to your Shepherd, not just about him. Say, thou art with me. And look for one way his goodness and mercy are following you today. Thank him for it.

QUESTION: In the dark valley, David stops talking about God, and starts talking to him: for thou art with me. Why do you think hard times can bring us closer to God?

PRAYER: The LORD is my shepherd; I shall not want. Thank you, Lord, for leading me to green pastures and still waters, and for restoring my soul. When I walk through the valley of the shadow of death, I will fear no evil, for you are with me. Thank you that my cup runs over, and that goodness and mercy will follow me all the days of my life. And thank you, Jesus, my good shepherd, who gave his life for me. Amen.
