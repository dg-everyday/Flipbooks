# Psalm 84: for the sons of Korah, upon Gittith; how amiable are thy tabernacles, my soul longeth even fainteth for the courts of the LORD, the sparrow and the swallow nesting by the altars, blessed are they that dwell in thy house, blessed is the man whose strength is in thee, in whose heart are the ways (the pilgrim roads), the valley of Baca (weeping) made a well, from strength to strength, a day in thy courts better than a thousand, a doorkeeper (the sons of Korah were doorkeepers, 1 Chronicles 9:19), a sun and shield, no good thing will he withhold. Keys 84:2, 84:10, 84:11.
TITLE: A Day in Thy Courts

HOOK: Think of your favourite place in all the world. Perhaps it is your grandparents’ house, or a beach, or a place in the countryside. When you are far away from it, you miss it. You long to go back. You count the days. And when you finally arrive, your heart leaps with joy. For the writer of Psalm eighty four, that place was the house of God.

WHERE: Before this, in Psalm one hundred and seven, people in trouble cried unto the LORD, and each time he delivered them.

Now we come to psalms about worship and God’s house. Our thirty favourite psalms are grouped by theme, not in number order, so we go back to Psalm eighty four, the twenty fifth of the thirty. It is a song of longing for God’s house. Listen for two little birds. And listen for the job the writer would rather have than anything else in the world.

STORY: Psalm eighty four is a psalm for the sons of Korah, the family of temple singers we met in Psalms forty two and forty six. And like Psalm forty two, it is full of longing to be in God’s house. But this time, the longing is happy, full of joy and hope.

It begins with a cry of love. 'How amiable are thy tabernacles, O LORD of hosts!' Amiable means lovely, dearly loved. Tabernacles here means dwelling places, God’s house, the temple in Jerusalem. How lovely is your house, Lord!

Then the writer tells us how much he longs to be there.

And then he notices something. Little birds, flying in and out of the temple courts. 'Yea, the sparrow hath found an house, and the swallow a nest for herself, where she may lay her young, even thine altars, O LORD of hosts, my King, and my God.' Sparrows and swallows had built their nests in the walls and ledges of the temple, near the great altars. The writer almost envies them. Even the smallest, most ordinary birds have found a home near God. How wonderful it would be to live there too!

Sparrows were among the cheapest birds in the market. Jesus once said that not one sparrow falls to the ground without our Father knowing. And he said, ye are of more value than many sparrows. If God makes room for sparrows near his altar, he certainly has room for us.

'Blessed are they that dwell in thy house: they will be still praising thee. Selah.' Still here means always, continually. Those who live near God are always praising him.

Then the psalm turns to the pilgrims, travelling to Jerusalem for the great feasts. 'Blessed is the man whose strength is in thee; in whose heart are the ways of them.' The ways means the roads, the pilgrim roads that lead to Jerusalem. Blessed is the person whose heart is set on the journey to God.

'Who passing through the valley of Baca make it a well; the rain also filleth the pools.' Baca means weeping. The valley of Baca was a dry, difficult place on the way, perhaps a place of tears. But the pilgrims, because their strength is in God, turn it into a place of springs. Even the hard, sad places on our journey can become places of blessing, when God is with us.

'They go from strength to strength, every one of them in Zion appeareth before God.' Normally, a long, hard journey makes you weaker and weaker. But these pilgrims grow stronger and stronger, the closer they get to God. And at the end, every one of them arrives, and stands before God.

Then the writer prays. 'O LORD God of hosts, hear my prayer: give ear, O God of Jacob. Selah. Behold, O God our shield, and look upon the face of thine anointed.' The anointed is the king. He prays for God to bless the king, who protects God’s people like a shield.

And then the most famous verse in the psalm.

Now, here is something interesting. The sons of Korah were not only singers. Some of them were also doorkeepers at the house of God. The first book of Chronicles tells us that they were keepers of the gates of the tabernacle. So the writer may have been talking about his own job. And he says, I would rather do my humble job at God’s door, than live in luxury with wicked people.

Then the psalm tells us why God is worth it.

And the psalm ends with a blessing. 'O LORD of hosts, blessed is the man that trusteth in thee.'

For Christians, the temple in Jerusalem was a picture of something greater. Jesus said that a time would come when true worshippers would worship the Father in spirit and in truth, not only in one building. And the New Testament says that God now lives in his people, by his Spirit. Wherever we are, we can come close to God. But we still gather together with other believers to worship him, and there is a special joy in that.

KEY 84:2: 'My soul longeth, yea, even fainteth for the courts of the LORD: my heart and my flesh crieth out for the living God.' Fainteth means feels weak with longing. The courts were the open courtyards of the temple, where people gathered to worship. The writer does not just long for a building. He longs for the living God. Every part of him, his heart and his body, cries out to be near God. Do we long for God like that?

KEY 84:10: 'For a day in thy courts is better than a thousand. I had rather be a doorkeeper in the house of my God, than to dwell in the tents of wickedness.' One day near God is better than a thousand days anywhere else. A doorkeeper had a humble job, standing at the gate. But the writer would rather stand at the doorstep of God’s house, than live in the finest tent of the wicked. Being close to God, even in the lowest place, is better than anything the world can offer.

KEY 84:11: 'For the LORD God is a sun and shield: the LORD will give grace and glory: no good thing will he withhold from them that walk uprightly.' A sun gives light, warmth and life. A shield gives protection. God is both. And he gives grace, his undeserved kindness, and glory. Withhold means keep back. God will not keep back any good thing from those who walk with him. If something is truly good for us, God will give it.

THREAD: Jesus said, 'Fear ye not therefore, ye are of more value than many sparrows.' And he said, 'God is a Spirit: and they that worship him must worship him in spirit and in truth.'

TODAY: Do you look forward to worshipping God with others? This week, when you gather with other believers, go gladly, as the pilgrims did. And remember the sparrow. If God has room for her near his altar, he has room for you.

QUESTION: The writer says a day in God’s courts is better than a thousand anywhere else. What do you think makes being close to God so wonderful?

PRAYER: How lovely is your house, O LORD of hosts! My soul longs for you, the living God. Thank you that even the sparrow finds a home near you, and that you have room for me too. Help me to go from strength to strength. Thank you that you are a sun and shield, and that no good thing will you withhold from those who walk uprightly. Blessed is the one who trusts in you. Amen.
