# Psalm 100: a psalm of praise (or of thanksgiving: sung with the thank offering, Leviticus 7:12); five verses, seven commands (make a joyful noise, serve, come, know, enter, be thankful, bless) and the reasons (he made us, we are his people and the sheep of his pasture, the LORD is good, his mercy everlasting, his truth to all generations); all ye lands (every nation, not only Israel; Revelation 7:9); the gates and courts of the temple; God's mercy (steadfast love) and truth (faithfulness). Keys 100:2, 100:3, 100:5.
TITLE: Enter into His Gates with Thanksgiving

HOOK: A great crowd gathers outside the gates of Jerusalem. Families have come from all over the land for one of the great feasts. Some carry baskets of fruit. Some lead lambs. The children are bouncing with excitement. Then the gates swing open, and the people stream through, up the steps, towards the temple courts. And as they go, they sing. Make a joyful noise unto the LORD, all ye lands! Psalm one hundred may have been sung at moments just like that.

WHERE: In Psalm one hundred and thirty three, we heard, behold, how good and how pleasant it is for brethren to dwell together in unity! Unity is like the precious oil poured on Aaron’s head, and like the dew of Hermon falling on the mountains of Zion. For there the LORD commanded the blessing, even life for evermore.

Now we come to Psalm one hundred, one of the best loved songs of praise in the Bible. Listen for the commands it gives us. And listen for the reasons it gives for obeying them.

STORY: Psalm one hundred has a short title. A psalm of praise. Some translations say, a psalm of thanksgiving. In the book of Leviticus, God told the Israelites about a special offering called the thanksgiving offering, which a person could bring to the temple to thank God for some special kindness. Perhaps they recovered from an illness, or came home safe from a journey. This psalm may have been sung as people came to the temple with their thank offerings.

It is only five verses long. But it is packed full. In those five verses, there are seven commands, seven things to do. And there are reasons, too. The psalm does not just tell us to praise God. It tells us why.

The first command is a big one. 'Make a joyful noise unto the LORD, all ye lands.' A joyful noise. Not necessarily beautiful singing. Just a loud, happy sound of praise. If you cannot sing well, that does not matter. God loves a joyful noise.

And notice who is invited. All ye lands. Not just Israel. Every land, every nation, every people on earth. Even in the time of the Old Testament, God wanted the whole world to praise him. And at the very end of the Bible, in the book of Revelation, John sees a great multitude, which no man could number, of all nations, and kindreds, and people, and tongues, standing before God’s throne, and praising him.

The second and third commands come next.

Then comes the fourth command, and the first reason.

And then the fifth and sixth commands. 'Enter into his gates with thanksgiving, and into his courts with praise'. The temple in Jerusalem had great gates, and inside them, large open courtyards, called courts, where the people gathered to worship. The psalm says, as you come through the gates, give thanks. As you come into the courts, praise. Begin with thankfulness, and let it grow into praise.

'be thankful unto him, and bless his name.' To bless God’s name means to speak well of him, to honour him, to praise who he is.

And then the psalm ends with the final reasons.

So there are seven commands. Make a joyful noise. Serve the LORD with gladness. Come before his presence with singing. Know that the LORD is God. Enter into his gates with thanksgiving. Be thankful unto him. Bless his name.

And there are reasons. He made us. We belong to him. He looks after us like a shepherd. He is good. His mercy lasts for ever. And his truth goes on through every generation.

The commands and the reasons go together. We do not praise God just because we are told to. We praise him because of who he is, and what he has done. The more we know about God, the more we want to praise him.

Today, we do not go up to the temple in Jerusalem. But the New Testament tells us that, through Jesus, we can come boldly before God’s throne at any time, from anywhere. Every time we pray, we enter his gates. And this psalm teaches us how to come: with joy, with gladness, with singing, with thanksgiving, and with praise.

KEY 100:2: 'Serve the LORD with gladness: come before his presence with singing.' Serving God is not meant to be a heavy, miserable duty. It is meant to be done with gladness. Think of the difference between doing chores because you have to, grumbling all the way, and doing something kind for someone you love, happily. God wants us to serve him like that, gladly, because we love him. And to come into his presence, not with long faces, but with songs.

KEY 100:3: 'Know ye that the LORD he is God: it is he that hath made us, and not we ourselves; we are his people, and the sheep of his pasture.' Know means be sure of it. Be certain. The LORD is God. Not idols, not money, not ourselves. And he made us. We did not make ourselves. So we belong to him. We are his people, and the sheep of his pasture. Do you remember Psalm twenty three? The LORD is my shepherd. Here it is again. We are his sheep, and he cares for us.

KEY 100:5: 'For the LORD is good; his mercy is everlasting; and his truth endureth to all generations.' Three great reasons to praise God. He is good, always good, in everything he does. His mercy, his faithful love, is everlasting. It never runs out. And his truth, his faithfulness, endures to all generations. He was faithful to your grandparents, and to their grandparents, all the way back to Abraham. And he will be faithful to your children, and your grandchildren, and on and on.

THREAD: The letter to the Hebrews says, 'Let us therefore come boldly unto the throne of grace, that we may obtain mercy'. And John saw 'a great multitude, which no man could number, of all nations, and kindreds, and people, and tongues'.

TODAY: Make a joyful noise to the LORD today. Sing a song of praise, even if you think you cannot sing well. And before you pray, start with thanksgiving. Thank God for three things, before you ask him for anything.

QUESTION: Psalm one hundred says, serve the LORD with gladness. What helps you to serve God gladly, instead of grudgingly?

PRAYER: Lord, I make a joyful noise to you. I want to serve you with gladness, and come before your presence with singing. I know that you are God. You made me, and I am yours, one of the sheep of your pasture. I enter your gates with thanksgiving, and your courts with praise. For you are good, your mercy is everlasting, and your truth endures to all generations. Amen.
