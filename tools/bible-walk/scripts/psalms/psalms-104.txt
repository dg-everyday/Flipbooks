# Psalm 104: bless the LORD O my soul, clothed with honour and majesty, light as a garment, the heavens like a curtain, clouds his chariot, the wings of the wind; the earth's foundations, the waters fled at thy rebuke, a bound they may not pass (Genesis 1, the flood); springs and the creatures that drink, birds singing, grass for cattle, food, wine and oil and bread, the cedars of Lebanon, the stork, wild goats and conies; the moon for seasons, the sun knows his going down, the beasts by night and man to his work (time words inside quotes only), how manifold are thy works, the great and wide sea, leviathan to play, all wait upon thee, thou openest thine hand, thou sendest forth thy spirit, the glory of the LORD shall endure, I will sing unto the LORD as long as I live. 104:35a not quoted. Keys 104:24, 104:30, 104:33.
TITLE: O LORD, How Manifold Are Thy Works

HOOK: Imagine a walk through the greatest nature park in the world, with a guide. Look, he says, there are the wild goats, leaping on the rocks. There, in the cedar trees, the storks have built their nests. Listen, can you hear the birds singing by the stream? Down in the valley, the farmers are working in the fields. And far away, out on the sea, the ships are sailing, and great creatures are splashing in the waves. Psalm one hundred and four is like a walk through God’s creation.

WHERE: Before this, in Psalm eight, David looked up at the heavens, and asked, what is man, that thou art mindful of him?

Now, still with God the Creator, we jump ahead to Psalm one hundred and four, the sixth of our thirty favourite psalms, which are grouped by theme, not in number order. Here we take a longer walk through God’s creation. Listen for how many creatures the psalm names. And listen for how everything depends on God.

STORY: The psalm begins with a call to praise. 'Bless the LORD, O my soul.' The writer speaks to his own soul, and tells it to praise God. 'O LORD my God, thou art very great; thou art clothed with honour and majesty.'

And then the psalm paints God as a great king. 'Who coverest thyself with light as with a garment: who stretchest out the heavens like a curtain'. God wears light like a robe. He spreads out the sky as easily as someone putting up a tent. 'who maketh the clouds his chariot: who walketh upon the wings of the wind'. The clouds are his chariot, and the wind is like wings carrying him.

Then the psalm describes how God made the earth. He laid its foundations, so that it would not be moved. At first, the deep waters covered everything, even the mountains. Do you remember the first chapter of Genesis, when the earth was covered with water? 'At thy rebuke they fled; at the voice of thy thunder they hasted away.' At God’s command, the waters rushed away, down the mountains and into the valleys, to the place God made for them. 'Thou hast set a bound that they may not pass over; that they turn not again to cover the earth.' God set a boundary for the seas, a line they cannot cross.

Then the psalm describes how God cares for his creation. He sends springs into the valleys, flowing among the hills. They give water to every wild animal, and the wild donkeys quench their thirst. 'By them shall the fowls of the heaven have their habitation, which sing among the branches.' Birds build their nests beside the streams, and sing in the trees.

God waters the mountains with rain. He makes grass grow for the cattle, and plants for people to grow, so they can bring food out of the earth. 'And wine that maketh glad the heart of man, and oil to make his face to shine, and bread which strengtheneth man’s heart.' God gives us not just what we need to survive, but good things to enjoy.

'The trees of the LORD are full of sap; the cedars of Lebanon, which he hath planted'. In those great trees, the birds make their nests, and the stork lives in the fir trees. 'The high hills are a refuge for the wild goats; and the rocks for the conies.' Every creature has its own home.

Then the psalm talks about time. 'He appointed the moon for seasons: the sun knoweth his going down.' God made the moon to mark the seasons, and the sun knows exactly when to set. 'Thou makest darkness, and it is night: wherein all the beasts of the forest do creep forth.' When darkness falls, the forest animals come out. The young lions roar for their prey, and look to God for their food. 'The sun ariseth, they gather themselves together, and lay them down in their dens.' And then, 'Man goeth forth unto his work and to his labour until the evening.'

And then the writer bursts out in wonder.

Then he looks at the sea. 'So is this great and wide sea, wherein are things creeping innumerable, both small and great beasts.' Countless creatures, tiny and enormous. 'There go the ships: there is that leviathan, whom thou hast made to play therein.' Leviathan is a great sea creature. And God made it to play in the sea! God enjoys his creation, and his creatures enjoy it too.

'These wait all upon thee; that thou mayest give them their meat in due season.' Every creature depends on God for its food. 'thou openest thine hand, they are filled with good.' But when God hides his face, they are troubled. When he takes away their breath, they die, and return to the dust.

And then a wonderful verse about God’s Spirit.

'The glory of the LORD shall endure for ever: the LORD shall rejoice in his works.' God is glad about what he has made.

And then the writer makes a promise.

'My meditation of him shall be sweet: I will be glad in the LORD.' And the psalm ends where it began. 'Bless thou the LORD, O my soul. Praise ye the LORD.'

KEY 104:24: 'O LORD, how manifold are thy works! in wisdom hast thou made them all: the earth is full of thy riches.' Manifold means many and varied. Think of elephants and ants, eagles and penguins, whales and goldfish. And every one was made with wisdom. Nothing in creation is an accident. The earth is full of God’s riches, the treasures of his creativity.

KEY 104:30: 'Thou sendest forth thy spirit, they are created: and thou renewest the face of the earth.' Every new life, every newborn lamb, every spring flower, comes from God’s Spirit. He keeps giving life, and renewing the earth, every spring, every day. Every breath we take is a gift from his Spirit.

KEY 104:33: 'I will sing unto the LORD as long as I live: I will sing praise to my God while I have my being.' While I have my being means as long as I exist. After looking at everything God has made, the writer cannot help singing. And he decides he will keep singing, his whole life long. Praise is the right response to a God who made such a wonderful world, and who keeps caring for it.

THREAD: Jesus said, 'Behold the fowls of the air: for they sow not, neither do they reap, nor gather into barns; yet your heavenly Father feedeth them.' And Genesis tells us, 'And God saw every thing that he had made, and, behold, it was very good.'

TODAY: Go outside today, or look out of a window, and notice three things God has made. A tree, a bird, a cloud. Thank him for each one. And remember that the God who feeds the birds and the lions cares for you too.

QUESTION: Psalm one hundred and four says God made his creatures with wisdom, and that he still gives them life and food. What part of God’s creation fills you with the most wonder, and why?

PRAYER: Bless the LORD, O my soul. O LORD my God, you are very great. How manifold are your works! In wisdom you have made them all. Thank you for the mountains and the seas, the birds and the animals, and for food and every good gift. Thank you that you send forth your Spirit and renew the face of the earth. I will sing to you as long as I live. Amen.
