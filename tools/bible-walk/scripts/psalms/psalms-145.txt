# Psalm 145: David's psalm of praise (the last psalm titled for David); an alphabet psalm; I will extol thee my God O king, every day will I bless thee, great is the LORD and his greatness unsearchable, one generation shall praise thy works to another, gracious and full of compassion slow to anger (Exodus 34 again), good to all, all thy works shall praise thee, an everlasting kingdom, upholdeth all that fall, the eyes of all wait upon thee, thou openest thine hand, nigh unto all them that call upon him in truth, preserveth all them that love him (145:20b told gently), let all flesh bless his holy name. Keys 145:3, 145:8, 145:18.
TITLE: Great Is the LORD, and Greatly to Be Praised

HOOK: A grandmother sits with her grandchildren around her. Let me tell you what God has done, she says. And she tells them about the time God answered her prayer when she was a little girl. About how he helped her family through hard times. About his faithfulness, year after year. The children listen, wide eyed. Years later, those children are grandparents themselves. And they tell the same stories, and their own, to their grandchildren. Psalm one hundred and forty five says that is how praise travels.

WHERE: Before this, in Psalm one hundred, we heard, make a joyful noise unto the LORD, all ye lands. Serve the LORD with gladness. Know ye that the LORD he is God; we are his people, and the sheep of his pasture. Enter into his gates with thanksgiving, and into his courts with praise. For the LORD is good, and his mercy is everlasting.

Now, with more praise, we jump ahead to Psalm one hundred and forty five, the twenty ninth of our thirty favourite psalms, which are grouped by theme, not in number order. It is the last psalm in the book with David’s name on it. Listen for the word all, which appears again and again. And listen for who God is near to.

STORY: Psalm one hundred and forty five has a title that is found nowhere else in the Psalms. It simply says, David’s psalm of praise. It is the last psalm in the book with David’s name. After a lifetime of psalms, prayers in caves, cries of sorrow, songs of repentance, and shouts of joy, David ends with pure praise.

And like Psalm thirty four and Psalm one hundred and nineteen, it is an alphabet psalm. Each verse begins with the next letter of the Hebrew alphabet. It is as if David is praising God from A to Z.

It begins, 'I will extol thee, my God, O king; and I will bless thy name for ever and ever.' Extol means lift up high, praise. Notice that David, the great king of Israel, calls God, O king. David knew that he was not the real king. God was.

'Every day will I bless thee; and I will praise thy name for ever and ever.' Praise is for every single day, and for all eternity.

Then David says something about how great God is.

'One generation shall praise thy works to another, and shall declare thy mighty acts.' Parents tell their children. Children tell their own children. And so the praise of God is passed on, from one generation to the next, like a torch handed from runner to runner.

'They shall abundantly utter the memory of thy great goodness, and shall sing of thy righteousness.' Abundantly utter means pour out, like water bubbling from a spring. When people remember God’s goodness, they cannot keep quiet about it.

Then David describes what God is like.

'The LORD is good to all: and his tender mercies are over all his works.' Good to all. Not just to some. God sends sunshine and rain on everyone. Every living thing is under his tender care.

'All thy works shall praise thee, O LORD; and thy saints shall bless thee.' Saints here means God’s faithful people. All creation praises God, and his people bless him. 'They shall speak of the glory of thy kingdom, and talk of thy power'.

'Thy kingdom is an everlasting kingdom, and thy dominion endureth throughout all generations.' Every human kingdom rises and falls. God’s never ends.

Then David shows us that this great King is also very kind. 'The LORD upholdeth all that fall, and raiseth up all those that be bowed down.' Upholdeth means holds up. Bowed down means bent over with heavy burdens, or sorrow. God picks up those who fall, and lifts up those who are weighed down.

'The eyes of all wait upon thee; and thou givest them their meat in due season. Thou openest thine hand, and satisfiest the desire of every living thing.' Do you remember Psalm one hundred and four? Every creature waits for God to feed it. God opens his hand, and every living thing is satisfied.

And then a wonderful promise.

'He will fulfil the desire of them that fear him: he also will hear their cry, and will save them. The LORD preserveth all them that love him'. God keeps safe all who love him. David adds that in the end, the wicked will not escape God’s justice. God is kind, but he is also just. He will put everything right.

And the psalm ends with the whole world praising God. 'My mouth shall speak the praise of the LORD: and let all flesh bless his holy name for ever and ever.' All flesh means every living person. David starts with himself, my mouth, and ends with everyone.

Did you notice how many times the word all appears? Good to all. Over all his works. All thy works shall praise thee. Upholdeth all that fall. The eyes of all wait upon thee. Righteous in all his ways. Nigh unto all them that call upon him. All flesh. David’s God is not small. His goodness, his care and his kingdom reach everyone and everything.

KEY 145:3: 'Great is the LORD, and greatly to be praised; and his greatness is unsearchable.' Unsearchable means we can never find the end of it. There will always be more to discover, more to wonder at, more to praise. That is why heaven will never be boring. We will spend eternity discovering how great God is.

KEY 145:8: 'The LORD is gracious, and full of compassion; slow to anger, and of great mercy.' These are the words God himself spoke to Moses on Mount Sinai, and David knew them by heart. Gracious means kind to those who do not deserve it. Full of compassion means deeply caring, feeling for us. Slow to anger means patient. God does not lose his temper. And of great mercy means his faithful love is enormous. This is the God we praise.

KEY 145:18: 'The LORD is nigh unto all them that call upon him, to all that call upon him in truth.' Nigh means near. The great King, whose greatness is unsearchable, whose kingdom is everlasting, is near to everyone who calls on him. Not just the important people. Not just the very good people. All who call upon him in truth, that is, honestly, sincerely, from the heart. You do not need special words. Just call on him. He is near.

THREAD: James wrote, 'Draw nigh to God, and he will draw nigh to you.' And Jesus said, 'he maketh his sun to rise on the evil and on the good, and sendeth rain on the just and on the unjust.'

TODAY: Be one generation that praises God’s works to another. Tell someone younger than you about something God has done for you. Or ask an older person to tell you about God’s faithfulness in their life.

QUESTION: David says the great King of the universe is near to all who call upon him in truth. What do you think it means to call upon God in truth?

PRAYER: I will extol you, my God, O King, and I will bless your name for ever and ever. Great is the LORD, and greatly to be praised. Thank you that you are gracious and full of compassion, slow to anger, and of great mercy. Thank you that you hold up all who fall, and that you are near to all who call upon you in truth. Help me to tell the next generation what you have done. Amen.
