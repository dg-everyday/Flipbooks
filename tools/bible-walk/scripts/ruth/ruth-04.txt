# Ruth 4: Boaz at the gate, the nearer kinsman declines, the shoe, ye are witnesses, Boaz marries Ruth, Obed is born, Naomi's arms are full, the line of David. Keys 4:10, 4:14, 4:17.
TITLE: A Restorer of Thy Life

HOOK: The old men of the town sit down in the shade of the city gate. A crowd begins to gather. Boaz has called a meeting, and everyone wants to know why. Across from him sits the one man in Bethlehem who has a better claim than Boaz to redeem Naomi’s family. If he says yes, Boaz loses Ruth. Everything depends on what this man will say.

WHERE: In chapter three, Naomi sent Ruth to the threshing floor, and Ruth asked Boaz to spread his protection over her, for he was a near kinsman. Boaz blessed her, and called her a virtuous woman. But he told her there was another kinsman nearer than he. Naomi said, sit still, my daughter, for the man will not rest until he has finished the thing this day.

Now we come to the last chapter of Ruth. It all comes together in a single day. Listen for the word redeem, and for how Naomi, who came home empty, ends with her arms full.

STORY: Boaz goes up to the town gate and sits down. The gate was where business was done in those days, a bit like a town hall. And just then, the nearer kinsman comes walking by. Boaz calls to him, ho, such a one! Come here and sit down. And he sits down.

Then Boaz takes ten of the elders of the city, and says, sit ye down here. And they sit down. Now there are witnesses.

Boaz says to the kinsman, Naomi, who has come back from the country of Moab, is selling a piece of land that belonged to our brother Elimelech. I thought I should tell you. If you will redeem it, redeem it. If not, tell me. For there is no one else to redeem it but you, and I am next after you.

The man says, 'I will redeem it.'

For a moment, it seems that Boaz has lost.

But then Boaz tells him the rest. On the day you buy the field from Naomi, you must also take Ruth the Moabitess, the widow of the dead, to raise up the name of the dead upon his inheritance. In other words, if Ruth has a son, the land will belong to that son, to carry on her first husband’s family name. It will not belong to the kinsman’s own family.

The kinsman changes his mind. I cannot redeem it for myself, lest I mar mine own inheritance. He is afraid it will cost him too much, and harm what he has. Redeem it yourself, he says. I cannot.

In those days in Israel, there was a custom for making an agreement final. A man would take off his shoe, and give it to the other. That was how they signed the deal. So the kinsman says to Boaz, buy it for thee. And he draws off his shoe.

Then Boaz speaks to the elders, and to all the people. You are witnesses this day, that I have bought all that was Elimelech’s, and all that was Chilion’s and Mahlon’s, from the hand of Naomi.

He goes on.

All the people at the gate, and the elders, say, we are witnesses. And they bless him. May the LORD make the woman who is coming into your house like Rachel and like Leah, who together built the house of Israel. May you do well in Ephratah, and be famous in Bethlehem.

So Boaz takes Ruth, and she becomes his wife. And the LORD gives her a son.

The women of the town gather around Naomi.

And they go on. This child shall be unto thee a restorer of thy life, and a nourisher of thine old age. For your daughter in law, who loves you, who is better to you than seven sons, has given birth to him.

Better than seven sons. In that culture, seven sons was the most a family could hope for. And the women of Bethlehem say that Ruth, the foreign widow, is better than all of that.

Then Naomi takes the child, lays him in her arms, and cares for him as his nurse. The woman who said, I went out full, and the LORD hath brought me home again empty, now holds a baby in her arms. Her life has been filled again.

The neighbours give the child a name.

The book ends with a short family line. Pharez, Hezron, Ram, Amminadab, Nahshon, Salmon, Boaz, Obed, Jesse, and David. That last name is the point. The story of a poor widow from Moab and a kind farmer from Bethlehem leads to the greatest king Israel ever had.

KEY 4:10: 'Moreover Ruth the Moabitess, the wife of Mahlon, have I purchased to be my wife, to raise up the name of the dead upon his inheritance, that the name of the dead be not cut off from among his brethren, and from the gate of his place: ye are witnesses this day.' The nearer kinsman would not redeem, because it would cost him. Boaz redeemed gladly, though it cost him. He paid the price, and took Ruth as his wife, and gave her a home and a future. Christians have long seen in Boaz a picture of Jesus, who redeemed his people at great cost to himself, not because he had to, but because he loved them.

KEY 4:14: 'And the women said unto Naomi, Blessed be the LORD, which hath not left thee this day without a kinsman, that his name may be famous in Israel.' In chapter one, Naomi said that the LORD had dealt very bitterly with her. Now the women say, blessed be the LORD. He had not abandoned her after all. All through the dark days, the famine, the funerals and the long road home, God was quietly working. He had not left her without a redeemer.

KEY 4:17: 'And the women her neighbours gave it a name, saying, There is a son born to Naomi; and they called his name Obed: he is the father of Jesse, the father of David.' Obed means servant, or one who serves. And look where his line leads: to Jesse, and to David the king. Ruth and Naomi never knew that their story was part of something so much bigger. And many generations later, Jesus was born in Bethlehem, of the family of David. Ruth the Moabitess is named in his family line.

THREAD: The Gospel of Matthew lists the family line of Jesus, and says, 'Booz begat Obed of Ruth; and Obed begat Jesse; And Jesse begat David the king'. And Paul wrote of Jesus, 'In whom we have redemption through his blood, the forgiveness of sins, according to the riches of his grace'.

TODAY: Naomi could not see how her story would end, and you may not be able to see how yours will end either. But God is faithful, and he wastes nothing. Today, thank him for one way he has filled an empty place in your life.

QUESTION: Boaz redeemed Ruth and Naomi gladly, even though it cost him. What does it mean to you that Jesus has redeemed you, at great cost to himself?

PRAYER: Lord God, thank you that you never left Naomi without a redeemer, and you have not left me without one either. Thank you for Jesus, who paid the price to bring me home. When I feel empty, help me to trust that you are still at work, filling my life with your kindness. Amen.
