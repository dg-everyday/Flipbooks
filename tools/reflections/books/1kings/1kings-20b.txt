Resentful. Notice and came to Samaria. Home. Notice that Ahab was sullen. Notice that he did not repent. Notice that he was upset, not sorrowful. Notice that displeasure is not repentance. Notice that he resented God's rebuke. Notice that a heavy heart can lead to change or bitterness. Notice that Ahab chose bitterness. Notice that worldly sorrow does not change the heart. Notice that godly sorrow leads to repentance. Notice that Ahab had another chance and missed it. Notice that God's warnings were meant to turn him. Notice that heaviness without humility is wasted. Notice that the chapter ends sadly. Second Corinthians 7 says, 'For godly sorrow worketh repentance to salvation… but the sorrow of the world worketh death.' Notice two sorrows. Today, let God's rebuke lead you to repentance, not resentment. Choose godly sorrow.
S: The king of Israel went home to Samaria sullen and angry. Ahab was displeased rather than repentant, showing worldly sorrow that resents God's rebuke instead of turning to Him. Let God's rebuke lead you to repentance, for godly sorrow worketh repentance to salvation, but the sorrow of the world worketh death.
