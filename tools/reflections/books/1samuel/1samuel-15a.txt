# 1 Samuel 15:1-17: Saul's incomplete obedience

== 15:1
T: Hearken unto the voice of the words of the LORD
R: 'Samuel also said unto Saul, The LORD sent me to anoint thee to be king over his people, over Israel: now therefore hearken thou unto the voice of the words of the LORD.' Notice the LORD sent me to anoint thee. A reminder of God's grace. Notice to be king over his people. Israel belonged to God. Notice now therefore hearken thou. A call to listen. Notice unto the voice of the words of the LORD. God's command. Notice that Samuel grounded the command in Saul's calling. Notice that the one who anointed him now spoke for God. Notice that privilege brings responsibility. Notice that Saul was given another opportunity to obey. Notice that God's grace preceded His command. Notice that kingship meant listening to the true King. Deuteronomy 6 begins, 'Hear, O Israel.' Notice that hearing is the root of obedience. Today, remember who called you. Let gratitude for His grace make you eager to hear and obey His voice.
S: Samuel reminded Saul that the LORD had sent him to anoint Saul king over His people and told him to listen to the words of the LORD. God's grace in calling Saul came before His command, and privilege brought responsibility. Remember who called you and let gratitude make you eager to hear and obey His voice.
P: Hearken. 1 Samuel 15:1: 'The LORD sent me to anoint thee… now therefore hearken thou unto the voice of the words of the LORD.' Grace before command. Hear, O Israel.

When Israel was weak. Notice that Amalek attacked the weary and the stragglers. Notice that God remembered centuries later. Notice that justice delayed is not justice denied. Notice that God's patience had given Amalek generations to change. Notice that Amalek had remained hostile throughout. Deuteronomy 25 recalls how Amalek 'smote the hindmost of thee, even all that were feeble behind thee… and he feared not God.' Notice the cruelty remembered. Today, trust that God sees every wrong done to the weak. He remembers, and His justice will come in His time.
S: The LORD of hosts said He remembered how Amalek ambushed Israel on the way up from Egypt. Amalek had attacked the weary and feeble, and God's long patience had not changed their hostility. Trust that God sees every wrong done to the weak, for He remembers and His justice comes in His time.
P: God remembers. 1 Samuel 15:2: 'I remember that which Amalek did to Israel, how he laid wait for him in the way.' He sees wrongs done to the weak. His justice comes in time.

A command of judgment. Notice and utterly destroy all that they have. Complete. Notice and spare them not. No exceptions. Notice that this is one of the hardest commands in Scripture. Notice that it was a specific judgment on a specific nation, not a pattern for us. Notice that God alone, who gives life, has the right to judge. Notice that the command left no room for profit. Notice that the test was whether Saul would obey fully. Notice that we should read such passages with sorrow and reverence. Notice that the New Testament forbids us to take vengeance. Romans 12 says, 'Vengeance is mine; I will repay, saith the Lord.' Notice that judgment belongs to God. Today, leave judgment to God. Read hard passages humbly, and look to the cross, where Christ bore judgment for sinners.
S: God commanded Saul to strike Amalek and destroy everything they had, sparing nothing, as a specific judgment and a test of full obedience. This hard command was not a pattern for us, for judgment belongs to God alone, who gives life. Leave vengeance to the Lord and look to the cross, where Christ bore judgment for sinners.
P: A hard command. 1 Samuel 15:3: 'go and smite Amalek, and utterly destroy all that they have.' Judgment is God's alone. Vengeance is mine; I will repay, saith the Lord.

Notice that greed shaped their obedience. Notice that obedience that costs nothing is not real obedience. Second Samuel 24 records David saying, 'neither will I offer… unto the LORD my God of that which doth cost me nothing.' Notice that real obedience costs. Today, do not obey only where it is easy. S: Saul and the people spared Agag and the best of the livestock and everything good, destroying only what was worthless. They kept the best and gave up the worst, letting greed shape their obedience. Do not obey only where it is easy, for real obedience will not offer God what costs nothing.
P: Keeping the best. 1 Samuel 15:9: 'spared Agag, and the best of the sheep… but every thing that was vile… they destroyed.' Easy obedience is not obedience.
