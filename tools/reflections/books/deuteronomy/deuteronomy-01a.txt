# Deuteronomy 1:1-23: Moses recalls Horeb, the judges and the way to Kadesh-barnea

== 1:1
T: The words which Moses spake
R: 'These be the words which Moses spake unto all Israel on this side Jordan in the wilderness, in the plain over against the Red sea…' These are the words Moses spoke to all Israel east of the Jordan, in the wilderness plain. Notice the words which Moses spake. Deuteronomy is a book of speeches, an old shepherd's last sermons to his flock. Notice unto all Israel. Not only the leaders but every family heard them. Notice on this side Jordan. They stood at the edge of the promise, close enough to see it but not yet inside. The place names recall forty years of wandering, a map of lessons learned the hard way. Before Israel crossed over, God gave them time to remember. Psalm 119 prays, 'The entrance of thy words giveth light.' Today, stand at the edge of whatever is next and listen first. God often speaks most clearly to us before a crossing. Let His word, not your nerves, prepare you for what lies ahead.
S: Deuteronomy opens with the words Moses spoke to all Israel east of the Jordan, in the wilderness plain. The place names recall forty years of wandering, and God gave His people time to remember before they crossed. Stand at the edge of whatever is next and listen first, letting His word prepare you.
P: Listen before you cross. Deuteronomy 1:1: 'These be the words which Moses spake unto all Israel on this side Jordan.' God gave His people time to remember before they went in. Let His word prepare you for what's next.

== 1:2
T: Eleven days' journey
R: '(There are eleven days… journey from Horeb by the way of mount Seir unto Kadesh-barnea.)' It is an eleven-day journey from Horeb to Kadesh-barnea by the Mount Seir road. Notice eleven days. That was all the distance between the mountain of the law and the door of the promised land. Notice that Israel took forty years. The trip was short; the delay was long. Notice that the gap was not geography but unbelief. They reached Kadesh quickly, then refused to go in, and the wilderness became their classroom. Notice that Moses tucks this fact in like a quiet sigh in parentheses. Hebrews 3 says, 'So we see that they could not enter in because of unbelief.' Some of our longest seasons are not caused by the road but by our hearts. Today, ask whether fear is turning an eleven-day journey into a forty-year detour. Trust God and take the next step He has shown you.
S: The journey from Horeb to Kadesh-barnea took only eleven days, yet Israel spent forty years reaching the promised land. The delay came not from the distance but from unbelief, which turned a short trip into a long wilderness. Ask whether fear is lengthening your journey, and trust God enough to take the next step.
P: A short road, a long delay. Deuteronomy 1:2: 'There are eleven days… journey from Horeb… unto Kadesh-barnea.' Israel took forty years. Unbelief, not distance, made the detour. Trust God and take the next step.

== 1:23
T: The saying pleased me well
R: 'And the saying pleased me well: and I took twelve men of you, one of a tribe.' The idea seemed good to me, so I chose twelve of you, one man from each tribe. Notice the saying pleased me well. Even Moses thought it was a good plan. Notice twelve men… one of a tribe. Every tribe was represented, so no one could later say their voice was missing. Notice that Moses is honest about his part. He does not blame the people alone. Notice that good plans can still become the setting for failure. What went wrong was not the scouting but the report and the unbelief that followed. Notice that wise structure cannot replace faithful hearts. Proverbs 16 says, 'There is a way that seemeth right unto a man.' Today, be honest about your part in things that went wrong. Even good decisions need hearts that trust God to carry them through.
S: The request to send spies pleased Moses, and he chose twelve men, one from each tribe. Moses honestly owns his part, and the plan itself was not the failure; the unbelief that followed was. Be honest about your part when things go wrong, and remember that good plans still need trusting hearts.
