# Deuteronomy 1:24-46: The spies, the refusal at Kadesh and the defeat at Hormah

== 1:24
T: They searched it out
R: 'And they turned and went up into the mountain, and came unto the valley of Eshcol, and searched it out.' The spies left, went up into the hill country, came to the Valley of Eshcol and explored it. Notice they went up. The spies did what the whole nation would later refuse to do. They walked into the land and came back alive. Notice the valley of Eshcol. Its name means cluster, for the great bunch of grapes they found there. Notice searched it out. They saw the land with their own eyes. Their experience proved that the land could be entered. Notice that twelve men walked safely through the very place the people would fear to go. God protected them as a foretaste of what He would do for all. Psalm 34 says, 'O taste and see that the LORD is good.' Today, notice the small proofs God gives you that His way is safe. Let them grow your courage for the larger steps ahead.
S: The spies went up into the hill country, reached the valley of Eshcol and searched out the land. They walked safely through the very place the people would later fear to enter. Notice the small proofs God gives you that His way is safe and let them grow your courage for larger steps.
P: A foretaste of the promise. Deuteronomy 1:24: 'they turned and went up into the mountain, and came unto the valley of Eshcol, and searched it out.' Twelve men walked in safely. Let God's small proofs grow your courage.

== 1:25
T: It is a good land
R: 'And they took of the fruit of the land in their hands, and brought it down unto us, and brought us word again, and said, It is a good land which the LORD our God doth give us.' They took some of the fruit of the land, brought it down to us and reported, The land the LORD our God is giving us is good. Notice the fruit… in their hands. They held the evidence. The goodness of the land was not a rumour; it was something they could taste. Notice it is a good land. Even the frightened spies admitted this. Notice which the LORD our God doth give us. The report itself named God as the giver. Notice that Israel had proof, testimony and promise all at once. What more could they need? Psalm 16 says, 'yea, I have a goodly heritage.' Today, hold onto the evidence of God's goodness in your hands. When fear speaks later, remember the fruit you have already tasted.
S: The spies brought back fruit from the land and reported that it was a good land which the LORD was giving them. Israel held the evidence in their hands and heard the testimony that God was the giver. Hold onto the evidence of God's goodness you have already tasted when fear begins to speak.
P: A good land. Deuteronomy 1:25: 'they took of the fruit of the land in their hands… and said, It is a good land which the LORD our God doth give us.' They held the proof. Remember the goodness you've already tasted.

== 1:26
T: Ye would not go up
R: 'Notwithstanding ye would not go up, but rebelled against the commandment of the LORD your God.' But you were unwilling to go up; you rebelled against the command of the LORD your God. Notice notwithstanding. Despite the good fruit, the good report and the good promise, they refused. Notice ye would not. It was not that they could not; they would not. The issue was the will. Notice rebelled. Moses does not soften it. Refusing God's clear command is rebellion, even when it is dressed as caution. Notice against the commandment of the LORD your God. Their quarrel was not with Moses or the spies but with God Himself. Hebrews 3 warns, 'To day if ye will hear his voice, harden not your hearts.' Today, be honest about any place where you would not rather than could not. Name it before God. Surrender is often a matter of the will before it is a matter of strength.
S: Despite the good fruit and the good report, Israel would not go up and rebelled against the commandment of the LORD. Their refusal was a matter of the will, not of ability, and their quarrel was with God Himself. Be honest about any place where you will not obey, and surrender your will to Him.
P: Would not, not could not. Deuteronomy 1:26: 'Notwithstanding ye would not go up, but rebelled against the commandment of the LORD.' Refusing God's clear word is rebellion, even when it looks like caution. Surrender your will.

== 1:46
T: Ye abode in Kadesh many days
R: 'So ye abode in Kadesh many days, according unto the days that ye abode there.' And so you stayed in Kadesh many days, all the time that you spent there. Notice abode in Kadesh. The place that should have been a launching point became a waiting room. Notice many days. The phrase is vague on purpose, as if Moses did not want to count the wasted time. Notice the quiet ending. The chapter that began with eleven days' journey ends with many days of waiting. Yet even here God did not abandon them. He fed them, led them and kept His promise for their children. Notice that God's patience outlasted their failure. Lamentations 3 says, 'his compassions fail not. They are new every morning.' Today, if you are in a long season of waiting because of past choices, do not despair. God is still present in Kadesh. Use the waiting to learn trust, so the next crossing finds you ready.
