# Deuteronomy 2:1-19: Passing Edom and Moab, and the end of the wilderness generation

== 2:1
T: We compassed mount Seir many days
R: 'Then we turned, and took our journey into the wilderness by the way of the Red sea, as the LORD spake unto me: and we compassed mount Seir many days.' Then we turned back into the wilderness along the Red Sea road, as the LORD told me, and for a long time we went around Mount Seir. Notice we turned. This time they obeyed. After the disaster at Hormah, Israel finally followed God's direction. Notice as the LORD spake unto me. Even the wilderness years were guided by God's word. Notice compassed mount Seir many days. Circling the same mountain, year after year. It must have felt like going nowhere. Yet God was at work, raising a new generation and teaching them dependence. Notice that circling is not wasted when God is leading it. Psalm 31 says, 'My times are in thy hand.' Today, if you feel you are walking in circles, keep following God's word. He is shaping you even in the repetitive seasons.
S: Israel turned back into the wilderness as the LORD commanded and circled mount Seir for many days. The years felt like going nowhere, yet God was raising a new generation and teaching them dependence. If you feel you are walking in circles, keep following God's word, for He is shaping you in repetitive seasons.
P: Circling the mountain. Deuteronomy 2:1: 'we compassed mount Seir many days.' Years of going around the same ground, yet God was at work. Keep following Him in the repetitive seasons.

== 2:2
T: The LORD spake unto me
R: 'And the LORD spake unto me, saying…' Then the LORD said to me. Notice how short this verse is. After many days of circling, it simply says the LORD spoke. Notice that God broke the silence. The long wilderness season did not end because Israel grew tired, but because God gave a new word. Notice unto me. God spoke to Moses, who would pass the word on to the people. He still speaks through His word and through those who faithfully share it. Notice that there is a time for waiting and a time for a fresh command. Neither lasts forever. Notice that God's timing is precise. He knew exactly when the generation of Kadesh had passed and when the new generation was ready. Ecclesiastes 3 says, 'To every thing there is a season.' Today, keep listening in the long seasons. God has not forgotten you, and when the time is right, He will speak the word that moves you forward. Stay near His word so you are ready to hear.
S: After many days of circling mount Seir, the LORD spoke to Moses again. The long wilderness season ended not because Israel grew tired but because God gave a new word at the right time. Keep listening in your long seasons, for God has not forgotten you and will speak when the time is right.
P: God speaks again. Deuteronomy 2:2: 'And the LORD spake unto me, saying.' After the long circling, a fresh word. Stay near His word so you're ready to hear when He moves you forward.

== 2:3
T: Turn you northward
R: 'Ye have compassed this mountain long enough: turn you northward.' You have been circling this hill country long enough. Now turn north. Notice long enough again. God said these words at Horeb, and now He says them at Seir. Every season has an end that God determines. Notice turn you northward. North meant toward the promised land. After years of circling, the direction finally changed. Notice how simple the command is. No long explanation, just a new direction. When God moves us, He often gives just enough light for the next step. Notice that the generation who had refused was gone; this generation would go forward. God's purposes had not been abandoned, only delayed. Isaiah 43 says, 'Behold, I will do a new thing; now it shall spring forth.' Today, listen for God's long enough. When He turns you in a new direction, go willingly. His delays are never denials of His promises.
S: God told Israel they had circled the mountain long enough and should now turn northward. After years of wandering, the direction finally changed toward the land He had promised. Listen for God's long enough, and when He turns you in a new direction, follow Him willingly.
P: Long enough; turn north. Deuteronomy 2:3: 'Ye have compassed this mountain long enough: turn you northward.' God's delays are not denials. When He turns you, go.

== 2:4
T: Take good heed unto yourselves
R: 'And command thou the people, saying, Ye are to pass through the coast of your brethren the children of Esau, which dwell in Seir; and they shall be afraid of you: take ye good heed unto yourselves therefore.' Give the people these orders: You are about to pass through the territory of your relatives, the descendants of Esau who live in Seir. They will be afraid of you, so be very careful. Notice your brethren. Edom descended from Esau, Jacob's twin. Notice they shall be afraid of you. Israel now had power over others. Notice take ye good heed unto yourselves. When others fear us, we must guard our own hearts. Power can tempt us to bully. Notice that God cared about how Israel treated a frightened neighbour. Romans 12 says, 'If it be possible, as much as lieth in you, live peaceably with all men.' Today, if you have influence over someone who fears you, handle it gently. Strength under God's control is a gift, not a weapon.
S: God told Israel to pass through the land of their brethren, the children of Esau, who would be afraid of them. He warned them to take good heed to themselves rather than exploit that fear. If someone fears your power or influence, handle it gently, remembering that strength under God's control is a gift.
P: Guard your heart when others fear you. Deuteronomy 2:4: 'they shall be afraid of you: take ye good heed unto yourselves therefore.' Power can tempt us to bully. Use strength gently.

== 2:5
T: Not so much as a footbreadth
R: 'Meddle not with them; for I will not give you of their land, no, not so much as a footbreadth; because I have given mount Seir unto Esau for a possession.' Do not provoke them to war, for I will not give you any of their land, not even enough to put your foot on. I have given Esau the hill country of Seir as his own. Notice meddle not. Israel was to leave Edom alone. Notice not so much as a footbreadth. God set a firm boundary. Notice I have given mount Seir unto Esau. God had made promises to Esau too. He is Lord of every nation, not just Israel. Notice that what God has given to others is not ours to take. Contentment means respecting His gifts to others as much as His gifts to us. Exodus 20 says, 'Thou shalt not covet.' Today, honour the boundaries God has set. Rejoice in what He has given you, and do not reach for what He has given someone else.
S: God told Israel not to meddle with Edom, for He would not give them even a footbreadth of the land He had given to Esau. God is Lord of every nation and keeps His promises to others too. Honour the boundaries God has set, rejoice in your own portion, and do not reach for what He has given someone else.
P: Not a footbreadth. Deuteronomy 2:5: 'I will not give you of their land, no, not so much as a footbreadth; because I have given mount Seir unto Esau.' Respect what God has given others. Be content with your portion.

== 2:6
T: Buy meat for money
R: 'Ye shall buy meat of them for money, that ye may eat; and ye shall also buy water of them for money, that ye may drink.' You are to pay them in silver for the food you eat and the water you drink. Notice buy… for money. Israel was to pay its way honestly. They were not to plunder or take by force. Notice even water. In the desert, water was precious, and Israel was to pay for every drop. Notice that this was a test of character. A large, strong nation could have simply taken what it wanted. Instead God required fair dealing. Notice that honesty in small trades reflects the God we serve. Romans 13 says, 'Owe no man any thing, but to love one another.' Today, deal honestly in your transactions. Pay what you owe, do not take advantage of the weaker party, and let your fairness testify to the character of God. Integrity in daily business is part of worship.
S: God told Israel to buy food and even water from Edom with money rather than taking it by force. A strong nation was required to deal fairly and pay its way honestly. Deal honestly in your transactions, pay what you owe, and let your fairness testify to the character of God.
P: Pay your way. Deuteronomy 2:6: 'Ye shall buy meat of them for money… and ye shall also buy water of them for money.' Even the strong must deal fairly. Let your honesty honour God.

== 2:7
T: Thou hast lacked nothing
R: 'For the LORD thy God hath blessed thee in all the works of thy hand: he knoweth thy walking through this great wilderness: these forty years the LORD thy God hath been with thee; thou hast lacked nothing.' The LORD your God has blessed all your work. He has watched over your journey through this vast wilderness. These forty years He has been with you, and you have not lacked anything. Notice hath blessed thee. Israel could pay because God had provided. Notice he knoweth thy walking. God knew every step. Notice these forty years. Even the years of judgment were years of His presence. Notice thou hast lacked nothing. Manna, water, clothes that did not wear out: all their needs were met. Philippians 4 says, 'my God shall supply all your need.' Today, look back over your own wilderness years. Count what God has provided. You may have lacked comfort or ease, but in Him you have not lacked what you truly needed.
S: Moses reminded Israel that God had blessed their work, known their every step and been with them for forty years so that they lacked nothing. Even the years of judgment were years of His presence and provision. Look back over your own wilderness and count how God has supplied what you truly needed.
P: Lacked nothing. Deuteronomy 2:7: 'these forty years the LORD thy God hath been with thee; thou hast lacked nothing.' Even in the wilderness, He provided. Count what He has supplied you.

== 2:8
T: We passed by
R: 'And when we passed by from our brethren the children of Esau, which dwelt in Seir, through the way of the plain from Elath, and from Ezion-gaber, we turned and passed by the way of the wilderness of Moab.' Moses still called Edom brothers, even though Edom had once refused them passage. Notice the careful route. Elath, Ezion-gaber, then the wilderness of Moab. Obedience is often worked out in ordinary geography, one road at a time. Notice that walking past a fight can be an act of faith. Proverbs 20 says, 'It is an honour for a man to cease from strife.' Today, choose to pass by a quarrel God has not called you into. Treat difficult relatives as family, and keep walking the road He has shown you.
S: Israel passed by their brethren, the children of Esau, taking the road from Elath and Ezion-gaber toward the wilderness of Moab. They obeyed God by not fighting Edom, and Moses still called them brothers. Choose to pass by quarrels God has not called you into, and keep walking the road He has shown you.
P: Walk on by. Deuteronomy 2:8: 'when we passed by from our brethren the children of Esau… we turned and passed by.' Not every fight is yours. Cease from strife and keep walking God's road.

== 2:10
T: A people great, and many, and tall
R: 'The Emims dwelt therein in times past, a people great, and many, and tall, as the Anakims.' The Emites used to live there, a people strong and numerous and as tall as the Anakites. Notice the Emims. Before Moab lived there, giants occupied the land. Notice great, and many, and tall. These are the very words the frightened spies used of Canaan. Notice the quiet lesson. Moses inserts this history note to show that giants had been driven out before. If Lot's descendants could inherit a land of giants, so could Israel. Notice that past victories, even other people's, can strengthen our faith. God had already shown He was greater than giants. First Samuel 17 records David saying, 'The LORD that delivered me out of the paw of the lion… he will deliver me.' Today, remember that the giants you fear are not new to God. He has already overcome far greater ones throughout history.
S: Moses noted that the Emims, a people great, many and tall like the Anakims, had once lived in Moab's land. Lot's descendants had inherited a land of giants, showing that such enemies could be overcome. Remember that the giants you fear are not new to God, for He has overcome greater ones before.
P: Giants have fallen before. Deuteronomy 2:10: 'The Emims dwelt therein in times past, a people great, and many, and tall, as the Anakims.' God has overcome giants before. He can again.

== 2:11
T: Accounted giants
R: 'Which also were accounted giants, as the Anakims; but the Moabites call them Emims.' They too were considered Rephaim, like the Anakites, but the Moabites called them Emites. Notice accounted giants. They had a fearsome reputation. Notice the different names. Each people had its own word for the giants who once lived among them. Notice the point Moses is making: giants were common in the region's history, and they had all been removed. Israel's dread of the Anakims was not unique. Others had faced similar enemies and lived. Notice that fears seem less overwhelming when we see them in the wider story of God's dealings. First Corinthians 10 says, 'There hath no temptation taken you but such as is common to man.' Today, remember that you are not the first to face your giant. Others have stood where you stand, and God brought them through. Their stories are reminders that He can do the same for you.
S: The Emims were accounted giants like the Anakims, though the Moabites had their own name for them. Giants were part of the region's history, and they had all been removed before Israel arrived. Remember that you are not the first to face your giant, and that God has brought others through.
P: Not the first. Deuteronomy 2:11: 'Which also were accounted giants, as the Anakims.' Others faced giants and God brought them through. Your struggle is not new to Him.

The Horites used to live in Seir, but the descendants of Esau drove them out and settled in their place, just as Israel did in the land the LORD gave them. Notice the Horims. Another ancient people, replaced by Esau's family. Notice as Israel did. Israel had already begun to possess land east of the Jordan. Notice which the LORD gave. Daniel 2 says, 'he removeth kings, and setteth up kings.' Today, hold your possessions with an open hand. Everything you have is held by God's permission. Thank Him as the giver.
S: The Horims had once lived in Seir until the children of Esau replaced them, just as Israel was receiving its land from the LORD. History shows nations rising and falling under God's hand, and possession is first His gift. Hold your possessions with an open hand and thank God as the giver of all you have.
P: Held by His hand. Deuteronomy 2:12: 'as Israel did unto the land of his possession, which the LORD gave unto them.' Every possession is God's gift. Hold yours with thanks and open hands.
