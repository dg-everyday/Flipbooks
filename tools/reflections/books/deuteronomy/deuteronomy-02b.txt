# Deuteronomy 2:20-37: Giants of old, and the defeat of Sihon king of Heshbon

== 2:20
T: A land of giants
R: '(That also was accounted a land of giants: giants dwelt therein in old time; and the Ammonites call them Zamzummims.' That too was considered a land of the Rephaim, who used to live there; the Ammonites called them Zamzummites. Notice that also. Moses again pauses to tell the story of giants. Notice giants dwelt therein in old time. Another nation, another land of giants, another story of them being removed. Notice the repetition. Moses tells this kind of history three times in one chapter. He wants the new generation to grasp something their parents missed: giants are not invincible. Notice Zamzummims. Some think the name meant murmurers or whisperers. Fear often whispers. Notice that Moses uses history as medicine for fear. Psalm 77 says, 'I will remember the works of the LORD: surely I will remember thy wonders of old.' Today, rehearse the stories of how God has overcome what seemed impossible. Remembering builds courage that feelings alone cannot.
S: Moses noted that Ammon's land had also been a land of giants, called Zamzummims by the Ammonites. He told such stories repeatedly so that the new generation would learn that giants are not invincible. Rehearse the stories of how God has overcome the impossible, and let remembering build your courage.
P: Another land of giants. Deuteronomy 2:20: 'That also was accounted a land of giants.' Moses kept telling these stories so Israel would learn: giants fall. Remember God's wonders of old.

== 2:21
T: The LORD destroyed them
R: 'A people great, and many, and tall, as the Anakims; but the LORD destroyed them before them; and they succeeded them, and dwelt in their stead.' They were a people strong and numerous and as tall as the Anakites. The LORD destroyed them from before the Ammonites, who drove them out and settled in their place. Notice great, and many, and tall again. The same description as the giants Israel feared. Notice but the LORD destroyed them. Here is the key. It was not Ammon's strength but God's action that removed the giants. Notice that God did this for Ammon, a people outside the covenant. If He did this for Lot's descendants, how much more for His own people? Notice that every but the LORD in Scripture changes the story. Psalm 124 says, 'If it had not been the LORD who was on our side… Then they had swallowed us up quick.' Today, place a but the LORD over your fears. Great, many and tall are no match for Him.
S: The Zamzummims were great, many and tall like the Anakims, but the LORD destroyed them before the Ammonites, who took their place. It was God's action, not Ammon's strength, that removed the giants. Place a but the LORD over your fears, because great, many and tall are no match for Him.
P: But the LORD. Deuteronomy 2:21: 'A people great, and many, and tall… but the LORD destroyed them.' Those two words change everything. Put 'but the LORD' over your fears.

== 2:22
T: As he did to the children of Esau
R: 'As he did to the children of Esau, which dwelt in Seir, when he destroyed the Horims from before them; and they succeeded them, and dwelt in their stead even unto this day.' The LORD had done the same for Esau's descendants in Seir, removing the Horites so they could live there to this day. Notice as he did. God's actions form a pattern. What He did for Esau, He did for Ammon, and He would do for Israel. Notice he destroyed. Again the credit goes to God. Notice even unto this day. The results were lasting. Edom still lived in the land God had given them. Notice that God's faithfulness can be traced in the lives of others. Their testimonies are evidence for our faith. Hebrews 13 says, 'Jesus Christ the same yesterday, and to day, and for ever.' Today, look for the patterns of God's faithfulness in other people's stories. The God who acted for them is the same God who acts for you.
S: Moses explained that God had done the same for the children of Esau, destroying the Horims so Edom could live in Seir to that day. God's actions formed a pattern of faithfulness that Israel could trust. Look for the patterns of God's faithfulness in others' stories, for He is the same God who acts for you.
P: A pattern of faithfulness. Deuteronomy 2:22: 'As he did to the children of Esau… when he destroyed the Horims from before them.' What God did for others, He can do for you. He doesn't change.

== 2:23
T: The Avims and the Caphtorims
R: 'And the Avims which dwelt in Hazerim, even unto Azzah, the Caphtorims, which came forth out of Caphtor, destroyed them, and dwelt in their stead.)' And as for the Avvites who lived in villages as far as Gaza, the Caphtorites coming out from Caphtor destroyed them and settled in their place. Notice the Avims and the Caphtorims. Even the coastal peoples had changed hands. The Caphtorims are often linked with the Philistines, who came from across the sea. Notice that this is the last of Moses' historical notes. The whole region had seen peoples come and go. Notice that God is Lord of all history, not just Israel's. Every border and migration falls under His rule. Notice that no nation is permanent except by God's will. Psalm 22 says, 'For the kingdom is the LORD'S: and he is the governor among the nations.' Today, take comfort that world events are not out of God's hands. Nations rise and fall, but His kingdom stands, and His purposes for you are secure in it.
S: The Avims who lived as far as Azzah were destroyed by the Caphtorims from Caphtor, who settled in their place. The whole region had seen peoples come and go, showing that God is Lord of all history. Take comfort that world events are in God's hands and that His kingdom and purposes stand secure.
P: Lord of all history. Deuteronomy 2:23: 'the Caphtorims, which came forth out of Caphtor, destroyed them, and dwelt in their stead.' Nations rise and fall; God's kingdom stands.

== 2:24
T: Begin to possess it
R: 'Rise ye up, take your journey, and pass over the river Arnon: behold, I have given into thine hand Sihon the Amorite, king of Heshbon, and his land: begin to possess it, and contend with him in battle.' Notice I have given into thine hand. The victory was promised before the battle began. Notice begin to possess it. This was the beginning of the inheritance, a first step toward the whole. Notice contend with him in battle. Faith still had to fight. The gift had to be received through obedience. First Timothy 6 says, 'Fight the good fight of faith, lay hold on eternal life.' Today, begin to take hold of what God has promised. Do the first part, and trust Him for the rest.
S: God told Israel to cross the river Arnon, for He had given Sihon king of Heshbon and his land into their hand. Israel was to begin to possess it and fight, receiving the promised victory through obedience. Begin to take hold of what God has promised, doing the first part and trusting Him for the rest.
P: Begin to possess it. Deuteronomy 2:24: 'behold, I have given into thine hand Sihon… begin to possess it.' The victory was promised before the battle. Take the first step and trust Him for the rest.

== 2:25
T: The dread of thee upon the nations
R: 'This day will I begin to put the dread of thee and the fear of thee upon the nations that are under the whole heaven, who shall hear report of thee, and shall tremble, and be in anguish because of thee.' Today I will begin to put the fear of you on the nations; they will hear of you and tremble. Notice this day will I begin. God set the timing. Notice the dread of thee. Forty years earlier Israel had been afraid. Now the nations would be afraid of Israel. Notice who shall hear report. News of God's acts would go before them. Rahab in Jericho would later say, 'our hearts did melt.' Notice that the reversal was God's work. Israel's reputation came from His power, not their strength. Notice that God goes ahead of His people to prepare hearts. Today, trust that God can prepare the way ahead of you. He works in places you have not yet reached.
S: God promised to begin putting the dread of Israel on all nations who heard reports of them, so they would tremble. The people who once feared now became the ones others feared, entirely through God's power. Trust that God is preparing the way ahead of you in places you have not yet reached.
P: God goes ahead. Deuteronomy 2:25: 'This day will I begin to put the dread of thee and the fear of thee upon the nations.' The fearful became feared, by God's power. He prepares the way ahead of you.

== 2:26
T: With words of peace
R: 'And I sent messengers out of the wilderness of Kedemoth unto Sihon king of Heshbon with words of peace, saying…' From the desert of Kedemoth I sent messengers to Sihon king of Heshbon offering peace. Notice words of peace. Even though God had promised victory, Moses first offered peace. Notice that Israel did not start the war. They sought a peaceful passage. Notice the wisdom here. Knowing God would give you victory does not mean you should seek a fight. Moses gave Sihon the opportunity to choose peace. Notice that God's people should be peacemakers even when they are strong. Matthew 5 says, 'Blessed are the peacemakers: for they shall be called the children of God.' Today, offer words of peace first, even when you expect conflict. Give others the chance to choose well. If conflict comes, let it not be because you refused to seek peace. Strength and gentleness can live together in a person who trusts God.
S: Moses sent messengers to Sihon king of Heshbon with words of peace, even though God had promised victory. Israel did not start the war but gave Sihon the chance to choose peace. Offer words of peace first, even when you expect conflict, and give others the opportunity to choose well.
P: Peace first. Deuteronomy 2:26: 'I sent messengers… unto Sihon king of Heshbon with words of peace.' Even with victory promised, Moses sought peace. Blessed are the peacemakers.

== 2:27
T: Neither to the right hand nor the left
R: 'Let me pass through thy land: I will go along by the high way, I will neither turn unto the right hand nor to the left.' Let us pass through your country. We will stay on the main road; we will not turn aside to the right or to the left. Notice let me pass. A humble request, not a demand. Notice by the high way. Israel promised to stay on the public road. Notice neither to the right hand nor to the left. They promised not to wander into fields or vineyards or take anything. Notice that this phrase appears elsewhere in Deuteronomy about keeping God's law. Staying on the road is a picture of faithful living. Notice that Israel's promise was specific and restrained. Proverbs 4 says, 'Turn not to the right hand nor to the left: remove thy foot from evil.' Today, keep to the road God has shown you. Make clear, honest promises and keep them. Walk straight without wandering into what is not yours.
S: Israel asked Sihon to let them pass through his land, promising to stay on the highway without turning to the right or left. Their request was humble and specific, and the phrase pictures faithful living on God's road. Keep to the road God has shown you and make honest promises that you faithfully keep.
P: Stay on the road. Deuteronomy 2:27: 'I will go along by the high way, I will neither turn unto the right hand nor to the left.' Walk straight. Keep your promises. Don't wander into what isn't yours.

== 2:28
T: Only I will pass through on my feet
R: 'Thou shalt sell me meat for money, that I may eat; and give me water for money, that I may drink: only I will pass through on my feet.' Sell us food to eat and water to drink for their price in silver. Only let us pass through on foot. Notice for money. Israel offered to pay for everything, as they had with Edom. Notice only I will pass through on my feet. They wanted nothing but passage. No land, no plunder. Notice that Israel's offer was fair, generous and peaceful. Sihon had every reason to accept. Notice that when we act honourably, any conflict that follows is not on our account. Our responsibility is to deal rightly; others are responsible for their response. Romans 12 says, 'Provide things honest in the sight of all men.' Today, make your dealings so fair that no one can reasonably object. You cannot control others' responses, but you can control your own integrity.
S: Israel offered to buy food and water from Sihon with money and asked only to pass through on foot. Their offer was fair, generous and peaceful, leaving Sihon every reason to accept. Make your dealings so fair that no one can reasonably object, and leave others' responses to God.
P: A fair offer. Deuteronomy 2:28: 'sell me meat for money… give me water for money… only I will pass through on my feet.' Deal so honestly no one can object. Leave the response to God.

== 2:34
T: Utterly destroyed
R: 'And we took all his cities at that time, and utterly destroyed the men, and the women, and the little ones, of every city, we left none to remain.' At that time we took all his towns and completely destroyed them: men, women and children. We left no survivors. Notice utterly destroyed. This is one of the hardest verses in Scripture. Israel acted as God's instrument of judgment on the Amorites, whose iniquity, God told Abraham long before, would one day be full. Notice that this was not ordinary warfare or a pattern for today. It was a unique judgment at a unique moment in salvation history. Notice that it shows how seriously God takes sin. Generations of evil had reached their end. Notice too that God had waited four hundred years. Genesis 15 says, 'for the iniquity of the Amorites is not yet full.' Today, let hard verses humble you. God's justice is real and His patience is long. Run to the mercy He offers now in Christ.
S: Israel took all of Sihon's cities and utterly destroyed the men, women and little ones, leaving none to remain. This was a unique act of God's judgment on the Amorites after centuries of patience, not a pattern for today. Let hard verses humble you, remembering that God's justice is real, His patience long and His mercy offered now in Christ.
P: A hard verse. Deuteronomy 2:34: 'we took all his cities at that time, and utterly destroyed.' A unique judgment after centuries of patience. God's justice is real; His mercy is offered now.
