# Deuteronomy 5:1-16: The covenant at Horeb and the first five commandments

== 5:1
T: Learn them, and keep, and do them
R: 'And Moses called all Israel, and said unto them, Hear, O Israel, the statutes and judgments which I speak in your ears this day, that ye may learn them, and keep, and do them.' Moses summoned all Israel and said: Hear the decrees and laws I declare today; learn them and follow them. Notice called all Israel. Everyone was invited to listen. Notice hear… learn… keep… do. Four steps that move God's word from the ear to the life. Hearing is the start, learning makes it ours, keeping guards it, doing lives it. Notice in your ears this day. The word was personal and present. Notice that obedience is a process. We do not jump from hearing to doing without learning and holding on. James 1 says, 'be ye doers of the word, and not hearers only.' Today, take one part of God's word through all four steps. Hear it, learn it, keep it in your heart and do it before the day ends.
S: Moses called all Israel to hear the statutes and judgments he spoke that day so that they would learn, keep and do them. The four steps move God's word from the ear into daily life. Take one part of God's word through all four steps today, hearing, learning, keeping and doing it.
P: Hear, learn, keep, do. Deuteronomy 5:1: 'Hear, O Israel, the statutes and judgments… that ye may learn them, and keep, and do them.' Take one truth all the way from your ears to your life today.

== 5:2
T: A covenant with us in Horeb
R: 'The LORD our God made a covenant with us in Horeb.' The LORD our God made a covenant with us at Horeb. Notice the LORD our God. Personal and relational. Not a distant deity, but our God. Notice made a covenant. A binding, solemn relationship, like a marriage. God committed Himself to Israel, and Israel to Him. Notice with us. The covenant included the people listening, not just their ancestors. Notice in Horeb. A real place and a real moment in history. Notice that a covenant is more than rules. It is a promise of belonging. God said, I will be your God and you will be My people. Jeremiah 31 looks ahead: 'I will make a new covenant with the house of Israel.' Today, remember that God has bound Himself to His people by covenant. In Christ, you are included in a new covenant sealed by His blood. You belong to Him by His own promise.
S: Moses reminded Israel that the LORD their God made a covenant with them at Horeb. The covenant was a binding relationship of belonging, not merely a set of rules. Remember that God has bound Himself to His people by covenant, and in Christ you belong to Him through a new covenant sealed by His blood.
P: Bound by covenant. Deuteronomy 5:2: 'The LORD our God made a covenant with us in Horeb.' More than rules, a promise of belonging. In Christ, you belong to Him.
H: #DailyGrace #Deuteronomy52 #Covenant #Belonging #Horeb #BibleVerse

== 5:3
T: With us, even us
R: 'The LORD made not this covenant with our fathers, but with us, even us, who are all of us here alive this day.' It was not with our ancestors that the LORD made this covenant, but with us, with all of us who are alive here today. Notice not… with our fathers. Moses means not only with them. The covenant was not merely history. Notice but with us, even us. Every generation must make the covenant its own. Notice all of us here alive this day. Most of the listeners had been children at Horeb, yet the covenant was theirs. Notice that faith cannot be inherited secondhand. Each person must receive God's promises personally. Notice the emphasis: us, even us, all of us. Second Corinthians 6 says, 'now is the day of salvation.' Today, receive God's covenant as yours. Not your parents' faith or your church's tradition, but your own living relationship with Him today.
S: Moses told Israel that the covenant was not only with their fathers but with all of them alive that day. Each generation must receive God's covenant personally, for faith cannot be inherited secondhand. Receive God's covenant as your own, not merely your parents' faith but your own living relationship with Him.
P: With us, even us. Deuteronomy 5:3: 'with us, even us, who are all of us here alive this day.' Faith can't be inherited secondhand. Make God's covenant your own today.
H: #DailyGrace #Deuteronomy53 #PersonalFaith #Covenant #Today #BibleVerse

== 5:4
T: Face to face out of the fire
R: 'The LORD talked with you face to face in the mount out of the midst of the fire.' The LORD spoke to you face to face out of the fire on the mountain. Notice talked with you. God did not merely issue rules; He spoke to His people. Notice face to face. Direct and personal. Not through a messenger only, but in a meeting. Notice out of the midst of the fire. Holy, overwhelming, yet personal. God's holiness and His closeness met at Sinai. Notice that this relationship began with God's initiative. He came down and spoke. Notice that the God who spoke face to face at Sinai later came in the flesh. John 1 says, 'And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us.' Today, remember that God desires personal communion with you. He has spoken in His word and in His Son. Come near and listen. He still speaks to His people with personal care.
S: Moses reminded Israel that the LORD talked with them face to face out of the fire on the mountain. God took the initiative to speak personally, joining His holiness with His closeness. Remember that God desires personal communion with you, and come near to listen to Him through His word and His Son.
P: Face to face. Deuteronomy 5:4: 'The LORD talked with you face to face in the mount out of the midst of the fire.' Holy, yet personal. He still speaks to His people.
H: #DailyGrace #Deuteronomy54 #Presence #VoiceOfGod #Relationship #BibleVerse

== 5:5
T: I stood between the LORD and you
R: '(I stood between the LORD and you at that time, to shew you the word of the LORD: for ye were afraid by reason of the fire, and went not up into the mount;) saying…' At that time I stood between the LORD and you to declare His word, because you were afraid of the fire and did not go up the mountain. Notice I stood between. Moses was a mediator. Notice to shew you the word. His role was to bring God's word to the people. Notice ye were afraid. The fire was too much for them. They needed someone to stand in the gap. Notice that this is a picture of Christ. First Timothy 2 says, 'For there is one God, and one mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus.' Notice that Moses' mediation was partial; Jesus' is complete. He not only brings God's word but brings us to God. Today, thank Jesus for standing between you and the fire. Through Him, you can approach God without fear.
S: Moses explained that he stood between the LORD and Israel to show them God's word because they were afraid of the fire. His role as mediator foreshadowed Christ, the one mediator between God and men. Thank Jesus for standing between you and the fire, and approach God through Him without fear.
P: A mediator. Deuteronomy 5:5: 'I stood between the LORD and you at that time, to shew you the word of the LORD.' Moses foreshadowed Jesus, our perfect Mediator. Draw near through Him.

== 5:6
T: I am the LORD thy God
R: 'I am the LORD thy God, which brought thee out of the land of Egypt, from the house of bondage.' I am the LORD your God, who brought you out of Egypt, out of the land of slavery. Notice I am the LORD. The commandments begin not with a rule but with a name. Notice thy God. A personal relationship. Notice which brought thee out. God's rescue comes before His commands. Notice from the house of bondage. Israel had been slaves. God freed them. The law was given to free people, not to make them slaves again. Notice that every command that follows rests on this foundation: who God is and what He has done. Obedience flows from gratitude. Galatians 5 says, 'Stand fast therefore in the liberty wherewith Christ hath made us free.' Today, before you consider what God asks of you, remember who He is and what He has done. Your obedience is the response of a freed person to a rescuing God.
S: The ten commandments begin with God's name and His rescue: I am the LORD thy God who brought thee out of Egypt. His deliverance came before His commands, so obedience flows from gratitude rather than fear. Before you consider what God asks of you, remember who He is and what He has done for you.
P: Rescue before rules. Deuteronomy 5:6: 'I am the LORD thy God, which brought thee out of the land of Egypt, from the house of bondage.' Obedience is a freed person's response to a rescuing God.
H: #DailyGrace #Deuteronomy56 #Freedom #Redemption #TenCommandments #BibleVerse

== 5:7
T: None other gods before me
R: 'Thou shalt have none other gods before me.' You shall have no other gods before Me. Notice none other gods. This is the first and foundational commandment. Everything else flows from it. Notice before me. Literally, before My face. Nothing is to stand in God's presence as a rival. Notice that other gods are not always statues. Money, success, approval, comfort or even family can take the first place in our hearts. Notice that God asks for exclusive loyalty because He alone is worthy and He alone can satisfy. Notice that this command protects us. Lesser gods always disappoint. Matthew 6 says, 'Ye cannot serve God and mammon.' Today, ask honestly what competes with God for first place in your life. Name it, and give the throne of your heart back to Him. He will not share it, and nothing else deserves it.
S: The first commandment says Israel shall have no other gods before the LORD. Rival gods today may be money, success, approval or comfort, and anything that takes first place in the heart becomes an idol. Ask honestly what competes with God for first place in your life, and give your heart's throne back to Him.
P: First place. Deuteronomy 5:7: 'Thou shalt have none other gods before me.' Money, approval, comfort can all become gods. What competes for first place in your heart? Give Him the throne.
H: #DailyGrace #Deuteronomy57 #FirstCommandment #Worship #Loyalty #BibleVerse

== 5:8
T: Any graven image
R: 'Thou shalt not make thee any graven image, or any likeness of any thing that is in heaven above, or that is in the earth beneath, or that is in the waters beneath the earth.' You shall not make for yourself an image in the form of anything in heaven above or on the earth beneath or in the waters below. Notice make thee. The danger is creating a god we can control. Notice any likeness. God refuses to be reduced to any created form. Notice heaven… earth… waters. The whole created order is ruled out. Notice that the first commandment forbids worshipping the wrong God; the second forbids worshipping the true God in the wrong way. Notice that images shrink God to our size. Colossians 1 says Christ is 'the image of the invisible God.' The only true image of God is the Son. Today, let God define Himself through His word and through Jesus. Resist shaping Him into an image that suits you.
S: The second commandment forbids making any graven image or likeness of anything in heaven, earth or the waters. While the first forbids worshipping false gods, the second forbids worshipping the true God in a false way. Let God define Himself through His word and through Jesus, the true image of the invisible God.
P: No graven image. Deuteronomy 5:8: 'Thou shalt not make thee any graven image.' Images shrink God to our size. Let Him define Himself through His word and through Jesus.
H: #DailyGrace #Deuteronomy58 #SecondCommandment #Worship #Truth #BibleVerse

== 5:9
T: A jealous God
R: 'Thou shalt not bow down thyself unto them, nor serve them: for I the LORD thy God am a jealous God, visiting the iniquity of the fathers upon the children unto the third and fourth generation of them that hate me…' Do not bow down to them or worship them; for I, the LORD your God, am a jealous God, punishing children for the sin of parents to the third and fourth generation of those who hate Me. Notice bow down… serve. Worship involves the body and the life. Notice a jealous God. His jealousy is the protective love of a faithful husband. Notice the third and fourth generation. Sin has ripple effects. Children often repeat the patterns of parents who hate God. Notice of them that hate me. The consequences follow those who continue in that hatred. Ezekiel 18 makes clear that each person answers for his own sin. Today, take your influence seriously. Your choices shape those who come after you, for harm or for good.
S: God forbids bowing down to idols, for He is a jealous God whose judgment on those who hate Him can ripple to the third and fourth generation. His jealousy is protective covenant love, and sin's patterns often repeat in families. Take your influence seriously, knowing that your choices shape those who come after you.
P: Ripples across generations. Deuteronomy 5:9: 'I the LORD thy God am a jealous God, visiting the iniquity of the fathers upon the children.' Sin's patterns ripple. Your choices shape those after you.
H: #DailyGrace #Deuteronomy59 #JealousGod #Generations #Influence #BibleVerse

== 5:10
T: Mercy unto thousands
R: 'And shewing mercy unto thousands of them that love me and keep my commandments.' But showing love to a thousand generations of those who love Me and keep My commandments. Notice and shewing mercy. The warning of verse 9 is followed by a promise far larger. Notice unto thousands. Compare three or four generations with thousands. God's mercy vastly outweighs His judgment. Notice them that love me. The heart of obedience is love. Notice and keep my commandments. Love shows itself in faithful living. Notice that blessing also ripples through families. A faithful life can bless generations far beyond our own lifetime. Psalm 103 says, 'the mercy of the LORD is from everlasting to everlasting upon them that fear him.' Today, take heart. God's mercy is far greater than His judgment. Love Him and keep His word, and trust that your faithfulness will bless generations you will never meet.
S: God promised to show mercy to thousands of those who love Him and keep His commandments. His mercy vastly outweighs His judgment, rippling across far more generations than sin's consequences. Love God and keep His word, trusting that your faithfulness can bless generations you will never meet.
P: Mercy to thousands. Deuteronomy 5:10: 'shewing mercy unto thousands of them that love me and keep my commandments.' Judgment to a few generations; mercy to thousands. Your faithfulness can bless generations.

== 5:11
T: Not take the name in vain
R: 'Thou shalt not take the name of the LORD thy God in vain: for the LORD will not hold him guiltless that taketh his name in vain.' You shall not misuse the name of the LORD your God, for the LORD will not hold anyone guiltless who misuses His name. Notice the name of the LORD. God's name represents His character and presence. Notice in vain. Empty, careless, false or worthless. This includes cursing, but also using God's name to back up lies or to manipulate others. Notice will not hold him guiltless. God takes His name seriously. Notice that we carry His name as His people. Living carelessly can take His name in vain too. Matthew 6 teaches us to pray, 'Hallowed be thy name.' Today, speak of God with reverence. Do not use His name lightly, falsely or for your own ends. Let your words and life honour the name you bear.
S: The third commandment forbids taking the LORD's name in vain, for He will not hold guiltless anyone who misuses it. This includes careless speech, false oaths and using God's name to manipulate, as well as living in a way that dishonours it. Speak of God with reverence and let your words and life honour the name you bear.
P: Hallowed be His name. Deuteronomy 5:11: 'Thou shalt not take the name of the LORD thy God in vain.' Not just careless words, but careless living. Honour the name you bear.

== 5:12
T: Keep the sabbath day
R: 'Keep the sabbath day to sanctify it, as the LORD thy God hath commanded thee.' Observe the Sabbath day by keeping it holy, as the LORD your God has commanded you. Notice keep. In Exodus the word was remember; here it is keep, guard, observe. Notice to sanctify it. To set it apart as holy, different from other days. Notice as the LORD thy God hath commanded. Rest was not a suggestion but a command. Notice that God built rest into the rhythm of life. Constant work is not a sign of virtue but often of distrust. Notice that the Sabbath declares that God is the provider, not our endless labour. Mark 2 records Jesus saying, 'The sabbath was made for man, and not man for the sabbath.' Today, set apart time for rest and worship. Guard it from the pull of work and worry. Trust God enough to stop.
S: The fourth commandment tells Israel to keep the sabbath day and set it apart as holy, as God commanded. God built rest into life's rhythm, declaring that He, not endless labour, is the provider. Set apart time for rest and worship, guarding it from work and worry, and trust God enough to stop.
P: Guard your rest. Deuteronomy 5:12: 'Keep the sabbath day to sanctify it.' Rest declares God is your provider. Trust Him enough to stop.
H: #DailyGrace #Deuteronomy512 #Sabbath #Rest #Trust #BibleVerse

== 5:13
T: Six days thou shalt labour
R: 'Six days thou shalt labour, and do all thy work.' Six days you shall labour and do all your work. Notice six days. The Sabbath command includes a command to work. God honours labour. Notice thou shalt labour. Work is not a curse but a calling. Adam was given work in Eden before the fall. Notice do all thy work. Diligence on six days makes rest possible on the seventh. Rest is not laziness, and work is not the whole of life. Notice that God sets both in their place. Notice that faithful work is part of worship. Colossians 3 says, 'whatsoever ye do, do it heartily, as to the Lord, and not unto men.' Today, work diligently at what God has given you. Do it heartily, as service to Him. Then rest with a clear conscience, knowing you have done your part and trusting Him with the rest.
S: The sabbath command includes six days of labour, in which Israel was to do all their work. God honours work as a calling and sets both labour and rest in their proper place. Work diligently and heartily as service to the Lord, then rest with a clear conscience, trusting Him with the rest.
P: Work, then rest. Deuteronomy 5:13: 'Six days thou shalt labour, and do all thy work.' Work is a calling, not a curse. Do it heartily, then rest in trust.
H: #DailyGrace #Deuteronomy513 #Work #Diligence #Calling #BibleVerse

== 5:14
T: May rest as well as thou
R: 'But the seventh day is the sabbath of the LORD thy God: in it thou shalt not do any work… that thy manservant and thy maidservant may rest as well as thou.' The seventh day is the LORD's Sabbath; no one in your household may work, so your servants can rest as you do. Notice the sabbath of the LORD. The day belongs to God. Notice the long list. Sons, daughters, servants, animals, strangers. Everyone rests. Notice that thy manservant… may rest as well as thou. Here Deuteronomy adds a reason: compassion. Rest was a gift to the weakest in the household. Notice that God cares for the worker who cannot choose to stop. Notice that our rest should not depend on others' endless work. Matthew 11 records Jesus saying, 'Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest.' Today, make sure those who serve you can rest too.
S: The seventh day is the sabbath of the LORD, when no one in the household, including servants, animals and strangers, was to work. Deuteronomy adds that servants may rest as well as their masters, making rest a gift of compassion to the weakest. Make sure those who serve you can rest too, for God cares for every worker.
P: Rest for everyone. Deuteronomy 5:14: 'that thy manservant and thy maidservant may rest as well as thou.' Sabbath is compassion for the weakest. Make sure those who serve you can rest too.

== 5:15
T: Remember thou wast a servant
R: 'And remember that thou wast a servant in the land of Egypt, and that the LORD thy God brought thee out thence through a mighty hand and by a stretched out arm…' Remember that you were slaves in Egypt and that the LORD your God brought you out with a mighty hand and an outstretched arm. That is why He commanded you to keep the Sabbath. Notice remember that thou wast a servant. Israel knew what it was to work without rest. Notice brought thee out. God had freed them from endless toil. Notice therefore. In Exodus, the Sabbath points back to creation; here it points back to redemption. Notice that the Sabbath was a weekly celebration of freedom. Slaves cannot rest; free people can. Notice that rest is a mark of redemption. Hebrews 4 says, 'There remaineth therefore a rest to the people of God.' Today, let your rest remind you that you are free. You are not a slave to work, fear or the world's demands. Christ has set you free.
S: Moses told Israel to remember they were slaves in Egypt until the LORD brought them out, and that is why He commanded the sabbath. In Deuteronomy the sabbath points back to redemption, a weekly celebration of freedom from endless toil. Let your rest remind you that you are free in Christ, no longer a slave to work or fear.
P: Rest is freedom. Deuteronomy 5:15: 'remember that thou wast a servant in the land of Egypt… therefore… keep the sabbath day.' Slaves can't rest; free people can. Rest as one set free.

== 5:16
T: Honour thy father and thy mother
R: 'Honour thy father and thy mother, as the LORD thy God hath commanded thee; that thy days may be prolonged, and that it may go well with thee…' Honour your father and your mother, as the LORD your God has commanded you, so that you may live long and that it may go well with you in the land the LORD your God is giving you. Notice honour. The word means to give weight, to treat as important. Notice father and… mother. Both parents are named. Notice as the LORD thy God hath commanded. Honouring parents is part of honouring God. Notice that thy days may be prolonged. This is the first commandment with a promise. Notice that honour is not the same as blind obedience, but it does mean respect, care and gratitude throughout life. Ephesians 6 repeats this command as 'the first commandment with promise.' Today, show honour to your parents in a concrete way. A call, a visit, a word of thanks or practical care can reflect God's heart.
S: The fifth commandment calls Israel to honour father and mother, with the promise that their days would be prolonged and it would go well with them. To honour means to treat as weighty, showing respect, care and gratitude throughout life. Show honour to your parents in a concrete way today, reflecting God's heart in how you treat them.
P: Honour them. Deuteronomy 5:16: 'Honour thy father and thy mother… that thy days may be prolonged.' The first commandment with a promise. Show honour in a concrete way today.
