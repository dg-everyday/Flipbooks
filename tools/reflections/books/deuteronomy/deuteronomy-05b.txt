# Deuteronomy 5:17-33: The last five commandments and the people's request for a mediator

== 5:17
T: Thou shalt not kill
R: 'Thou shalt not kill.' You shall not murder. Notice how short this commandment is. Four words in English, two in Hebrew. Notice that the word refers to unlawful killing, murder. Human life is sacred because people are made in God's image. Notice that this command protects the weak, the unborn, the old and the vulnerable. Notice that Jesus deepened it. He said anger and contempt are murder in the heart. Notice that the command is not only to avoid violence but to protect life. Love builds up rather than tearing down. First John 3 says, 'Whosoever hateth his brother is a murderer.' Today, examine your heart for anger, contempt or bitterness toward anyone. Deal with it before it grows. And look for ways to protect and nourish life around you, speaking words that build up and acting to defend those who cannot defend themselves.
S: The sixth commandment forbids murder, protecting human life as sacred because people bear God's image. Jesus deepened it, showing that anger and contempt are murder in the heart. Examine your heart for anger or bitterness and look for ways to protect and build up the lives around you.
P: Life is sacred. Deuteronomy 5:17: 'Thou shalt not kill.' Jesus traced it to anger and contempt in the heart. Deal with bitterness, and protect and build up life.

== 5:18
T: Neither commit adultery
R: 'Neither shalt thou commit adultery.' You shall not commit adultery. Notice neither. Each commandment in this section is linked by this word, like a chain of protections for the neighbour. Notice that adultery breaks a covenant. Marriage reflects God's faithfulness to His people, so unfaithfulness distorts that picture. Notice that this command protects families, children and trust. Notice that Jesus said lust in the heart is adultery too. Purity begins inside. Notice that God's design for sex within marriage is not a restriction but a protection of something precious. Hebrews 13 says, 'Marriage is honourable in all, and the bed undefiled.' Today, guard your faithfulness in thought, word and action. If you are married, honour your spouse. If you are single, honour your future and God's design. And where there has been failure, remember that Jesus offers forgiveness and a new beginning.
S: The seventh commandment forbids adultery, protecting marriage as a covenant that reflects God's faithfulness. Jesus taught that purity begins in the heart and that lust is adultery within. Guard your faithfulness in thought, word and action, and where there has been failure, receive Christ's forgiveness and a new beginning.
P: Faithful in heart and life. Deuteronomy 5:18: 'Neither shalt thou commit adultery.' Marriage reflects God's faithfulness. Guard purity in thought and action, and find grace where you've failed.
H: #DailyGrace #Deuteronomy518 #Faithfulness #Marriage #Purity #BibleVerse

== 5:19
T: Neither shalt thou steal
R: 'Neither shalt thou steal.' You shall not steal. Notice that stealing takes what belongs to another. It violates trust and harms the neighbour. Notice that it includes more than burglary. Cheating at work, not paying debts, taking credit for others' efforts and wasting an employer's time are all forms of theft. Notice that God gives property and possessions as stewardships. Respecting them respects God's provision for others. Notice that Ephesians gives the cure: work and generosity. Ephesians 4 says, 'Let him that stole steal no more: but rather let him labour… that he may have to give to him that needeth.' Notice that the opposite of stealing is not just keeping your hands to yourself; it is giving. Today, examine your dealings for any hidden theft, whether of money, time or credit. Make it right. Then go further: look for ways to give to someone in need.
S: The eighth commandment forbids stealing, which includes cheating, unpaid debts and taking credit or time that belongs to others. Ephesians teaches that the cure is honest work and generosity toward those in need. Examine your dealings for any hidden theft and make it right, then look for ways to give to someone in need.
P: Don't take; give. Deuteronomy 5:19: 'Neither shalt thou steal.' Theft includes time, credit and unpaid debts. Make it right, then go further and give.
H: #DailyGrace #Deuteronomy519 #Honesty #Generosity #Integrity #BibleVerse

== 5:20
T: Neither bear false witness
R: 'Neither shalt thou bear false witness against thy neighbour.' You shall not give false testimony against your neighbour. Notice false witness. The command first applied to lying in court, where a false word could ruin a life. Notice against thy neighbour. Lies harm real people. Notice that it also covers gossip, slander, exaggeration and spinning half-truths. Any word that misrepresents others damages them. Notice that God is truth, and His people must reflect His truthfulness. Notice how much power our words carry. A reputation built over years can be damaged in a moment. Ephesians 4 says, 'Wherefore putting away lying, speak every man truth with his neighbour.' Today, guard your words about others. Speak truthfully, fairly and kindly, even about those you disagree with. Refuse to pass on gossip. Let your speech protect your neighbour's good name rather than harm it.
S: The ninth commandment forbids bearing false witness against a neighbour, whether in court, in gossip or in exaggeration. Words carry great power to damage reputations, and God's people must reflect His truthfulness. Guard your words about others, speaking truthfully and kindly, and refuse to pass on gossip.
P: Guard your words. Deuteronomy 5:20: 'Neither shalt thou bear false witness against thy neighbour.' Gossip and half-truths wound. Speak truth that protects others' good name.
H: #DailyGrace #Deuteronomy520 #Truth #Words #Gossip #BibleVerse

== 5:21
T: Neither shalt thou covet
R: 'Neither shalt thou desire thy neighbour's wife, neither shalt thou covet thy neighbour's house, his field, or his manservant…' You shall not desire your neighbour's wife, nor covet his house, field, servants, ox, donkey or anything that belongs to your neighbour. Notice desire… covet. This command goes inside, to the heart. Notice his field. Deuteronomy adds the field, fitting for a people about to receive land. Notice any thing. Covetousness can attach to anything. Notice that coveting is the root of many other sins. Theft, adultery and lies often begin with wanting what is not ours. Notice that this commandment shows the law is not just about outward behaviour. Paul said this command revealed his sin. Philippians 4 says, 'I have learned, in whatsoever state I am, therewith to be content.' Today, notice where envy stirs in you. Thank God for what He has given you, and ask Him to teach you contentment.
S: The tenth commandment forbids desiring a neighbour's wife or coveting anything that belongs to him. Coveting reaches into the heart and is the root of theft, adultery and lies. Notice where envy stirs in you, thank God for what He has given you and ask Him to teach you contentment.
P: Contentment. Deuteronomy 5:21: 'neither shalt thou covet thy neighbour's house, his field.' Coveting is the root of many sins. Thank God for what you have and learn contentment.
H: #DailyGrace #Deuteronomy521 #Contentment #Covet #Heart #BibleVerse

== 5:22
T: He added no more
R: 'These words the LORD spake unto all your assembly in the mount out of the midst of the fire, of the cloud, and of the thick darkness, with a great voice: and he added no more…' These are the commandments the LORD proclaimed in a loud voice to your whole assembly from the fire, cloud and darkness on the mountain; He added nothing more. Then He wrote them on two stone tablets. Notice with a great voice. Everyone heard. Notice he added no more. The ten words were complete in themselves. Notice he wrote them. God wrote them personally in stone. Notice that the ten commandments hold a special place. They were spoken directly by God and written by His own hand. Notice that they summarise our duty to God and to our neighbour. Romans 13 says, 'love is the fulfilling of the law.' Today, treasure the ten words God spoke. Let them guide your love for Him and for those around you.
S: The LORD spoke the ten commandments with a great voice to the whole assembly from the fire, cloud and darkness, added no more, and wrote them on two tables of stone. The ten words were complete, spoken directly and written by God's own hand. Treasure the ten words God spoke and let them guide your love for Him and for others.
P: Ten words, complete. Deuteronomy 5:22: 'These words the LORD spake… with a great voice: and he added no more.' Spoken by God, written by His hand. Let them guide your love.

== 5:23
T: All the heads of your tribes came near
R: 'And it came to pass, when ye heard the voice out of the midst of the darkness, (for the mountain did burn with fire,) that ye came near unto me, even all the heads of your tribes, and your elders.' When you heard the voice out of the darkness, while the mountain was ablaze with fire, all the leaders of your tribes and your elders came to me. Notice when ye heard the voice. The experience was overwhelming. Notice the mountain did burn with fire. Even the setting was terrifying. Notice ye came near unto me. The leaders came to Moses, not to the mountain. They sought a mediator. Notice all the heads… and your elders. The request came from the leadership, representing everyone. Notice that awe before God is the right response to His holiness. Psalm 2 says, 'Serve the LORD with fear, and rejoice with trembling.' Today, take God's holiness seriously. Let it lead you to seek the Mediator He has provided, Jesus Christ, through whom you may draw near.
S: When Israel heard God's voice from the burning mountain, all the heads of the tribes and the elders came near to Moses. Overwhelmed by God's holiness, they sought a mediator. Take God's holiness seriously and let it lead you to seek Jesus Christ, the Mediator through whom you may draw near.
P: Seeking a mediator. Deuteronomy 5:23: 'when ye heard the voice out of the midst of the darkness… ye came near unto me.' Holiness drove them to a mediator. Draw near through Jesus.

== 5:24
T: God doth talk with man, and he liveth
R: 'And ye said, Behold, the LORD our God hath shewed us his glory and his greatness, and we have heard his voice out of the midst of the fire: we have seen this day that God doth talk with man, and he liveth.' You said, The LORD our God has shown us His glory and majesty, and we have heard His voice from the fire. Today we have seen that a person can live even if God speaks with them. Notice his glory and his greatness. The people recognised what they had seen. Notice we have heard his voice. They were witnesses. Notice God doth talk with man, and he liveth. This was astonishing. The holy God spoke to sinners, and they survived. Notice that their words were true and good. They had grasped something wonderful about grace. Hebrews 1 says God 'hath in these last days spoken unto us by his Son.' Today, marvel that God speaks to people and they live. In Christ, His voice brings life, not death.
S: Israel's leaders said they had seen God's glory and greatness and heard His voice from the fire, discovering that God can speak with man and he lives. They grasped something wonderful about grace, that the holy God speaks to sinners and spares them. Marvel that God speaks to you in Christ and that His voice brings life.
P: God speaks, and we live. Deuteronomy 5:24: 'we have seen this day that God doth talk with man, and he liveth.' The holy God speaks to sinners and they live. In Christ, His voice brings life.

== 5:25
T: Why should we die?
R: 'Now therefore why should we die? for this great fire will consume us: if we hear the voice of the LORD our God any more, then we shall die.' But now, why should we die? This great fire will consume us, and we will die if we hear the voice of the LORD our God any longer. Notice why should we die. The people were terrified. Notice this great fire will consume us. They knew they were sinners before a holy God. Notice any more. They could bear it once but feared another encounter. Notice that their fear was not foolish. It was an honest recognition of their need. Sinful people cannot stand in unfiltered holiness. Notice that this fear prepared them for the gift of a mediator. Isaiah 6 records the prophet saying, 'Woe is me! for I am undone.' Today, do not take God's holiness lightly. Let the awareness of your need lead you not to run from God but to the One who stands between and brings you safely near.
S: The people feared that if they heard God's voice any more, the great fire would consume them and they would die. Their fear was an honest recognition that sinners cannot stand in unfiltered holiness. Let awareness of your need lead you not away from God but to Christ, who brings you safely near.
P: An honest fear. Deuteronomy 5:25: 'why should we die? for this great fire will consume us.' Sinners can't stand in raw holiness. That's why we need a Mediator. Run to Him.
H: #DailyGrace #Deuteronomy525 #Holiness #FearOfGod #Need #BibleVerse

== 5:26
T: The voice of the living God
R: 'For who is there of all flesh, that hath heard the voice of the living God speaking out of the midst of the fire, as we have, and lived?' For what mortal has ever heard the voice of the living God speaking out of fire, as we have, and survived? Notice of all flesh. Frail, mortal humanity. Notice the living God. Not a dead idol, but a God who speaks and acts. Notice that the people recognised their privilege. No other nation had experienced this. Notice and lived. They knew their survival was mercy. Notice that this verse holds both awe and gratitude. They were terrified and thankful at once. Notice that the right response to God includes both. Hebrews 10 says, 'It is a fearful thing to fall into the hands of the living God.' Yet in Christ, we are held by those same hands in love. Today, hold awe and gratitude together. The living God has spoken to you, and by His mercy you live.
S: The people asked who among all flesh had heard the voice of the living God from the fire and lived, as they had. They recognised both the terror and the mercy of their privilege. Hold awe and gratitude together, for the living God has spoken to you and by His mercy you live.
P: The living God spoke. Deuteronomy 5:26: 'who is there of all flesh, that hath heard the voice of the living God… and lived?' Awe and gratitude together. By His mercy, you live.

== 5:27
T: We will hear it, and do it
R: 'Go thou near, and hear all that the LORD our God shall say: and speak thou unto us all that the LORD our God shall speak unto thee; and we will hear it, and do it.' Go near and listen to all that the LORD our God says. Then tell us whatever He tells you. We will listen and obey. Notice go thou near. They asked Moses to stand in God's presence for them. Notice speak thou unto us. They would receive God's word through him. Notice we will hear it, and do it. A beautiful promise. They committed to obey. Notice that their request was reasonable and their promise sincere. But keeping it would prove harder than making it. Notice how easily we promise obedience in a moment of awe. The test comes later. Matthew 21 tells of a son who said, 'I go, sir: and went not.' Today, make your promises to God thoughtfully, and ask Him for strength to keep them when the moment of awe has passed.
S: The people asked Moses to go near and hear all God would say, then tell them, promising to hear and do it. Their request was sincere, but keeping the promise would prove harder than making it. Make your promises to God thoughtfully and ask for strength to keep them after the moment of awe has passed.
P: Easy to promise. Deuteronomy 5:27: 'speak thou unto us all that the LORD our God shall speak unto thee; and we will hear it, and do it.' Promises are easy in awe. Ask God for strength to keep them.

== 5:28
T: They have well said
R: 'And the LORD heard the voice of your words, when ye spake unto me; and the LORD said unto me, I have heard the voice of the words of this people… they have well said all that they have spoken.' God heard and said, Everything they said was good. Notice the LORD heard. God listened to their conversation with Moses. Notice they have well said. God approved their words. Their fear of His holiness and their request for a mediator were right. Notice that God is pleased when we recognise our need. Humility before His holiness is wise. Notice that God affirmed them. He does not only correct; He also commends. Notice that this request for a mediator led to God's promise of a greater Prophet in chapter 18. Psalm 51 says, 'a broken and a contrite heart, O God, thou wilt not despise.' Today, know that God hears and honours humble words. Speak to Him honestly about your need.
S: The LORD heard the people's words to Moses and said that everything they had spoken was good. God approved their humble recognition of His holiness and their need for a mediator. Know that God hears and honours humble words, and speak to Him honestly about your need.
P: God approved. Deuteronomy 5:28: 'I have heard the voice of the words of this people… they have well said all that they have spoken.' God honours humble hearts. Speak to Him honestly.

== 5:29
T: O that there were such an heart
R: 'O that there were such an heart in them, that they would fear me, and keep all my commandments always, that it might be well with them, and with their children for ever!' Oh, that their hearts would be inclined to fear Me and keep all My commands always, so that it might go well with them and their children forever! Notice O that. God expresses longing. This is one of the most tender cries in the Old Testament. Notice such an heart. The issue was the heart. Good words needed a changed heart to sustain them. Notice fear me, and keep… always. God wanted lasting reverence, not a passing feeling. Notice well with them, and with their children. His longing was for their good. Notice that God knew they lacked the heart they needed. Ezekiel 36 promises, 'A new heart also will I give you.' Today, hear God's longing for you. Ask Him for the new heart that only He can give.
S: God cried out with longing that Israel would have such a heart to fear Him and keep His commandments always, for their good and their children's. Their good words needed a changed heart to sustain them, which God later promised to give. Hear God's longing for your good and ask Him for the new heart only He can give.
P: God's longing. Deuteronomy 5:29: 'O that there were such an heart in them, that they would fear me.' Good words need a new heart. Ask God for the heart only He can give.

== 5:30
T: Get you into your tents again
R: 'Go say to them, Get you into your tents again.' Go, tell them to return to their tents. Notice go say to them. God sent Moses back with a message. Notice get you into your tents again. The meeting at the mountain was ending. The people could return to daily life. Notice that God released them from the overwhelming encounter. He knew they could not stay at the mountain forever. Notice that ordinary life is where the commandments would be lived out. The tents, the meals, the families and the work. Faith is not only for mountain moments. Notice that God's word goes home with us. What we hear in worship must be lived in the tent. Colossians 3 says, 'whatsoever ye do in word or deed, do all in the name of the Lord Jesus.' Today, take what you hear from God back into ordinary life. Your tent, your kitchen and your workplace are where obedience happens.
S: God told Moses to send the people back to their tents after the great encounter at the mountain. Ordinary life, with its homes, meals and work, was where the commandments would be lived out. Take what you hear from God back into daily life, for obedience happens in the kitchen and the workplace.
P: Back to the tents. Deuteronomy 5:30: 'Go say to them, Get you into your tents again.' The mountain moment ended; daily obedience began. Live God's word where you live.

== 5:31
T: Stand thou here by me
R: 'But as for thee, stand thou here by me, and I will speak unto thee all the commandments, and the statutes, and the judgments, which thou shalt teach them…' But you stay here with Me so that I may give you all the commands, decrees and laws you are to teach them to follow in the land. Notice but as for thee. Moses was set apart for a different task. Notice stand thou here by me. An extraordinary invitation. Moses remained in God's presence. Notice which thou shalt teach them. He received in order to give. His closeness to God was for the people's sake. Notice that leaders who teach must first listen. Time alone with God comes before teaching others. Notice that the people went home, but Moses stayed. Mark 3 says Jesus 'ordained twelve, that they should be with him, and that he might send them forth to preach.' Today, if you teach or lead others, first stand by God. Spend time listening before you speak.
S: God told Moses to stand by Him so He could speak all the commandments, statutes and judgments that Moses would teach the people. Moses received in God's presence in order to give to others. If you teach or lead, first spend time standing by God and listening before you speak.
P: Stand by Him first. Deuteronomy 5:31: 'stand thou here by me, and I will speak unto thee… which thou shalt teach them.' Receive before you give. Listen before you lead.

== 5:32
T: Turn not aside
R: 'Ye shall observe to do therefore as the LORD your God hath commanded you: ye shall not turn aside to the right hand or to the left.' So be careful to do what the LORD your God has commanded you; do not turn aside to the right or to the left. Notice observe to do. Careful, attentive obedience. Notice as the LORD your God hath commanded. The standard is God's word, not human opinion. Notice not turn aside to the right hand or to the left. There are two ditches beside the road. One is adding to God's word, the other is taking away from it. Legalism on one side, compromise on the other. Notice that faithfulness walks the middle of the road God has laid out. Proverbs 4 says, 'Let thine eyes look right on… Turn not to the right hand nor to the left.' Today, keep your eyes on the path God has shown. Avoid both harshness and laxity. Walk steadily and faithfully in His way.
S: Moses urged Israel to observe and do what the LORD commanded, turning neither to the right nor to the left. Faithfulness avoids both the ditch of legalism and the ditch of compromise. Keep your eyes on the path God has shown, avoiding both harshness and laxity, and walk steadily in His way.
P: Stay on the road. Deuteronomy 5:32: 'ye shall not turn aside to the right hand or to the left.' Two ditches: legalism and compromise. Walk steadily in God's way.

== 5:33
T: That ye may live
R: 'Ye shall walk in all the ways which the LORD your God hath commanded you, that ye may live, and that it may be well with you, and that ye may prolong your days in the land which ye shall possess.' Walk in all God's commands, so that you may live and prosper in the land. Notice walk. Obedience is a journey, step by step, not a single event. Notice in all the ways. Not selected parts but the whole. Notice that ye may live. God's ways lead to life. Notice that it may be well with you. Flourishing, peace and blessing. Notice prolong your days. Stability and security in the land. Notice that God's commands are not obstacles to happiness but the path to it. John 10 records Jesus saying, 'I am come that they might have life, and that they might have it more abundantly.' Today, walk in God's ways one step at a time. They are the road to true life.
S: Moses told Israel to walk in all the ways the LORD commanded so that they would live, prosper and prolong their days in the land. Obedience is a step-by-step journey, and God's ways are not obstacles to happiness but the road to life. Walk in God's ways one step at a time, trusting that they lead to true life.
P: The road to life. Deuteronomy 5:33: 'Ye shall walk in all the ways which the LORD your God hath commanded you, that ye may live.' God's ways aren't obstacles to life; they are the road to it.
