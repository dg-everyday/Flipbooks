# Deuteronomy 6: The Shema, teaching the children and the danger of forgetting in plenty

== 6:1
T: Commanded to teach you
R: 'Now these are the commandments, the statutes, and the judgments, which the LORD your God commanded to teach you, that ye might do them in the land whither ye go to possess it.' These are the laws the LORD told me to teach you. Notice commanded to teach you. God's word was meant to be taught, explained and passed on. Notice that ye might do them. Teaching aimed at practice. Notice in the land. The commandments were for real life in a real place. Notice that this verse opens the heart of Deuteronomy. What follows in this chapter is the centre of Israel's faith. Notice that God's commands come with purpose. Each one was meant to shape a people who reflected His goodness in their land. Psalm 119 says, 'Teach me, O LORD, the way of thy statutes; and I shall keep it unto the end.' Today, ask God to teach you His ways. Then carry what you learn into the ordinary places of your life.
S: Moses introduced the commandments, statutes and judgments the LORD commanded him to teach so that Israel would do them in the land. God's word was meant to be taught and practised in real life. Ask God to teach you His ways and carry what you learn into the ordinary places of your life.
P: Taught to be lived. Deuteronomy 6:1: 'these are the commandments… which the LORD your God commanded to teach you, that ye might do them.' Ask God to teach you His ways, then live them.

== 6:2
T: Thou, and thy son, and thy son's son
R: 'That thou mightest fear the LORD thy God, to keep all his statutes and his commandments, which I command thee, thou, and thy son…' So that you, your children and their children after them may fear the LORD your God as long as you live, by keeping all His decrees and commands, and so that you may enjoy long life. Notice that thou mightest fear the LORD. The goal of the law was reverence, a heart that honours God. Notice thou, and thy son. Three generations are named. Faith is meant to pass from parent to child to grandchild. Notice all the days of thy life. Not a phase, but a lifetime. Notice that reverence for God grows through keeping His word. Obedience and the fear of the LORD feed each other. Notice that God thinks in generations. Psalm 145 says, 'One generation shall praise thy works to another.' Today, think about the generations after you. Live your reverence for God in a way that invites them to share it.
S: Moses taught the commandments so that Israel, their children and their grandchildren would fear the LORD and keep His statutes all their days. The goal of the law was lifelong reverence passed from generation to generation. Live your reverence for God in a way that invites the generations after you to share it.
P: Three generations. Deuteronomy 6:2: 'That thou mightest fear the LORD thy God… thou, and thy son.' God thinks in generations. Live your faith so those after you will share it.

Psalm 1 says the righteous man is 'like a tree planted by the rivers of water.' Today, trust that God's ways lead to good things. Listen, obey and expect His faithfulness.
S: Moses urged Israel to hear and observe God's word so that it would be well with them and they would increase in the land flowing with milk and honey. God's commands were given for their flourishing, in keeping with His promise. Listen to God, obey Him and trust that His ways lead to good things.

== 6:4
T: The LORD our God is one LORD
R: 'Hear, O Israel: The LORD our God is one LORD.' Hear, O Israel: The LORD our God, the LORD is one. Notice hear. This is the Shema, named from its first Hebrew word. Faithful Jews have recited it morning and evening for thousands of years. Notice O Israel. It is addressed to the whole people. Notice the LORD our God. A personal relationship. Notice is one LORD. There is only one God, undivided, without rival. He is not one god among many, and He is not divided in His purposes. Notice that everything else in the faith flows from this truth. Because God is one, our loyalty must be undivided. Notice that Jesus called this the beginning of the greatest commandment. Mark 12 records Him quoting, 'Hear, O Israel; The Lord our God is one Lord.' Today, say these words slowly and let them settle in your heart. There is one God, and He is yours. Let your heart be as undivided toward Him as He is in His being.
S: The Shema declares, Hear, O Israel: the LORD our God is one LORD, a truth recited by the faithful for thousands of years. Because there is only one God, undivided and without rival, our loyalty to Him must also be undivided. Say these words slowly today and let your heart be as undivided toward God as He is in His being.
P: The Shema. Deuteronomy 6:4: 'Hear, O Israel: The LORD our God is one LORD.' One God, no rivals. Let your heart be undivided toward Him.

== 6:5
T: With all thine heart, soul and might
R: 'And thou shalt love the LORD thy God with all thine heart, and with all thy soul, and with all thy might.' Love the LORD your God with all your heart and with all your soul and with all your strength. Notice love. God's first command is not fear or duty but love. Notice all thine heart. Your thoughts, desires and will. Notice all thy soul. Your very life and self. Notice all thy might. Your energy, abilities and resources. The word means muchness, everything you have. Notice the word all, three times. God wants not a portion but the whole person. Notice that Jesus called this the first and great commandment. Notice that we love Him because He first loved us. Matthew 22 records Jesus saying, 'This is the first and great commandment.' Today, ask which part of you is holding back from loving God. Offer Him your heart, soul and strength, not out of obligation but in response to His love for you.
S: Moses commanded Israel to love the LORD with all their heart, soul and might. God's first command is love that engages the whole person, thoughts, life and every resource, and Jesus called it the first and great commandment. Ask which part of you is holding back from loving God, and offer Him your whole self in response to His love.
P: The greatest commandment. Deuteronomy 6:5: 'thou shalt love the LORD thy God with all thine heart, and with all thy soul, and with all thy might.' All, three times. Offer Him your whole self.

Let faith be part of ordinary moments, not just Sunday lessons.

== 6:8
T: A sign upon thine hand
R: 'And thou shalt bind them for a sign upon thine hand, and they shall be as frontlets between thine eyes.' Tie them as symbols on your hands and bind them on your foreheads. Notice upon thine hand. The hand represents what we do. God's word should guide our actions. Notice between thine eyes. The forehead represents what we think and see. God's word should shape our thoughts and outlook. Notice that later Jews took this literally, wearing small boxes called phylacteries containing Scripture. Notice that the deeper meaning is that God's word should govern both thinking and doing. Notice that Jesus criticised those who made phylacteries broad for show while neglecting the heart. The outward sign means nothing without inward reality. Proverbs 3 says, 'bind them about thy neck; write them upon the table of thine heart.' Today, let God's word guide what your hands do and what your eyes see. Ask Him to shape both your thinking and your actions.
S: Moses told Israel to bind God's words as a sign on their hands and as frontlets between their eyes. The hand stands for action and the forehead for thought, so God's word should govern both. Let God's word guide what your hands do and what your eyes see, shaping both your thinking and your actions.
P: Hands and eyes. Deuteronomy 6:8: 'bind them for a sign upon thine hand, and they shall be as frontlets between thine eyes.' Let God's word shape what you do and how you see.

S: Moses warned Israel not to tempt the LORD as they had at Massah, where they doubted His presence and demanded water. Trust God without demanding signs, resting in His presence even when circumstances are hard.
P: Don't test Him. Deuteronomy 6:16: 'Ye shall not tempt the LORD your God, as ye tempted him in Massah.' Jesus quoted this too. Trust without demanding proof. He is with you.
