# Deuteronomy 7: A holy people, chosen in love, and victory little by little

== 7:1
T: Seven nations greater and mightier
R: 'When the LORD thy God shall bring thee into the land whither thou goest to possess it, and hath cast out many nations before thee… seven nations greater and mightier than thou.' When the LORD your God brings you into the land and drives out before you many nations, seven nations larger and stronger than you. Notice shall bring thee. God would do the bringing. Notice hath cast out. He would do the driving out. Notice seven nations. Hittites, Girgashites, Amorites, Canaanites, Perizzites, Hivites and Jebusites. A long list of opponents. Notice greater and mightier than thou. Moses was honest about the odds. Israel was outnumbered. Notice that God never hides the size of the challenge, but He always sets it beside His own power. Romans 8 says, 'Nay, in all these things we are more than conquerors through him that loved us.' Today, list honestly what you face. Then set that list beside God's promise to go before you.
S: Moses told Israel that God would bring them into the land and cast out seven nations greater and mightier than they. He was honest about the odds while setting them beside God's power to drive the nations out. List honestly what you face, then set that list beside God's promise to go before you.
P: Outnumbered, not outmatched. Deuteronomy 7:1: 'seven nations greater and mightier than thou.' God never hides the challenge but always sets it beside His power. You are more than a conqueror.

== 7:2
T: Make no covenant with them
R: 'And when the LORD thy God shall deliver them before thee; thou shalt smite them, and utterly destroy them; thou shalt make no covenant with them, nor shew mercy unto them.' When the LORD has delivered them over to you and you have defeated them, you must destroy them totally. Make no treaty with them and show them no mercy. Notice the LORD… shall deliver them. Victory was God's gift. Notice utterly destroy. This was God's specific judgment on these Canaanite nations, whose practices included child sacrifice. Notice make no covenant. No alliances that would draw Israel into their gods. Notice that this was a unique command for a unique moment, not a general rule for how God's people treat others. Jesus later commanded love even for enemies. Notice that the deeper issue was spiritual compromise. Second Corinthians 6 asks, 'what communion hath light with darkness?' Today, refuse alliances that would pull your heart from God. Be ruthless with sin, while loving people as Christ loves them.
S: God commanded Israel to utterly destroy the Canaanite nations He delivered to them and make no covenant with them. This was a unique judgment on peoples whose practices included child sacrifice, not a general rule, and its deeper concern was spiritual compromise. Refuse alliances that pull your heart from God, being ruthless with sin while loving people as Christ does.
P: No compromise with darkness. Deuteronomy 7:2: 'thou shalt make no covenant with them.' A unique judgment, with a lasting lesson: refuse what pulls your heart from God, and love people as Christ does.

== 7:3
T: Neither make marriages with them
R: 'Neither shalt thou make marriages with them; thy daughter thou shalt not give unto his son, nor his daughter shalt thou take unto thy son.' Do not intermarry with them. Do not give your daughters to their sons or take their daughters for your sons. Notice make marriages. The issue was not race, for Rahab and Ruth would later join Israel by faith. Notice that the concern was spiritual. Marriage joins two lives, including their loyalties. Notice that the closest relationships shape us most. A spouse who serves other gods would pull the heart away. Notice that God's concern was for the next generation. Children grow up in the faith of their home. Notice that Solomon later ignored this, and his foreign wives turned his heart away. Second Corinthians 6 says, 'Be ye not unequally yoked together with unbelievers.' Today, choose your closest relationships wisely. Those you bind your life to will shape your walk with God.
S: God forbade Israel to make marriages with the Canaanite nations, giving or taking sons and daughters. The concern was spiritual rather than racial, since marriage joins loyalties and shapes the faith of the next generation. Choose your closest relationships wisely, for those you bind your life to will shape your walk with God.
P: Choose wisely. Deuteronomy 7:3: 'Neither shalt thou make marriages with them.' Not about race but loyalty. Your closest relationships shape your walk with God.

== 7:4
T: They will turn away thy son

For they will turn your children away from following Me to serve other gods, and the LORD's anger will burn against you. Notice turn away thy son. The danger was to the children. Notice from following me. Faith is pictured as following, walking behind God. Notice that compromise in one generation leads to apostasy in the next. What parents tolerate, children often embrace. Notice that God explains His reasons. His commands are not arbitrary; He sees where paths lead. Notice that this happened again and again in Israel's history. Proverbs 13 says, 'He that walketh with wise men shall be wise: but a companion of fools shall be destroyed.' Today, think about the influences shaping the children around you. Protect their path of following God, and model wholehearted loyalty yourself.
S: God explained that intermarriage with the Canaanites would turn Israel's children away from following Him to serve other gods. Compromise in one generation often leads to apostasy in the next. Think about the influences shaping the children around you, and model wholehearted loyalty to God yourself.
P: Protect their path. Deuteronomy 7:4: 'they will turn away thy son from following me.' What one generation tolerates, the next often embraces. Model wholehearted loyalty.

== 7:5
T: Destroy their altars
R: 'But thus shall ye deal with them; ye shall destroy their altars, and break down their images, and cut down their groves, and burn their graven images with fire.' This is what you are to do: break down their altars, smash their sacred stones, cut down their Asherah poles and burn their idols in the fire. Notice destroy… break down… cut down… burn. Four strong verbs. No half-measures. Notice their altars… images… groves. Every tool of false worship was to be removed. Notice that leaving idols standing would invite temptation. What remains within reach can draw us back. Notice that God asks for decisive action against what competes with Him. Notice that this was about objects, not people. Acts 19 tells of new believers in Ephesus who burned their magic books publicly. Today, identify anything in your life that feeds a rival loyalty. Remove it decisively. Do not keep a shrine to old idols just in case.
S: God told Israel to destroy the altars, images, groves and graven images of the Canaanites. Leaving the tools of false worship standing would invite temptation, so decisive action was required. Identify anything in your life that feeds a rival loyalty and remove it decisively rather than keeping it within reach.
P: No half-measures. Deuteronomy 7:5: 'ye shall destroy their altars, and break down their images.' What stays within reach can draw you back. Remove rival loyalties decisively.

== 7:6
T: An holy people, a special people
R: 'For thou art an holy people unto the LORD thy God: the LORD thy God hath chosen thee to be a special people unto himself, above all people that are upon the face of the earth.' For you are a people holy to the LORD your God. The LORD has chosen you out of all the peoples on earth to be His treasured possession. Notice thou art an holy people. Holy means set apart, belonging to God. Notice hath chosen thee. Their identity came from God's choice. Notice a special people unto himself. Treasured, His own possession, like a king's personal treasure. Notice that the commands of verses 1 to 5 flow from this identity. Because you are His, live like it. Notice that holiness is first a gift, then a calling. First Peter 2 says, 'But ye are a chosen generation, a royal priesthood, an holy nation, a peculiar people.' Today, remember who you are in Christ: chosen, treasured, set apart. Let that identity shape your choices.
S: Moses told Israel they were a holy people, chosen by the LORD to be a special people unto Himself above all peoples. Their identity as God's treasured possession was the reason for the commands to stay separate from idolatry. Remember who you are in Christ, chosen, treasured and set apart, and let that identity shape your choices.
P: Treasured and set apart. Deuteronomy 7:6: 'the LORD thy God hath chosen thee to be a special people unto himself.' Holiness is a gift before it's a calling. Live as who you are in Christ.

== 7:7
T: The fewest of all people
R: 'The LORD did not set his love upon you, nor choose you, because ye were more in number than any people; for ye were the fewest of all people.' God did not choose you for your size; you were the smallest of nations. Notice did not set his love upon you… because. God's love was not a reward. Notice more in number. Israel was not chosen for size, strength or impressiveness. Notice the fewest of all. They were small and weak. Notice that God delights to choose the unlikely. This guards against pride. If Israel thought they had earned God's love, they would become arrogant. Notice that this is the logic of grace. First Corinthians 1 says, 'not many wise men after the flesh, not many mighty, not many noble, are called.' Today, rest in a love you did not earn. God did not choose you because you were impressive. That means you cannot lose His love by being unimpressive either.
S: Moses told Israel that God did not set His love on them because they were numerous, for they were the fewest of all peoples. God's choice was not a reward for strength but an act of grace that guards against pride. Rest in a love you did not earn, knowing you cannot lose it by being unimpressive.
P: Not because you were impressive. Deuteronomy 7:7: 'The LORD did not set his love upon you… because ye were more in number… for ye were the fewest of all people.' Love you didn't earn can't be lost by weakness.

== 7:8
T: Because the LORD loved you
R: 'But because the LORD loved you, and because he would keep the oath which he had sworn unto your fathers, hath the LORD brought you out with a mighty hand…' It was because the LORD loved you and kept the oath He swore to your ancestors that He brought you out with a mighty hand and redeemed you from slavery under Pharaoh. Notice but because the LORD loved you. The reason for God's love is His love. There is no deeper cause. He loved them because He chose to love them. Notice because he would keep the oath. His faithfulness to His promises. Notice redeemed you. Bought back from slavery. Notice that love and faithfulness are the two pillars of Israel's salvation. Notice that our salvation rests on the same foundations. John 3 says, 'For God so loved the world, that he gave his only begotten Son.' Today, stop looking for reasons in yourself why God loves you. He loves you because He loves you. Rest in it.
S: Moses told Israel that God brought them out of Egypt because He loved them and kept His oath to their fathers. The reason for God's love is simply His love, and His faithfulness to His promises secures it. Stop looking for reasons in yourself why God loves you, and rest in the love He has freely set on you.
P: Because He loved you. Deuteronomy 7:8: 'But because the LORD loved you, and because he would keep the oath.' There's no deeper reason than His love. Rest in it.

S: Moses urged Israel to know that the LORD is God, the faithful God who keeps covenant and mercy to a thousand generations of those who love Him. His faithfulness is reliable and practically limitless. Build your life on God's faithfulness, loving Him and trusting Him to keep every promise.
