# Deuteronomy 9: Not for your righteousness; the golden calf and Moses' intercession

== 9:1
T: Thou art to pass over Jordan this day
R: 'Hear, O Israel: Thou art to pass over Jordan this day, to go in to possess nations greater and mightier than thyself, cities great and fenced up to heaven.' Hear, Israel: you are now about to cross the Jordan to dispossess nations greater and stronger than you, with large cities walled up to the sky. Notice hear, O Israel. Another call to listen closely. Notice this day. The crossing was near. Notice greater and mightier than thyself. Moses does not pretend the task is easy. Notice fenced up to heaven. The same words the frightened spies had used forty years before. Moses deliberately repeats the old fear so that this generation can face it with faith. Notice that God does not ask us to deny difficulty but to see it in the light of His presence. Psalm 18 says, 'by my God have I leaped over a wall.' Today, name the walls in front of you honestly. Then hear what God says about them next.
S: Moses called Israel to hear that they were about to cross the Jordan to possess nations greater and mightier than they, with cities fenced up to heaven. He repeated the old fears of the spies so this generation could face them with faith. Name the walls before you honestly, then listen to what God says about them.
P: Walls up to heaven, again. Deuteronomy 9:1: 'Thou art to pass over Jordan this day… cities great and fenced up to heaven.' Same walls, new faith. By my God I can leap over a wall.

== 9:2
T: Who can stand before the children of Anak?
R: 'A people great and tall, the children of the Anakims, whom thou knowest, and of whom thou hast heard say, Who can stand before the children of Anak!' The people are strong and tall, Anakites. You know about them and have heard it said: Who can stand up against the Anakites? Notice great and tall. The Anakims were the giants that had terrified the spies. Notice whom thou knowest. Their reputation was well known. Notice who can stand before. It was a proverb, a saying everyone repeated. Fear had become a common saying. Notice that sayings shape us. When a whole culture repeats who can stand, people begin to believe nobody can. Notice that God was about to give a different answer. Joshua and Caleb later defeated the Anakims. Romans 8 asks, 'If God be for us, who can be against us?' Today, notice the fearful sayings you repeat about your challenges. Replace them with God's truth. Ask not who can stand before them, but who can stand against God.
S: Moses reminded Israel of the tall Anakims and the well-known saying, Who can stand before the children of Anak? Fear had become a common proverb, shaping how people saw the challenge ahead. Notice the fearful sayings you repeat about your challenges and replace them with God's truth.
P: Change the saying. Deuteronomy 9:2: 'Who can stand before the children of Anak!' Fear becomes a proverb. Replace it with this: If God be for us, who can be against us?

Israel still had a part to play. Notice as the LORD hath said. His word guaranteed the outcome. Isaiah 52 says, 'for the LORD will go before you.' Today, picture God going ahead of you into the challenge you fear. He is a consuming fire against your enemies. Follow Him in, and do your part.
S: Moses told Israel to understand that the LORD would go over before them as a consuming fire to destroy and bring down their enemies. God would do the decisive work, while Israel would follow and drive them out as He had promised. Picture God going ahead of you into the challenge you fear, and follow Him in to do your part.

== 9:4
T: Not for my righteousness
R: 'Speak not thou in thine heart, after that the LORD thy God hath cast them out from before thee, saying, For my righteousness the LORD hath brought me in to possess this land…' After the LORD has driven them out before you, do not say to yourself, The LORD brought me here because of my righteousness. No, it is because of the wickedness of these nations. Notice speak not thou in thine heart. God watches the private thoughts that follow success. Notice for my righteousness. The temptation after victory is to assume we deserved it. Notice for the wickedness of these nations. The Canaanites were being judged for their own sins. Israel's victory did not prove Israel's goodness. Notice that self-righteousness is a subtle pride that can follow blessing. Luke 18 tells of a Pharisee who prayed, 'God, I thank thee, that I am not as other men are.' Today, when God blesses you, resist the thought that you earned it. Receive it as grace.
S: Moses warned Israel not to say in their hearts after victory that God had brought them in because of their righteousness. The nations were being driven out for their own wickedness, and Israel's success did not prove Israel's goodness. When God blesses you, resist the thought that you earned it and receive it as grace.
P: Not because you're righteous. Deuteronomy 9:4: 'Speak not thou in thine heart… For my righteousness the LORD hath brought me in.' Success doesn't prove goodness. Receive blessing as grace.

== 9:5
T: That he may perform the word
R: 'Not for thy righteousness, or for the uprightness of thine heart, dost thou go to possess their land: but for the wickedness of these nations the LORD thy God doth drive them out from before thee, and that he may perform the word…' It is not because of your righteousness or integrity that you are going in to take their land, but because of these nations' wickedness, and to fulfil what He swore to Abraham, Isaac and Jacob. Notice not for thy righteousness. Moses repeats it to make sure it sinks in. Notice or for the uprightness of thine heart. Not even their inner sincerity earned the land. Notice two reasons: the nations' wickedness and God's faithfulness to His promise. Israel's merit is not on the list. Notice that God's promises rest on His character, not ours. Titus 3 says, 'Not by works of righteousness which we have done, but according to his mercy he saved us.' Today, rest your hope on God's faithfulness, not on your own goodness.
S: Moses told Israel that they were not receiving the land for their righteousness or uprightness, but because of the nations' wickedness and God's oath to the patriarchs. Israel's merit was not one of the reasons; God's faithfulness was. Rest your hope on God's faithfulness to His word rather than on your own goodness.
P: His faithfulness, not our goodness. Deuteronomy 9:5: 'Not for thy righteousness… that he may perform the word which the LORD sware unto thy fathers.' Your hope rests on His character.

== 9:6
T: A stiffnecked people
R: 'Understand therefore, that the LORD thy God giveth thee not this good land to possess it for thy righteousness; for thou art a stiffnecked people.' Understand then that it is not because of your righteousness that the LORD your God is giving you this good land, for you are a stiff-necked people. Notice understand therefore. A third time, the same truth. Notice giveth thee… this good land. It is a gift, plainly. Notice a stiffnecked people. Like an ox that refuses to bend its neck to the yoke. Israel had been stubborn and resistant. Notice that Moses is blunt because he loves them. Flattery would set them up to fall. Notice that God gives good gifts to stubborn people. That is the astonishing nature of grace. Acts 7 records Stephen saying, 'Ye stiffnecked… ye do always resist the Holy Ghost.' Today, admit your own stubbornness before God. Then marvel that He still gives good things. Ask Him to soften your neck so you bend willingly to His ways.
S: For the third time Moses stressed that God was not giving Israel the good land for their righteousness, for they were a stiffnecked people. Like a stubborn ox resisting the yoke, they had resisted God, yet He still gave them good gifts. Admit your own stubbornness before God, marvel at His grace and ask Him to soften your heart.
P: Grace to the stubborn. Deuteronomy 9:6: 'the LORD thy God giveth thee not this good land… for thy righteousness; for thou art a stiffnecked people.' God gives good gifts to stubborn people. Ask Him to soften you.

Let it deepen your gratitude for His patience, not your shame.
