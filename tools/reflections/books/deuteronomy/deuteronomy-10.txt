# Deuteronomy 10: New tablets, the Levites, and what the LORD requires

== 10:1
T: Hew thee two tables like unto the first
R: 'At that time the LORD said unto me, Hew thee two tables of stone like unto the first, and come up unto me into the mount, and make thee an ark of wood.' God told Moses to cut two new tablets and make a wooden ark to hold them. Notice at that time. Right after Moses' intercession, God answered. Notice hew thee two tables. The first tablets were broken, but God offered new ones. This is grace after failure. Notice like unto the first. The covenant was not changed or lowered. God renewed it on the same terms. Notice come up unto me. Restored fellowship. Moses was invited back into God's presence. Notice make thee an ark. A safe place to keep the renewed covenant. Lamentations 3 says, 'his compassions fail not. They are new every morning.' Today, if you have broken something precious in your walk with God, hear Him say, Hew thee new tables. He offers fresh starts.
S: After Moses' intercession, God told him to hew two new tables of stone like the first and make an ark of wood. The broken covenant was renewed on the same terms, showing grace after failure. If you have broken something precious in your walk with God, receive the fresh start He offers.
P: New tablets. Deuteronomy 10:1: 'Hew thee two tables of stone like unto the first, and come up unto me.' After the broken covenant, God offered a fresh start. He still does.

== 10:2
T: The words that were in the first tables
R: 'And I will write on the tables the words that were in the first tables which thou brakest, and thou shalt put them in the ark.' God promised to rewrite the same words and have them kept safe in the ark. Notice I will write. God Himself would write again. He did not delegate the task. Notice the words that were in the first tables. The same words. God's standards did not change because of Israel's failure. Notice which thou brakest. God named what had happened but did not hold it against Moses. Notice thou shalt put them in the ark. The renewed covenant would be guarded. Notice that God restores what we break, not by lowering His word but by writing it again. Hebrews 8 says, 'I will put my laws into their mind, and write them in their hearts.' Today, thank God that He rewrites His covenant in the lives of those who fail. His word stands, and His grace restores.
S: God promised to write again on the new tables the same words that were on the tables Moses broke, to be placed in the ark. He restored the covenant without lowering His standards. Thank God that He rewrites His covenant in the lives of those who fail, for His word stands and His grace restores.
P: Written again. Deuteronomy 10:2: 'I will write on the tables the words that were in the first tables which thou brakest.' Same standard, renewed grace. God restores what we break.

== 10:3

R: 'And I made an ark of shittim wood, and hewed two tables of stone like unto the first, and went up into the mount, having the two tables in mine hand.' Moses obeyed: he built the ark, cut the tablets and climbed the mountain. Notice I made… hewed… went up. Three actions of obedience. Notice shittim wood. Acacia, a hardy desert wood. Simple materials for a sacred purpose. Notice like unto the first. Moses followed God's instructions exactly. Notice having the two tables in mine hand. Blank tablets, carried up in faith. Moses brought empty stones for God to fill. Notice that renewal requires our participation. Moses had to cut the stone and climb the mountain. God would do the writing. Psalm 51 prays, 'Create in me a clean heart, O God; and renew a right spirit within me.' Today, bring God your empty tablets. Do your part in obedience, and trust Him to write His word on what you offer.
S: Moses made an ark of shittim wood, hewed two new tables like the first and went up the mountain carrying them. He brought blank stones for God to write on, doing his part in renewing the covenant. Bring God your empty tablets in obedience, trusting Him to write His word on what you offer.
P: Empty tablets carried up. Deuteronomy 10:3: 'I… hewed two tables of stone… and went up into the mount, having the two tables in mine hand.' Do your part; let God write.

His truth is the same yesterday, today and for ever.
S: God wrote the ten commandments on the new tables exactly according to the first writing and gave them to Moses. Not a word changed, showing the consistency and faithfulness of God's word. Rest in the unchanging nature of God's word, for what He said, He still says.

Notice that this growth happened in Egypt, even during slavery. God was working in the hardest place. Zechariah 4 asks, 'who hath despised the day of small things?' S: Moses reminded Israel that their fathers went down into Egypt with seventy persons, and now God had made them as the stars of heaven. God multiplied a single family into a nation, even during slavery. Do not despise small beginnings, but trust God with your small seeds of faith and service.
P: Seventy to the stars. Deuteronomy 10:22: 'Thy fathers went down into Egypt with threescore and ten persons; and now… as the stars of heaven.' Don't despise small beginnings. God multiplies.
