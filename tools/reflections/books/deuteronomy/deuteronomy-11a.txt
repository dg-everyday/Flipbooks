# Deuteronomy 11:1-16: Love and keep alway; a land that drinks the rain of heaven

== 11:1
T: Love the LORD, and keep his charge alway
R: 'Therefore thou shalt love the LORD thy God, and keep his charge, and his statutes, and his judgments, and his commandments, alway.' Love the LORD and keep His requirements always. Notice therefore. Because of all God has done, love Him. Notice love the LORD. Love comes first, before the list of commands. Notice keep his charge. His charge means what He has entrusted to us, like a watchman's duty. Notice statutes… judgments… commandments. The whole of God's word, received in love. Notice alway. Not occasionally, not when it suits us, but always. Love that lasts shows itself in faithful keeping day after day. Notice that love and obedience are joined in one sentence. They cannot be separated. First John 5 says, 'For this is the love of God, that we keep his commandments: and his commandments are not grievous.' Today, let love be the motive for your obedience. Keep God's word always, not out of fear but because you love Him.
S: Moses called Israel to love the LORD and keep His charge, statutes, judgments and commandments always. Love comes first, and obedience is how lasting love shows itself day after day. Let love be the motive for your obedience, keeping God's word always because you love Him.
P: Love that keeps. Deuteronomy 11:1: 'thou shalt love the LORD thy God, and keep his charge… alway.' Love comes first; faithful obedience follows. His commandments are not grievous.

== 11:2
T: Know ye this day
R: 'And know ye this day: for I speak not with your children which have not known, and which have not seen the chastisement of the LORD your God, his greatness, his mighty hand…' Remember today that it was you, not your children, who saw the LORD's discipline and His mighty power. Notice know ye this day. Recognise what you have experienced. Notice I speak not with your children. Moses addresses the eyewitnesses. Notice the chastisement of the LORD. His discipline and training. Notice his greatness, his mighty hand. They had seen God's power directly. Notice that eyewitnesses carry special responsibility. What we have seen, we must pass on. Notice that the next generation would depend on their testimony. First John 1 says, 'That which we have seen and heard declare we unto you.' Today, recognise what you have personally seen of God's work. You are a witness. Do not keep it to yourself, but tell those who have not yet seen.
S: Moses told the generation before him to know that day that they, not their children, had seen the LORD's discipline, greatness and mighty hand. Eyewitnesses carry the responsibility of passing on what they have seen to those who have not. Recognise what you have personally seen of God's work and declare it to others.
P: You saw it. Deuteronomy 11:2: 'know ye this day: for I speak not with your children which have not known, and which have not seen.' Eyewitnesses must pass it on. Tell what you've seen.

== 11:3
T: His miracles and his acts in Egypt
R: 'And his miracles, and his acts, which he did in the midst of Egypt unto Pharaoh the king of Egypt, and unto all his land.' They saw the signs He performed in Egypt against Pharaoh and his land. Notice his miracles, and his acts. God's works were both wonders and deeds. Notice in the midst of Egypt. God acted at the very centre of the world's strongest empire. Notice unto Pharaoh. The proudest king was humbled. Notice unto all his land. God's power reached every corner. Notice that Moses is building a list of evidence, one memory at a time. Faith rests on a record. Notice that God's power was displayed in the place of Israel's bondage. He shows His glory right where His people suffer. Exodus 9 records God saying, 'that my name may be declared throughout all the earth.' Today, remember that God can act in the midst of your hardest place. The place of your bondage can become the stage of His power.
S: Moses reminded Israel of God's miracles and acts in the midst of Egypt against Pharaoh and all his land. God displayed His power at the centre of the world's strongest empire, right where His people suffered. Remember that God can act in the midst of your hardest place and make it the stage of His power.
P: In the midst of Egypt. Deuteronomy 11:3: 'his miracles, and his acts, which he did in the midst of Egypt unto Pharaoh.' God acts right where His people suffer.

== 11:4
T: The water of the Red sea overflowed them
R: 'And what he did unto the army of Egypt, unto their horses, and to their chariots; how he made the water of the Red sea to overflow them as they pursued after you…' They saw how He drowned Egypt's army, horses and chariots in the Red Sea as they chased Israel. Notice the army of Egypt… horses… chariots. The most advanced military force of the day. Notice as they pursued after you. The enemy was close behind. Notice he made the water… to overflow them. God turned the sea into both a road and a grave. Notice unto this day. Egypt's power never recovered. Notice that God deals with pursuing enemies. What chases us is not beyond His reach. Exodus 14 says, 'The Egyptians whom ye have seen to day, ye shall see them again no more for ever.' Today, if something from your past is pursuing you, remember the Red Sea. God can close the waters over what chases you and set you free.
S: Moses reminded Israel how God made the Red Sea overflow Egypt's army, horses and chariots as they pursued them. God turned the sea into both a road of escape and a grave for the enemy. If something from your past is pursuing you, remember the Red Sea and trust God to set you free.
P: The sea closed. Deuteronomy 11:4: 'he made the water of the Red sea to overflow them as they pursued after you.' What chases you is not beyond God's reach.

== 11:5
T: What he did in the wilderness
R: 'And what he did unto you in the wilderness, until ye came into this place.' They saw what He did for them in the wilderness until they reached this place. Notice what he did unto you. The focus shifts from Egypt to Israel's own journey. Notice in the wilderness. Forty years of manna, water, guidance and discipline. Notice until ye came into this place. God brought them all the way. Notice how short this verse is. Moses could not list everything; it would take a book. Notice that the wilderness held both provision and correction. God did many things to and for them. Notice that every step of the journey was under His care. Psalm 136 says, 'To him which led his people through the wilderness: for his mercy endureth for ever.' Today, look back over your own wilderness. Recall what God did for you there, the provisions and the lessons. He has brought you all the way to this place.
S: Moses reminded Israel of what God did for them in the wilderness until they reached that place. The forty years held both provision and correction, and every step was under God's care. Look back over your own wilderness and recall what God did for you there, for He has brought you all the way.
P: All the way here. Deuteronomy 11:5: 'what he did unto you in the wilderness, until ye came into this place.' Provision and correction, every step. He has brought you this far.

== 11:6
T: The earth opened her mouth
R: 'And what he did unto Dathan and Abiram, the sons of Eliab, the son of Reuben: how the earth opened her mouth, and swallowed them up, and their households…' They saw how the earth swallowed Dathan and Abiram with their households, tents and possessions. Notice Dathan and Abiram. They joined Korah's rebellion against Moses and Aaron. Notice the earth opened her mouth. A terrifying, unmistakable judgment. Notice in the midst of all Israel. Everyone saw it. Notice that this was a judgment on rebellion against God's appointed leadership. Notice that Moses includes judgment in the list of God's works. His discipline is part of His story, not separate from it. Notice that rebellion damaged whole households. Numbers 16 records that they 'went down alive into the pit.' Today, take seriously the danger of rebellion against God. Remember that His holiness is real, and let reverence keep you humble and teachable.
S: Moses reminded Israel how the earth opened and swallowed Dathan and Abiram with their households and possessions in the sight of all Israel. Their rebellion against God's appointed leaders brought a terrifying judgment that affected whole families. Take the danger of rebellion against God seriously and let reverence keep you humble and teachable.
P: A sobering memory. Deuteronomy 11:6: 'what he did unto Dathan and Abiram… how the earth opened her mouth, and swallowed them up.' Rebellion has real consequences. Stay humble and teachable.

== 11:7
T: Your eyes have seen
R: 'But your eyes have seen all the great acts of the LORD which he did.' But you saw with your own eyes all the great things the LORD did. Notice but your eyes. Personal, eyewitness experience. Notice have seen all. Not a selection, but the whole record. Notice the great acts of the LORD. Mighty, memorable works. Notice that this verse concludes the list. Egypt, the sea, the wilderness and Dathan: they had seen it all. Notice that seeing brings accountability. Those who have seen much are responsible for much. Notice that faith based on God's acts is solid ground. John 20 records Jesus saying, 'blessed are they that have not seen, and yet have believed.' We may not see what Israel saw, but we have their testimony and our own. Today, list the great acts of God you have seen in your life. Let them deepen your trust and your obedience. You are not without evidence.
S: Moses concluded his list by telling Israel that their own eyes had seen all the great acts of the LORD. Eyewitness experience brought both solid ground for faith and accountability for obedience. List the great acts of God you have seen in your life and let them deepen your trust and obedience.
P: You've seen His works. Deuteronomy 11:7: 'But your eyes have seen all the great acts of the LORD which he did.' Seeing brings accountability. List what you've seen and trust Him more.

== 11:8
T: That ye may be strong
R: 'Therefore shall ye keep all the commandments which I command you this day, that ye may be strong, and go in and possess the land, whither ye go to possess it.' Keep all the commands I give you today, so that you may be strong enough to go in and take the land. Notice therefore. Because they had seen God's works, they should obey. Notice keep all the commandments. Full obedience. Notice that ye may be strong. Obedience produces strength. Notice go in and possess. Strength was needed for the conquest ahead. Notice that spiritual strength comes from walking in God's ways, not from our own resources. Disobedience weakens; obedience strengthens. Notice that strength is a result, not a requirement. They did not need to be strong first; obedience would make them strong. Isaiah 40 says, 'they that wait upon the LORD shall renew their strength.' Today, if you feel weak, look to obedience rather than self-effort. Walking in God's ways is the path to strength.
S: Moses told Israel to keep all the commandments so that they would be strong to go in and possess the land. Obedience produces spiritual strength rather than requiring it first. If you feel weak, look to faithful obedience rather than self-effort, for walking in God's ways is the path to strength.
P: Obedience makes strong. Deuteronomy 11:8: 'keep all the commandments… that ye may be strong, and go in and possess the land.' Strength is a result, not a requirement.

== 11:10
T: Not as the land of Egypt
R: 'For the land, whither thou goest in to possess it, is not as the land of Egypt, from whence ye came out, where thou sowedst thy seed, and wateredst it with thy foot, as a garden of herbs.' The land is not like Egypt, watered by foot. Notice not as the land of Egypt. Egypt depended on the Nile. Notice wateredst it with thy foot. Farmers worked irrigation channels by foot. Notice that Egypt's system gave a sense of control. Humans managed the water. Notice that the new land would be different. It would depend on rain from heaven. Notice that this was a shift from self-reliance to God-reliance. Israel would have to trust God for every harvest. Matthew 6 records Jesus saying, 'Behold the fowls of the air: for they sow not… yet your heavenly Father feedeth them.' Today, notice where you try to control everything by your own effort. God invites you into a life of trusting Him for the rain.
S: Moses explained that the promised land was not like Egypt, where farmers watered their seed by foot through irrigation channels. Egypt's system gave a sense of human control, but the new land would require trusting God for rain. Notice where you try to control everything by your own effort, and learn to trust God for the rain.
P: From control to trust. Deuteronomy 11:10: 'not as the land of Egypt… where thou sowedst thy seed, and wateredst it with thy foot.' God invites you to trust Him for the rain.

== 11:11
T: Drinketh water of the rain of heaven
R: 'But the land, whither ye go to possess it, is a land of hills and valleys, and drinketh water of the rain of heaven.' But the land you are entering is a land of hills and valleys that drinks rain from heaven. Notice hills and valleys. Varied, beautiful terrain, impossible to irrigate like Egypt's flat river plain. Notice drinketh water of the rain of heaven. The land depended on God's gift from above. Notice that this was both a blessing and a test. Rain was free, but it was not in Israel's control. Notice that dependence on God is not weakness but a privilege. It keeps us close to Him. Notice that hills and valleys picture life's ups and downs, all watered by the same faithful God. Psalm 65 says, 'Thou visitest the earth, and waterest it… with the river of God, which is full of water.' Today, embrace dependence on God. In the hills and valleys of your life, look up for His provision. He sends rain from heaven.
S: Moses described the promised land as a land of hills and valleys that drinks the rain of heaven. Unlike Egypt, it depended entirely on God's gift from above, a blessing that also kept Israel dependent on Him. Embrace dependence on God through the hills and valleys of life, looking up for His provision.
P: Rain from heaven. Deuteronomy 11:11: 'a land of hills and valleys, and drinketh water of the rain of heaven.' Dependence on God is a privilege. Look up for His provision.

God will send both autumn and spring rain in season. Notice I will give you. God promises personally. Notice in his due season. Rain at the right time. Not too early, not too late. Notice the first rain and the latter rain. Autumn rain softened the ground for planting; spring rain ripened the crops. Both were needed. Notice corn… wine… oil. The staples of life depended on God's timing. Notice that God's provision comes in seasons. Joel 2 says, 'he will cause to come down for you the rain, the former rain, and the latter rain.' Today, trust God's timing for the rain in your life. He sends what you need in its season, the first rain to begin and the latter rain to complete.
S: God promised to give rain in its due season, the first rain and the latter rain, so Israel could gather corn, wine and oil. Both the autumn and spring rains were needed, and God's provision came in its proper seasons. Trust God's timing for the rain in your life, for He sends what you need in its season.
P: Rain in its season. Deuteronomy 11:14: 'I will give you the rain of your land in his due season, the first rain and the latter rain.' God's provision comes on time. Trust His seasons.

== 11:16
T: That your heart be not deceived
R: 'Take heed to yourselves, that your heart be not deceived, and ye turn aside, and serve other gods, and worship them.' Be careful, or you will be enticed to turn away and worship other gods. Notice take heed. Pay attention. Notice your heart be not deceived. Idolatry begins with deception. The heart is persuaded that something else will satisfy. Notice ye turn aside. A small step off the path. Notice serve… worship. Then deeper commitment. Notice that the warning comes right after the promise of rain and plenty. The Canaanites worshipped Baal as the storm god who brought rain. Israel would be tempted to credit him for their harvests. Notice that idolatry often looks like practical wisdom. Jeremiah 17 says, 'The heart is deceitful above all things.' Today, ask where your heart might be deceived about the true source of your blessings. Give the credit for your rain to God alone.
S: Moses warned Israel to take heed that their hearts would not be deceived into turning aside to serve other gods. The Canaanites credited Baal with rain, and Israel would be tempted to do the same with their harvests. Ask where your heart might be deceived about the source of your blessings, and give God alone the credit.
P: A deceived heart. Deuteronomy 11:16: 'Take heed to yourselves, that your heart be not deceived, and ye turn aside.' Idolatry often looks practical. Credit your blessings to God alone.
