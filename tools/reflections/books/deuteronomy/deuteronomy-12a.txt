# Deuteronomy 12:1-16: The place the LORD shall choose, and rejoicing before Him

== 12:1
T: All the days that ye live upon the earth
R: 'These are the statutes and judgments, which ye shall observe to do in the land, which the LORD God of thy fathers giveth thee to possess it, all the days that ye live upon the earth.' These are the laws you must follow in the land, for as long as you live. Notice these are the statutes. A new section begins. Chapters 12 to 26 lay out the detailed laws. Notice observe to do. Again, careful obedience. Notice in the land. These laws were for settled life in Canaan. Notice all the days that ye live. Not a season of obedience but a lifetime. Notice that faith is not a sprint but a long walk. God's ways are for every day of our lives, through every change. Psalm 23 says, 'I will dwell in the house of the LORD for ever.' Today, commit to walking with God for the long haul. Faithfulness is measured in years, not moments.
S: Moses introduced the statutes and judgments Israel was to observe in the land all the days of their lives. A new section of detailed laws began, reaching every part of settled life. Commit to walking with God for the long haul, for faithfulness is measured in years, not moments.
P: For a lifetime. Deuteronomy 12:1: 'These are the statutes and judgments, which ye shall observe to do… all the days that ye live upon the earth.' Faith is a long walk. Commit for the long haul.

== 12:2
T: Under every green tree
R: 'Ye shall utterly destroy all the places, wherein the nations which ye shall possess served their gods, upon the high mountains, and upon the hills, and under every green tree.' Destroy every site where the nations worshipped their gods, on mountains, hills and under every spreading tree. Notice all the places. Not some, but all. Notice high mountains… hills… every green tree. Canaanite worship was everywhere. Shrines dotted the landscape. Notice that false worship had colonised the land. Israel was to reclaim it for God. Notice that these places would remain temptations if left standing. Later Israel failed to remove the high places, and they became a constant snare. Notice that what we leave standing can draw us back. Second Kings 17 laments that Israel 'set them up images and groves in every high hill, and under every green tree.' Today, look for the places in your life where old idols still stand. Do not leave them as future temptations. Clear them out.
S: Moses commanded Israel to destroy every place where the nations served their gods, on mountains, hills and under every green tree. False worship had spread across the land, and any shrine left standing would remain a temptation. Look for places in your life where old idols still stand and clear them out before they draw you back.
P: Clear out the old shrines. Deuteronomy 12:2: 'Ye shall utterly destroy all the places… upon the high mountains, and upon the hills, and under every green tree.' What stays can draw you back.

== 12:3
T: Destroy the names of them
R: 'And ye shall overthrow their altars, and break their pillars, and burn their groves with fire; and ye shall hew down the graven images of their gods, and destroy the names of them out of that place.' Tear down their altars, smash their pillars, burn their poles, cut down their idols and wipe out their names. Notice overthrow… break… burn… hew down. Thorough and complete. Notice destroy the names of them. Even the memory of the false gods was to be removed. Names carry influence. Notice that Israel was to erase the old allegiances so that only God's name remained. Notice the contrast with the next verses, where God's name will dwell in His chosen place. Notice that what we name and honour shapes our hearts. Psalm 16 says, 'their drink offerings of blood will I not offer, nor take up their names into my lips.' Today, examine what names you honour and speak of with devotion. Let God's name, not the names of lesser things, fill your heart and lips.
S: God told Israel to overthrow the altars, break the pillars, burn the groves, hew down the images and destroy the very names of the false gods. Even the memory of old allegiances was to be removed so that only God's name would be honoured. Examine what names you honour with devotion and let God's name alone fill your heart and lips.
P: Erase the old names. Deuteronomy 12:3: 'destroy the names of them out of that place.' What we name and honour shapes us. Let God's name fill your heart.

== 12:4
T: Ye shall not do so unto the LORD
R: 'Ye shall not do so unto the LORD your God.' You must not worship the LORD your God in their way. Notice how short and sharp this verse is. Notice ye shall not do so. Israel was not to copy Canaanite worship, even when worshipping the true God. Notice unto the LORD your God. The issue is not only whom we worship but how. God sets the terms of His own worship. Notice that it would be tempting to use Canaanite shrines and methods for the LORD. They were already there, convenient and familiar. But God refused to be worshipped like an idol. Notice that sincere worship is not enough if it ignores God's revealed will. Notice that God is holy and different. John 4 records Jesus saying, 'the true worshippers shall worship the Father in spirit and in truth.' Today, ask whether your worship is shaped by God's word or by convenience and culture. Worship Him as He has asked, not as the world around you does.
S: God told Israel not to worship Him the way the Canaanites worshipped their gods. The issue was not only whom they worshipped but how, for God sets the terms of His own worship. Ask whether your worship is shaped by God's word or by convenience and culture, and worship Him as He has asked.
P: Not their way. Deuteronomy 12:4: 'Ye shall not do so unto the LORD your God.' God sets the terms of His worship. Worship Him in spirit and in truth.
H: #DailyGrace #Deuteronomy124 #Worship #Truth #Holiness #BibleVerse

== 12:5
T: To put his name there
R: 'But unto the place which the LORD your God shall choose out of all your tribes to put his name there, even unto his habitation shall ye seek, and thither thou shalt come.' Instead, seek the place God will choose among your tribes as His dwelling, and go there. Notice the place which the LORD… shall choose. God, not the people, would decide where He was worshipped. Notice to put his name there. God's name represented His presence and character. Notice his habitation. His dwelling place among His people. Notice shall ye seek. Worship involves seeking God, going to Him. Notice that this place would later be Jerusalem. Notice that Jesus fulfilled this. In Him, God's name and presence dwell fully. John 2 records Jesus speaking of 'the temple of his body.' Today, seek God where He has chosen to make Himself known: in Christ. Come to Him through the way He has provided, not by paths of your own making.
S: Moses told Israel to seek the place the LORD would choose among their tribes to put His name and dwell. God, not the people, decided where He would be worshipped, later Jerusalem and ultimately Christ Himself. Seek God where He has chosen to make Himself known, coming to Him through Christ rather than paths of your own making.
P: The place He chooses. Deuteronomy 12:5: 'unto the place which the LORD your God shall choose… to put his name there… shall ye seek.' God chose where to meet His people. Seek Him in Christ.

== 12:6
T: Your offerings and freewill gifts
R: 'And thither ye shall bring your burnt offerings, and your sacrifices, and your tithes, and heave offerings of your hand, and your vows, and your freewill offerings…' Bring there your burnt offerings, sacrifices, tithes, special gifts, vows, freewill offerings and the firstborn of your herds. Notice thither ye shall bring. Worship involved bringing. Notice burnt offerings… sacrifices. Offerings for atonement and fellowship. Notice tithes. A tenth returned to God. Notice vows. Promises kept. Notice freewill offerings. Spontaneous gifts from a grateful heart. Notice the firstlings. The first and best belonged to God. Notice that worship engages our resources. God asks for our substance as well as our songs. Notice the variety. Some gifts were required, some voluntary, all expressions of devotion. Second Corinthians 9 says, 'God loveth a cheerful giver.' Today, consider what you bring to God. Give your first and best with a cheerful heart, as worship rather than duty.
S: Moses told Israel to bring to God's chosen place their burnt offerings, sacrifices, tithes, vows, freewill offerings and firstborn animals. Worship involved giving their substance, some required and some voluntary, all expressing devotion. Consider what you bring to God and give your first and best with a cheerful heart.
P: Bring your best. Deuteronomy 12:6: 'thither ye shall bring your burnt offerings… and your tithes… and your freewill offerings.' Worship includes giving. God loves a cheerful giver.

== 12:7
T: Ye shall rejoice
R: 'And there ye shall eat before the LORD your God, and ye shall rejoice in all that ye put your hand unto, ye and your households, wherein the LORD thy God hath blessed thee.' There, before the LORD, you and your families shall eat and rejoice in all God has blessed you with. Notice ye shall eat before the LORD. Worship included a meal. Notice ye shall rejoice. Joy was commanded. Worship was meant to be glad. Notice in all that ye put your hand unto. Their daily work became cause for celebration. Notice ye and your households. Families worshipped together. Notice wherein the LORD… hath blessed thee. Joy flowed from recognising God's blessing. Notice that God wants His people to enjoy His goodness in His presence. Philippians 4 says, 'Rejoice in the Lord alway: and again I say, Rejoice.' Today, celebrate God's blessings with your family. Share a meal with thanksgiving. Worship is not only solemn; it is joyful.
S: Moses told Israel to eat before the LORD at His chosen place and rejoice with their households in all He had blessed. Worship included a shared meal, and joy was commanded as a response to God's blessing on their work. Celebrate God's blessings with your family, sharing a meal with thanksgiving and joy.
P: Commanded to rejoice. Deuteronomy 12:7: 'ye shall eat before the LORD your God, and ye shall rejoice… ye and your households.' Worship is joyful. Celebrate His blessings together.

== 12:8
T: Right in his own eyes
R: 'Ye shall not do after all the things that we do here this day, every man whatsoever is right in his own eyes.' Do not do as we do here today, everyone doing what seems right to himself. Notice that we do here this day. Moses admitted that worship in the wilderness had been loose and irregular. Notice every man whatsoever is right in his own eyes. Personal preference ruled. Notice that this phrase becomes the refrain of the book of Judges, describing Israel's chaos. Notice that God wanted order and faithfulness, not individual improvisation. Notice that doing what seems right to us is the root of much confusion. Proverbs 14 says, 'There is a way which seemeth right unto a man, but the end thereof are the ways of death.' Today, examine where you do what is right in your own eyes rather than what is right in God's eyes. Submit your preferences to His word. Freedom is found in His way, not in our own.
S: Moses told Israel not to continue as they had in the wilderness, with every man doing whatever was right in his own eyes. This phrase later described the chaos of Judges, showing the danger of personal preference over God's order. Examine where you do what seems right to you rather than to God, and submit your preferences to His word.
P: Right in whose eyes? Deuteronomy 12:8: 'every man whatsoever is right in his own eyes.' The root of chaos in Judges. Submit your preferences to God's word.
H: #DailyGrace #Deuteronomy128 #Obedience #Submission #Wisdom #BibleVerse

== 12:9
T: Not as yet come to the rest
R: 'For ye are not as yet come to the rest and to the inheritance, which the LORD your God giveth you.' For you have not yet reached the rest and inheritance God is giving you. Notice not as yet. The full promise was still ahead. Notice the rest. Not idleness, but settled peace, security and belonging. Notice the inheritance. A permanent home. Notice which the LORD your God giveth you. God was the giver of rest. Notice that Israel lived between promise and fulfilment, as we do. Notice that some things wait for the place of rest. Not every practice can be fully established on the journey. Hebrews 4 says, 'For if Jesus had given them rest, then would he not afterward have spoken of another day.' Today, remember that you also live between promise and fulfilment. God's full rest is still ahead. Walk faithfully in the meantime, trusting that He will bring you home.
S: Moses explained that Israel had not yet come to the rest and inheritance the LORD was giving them. They lived between promise and fulfilment, as believers still do. Walk faithfully in the meantime, trusting that God will bring you into the full rest He has promised.
P: Not yet home. Deuteronomy 12:9: 'ye are not as yet come to the rest and to the inheritance.' We live between promise and fulfilment. His rest is ahead. Walk faithfully until then.

== 12:10
T: That ye dwell in safety
R: 'But when ye go over Jordan, and dwell in the land which the LORD your God giveth you to inherit, and when he giveth you rest from all your enemies round about, so that ye dwell in safety.' When God gives you rest and safety in the land. Notice when ye go over. Confidence that it would happen. Notice dwell in the land. Settling, not just passing through. Notice he giveth you rest. God gives rest; we do not achieve it alone. Notice from all your enemies round about. Peace on every side. Notice dwell in safety. Security and freedom from fear. Notice that God's goal for His people included safety and peace. Psalm 4 says, 'I will both lay me down in peace, and sleep: for thou, LORD, only makest me dwell in safety.' Today, thank God for any safety and rest you enjoy. And trust Him to give you rest from what threatens you. He delights to give His people peace.
S: Moses spoke of the day when Israel would cross the Jordan, dwell in the land and receive rest from all their enemies so they lived in safety. God's goal for His people included peace and security, which He gives rather than they achieve alone. Thank God for any safety you enjoy and trust Him to give you rest from what threatens you.
P: Rest and safety. Deuteronomy 12:10: 'when he giveth you rest from all your enemies round about, so that ye dwell in safety.' God gives rest. Trust Him with what threatens you.

== 12:11
T: To cause his name to dwell there
R: 'Then there shall be a place which the LORD your God shall choose to cause his name to dwell there; thither shall ye bring all that I command you…' Then bring everything I command to the place God chooses for His name to dwell. Notice then. Once there was rest, the central place of worship would be established. Notice to cause his name to dwell there. God would make His presence known in one place. Notice thither shall ye bring all. Worship was centred on God's chosen place. Notice all your choice vows. The best promises, freely made. Notice that God's presence was the centre of Israel's life. Everything revolved around where His name dwelt. Notice that David later brought the ark to Jerusalem and Solomon built the temple there. First Kings 8 records Solomon praying, 'that thine eyes may be open toward this house… even toward the place of which thou hast said, My name shall be there.' Today, make God's presence the centre of your life. Let everything revolve around Him.
S: Moses said that when Israel had rest, God would choose a place to cause His name to dwell, and there they would bring all their offerings. God's presence would be the centre of Israel's life, later fulfilled in Jerusalem's temple. Make God's presence the centre of your life and let everything you bring revolve around Him.
P: His name dwells there. Deuteronomy 12:11: 'a place which the LORD your God shall choose to cause his name to dwell there.' His presence at the centre. Let your life revolve around Him.

== 12:12
T: Ye, and your sons, and the Levite
R: 'And ye shall rejoice before the LORD your God, ye, and your sons, and your daughters, and your menservants, and your maidservants, and the Levite that is within your gates…' Rejoice before God with your sons and daughters, your servants and the Levites in your towns, who have no inheritance. Notice ye shall rejoice. Joy again. Notice the long list: sons, daughters, servants, Levites. Everyone was included in the celebration. Notice your menservants… maidservants. Servants were not left out; they rejoiced too. Notice the Levite… hath no part nor inheritance. Those without land were brought to the table. Notice that God's joy is inclusive. No one was meant to watch from outside. Notice that worship crosses social lines. Galatians 3 says, 'there is neither bond nor free… for ye are all one in Christ Jesus.' Today, make room at your table and in your celebrations for those who might be left out. Joy before God is meant to be shared.
S: Moses commanded Israel to rejoice before the LORD with their sons, daughters, servants and the Levites who had no inheritance. God's joy was inclusive, crossing social lines so that no one watched from outside. Make room in your celebrations for those who might be left out, for joy before God is meant to be shared.
P: Everyone at the table. Deuteronomy 12:12: 'ye shall rejoice before the LORD your God, ye, and your sons… and your maidservants, and the Levite.' God's joy is inclusive. Make room for others.

== 12:13
T: Not in every place that thou seest
R: 'Take heed to thyself that thou offer not thy burnt offerings in every place that thou seest.' Be careful not to offer your burnt offerings anywhere you please. Notice take heed to thyself. A personal warning. Notice in every place that thou seest. Convenient spots, attractive hills, old shrines. Notice that the temptation was to worship wherever it seemed good. Notice that this would lead to mixing true and false worship. Old Canaanite sites carried old associations. Notice that God's way required discipline and travel. Worship would cost effort. Notice that convenience is not the measure of right worship. Notice that unity of worship protected unity of faith. One place, one God, one people. Second Samuel 24 records David saying, 'neither will I offer burnt offerings unto the LORD my God of that which doth cost me nothing.' Today, do not let convenience shape your devotion. Worship that costs effort is often worship that matters most.
S: Moses warned Israel not to offer burnt offerings wherever they pleased. Convenient sites, especially old shrines, would lead to mixing true and false worship, so God's way required discipline and effort. Do not let convenience shape your devotion, for worship that costs effort often matters most.
P: Not wherever is convenient. Deuteronomy 12:13: 'offer not thy burnt offerings in every place that thou seest.' Convenience isn't the measure of worship. Offer what costs you something.

== 12:14
T: In the place which the LORD shall choose
R: 'But in the place which the LORD shall choose in one of thy tribes, there thou shalt offer thy burnt offerings, and there thou shalt do all that I command thee.' Offer them only at the place God will choose in one of your tribes, and do there all I command. Notice in the place which the LORD shall choose. God's choice, not human preference. Notice in one of thy tribes. A specific, real location. Notice there… there. The repetition emphasises one place. Notice all that I command thee. Obedience in worship meant following God's instructions fully. Notice that God's chosen place pointed forward. Ultimately, God chose a person rather than a place. Jesus is where God's name dwells and where sacrifice is offered once for all. John 14 records Jesus saying, 'I am the way, the truth, and the life: no man cometh unto the Father, but by me.' Today, come to God through the way He has chosen. Christ is the place where sacrifice and worship meet.
S: Moses told Israel to offer burnt offerings only at the place the LORD would choose in one of their tribes. God's choice of one place for sacrifice pointed forward to Christ, the one way to the Father. Come to God through the way He has chosen, for Christ is where sacrifice and worship meet.
P: God's chosen way. Deuteronomy 12:14: 'in the place which the LORD shall choose… there thou shalt offer thy burnt offerings.' One place pointed to one Person. Come to God through Christ.

== 12:15
T: According to the blessing of the LORD
R: 'Notwithstanding thou mayest kill and eat flesh in all thy gates, whatsoever thy soul lusteth after, according to the blessing of the LORD thy God which he hath given thee…' However, you may slaughter and eat meat in any of your towns as much as you want, as God blesses you. Notice notwithstanding. A permission alongside the restriction. Notice thou mayest kill and eat flesh in all thy gates. Ordinary meals did not require travelling to the central place. Notice whatsoever thy soul lusteth after. Here simply meaning whatever you desire; God was generous. Notice according to the blessing. Enjoy what God provides. Notice the unclean and the clean may eat. Everyday meat was not a sacred offering. Notice that God distinguishes between worship and ordinary life, and blesses both. First Timothy 4 says, 'every creature of God is good… if it be received with thanksgiving.' Today, enjoy God's provision in ordinary meals. Receive it with thanks as His blessing.
S: Moses permitted Israel to kill and eat meat in their own towns as they desired, according to the blessing God had given them. Ordinary meals did not require travel to the sanctuary, and God generously blessed everyday life as well as worship. Enjoy God's provision in ordinary meals and receive it with thanksgiving as His blessing.
P: Ordinary meals, real blessing. Deuteronomy 12:15: 'thou mayest kill and eat flesh in all thy gates… according to the blessing of the LORD thy God.' Receive daily food with thanks.

== 12:16
T: Ye shall not eat the blood
R: 'Only ye shall not eat the blood; ye shall pour it upon the earth as water.' But do not eat the blood; pour it out on the ground like water. Notice only. One limit within the freedom. Notice not eat the blood. Blood represented life, which belongs to God. Notice pour it upon the earth. A simple act of respect. Life was returned to the Giver. Notice that this rule went back to Noah. Life, even animal life, is sacred to God. Notice that eating meat was permitted, but it was to be done with reverence for life. Notice that blood also pointed to atonement. Leviticus teaches that blood made atonement on the altar. Notice that Christ's blood is the life given for us. Hebrews 9 says, 'without shedding of blood is no remission.' Today, enjoy God's gifts with reverence. Remember that all life belongs to Him, and give thanks for the life of Christ poured out for you.
S: Moses told Israel not to eat the blood but to pour it on the earth like water. Blood represented life, which belongs to God, and pointed to the atonement fulfilled in Christ's blood. Enjoy God's gifts with reverence for life, and give thanks for the life of Christ poured out for you.
P: Life belongs to God. Deuteronomy 12:16: 'Only ye shall not eat the blood; ye shall pour it upon the earth as water.' Reverence for life, and thanks for Christ's blood poured out for you.
