# Deuteronomy 12:17-32: Holy things before the LORD, the blood is the life, and no copying the nations

== 12:17
T: Not within thy gates
R: 'Thou mayest not eat within thy gates the tithe of thy corn, or of thy wine, or of thy oil, or the firstlings of thy herds or of thy flock…' Your tithes, firstborn animals, vows and freewill gifts must not be eaten at home. Notice thou mayest not eat within thy gates. Some things were holy and could not be treated as ordinary. Notice the tithe… the firstlings… vows… freewill offerings. These belonged to God in a special way. Notice the distinction between ordinary meals and holy meals. Not everything is the same. Notice that holy things required a holy setting. They were to be enjoyed in God's presence. Notice that consecrated things lose their meaning when treated casually. Notice that God's gifts set apart for Him deserve honour. Malachi 3 asks, 'Will a man rob God? Yet ye have robbed me… In tithes and offerings.' Today, distinguish between what is yours to use freely and what you have set apart for God. Treat holy things with honour.
S: Moses told Israel not to eat their tithes, firstborn animals, vows or freewill offerings at home within their gates. These were holy things belonging to God, to be enjoyed only in His presence rather than treated as ordinary. Distinguish between what is yours to use freely and what you have set apart for God, and treat holy things with honour.
P: Holy things are different. Deuteronomy 12:17: 'Thou mayest not eat within thy gates the tithe of thy corn.' Some things belong to God. Treat what's set apart for Him with honour.

== 12:18

Eat them before the LORD at His chosen place with your family, servants and the Levites, rejoicing in all your work. Notice before the LORD. Holy meals happened in God's presence. Notice thou, and thy son, and thy daughter. Family together. Notice the Levite. Again, the landless were included. Notice thou shalt rejoice. Joy is commanded again. Notice in all that thou puttest thine hands unto. Celebrating God's blessing on ordinary work. Notice that tithes were not only given away; they were also shared in a joyful feast before God. Generosity and celebration went together. Ecclesiastes 9 says, 'Go thy way, eat thy bread with joy.' Today, let your giving be joyful. Celebrate God's blessing on your work with those around you, in His presence.
S: Moses told Israel to eat their holy offerings before the LORD at His chosen place with their families, servants and Levites, rejoicing in all their work. Tithes were not only given but shared in joyful feasts, joining generosity and celebration. Let your giving be joyful and celebrate God's blessing on your work with others in His presence.
P: Joyful giving. Deuteronomy 12:18: 'thou shalt rejoice before the LORD thy God in all that thou puttest thine hands unto.' Generosity and celebration go together. Give with joy.

== 12:19
T: Forsake not the Levite
R: 'Take heed to thyself that thou forsake not the Levite as long as thou livest upon the earth.' Be careful not to neglect the Levites as long as you live in your land. Notice take heed. A personal warning. Notice forsake not the Levite. The Levites depended on the people's faithfulness. Notice as long as thou livest. A lifelong responsibility. Notice that those who serve God full-time need the support of God's people. Notice that neglecting them would harm both them and the worship they led. Notice that this concern appears again and again in Deuteronomy. God cares for His servants. Notice that the New Testament carries this principle forward. First Corinthians 9 says, 'Even so hath the Lord ordained that they which preach the gospel should live of the gospel.' Today, think about those who serve God's people around you. Support them in prayer, encouragement and practical ways. Do not forsake those who have given their lives to serve.
S: Moses warned Israel never to forsake the Levite as long as they lived in the land. The Levites depended on the people's faithfulness, and neglecting them would harm both them and the worship they led. Support those who serve God's people around you through prayer, encouragement and practical help.
P: Don't forget those who serve. Deuteronomy 12:19: 'Take heed to thyself that thou forsake not the Levite as long as thou livest.' Support those who've given their lives to serve.

== 12:20

Notice when the LORD… shall enlarge thy border. God planned growth. Notice as he hath promised. His promise of expansion was sure. Notice thy soul longeth. God acknowledged natural desires. Notice thou mayest eat. He permitted them. Notice that God is not stingy or suspicious of ordinary pleasures. He provides for them generously. Notice that the law adapts to new circumstances. As Israel spread out, travelling to the central place for every meal would be impossible. God made provision. Psalm 145 says, 'Thou openest thine hand, and satisfiest the desire of every living thing.' Today, thank God for the ordinary desires He satisfies. He is a generous Father who delights to provide.
S: God permitted Israel, once He enlarged their borders as promised, to eat meat freely whenever they desired it. He acknowledged natural desires and adapted His provision to their growing territory. Thank God for the ordinary desires He satisfies, for He is a generous Father who delights to provide.
P: A generous Father. Deuteronomy 12:20: 'When the LORD thy God shall enlarge thy border… thou mayest eat flesh.' God isn't stingy with ordinary pleasures. Thank Him for His generosity.

== 12:21

R: 'If the place which the LORD thy God hath chosen to put his name there be too far from thee, then thou shalt kill of thy herd and of thy flock… and thou shalt eat in thy gates…' If the chosen place is too far away, you may slaughter animals from your herds at home, as I have commanded. Notice be too far from thee. God understood distance and practical limits. Notice then thou shalt kill… in thy gates. A sensible provision for those far away. Notice which the LORD hath given thee. The herds were God's gift. Notice as I have commanded thee. Even the permission came with instruction. Freedom was within God's order. Notice that God's law was humane and realistic. He does not demand the impossible. Matthew 11 records Jesus saying, 'For my yoke is easy, and my burden is light.' Today, remember that God understands your circumstances. His commands fit real life. Where He gives freedom, enjoy it with gratitude and within His ways.
S: God allowed those who lived too far from His chosen place to slaughter animals from their herds and eat them at home as He commanded. His law was humane and realistic, taking practical limits into account. Remember that God understands your circumstances, and enjoy the freedom He gives with gratitude and within His ways.
P: God understands distance. Deuteronomy 12:21: 'If the place… be too far from thee, then thou shalt kill of thy herd… and thou shalt eat in thy gates.' His yoke is easy. He knows your circumstances.

== 12:22
T: As the roebuck and the hart
R: 'Even as the roebuck and the hart is eaten, so thou shalt eat them: the unclean and the clean shall eat of them alike.' Eat them as you would gazelle or deer; both clean and unclean people may eat. Notice as the roebuck and the hart. Wild game, eaten freely without sacrifice. Notice so thou shalt eat them. Domestic animals slaughtered at home would be treated the same way. Notice the unclean and the clean… alike. Ritual cleanness was not required for ordinary meals. Notice that God drew a clear line between sacred offerings and everyday food. Not every meal is a sacrifice, and not every act is worship in the formal sense. Notice that this freedom removed unnecessary burdens. Romans 14 says, 'For the kingdom of God is not meat and drink; but righteousness, and peace, and joy in the Holy Ghost.' Today, enjoy ordinary things as ordinary gifts. Do not turn everyday life into a burden of religious rules God never gave.
S: Moses said that meat slaughtered at home could be eaten like wild deer, by clean and unclean alike. God drew a clear line between sacred offerings and everyday food, removing unnecessary burdens. Enjoy ordinary things as ordinary gifts, without turning everyday life into a burden of rules God never gave.
P: Ordinary food, ordinary freedom. Deuteronomy 12:22: 'Even as the roebuck and the hart is eaten, so thou shalt eat them.' Don't burden everyday life with rules God never gave.

== 12:23
T: The blood is the life
R: 'Only be sure that thou eat not the blood: for the blood is the life; and thou mayest not eat the life with the flesh.' But be sure not to eat the blood, because the blood is the life. Notice only be sure. Within freedom, one firm boundary. Notice for the blood is the life. Blood carries life. Without it, a creature dies. Notice thou mayest not eat the life. Life belongs to God alone. Notice that this law taught reverence. Even in ordinary meals, Israel was reminded that life is sacred and God-given. Notice that this truth reaches its fulfilment in Christ. His blood, His life, was given for us. Leviticus 17 says, 'For the life of the flesh is in the blood: and I have given it to you upon the altar to make an atonement for your souls.' Today, let every ordinary meal remind you that life is God's gift. And let it point you to the life of Christ given for yours.
S: Moses told Israel to be sure not to eat the blood, for the blood is the life and life belongs to God. Even ordinary meals taught reverence for life, a truth fulfilled in Christ's blood given for us. Let every meal remind you that life is God's gift and point you to the life of Christ given for yours.
P: The blood is the life. Deuteronomy 12:23: 'be sure that thou eat not the blood: for the blood is the life.' Life belongs to God. Christ's life was given for yours.

Life was returned to the earth and to the God who made it. Notice that obedience can be very practical. Faith was expressed in how Israel prepared a meal. Notice that repetition is God's way of teaching. What He says twice, He wants us to remember. Notice that small acts of obedience shape a reverent heart. Luke 16 says, 'He that is faithful in that which is least is faithful also in much.' Today, practise faithfulness in small, everyday things. Ordinary obedience, repeated daily, forms a heart that honours God in everything.
S: Moses repeated that Israel must not eat the blood but pour it on the earth like water. Faith was expressed in the practical details of preparing a meal, and repetition showed how much it mattered. Practise faithfulness in small everyday things, for ordinary obedience repeated daily forms a heart that honours God.
P: Faithful in small things. Deuteronomy 12:24: 'Thou shalt not eat it; thou shalt pour it upon the earth as water.' Ordinary obedience forms a reverent heart.

== 12:27
T: Upon the altar of the LORD
R: 'And thou shalt offer thy burnt offerings, the flesh and the blood, upon the altar of the LORD thy God: and the blood of thy sacrifices shall be poured out upon the altar…' Offer your burnt offerings, flesh and blood, on the LORD's altar; pour out the blood of other sacrifices there, but you may eat their meat. Notice burnt offerings, the flesh and the blood. Wholly given to God. Notice upon the altar of the LORD. The proper place for sacrifice. Notice the blood… poured out upon the altar. At home, blood went on the ground; at the altar, it was offered to God. Notice and thou shalt eat the flesh. In fellowship offerings, the worshipper shared the meal. Notice that some offerings were wholly God's; others were shared. Both expressed relationship. Romans 12 says, 'present your bodies a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable unto God.' Today, offer yourself wholly to God. Give Him your whole life on His altar, and enjoy the fellowship He shares with you.
S: Moses told Israel to offer burnt offerings wholly on the LORD's altar and pour the blood of other sacrifices there, while eating the meat of fellowship offerings. Some offerings were wholly God's and others were shared, both expressing relationship with Him. Offer yourself wholly to God as a living sacrifice and enjoy the fellowship He shares with you.
P: On His altar. Deuteronomy 12:27: 'thou shalt offer thy burnt offerings… upon the altar of the LORD thy God.' Offer yourself wholly to Him and enjoy His fellowship.

S: Moses warned Israel not to be snared by asking how the defeated nations served their gods in order to imitate them. == 12:32
T: Thou shalt not add thereto
R: 'What thing soever I command you, observe to do it: thou shalt not add thereto, nor diminish from it.' Be careful to do everything I command; do not add to it or take away from it. Notice what thing soever I command. God's word is the standard. Notice observe to do it. Careful obedience. Notice not add thereto. Do not invent extra rules or practices. Notice nor diminish from it. Do not trim what God has said. Notice that this echoes chapter 4. It brackets the worship laws with a warning to keep God's word intact. Notice that both adding and subtracting distort worship. One creates burdens; the other loses truth. Revelation 22 warns, 'If any man shall add unto these things, God shall add unto him the plagues.' Today, hold God's word as it is. Do not add your preferences or remove what is uncomfortable. Receive it whole, and obey it whole.
S: Moses told Israel to observe everything God commanded without adding to it or diminishing from it. Echoing chapter 4, he warned that both adding and subtracting distort worship, one creating burdens and the other losing truth. Hold God's word as it is, receiving it whole and obeying it whole.
P: Whole, as given. Deuteronomy 12:32: 'thou shalt not add thereto, nor diminish from it.' Adding creates burdens; subtracting loses truth. Receive His word whole.
