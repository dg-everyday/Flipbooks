# Deuteronomy 13: Testing prophets, refusing enticement, and loyalty to the LORD alone

== 13:1
T: A prophet, or a dreamer of dreams
R: 'If there arise among you a prophet, or a dreamer of dreams, and giveth thee a sign or a wonder.' Suppose a prophet or a dreamer appears among you and announces a sign or wonder. Notice among you. The danger would come from inside the community. Notice a prophet, or a dreamer. Someone with apparent spiritual authority. Notice a sign or a wonder. Something impressive and supernatural. Notice that spiritual experiences can be powerful and persuasive. People are drawn to the extraordinary. Notice that God warns His people in advance. Not every sign comes from Him. Notice that the test of a messenger is not his power but his message. Matthew 24 records Jesus warning, 'there shall arise false Christs, and false prophets, and shall shew great signs and wonders.' Today, do not be swept along by impressive experiences alone. Ask where a message leads. Spiritual power is not proof of truth; faithfulness to God's word is.
S: Moses warned that a prophet or dreamer of dreams might arise among Israel and give a sign or wonder. Spiritual experiences can be powerful and persuasive, but not every sign comes from God. Do not be swept along by impressive experiences alone, but test every message by faithfulness to God's word.
P: Signs aren't proof. Deuteronomy 13:1: 'If there arise among you a prophet, or a dreamer of dreams, and giveth thee a sign or a wonder.' Test the message, not just the power.

== 13:2
T: And the sign come to pass
R: 'And the sign or the wonder come to pass, whereof he spake unto thee, saying, Let us go after other gods, which thou hast not known, and let us serve them.' And the sign comes true, and he says, Let us follow other gods and worship them. Notice the sign… come to pass. The prediction was accurate. This would make the prophet seem credible. Notice let us go after other gods. Here is the real message. Notice which thou hast not known. Gods with no history of rescuing Israel. Notice that a true sign can accompany a false message. Accuracy does not guarantee faithfulness. Notice that the test is where the message leads: toward God or away from Him. Galatians 1 says, 'though we, or an angel from heaven, preach any other gospel… let him be accursed.' Today, measure every teaching by whether it draws you to the true God. If it leads away from Him, reject it, however impressive.
S: Moses warned that even if a prophet's sign came true, his message to follow other gods must be rejected. Accuracy does not guarantee faithfulness, and the true test is whether a message leads toward God or away from Him. Measure every teaching by whether it draws you to the true God, and reject it if it leads away, however impressive.
P: Where does it lead? Deuteronomy 13:2: 'the sign or the wonder come to pass… saying, Let us go after other gods.' A true sign can carry a false message. Test where it leads.

== 13:3
T: The LORD your God proveth you
R: 'Thou shalt not hearken unto the words of that prophet, or that dreamer of dreams: for the LORD your God proveth you, to know whether ye love the LORD your God with all your heart and with all your soul.' Do not listen to that prophet, for God is testing you to see whether you love Him with all your heart and soul. Notice thou shalt not hearken. Refuse to listen. Notice the LORD your God proveth you. God allows such tests. Notice to know whether ye love. The test reveals love. Notice with all your heart and… soul. Wholehearted love will not be lured away by signs. Notice that false teaching becomes a test of loyalty. Those who truly love God will hold to Him. Notice that God permits testing for our growth. First John 4 says, 'Beloved, believe not every spirit, but try the spirits whether they are of God.' Today, let love for God anchor you when persuasive voices pull you elsewhere. Your loyalty is shown in what you refuse.
S: Moses told Israel not to listen to a false prophet, for God was testing whether they loved Him with all their heart and soul. False teaching becomes a test of loyalty, revealing whether love for God is wholehearted. Let love for God anchor you when persuasive voices pull you elsewhere, for loyalty is shown in what you refuse.
P: A test of love. Deuteronomy 13:3: 'the LORD your God proveth you, to know whether ye love the LORD your God with all your heart.' Loyalty shows in what you refuse. Try the spirits.

== 13:4
T: Walk after the LORD, and cleave unto him
R: 'Ye shall walk after the LORD your God, and fear him, and keep his commandments, and obey his voice, and ye shall serve him, and cleave unto him.' Follow the LORD, revere Him, keep His commands, obey Him, serve Him and hold fast to Him. Notice six verbs: walk, fear, keep, obey, serve, cleave. Together they describe complete loyalty. Notice walk after. Follow God, not the prophet. Notice obey his voice. God's voice, not the dreamer's. Notice cleave unto him. Hold tight when others pull. Notice that the antidote to false teaching is deeper devotion to God. Not just avoiding error but embracing Him more fully. Notice that this verse answers verse 2's let us go after other gods with ye shall walk after the LORD. John 10 records Jesus saying, 'My sheep hear my voice, and I know them, and they follow me.' Today, answer every competing voice by drawing closer to God. Walk after Him, obey His voice and cleave to Him.
S: Moses answered the false prophet's call with six commands: walk after the LORD, fear Him, keep His commandments, obey His voice, serve Him and cleave to Him. The antidote to false teaching is deeper devotion to God. Answer every competing voice by drawing closer to God, following His voice and holding fast to Him.
P: Walk after Him. Deuteronomy 13:4: 'Ye shall walk after the LORD your God… and obey his voice… and cleave unto him.' The antidote to false voices is deeper devotion.

== 13:5
T: Put the evil away from the midst of thee
R: 'And that prophet, or that dreamer of dreams, shall be put to death; because he hath spoken to turn you away from the LORD your God… So shalt thou put the evil away from the midst of thee.' That prophet must die for preaching rebellion. Notice shall be put to death. In Israel's covenant nation, leading people to other gods was treason against their King. Notice because he hath spoken to turn you away. The crime was spiritual seduction. Notice which brought you out… and redeemed you. Turning from such a God was the deepest betrayal. Notice put the evil away. The community's health depended on removing what corrupted it. Notice that this civil penalty belonged to Israel's theocracy and is not a model for believers today. The church deals with false teaching by correction and separation, not violence. Titus 3 says, 'A man that is an heretick after the first and second admonition reject.' Today, take false teaching seriously. Guard the community's faith with truth and love.
S: In Israel's covenant nation, a prophet who urged rebellion against the God who redeemed them was to be put to death to put away the evil. This civil penalty belonged to Israel's theocracy, and the church now addresses false teaching through correction and separation, not violence. Take false teaching seriously and guard the community's faith with truth and love.
P: Taking false teaching seriously. Deuteronomy 13:5: 'he hath spoken to turn you away from the LORD your God… So shalt thou put the evil away.' Today the church guards truth by correction, not violence.

== 13:6
T: Thy friend, which is as thine own soul
R: 'If thy brother, the son of thy mother, or thy son, or thy daughter, or the wife of thy bosom, or thy friend, which is as thine own soul, entice thee secretly, saying, Let us go and serve other gods…' If someone close entices you secretly. Notice the list: brother, son, daughter, wife, friend. The closest people in life. Notice the wife of thy bosom. The most intimate relationship. Notice thy friend, which is as thine own soul. A soul friend. Notice entice thee secretly. Quiet persuasion in private. Notice that the most dangerous temptations often come through those we love most. We trust them, so we lower our guard. Notice that loyalty to God must come before even the dearest relationships. Luke 14 records Jesus saying, 'If any man come to me, and hate not his father, and mother, and wife… he cannot be my disciple.' Today, love your family and friends deeply, but let no one, however dear, lead you away from God.
S: Moses warned that a brother, child, spouse or closest friend might secretly entice someone to serve other gods. The most dangerous temptations often come through those we love most, because we lower our guard. Love your family and friends deeply, but let no one, however dear, lead you away from God.
P: Even the closest. Deuteronomy 13:6: 'thy friend, which is as thine own soul, entice thee secretly.' Temptation often comes through those we love. Let no one lead you from God.

== 13:7
T: Nigh unto thee, or far off
R: 'Namely, of the gods of the people which are round about you, nigh unto thee, or far off from thee, from the one end of the earth even unto the other end of the earth.' Gods near or far, anywhere on earth. Notice nigh unto thee, or far off. Familiar or exotic. Notice that the temptation could be to a nearby, familiar religion or to something new and distant. Notice that God rules out every alternative, wherever it comes from. There is no exception for the fashionable or the traditional. Notice that the pull of the exotic can be as strong as the pull of the familiar. Acts 4 says, 'Neither is there salvation in any other: for there is none other name under heaven.' Today, be alert to alternatives that compete for your trust, whether familiar or new. There is no rival to the true God anywhere on earth.
S: Moses explained that the enticement might be to gods near or far, from one end of the earth to the other. Whether familiar local religion or something new and exotic, every alternative to the true God was ruled out. Be alert to alternatives that compete for your trust, familiar or new, for God has no rival anywhere.
P: Near or far, no rival. Deuteronomy 13:7: 'nigh unto thee, or far off from thee, from the one end of the earth even unto the other.' Familiar or exotic, there is none other name.

== 13:8
T: Thou shalt not consent unto him
R: 'Thou shalt not consent unto him, nor hearken unto him; neither shall thine eye pity him, neither shalt thou spare, neither shalt thou conceal him.' Do not yield or listen; do not pity, spare or shield him. Notice not consent. Do not agree. Notice nor hearken. Do not even listen. Notice neither… pity… spare… conceal. Five refusals. Loyalty to God was to override personal affection. Notice that the temptation would be to protect a loved one by hiding the matter. Notice that in Israel's covenant society, concealing treason against God endangered the whole nation. Notice that today the principle is not violence but refusal to compromise. We must not cover up what leads others away from God. Notice that love sometimes means saying no firmly. Ephesians 5 says, 'have no fellowship with the unfruitful works of darkness, but rather reprove them.' Today, do not consent to what leads you away from God, even when it comes from someone you love. Say no clearly and lovingly.
S: Moses commanded Israel not to consent to, listen to, pity, spare or conceal a loved one who enticed them to other gods. In the covenant nation, hiding such treason endangered everyone, and today the principle is a firm refusal to compromise. Do not consent to what leads you from God, even from someone you love, but say no clearly and lovingly.
P: Say no, even to loved ones. Deuteronomy 13:8: 'Thou shalt not consent unto him, nor hearken unto him.' Love sometimes means a firm no. Don't cover up what leads you from God.

== 13:9
T: Thine hand shall be first
R: 'But thou shalt surely kill him; thine hand shall be first upon him to put him to death, and afterwards the hand of all the people.' Your hand must be first. Notice thine hand shall be first. The one enticed had to take responsibility first. No hiding behind others. Notice afterwards the hand of all the people. The whole community shared in the judgment. Notice that this law, harsh to modern ears, belonged to Israel's unique covenant as a nation under God's direct rule. Notice that Jesus did not call His followers to execute heretics. When the disciples wanted fire on a Samaritan village, He rebuked them. Notice the lasting principle: personal responsibility for faithfulness, and a community that takes loyalty to God seriously. Luke 9 records Jesus saying, 'Ye know not what manner of spirit ye are of.' Today, take personal responsibility for guarding your faith. Do not leave it to others. Stand first for truth, with the spirit of Christ.
S: Under Israel's covenant law, the one enticed was to be first to carry out judgment on the enticer, followed by the whole community. Jesus did not call His followers to such punishment and rebuked those who wanted fire on a village, but the principle of personal responsibility for faithfulness remains. Take personal responsibility for guarding your faith and stand first for truth with the spirit of Christ.
P: Personal responsibility. Deuteronomy 13:9: 'thine hand shall be first upon him.' A law for ancient Israel; Jesus showed a different spirit. The lasting lesson: stand first for truth.

Notice which brought thee out… from the house of bondage. Turning from the Redeemer was ingratitude at its deepest. Notice that the seriousness of the penalty matched the seriousness of the betrayal. Notice that God's rescue makes faithlessness all the more grievous. Notice that in Christ, the penalty for our faithlessness fell on Him. Romans 5 says, 'while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us.' Today, consider how serious it is to draw others away from God. Guard your influence, and thank Christ, who bore the penalty for every betrayal.
S: The covenant law required that one who sought to thrust another away from the God who redeemed them from bondage be stoned. The seriousness of the penalty matched the seriousness of turning from the Redeemer, a penalty Christ bore for our own faithlessness. Guard your influence so you never draw others from God, and thank Christ for bearing the penalty for every betrayal.
P: The gravity of turning away. Deuteronomy 13:10: 'he hath sought to thrust thee away from the LORD thy God, which brought thee out.' Christ bore the penalty for our betrayals. Guard your influence.
