# Deuteronomy 14: Children of the LORD, clean and unclean food, and the tithe that feeds the needy

== 14:1
T: Ye are the children of the LORD
R: 'Ye are the children of the LORD your God: ye shall not cut yourselves, nor make any baldness between your eyes for the dead.' You are God's children; do not cut yourselves or shave your heads for the dead. Notice ye are the children. Identity comes first. Israel belonged to God as children to a father. Notice ye shall not cut yourselves. Pagan mourning involved self-harm. Notice nor make any baldness. Shaving the forehead was a sign of despair in other religions. Notice that God's children grieve differently. Their sorrow is real, but it is not hopeless or self-destructive. Notice that God cares about how we treat our bodies, even in grief. First Thessalonians 4 says, 'that ye sorrow not, even as others which have no hope.' Today, remember whose child you are, especially in sorrow. Grieve honestly, but do not harm yourself. Your Father holds both you and those you have lost.
S: Moses told Israel that as children of the LORD they must not cut themselves or shave their foreheads for the dead as pagans did. God's children grieve with real sorrow but not with hopeless self-harm. Remember whose child you are in sorrow, grieving honestly while trusting your Father with you and those you have lost.
P: Children of God in grief. Deuteronomy 14:1: 'Ye are the children of the LORD your God: ye shall not cut yourselves… for the dead.' Grieve, but not without hope. Your Father holds you.

== 14:2
T: A peculiar people unto himself
R: 'for thou art an holy people unto the LORD thy God, and the LORD hath chosen thee to be a peculiar people unto himself, above all the nations that are upon the earth.' For you are holy to the LORD; He chose you as His treasured possession out of all nations. Notice for. This verse explains the previous one. Israel's distinct practices flowed from their identity. Notice an holy people. Set apart. Notice hath chosen thee. God's initiative. Notice a peculiar people. Not odd, but treasured, His own special possession. Notice unto himself. They belonged to Him personally. Notice that the food laws that follow rest on this foundation. Holiness touched every area of life, even the dinner table. Notice that being chosen brings both privilege and difference. Titus 2 says Christ 'gave himself for us, that he might… purify unto himself a peculiar people, zealous of good works.' Today, embrace being different because you belong to God. Let your identity as His treasured possession shape even ordinary choices.
S: Moses explained that Israel was a holy people, chosen by the LORD to be a peculiar people unto Himself above all nations. Their distinct practices, even at the table, flowed from this treasured identity. Embrace being different because you belong to God, and let your identity as His possession shape ordinary choices.
P: Treasured and different. Deuteronomy 14:2: 'the LORD hath chosen thee to be a peculiar people unto himself.' Not odd, but His own. Let belonging shape your choices.

== 14:3
T: Not eat any abominable thing
R: 'Thou shalt not eat any abominable thing.' Do not eat anything detestable. Notice how simple this verse is. A clear principle before the details. Notice abominable thing. Something God declared unfit for His people. Notice that God cared about Israel's diet. Holiness reached into daily habits. Notice that the food laws set Israel apart from surrounding nations every day. Each meal was a reminder of their identity. Notice that some unclean animals carried health risks; others were linked with pagan practices or with death. But the main point was obedience and distinction. Notice that in Christ these food laws are fulfilled. Mark 7 records that Jesus said this, 'purging all meats.' Notice that the principle remains: God's people are called to holiness in everyday choices. Today, consider how your daily habits reflect your belonging to God. Holiness is not only for worship services; it shapes what you take in and how you live.
S: Moses gave the simple principle that Israel must not eat any abominable thing. The food laws set Israel apart daily, and though Christ has declared all foods clean, the call to holiness in everyday choices remains. Consider how your daily habits reflect your belonging to God, for holiness shapes what you take in and how you live.
P: Holiness at the table. Deuteronomy 14:3: 'Thou shalt not eat any abominable thing.' Christ fulfilled the food laws, but holiness still reaches daily habits.

Today, notice how much God has permitted and provided before you focus on what He forbids. His generosity comes first.
S: God began the food laws with what was permitted: the ox, the sheep and the goat. The common farm animals that fed Israel were also those used in sacrifice, showing God's generous provision. Notice how much God has permitted and provided before you focus on what He forbids.
P: Freedom first. Deuteronomy 14:4: 'These are the beasts which ye shall eat: the ox, the sheep, and the goat.' God starts with what He gives. His generosity comes first.

== 14:6
T: Parteth the hoof and cheweth the cud
R: 'And every beast that parteth the hoof, and cleaveth the cleft into two claws, and cheweth the cud among the beasts, that ye shall eat.' You may eat any animal with a split hoof divided in two that also chews the cud. Notice parteth the hoof. A physical sign. Notice cheweth the cud. Another sign. Both together made an animal clean. Notice that God gave clear, observable tests. Israel did not have to guess. Notice that later teachers saw a picture here: chewing the cud as meditating on God's word, and a divided hoof as walking carefully. Notice that God's law is practical and clear. He makes His will knowable. Psalm 1 says the blessed man's 'delight is in the law of the LORD; and in his law doth he meditate day and night.' Today, appreciate God's clarity. And like an animal that chews the cud, take time to meditate on His word, turning it over again and again.
S: Moses gave the test for clean animals: those that part the hoof into two claws and chew the cud. God gave clear, observable signs so Israel did not have to guess, and later teachers saw chewing the cud as a picture of meditation. Appreciate God's clarity and take time to meditate on His word, turning it over again and again.
P: Clear signs. Deuteronomy 14:6: 'every beast that parteth the hoof… and cheweth the cud.' God makes His will knowable. Meditate on His word day and night.

== 14:8
T: The swine is unclean
R: 'And the swine, because it divideth the hoof, yet cheweth not the cud, it is unclean unto you: ye shall not eat of their flesh, nor touch their dead carcase.' The pig is unclean; do not eat or touch it. Notice the swine. The most famous unclean animal. Notice divideth the hoof, yet cheweth not the cud. It looked clean on the outside but failed the inner test. Notice nor touch their dead carcase. Contact with death made one unclean. Notice that the pig appears clean by one sign but is not. Outward appearance can mislead. Notice that in the parable of the prodigal son, feeding pigs marked the son's lowest point. Notice that Jesus later declared all foods clean, and Peter's vision in Acts 10 opened the gospel to the Gentiles. Acts 10 records God saying, 'What God hath cleansed, that call not thou common.' Today, look beyond appearances to the heart. And rejoice that God's grace cleanses what was once unclean.
S: Moses declared the swine unclean because it divides the hoof but does not chew the cud, and forbade eating or touching its carcass. Outward appearance could mislead, though Christ later declared all foods clean and God opened the gospel to the Gentiles. Look beyond appearances to the heart and rejoice that God's grace cleanses what was once unclean.
P: Looks clean, isn't. Deuteronomy 14:8: 'the swine, because it divideth the hoof, yet cheweth not the cud, it is unclean.' Appearances mislead. And what God cleanses, call not common.

== 14:22

R: 'Thou shalt truly tithe all the increase of thy seed, that the field bringeth forth year by year.' Set aside a tenth of all your crops every year. Notice truly tithe. Faithfully, honestly, fully. Notice all the increase. Not a portion of the tithe, but a tenth of everything. Notice year by year. A regular, ongoing practice. Notice that the tithe acknowledged that all increase came from God. Giving back a tenth was a way of saying everything belongs to Him. Notice that tithing trained Israel in trust. If God provided the harvest, He could provide even after they gave. Notice that Jacob had promised a tithe at Bethel long before. Malachi 3 says, 'Bring ye all the tithes into the storehouse… and prove me now herewith, saith the LORD of hosts.' Today, consider your giving. Do you give faithfully and proportionally? Let your generosity declare that all you have comes from God and that you trust Him for the rest.
S: Moses commanded Israel to truly tithe all the increase of their fields year by year. The tithe acknowledged that everything came from God and trained Israel to trust Him after giving. Consider whether you give faithfully and proportionally, letting your generosity declare that all you have comes from God.
P: Truly tithe. Deuteronomy 14:22: 'Thou shalt truly tithe all the increase of thy seed.' Giving declares that everything comes from God. Trust Him with the rest.

Eat your tithe before the LORD at His chosen place, so that you learn to revere Him always. Notice thou shalt eat. The tithe was not only given away but eaten in a sacred feast. Notice before the LORD. In His presence. Notice that thou mayest learn. Tithing was a school. Notice to fear the LORD… always. The goal was lifelong reverence. Notice that generosity shapes the heart. As Israel gave and feasted before God, they learned to honour Him. Notice that giving is not only for others' benefit; it changes the giver. Matthew 6 says, 'For where your treasure is, there will your heart be also.' Today, let your giving teach your heart. As you give to God, you will learn to honour Him more. Generosity is a school of reverence.

Tithing was a school in which giving and feasting before God shaped the heart in reverence. Let your giving teach your heart, for generosity is a school of reverence where your treasure leads your heart.

Notice lay it up within thy gates. Stored locally, not taken to the central sanctuary. Notice that this third-year tithe served the local community. It was a social safety net. Notice that God built care for the needy into Israel's regular rhythm. Generosity was planned, not just spontaneous. Notice that local storehouses made help accessible.
