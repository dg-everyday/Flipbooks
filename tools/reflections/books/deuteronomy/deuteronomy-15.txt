# Deuteronomy 15: The year of release, an open hand to the poor, and freeing servants generously

== 15:1
T: Every seven years a release
R: 'At the end of every seven years thou shalt make a release.' Every seventh year you must cancel debts. Notice at the end of every seven years. A regular rhythm, like the Sabbath day but on a larger scale. Notice thou shalt make a release. A fresh start. Debts were cancelled or suspended. Notice that God built mercy into the calendar. No one would be trapped in debt forever. Notice that seven is the number of completion and rest. The seventh year gave people a chance to breathe. Notice that God cares about economic life. Faith is not only private but touches money, work and debt. Luke 4 records Jesus reading, 'to preach deliverance to the captives… To preach the acceptable year of the Lord.' Today, consider whether you hold anyone in debt, financially or relationally, without mercy. God's heart is for release. And thank Him for the release from your debt of sin in Christ.
S: Moses commanded that at the end of every seven years Israel should make a release of debts. God built mercy into the calendar so that no one would be trapped in debt forever. Consider whether you hold anyone in debt without mercy, and thank God for releasing you from your debt of sin in Christ.
P: The year of release. Deuteronomy 15:1: 'At the end of every seven years thou shalt make a release.' God built mercy into the calendar. He has released you; release others.

== 15:2
T: The LORD'S release
R: 'And this is the manner of the release: Every creditor that lendeth ought unto his neighbour shall release it; he shall not exact it of his neighbour, or of his brother; because it is called the LORD'S release.' Every creditor shall cancel what he has lent his neighbour; he must not demand payment, for the LORD's release has been proclaimed. Notice every creditor. No exceptions among Israelites. Notice shall release it. Let it go. Notice he shall not exact it. Do not demand payment. Notice of his neighbour, or of his brother. These were family and community, not strangers. Notice it is called the LORD'S release. This was God's act, not merely a human policy. The creditor released because God commanded it. Notice that forgiveness of debt reflects God's forgiveness of us. Matthew 6 teaches us to pray, 'And forgive us our debts, as we forgive our debtors.' Today, think about the LORD's release in your own life. Because He has forgiven you, forgive others what they owe you.
S: Moses explained that every creditor must release what he had lent his neighbour and brother, because it was called the LORD's release. Debt forgiveness was God's act and reflected His forgiveness of His people. Because God has released you from your debts, forgive others what they owe you.
P: The LORD's release. Deuteronomy 15:2: 'he shall not exact it of his neighbour… because it is called the LORD'S release.' Forgive our debts as we forgive our debtors.

A trading foreigner was not under the same obligations. Notice thy brother. Within God's family, a special generosity applied. Notice thine hand shall release. The hand that lent must open and let go. Notice that the covenant community was meant to be a place of extraordinary mercy. Notice that God's people are to treat one another as family. Galatians 6 says, 'let us do good unto all men, especially unto them who are of the household of faith.' Today, ask how you can show extra generosity to your brothers and sisters in faith. Let the church be a place where release and mercy are practised freely.
S: Moses explained that debts from a foreigner could be collected, but the hand must release what a brother owed. The covenant community was meant to be a family marked by extraordinary mercy. Show extra generosity to your brothers and sisters in faith, letting God's family be a place of release and mercy.
P: Family mercy. Deuteronomy 15:3: 'that which is thine with thy brother thine hand shall release.' Do good to all, especially the household of faith.
