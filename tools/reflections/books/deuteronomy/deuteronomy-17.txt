# Deuteronomy 17: Unblemished offerings, two witnesses, hard cases and the law of the king

== 17:1
T: Wherein is blemish
R: 'Thou shalt not sacrifice unto the LORD thy God any bullock, or sheep, wherein is blemish, or any evilfavouredness: for that is an abomination unto the LORD thy God.' Do not sacrifice to God an ox or sheep with any defect or flaw; that would be detestable to Him. Notice any bullock, or sheep, wherein is blemish. Damaged offerings were not acceptable. Notice evilfavouredness. Anything ugly or defective. Notice an abomination unto the LORD. Offering God second-best insults Him. Notice that this verse links the section on justice with worship. Honouring God and honouring people go together. Notice that offering God our leftovers reveals what we really think of Him. Malachi 1 asks, 'offer it now unto thy governor; will he be pleased with thee?' Today, examine what you offer God in time, energy and resources. Would you offer the same to someone you deeply respect? Give Him your best, not what is left over.
S: Moses forbade sacrificing any bullock or sheep with a blemish or defect, for that is an abomination to the LORD. Offering God second-best reveals what we really think of Him and insults His honour. Examine what you offer God in time, energy and resources, and give Him your best rather than what is left over.
P: Not second-best. Deuteronomy 17:1: 'Thou shalt not sacrifice unto the LORD thy God any bullock, or sheep, wherein is blemish.' What you offer reveals what you think of Him. Give your best.

== 17:2
T: Transgressing his covenant

Both equally accountable. Notice wrought wickedness in the sight of the LORD. God sees all. Notice in transgressing his covenant. The sin was breaking a sacred relationship, not just a rule. Notice that covenant unfaithfulness is like marital betrayal. Notice that God holds men and women alike responsible. Hosea 6 says, 'they like men have transgressed the covenant: there have they dealt treacherously against me.' Today, remember that your relationship with God is a covenant. Faithfulness to Him is not about rules alone but about loyal love. Guard that covenant in your heart.
S: Moses described the case of a man or woman found working wickedness in God's sight by transgressing His covenant. Covenant unfaithfulness is a betrayal of a sacred relationship, and men and women are equally accountable. Remember that your relationship with God is a covenant of loyal love, and guard it in your heart.
P: Covenant, not just rules. Deuteronomy 17:2: 'man or woman, that hath wrought wickedness in the sight of the LORD thy God, in transgressing his covenant.' Faithfulness is loyal love. Guard it.

== 17:3
T: The sun, or moon, or host of heaven
R: 'And hath gone and served other gods, and worshipped them, either the sun, or moon, or any of the host of heaven, which I have not commanded.' And has worshipped other gods, bowing down to the sun, moon or stars, against My command. Notice hath gone and served. Idolatry is active, a going after. Notice the sun, or moon, or… the host of heaven. Astral worship was common in the ancient world. Notice which I have not commanded. God's word is the standard. Notice that the sun and moon are good creations but terrible gods. They serve; they do not rule. Notice that people still look to the stars for guidance and meaning. Astrology is an ancient temptation in modern form. Notice that God alone directs our lives. Jeremiah 10 says, 'be not dismayed at the signs of heaven; for the heathen are dismayed at them.' Today, look to the Creator, not His creations, for guidance. Your future is in God's hands, not written in the stars.
S: Moses described the sin of serving other gods, including the sun, moon and host of heaven, which God had not commanded. Sun and moon are good creations but false gods, and the ancient temptation to look to the stars persists today. Look to the Creator rather than His creations for guidance, for your future is in God's hands, not the stars.
P: Not in the stars. Deuteronomy 17:3: 'served other gods… either the sun, or moon, or any of the host of heaven.' Your future is in God's hands. Look to the Creator, not creation.

== 17:4
T: Enquired diligently, and it be true
R: 'And it be told thee, and thou hast heard of it, and enquired diligently, and, behold, it be true, and the thing certain, that such abomination is wrought in Israel.' If you hear of it, investigate thoroughly; if it is proven true that this detestable thing has been done. Notice it be told thee. Reports would come. Notice enquired diligently. Careful investigation, not quick reaction. Notice it be true, and the thing certain. Proof was required. Notice that serious accusations demanded serious care. Notice that God's justice protects the accused as well as the community. No one was to be punished on rumour. Notice that this is the same standard as in chapter 13. God consistently requires truth before action. Proverbs 25 says, 'Go not forth hastily to strive.' Today, practise patient investigation before forming judgments. Do not condemn people on hearsay. Seek certainty, and treat even the accused with fairness.
S: Moses required that any report of idolatry be investigated diligently and proven true and certain before action was taken. God's justice protected the accused as well as the community, refusing punishment on rumour. Practise patient investigation before judging, and treat even the accused with fairness.
P: Certainty before action. Deuteronomy 17:4: 'enquired diligently, and, behold, it be true, and the thing certain.' No condemnation on hearsay. Seek truth patiently.

== 17:5
T: Unto thy gates
R: 'Then shalt thou bring forth that man or that woman, which have committed that wicked thing, unto thy gates, even that man or that woman, and shalt stone them with stones, till they die.' Then take the guilty person to your city gate and stone them to death. Notice bring forth… unto thy gates. Judgment was public, at the city gate where justice was done. Notice that man or that woman. Repeated for emphasis. Equal accountability. Notice stone them. The covenant penalty for idolatry in Israel's theocracy. Notice that this was the gravest charge in Israel, treason against their divine King. Notice that this penalty belonged to Israel's unique national covenant. The New Testament does not command believers to punish unbelief with death. Notice that Christ came to bear judgment for sinners. John 8 records Jesus saying to accusers, 'He that is without sin among you, let him first cast a stone.' Today, take sin seriously, starting with your own. And thank Jesus, who took the judgment we deserved.
S: Under Israel's covenant law, a proven idolater was brought to the city gate and stoned, the gravest penalty for treason against their divine King. This belonged to Israel's theocracy, and the New Testament does not command believers to punish unbelief with death. Take sin seriously, beginning with your own, and thank Jesus who took the judgment we deserved.
P: Grave sin, greater grace. Deuteronomy 17:5: 'that man or that woman… shalt stone them with stones.' A law for ancient Israel. Jesus bore our judgment. Let him without sin cast the first stone.

== 17:6
T: Two or three witnesses
R: 'At the mouth of two witnesses, or three witnesses, shall he that is worthy of death be put to death; but at the mouth of one witness he shall not be put to death.' A person may be put to death only on the testimony of two or three witnesses, never on one. Notice two witnesses, or three. Multiple testimonies were required. Notice not at the mouth of one witness. One person's word was not enough. Notice that this protected people from false accusation, personal vendettas and mistakes. Notice that this principle runs through Scripture and into the church. Jesus applied it to conflict resolution. Matthew 18 says, 'in the mouth of two or three witnesses every word may be established.' Notice that God's law built safeguards against injustice. Today, apply this wisdom. Do not accept a serious accusation against someone on a single, unconfirmed account. Look for corroboration, and protect people's reputations.
S: Moses required the testimony of two or three witnesses before anyone could be put to death, never just one. This safeguard protected people from false accusation and personal vendettas, a principle Jesus applied to the church. Do not accept a serious accusation on one unconfirmed account, but look for corroboration and protect people's reputations.
P: Two or three witnesses. Deuteronomy 17:6: 'at the mouth of one witness he shall not be put to death.' A safeguard against injustice. Don't accept accusations on one voice alone.

Let your words carry the weight of truth and responsibility.
S: Moses required the witnesses' hands to be first in carrying out a death sentence, followed by all the people. This made witnesses accountable for their testimony and discouraged false accusation. Speak only what you are willing to stand behind, and do not accuse others lightly.
P: Stand behind your words. Deuteronomy 17:7: 'The hands of the witnesses shall be first upon him.' Witnesses were accountable for testimony. Don't accuse lightly.
