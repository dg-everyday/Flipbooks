# Deuteronomy 18: The Levites' portion, forbidden practices, and the Prophet like Moses

== 18:1
T: The offerings of the LORD made by fire
R: 'The priests the Levites, and all the tribe of Levi, shall have no part nor inheritance with Israel: they shall eat the offerings of the LORD made by fire, and his inheritance.' The Levitical priests and the whole tribe of Levi have no land; they live on the offerings made to the LORD. Notice no part nor inheritance. No land of their own. Notice they shall eat the offerings. God provided through the people's worship. Notice and his inheritance. What belonged to God was shared with His servants. Notice that the Levites' livelihood was tied directly to the faithfulness of Israel's worship. When Israel neglected God, the Levites suffered. Notice that those who serve God depend on Him through His people. Notice that faith was the Levites' daily bread. Philippians 4 records Paul thanking believers, 'ye sent once and again unto my necessity.' Today, recognise that those who serve in ministry depend on the faithfulness of God's people. Be part of His provision for them.
S: Moses explained that the priests and the tribe of Levi had no land inheritance but would eat the offerings made to the LORD. Their livelihood was tied to the faithfulness of Israel's worship, and when the people neglected God, the Levites suffered. Recognise that those who serve in ministry depend on God's people, and be part of His provision for them.
P: Provided through worship. Deuteronomy 18:1: 'they shall eat the offerings of the LORD made by fire, and his inheritance.' Ministry depends on faithful people. Be part of God's provision.

== 18:2

Their security rested in Him, not in fields. Notice that every believer is invited into this. In Christ, God Himself is our inheritance. Notice that earthly possessions are temporary; God is eternal. Lamentations 3 says, 'The LORD is my portion, saith my soul; therefore will I hope in him.' Today, ask whether God Himself is your treasure. Possessions may come and go, but if the LORD is your inheritance, you have what can never be lost.
S: Moses repeated that the Levites would have no inheritance among their brethren, for the LORD Himself was their inheritance as He promised. Their security rested in God rather than land, a privilege now offered to every believer in Christ. Ask whether God Himself is your treasure, for if the LORD is your inheritance you have what can never be lost.
P: The LORD is my portion. Deuteronomy 18:2: 'the LORD is their inheritance, as he hath said unto them.' Possessions fade. If God is your inheritance, you can never lose it.

== 18:3
T: The priest's due
R: 'And this shall be the priest… due from the people, from them that offer a sacrifice, whether it be ox or sheep; and they shall give unto the priest the shoulder, and the two cheeks, and the maw.' The priests' share from those who sacrifice an ox or sheep is the shoulder, the jowls and the stomach. Notice the priest's due. What rightly belonged to the priests. Notice from them that offer a sacrifice. Worship supported the worship leaders. Notice the shoulder, and the two cheeks, and the maw. Specific portions. God was precise. Notice that God set clear expectations so priests would not take more than their share. Eli's sons later abused this and took the best by force. Notice that clarity protects both givers and receivers. Notice that God cares about fair provision for His servants. First Timothy 5 says, 'The labourer is worthy of his reward.' Today, support those who serve you spiritually, fairly and faithfully. And if you serve, receive only what is rightly yours.
S: Moses specified the priest's due from sacrifices of ox or sheep: the shoulder, the two cheeks and the maw. Clear expectations protected both worshippers and priests from abuse, which Eli's sons later ignored. Support those who serve you spiritually fairly and faithfully, and if you serve, receive only what is rightly yours.
P: Fair provision. Deuteronomy 18:3: 'this shall be the priest's due from the people… the shoulder, and the two cheeks, and the maw.' The labourer is worthy. Clarity protects everyone.
