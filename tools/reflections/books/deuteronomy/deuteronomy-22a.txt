# Deuteronomy 22:1-15: Lost things returned, help for the fallen, care for creation, and a safe roof

== 22:1
T: Thou shalt not hide thyself
R: 'Thou shalt not see the brother… ox or his sheep go astray, and hide thyself from them: thou shalt in any case bring them again unto thy brother.' If you see your neighbour's ox or sheep wandering, do not ignore it; take it back to him. Notice thou shalt not see… and hide thyself. Looking away is not neutral. Notice go astray. A lost animal meant real loss for a family. Notice thou shalt in any case bring them again. Active help was required. Notice unto thy brother. Your neighbour is your brother. Notice that God's law forbids indifference. It is not enough to avoid harming others; we must help. Notice that Exodus 23 extends this even to an enemy's animal. Notice that the parable of the Good Samaritan condemns those who passed by on the other side. Luke 10 says the priest 'passed by on the other side.' Today, do not look away from your neighbour's need. When you see something you can fix or return, do it. Care is active.
S: Moses commanded Israel not to see a brother's ox or sheep going astray and hide themselves, but to bring it back to him. God's law forbids indifference, requiring active help rather than looking away. Do not look away from your neighbour's need, but when you can return or fix something for them, do it.
P: Don't look away. Deuteronomy 22:1: 'Thou shalt not see the brother's ox or his sheep go astray, and hide thyself from them.' Indifference isn't neutral. Care is active.

== 22:2
T: Until thy brother seek after it
R: 'And if thy brother be not nigh unto thee, or if thou know him not, then thou shalt bring it unto thine own house, and it shall be with thee until thy brother seek after it, and thou shalt restore it to him again.' If the owner is far or unknown, keep it safe for him. Notice be not nigh… or… know him not. Distance or anonymity did not cancel the duty. Notice bring it unto thine own house. Care for it yourself. Notice until thy brother seek after it. Patient safekeeping. Notice restore it to him again. Return it fully. Notice that this required time, space and expense. Kindness cost something. Notice that God wanted His people to be trustworthy keepers of others' property. Luke 16 says, 'He that is faithful in that which is least is faithful also in much.' Today, be trustworthy with what belongs to others. Keep it safe and return it faithfully, even when it costs you effort.
S: Moses told Israel that if a lost animal's owner lived far away or was unknown, they should keep it at home until he came seeking it, then restore it. Kindness cost time, space and expense, and God wanted His people to be trustworthy keepers of others' property. Be trustworthy with what belongs to others, keeping it safe and returning it faithfully even when it costs you.
P: Trustworthy keeper. Deuteronomy 22:2: 'it shall be with thee until thy brother seek after it, and thou shalt restore it to him again.' Faithfulness in small things. Return what isn't yours.

== 22:3
T: All lost things of thy brother's
R: 'In like manner shalt thou do with his ass; and so shalt thou do with his raiment; and with all lost things of thy brother… which he hath lost, and thou hast found, shalt thou do likewise: thou mayest not hide thyself.' Do the same with his donkey, his cloak or anything he loses; do not ignore it. Notice his ass… his raiment… all lost things. Every kind of lost property. Notice which he hath lost, and thou hast found. Finders were not keepers. Notice thou mayest not hide thyself. Again, no looking away. Notice that this law covered everything, large or small. Notice that honesty is tested most when no one would know. Finding something gives us a private choice. Notice that God's people return what is not theirs. Ephesians 4 says, 'Let him that stole steal no more.' Today, return what you find. Whether a wallet, a tool or an overpayment, give back what belongs to someone else. Honesty in secret honours God.
S: Moses extended the law to donkeys, garments and all lost things of a brother, forbidding anyone to hide themselves from the duty to return them. Finders were not keepers, and honesty is tested most when no one would know. Return what you find, whether large or small, for honesty in secret honours God.
P: Finders aren't keepers. Deuteronomy 22:3: 'with all lost things of thy brother's… thou mayest not hide thyself.' Honesty in secret honours God. Return what you find.

== 22:4
T: Help him to lift them up
R: 'Thou shalt not see thy brother… ass or his ox fall down by the way, and hide thyself from them: thou shalt surely help him to lift them up again.' If you see your neighbour's donkey or ox fallen on the road, do not ignore it; help him get it to its feet. Notice fall down by the way. A loaded animal collapsed. Notice hide thyself from them. The temptation to pass by. Notice thou shalt surely help him. Physical help was required. Notice to lift them up again. Working alongside the owner. Notice that this is a picture of bearing one another's burdens. Notice that the help was done together: help him to lift. Not taking over, but partnering. Galatians 6 says, 'Bear ye one another's burdens, and so fulfil the law of Christ.' Today, look for someone whose load has fallen. Do not pass by. Come alongside and help lift. Shared effort turns burdens into community.
S: Moses commanded Israel not to ignore a brother's donkey or ox fallen by the way but to help him lift it up again. The help was done together, a picture of bearing one another's burdens. Look for someone whose load has fallen, and come alongside to help lift rather than passing by.
P: Help lift. Deuteronomy 22:4: 'thou shalt surely help him to lift them up again.' Don't pass by fallen burdens. Come alongside and lift together.

== 22:5
T: That which pertaineth unto a man
R: 'The woman shall not wear that which pertaineth unto a man, neither shall a man put on a woman… garment: for all that do so are abomination unto the LORD thy God.' A woman must not wear men's clothing, nor a man wear women's clothing, for God detests this. Notice that which pertaineth unto a man. Clothing and items associated with men. Notice a woman's garment. Clothing associated with women. Notice that God created male and female with distinct and complementary identities. Notice that some see a link to pagan rituals that blurred these distinctions. Notice that the principle honours the goodness of how God made us. Notice that outward expression reflects inward identity. Notice that God's design is a gift, not a cage. Genesis 1 says, 'So God created man in his own image… male and female created he them.' Today, thank God for how He made you. Receive your identity as His gift. And treat every person, whatever their struggles, with the respect due to one made in His image.
S: Moses forbade a woman to wear what pertains to a man or a man to put on a woman's garment. God created male and female with distinct, complementary identities, and His design is a gift rather than a cage. Thank God for how He made you, receive your identity as His gift and treat every person with the respect due to His image.
P: Made male and female. Deuteronomy 22:5: 'The woman shall not wear that which pertaineth unto a man.' God's design is a gift. Thank Him for how He made you, and respect every image-bearer.

Do not take a mother bird with her young. Notice chance to be before thee. A small, everyday encounter. Notice the dam sitting upon the young. A mother caring for her brood. Notice thou shalt not take the dam with the young. Spare the mother. Notice that God's care extends to the smallest creatures. Notice that sparing the mother allowed her to raise future generations. Wise use, not destruction. Notice that God teaches compassion in small things. Matthew 23 records Jesus saying, 'how often would I have gathered thy children together, even as a hen gathereth her chickens under her wings.' Today, practise compassion in small encounters. How you treat the vulnerable, even creatures, reflects your heart.
S: Moses forbade taking a mother bird along with her young or eggs when finding a nest by the way. God's care extends to the smallest creatures, and sparing the mother allowed future generations to flourish. Practise compassion in small encounters, for how you treat the vulnerable reflects your heart.
P: Spare the mother. Deuteronomy 22:6: 'the dam sitting upon the young… thou shalt not take the dam with the young.' God's care reaches the smallest nest. Practise compassion in small things.

== 22:7

R: 'But thou shalt in any wise let the dam go, and take the young to thee; that it may be well with thee, and that thou mayest prolong thy days.' Let the mother go and take only the young, so that it may go well with you and you may live long. Notice let the dam go. Freedom for the mother. Notice take the young. Provision was still allowed. Notice that it may be well with thee. A promise of blessing. Notice prolong thy days. The same promise as for honouring parents. Notice that this small kindness carries the same reward as the fifth commandment. God connects respect for motherhood across creation. Notice that God sees small acts of compassion and blesses them. Notice that sustainability honours God. Proverbs 12 says, 'A righteous man regardeth the life of his beast.' Today, do not think small acts of kindness are unimportant. God notices them and attaches blessing to them. Care for creation with wisdom.
S: Moses told Israel to let the mother bird go and take only the young, so that it would be well with them and they would prolong their days. This small kindness carried the same promise as honouring parents, showing God sees and blesses compassion. Do not think small acts of kindness are unimportant, for God notices them and attaches blessing to them.
P: Small kindness, big promise. Deuteronomy 22:7: 'let the dam go… that it may be well with thee, and that thou mayest prolong thy days.' God notices small compassion.

== 22:8
T: A battlement for thy roof
R: 'When thou buildest a new house, then thou shalt make a battlement for thy roof, that thou bring not blood upon thine house, if any man fall from thence.' When you build a new house, put a parapet around your roof so that no one falls and you are not guilty of bloodshed. Notice when thou buildest a new house. Practical life. Notice a battlement for thy roof. Flat roofs were used as living space. A low wall prevented falls. Notice that thou bring not blood upon thine house. Negligence could make you guilty. Notice if any man fall from thence. Others' safety was your responsibility. Notice that God cares about safety and prevention. Love takes precautions. Notice that responsibility includes thinking ahead about how others might be harmed. Romans 13 says, 'Love worketh no ill to his neighbour.' Today, consider how your home, work and habits affect others' safety. Build battlements where people could fall. Prevention is a form of love.
S: Moses commanded that when building a new house, Israel must put a battlement around the roof so no one would fall and bring blood guilt on the home. God cares about safety and prevention, and love thinks ahead about how others might be harmed. Consider how your home, work and habits affect others' safety, and build battlements where people could fall.
P: Build the railing. Deuteronomy 22:8: 'thou shalt make a battlement for thy roof, that thou bring not blood upon thine house.' Prevention is love. Think ahead for others' safety.

== 22:9
T: Not divers seeds
R: 'Thou shalt not sow thy vineyard with divers seeds: lest the fruit of thy seed which thou hast sown, and the fruit of thy vineyard, be defiled.' Do not plant two kinds of seed in your vineyard, or the whole crop will be forfeited. Notice not sow… with divers seeds. Mixing was forbidden. Notice the fruit… be defiled. Literally, become holy, forfeited to the sanctuary. Notice that this law, like the next two, taught Israel about distinction. God's people were to be set apart, not mixed. Notice that these laws were object lessons. Everyday farming reminded Israel of holiness. Notice that the New Testament applies the principle to spiritual mixture. Matthew 6 says, 'No man can serve two masters.' Today, consider where you mix loyalties that do not belong together. God calls for undivided devotion. Let the ordinary parts of your life remind you that you belong wholly to Him.
S: Moses forbade sowing a vineyard with mixed seeds, lest the whole crop be defiled. Like the laws that follow, this taught Israel about distinction and holiness through everyday farming. Consider where you mix loyalties that do not belong together, and let ordinary life remind you that you belong wholly to God.
P: Not mixed. Deuteronomy 22:9: 'Thou shalt not sow thy vineyard with divers seeds.' Everyday lessons in holiness. No man can serve two masters.

== 22:10
T: An ox and an ass together
R: 'Thou shalt not plow with an ox and an ass together.' Do not plough with an ox and a donkey yoked together. Notice an ox and an ass. Different strengths, sizes and gaits. Notice together. Yoked as one team. Notice that the pairing would be unfair and painful. The weaker animal would strain; the stronger would be held back. Notice that God cared about animals' welfare. Notice that Paul applied the principle of yoking to relationships. Second Corinthians 6 says, 'Be ye not unequally yoked together with unbelievers.' Notice that close partnerships between those heading in different directions cause strain. Notice that yokes shape direction. You go where your partner goes. Today, consider your closest partnerships in marriage, business and friendship. Are you yoked with those who share your direction toward God? Choose partners wisely, for yokes shape your path.
S: Moses forbade ploughing with an ox and an ass yoked together. The mismatched pairing would be painful for both animals, and Paul applied the principle to close partnerships with those heading in different directions. Consider your closest partnerships and choose wisely, for yokes shape your path toward or away from God.
P: Equally yoked. Deuteronomy 22:10: 'Thou shalt not plow with an ox and an ass together.' Mismatched yokes strain both. Choose partners who share your direction.

Tassels on the corners of garments. Notice upon the four quarters. All four corners. Notice that Numbers 15 explains their purpose: to look at them and remember the commandments. Notice that Jesus wore such a garment. The woman with the issue of blood touched its fringe and was healed. Notice that God takes slander extremely seriously, especially against the vulnerable. Proverbs 22 says, 'A good name is rather to be chosen than great riches.' Notice that God provided a way for the falsely accused to be vindicated. Evidence mattered more than accusation. Notice the power of family standing with the vulnerable. Psalm 82 says, 'Defend the poor and fatherless: do justice to the afflicted and needy.' Today, stand with those who are falsely accused. Do not leave them to face slander alone. S: Her father and mother were to bring evidence of her innocence to the elders at the city gate. Stand with those who are falsely accused, bringing truth and support rather than leaving them alone.
