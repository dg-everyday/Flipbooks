# Deuteronomy 23: The assembly of the LORD, a holy camp, refuge for the runaway and keeping your word

== 23:1
T: Shall not enter into the congregation
R: 'He that is wounded in the stones, or hath his privy member cut off, shall not enter into the congregation of the LORD.' No one who has been emasculated may enter the assembly of the LORD. Notice shall not enter into the congregation. A restriction on full participation in Israel's assembly. Notice that this likely targeted pagan practices of self-mutilation in worship. Notice that the law emphasised wholeness as a picture of holiness. Notice that God later gave a beautiful promise to such people. Isaiah 56 says to faithful eunuchs, 'Even unto them will I give in mine house and within my walls a place and a name better than of sons and of daughters.' Notice that in Acts 8, an Ethiopian eunuch was baptised and welcomed into God's people. Notice that the gospel opens doors the law kept closed. Today, if you feel excluded or incomplete, hear God's promise. In Christ, He gives you a place and a name better than any you could have had.
S: Moses excluded men who had been emasculated from the congregation of the LORD, perhaps targeting pagan self-mutilation and picturing holiness as wholeness. Yet Isaiah promised faithful eunuchs a place and a name better than sons and daughters, and an Ethiopian eunuch was welcomed in Acts. If you feel excluded or incomplete, hear God's promise that in Christ He gives you a place and a name.
P: A place and a name. Deuteronomy 23:1: 'shall not enter into the congregation of the LORD.' Yet God promised the faithful excluded 'a place and a name better than of sons and of daughters.' In Christ, you belong.

== 23:2
T: Even to his tenth generation
R: 'A bastard shall not enter into the congregation of the LORD; even to his tenth generation shall he not enter into the congregation of the LORD.' No one born of a forbidden union may enter the assembly, even to the tenth generation. Notice a bastard. Likely someone born of an incestuous or forbidden union. Notice to his tenth generation. A very long exclusion. Notice that this law guarded the purity of Israel's worshipping assembly. Notice that it sounds harsh, and the person born had done nothing wrong. Notice that the gospel answers this. Jesus' own genealogy includes Tamar, Rahab, Ruth and Bathsheba, women whose stories involved irregular unions or foreign birth. Notice that no one's birth disqualifies them from God's family in Christ. John 1 says believers are born 'not of blood, nor of the will of the flesh… but of God.' Today, do not let the circumstances of your birth or family history define your worth. In Christ, you are born of God.
S: Moses excluded anyone born of a forbidden union from the congregation even to the tenth generation, guarding the purity of Israel's assembly. Yet Jesus' own genealogy includes women with irregular stories, and in Christ believers are born of God, not of blood. Do not let the circumstances of your birth or family history define your worth, for in Christ you are born of God.
