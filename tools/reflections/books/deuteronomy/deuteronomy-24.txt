# Deuteronomy 24: Protecting wives, the poor, the worker, the widow and the stranger

== 24:1
T: A bill of divorcement
R: 'When a man hath taken a wife, and married her, and it come to pass that she find no favour in his eyes, because he hath found some uncleanness in her: then let him write her a bill of divorcement…' If a man divorces his wife with a written certificate. A written document protected the woman, proving she was free to remarry. Notice that this law regulated divorce; it did not command or approve it. Notice that Jesus explained this. Matthew 19 records Him saying, 'Moses because of the hardness of your hearts suffered you to put away your wives: but from the beginning it was not so.' Today, honour God's original design for marriage as lifelong. Where marriages have broken, receive His grace. And treat those affected by divorce with compassion, not condemnation.
S: Moses regulated the case of a man who wrote his wife a bill of divorcement and sent her away. The written document protected the woman's freedom to remarry, but Jesus explained that Moses permitted divorce because of hard hearts, while from the beginning it was not so. Honour God's design of lifelong marriage, receive His grace where marriages have broken and treat those affected with compassion.
P: From the beginning it was not so. Deuteronomy 24:1: 'let him write her a bill of divorcement.' Moses regulated what hard hearts caused. Honour God's design and show compassion.

Let that memory keep you humble and generous toward everyone still in need.
S: Moses closed the chapter by reminding Israel that they had been bondmen in Egypt, which was why God commanded them to leave gleanings for the poor. Their past of hunger and powerlessness was meant to keep them generous, for forgetting makes the heart hard. Remember what you were before God's grace found you, and let that keep you humble and generous.
P: Remember, and be generous. Deuteronomy 24:22: 'remember that thou wast a bondman in the land of Egypt: therefore I command thee to do this thing.' Memory keeps hearts soft.
