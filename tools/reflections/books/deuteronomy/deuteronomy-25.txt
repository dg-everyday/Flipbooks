# Deuteronomy 25: Limits on punishment, the unmuzzled ox, a brother's name, honest weights and Amalek

== 25:1
T: Justify the righteous, condemn the wicked
R: 'If there be a controversy between men, and they come unto judgment, that the judges may judge them; then they shall justify the righteous, and condemn the wicked.' When people have a dispute and go to court, the judges shall acquit the innocent and condemn the guilty. Notice a controversy between men. Disputes are part of life. Notice come unto judgment. Courts existed to resolve them. Notice justify the righteous. Declare the innocent innocent. Notice condemn the wicked. Declare the guilty guilty. Notice that this sounds simple, but corruption often reverses it. Proverbs 17 warns, 'He that justifieth the wicked, and he that condemneth the just, even they both are abomination to the LORD.' Notice that God's justification of sinners in Christ does not overlook sin; Christ bore it. Romans 3 says God is 'just, and the justifier of him which believeth in Jesus.' Today, be fair in your judgments. Call right right and wrong wrong. And marvel that God justifies the ungodly through Christ without compromising justice.
S: Moses commanded judges to justify the righteous and condemn the wicked when disputes came to court. Corruption often reverses this, but God's justification of sinners in Christ upholds justice because Christ bore their sin. Be fair in your judgments, calling right right and wrong wrong, and marvel that God justifies believers without compromising justice.
P: Call right right. Deuteronomy 25:1: 'they shall justify the righteous, and condemn the wicked.' God is just and the justifier of those who believe in Jesus.

== 25:2
T: According to his fault
R: 'And it shall be, if the wicked man be worthy to be beaten, that the judge shall cause him to lie down, and to be beaten before his face, according to his fault, by a certain number.' If the guilty deserves flogging, the judge shall have him lie down and be beaten in his presence with the number of lashes his crime deserves. Notice worthy to be beaten. Punishment followed proven guilt. Notice before his face. The judge supervised, preventing abuse. Notice according to his fault. Proportional to the offence. Notice by a certain number. A fixed, limited count. Notice that God's law controlled punishment carefully. It could not be arbitrary or excessive. Notice that the judge's presence ensured accountability. Notice that justice requires limits even for the guilty. Jeremiah 10 prays, 'O LORD, correct me, but with judgment; not in thine anger.' Today, when you correct others, be proportional. Let discipline fit the fault, and never let anger push it beyond what is right.
S: Moses commanded that a guilty man deserving a beating be punished in the judge's presence, according to his fault, with a set number of strokes. God's law made punishment proportional, supervised and limited rather than arbitrary. When you correct others, keep discipline proportional to the fault and never let anger push it beyond what is right.
P: Proportional correction. Deuteronomy 25:2: 'according to his fault, by a certain number.' Justice has limits, even for the guilty. Correct with judgment, not anger.

== 25:3
T: Lest thy brother seem vile
R: 'Forty stripes he may give him, and not exceed: lest, if he should exceed, and beat him above these with many stripes, then thy brother should seem vile unto thee.' He may not receive more than forty lashes, or your fellow Israelite will be degraded in your eyes. Notice forty stripes… and not exceed. A strict upper limit. Notice lest… thy brother should seem vile. Excessive punishment dehumanises. Notice thy brother. Even the guilty was still a brother. Notice that God protected the dignity of offenders. Punishment must not destroy humanity. Notice that later Jews gave thirty-nine to avoid accidentally exceeding forty. Paul received this. Second Corinthians 11 says, 'Of the Jews five times received I forty stripes save one.' Today, remember that even those who do wrong remain people made in God's image. Correct without degrading. Never let punishment strip someone of their dignity.
S: Moses limited punishment to forty stripes so that the offender, still a brother, would not be made vile in the community's eyes. God protected the dignity of offenders, and later Jews gave thirty-nine, as Paul received five times. Correct without degrading, remembering that even wrongdoers are made in God's image.
P: Still a brother. Deuteronomy 25:3: 'Forty stripes he may give him, and not exceed: lest… thy brother should seem vile unto thee.' Correct without degrading. Dignity remains.

First Timothy 5 says, 'Thou shalt not muzzle the ox that treadeth out the corn. And, The labourer is worthy of his reward.' Notice that workers deserve to benefit from their labour. Today, make sure those who work for you or serve you share fairly in the fruit of their work. Do not muzzle the ox.
S: Moses forbade muzzling an ox while it trod out the grain, letting it eat as it worked. God cared for working animals, and Paul applied this to those who labour in ministry, for the labourer is worthy of his reward. Make sure those who work for or serve you share fairly in the fruit of their labour.

== 25:5
T: The duty of an husband's brother
R: 'If brethren dwell together, and one of them die, and have no child, the wife of the dead shall not marry without unto a stranger: her husband… brother shall go in unto her, and take her to him to wife…' If a man dies childless, his brother shall marry the widow. Notice one of them die, and have no child. A family line threatened. Notice the wife of the dead. A vulnerable widow. Notice her husband's brother shall… take her. The brother-in-law provided for her. Notice that this protected the widow from poverty and gave her a future. Notice that it also preserved the dead brother's name and inheritance. Notice that Boaz acted as a kinsman-redeemer for Ruth in a similar way. Ruth 4 records Boaz saying, 'to raise up the name of the dead upon his inheritance.' Today, care for the vulnerable in your own family. Family responsibility is a sacred duty.
S: Moses commanded that if a man died childless, his brother should marry the widow and fulfil the duty of a husband's brother. This protected the widow from poverty and preserved the dead man's name, as Boaz later did for Ruth. Care for the vulnerable in your own family, for family responsibility is a sacred duty.
P: Caring for the widow. Deuteronomy 25:5: 'her husband's brother shall go in unto her, and take her to him to wife.' Protection for the vulnerable. Boaz did this for Ruth. Care for your family.

== 25:12
T: Thine eye shall not pity her
R: 'Then thou shalt cut off her hand, thine eye shall not pity her.' You shall cut off her hand; show her no pity. Notice cut off her hand. One of the only bodily penalties in Israel's law. Notice that it matched the offending act. Notice thine eye shall not pity her. Sympathy for her motive was not to override justice. Notice that the severity shows how seriously God protected human dignity and the ability to bear children. Notice that some Jewish interpreters understood this as a heavy fine equal to the value of a hand. Notice that this law, like others in Israel's covenant, is not a command for Christians to carry out today. Notice the principle behind it: God fiercely protects human dignity and the gift of future generations. Psalm 139 says, 'I will praise thee; for I am fearfully and wonderfully made.' Today, honour the dignity of every human body as God's handiwork. Never degrade or harm others, even in conflict.
S: The covenant law ordered the wife's hand to be cut off without pity, a rare bodily penalty matching the offence, though some Jewish interpreters understood it as a fine. Its severity shows how fiercely God protected human dignity and the gift of future generations, and it is not a command for Christians today. Honour the dignity of every human body as God's handiwork, and never degrade or harm others, even in conflict.
P: Fearfully and wonderfully made. Deuteronomy 25:12: 'thou shalt cut off her hand, thine eye shall not pity her.' A severe ancient law protecting dignity. Honour every body as God's handiwork.
