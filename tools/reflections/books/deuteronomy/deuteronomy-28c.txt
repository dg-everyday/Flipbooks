# Deuteronomy 28:46-68: Joyless service, the siege, the scattering and the trembling heart

== 28:46
T: A sign and a wonder
R: 'And they shall be upon thee for a sign and for a wonder, and upon thy seed for ever.' These curses will be a sign and a wonder to you and your descendants forever. Notice for a sign. A visible warning. Notice and for a wonder. Something that astonishes. Notice upon thy seed. Affecting descendants. Notice that the curses would become a lesson for the world. Notice that Israel's history became a sign of God's faithfulness to His word, both in judgment and later in restoration. Notice that God's dealings with His people teach the nations. Notice that even judgment points to God's reality. First Corinthians 10 says, 'Now all these things happened unto them for ensamples: and they are written for our admonition.' Today, let Israel's story teach you. Learn from their warnings and their restoration. God's word proves true in both judgment and mercy.
S: Moses warned that the curses would be a sign and a wonder upon Israel and their descendants. Israel's history became a lesson to the world of God's faithfulness to His word in both judgment and later restoration. Let Israel's story teach you, learning from their warnings and their restoration, for God's word proves true.
P: A sign for us. Deuteronomy 28:46: 'they shall be upon thee for a sign and for a wonder.' These things were written for our admonition. Learn from their story.

== 28:47
T: Not with joyfulness
R: 'Because thou servedst not the LORD thy God with joyfulness, and with gladness of heart, for the abundance of all things.' Because you did not serve the LORD joyfully and gladly in the time of prosperity. Notice because thou servedst not. The reason for the curse. Notice with joyfulness, and with gladness of heart. God wanted joyful service, not grudging duty. Notice for the abundance of all things. In times of plenty. Notice that the sin was not only disobedience but joyless, ungrateful service. Notice that God cares about the attitude of our hearts. Notice that abundance should produce gratitude and joy, not complacency. Psalm 100 says, 'Serve the LORD with gladness: come before his presence with singing.' Today, examine the joy in your walk with God. Do you serve Him gladly, grateful for His abundance? Ask Him to restore the joy of your salvation.
S: Moses explained that the curses would come because Israel did not serve the LORD with joyfulness and gladness of heart in the abundance of all things. God cared about the attitude of the heart, wanting joyful, grateful service rather than grudging duty. Examine the joy in your walk with God and ask Him to restore the gladness of your salvation.
P: Serve with gladness. Deuteronomy 28:47: 'thou servedst not the LORD thy God with joyfulness, and with gladness of heart.' God wants joyful service, not grudging duty.

== 28:48
T: A yoke of iron
R: 'Therefore shalt thou serve thine enemies which the LORD shall send against thee, in hunger, and in thirst, and in nakedness, and in want of all things: and he shall put a yoke of iron upon thy neck…' So in hunger, thirst, nakedness and poverty you will serve your enemies, and they will put an iron yoke on your neck. Notice therefore shalt thou serve thine enemies. Those who would not serve God joyfully would serve enemies miserably. Notice in hunger… thirst… nakedness… want. Every need unmet. Notice a yoke of iron. Heavy, unbreakable bondage. Notice the exchange. Refusing God's easy yoke leads to the world's iron yoke. Notice that everyone serves someone. The question is whom. Notice that Jesus offers a different yoke. Matthew 11 records Him saying, 'Take my yoke upon you… For my yoke is easy, and my burden is light.' Today, choose whom you will serve. God's service brings freedom and joy; other masters bring iron yokes. Take Christ's yoke.
S: Moses warned that because Israel would not serve God joyfully, they would serve their enemies in hunger, thirst, nakedness and want under a yoke of iron. Refusing God's easy yoke leads to the world's heavy one, for everyone serves someone. Choose to take Christ's easy yoke, for His service brings freedom and joy while other masters bring iron.
P: Iron yoke or easy yoke. Deuteronomy 28:48: 'he shall put a yoke of iron upon thy neck.' Everyone serves someone. Christ's yoke is easy and His burden light.

== 28:49

From distant lands. Notice as swift as the eagle. Sudden and fierce. Notice whose tongue thou shalt not understand. Strangers with no shared language. Notice that this describes Babylon and later Rome. Notice that the eagle, which once pictured God carrying Israel, now pictures an enemy swooping down. Notice that turning from God can turn blessings into threats. Habakkuk 1 describes the Chaldeans: 'they shall fly as the eagle that hasteth to eat.' Today, remember that God's protection is precious. Stay under His wings, where you are carried rather than pursued.
S: Moses warned that the LORD would bring a distant nation against disobedient Israel, swift as an eagle, speaking a language they could not understand. The eagle that once pictured God carrying Israel now pictured an enemy swooping down, fulfilled in Babylon and Rome. Stay under God's wings, where you are carried rather than pursued.
P: The eagle reversed. Deuteronomy 28:49: 'a nation… as swift as the eagle flieth; a nation whose tongue thou shalt not understand.' Stay under His wings, carried not pursued.

== 28:52
T: Walls wherein thou trustedst
R: 'And he shall besiege thee in all thy gates, until thy high and fenced walls come down, wherein thou trustedst, throughout all thy land…' They will lay siege to all your cities until your high fortified walls, in which you trust, fall down. Notice besiege thee in all thy gates. Every city surrounded. Notice thy high and fenced walls come down. Fortifications fail. Notice wherein thou trustedst. The real problem: misplaced trust. Notice that Israel would trust walls instead of God. Notice the irony. Their ancestors feared Canaan's walls; now they would trust their own, and those walls would fall. Notice that human security fails when God is not our refuge. Psalm 20 says, 'Some trust in chariots, and some in horses: but we will remember the name of the LORD our God.' Today, examine where you put your trust. Savings, health, relationships and plans are good gifts, but they are poor gods. Let God be your fortress.
S: Moses warned that enemies would besiege all Israel's cities until the high fenced walls they trusted came down. The problem was misplaced trust, for human security fails when God is not our refuge. Examine where you put your trust, for good gifts make poor gods, and let God be your fortress.
P: Walls that fail. Deuteronomy 28:52: 'until thy high and fenced walls come down, wherein thou trustedst.' Good gifts are poor gods. Let God be your fortress.

Let this terrible verse show the darkness from which God wants to save us, and turn from sin early.

== 28:67
T: Would God it were morning
R: 'In the morning thou shalt say, Would God it were even! and at even thou shalt say, Would God it were morning! for the fear of thine heart wherewith thou shalt fear…' Dread will fill every hour. Notice would God it were even. Wishing the day would end. Notice would God it were morning. Wishing the night would end. Notice that every hour brings dread. Neither day nor night gives relief. Notice that anxiety makes time feel unbearable. Notice that this describes deep distress, perhaps sleeplessness and despair. Notice that many people know this feeling. Notice that God offers a different experience: peace in every hour. Psalm 4 says, 'I will both lay me down in peace, and sleep: for thou, LORD, only makest me dwell in safety.' Today, if you dread both the day and the night, bring your fear to God. He offers peace for the morning and rest for the evening. You can lie down in peace.
S: Moses warned that exiled Israel would wish for evening in the morning and for morning in the evening because of the fear in their hearts. Anxiety makes every hour unbearable, but God offers peace for each part of the day. If you dread both day and night, bring your fear to God, who lets you lie down in peace and dwell in safety.
P: Dreading every hour. Deuteronomy 28:67: 'In the morning thou shalt say, Would God it were even! and at even… Would God it were morning!' God gives peace for day and night.

== 28:68
T: Into Egypt again
R: 'And the LORD shall bring thee into Egypt again with ships… and there ye shall be sold unto your enemies for bondmen and bondwomen, and no man shall buy you.' Back to Egypt as unwanted slaves. Notice into Egypt again. The great reversal. Back to bondage. Notice with ships. Not even walking, but carried away. Notice thou shalt see it no more again. God had promised they would never return. Disobedience would undo it. Notice no man shall buy you. Not even valued as slaves. Notice that this is the final curse: the exodus undone. Notice that the whole story of redemption could be reversed by turning from God. Notice that Christ offers a better exodus that cannot be undone. John 8 says, 'If the Son therefore shall make you free, ye shall be free indeed.' Today, never go back to the bondage God brought you out of. In Christ, your freedom is real. Walk in it.
S: Moses closed the curses by warning that the LORD would bring disobedient Israel back into Egypt in ships, where they would offer themselves as slaves and no one would buy them. The final curse was the exodus undone, but Christ offers a better freedom that cannot be reversed. Never go back to the bondage God brought you out of, but walk in the freedom Christ gives.
P: Back to Egypt? Never. Deuteronomy 28:68: 'the LORD shall bring thee into Egypt again with ships.' The exodus undone. In Christ you are free indeed. Walk in it.
