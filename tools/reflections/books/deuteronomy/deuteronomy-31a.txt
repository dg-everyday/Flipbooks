# Deuteronomy 31:1-15: Be strong and of a good courage; the law read every seven years

== 31:1
T: Moses went and spake
R: 'And Moses went and spake these words unto all Israel.' Then Moses went out and spoke these words to all Israel. Notice Moses went. At a hundred and twenty, he still went to the people. Notice and spake these words. His final messages. Notice unto all Israel. Everyone was addressed. Notice that Moses' last days were spent serving and speaking. He did not retreat into private rest. Notice that faithful servants keep giving until the end. Notice that the words he spoke would be remembered for thousands of years. Notice that a leader's final words often carry special weight. Second Timothy 4 records Paul near the end: 'I have fought a good fight, I have finished my course, I have kept the faith.' Today, consider how you want to finish. Keep serving and speaking life to others, whatever your age or season. Your words can outlast you.
S: Moses, at a hundred and twenty, went and spoke these words to all Israel. He spent his last days serving and speaking rather than retreating, and his words would be remembered for thousands of years. Keep serving and speaking life to others whatever your age, for your words can outlast you.
P: Finishing well. Deuteronomy 31:1: 'And Moses went and spake these words unto all Israel.' At 120, still serving. Keep giving to the end. Your words can outlast you.

== 31:2
T: An hundred and twenty years old
R: 'And he said unto them, I am an hundred and twenty years old this day; I can no more go out and come in: also the LORD hath said unto me, Thou shalt not go over this Jordan.' I am 120 and will not cross the Jordan. Notice an hundred and twenty years old. Forty in Egypt, forty in Midian, forty in the wilderness. Notice I can no more go out and come in. Moses acknowledged his limits. Notice also the LORD hath said. He accepted God's decision. Notice that Moses was honest about his age and calling. He did not cling to leadership. Notice that knowing when to step aside is wisdom. Notice that his eyes were still strong, as chapter 34 says, yet he recognised his season was ending. Ecclesiastes 3 says, 'To every thing there is a season.' Today, accept the seasons of your life with grace. Know your limits, and trust God's timing for each new chapter.
S: Moses told Israel he was a hundred and twenty years old, could no longer go out and come in, and the LORD had said he would not cross the Jordan. He acknowledged his limits honestly and accepted God's decision without clinging to leadership. Accept the seasons of your life with grace, knowing your limits and trusting God's timing for each new chapter.
P: Knowing your season. Deuteronomy 31:2: 'I am an hundred and twenty years old this day; I can no more go out and come in.' Moses accepted his limits. To every thing there is a season.
H: #DailyGrace #Deuteronomy312 #Seasons #Humility #Acceptance #BibleVerse

== 31:3
T: He will go over before thee
R: 'The LORD thy God, he will go over before thee, and he will destroy these nations from before thee, and thou shalt possess them: and Joshua, he shall go over before thee, as the LORD hath said.' The LORD your God will cross ahead of you and destroy these nations; Joshua also will lead you across, as God said. Notice the LORD thy God, he will go over before thee. God Himself would lead. Notice he will destroy these nations. God would fight. Notice and Joshua, he shall go over. A new human leader. Notice that God came first, then Joshua. Leadership changes, but God remains. Notice that Moses' departure did not leave Israel leaderless. Notice that God provides continuity through transitions. Notice that the people's confidence should rest on God, not only on Moses. Hebrews 13 says, 'Jesus Christ the same yesterday, and to day, and for ever.' Today, when leaders change in your life, remember that God stays the same. He goes before you through every transition.
S: Moses assured Israel that the LORD would go over before them and destroy the nations, and Joshua would lead them across as God said. God came first and Joshua second, for leadership changes but God remains. When leaders change in your life, remember that God stays the same and goes before you through every transition.
P: Leaders change; God goes first. Deuteronomy 31:3: 'The LORD thy God, he will go over before thee… and Joshua, he shall go over before thee.' He's the same through every transition.

== 31:4
T: As he did to Sihon and Og
R: 'And the LORD shall do unto them as he did to Sihon and to Og, kings of the Amorites, and unto the land of them, whom he destroyed.' God will do to them what He did to Sihon and Og, the Amorite kings, whom He destroyed with their land. Notice as he did. A pattern of faithfulness. Notice Sihon… Og. Recent victories everyone remembered. Notice whom he destroyed. God had done it before. Notice that Moses pointed to fresh evidence to build faith for what was ahead. Notice that past victories are promises for the future. Notice that God's character does not change. What He has done, He can do again. Notice that remembering builds courage for new battles. First Samuel 17 records David saying, 'The LORD that delivered me out of the paw of the lion… he will deliver me out of the hand of this Philistine.' Today, recall what God has already done for you. Let those memories become confidence for the next challenge.
S: Moses promised that the LORD would do to the nations ahead as He did to Sihon and Og, whom He destroyed. Past victories became promises for the future, for God's character does not change, as David later reasoned before Goliath. Recall what God has already done for you and let those memories become confidence for the next challenge.
P: As He did before. Deuteronomy 31:4: 'the LORD shall do unto them as he did to Sihon and to Og.' Past victories are promises. Let memory become confidence.

== 31:5
T: The LORD shall give them up
R: 'And the LORD shall give them up before your face, that ye may do unto them according unto all the commandments which I have commanded you.' The LORD will deliver them to you, and you must do to them all I have commanded. Notice the LORD shall give them up. Victory would be God's gift. Notice before your face. In their sight. Notice that ye may do… according unto all the commandments. Obedience in victory. Notice that God's help came with responsibility. Israel was to follow His instructions in the conquest. Notice that victory without obedience could become disaster. Notice that God gives and expects faithfulness in how we use what He gives. Notice that success is a test as much as failure. Luke 12 says, 'unto whomsoever much is given, of him shall be much required.' Today, when God gives you success, use it according to His ways. Victory is a stewardship, not a licence.
S: Moses promised that the LORD would give the nations up before Israel, who must then act according to all His commandments. God's help came with responsibility, for victory without obedience could become disaster. When God gives you success, use it according to His ways, treating victory as a stewardship rather than a licence.
P: Victory with responsibility. Deuteronomy 31:5: 'the LORD shall give them up… that ye may do unto them according unto all the commandments.' Success is stewardship.

== 31:6
T: He will not fail thee, nor forsake thee
R: 'Be strong and of a good courage, fear not, nor be afraid of them: for the LORD thy God, he it is that doth go with thee; he will not fail thee, nor forsake thee.' Be strong and courageous. Do not be afraid or terrified, for the LORD your God goes with you; He will never leave you nor forsake you. Notice be strong and of a good courage. A double call to courage. Notice fear not, nor be afraid. Another double. Notice he it is that doth go with thee. God's presence. Notice he will not fail thee. He will not let you down. Notice nor forsake thee. He will not abandon you. Notice that this promise was for all Israel, not just leaders. Notice that Hebrews applies it to every believer. Hebrews 13 says, 'for he hath said, I will never leave thee, nor forsake thee.' Today, hold this promise close. Whatever you face, God goes with you. He will not fail you and He will never leave you.
S: Moses told all Israel to be strong and of good courage and not to fear, for the LORD went with them and would not fail or forsake them. This promise was for the whole people, and Hebrews later applies it to every believer. Hold this promise close, for whatever you face God goes with you and will never leave you.
P: Never leave, never forsake. Deuteronomy 31:6: 'Be strong and of a good courage… he will not fail thee, nor forsake thee.' Hebrews 13 makes it yours. He won't leave you.

== 31:7
T: In the sight of all Israel
R: 'And Moses called unto Joshua, and said unto him in the sight of all Israel, Be strong and of a good courage: for thou must go with this people unto the land… and thou shalt cause them to inherit it.' Moses charged Joshua before everyone. Notice Moses called unto Joshua. A personal charge. Notice in the sight of all Israel. Public commissioning. Notice be strong and of a good courage. The same words given to the people, now to their new leader. Notice thou must go with this people. Joshua would share their journey. Notice thou shalt cause them to inherit it. His task was to lead others into God's gifts. Notice that public affirmation gave Joshua authority and the people confidence. Notice that good transitions are done openly. First Timothy 4 says, 'Let no man despise thy youth.' Today, publicly encourage those stepping into new roles. Your affirmation can strengthen them and unite others behind them.
S: Moses called Joshua and, in the sight of all Israel, told him to be strong and of good courage, for he would lead the people into the land and cause them to inherit it. The public commissioning gave Joshua authority and the people confidence. Publicly encourage those stepping into new roles, for your affirmation strengthens them and unites others behind them.
P: Public encouragement. Deuteronomy 31:7: 'Moses called unto Joshua, and said unto him in the sight of all Israel, Be strong and of a good courage.' Affirm new leaders openly.

== 31:8
T: He will be with thee
R: 'And the LORD, he it is that doth go before thee; he will be with thee, he will not fail thee, neither forsake thee: fear not, neither be dismayed.' God goes before you and stays with you. Notice he it is that doth go before thee. God leads ahead. Notice he will be with thee. God walks alongside. Notice he will not fail thee, neither forsake thee. The same promise, now personal to Joshua. Notice fear not, neither be dismayed. The answer to a leader's fears. Notice that God goes both before and with. Ahead to prepare, beside to sustain. Notice that leaders need this promise as much as anyone. Notice that Joshua would hear these words again from God Himself. Joshua 1 says, 'as I was with Moses, so I will be with thee: I will not fail thee, nor forsake thee.' Today, whatever responsibility you carry, God goes before you and walks with you. Do not be dismayed.
S: Moses assured Joshua that the LORD went before him and would be with him, never failing or forsaking him, so he need not fear or be dismayed. God goes ahead to prepare and alongside to sustain, a promise Joshua later heard from God Himself. Whatever responsibility you carry, God goes before you and walks with you, so do not be dismayed.
P: Before and beside. Deuteronomy 31:8: 'the LORD, he it is that doth go before thee; he will be with thee.' He prepares ahead and sustains beside. Fear not.

Thank God for His written word preserved for you, and treasure it, read it and pass it on.
P: Written and entrusted. Deuteronomy 31:9: 'Moses wrote this law, and delivered it unto the priests… and unto all the elders.' God's word was preserved for you. Treasure it.

== 31:10
T: At the end of every seven years
R: 'And Moses commanded them, saying, At the end of every seven years, in the solemnity of the year of release, in the feast of tabernacles.' Moses commanded them: At the end of every seven years, in the year for cancelling debts, during the Festival of Tabernacles. Notice at the end of every seven years. A regular rhythm. Notice the year of release. When debts were cancelled and slaves freed. Notice in the feast of tabernacles. A joyful harvest festival. Notice that the reading of the law was tied to freedom and celebration. Notice that God's word was to be heard in a context of grace and joy. Notice that hearing Scripture together at set times kept it alive. Notice that regular rhythms of reading God's word protect against forgetting. Nehemiah 8 records that Ezra read the law at this very feast, and 'there was very great gladness.' Today, build regular rhythms of hearing God's word, alone and with others. Let His word be part of your celebrations.
S: Moses commanded that every seven years, in the year of release during the feast of tabernacles, the law should be read. God's word was heard in a context of freedom and joy, and regular rhythms kept it alive, as Ezra later did with great gladness. Build regular rhythms of hearing God's word, alone and with others, and let it be part of your celebrations.
P: Regular rhythms. Deuteronomy 31:10: 'At the end of every seven years… in the feast of tabernacles.' God's word with freedom and joy. Build regular rhythms of reading it.

Notice that they may hear… learn… fear… observe. Four steps from hearing to doing. Notice that God's word was for everyone, not only priests or leaders. Notice that children were present to learn early. Notice that the goal was reverence and obedience, not just information. Help them hear, learn and live it.
S: Moses commanded that men, women, children and strangers be gathered to hear the law so they might learn, fear the LORD and observe all His words. God's word was for everyone, and the goal was reverence and obedience rather than mere information. Make sure everyone in your community, young and old, has access to God's word, and help them hear, learn and live it.
P: Everyone hears. Deuteronomy 31:12: 'Gather the people together, men, and women, and children, and thy stranger… that they may hear, and that they may learn.' God's word is for all.

Notice that the familiar sign of God's guidance assured the people that He would continue leading. Notice that God does not abandon His people at turning points. Notice that His presence is the greatest assurance in change. Exodus 13 says, 'He took not away the pillar of the cloud by day.' Today, look for signs of God's presence in times of transition. He does not leave you at life's turning points. He stays.
S: The LORD appeared in the tabernacle in a pillar of cloud that stood over the door. The familiar cloud that had led Israel forty years confirmed Joshua's commissioning and assured the people that God would keep leading. Look for God's presence in times of transition, for He does not leave you at life's turning points.
