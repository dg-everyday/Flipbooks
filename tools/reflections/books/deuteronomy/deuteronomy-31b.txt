# Deuteronomy 31:16-30: A song as witness, God's hidden face, and the book beside the ark

== 31:16
T: Thou shalt sleep with thy fathers
R: 'And the LORD said unto Moses, Behold, thou shalt sleep with thy fathers; and this people will rise up, and go a whoring after the gods of the strangers of the land… and will forsake me, and break my covenant…' After you die, they will turn to other gods. Notice thou shalt sleep with thy fathers. A gentle description of death. Notice this people will rise up. After Moses' death. Notice go a whoring. Spiritual unfaithfulness described as adultery. Notice forsake me, and break my covenant. Betrayal of relationship. Notice that God knew in advance what Israel would do. Notice that He still gave them the land and His word. Grace was given despite foreknown failure. Romans 5 says, 'God commendeth his love toward us, in that, while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us.' Today, marvel that God loves you knowing all your future failures. His grace is not naive. It is deliberate and steadfast.
S: The LORD told Moses that he would sleep with his fathers and the people would afterward turn to foreign gods, forsake Him and break His covenant. God knew Israel's future unfaithfulness in advance, yet still gave them the land and His word. Marvel that God loves you knowing all your future failures, for His grace is deliberate and steadfast.
P: Loved despite foreknown failure. Deuteronomy 31:16: 'this people will rise up… and will forsake me.' God knew, and gave grace anyway. While we were yet sinners, Christ died.

== 31:17

Notice because our God is not among us. Eventually the people would recognise the cause. Notice that God's hidden face is the deepest form of loss. Notice that suffering can lead people to ask the right question. Psalm 13 cries, 'How long wilt thou hide thy face from me?' Today, if God feels hidden, ask honestly what might stand between you. Seek His face again. He hides it to draw us back.
S: God warned that when Israel forsook Him, His anger would be kindled, He would hide His face and many troubles would come until they asked whether their God was not among them. God's hidden face is the deepest loss, yet suffering can lead people to ask the right question. If God feels hidden, ask honestly what might stand between you and seek His face again.
P: When God hides His face. Deuteronomy 31:17: 'I will hide my face from them… Are not these evils come upon us, because our God is not among us?' Seek His face again.

Isaiah 59 says, 'your sins have hid his face from you, that he will not hear.' Today, turn back toward God's face. Put away anything that has taken His place. His face is still willing to shine on those who return.
S: God repeated that He would surely hide His face because of all the evils Israel did in turning to other gods. His hiddenness was the consequence of their turning away first, losing the light of His shining face. Turn back toward God's face, putting away anything that has taken His place, for His face still shines on those who return.
P: Turn back to His face. Deuteronomy 31:18: 'I will surely hide my face in that day… in that they are turned unto other gods.' Sin hides His face. Return and He shines again.

S: God told Moses to write a song and teach it to Israel, putting it in their mouths as a witness for Him. Fill your mouth with songs of truth, for they will remind you of God when other voices pull you away.

== 31:20
T: Waxen fat
R: 'For when I shall have brought them into the land… that floweth with milk and honey; and they shall have eaten and filled themselves, and waxen fat; then will they turn unto other gods…' When I have brought them into the land and they eat their fill and thrive, they will turn to other gods, worship them, reject Me and break My covenant. Notice brought them into the land. God's faithfulness. Notice eaten and filled themselves. Abundance. Notice and waxen fat. Comfortable and complacent. Notice then will they turn. The danger of prosperity. Notice that God foresaw that blessing would lead to forgetting. Notice that satisfaction can breed spiritual laziness. Notice that this pattern is repeated through history. Hosea 13 says, 'they were filled, and their heart was exalted; therefore have they forgotten me.' Today, beware the danger of comfort. When life is full, guard your heart from forgetting the Giver. Gratitude keeps prosperity from becoming a trap.
S: God foresaw that when Israel was brought into the land, ate, filled themselves and grew fat, they would turn to other gods and break His covenant. Satisfaction can breed spiritual laziness, and blessing can lead to forgetting the Giver, as Hosea later lamented. Beware the danger of comfort, and let gratitude keep prosperity from becoming a trap.
P: The danger of fullness. Deuteronomy 31:20: 'they shall have eaten and filled themselves, and waxen fat; then will they turn unto other gods.' Gratitude guards against complacency.

== 31:22

Immediate obedience. Notice the same day. No delay. Notice and taught it. He did not just write it; he taught it. Notice that Moses obeyed promptly even in his last days. Notice that teaching requires effort: repeating, explaining and helping others remember. Notice that Moses invested his final energy in teaching truth. Notice that faithful servants act on God's instructions without procrastination. Psalm 119 says, 'I made haste, and delayed not to keep thy commandments.' Today, when God shows you something to do, do it the same day. Do not wait. And when you learn truth, teach it to others. Moses' example shows prompt obedience to the very end.
S: Moses wrote the song the same day and taught it to the children of Israel. He obeyed promptly even in his last days and invested his final energy in teaching truth. When God shows you something to do, do it the same day without procrastinating, and teach others what you learn.
P: The same day. Deuteronomy 31:22: 'Moses therefore wrote this song the same day, and taught it.' Prompt obedience to the very end. Don't delay what God shows you.

== 31:23
T: I will be with thee
R: 'And he gave Joshua the son of Nun a charge, and said, Be strong and of a good courage: for thou shalt bring the children of Israel into the land which I sware unto them: and I will be with thee.' God charged Joshua and promised His presence. Notice he gave Joshua… a charge. God commissioned Joshua personally. Notice be strong and of a good courage. The third time in this chapter. Notice thou shalt bring the children of Israel into the land. His mission. Notice and I will be with thee. The promise that makes courage possible. Notice that courage rests on presence. Joshua could be strong because God would be with him. Notice that God's commands always come with God's presence. Matthew 28 records Jesus commissioning His disciples: 'and, lo, I am with you alway, even unto the end of the world.' Today, whatever God calls you to, He goes with you. Let His presence be the source of your courage.
S: The LORD gave Joshua a charge to be strong and of good courage, for he would bring Israel into the land, and God would be with him. Courage rested on God's presence, for His commands always come with His company, as Jesus later promised His disciples. Whatever God calls you to, let His presence be the source of your courage, for He goes with you.
P: Courage from presence. Deuteronomy 31:23: 'Be strong and of a good courage… and I will be with thee.' His commands come with His presence. Lo, I am with you always.
