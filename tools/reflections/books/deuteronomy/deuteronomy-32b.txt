# Deuteronomy 32:27-52: Their rock is not as our Rock; I kill and I make alive; it is your life

== 32:27
T: Lest they should say, Our hand is high
R: 'Were it not that I feared the wrath of the enemy, lest their adversaries should behave themselves strangely, and lest they should say, Our hand is high, and the LORD hath not done all this.' God held back, lest enemies boast. Notice lest their adversaries… say. God considered how enemies would interpret Israel's destruction. Notice our hand is high. The enemy would boast. Notice the LORD hath not done all this. They would deny God's hand. Notice that God's reputation was at stake. Notice that God's mercy toward Israel was partly for His own name's sake. Notice that this is the same argument Moses used in his intercession. Notice that God's glory is tied to His people's preservation. Ezekiel 36 says, 'I do not this for your sakes… but for mine holy name's sake.' Today, rejoice that God's commitment to you is bound up with His own glory. He will not let His name be mocked by abandoning His people.
S: Moses sang that God held back complete judgment lest Israel's enemies boast that their own hand had triumphed and the LORD had not done it. God's mercy toward Israel was partly for His own name's sake, so His reputation was tied to His people's preservation. Rejoice that God's commitment to you is bound up with His own glory, so He will not abandon His people.

How could one man chase a thousand, or two put ten thousand to flight, unless their Rock had sold them, unless the LORD had given them up? Notice one chase a thousand. Impossible odds. Notice two put ten thousand to flight. Even more impossible. Notice except their Rock had sold them. Only God's withdrawal could explain such defeat. Notice the LORD had shut them up. God handed them over. Notice that Israel's defeats were not due to enemy strength but God's absence. Notice the reverse is also true: with God, one can chase a thousand. == 32:49
T: Behold the land of Canaan
R: 'Get thee up into this mountain Abarim, unto mount Nebo, which is in the land of Moab, that is over against Jericho; and behold the land of Canaan, which I give unto the children of Israel for a possession.' Climb Nebo and view the land. Notice get thee up. A final climb. Notice mount Nebo. A peak overlooking the promised land. Notice over against Jericho. Facing the first city Israel would conquer. Notice and behold the land of Canaan. Moses would see the promise. Notice which I give unto the children of Israel. The promise fulfilled for others. Notice that God gave Moses a gift at the end: a view of what his life's work led to. Notice that faithful servants may not enjoy every result, but God lets them see enough. Hebrews 11 says, 'These all died in faith, not having received the promises, but having seen them afar off.' Today, trust God with outcomes you may not see completed. Your faithfulness contributes to promises others will enter.
S: God told Moses to climb mount Nebo in Moab, over against Jericho, and behold the land of Canaan He was giving to Israel. God gave Moses a view of what his life's work led to, as the faithful die having seen the promises afar off. Trust God with outcomes you may not see completed, for your faithfulness contributes to promises others will enter.
P: The view from Nebo. Deuteronomy 32:49: 'Get thee up… unto mount Nebo… and behold the land of Canaan.' Seen afar off. Your faithfulness builds what others will enter.

== 32:50
T: Gathered unto thy people
R: 'And die in the mount whither thou goest up, and be gathered unto thy people; as Aaron thy brother died in mount Hor, and was gathered unto his people.' There on the mountain you will die and be gathered to your people, just as your brother Aaron died on Mount Hor and was gathered to his people. Notice die in the mount. Moses' death would be on a mountain, close to God. Notice be gathered unto thy people. A beautiful phrase. Death was not the end but a reunion. Notice as Aaron thy brother. Moses would follow his brother. Notice that God spoke of death with gentleness. Notice that being gathered to one's people hints at life beyond death. Notice that Moses and Aaron, who served together, would rest together. Hebrews 11 says they 'desire a better country, that is, an heavenly.' Today, if you think about death, remember this phrase: gathered unto thy people. For those who belong to God, death is a homecoming.
S: God told Moses he would die on the mountain and be gathered to his people, as Aaron died on mount Hor and was gathered to his. God spoke of death gently, hinting at reunion and life beyond, for the faithful desire a heavenly country. When you think about death, remember that for those who belong to God it is a homecoming.
P: Gathered home. Deuteronomy 32:50: 'die in the mount… and be gathered unto thy people.' For those who belong to God, death is a homecoming.

== 32:51
T: Ye sanctified me not
R: 'Because ye trespassed against me among the children of Israel at the waters of Meribah-Kadesh, in the wilderness of Zin; because ye sanctified me not in the midst of the children of Israel.' Because you did not honour Me at Meribah. Notice ye trespassed against me. Moses and Aaron sinned. Notice at the waters of Meribah-Kadesh. Where Moses struck the rock. Notice ye sanctified me not. They failed to honour God as holy before the people. Notice in the midst of the children of Israel. Leaders' sins are public. Notice that God gives the reason honestly. Even Moses faced consequences. Notice that leaders are held to a high standard because their actions represent God. Notice that one moment of anger had lasting consequences. James 3 says, 'we shall receive the greater condemnation.' Today, guard your heart in moments of frustration. Represent God faithfully, especially before others. And remember that God's grace still held Moses close to the end.
S: God explained that Moses would not enter the land because he and Aaron trespassed at Meribah-Kadesh and did not sanctify Him before Israel. Leaders are held to a high standard because their actions represent God, and one moment of anger had lasting consequences. Guard your heart in moments of frustration and represent God faithfully before others.
P: Representing God. Deuteronomy 32:51: 'because ye sanctified me not in the midst of the children of Israel.' One angry moment had lasting cost. Guard your heart.

== 32:52
T: Thou shalt see the land
R: 'Yet thou shalt see the land before thee; but thou shalt not go thither unto the land which I give the children of Israel.' Therefore you will see the land only from a distance; you will not enter the land I am giving to Israel. Notice yet. Grace within consequence. Notice thou shalt see the land. A gift. Notice before thee. Right in front of him. Notice but thou shalt not go thither. The consequence stood. Notice that God's discipline and God's kindness came together. Moses could not enter, but he would see. Notice that later Moses did stand in the land, on the mount of transfiguration with Jesus. Matthew 17 says, 'there appeared unto them Moses and Elias talking with him.' Notice that God's final word for Moses was not exclusion but glory. Today, trust that God's consequences are never His last word for His children. Grace has the final say.
S: God told Moses he would see the land before him but not go into it. Discipline and kindness came together, and later Moses did stand in the land with Jesus on the mount of transfiguration. Trust that God's consequences are never His last word for His children, for grace has the final say.
P: Seen, and later entered. Deuteronomy 32:52: 'thou shalt see the land before thee; but thou shalt not go thither.' Yet Moses stood there with Jesus at the transfiguration. Grace has the last word.
