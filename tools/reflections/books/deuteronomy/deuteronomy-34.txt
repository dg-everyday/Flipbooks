# Deuteronomy 34: Moses views the land, dies on Nebo, and Joshua takes up the work

== 34:1
T: The LORD shewed him all the land
R: 'And Moses went up from the plains of Moab unto the mountain of Nebo, to the top of Pisgah, that is over against Jericho. And the LORD shewed him all the land of Gilead, unto Dan.' Moses climbed Nebo, and God showed him the land. Notice Moses went up. At a hundred and twenty, he climbed. Notice the top of Pisgah. A high vantage point. Notice over against Jericho. Facing the land of promise. Notice the LORD shewed him. God Himself was Moses' guide. Notice all the land. The whole sweep of the promise. Notice that Moses' last climb was to meet God and see His promise. Notice that God gave Moses a personal tour at the end. Notice that Moses had met God on mountains before: Sinai, and now Nebo. Hebrews 11 says Moses 'endured, as seeing him who is invisible.' Today, climb toward God in prayer. Let Him show you the scope of His promises. He loves to give His servants a view of His purposes.
S: Moses went up from the plains of Moab to the top of Pisgah on mount Nebo, and the LORD showed him all the land from Gilead to Dan. At a hundred and twenty, his last climb was to meet God and see His promise, with God Himself as his guide. Climb toward God in prayer and let Him show you the scope of His promises.
P: The view from the top. Deuteronomy 34:1: 'Moses went up… unto the mountain of Nebo… And the LORD shewed him all the land.' God showed His servant the promise.

== 34:2
T: Unto the utmost sea
R: 'And all Naphtali, and the land of Ephraim, and Manasseh, and all the land of Judah, unto the utmost sea.' All of Naphtali, Ephraim and Manasseh, all the land of Judah as far as the Mediterranean Sea. Notice all Naphtali. The north. Notice the land of Ephraim, and Manasseh. The centre, Joseph's portion. Notice all the land of Judah. The south, the royal tribe. Notice unto the utmost sea. To the western horizon. Notice that Moses saw each tribe's future home. Notice that the blessings of chapter 33 now had places attached. Notice that God showed Moses the fulfilment of his life's work. Notice that the land he could not enter, he could survey with his own eyes. Psalm 16 says, 'The lines are fallen unto me in pleasant places.' Today, imagine the view God gave Moses: the full extent of His promise. Trust that He sees the whole of your life's purpose, even the parts you will not personally enter.
S: The LORD showed Moses all Naphtali, Ephraim, Manasseh and Judah to the utmost sea. Moses saw each tribe's future home, the fulfilment of his life's work, though he could not enter it. Trust that God sees the whole of your life's purpose, even the parts you will not personally enter.
P: The whole promise. Deuteronomy 34:2: 'all Naphtali, and the land of Ephraim, and Manasseh, and all the land of Judah, unto the utmost sea.' God sees your whole purpose.

== 34:3
T: The city of palm trees
R: 'And the south, and the plain of the valley of Jericho, the city of palm trees, unto Zoar.' The Negev and the whole region from the Valley of Jericho, the City of Palms, as far as Zoar. Notice the south. The Negev desert. Notice the plain of the valley of Jericho. Fertile ground near the Jordan. Notice the city of palm trees. Jericho, an oasis. Notice unto Zoar. The southern edge near the Dead Sea. Notice that Moses' view ended near Zoar, where Lot once fled. The story of the land ran back to Abraham. Notice that Jericho, the city of palm trees, would be the first city Israel conquered. Moses saw where the story would begin. Notice that God's promises have a geography; they are real places. Joshua 6 records, 'the wall fell down flat.' Today, remember that God's promises are concrete. He works in real places and real lives, including yours.
S: The LORD showed Moses the south, the plain of Jericho, the city of palm trees, as far as Zoar. Moses saw Jericho, where the conquest would begin, for God's promises are tied to real places. Remember that God's promises are concrete, working in real places and real lives, including yours.
P: Real places. Deuteronomy 34:3: 'the plain of the valley of Jericho, the city of palm trees, unto Zoar.' Moses saw where the story would begin. God works in real places.

== 34:4
T: This is the land which I sware
R: 'And the LORD said unto him, This is the land which I sware unto Abraham, unto Isaac, and unto Jacob… I have caused thee to see it with thine eyes, but thou shalt not go over thither.' God showed Moses the promised land. Notice this is the land. God pointed to it Himself. Notice which I sware unto Abraham. A promise centuries old. Notice I will give it unto thy seed. Now about to be fulfilled. Notice I have caused thee to see it. A gift of grace. Notice but thou shalt not go over thither. The consequence remained. Notice that Moses witnessed God's faithfulness to a promise made five hundred years earlier. Notice that God keeps promises across centuries. Hebrews 11 says, 'These all died in faith, not having received the promises, but having seen them afar off.' Today, trust that God keeps His promises even when they take generations. You may see fulfilment, or you may see it afar off. Either way, He is faithful.
S: The LORD told Moses that this was the land He had sworn to Abraham, Isaac and Jacob, and that he had seen it with his eyes but would not go over. Moses witnessed God's faithfulness to a promise centuries old, seeing it afar off as the faithful do. Trust that God keeps His promises even across generations, whether you see their fulfilment or see it afar off.
P: Promise across centuries. Deuteronomy 34:4: 'This is the land which I sware unto Abraham, unto Isaac, and unto Jacob.' God keeps promises across generations. He is faithful.

== 34:5
T: Moses the servant of the LORD died
R: 'So Moses the servant of the LORD died there in the land of Moab, according to the word of the LORD.' And Moses the servant of the LORD died there in Moab, as the LORD had said. Notice Moses the servant of the LORD. His highest title. Notice died there. A real death. Notice in the land of Moab. Outside the promised land. Notice according to the word of the LORD. Literally, at the mouth of the LORD. Jewish tradition says God took him with a kiss. Notice that Moses' death was not abandonment but God's own timing. Notice that the great leader is remembered first as a servant. Notice that faithful service is the highest honour. Hebrews 3 says, 'Moses verily was faithful in all his house, as a servant.' Matthew 25 says, 'Well done, thou good and faithful servant.' Today, aim to be remembered as God's servant. Titles fade, but faithful service endures. Live so that you may hear, Well done.
S: Moses the servant of the LORD died in the land of Moab according to the word of the LORD. His death came in God's own timing, and the great leader is remembered first as a servant, the highest honour. Aim to be remembered as God's faithful servant, living so that you may hear, Well done.
P: The servant of the LORD. Deuteronomy 34:5: 'So Moses the servant of the LORD died there… according to the word of the LORD.' Faithful service is the highest honour. Well done.

== 34:6
T: He buried him
R: 'And he buried him in a valley in the land of Moab, over against Beth-peor: but no man knoweth of his sepulchre unto this day.' He buried him in Moab, in the valley opposite Beth Peor, but to this day no one knows where his grave is. Notice and he buried him. God Himself buried Moses. Notice in a valley in the land of Moab. A quiet place. Notice over against Beth-peor. Near the site of Israel's sin. Notice no man knoweth of his sepulchre. The grave was hidden. Notice that God personally cared for Moses' body. Notice that the hidden grave prevented it from becoming a shrine. People would not worship Moses. Notice that Jude 1 mentions Michael disputing about Moses' body. Notice that Moses' resting place was known only to God. Psalm 116 says, 'Precious in the sight of the LORD is the death of his saints.' Today, take comfort that God cares for His servants even in death. Your life and your death are precious to Him.
S: God Himself buried Moses in a valley in the land of Moab over against Beth-peor, and no one knows his grave to this day. God personally cared for Moses' body, and the hidden grave kept it from becoming a shrine. Take comfort that God cares for His servants even in death, for your life and death are precious to Him.
P: Buried by God. Deuteronomy 34:6: 'he buried him in a valley… but no man knoweth of his sepulchre.' God cared for His servant in death. Precious is the death of His saints.

== 34:7

R: 'And Moses was an hundred and twenty years old when he died: his eye was not dim, nor his natural force abated.' Moses was a hundred and twenty years old when he died, yet his eyes were not weak nor his strength gone. Notice an hundred and twenty years old. A full life. Notice his eye was not dim. Clear vision to the end. Notice nor his natural force abated. Physical vigour remained. Notice that Moses did not die of weakness but at God's appointed time. Notice that God sustained him for his whole task. Notice that faithful service can keep a person vibrant. Notice that Moses' spiritual sight was also clear to the end. Isaiah 40 says, 'they that wait upon the LORD shall renew their strength.' Psalm 92 says, 'They shall still bring forth fruit in old age.' Today, whatever your age, seek clear eyes and renewed strength in God. Keep serving and bearing fruit. He can sustain you to the very end.
S: Moses died at a hundred and twenty with his eye not dim and his natural force not abated. He did not die of weakness but at God's appointed time, sustained for his whole task. Whatever your age, seek clear eyes and renewed strength in God, keeping on serving and bearing fruit to the end.
P: Strong to the end. Deuteronomy 34:7: 'his eye was not dim, nor his natural force abated.' They shall still bring forth fruit in old age. Keep serving.

== 34:8
T: Thirty days of weeping
R: 'And the children of Israel wept for Moses in the plains of Moab thirty days: so the days of weeping and mourning for Moses were ended.' The Israelites grieved for Moses in the plains of Moab thirty days, until the time of weeping and mourning was over. Notice the children of Israel wept. Deep grief. Notice for Moses. They had often complained against him, yet they mourned him. Notice thirty days. A full period of mourning. Notice so the days of weeping… were ended. Grief had a season, then life moved forward. Notice that grieving is right and healthy. Israel did not rush past their loss. Notice that mourning also has an end. They would then cross the Jordan. Ecclesiastes 3 says, 'A time to weep… a time to mourn, and a time to dance.' Today, if you are grieving, give yourself time. God honours mourning. And trust that He will lead you through grief into what comes next.
S: The children of Israel wept for Moses in the plains of Moab thirty days until the days of mourning were ended. Grieving was right and healthy, yet mourning had a season before life moved forward toward the crossing. If you are grieving, give yourself time, and trust God to lead you through grief into what comes next.
P: A time to weep. Deuteronomy 34:8: 'the children of Israel wept for Moses in the plains of Moab thirty days.' Grief has a season. God leads you through it.

== 34:10
T: Whom the LORD knew face to face
R: 'And there arose not a prophet since in Israel like unto Moses, whom the LORD knew face to face.' Since then, no prophet has risen in Israel like Moses, whom the LORD knew face to face. Notice there arose not a prophet since. Moses was unique. Notice like unto Moses. No equal in the Old Testament. Notice whom the LORD knew face to face. Intimate friendship with God. Notice that Moses' greatness was not mainly his miracles but his closeness to God. Notice that this verse looks forward. Moses had promised a prophet like him in chapter 18. Notice that Jesus is that Prophet, and greater. Hebrews 3 says, 'For this man was counted worthy of more glory than Moses.' Notice that through Christ we too can know God. Today, seek to know God, not just to know about Him. Moses' greatness was intimacy. In Jesus, you are invited to that closeness.
S: Scripture declares that no prophet arose in Israel like Moses, whom the LORD knew face to face. His greatness was his intimacy with God, and the verse looks ahead to the promised Prophet, Jesus, counted worthy of more glory than Moses. Seek to know God rather than merely knowing about Him, for in Jesus you are invited to that closeness.
P: Face to face. Deuteronomy 34:10: 'there arose not a prophet since in Israel like unto Moses, whom the LORD knew face to face.' Until Jesus. In Him, you can know God.

== 34:11
T: All the signs and the wonders
R: 'In all the signs and the wonders, which the LORD sent him to do in the land of Egypt to Pharaoh, and to all his servants, and to all his land.' No one matched the signs God did through him. Notice all the signs and the wonders. The plagues and the exodus. Notice which the LORD sent him to do. God was the source; Moses was the instrument. Notice in the land of Egypt. Where it began. Notice to Pharaoh. Confronting the world's greatest power. Notice that Moses' miracles were God's works through him. Notice that a humble shepherd confronted an empire because God sent him. Notice that God uses ordinary people for extraordinary purposes. Exodus 3 records Moses asking, 'Who am I, that I should go unto Pharaoh?' And God answered, 'Certainly I will be with thee.' Today, if you feel inadequate for God's calling, remember Moses. It was never about his ability but God's presence.
S: Scripture remembers Moses for all the signs and wonders the LORD sent him to do in Egypt against Pharaoh, his servants and his land. Moses was God's instrument, a humble shepherd confronting an empire because God sent him and promised to be with him. If you feel inadequate for God's calling, remember Moses, for it was never about his ability but God's presence.
P: God's works through a shepherd. Deuteronomy 34:11: 'all the signs and the wonders, which the LORD sent him to do.' 'Who am I?' 'Certainly I will be with thee.'

== 34:12
T: In the sight of all Israel
R: 'And in all that mighty hand, and in all the great terror which Moses shewed in the sight of all Israel.' For no one has ever shown the mighty power or performed the awesome deeds that Moses did in the sight of all Israel. Notice all that mighty hand. God's power displayed through Moses. Notice the great terror. Awe-inspiring deeds. Notice in the sight of all Israel. Witnessed by everyone. Notice that the book of Deuteronomy ends by honouring God's work through a faithful servant. Notice that the final word is about what God did in public view. Notice that Moses' life pointed beyond himself to God's mighty hand. Notice that the story does not end here; it continues in Joshua and ultimately in Christ. John 1 says, 'For the law was given by Moses, but grace and truth came by Jesus Christ.' Today, let your life point beyond yourself to God. And rejoice that the story Moses began continues in the grace and truth of Jesus Christ.
S: Deuteronomy ends by honouring the mighty hand and great terror that Moses showed in the sight of all Israel. Moses' life pointed beyond himself to God's power, and the story he began continues in Joshua and ultimately in Christ, through whom grace and truth came. Let your life point beyond yourself to God, and rejoice that Moses' story continues in Jesus.
P: The story continues. Deuteronomy 34:12: 'all that mighty hand… which Moses shewed in the sight of all Israel.' The law came by Moses; grace and truth by Jesus Christ.
