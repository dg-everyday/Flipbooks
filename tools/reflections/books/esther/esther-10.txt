# Esther 10: Speaking peace to all his seed

== 10:1
T: Tribute upon the land
R: 'And the king Ahasuerus laid a tribute upon the land, and upon the isles of the sea.' Notice and the king Ahasuerus. The ruler. Notice laid a tribute. Taxation. Notice upon the land. The mainland. Notice and upon the isles of the sea. Distant coasts. Notice that the book ends with ordinary government. Notice that the king still ruled. Notice that taxes still flowed. Notice that life returned to normal. Notice that the empire continued. Notice that God's people lived within it. Notice that deliverance did not end the empire. Notice that kings would bow. Notice that earthly tribute points to heavenly worship. Notice that every empire will bow to Christ. Notice that the story ends quietly. Notice that God's rule continues quietly. Notice that the ordinary is held by Him. Psalm 72 says, 'The kings of Tarshish and of the isles shall bring presents.' Notice gifts from the isles. Today, trust God's quiet rule in ordinary days. Every empire will bow to Christ.
S: King Xerxes imposed tribute throughout the empire, to its distant shores. The book ends with ordinary government, yet a greater King's reach is wider, and one day every empire will bring Him honour. Trust God's quiet rule in ordinary days, for the kings of Tarshish and of the isles shall bring presents.
P: Ordinary days. Esther 10:1: 'And the king Ahasuerus laid a tribute upon the land.' A greater King. The kings of the isles shall bring presents.

== 10:2
T: The greatness of Mordecai
R: 'and the declaration of the greatness of Mordecai, whereunto the king advanced him,' Notice that all the king's acts of power are recorded. History. Notice that his might is noted. Strength. Notice and the declaration of the greatness of Mordecai. Honour. Notice whereunto the king advanced him. Promotion. Notice that it is written in the chronicles of Media and Persia. Record. Notice that Mordecai's greatness was officially recorded. Notice that a man from the gate became great. Notice that humility was exalted. Notice that he never sought it. Notice that Mordecai waited years. Notice that he was overlooked. Notice that he faced death. Notice that he was lifted. Notice that God's due time is perfect. Notice that faithfulness is recorded in heaven and earth. Notice that humble service is never wasted. 1 Peter 5 says, 'Humble yourselves therefore under the mighty hand of God, that he may exalt you in due time.' Notice due time. Today, humble yourself under God's hand. He exalts in due time.
S: All Xerxes' acts of power and might, together with a full account of the greatness of Mordecai, whom the king had promoted, are written in the book of the annals of the kings of Media and Persia. The man once overlooked at the gate was exalted in God's perfect time, his greatness officially recorded. Humble yourself under God's hand, for humble yourselves therefore under the mighty hand of God, that He may exalt you in due time.
P: Exalted. Esther 10:2: 'the declaration of the greatness of Mordecai.' In due time. That he may exalt you in due time. Trust Him with it today.

== 10:3
T: Speaking peace to all his seed
R: 'seeking the wealth of his people, and speaking peace to all his seed.' Notice that Mordecai the Jew was second to the king. High office. Notice that he was great among the Jews. Respected. Notice that he was accepted by his many brethren. Loved. Notice seeking the wealth of his people. Their welfare. Notice and speaking peace to all his seed. Peacemaking. Notice that the book ends with a peacemaker. Notice that power was used for others' good. Notice that Mordecai sought his people's welfare. Notice that he spoke peace. Notice that Christ is the true peacemaker. Notice that He sought our good. Notice that He speaks peace to all His people. Notice that God was hidden through this book. Notice that His purposes were never hidden. Notice that the story ends in peace. Matthew 5 says, 'Blessed are the peacemakers: for they shall be called the children of God.' Notice peacemakers. Today, use whatever influence you have to seek others' good and speak peace. Peacemakers are blessed.
S: Mordecai the Jew was second in rank to King Xerxes, preeminent among the Jews and held in high esteem by his many fellow Jews, because he worked for the good of his people and spoke up for the welfare of all the Jews. The book ends with a peacemaker using power for others' good, foreshadowing Christ who speaks peace to all His people. Use your influence to seek others' good, for blessed are the peacemakers: for they shall be called the children of God.
P: Peacemaker. Esther 10:3: 'seeking the wealth of his people, and speaking peace to all his seed.' Power for good. Blessed are the peacemakers.
