# Exodus 1: Israel oppressed in Egypt

== 1:1
T: Names God remembers
R: 'Now these are the names of the children of Israel, which came into Egypt; every man and his household came with Jacob.' Exodus opens with names. Before the story of slavery and deliverance, God records the names of the families who came into Egypt. Each household is counted. Each name matters. The Hebrew title of this book is simply Names. This is a quiet reminder that God does not deal with crowds as faceless masses. He knows each person. The families who came with Jacob would soon be oppressed and forgotten by Egypt, but they were never forgotten by God. Isaiah 49 says, I have graven thee upon the palms of my hands. Jesus said the very hairs of your head are all numbered. Your name is known in heaven. Today, take comfort that God knows you personally. Whatever you face, you are not a number to Him. He remembers your name and your household.
S: Exodus opens by listing the names of Israel's sons who came into Egypt with their households. Before the story of slavery begins, God records each family by name. Take comfort that God knows you personally and remembers your name.
P: God knows your name. Exodus 1:1: 'Now these are the names of the children of Israel, which came into Egypt.' Before the hard years began, every family was named. You are known by God today.

== 1:2
T: Every son counted
R: 'Reuben, Simeon, Levi, and Judah.' The list begins with Leah's first four sons. Reuben had lost his birthright. Simeon and Levi had been rebuked for their violence. Judah had once proposed selling Joseph. Yet here they stand, named among the fathers of Israel. Their failures did not erase them from God's story. This short verse holds a lot of grace. God did not build His nation from flawless men. He built it from flawed brothers who had been humbled and changed. Levi's line would produce Moses, Aaron, and the priesthood. Judah's line would produce David and, in time, Jesus Christ. First Corinthians 1 says God hath chosen the weak things of the world. Our past does not disqualify us from God's purposes when we come to Him in repentance. Today, remember that God includes you in His story despite your failures. He works through imperfect people to accomplish His perfect plan.
S: The list begins with Reuben, Simeon, Levi and Judah, brothers with notable failures. Yet they were named among the fathers of Israel, and from Levi and Judah came priests and kings. Remember that God includes imperfect people in His story.
P: Imperfect, yet included. Exodus 1:2: 'Reuben, Simeon, Levi, and Judah.' Men with failures became fathers of a nation. God works through imperfect people, including you.

== 1:3
T: The youngest included
R: 'Issachar, Zebulun, and Benjamin.' The list continues with Leah's last two sons and Rachel's youngest, Benjamin. Benjamin had been the beloved son Jacob feared to lose. Joseph is not listed here because he was already in Egypt. Each name represents a tribe with its own story and calling. Issachar would be known for understanding the times. Zebulun would dwell by the sea. Benjamin would produce Saul and, centuries later, the apostle Paul. Small names in a list can carry great futures. God sees potential where we see only a name. Each of these men had simple beginnings, yet God had plans for their descendants that reached far beyond their lifetimes. Jeremiah 29 says, I know the thoughts that I think toward you. Today, do not underestimate what God can do through ordinary lives. Your name may seem small, but in God's hands it can carry a great future.
S: The list continues with Issachar, Zebulun and Benjamin, each the father of a tribe with its own calling. Simple names in a list carried futures that reached far beyond their lifetimes. Do not underestimate what God can do through your ordinary life.
P: Small names, great futures. Exodus 1:3: 'Issachar, Zebulun, and Benjamin.' Each became a tribe with a calling. God has plans for your life that reach beyond what you can see.

== 1:4
T: The handmaids' sons
R: 'Dan, and Naphtali, Gad, and Asher.' The list ends with the sons of the handmaids, Bilhah and Zilpah. These sons were born in the middle of a family rivalry between Rachel and Leah. Their mothers were servants. Yet they are named equally with the others as heads of Israel's tribes. God's family does not rank people by the status of their birth. The sons of servant women received the same standing as the sons of wives. Each would inherit land. Each would have a place in Israel. Galatians 3 says, There is neither Jew nor Greek, there is neither bond nor free… for ye are all one in Christ Jesus. In God's family, there are no second-class children. Revelation 7 later lists the tribes together, sealed as God's servants. Today, if you feel your background makes you less important, remember these four names. God gives every one of His children full standing and a lasting inheritance.
S: The list ends with Dan, Naphtali, Gad and Asher, sons of the handmaids. They were named equally with the other sons as heads of Israel's tribes. Remember that God gives every one of His children full standing, whatever their background.
P: No second-class children. Exodus 1:4: 'Dan, and Naphtali, Gad, and Asher.' Sons of servant women stood equal with the rest. In God's family, you have full standing.

== 1:5
T: Seventy souls
R: 'And all the souls that came out of the loins of Jacob were seventy souls: for Joseph was in Egypt already.' Only seventy people came down to Egypt. It was a small family, easily overlooked. And Joseph was already there, sent ahead by God to prepare the way. God's plan was in place before the family arrived. Small beginnings are no measure of God's purposes. Seventy souls would become a nation of millions. Deuteronomy 10 says, Thy fathers went down into Egypt with threescore and ten persons; and now the LORD thy God hath made thee as the stars of heaven for multitude. Notice also the phrase Joseph was in Egypt already. Before the family faced their need, God had placed provision ahead of them. He often goes before us in ways we only see later. Today, trust God with your small beginnings. And trust that He has already gone ahead of you into whatever season is coming.
S: Only seventy souls came from Jacob into Egypt, and Joseph was already there. The small family would become a great nation, and God had prepared the way before they arrived. Trust God with small beginnings, for He goes ahead of you.

== 1:6
T: A generation passes
R: 'And Joseph died, and all his brethren, and all that generation.' Joseph died. His brothers died. That entire generation passed away. The people who had witnessed God's great rescue through Joseph were gone. A new chapter was beginning, and those who remembered were no longer there to tell the story. Every generation passes. The leaders and heroes of faith do not live forever. When they are gone, the next generation must carry the faith forward. Judges 2 describes a sobering moment: there arose another generation after them, which knew not the LORD. Faith is not automatically inherited. It must be taught, lived, and passed on. Psalm 145 says, One generation shall praise thy works to another. Today, consider who will carry your faith forward. Tell the next generation what God has done. Do not assume they will know unless someone tells them. Be faithful to pass on the story while you still can.
S: Joseph died, along with his brothers and all that generation. Those who had witnessed God's rescue were gone, and the next generation had to carry the faith. Tell the next generation what God has done while you still can.

== 1:7
T: Fruitful and multiplied
R: 'And the children of Israel were fruitful, and increased abundantly, and multiplied, and waxed exceeding mighty; and the land was filled with them.' Though Joseph's generation died, God's promise continued. Israel was fruitful, increased abundantly, multiplied, and grew exceedingly mighty. The land was filled with them. The words echo God's command in Genesis 1: Be fruitful, and multiply, and fill the earth. God's blessing does not depend on any single person. Joseph was gone, but God's promise to Abraham kept unfolding. Genesis 22 says, I will multiply thy seed as the stars of the heaven. Leaders come and go, but God's faithfulness remains. Notice that this growth happened in a foreign land. Israel was not yet in Canaan, yet God blessed them where they were. Today, trust that God's work continues even when familiar leaders are gone. His promises do not rest on human shoulders. He keeps His word from generation to generation.
S: After Joseph's generation passed, Israel was fruitful, multiplied and grew exceedingly mighty until the land was filled with them. God's promise to Abraham kept unfolding without any single leader. Trust that God's work continues from generation to generation.

== 1:8
T: A king who knew not Joseph
R: 'Now there arose up a new king over Egypt, which knew not Joseph.' A new king came to power who did not know Joseph. He did not remember how Joseph had saved Egypt from famine. The goodwill that had protected Israel was gone. Everything was about to change. Human favor is fragile. One generation honored Joseph; the next forgot him. Israel had enjoyed protection because of their connection to Joseph. But when a king arose who knew not Joseph, that protection disappeared. Psalm 146 says, Put not your trust in princes, nor in the son of man, in whom there is no help. Governments change, leaders forget, and favor fades. But God never forgets His people. Isaiah 49 says, they may forget, yet will I not forget thee. Today, do not build your security on the favor of people. Leaders and circumstances change. Build your life on the God who never forgets you.
S: A new king arose over Egypt who knew not Joseph, and the goodwill that had protected Israel disappeared. Human favor proved fragile. Build your security on the God who never forgets you rather than on the favor of people.
P: Favor fades; God remembers. Exodus 1:8: 'there arose up a new king over Egypt, which knew not Joseph.' People forget. God never forgets you. Trust Him, not princes.

== 1:9
T: Fear of the blessed
R: 'And he said unto his people, Behold, the people of the children of Israel are more and mightier than we.' The new king looked at Israel and felt threatened. They were more numerous and mightier than the Egyptians, he claimed. Israel's blessing became the reason for Egypt's fear. God's blessing on His people can provoke envy and fear in others. Israel had done nothing to threaten Egypt. They had simply grown under God's hand. But Pharaoh saw their growth as danger. Fear often distorts how we see others. Pharaoh exaggerated the threat to stir up his people. This pattern repeats throughout history. Those who are blessed are sometimes resented. Jesus said in John 15, If the world hate you, ye know that it hated me before it hated you. Today, do not be surprised if God's blessing on your life draws opposition. And guard your own heart from fearing or resenting those whom God is blessing.
S: The new king told his people that Israel was more numerous and mightier than they. Israel's blessing from God became the reason for Pharaoh's fear. Do not be surprised if blessing draws opposition, and guard your heart from resenting others God blesses.
P: Blessing can draw opposition. Exodus 1:9: 'the people of the children of Israel are more and mightier than we.' Israel's growth stirred fear. Stay faithful when blessing draws resistance.

== 1:10
T: Worldly wisdom
R: 'Come on, let us deal wisely with them; lest they multiply, and it come to pass, that, when there falleth out any war, they join also unto our enemies, and fight against us.' Pharaoh proposed a plan to deal wisely with Israel. He feared they would join Egypt's enemies in war. His so-called wisdom was really fear dressed up as strategy. Worldly wisdom often works against God's purposes. Pharaoh's plan was clever, calculated, and cruel. But it was set against the God who had promised to multiply Abraham's seed. First Corinthians 3 says, the wisdom of this world is foolishness with God. Psalm 2 asks why the rulers take counsel together against the LORD. Pharaoh thought he was in control. In reality, he was opposing the Almighty. No scheme can stop what God has promised. Proverbs 21 says, There is no wisdom nor understanding nor counsel against the LORD. Today, trust God's wisdom over the world's schemes. No plan against God's people can ultimately succeed.
S: Pharaoh proposed to deal wisely with Israel lest they multiply and join Egypt's enemies in war. His clever plan was fear dressed as strategy, set against God's promise. Trust God's wisdom, for no scheme against His purposes can ultimately succeed.

== 1:11
T: Taskmasters and burdens
R: 'Therefore they did set over them taskmasters to afflict them with their burdens. And they built for Pharaoh treasure cities, Pithom and Raamses.' Egypt appointed taskmasters to oppress Israel with heavy labor. The Israelites were forced to build store cities for Pharaoh. The family that had been honored guests became slaves. Their labor built the wealth of the nation that oppressed them. Suffering can come suddenly and unjustly. Israel had done nothing wrong, yet they were afflicted. God had told Abraham this would happen. Genesis 15 says, thy seed shall be a stranger in a land that is not theirs, and shall serve them; and they shall afflict them four hundred years. Even this suffering was not outside God's knowledge. He had also promised, afterward shall they come out with great substance. Today, if you are carrying heavy burdens you did not choose, remember that God sees. The affliction is real, but it is not the end. He has a deliverance planned.
S: Egypt set taskmasters over Israel to afflict them with burdens, forcing them to build Pharaoh's treasure cities. The honored guests became slaves, as God had foretold to Abraham. Remember that God sees your burdens and has a deliverance planned.
P: Burdens God sees. Exodus 1:11: 'they did set over them taskmasters to afflict them with their burdens.' Israel's suffering wasn't the end of the story. Neither is yours.

== 1:12
T: The more they afflicted
R: 'But the more they afflicted them, the more they multiplied and grew. And they were grieved because of the children of Israel.' Egypt's plan backfired. The more they oppressed Israel, the more Israel multiplied and grew. The Egyptians became alarmed and dreaded the Israelites. Oppression could not stop God's blessing. This is a pattern throughout Scripture and history. God's people often grow strongest under pressure. The early church spread when it was persecuted. Acts 8 says, they that were scattered abroad went every where preaching the word. Tertullian later wrote that the blood of the martyrs is the seed of the church. Affliction cannot cancel God's promise. In fact, God often uses it to strengthen and multiply His people. Second Corinthians 4 says, We are troubled on every side, yet not distressed. Today, if you are under pressure, do not lose heart. God can cause you to grow through the very things meant to crush you.
S: The more Egypt afflicted Israel, the more they multiplied and grew, until the Egyptians dreaded them. Oppression could not stop God's blessing. Do not lose heart under pressure, for God can make you grow through the very things meant to crush you.

== 1:13
T: Served with rigour
R: 'And the Egyptians made the children of Israel to serve with rigour.' The Egyptians increased the pressure. They forced Israel to serve with rigour, meaning with harshness and cruelty. The oppression was not just hard work; it was crushing labor designed to break their spirit. Bondage is a picture of what sin does. Sin promises freedom but delivers slavery. Jesus said in John 8, Whosoever committeth sin is the servant of sin. Like Pharaoh, sin is a cruel master that demands more and more. Romans 6 says, the wages of sin is death. No amount of effort can free us from this bondage. Israel could not free themselves from Egypt. They needed a deliverer. We also need a Deliverer. Jesus said, If the Son therefore shall make you free, ye shall be free indeed. Today, if you feel trapped by habits or sin that rule over you with rigour, look to Christ. He alone can break the chains.

== 1:14
T: Lives made bitter
R: 'And they made their lives bitter with hard bondage, in morter, and in brick, and in all manner of service in the field.' Israel's lives were made bitter. Mortar, brick, and field labor filled their days. Every kind of service was forced on them with cruelty. The bitterness of this season would later be remembered every year at the Passover with bitter herbs. God does not ask us to pretend suffering is sweet. Scripture names bitterness honestly. Israel's lives were bitter, and God later commanded them to remember it. Remembering the bitterness would make the deliverance sweeter. Naomi said in Ruth 1, the Almighty hath dealt very bitterly with me. The Psalms are full of honest laments. God welcomes our honesty. Yet bitterness of circumstance need not become bitterness of heart. Hebrews 12 warns, lest any root of bitterness springing up trouble you. Today, bring your bitter seasons honestly to God. Name your pain. And trust Him to turn bitterness into a testimony of His deliverance.

== 1:15
T: Shiphrah and Puah
R: 'And the king of Egypt spake to the Hebrew midwives, of which the name of the one was Shiphrah, and the name of the other Puah.' Pharaoh summoned two midwives, Shiphrah and Puah. Scripture records their names, while the mighty king of Egypt remains unnamed. This is striking. History would forget Pharaoh's name in this passage, but God preserved the names of two humble women. God honors the faithful, not the famous. Shiphrah and Puah had no political power. They were ordinary women doing ordinary work. Yet their courage would shape history. Their names are recorded forever in God's word. First Samuel 2 says, them that honour me I will honour. The world celebrates kings and rulers. God notices midwives. Malachi 3 speaks of a book of remembrance written for them that feared the LORD. Today, remember that God sees faithfulness in ordinary roles. You may not be famous, but if you fear God, your name is precious to Him.

== 1:16
T: An evil command
R: 'And he said, When ye do the office of a midwife to the Hebrew women, and see them upon the stools; if it be a son, then ye shall kill him: but if it be a daughter, then she shall live.' Pharaoh ordered the midwives to kill every Hebrew baby boy at birth. Those entrusted with bringing life were commanded to bring death. It was a cruel and secret plan to destroy Israel from within. This command reveals the depth of evil that fear can produce. Pharaoh attacked the most vulnerable: newborn babies. Throughout history, the powerful have often targeted the defenseless. Yet God cares deeply for the vulnerable. Psalm 139 says, thou hast covered me in my mother's womb. Every child is known by God. Later, another king, Herod, would order the death of baby boys in Bethlehem, trying to destroy Jesus. Both failed. Today, stand for the vulnerable. Pray for children and protect those who cannot protect themselves. God sees every life, and so should we.
S: Pharaoh ordered the midwives to kill every Hebrew son at birth but let the daughters live. Those entrusted with life were commanded to bring death to the most vulnerable. Stand for the vulnerable today, for God sees and values every life.
P: Every life matters to God. Exodus 1:16: 'if it be a son, then ye shall kill him.' Pharaoh targeted the defenseless. God sees every child. Stand for the vulnerable today.

== 1:17
T: The midwives feared God
R: 'But the midwives feared God, and did not as the king of Egypt commanded them, but saved the men children alive.' The midwives refused. They feared God more than they feared Pharaoh. So they did not obey the king's command, and they saved the baby boys alive. Their reverence for God gave them courage to defy the most powerful man in the world. This is one of the earliest examples of civil disobedience in Scripture. When human authority commands what God forbids, we must obey God. Acts 5 records Peter saying, We ought to obey God rather than men. The fear of God casts out the fear of man. Proverbs 29 says, The fear of man bringeth a snare: but whoso putteth his trust in the LORD shall be safe. These women risked their lives to protect children. Today, let the fear of God shape your choices. When pressure pushes you toward wrong, choose to honor God. Courage grows from reverence.
S: The midwives feared God and did not do as Pharaoh commanded, but saved the baby boys alive. Reverence for God gave them courage to defy the most powerful man on earth. Let the fear of God shape your choices and give you courage to do right.
P: Fear God, not man. Exodus 1:17: 'But the midwives feared God, and did not as the king of Egypt commanded them.' Reverence for God gives courage. Choose to honor Him today.

== 1:19

R: 'And the midwives said unto Pharaoh, Because the Hebrew women are not as the Egyptian women; for they are lively, and are delivered ere the midwives come in unto them.' The midwives answered that Hebrew women were vigorous and gave birth before the midwives could arrive. Their answer turned away Pharaoh's anger and protected both the children and themselves. Some readers struggle with this answer, wondering whether it was fully truthful. Scripture does not dwell on that question. Instead, it emphasizes their fear of God and their protection of life. What stands out is their quick wisdom in a dangerous moment. They faced a murderous king and found words that saved lives. Jesus said in Matthew 10, be ye therefore wise as serpents, and harmless as doves. God gives wisdom to those who stand for what is right. James 1 says, If any of you lack wisdom, let him ask of God. == 1:22
T: Cast into the river
R: 'And Pharaoh charged all his people, saying, Every son that is born ye shall cast into the river, and every daughter ye shall save alive.' When his secret plan failed, Pharaoh made it public. He commanded all Egyptians to throw every Hebrew baby boy into the Nile. The river that gave Egypt life was to become a place of death. Evil escalates when it is resisted. Pharaoh's cruelty grew bolder. Yet God was already at work. The very river Pharaoh chose for destruction would become the place where God saved Israel's deliverer. In the next chapter, a baby boy would be placed in the Nile and rescued by Pharaoh's own daughter. God would use Pharaoh's household to raise the one who would bring Pharaoh down. Psalm 76 says, Surely the wrath of man shall praise thee. Today, when evil seems to be winning, remember that God can turn its weapons against it. He is never outmaneuvered.
