# Exodus 2: The birth of Moses; Moses flees to Midian

== 2:1
T: An ordinary marriage
R: 'And there went a man of the house of Levi, and took to wife a daughter of Levi.' The story of Israel's deliverer begins quietly. A man from the tribe of Levi married a woman from the same tribe. No names are given yet. Later we learn they were Amram and Jochebed. It was an ordinary marriage in a terrible time. Pharaoh had ordered every baby boy thrown into the river. Yet this couple chose to marry and build a family. Their decision was an act of hope. They believed life was still worth giving, even under oppression. God often begins great works through ordinary faithfulness. Before the burning bush, the plagues, and the Red Sea, there was a simple marriage between two faithful people. Zechariah 4 asks, who hath despised the day of small things? Today, do not despise ordinary faithfulness. Your daily choices of hope and obedience may be the beginning of something God will use greatly.
S: A man of the house of Levi married a daughter of Levi during Pharaoh's cruel decree. Their ordinary marriage was an act of hope in a dark time. Do not despise ordinary faithfulness, for God often begins great works through it.
P: Hope in dark times. Exodus 2:1: 'there went a man of the house of Levi, and took to wife a daughter of Levi.' An ordinary marriage began a great deliverance. Choose hope today.

== 2:2
T: A goodly child
R: 'And the woman conceived, and bare a son: and when she saw him that he was a goodly child, she hid him three months.' A son was born, the very kind of child Pharaoh had condemned. His mother saw that he was a goodly child, and she hid him for three months. Hebrews 11 says, By faith Moses, when he was born, was hid three months of his parents… and they were not afraid of the king's commandment. Their hiding was an act of faith, not just instinct. They saw something special in this child and refused to surrender him to death. Every child is precious to God, but these parents also sensed that God had a purpose for their son. Faith often begins by seeing value where the world sees only a problem. Pharaoh saw a threat. His mother saw a gift. Today, see the people around you as God sees them. Protect what God has entrusted to you, and do not let fear of the powerful silence your faith.
S: The woman bore a son, saw that he was a goodly child, and hid him three months. Hebrews says his parents acted by faith and were not afraid of the king's commandment. See others as God sees them and protect what He has entrusted to you.
P: Faith that protects. Exodus 2:2: 'when she saw him that he was a goodly child, she hid him three months.' Pharaoh saw a threat; she saw a gift. Protect what God entrusts to you.

== 2:3
T: An ark of bulrushes
R: 'And when she could not longer hide him, she took for him an ark of bulrushes, and daubed it with slime and with pitch, and put the child therein; and she laid it in the flags by the river's brink.' When she could hide him no longer, Moses' mother built a small ark of reeds and sealed it with pitch. She placed her son inside and set it among the reeds by the Nile. The word ark is the same word used for Noah's ark. Just as God preserved Noah through the waters, He would preserve this child. First Peter 5 says, Casting all your care upon him; for he careth for you. Today, do what you can, then place what you love into God's hands.

== 2:4
T: His sister watched
R: 'And his sister stood afar off, to wit what would be done to him.' Moses' sister, Miriam, stood at a distance to see what would happen. She watched over her baby brother, waiting and hoping. She could not control the outcome, but she would not abandon him. Miriam's watchfulness shows love and courage. She was young, yet she stayed close to danger to care for her brother. Her presence would soon prove crucial. Sometimes our role is simply to watch and wait faithfully. We cannot always fix the situation, but we can stay present and ready. Habakkuk 2 says, I will stand upon my watch… and will watch to see what he will say unto me. Miriam's watching put her in the right place at the right moment. God often uses those who remain attentive. Today, stay watchful over those you love. Pray for them, stay near, and be ready. God may use your faithful presence at just the right moment.

== 2:24
T: God heard and remembered
R: 'And God heard their groaning, and God remembered his covenant with Abraham, with Isaac, and with Jacob.' God heard their groaning. And God remembered His covenant with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. To remember in Scripture does not mean God had forgotten. It means He was about to act on His promise. The time for deliverance had come. God's response is rooted in His covenant. He acts not because Israel deserved it, but because He had made promises. His faithfulness to His word is the foundation of our hope. Psalm 105 says, He hath remembered his covenant for ever. God has also made a covenant with us through Christ. Hebrews 8 says, I will be merciful to their unrighteousness, and their sins and their iniquities will I remember no more. God remembers His promises and forgets our forgiven sins. Today, rest in God's covenant faithfulness. He hears your groaning, and He remembers every promise He has made to you.

== 2:25
T: God looked and knew
R: 'And God looked upon the children of Israel, and God had respect unto them.' God looked on Israel, and God had respect unto them. The Hebrew suggests that God knew them, knew their condition intimately. Four times in two verses, God is the subject: God heard, God remembered, God looked, God knew. Heaven was turning toward Egypt. This verse is the turning point of the story. Until now, it seemed God was silent. But God had been watching all along. He saw every burden, every tear, every injustice. And now He was ready to act. God is never indifferent to suffering. Psalm 33 says, The LORD looketh from heaven; he beholdeth all the sons of men. Exodus 3 will record God saying, I have surely seen the affliction of my people… for I know their sorrows. Today, if you feel unseen in your suffering, know that God looks upon you and knows your sorrows. His silence is not absence. He sees, He knows, and He will act.
