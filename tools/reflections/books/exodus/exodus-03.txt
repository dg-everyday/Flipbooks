# Exodus 3: The burning bush

== 3:1
T: The backside of the desert
R: 'Now Moses kept the flock of Jethro his father in law, the priest of Midian: and he led the flock to the backside of the desert, and came to the mountain of God, even to Horeb.' Forty years had passed. Moses, once a prince, was now tending his father-in-law's sheep. He led them to the far side of the wilderness and came to Horeb, the mountain of God. Moses was doing ordinary work in an obscure place. He was not seeking a vision. He was simply being faithful with sheep. Yet this is exactly where God met him. God often meets us in the middle of ordinary faithfulness. David was keeping sheep when Samuel came to anoint him. The shepherds were watching their flocks when the angels announced Christ's birth. Ordinary work is not beneath God's notice. Today, be faithful in your daily tasks, however humble. God may meet you at the backside of the desert, right in the middle of an ordinary day.
S: Moses was keeping Jethro's flock and led it to the backside of the desert, coming to Horeb, the mountain of God. After forty quiet years, God met him in ordinary work. Be faithful in your daily tasks, for God may meet you in the middle of an ordinary day.
P: God meets you in ordinary days. Exodus 3:1: 'he led the flock to the backside of the desert, and came to the mountain of God.' Be faithful in humble work today.

== 3:2
T: The bush was not consumed
R: 'And the angel of the LORD appeared unto him in a flame of fire out of the midst of a bush: and he looked, and, behold, the bush burned with fire, and the bush was not consumed.' The angel of the LORD appeared to Moses in a flame of fire within a bush. The bush burned, yet it was not consumed. This was a sign of God's holy presence. Many have seen in the burning bush a picture of Israel. They were in the fire of affliction in Egypt, but they were not destroyed, because God was in their midst. Isaiah 43 says, when thou walkest through the fire, thou shalt not be burned. God's presence does not destroy His people; it sustains them. The bush also shows that God can fill ordinary things with His glory. Second Corinthians 4 speaks of this treasure in earthen vessels. Today, if you feel the heat of trials, remember the bush. With God in the fire, you will not be consumed.
S: The angel of the LORD appeared to Moses in a flame of fire in a bush that burned yet was not consumed. Like Israel in affliction, the bush was sustained by God's presence. With God in your fire, you will not be consumed.
P: Burning, not consumed. Exodus 3:2: 'the bush burned with fire, and the bush was not consumed.' With God in your trial, you won't be destroyed. His presence sustains you.

== 3:3
T: I will turn aside
R: 'And Moses said, I will now turn aside, and see this great sight, why the bush is not burnt.' Moses noticed the strange sight and decided to turn aside to look. He could have kept walking with his sheep. Instead, his curiosity led him toward God. That simple decision changed his life and the history of Israel. Turning aside requires attention and willingness. Many people are too busy to notice what God is doing. Moses paused his routine to investigate. Spiritual growth often begins with a willingness to stop and look. Jeremiah 29 says, ye shall seek me, and find me, when ye shall search for me with all your heart. God often places signs in our path to draw our attention. A verse that stands out, a conversation that lingers, a burden that will not leave us. Psalm 46 says, Be still, and know that I am God. Today, slow down enough to notice. Turn aside from your routine and pay attention to what God may be showing you.
S: Moses decided to turn aside and see why the bush was not burnt. His willingness to stop and look led him into God's presence. Slow down today and turn aside to notice what God may be showing you.
P: Turn aside and see. Exodus 3:3: 'I will now turn aside, and see this great sight.' Moses paused his routine and met God. Slow down and pay attention to Him today.

== 3:4
T: Moses, Moses
R: 'And when the LORD saw that he turned aside to see, God called unto him out of the midst of the bush, and said, Moses, Moses. And he said, Here am I.' When God saw that Moses turned aside, He called him by name, twice: Moses, Moses. And Moses answered, Here am I. God spoke once Moses showed interest. Moses' attention opened the door to God's voice. The double name expresses intimacy and urgency. God also called Abraham, Abraham, and Jacob, Jacob, and later Samuel, Samuel. Jesus would say, Martha, Martha, and Simon, Simon. It is the voice of One who knows us deeply and has something important to say. Moses' response is the right one: Here am I. It is a statement of availability. Isaiah would later say, Here am I; send me. Today, listen for God calling your name. He knows you personally. When you sense His call, answer like Moses: Here am I. Make yourself available to Him.
S: When the LORD saw Moses turn aside, God called him by name twice from the bush, and Moses answered, Here am I. God knows us personally and calls us by name. Listen for God's voice today and answer with availability.
P: Here am I. Exodus 3:4: 'God called unto him out of the midst of the bush, and said, Moses, Moses. And he said, Here am I.' God knows your name. Make yourself available today.

== 3:5
T: Holy ground
R: 'And he said, Draw not nigh hither: put off thy shoes from off thy feet, for the place whereon thou standest is holy ground.' God told Moses not to come closer and to remove his sandals, because he was standing on holy ground. The ground was ordinary desert soil, but God's presence made it holy. Moses had to approach with reverence. Holiness means set apart. God is utterly pure and other than we are. We cannot approach Him casually. Removing shoes was a sign of humility and respect. Moses had to recognize who he was dealing with. Reverence is often missing in our approach to God today. We rightly celebrate His nearness, but we must also remember His holiness. Hebrews 12 says, let us have grace, whereby we may serve God acceptably with reverence and godly fear. Yet God also invites us near through Christ. Today, approach God with both confidence and reverence. Wherever He meets you becomes holy ground.
S: God told Moses not to come near and to remove his shoes, for he stood on holy ground. God's presence made ordinary soil holy and required reverence. Approach God today with both confidence and reverence.
P: Holy ground. Exodus 3:5: 'put off thy shoes from off thy feet, for the place whereon thou standest is holy ground.' God's presence makes any place holy. Approach Him with reverence.

== 3:6
T: The God of thy father
R: 'Moreover he said, I am the God of thy father, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob. And Moses hid his face; for he was afraid to look upon God.' God introduced Himself as the God of Moses' father and of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. He was the same God who had made promises to the patriarchs. Moses hid his face in fear. He realized he was in the presence of the living God. God's self-introduction connects Moses to a long history of faithfulness. This was not a new god. This was the God who had kept His word for centuries. Jesus later quoted this verse to prove the resurrection. In Matthew 22 He said, God is not the God of the dead, but of the living. Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob still live to God. Moses' fear was appropriate. Isaiah also cried, Woe is me, when he saw the Lord. Today, remember that you serve the God of every generation. He is faithful, holy, and alive.
S: God introduced Himself as the God of Moses' father and of Abraham, Isaac and Jacob, and Moses hid his face in fear. This was the faithful God of every generation. Remember that you serve the living God who keeps His word through the ages.
P: The God of every generation. Exodus 3:6: 'I am the God of thy father, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob.' He is faithful and alive. Worship Him today.

== 3:7
T: I know their sorrows
R: 'And the LORD said, I have surely seen the affliction of my people which are in Egypt, and have heard their cry by reason of their taskmasters; for I know their sorrows.' God told Moses what He had seen and heard. I have surely seen the affliction of my people. I have heard their cry. I know their sorrows. Notice the phrase my people. Though enslaved, Israel still belonged to God. This verse reveals God's heart. He is not distant from human suffering. He sees affliction, hears cries, and knows sorrows. The word know implies personal, intimate awareness. God does not merely observe pain from afar; He enters into it. Isaiah 53 describes the Messiah as a man of sorrows, and acquainted with grief. In Christ, God fully knew human sorrow. Hebrews 4 says we have a high priest who can be touched with the feeling of our infirmities. Today, take comfort that God knows your sorrows. You are His, and He sees, hears, and understands.
S: The LORD told Moses that He had surely seen the affliction of His people, heard their cry and knew their sorrows. God is not distant from human suffering but knows it intimately. Take comfort that God knows your sorrows and that you are His.
P: God knows your sorrows. Exodus 3:7: 'I have surely seen the affliction of my people… for I know their sorrows.' He sees, hears, and understands your pain today.

== 3:8
T: I am come down to deliver
R: 'And I am come down to deliver them out of the hand of the Egyptians, and to bring them up out of that land unto a good land and a large, unto a land flowing with milk and honey.' God did not merely sympathize. He said, I am come down to deliver them. He would bring them out of Egypt and up into a good and spacious land, flowing with milk and honey. God's compassion leads to action. He comes down to rescue. This verse beautifully foreshadows the incarnation. In Jesus, God truly came down. John 1 says, the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us. He came down to deliver us from sin and bring us into abundant life. Notice the two movements: out of and into. God does not only rescue us from something; He brings us into something good. Today, rejoice that God has come down to deliver you. He brings you out of bondage and into a good and large place.
S: God told Moses He had come down to deliver Israel from Egypt and bring them into a good and large land flowing with milk and honey. His compassion leads to rescue and blessing. Rejoice that God has come down in Christ to bring you out of bondage and into abundant life.
P: God came down. Exodus 3:8: 'I am come down to deliver them… unto a land flowing with milk and honey.' In Jesus, God came down to rescue you. Rejoice today!

== 3:9
T: The cry is come unto me
R: 'Now therefore, behold, the cry of the children of Israel is come unto me: and I have also seen the oppression wherewith the Egyptians oppress them.' God repeated it: the cry of Israel has reached Me, and I have seen the oppression. The repetition emphasizes certainty. God was not uncertain or uninformed. He had heard and He had seen. Oppression does not go unnoticed by heaven. Throughout Scripture, God takes the side of the oppressed. Psalm 9 says, The LORD also will be a refuge for the oppressed, a refuge in times of trouble. James 5 warns that the cries of exploited workers have entered into the ears of the Lord of sabaoth. God sees injustice, and He will act. This should comfort the oppressed and warn the oppressor. Proverbs 22 says, Rob not the poor… For the LORD will plead their cause. Today, if you are suffering injustice, your cry has reached God. And if you have power over others, use it justly, for God sees how you treat them.
S: God declared that Israel's cry had come to Him and that He had seen how the Egyptians oppressed them. Heaven does not overlook injustice. Know that your cry reaches God, and use any power you have justly, for He sees.
P: Heaven sees injustice. Exodus 3:9: 'the cry of the children of Israel is come unto me.' God hears the oppressed. Bring Him your cry and treat others justly today.

== 3:10
T: I will send thee
R: 'Come now therefore, and I will send thee unto Pharaoh, that thou mayest bring forth my people the children of Israel out of Egypt.' After saying I am come down to deliver, God said, I will send thee. God would deliver Israel, and He would do it through Moses. Divine action and human calling work together. God chooses to work through people. He did not need Moses, but He chose to use him. This is both an honor and a responsibility. The man who once tried to deliver Israel in his own strength was now sent in God's strength. The difference was the sending. Romans 10 asks, how shall they preach, except they be sent? Jesus said in John 20, as my Father hath sent me, even so send I you. Every believer is sent into the world with a purpose. Today, recognize that God wants to work through you. He sends you to bring hope and freedom to others. Go in His strength, not your own.
S: God told Moses, I will send thee unto Pharaoh, to bring His people out of Egypt. God would deliver Israel, and He chose to do it through Moses. Recognize that God wants to work through you, and go in His strength.
P: Sent by God. Exodus 3:10: 'Come now therefore, and I will send thee unto Pharaoh.' God chooses to work through people. As the Father sent Jesus, He sends you.

== 3:11
T: Who am I?
R: 'And Moses said unto God, Who am I, that I should go unto Pharaoh, and that I should bring forth the children of Israel out of Egypt?' Moses responded with doubt: Who am I? He felt inadequate. Forty years earlier, he had confidently tried to deliver Israel. Now, humbled by failure and time, he felt unworthy. Moses' question is honest. He knew his limitations. He was a fugitive shepherd, not a powerful leader. But Moses was asking the wrong question. The issue was not who Moses was, but who God is. Our sense of inadequacy can actually prepare us for God's work, because it forces us to depend on Him. Second Corinthians 3 says, our sufficiency is of God. Paul wrote in Second Corinthians 12 that God's strength is made perfect in weakness. Many of God's greatest servants felt unqualified: Gideon, Jeremiah, Isaiah. Today, if you feel inadequate for what God is asking, do not focus on who you are. Focus on who He is.
S: Moses asked God who he was to go to Pharaoh and bring Israel out of Egypt. Humbled by failure, he felt inadequate, but the real issue was who God is. When you feel unqualified, focus on God's sufficiency rather than your own.
P: Who am I? Exodus 3:11: 'Who am I, that I should go unto Pharaoh?' The question isn't who you are but who God is. His strength is made perfect in weakness.

== 3:12
T: Certainly I will be with thee
R: 'And he said, Certainly I will be with thee; and this shall be a token unto thee, that I have sent thee: When thou hast brought forth the people out of Egypt, ye shall serve God upon this mountain.' God did not answer Moses by building up his self-esteem. He answered with a promise: Certainly I will be with thee. God's presence was the answer to Moses' inadequacy. God also gave Moses a sign: Israel would worship on this very mountain. But the sign would come after Moses obeyed. Faith had to come first. This is God's answer to all our insecurities. We are not enough, but God is with us. Joshua 1 says, as I was with Moses, so I will be with thee. Matthew 28 records Jesus saying, lo, I am with you alway. Our confidence rests not in our abilities but in His presence. Today, when you feel unequal to the task, hold on to this promise. God is with you, and that is enough.
S: God answered Moses' doubt with a promise, Certainly I will be with thee, and a sign that Israel would serve God on that mountain. God's presence was the answer to Moses' inadequacy. When you feel unequal to the task, hold on to the promise that God is with you.

Moses asked another question. When the Israelites ask the name of the God who sent him, what should he say? Moses needed to know God more deeply in order to represent Him faithfully. Names in the ancient world revealed character. Asking God's name was asking, Who are you really? What are you like? We cannot represent God well if we do not know Him well. Paul said in Philippians 3, That I may know him. Jesus prayed in John 17, this is life eternal, that they might know thee the only true God. Today, seek to know God more deeply. Ask Him to reveal Himself to you. The better you know Him, the better you will represent Him to others.

== 3:14
T: I AM THAT I AM
R: 'And God said unto Moses, I AM THAT I AM: and he said, Thus shalt thou say unto the children of Israel, I AM hath sent me unto you.' God revealed His name: I AM THAT I AM. He is the self-existent One, eternal, unchanging, dependent on nothing. He simply is. He was, He is, and He always will be. This name is the foundation of the name YHWH, translated LORD in capitals. It declares that God needs nothing outside Himself. Everything else depends on Him. He is always present tense. Jesus applied this name to Himself. In John 8 He said, Before Abraham was, I am. The crowd picked up stones, because they understood He was claiming to be God. Jesus also said I am the bread of life, the light of the world, the good shepherd, the resurrection and the life. Today, rest in the unchanging God. Whatever you need, He is. He does not change with circumstances. I AM is with you.
S: God revealed His name to Moses as I AM THAT I AM, the eternal, self-existent and unchanging One. Jesus later applied this name to Himself, saying, Before Abraham was, I am. Rest today in the unchanging God who is everything you need.

== 3:15
T: My name for ever
R: 'And God said moreover unto Moses, Thus shalt thou say unto the children of Israel, The LORD God of your fathers… hath sent me unto you: this is my name for ever, and this is my memorial unto all generations.' God gave His covenant name: the LORD, the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. And He declared this would be His name forever, His memorial to all generations. God joined His eternal name to His relationship with real people. The eternal I AM is also personal. He is the God who walked with Abraham, provided for Isaac, and wrestled with Jacob. A memorial is how something is remembered. God wanted every generation to remember Him as the faithful, covenant-keeping God. Malachi 3 says, I am the LORD, I change not. Hebrews 13 says, Jesus Christ the same yesterday, and to day, and for ever. Today, call on the LORD who has proved faithful to every generation. He will be faithful to yours.

== 3:16
T: I have surely visited you
R: 'Go, and gather the elders of Israel together, and say unto them, The LORD God of your fathers… appeared unto me, saying, I have surely visited you, and seen that which is done to you in Egypt.' God told Moses to gather the elders and tell them that God had appeared to him with a message: I have surely visited you. These words echo Joseph's dying promise in Genesis 50: God will surely visit you. Centuries later, God was keeping Joseph's word. Imagine how these words would land. For generations, Israel had repeated Joseph's promise. Some had perhaps given up hope. Now Moses came saying, the visit has come. God's promises may take long, but they are never forgotten. Second Peter 3 says, The Lord is not slack concerning his promise. Luke 1 echoes this language when Zacharias praises God, for he hath visited and redeemed his people. Today, hold on to God's promises even if they have been delayed. The day of His visitation will come.
S: God told Moses to gather Israel's elders and announce that God had surely visited them. The words fulfilled Joseph's dying promise after centuries of waiting. Hold on to God's promises even when they are delayed, for His visitation will come.

God repeated His promise: I will bring you up out of the affliction of Egypt into a land flowing with milk and honey. The message to the elders was clear. God had spoken, and His word would come to pass. Notice that God says, I have said. His word is already settled. Once God speaks, the outcome is certain. The journey from affliction to promise would be long and difficult. But the destination was secure. Romans 4 says Abraham was fully persuaded that, what he had promised, he was able also to perform. Today, build your hope on what God has said. His word is settled.

God delivers us so that we may serve Him. Today, trust God to prepare hearts before you. Seek partners in His work. And remember that freedom is for worship.

Pharaoh would resist, but God would stretch out His hand. He would strike Egypt with wonders, and then Pharaoh would let them go. The outcome was certain because God Himself would act. God's hand is stronger than any human resistance. Pharaoh was the most powerful ruler on earth, but he was no match for the Almighty. The wonders God would perform, the ten plagues, would demonstrate His power over Egypt and its gods. Notice the phrase after that. Resistance would come first, then release. There is often an after that in God's timing. We go through the struggle, and then comes the breakthrough. Isaiah 59 says, the LORD'S hand is not shortened, that it cannot save. Today, if you face a stubborn obstacle, trust God's outstretched hand. After the struggle, His deliverance will come.

God promised that Israel would receive favor from the Egyptians. When they left, they would not leave empty-handed. Slaves who had labored without wages for generations would leave with provision. God would ensure they were compensated. This fulfilled God's word to Abraham in Genesis 15: afterward shall they come out with great substance. God sees unpaid labor and unjust treatment, and He makes things right. God also controls the hearts of people. The same Egyptians who oppressed Israel would give them gifts. Proverbs 21 says, The king's heart is in the hand of the LORD. God can turn hearts in our favor when the time is right. Today, trust that God sees what you have lost unjustly. == 3:22
T: Spoiling the Egyptians
R: 'But every woman shall borrow of her neighbour… jewels of silver, and jewels of gold, and raiment: and ye shall put them upon your sons, and upon your daughters; and ye shall spoil the Egyptians.' God instructed the Israelite women to ask their Egyptian neighbors for silver, gold, and clothing. They would dress their children in these things. In this way, Israel would plunder Egypt, not by force, but by favor. The word borrow here means to ask. Israel asked, and the Egyptians gave. Later, much of this gold and silver would be given for building the tabernacle. Wealth taken from slavery would be used for worship. God redeems resources for His glory. What was once in the hands of oppressors became material for God's dwelling place. Haggai 2 says, The silver is mine, and the gold is mine, saith the LORD of hosts. Today, consider how the resources God gives you can be used for His purposes. What He provides is meant ultimately for His glory and His dwelling among His people.
S: God told the Israelite women to ask their neighbors for silver, gold and clothing, so they would spoil the Egyptians by favor rather than force. Much of this wealth later built the tabernacle. Use the resources God gives you for His glory and purposes.
