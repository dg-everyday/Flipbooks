# Exodus 4: Signs for Moses; the return to Egypt

== 4:1
T: But they will not believe me
R: 'And Moses answered and said, But, behold, they will not believe me, nor hearken unto my voice: for they will say, The LORD hath not appeared unto thee.' Moses raised another objection. What if the people do not believe him? What if they say God never appeared to him? Moses feared rejection, perhaps remembering how his own people had rejected him forty years earlier. Past rejection can make us hesitant to step out again. Moses had been wounded before. Yet God had just promised that the elders would listen. Moses' fear spoke louder than God's word. We often do the same. God speaks a promise, and we answer with but. Fear focuses on what might go wrong. Faith focuses on what God has said. Second Timothy 1 says, God hath not given us the spirit of fear; but of power, and of love, and of a sound mind. Today, notice where you answer God's promises with but. Let His word speak louder than your fear of rejection.
S: Moses objected that the people would not believe him or accept that the LORD had appeared to him. His fear of rejection spoke louder than God's promise. Notice where you answer God with but, and let His word speak louder than your fears.
P: Fear says but. Exodus 4:1: 'But, behold, they will not believe me, nor hearken unto my voice.' God had already promised they would listen. Let His word outweigh your fear today.

== 4:2
T: What is in thine hand?
R: 'And the LORD said unto him, What is that in thine hand? And he said, A rod.' God answered Moses' fear with a question: What is in your hand? Moses held a rod, a simple shepherd's staff. It was ordinary, worn, and familiar. Yet God was about to use it in extraordinary ways. God often uses what we already have. Moses did not need new tools. He needed to offer what was in his hand. David had a sling. The widow in Elijah's day had a handful of meal. The boy in John 6 had five loaves and two fishes. In God's hands, ordinary things become instruments of power. We often think we need more resources, more talent, or better circumstances. But God asks, What is in your hand? Your skills, your time, your experiences, your possessions. Romans 12 says, Having then gifts differing according to the grace that is given to us, let us use them. Today, offer God what is already in your hand.
S: God asked Moses what was in his hand, and Moses answered, A rod. The ordinary shepherd's staff would become an instrument of God's power. Offer God what is already in your hand, for He uses ordinary things in extraordinary ways.
P: What's in your hand? Exodus 4:2: 'What is that in thine hand? And he said, A rod.' God used a simple staff. Offer Him what you already have today.

== 4:3
T: Cast it down
R: 'And he said, Cast it on the ground. And he cast it on the ground, and it became a serpent; and Moses fled from before it.' God told Moses to throw his rod on the ground. When he did, it became a serpent, and Moses ran from it. What had been safe and familiar suddenly became frightening. Sometimes God asks us to release what we hold. The rod represented Moses' livelihood and identity as a shepherd. Casting it down meant letting go of control. What we cling to may look different once we surrender it to God. The serpent was also significant. In Egypt, the cobra symbolized Pharaoh's power. God was showing that He had authority over that symbol. Moses' fear was natural. Yet God would teach him that what is surrendered to God is under God's control. Today, consider what God might be asking you to cast down. Surrender is often frightening, but it is the first step toward seeing God's power at work.
S: God told Moses to cast his rod on the ground, and it became a serpent, so Moses fled from it. What had been familiar became frightening once surrendered to God. Consider what God may be asking you to cast down, trusting His control.
P: Cast it down. Exodus 4:3: 'he cast it on the ground, and it became a serpent.' Surrender can feel frightening. But what's surrendered to God is under His control.

== 4:4
T: Take it by the tail
R: 'And the LORD said unto Moses, Put forth thine hand, and take it by the tail. And he put forth his hand, and caught it, and it became a rod in his hand.' God told Moses to grab the serpent by the tail, the most dangerous way to hold a snake. Moses obeyed, and it became a rod again. Faith meant reaching toward what he feared. This was a lesson in trust. Moses had to overcome his fear and act on God's word. Faith often requires us to do what seems foolish or dangerous. Peter stepped out of the boat onto the water. The priests stepped into the Jordan before it parted. Obedience comes before the miracle. When Moses obeyed, the threat became a tool again. Joshua 1 says, Be strong and of a good courage; be not afraid. Today, if God is calling you to face something you fear, reach out in obedience. In His hand, what frightens you can become a tool for His purposes.
S: God told Moses to take the serpent by the tail, and when he obeyed, it became a rod again. Faith meant reaching toward what he feared. Reach out in obedience when God calls you to face your fears.
P: Reach out in faith. Exodus 4:4: 'Put forth thine hand, and take it by the tail.' Moses faced his fear, and the serpent became a rod. Obey God even when you're afraid.

== 4:5
T: That they may believe
R: 'That they may believe that the LORD God of their fathers, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob, hath appeared unto thee.' God explained the purpose of the sign: so that the people would believe that the God of their fathers had truly appeared to Moses. The miracle was not for show. It was to build faith. He knew that Israel, after centuries of slavery, would struggle to believe. So He provided evidence. God is patient with doubters. Jesus also performed signs so that people would believe. John 20 says, these are written, that ye might believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God. Signs point beyond themselves to the God who acts. Notice also that Moses' credibility rested not on his own authority but on the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. Today, thank God for the evidence He gives to strengthen faith. Let what He has done in Scripture and in your life point you to trust Him.
S: God gave the sign so that the people would believe that the God of their fathers had appeared to Moses. Miracles were meant to build faith in the God who acts. Thank God for the evidence He gives and let it lead you to trust Him.
P: Signs that build faith. Exodus 4:5: 'That they may believe that the LORD God of their fathers… hath appeared unto thee.' God is patient with doubters. Let His works lead you to trust.

== 4:6
T: Leprous as snow
R: 'And the LORD said furthermore unto him, Put now thine hand into thy bosom. And he put his hand into his bosom: and when he took it out, behold, his hand was leprous as snow.' God gave a second sign. Moses put his hand inside his cloak, and when he drew it out, it was diseased, white as snow. In a moment, healthy flesh became unclean. This sign was personal. Moses' own body was affected. It showed that God has power over health and disease, over clean and unclean. Leprosy in Scripture often pictures sin, which corrupts from within. The hand placed close to the heart came out defiled. Jesus said in Mark 7, from within, out of the heart of men, proceed evil thoughts. Yet the sign was not finished. What God allowed, He would also heal. Today, recognize that sin corrupts us from the inside. Only God can make us clean. Bring your heart to Him honestly, trusting His power to heal.
S: God told Moses to put his hand into his bosom, and it came out leprous as snow. The sign showed God's power over clean and unclean and pictured how sin corrupts from within. Bring your heart honestly to God, who alone can make you clean.
P: Corrupted from within. Exodus 4:6: 'when he took it out, behold, his hand was leprous as snow.' Sin defiles the heart, but God can make us clean. Bring Him your heart today.

== 4:7
T: Turned again as his other flesh
R: 'And he said, Put thine hand into thy bosom again. And he put his hand into his bosom again; and plucked it out of his bosom, and, behold, it was turned again as his other flesh.' God told Moses to put his hand into his cloak again. This time it came out fully restored, like the rest of his body. The God who could bring disease could also bring complete healing. This second movement completes the message. God is not only powerful to judge; He is powerful to restore. The same hand that was defiled was made clean. This is the gospel in picture. We are corrupted by sin, but God can make us whole again. Psalm 51 says, wash me, and I shall be whiter than snow. First John 1 says, the blood of Jesus Christ his Son cleanseth us from all sin. Jesus touched lepers and made them clean. Today, rejoice that God restores completely. Whatever has been defiled in your life, He can make new again.
S: When Moses put his hand into his bosom again, it came out restored like his other flesh. The God who allowed disease also brought complete healing. Rejoice that God can restore whatever has been defiled in your life.

== 4:8
T: The voice of the sign
R: 'And it shall come to pass, if they will not believe thee, neither hearken to the voice of the first sign, that they will believe the voice of the latter sign.' God said that if the people did not believe the first sign, they would believe the second. Notice the phrase the voice of the sign. Signs speak. They carry a message from God. God is patient and persistent with unbelief. He gives sign after sign, evidence upon evidence. He does not give up on people easily. Second Peter 3 says He is longsuffering to us-ward, not willing that any should perish. God also knows that different people respond to different things. Some are convinced by one sign, others by another. He meets people where they are. Paul said in First Corinthians 9, I am made all things to all men, that I might by all means save some. Today, be patient with those who struggle to believe. God has many ways to reach a heart. Keep praying and keep showing His love.
S: God said that if the people would not believe the first sign, they would believe the voice of the second. God is patient and persistent with unbelief, speaking in many ways. Be patient with those who struggle to believe, for God has many ways to reach a heart.

== 4:9
T: Water to blood
R: 'And it shall come to pass, if they will not believe also these two signs… thou shalt take of the water of the river, and pour it upon the dry land: and the water which thou takest out of the river shall become blood upon the dry land.' God gave a third sign. If the people still doubted, Moses would pour Nile water on the ground, and it would turn to blood. This sign anticipated the first plague. The river Pharaoh had used to drown Hebrew babies would itself become blood. God would expose the false gods of Egypt and bring judgment on its cruelty. God's patience with unbelief is great, but His signs also grow more serious. Each sign was stronger than the last. God gives many opportunities to believe. Today, respond to God while His voice is gentle. Hebrews 3 says, To day if ye will hear his voice, harden not your hearts.
S: God gave a third sign: Nile water poured on dry land would become blood. The sign showed His power over Egypt's lifeline and anticipated the first plague. Respond to God while His voice is gentle and do not harden your heart.

== 4:10
T: Slow of speech
R: 'And Moses said unto the LORD, O my Lord, I am not eloquent, neither heretofore, nor since thou hast spoken unto thy servant: but I am slow of speech, and of a slow tongue.' Moses raised yet another objection. I am not eloquent. I have never been a good speaker, and nothing has changed since You started talking to me. I am slow of speech and of tongue. Moses focused on his weakness. He may have genuinely struggled with speaking. But he was using his limitation as an excuse. He seemed to think God had not considered it. Yet God knew exactly who He was calling. Our weaknesses do not surprise God. Paul, too, was told his speech was contemptible, according to Second Corinthians 10. Yet God used him mightily. First Corinthians 2 records Paul saying, my speech and my preaching was not with enticing words… but in demonstration of the Spirit and of power. Today, do not let your weaknesses become excuses. God knew them when He called you. He works through them.
S: Moses told the Lord that he was not eloquent but slow of speech and tongue. He used his weakness as an excuse, though God knew it when He called him. Do not let your weaknesses become excuses, for God works through them.

== 4:11
T: Who hath made man's mouth?
R: 'And the LORD said unto him, Who hath made man's mouth? or who maketh the dumb, or deaf, or the seeing, or the blind? have not I the LORD?' God answered with questions. Who made the human mouth? Who makes people unable to speak or hear, see or not see? Is it not I, the LORD? God reminded Moses that He is the Creator. He designed Moses' mouth. He understood its limitations better than Moses did. This answer reframes our weaknesses. We are not accidents. God made us, and He knows our frame. Psalm 139 says, I will praise thee; for I am fearfully and wonderfully made. If God made the mouth, He can certainly use it. Our limitations are not barriers to the One who designed us. These words also affirm the dignity of every person, including those with disabilities. All are made by God. Today, trust that your Creator knows you completely. He made you on purpose and can use every part of you for His glory.
S: God asked Moses who made man's mouth and who makes people unable to speak, hear or see, if not the LORD. As Creator, God knows our limitations and can use them. Trust that your Creator made you on purpose and can use every part of you.

== 4:12
T: I will be with thy mouth
R: 'Now therefore go, and I will be with thy mouth, and teach thee what thou shalt say.' God gave Moses a command and a promise. Go. I will be with your mouth. I will teach you what to say. God did not change Moses' abilities first. He promised His presence and help in the very area of weakness. God's help often comes in the going. Moses would not feel eloquent before he left. But as he obeyed, God would give him words. Jesus made a similar promise in Luke 12: the Holy Ghost shall teach you in the same hour what ye ought to say. We do not need to have everything figured out before we obey. Jeremiah also said he could not speak, and God said in Jeremiah 1, I have put my words in thy mouth. Today, step out in obedience even if you feel unprepared. God will be with your mouth. He will give you what you need when you need it.

== 4:13
T: Send someone else
R: 'And he said, O my Lord, send, I pray thee, by the hand of him whom thou wilt send.' After all of God's assurances, Moses finally revealed his heart: Please send someone else. His objections had been excuses. The real issue was that he did not want to go. This is a sobering moment. God had answered every concern, given signs, and promised His presence. Yet Moses still resisted. Sometimes our questions to God are not honest searches for answers; they are ways to avoid obedience. Moses' reluctance is very human. Many of us know the feeling of hoping God will choose someone else. Jonah ran the other way. Yet God did not give up on Moses. He would still use him, though Moses' reluctance had consequences. Today, examine your heart. Are your objections to God's call genuine questions, or excuses to avoid going? Be honest with Him. And ask Him for the willingness to say yes.

Communion, baptism, and even a well-worn Bible can remind us of God's presence and power. They are not powerful in themselves, but they point to the God who is. Second Corinthians 4 says, the excellency of the power may be of God, and not of us. Today, take with you the reminders of God's faithfulness. Carry His word, remember His acts, and go in His authority.

== 4:22

This is the first time God calls Israel His son. The slaves Pharaoh mistreated were God's own children. To oppress them was to oppress God's family. This is a powerful statement of identity. Israel was not merely a labor force. They were beloved children of the Most High. Their worth came from their relationship with God, not from their condition. Firstborn also implies special privilege and inheritance. Israel held a unique place among the nations. This anticipates the New Testament. Jesus is God's true Son. And through Him, believers are adopted as children of God. Galatians 4 says, God sent forth his Son… that we might receive the adoption of sons. Today, remember who you are. However you are treated, you are God's child. Your worth comes from Him.

== 4:23
T: Let my son go
R: 'And I say unto thee, Let my son go, that he may serve me: and if thou refuse to let him go, behold, I will slay thy son, even thy firstborn.' God's demand was clear: Let My son go, that he may serve Me. And the warning was serious: if you refuse, I will slay your firstborn. From the beginning, Pharaoh was told the final consequence of refusal. Notice the purpose of freedom: that he may serve me. Israel was being freed from Pharaoh's service to enter God's service. Freedom in Scripture is not freedom to do whatever we want. It is freedom to worship and serve God. Romans 6 says, being then made free from sin, ye became the servants of righteousness. == 4:24

Moses could not call Israel to keep covenant while ignoring it himself. Leaders must first obey in their own households. First Timothy 3 says an overseer must rule well his own house. Small disobediences are not small to God. This passage is uncomfortable, and that is part of its message. Today, examine whether there is any area of known disobedience in your life or home. == 4:26
T: So he let him go

Through Jesus, we are brought into covenant with God, not by our own blood but by His. Today, thank God for the covenant made through Christ's blood. Through Him, judgment passes and life is given.
