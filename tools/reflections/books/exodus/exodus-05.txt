# Exodus 5: Pharaoh increases the burden

== 5:1
T: Let my people go
R: 'And afterward Moses and Aaron went in, and told Pharaoh, Thus saith the LORD God of Israel, Let my people go, that they may hold a feast unto me in the wilderness.' Moses and Aaron stood before the most powerful man on earth and delivered God's message: Let my people go. The fugitive shepherd now spoke with God's authority. They did not soften the message or ask timidly. They said, Thus saith the LORD. Notice the reason given: that they may hold a feast unto me. God's purpose for His people was celebration and worship. Freedom was not an end in itself. It was freedom to gather joyfully before God. Courage comes from knowing whose message we carry. Moses and Aaron were not speaking their own words. Acts 4 records the apostles praying for boldness to speak God's word. Today, speak God's truth with courage. And remember that He calls His people out of bondage to celebrate and worship Him.
S: Moses and Aaron told Pharaoh, Thus saith the LORD God of Israel, Let my people go, that they may hold a feast unto me. They spoke boldly with God's authority, and the purpose of freedom was joyful worship. Speak God's truth with courage and remember that He frees us to celebrate Him.
P: Let my people go! Exodus 5:1: 'Thus saith the LORD God of Israel, Let my people go, that they may hold a feast unto me.' God frees us to worship. Speak His truth boldly today.
H: #DailyGrace #Exodus51 #Freedom #Worship #Courage #BibleVerse

== 5:2
T: Who is the LORD?
R: 'And Pharaoh said, Who is the LORD, that I should obey his voice to let Israel go? I know not the LORD, neither will I let Israel go.' Pharaoh answered with contempt. Who is the LORD? I do not know Him, and I will not let Israel go. Pharaoh considered himself a god. He saw no reason to obey the God of slaves. Pharaoh's question would be answered over the coming chapters. Through the plagues, God would show exactly who He is. Again and again, Exodus says, that ye may know that I am the LORD. Pharaoh would come to know the LORD, though not in the way he hoped. Pride refuses to acknowledge God. Pharaoh's real problem was not ignorance but arrogance. Proverbs 16 says, Pride goeth before destruction. Many people today ask Pharaoh's question with the same attitude. Yet God patiently reveals Himself. Philippians 2 says every knee will one day bow. Today, humble yourself and answer Pharaoh's question rightly. The LORD is God, and He is worthy of obedience.
S: Pharaoh asked who the LORD was that he should obey Him, declaring that he did not know the LORD and would not let Israel go. His arrogance refused to acknowledge God, but the plagues would answer his question. Humble yourself today and acknowledge the LORD as worthy of obedience.
P: Who is the LORD? Exodus 5:2: 'Who is the LORD, that I should obey his voice?' Pharaoh asked in pride. Answer it humbly today: He is God, worthy of your obedience.

== 5:3
T: The God of the Hebrews met with us
R: 'And they said, The God of the Hebrews hath met with us: let us go, we pray thee, three days' journey into the desert, and sacrifice unto the LORD our God; lest he fall upon us with pestilence, or with the sword.' Moses and Aaron persisted. They explained that the God of the Hebrews had met with them, and they asked to go three days into the wilderness to sacrifice. They warned that disobeying God could bring disaster. Moses and Aaron did not give up after the first rejection. They repeated their request with respect and clarity. Persistence is often needed in doing God's will. They also spoke of a God who meets with His people. The LORD is not an abstract force. He meets with people personally. Their request was reasonable and humble: we pray thee. They respected Pharaoh's position even while standing firm. Today, be persistent in doing what God has asked. Do not give up after the first no. Stand firm, speak respectfully, and trust God with the outcome.
S: Moses and Aaron persisted, explaining that the God of the Hebrews had met with them and asking to go sacrifice in the desert. They stood firm after rejection while speaking respectfully. Be persistent in doing God's will and do not give up after the first no.

== 5:4
T: Get back to work
R: 'And the king of Egypt said unto them, Wherefore do ye, Moses and Aaron, let the people from their works? get you unto your burdens.' Pharaoh dismissed them. Why are you distracting the people from their work? Go back to your burdens. To Pharaoh, Israel's only value was their labor. Worship was a waste of productive time. This reveals a worldly view of people. Pharaoh saw Israel as workers, not worshipers. Many people today feel the same pressure. Work, productivity, and output can crowd out worship and rest. The world says, get back to your burdens. But God created us for more than labor. He created us for relationship with Him. Jesus said in Matthew 11, Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest. Martha was busy with much serving, but Mary chose the good part, sitting at Jesus' feet. Today, resist the voice that says worship is a waste of time. Make room for God. You were made for more than your work.
S: Pharaoh accused Moses and Aaron of distracting the people and told them to get back to their burdens. To him, Israel's only value was their labor, and worship was a waste. Resist the pressure that crowds out worship and make room for God today.
P: More than your work. Exodus 5:4: 'get you unto your burdens.' Pharaoh saw only workers. God made you for relationship with Him. Make room for worship today.

== 5:5
T: Valued only for output
R: 'And Pharaoh said, Behold, the people of the land now are many, and ye make them rest from their burdens.' Pharaoh complained that the people were many and that Moses was making them rest. The thought of slaves resting angered him. To Pharaoh, more people meant more labor, and rest meant lost profit. Pharaoh's attitude shows the cruelty of a system that values people only for what they produce. Rest was seen as a threat. Yet God would later give Israel the Sabbath, a weekly day of rest for everyone, including servants and animals. Deuteronomy 5 connects the Sabbath to Egypt: remember that thou wast a servant in the land of Egypt. God's design for His people includes rest. He does not treat us as machines. Mark 2 says, The sabbath was made for man. Today, receive rest as a gift from God. Do not let a Pharaoh-like voice, within or without, tell you that your worth is only in your work. And treat others with that same dignity.
S: Pharaoh complained that the people were many and that Moses made them rest from their burdens. He valued them only for their labor and saw rest as a threat. Receive rest as God's gift and remember that your worth is not only in your work.
P: Rest is God's gift. Exodus 5:5: 'ye make them rest from their burdens.' Pharaoh hated rest. God gives it. Your worth isn't measured by output. Receive His rest today.

== 5:6
T: The same day
R: 'And Pharaoh commanded the same day the taskmasters of the people, and their officers, saying.' The very same day, Pharaoh gave new orders to the taskmasters and officers. His response to God's request was swift and hostile. He did not consider it or wait. He moved immediately to tighten his grip. Opposition to God's work can be quick and fierce. When Moses and Aaron spoke God's word, Pharaoh reacted the same day. Sometimes stepping out in obedience brings immediate resistance. This does not mean we are wrong. It often means we have touched something that matters. When God begins to set people free, the enemy reacts. Ephesians 6 reminds us that we wrestle not against flesh and blood, but against spiritual powers. First Peter 5 says, your adversary the devil, as a roaring lion, walketh about. Do not be surprised by resistance. Today, if obedience brings swift opposition, stand firm. Pharaoh's quick reaction did not stop God's plan. Neither will the opposition you face.

== 5:7
T: No more straw
R: 'Ye shall no more give the people straw to make brick, as heretofore: let them go and gather straw for themselves.' Pharaoh ordered that the people would no longer receive straw for bricks. They would have to gather it themselves. Straw was essential for strong bricks, and gathering it would take enormous time and effort. The workload was suddenly doubled. Pharaoh punished the request for worship by making life harder. This is a strategy of oppression: increase the burden so people have no time or strength to think about freedom or God. Exhaustion can crush hope. Many people today feel similarly burdened. Responsibilities multiply, resources shrink, and there seems to be no time for God. The enemy loves to keep us too busy and too tired to seek the Lord. Isaiah 40 says, they that wait upon the LORD shall renew their strength. Today, if your burdens have increased and your strength is fading, bring them to God. Do not let exhaustion keep you from the One who gives rest.
S: Pharaoh ordered that the people would no longer be given straw but must gather it themselves. He punished the request for worship by doubling their burden. Do not let exhaustion keep you from God, who renews the strength of those who wait on Him.
P: When burdens double. Exodus 5:7: 'Ye shall no more give the people straw to make brick.' Exhaustion can crush hope. Wait on the LORD, and He will renew your strength.

== 5:8
T: They be idle
R: 'And the tale of the bricks, which they did make heretofore, ye shall lay upon them; ye shall not diminish ought thereof: for they be idle; therefore they cry, saying, Let us go and sacrifice to our God.' Pharaoh insisted that the brick quota remain the same. He called the people idle and claimed that their desire to worship came from laziness. In Pharaoh's eyes, wanting to seek God was a sign of having too much free time. This is a familiar accusation. People who prioritize worship are sometimes called lazy or impractical. Pharaoh twisted Israel's spiritual longing into a character flaw. Yet Jesus Himself was accused falsely. Matthew 5 says, Blessed are ye, when men shall revile you… and shall say all manner of evil against you falsely, for my sake. Today, do not let anyone shame you for seeking God. Your desire to worship is not idleness. It is the deepest purpose of your life.

== 5:9
T: Vain words
R: 'Let there more work be laid upon the men, that they may labour therein; and let them not regard vain words.' Pharaoh ordered heavier work so the people would be too busy to listen to vain words. He meant Moses' message from God. Pharaoh called God's promise of deliverance empty talk. This is a deliberate strategy: keep people so busy that they cannot hear God's word. Overwork drowns out hope. When we are exhausted, God's promises can begin to sound like vain words. Pharaoh wanted Israel to stop believing. But God's word is never vain. Isaiah 55 says, my word… shall not return unto me void. What Pharaoh dismissed as empty talk would soon shake his kingdom. The enemy still tries to make God's promises sound unrealistic. Busyness and weariness can make us cynical. Today, guard your heart against the idea that God's promises are vain words. Make time to listen to His voice, even when life is crowded. His word will prove true.

== 5:10
T: Thus saith Pharaoh
R: 'And the taskmasters of the people went out, and their officers, and they spake to the people, saying, Thus saith Pharaoh, I will not give you straw.' The taskmasters announced Pharaoh's decree: Thus saith Pharaoh, I will not give you straw. The phrase mimics the words Moses used: Thus saith the LORD. Two voices were now competing for Israel's attention. This contrast runs through the whole story. Thus saith the LORD versus thus saith Pharaoh. Which voice would Israel trust? Which word would prove true? The people now heard a harsh word from Pharaoh that seemed far more real than God's promise. We face competing voices too. The world, our fears, and our circumstances speak loudly. God's word can seem quiet in comparison. Yet only one word will stand. Isaiah 40 says, The grass withereth, the flower fadeth: but the word of our God shall stand for ever. Today, notice which voice you are listening to. Choose to trust Thus saith the LORD over every other voice.

When human resources run out, God's provision begins. God sometimes allows impossible situations to teach us dependence on Him. Today, if you face demands beyond your resources, cry out to God. He is not limited by what you lack. == 5:12
T: Scattered for stubble
R: 'So the people were scattered abroad throughout all the land of Egypt to gather stubble instead of straw.' The people scattered across Egypt searching for stubble, the leftover stalks in harvested fields. It was a poor substitute for straw, and gathering it consumed their time and strength. The nation that had been gathered to hear God's promise was now scattered in desperation. Oppression scatters. It isolates people and keeps them from gathering together. When we are overwhelmed, we often withdraw from community and worship. Yet gathering together is essential for faith. Hebrews 10 says, Not forsaking the assembling of ourselves together… but exhorting one another. Notice also that they settled for stubble instead of straw. Hardship can push us to accept less than God intends. But God would soon gather His scattered people and lead them out together. Jesus said in Matthew 12, he that gathereth not with me scattereth abroad. Today, resist the scattering. Stay connected to God's people even when you are overwhelmed.

== 5:13
T: Fulfil your daily tasks
R: 'And the taskmasters hasted them, saying, Fulfil your works, your daily tasks, as when there was straw.' The taskmasters pushed the people relentlessly. Finish your daily quota, just as when you had straw. The pressure was constant and unreasonable. There was no grace for the new conditions. This describes the experience of many people. Demands increase while support decreases. Bosses, systems, or even our own expectations push us to perform as if nothing has changed. The voice of the taskmaster is always hurrying: faster, more, no excuses. God's voice is very different. He is patient and gentle with His people. Psalm 103 says, he knoweth our frame; he remembereth that we are dust. Jesus said, my yoke is easy, and my burden is light. If you hear an inner voice driving you without mercy, ask whether it is God's voice or a taskmaster's. God disciplines in love, but He does not drive us like slaves. Today, receive God's gentle yoke instead of the taskmaster's whip.

T: Not delivered at all
R: 'For since I came to Pharaoh to speak in thy name, he hath done evil to this people; neither hast thou delivered thy people at all.' Moses continued his complaint. Since I spoke to Pharaoh in Your name, things have gotten worse, and You have not delivered Your people at all. Moses expected quick results. Instead, he saw only suffering. He felt that God had not kept His word. Moses' frustration came from impatience with God's timing. God had warned that Pharaoh would resist. But in the moment, the delay felt like failure. We often judge God's work too soon. We see the early chapters and assume the story is over. Yet the very next verse begins with God's answer: Now shalt thou see what I will do to Pharaoh. The delay was not abandonment; it was preparation for a greater display of power. Second Peter 3 says, The Lord is not slack concerning his promise. Today, do not judge God's work by its first chapter. Delay is not denial. He is still working.
