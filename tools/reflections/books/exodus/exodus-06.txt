# Exodus 6: God renews His promise; the family of Moses and Aaron

== 6:1
T: Now shalt thou see
R: 'Then the LORD said unto Moses, Now shalt thou see what I will do to Pharaoh: for with a strong hand shall he let them go, and with a strong hand shall he drive them out of his land.' God did not rebuke Moses for his honest complaint. He answered with a promise: Now you will see what I will do. Pharaoh would not merely let Israel go; he would drive them out. Notice the word now. Moses' discouragement was the setting for God's action. When human effort had failed and hope seemed lost, God stepped forward. Our lowest moments often come right before God's greatest works. The strong hand is God's hand. Pharaoh would release Israel because God's power would compel him. Deuteronomy 26 says, the LORD brought us forth out of Egypt with a mighty hand. Today, if you feel that nothing is changing, listen to God's word to Moses. Now you will see. He is about to act.
S: The LORD answered Moses' complaint by promising, Now shalt thou see what I will do to Pharaoh, who would let Israel go and even drive them out. Moses' lowest moment came just before God's great work. If nothing seems to change, trust that God is about to act.
P: Now you will see. Exodus 6:1: 'Now shalt thou see what I will do to Pharaoh.' Your lowest moment may come right before God's greatest work. Keep trusting.

== 6:2
T: I am the LORD
R: 'And God spake unto Moses, and said unto him, I am the LORD.' God began His answer with a simple, profound declaration: I am the LORD. Before giving further promises, God reminded Moses who He is. Everything else rests on this foundation. When we are discouraged, we need more than new plans. We need a fresh vision of God. Moses had been focusing on Pharaoh's power and the people's anger. God redirected his attention to Himself. I am the LORD means I am the self-existent, covenant-keeping God. I do not change. I keep My word. This phrase appears again and again in this chapter, like a drumbeat. Isaiah 45 says, I am the LORD, and there is none else. When everything else shakes, this truth stands firm. Our circumstances do not define God; God defines our circumstances. Today, in whatever discouragement you face, hear God's voice saying, I am the LORD. Let that truth anchor your heart.
S: God spoke to Moses and said, I am the LORD. Before giving more promises, He reminded the discouraged Moses who He is. Let the truth that the LORD does not change anchor your heart today.
P: I am the LORD. Exodus 6:2. When discouraged, we need a fresh vision of God, not just new plans. He is unchanging and faithful. Let that anchor you today.

== 6:3
T: Known by name
R: 'And I appeared unto Abraham, unto Isaac, and unto Jacob, by the name of God Almighty, but by my name JEHOVAH was I not known to them.' God explained that the patriarchs knew Him as God Almighty. But they had not known Him fully by His name JEHOVAH, the LORD. The patriarchs used the name, but they had not experienced its full meaning. The name LORD speaks of God as the covenant-keeper who acts to fulfill His promises. Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob received promises. Moses' generation would see those promises fulfilled. They would know God by experience. There is a difference between knowing about God and knowing Him through experience. The patriarchs trusted God for what they did not see. Israel would now witness God's faithfulness in action. Philippians 3 records Paul's longing: That I may know him, and the power of his resurrection. Today, ask God to make Himself known to you, not just in words but in experience. He wants to be known.
S: God told Moses that He appeared to the patriarchs as God Almighty but was not fully known to them by His name JEHOVAH. Israel would now experience Him as the LORD who keeps His promises. Ask God to make Himself known to you by experience, not only in words.
P: Knowing God by experience. Exodus 6:3: 'by my name JEHOVAH was I not known to them.' Israel would see promises fulfilled. Ask God to reveal Himself to you today.

== 6:4
T: The land of their pilgrimage
R: 'And I have also established my covenant with them, to give them the land of Canaan, the land of their pilgrimage, wherein they were strangers.' God reminded Moses of His covenant to give Canaan to the patriarchs. He called it the land of their pilgrimage, the place where they had lived as strangers. They had walked the land but never owned it. Now God was ready to give it to their descendants. God's covenant is established. It is fixed and certain. The patriarchs died without receiving the land, yet the promise remained. What they saw as pilgrims, their children would possess as heirs. Hebrews 11 says, These all died in faith, not having received the promises. God's promises often span generations. We may not see every promise fulfilled in our lifetime. Yet God remains faithful. Our faith today may lay the foundation for blessings our children will enjoy. Today, live faithfully as a pilgrim. Trust that God's established covenant will be fulfilled, in His time.
S: God reminded Moses of His established covenant to give Canaan, the land where the patriarchs had lived as strangers. What they walked as pilgrims, their children would possess. Live faithfully as a pilgrim and trust God to fulfill His promises in His time.
P: From pilgrims to heirs. Exodus 6:4: 'to give them the land of Canaan, the land of their pilgrimage.' God's promises can span generations. Live faithfully today.

== 6:5
T: I have heard and remembered
R: 'And I have also heard the groaning of the children of Israel, whom the Egyptians keep in bondage; and I have remembered my covenant.' God repeated that He had heard Israel's groaning and remembered His covenant. These words echo chapter 2. God wanted Moses to know that nothing had changed. Even though things looked worse, God was still listening and still committed. Discouragement can make us think God has stopped listening. Moses had just complained that God had not delivered His people at all. God's answer was gentle: I have heard. I have remembered. God's faithfulness does not depend on visible progress. His covenant is not weakened by delays. Psalm 105 says, He hath remembered his covenant for ever. When things get worse, God's attention has not wandered. He hears every groan, even the ones we cannot put into words. Today, if circumstances seem to contradict God's promises, hold on to this. God has heard you. He has remembered. His commitment to you has not changed.
S: God told Moses that He had heard Israel's groaning in bondage and remembered His covenant. Though things looked worse, God was still listening and committed. Hold on when circumstances seem to contradict God's promises, for He has heard and remembered you.

== 6:6
T: I will redeem you
R: 'Wherefore say unto the children of Israel, I am the LORD, and I will bring you out from under the burdens of the Egyptians, and I will rid you out of their bondage, and I will redeem you with a stretched out arm, and with great judgments.' God gave Moses a message filled with I will promises. I will bring you out. I will rid you of bondage. I will redeem you. This is the first time God speaks of redeeming Israel. A redeemer was a family member who paid to rescue a relative from slavery. God presented Himself as Israel's kinsman Redeemer. The stretched out arm speaks of God's power in action. This points to Christ. Galatians 4 says God sent forth his Son… To redeem them that were under the law. First Peter 1 says we were redeemed with the precious blood of Christ. God Himself paid the price for our freedom. Today, rejoice in God's I will promises. He has redeemed you with His own arm.

== 6:7
T: My people, your God
R: 'And I will take you to me for a people, and I will be to you a God: and ye shall know that I am the LORD your God, which bringeth you out from under the burdens of the Egyptians.' God continued: I will take you as My people, and I will be your God. This is the heart of the covenant. Deliverance from Egypt was not the final goal. The goal was relationship. God wanted a people who belonged to Him. This phrase echoes through Scripture. Jeremiah 31, Ezekiel 36, and Revelation 21 repeat it. Revelation 21 says, they shall be his people, and God himself shall be with them, and be their God. The whole story of the Bible moves toward this relationship. Salvation is not just rescue from something. It is adoption into a family. Notice also: ye shall know. Israel would come to know God personally through His acts. Today, rejoice that you are God's and He is yours. He has brought you out to bring you to Himself.

== 6:8
T: An heritage
R: 'And I will bring you in unto the land, concerning the which I did swear to give it to Abraham, to Isaac, and to Jacob; and I will give it you for an heritage: I am the LORD.' God completed His promises: I will bring you into the land I swore to give your fathers. I will give it to you as an inheritance. I am the LORD. God not only brings us out; He brings us in. Notice the pattern of verses 6 through 8. God would bring out, rid, redeem, take, and bring in. God does not leave us in the wilderness. He leads us to our inheritance. First Peter 1 speaks of an inheritance incorruptible, and undefiled, and that fadeth not away. Our inheritance in Christ is secure. And it all rests on the final phrase: I am the LORD. His character guarantees every promise. Today, trust that God who brought you out will also bring you in. Your inheritance is secure because He is the LORD.
S: God promised to bring Israel into the land He swore to the patriarchs and give it to them as a heritage, ending with I am the LORD. He not only brings His people out but brings them in. Trust that your inheritance is secure because of who God is.

== 6:9
T: Anguish of spirit
R: 'And Moses spake so unto the children of Israel: but they hearkened not unto Moses for anguish of spirit, and for cruel bondage.' Moses delivered God's beautiful promises, but the people would not listen. Their spirits were crushed, and their bondage was cruel. They were too broken to receive hope. This is honest and compassionate. Scripture does not condemn Israel here. It explains why they could not listen: anguish of spirit and cruel bondage. Deep suffering can make it hard to believe good news. When we are exhausted and broken, even true promises can sound empty. Proverbs 18 says, a wounded spirit who can bear? Notice that God did not give up on them. Their unbelief did not stop His plan. He would deliver them anyway and let His actions speak where words could not reach. Today, be patient with those too hurt to hear hope. And if you are that person, know that God's promises do not depend on your strength to believe them.
S: Moses told the people God's promises, but they did not listen because of anguish of spirit and cruel bondage. Deep suffering made hope hard to receive, yet God did not give up on them. Be patient with those too hurt to hear hope, for God's promises do not depend on our strength to believe.

He did not abandon His servant in discouragement. He gave fresh direction. God's word comes again and again. He does not give up on us after one failure. Lamentations 3 says, his compassions fail not. They are new every morning. When we feel that our efforts have been wasted, God speaks again. He renews our assignment and our strength. Isaiah 50 says, he wakeneth morning by morning, he wakeneth mine ear to hear as the learned. Today, if you feel discouraged by rejection, listen for God's voice again. He is still speaking to you. Nothing had changed in the message. Moses was sent to repeat what had already been rejected. This is hard. Moses had been rejected by Pharaoh and by his own people. Now he was sent back to the same king with the same demand. Faithfulness sometimes means repeating the same obedience without visible results. God was not looking for a new strategy. He was calling Moses to persevere. Galatians 6 says, let us not be weary in well doing: for in due season we shall reap, if we faint not. Many breakthroughs come after repeated faithfulness. The tenth plague came after nine refusals. Today, if God has called you to keep doing something that has not yet borne fruit, go in again. Repeat your faithful obedience. The season of reaping will come.

== 6:12
T: Uncircumcised lips
R: 'And Moses spake before the LORD, saying, Behold, the children of Israel have not hearkened unto me; how then shall Pharaoh hear me, who am of uncircumcised lips?' Moses objected again. If Israel will not listen to me, why would Pharaoh? I have uncircumcised lips. He meant that his speech was unskilled and unfit. Moses was drawing a logical conclusion from his failures. Moses' reasoning made sense on the surface. But it left God out of the equation. Success did not depend on Moses' eloquence or the people's response. It depended on God's power. Moses kept looking at himself rather than at God. Discouragement often sounds logical. It counts past failures and predicts future ones. But faith remembers that God is not limited by our track record. Second Corinthians 3 says, our sufficiency is of God. Paul himself said his speech was contemptible to some, yet God used him powerfully. Today, do not let past failures predict your future. Your sufficiency is from God.
S: Moses objected that if Israel would not listen, Pharaoh certainly would not hear a man of uncircumcised lips. His logic counted past failures but left God out. Do not let past failures predict your future, for your sufficiency is from God.

== 6:13

God did not argue further. He gave Moses and Aaron a charge, a formal command. They were responsible to Israel and to Pharaoh, and their mission was to bring Israel out of Egypt. God answered Moses' objections with renewed authority. Sometimes God does not debate our doubts. He simply restates our calling. Our assignments from God do not depend on how confident we feel. A charge is a solemn responsibility. Paul gave Timothy a similar charge in Second Timothy 4: I charge thee therefore before God… Preach the word. Notice that God gave the charge to both Moses and Aaron. They would carry it together. Today, receive God's charge on your life. Do not wait for confidence. Obey the calling He has given, and carry it with others.

== 6:14
T: Reuben's families
R: 'These be the heads of their fathers' houses: The sons of Reuben the firstborn of Israel; Hanoch, and Pallu, Hezron, and Carmi: these be the families of Reuben.' The narrative pauses for a genealogy. It begins with Reuben, Jacob's firstborn, and lists his sons. These were real families with real histories. Exodus is grounded in actual people, not myths. Why place a genealogy here, in the middle of a dramatic story? It introduces Moses and Aaron properly. Before they confront Pharaoh, Scripture shows where they come from. Their authority is rooted in a real family within Israel. Genealogies remind us that God works through generations. Each name represents a household that God remembered in bondage. Matthew 1 begins the story of Jesus the same way, with a genealogy. God's salvation unfolds in real history through real families. Today, remember that you are part of a story larger than yourself. God works through families and generations, and He knows each name.

== 6:16
T: The sons of Levi
R: 'And these are the names of the sons of Levi according to their generations; Gershon, and Kohath, and Merari: and the years of the life of Levi were an hundred thirty and seven years.' The genealogy now slows down and focuses on Levi, the tribe of Moses and Aaron. Levi had three sons: Gershon, Kohath, and Merari. Levi lived one hundred thirty-seven years. This tribe would become Israel's priestly tribe. Remember Jacob's words about Levi in Genesis 49. Levi had been rebuked for violence and told his descendants would be scattered. Yet God redeemed that scattering. Levi's descendants would serve throughout Israel as priests and caretakers of the tabernacle. The sons named here, Gershon, Kohath, and Merari, would each have specific duties in carrying the tabernacle. Numbers 3 describes their roles. God gave each family a place of service. Today, remember that God can redeem a troubled past. A tribe once known for cruelty became the tribe set apart for worship.

Amram and Jochebed's greatest legacy was their children: Aaron, Miriam, and Moses. Their faith in a dark time produced a priest, a prophetess, and a deliverer. == 6:21
T: Korah's beginning
R: 'And the sons of Izhar; Korah, and Nepheg, and Zichri.' Among Izhar's sons is Korah. Later, in Numbers 16, Korah would lead a rebellion against Moses and Aaron. He was their own cousin, from the same family line. Yet he would challenge their God-given authority and perish. Being close to godly leaders does not guarantee a godly heart. Korah grew up in the same family as Moses. He shared the same heritage and privileges. Yet envy and pride led him astray. Proximity to holiness is not the same as holiness. Yet there is grace even here. Numbers 26 says, the children of Korah died not. His descendants became worship leaders, and the sons of Korah wrote several psalms, including Psalm 42: As the hart panteth after the water brooks, so panteth my soul after thee, O God. God redeemed Korah's line. Today, guard your heart against pride and envy. And remember that God can redeem a family's story, even after a parent's failure.

== 6:23
T: Aaron's household
R: 'And Aaron took him Elisheba, daughter of Amminadab, sister of Naashon, to wife; and she bare him Nadab, and Abihu, Eleazar, and Ithamar.' Aaron married Elisheba, the daughter of Amminadab and sister of Nahshon. Nahshon was a leader of the tribe of Judah. So Aaron's marriage joined the priestly tribe of Levi with the royal tribe of Judah. Interestingly, Nahshon appears in the genealogy of Jesus in Matthew 1. Aaron and Elisheba had four sons. Nadab and Abihu would later die for offering strange fire. Eleazar would succeed Aaron as high priest, and Ithamar would also serve faithfully. The same household produced both tragedy and faithfulness. Children raised in the same home can choose very different paths. Each person must answer to God personally. Ezekiel 18 says, The soul that sinneth, it shall die. Yet faithful parents still matter. Aaron's priestly line continued through Eleazar and Ithamar. Today, pray for the children in your family by name. Each one must choose God personally.
S: Aaron married Elisheba, sister of Nahshon of Judah, and she bore Nadab, Abihu, Eleazar and Ithamar. From one household came both tragedy and faithful priests. Pray for the children in your family by name, for each must choose God personally.

== 6:24
T: The sons of Korah
R: 'And the sons of Korah; Assir, and Elkanah, and Abiasaph: these are the families of the Korhites.' Korah's sons are named: Assir, Elkanah, and Abiasaph. These are the families of the Korhites. Though Korah himself would rebel and die, these sons would survive and carry on a legacy of worship. Their line would later include the prophet Samuel. First Chronicles 6 traces Samuel's ancestry back through these Korahite names. And the sons of Korah would serve as singers in the temple, writing psalms such as Psalm 84: How amiable are thy tabernacles, O LORD of hosts! A father's failure does not determine a child's destiny. These sons chose a different path. They did not join their father's rebellion, and God blessed their descendants. Each generation can choose to follow God. Today, if you come from a broken family story, take heart. Your choices matter. You can choose worship over rebellion, and God can make your line a blessing.
S: The sons of Korah are named, the families of the Korhites. Though Korah rebelled, his descendants became temple singers and included the prophet Samuel. If you come from a broken family story, take heart, for your choices matter and God can make your line a blessing.
P: Your father's failure isn't your destiny. Exodus 6:24: 'And the sons of Korah.' They chose worship, not rebellion. God can make your family line a blessing.

== 6:25
T: Phinehas
R: 'And Eleazar Aaron's son took him one of the daughters of Putiel to wife; and she bare him Phinehas: these are the heads of the fathers of the Levites according to their families.' Eleazar, Aaron's son, married a daughter of Putiel, and she bore Phinehas. Phinehas would later become known for his zeal for God. In Numbers 25, he stood against sin in the camp, and God gave him a covenant of an everlasting priesthood. The genealogy reaches three generations: Aaron, Eleazar, and Phinehas. God was building a priestly line that would serve Him faithfully. Psalm 106 says of Phinehas, that was counted unto him for righteousness unto all generations for evermore. Zeal for God's holiness is a godly trait. Phinehas cared deeply about God's honor. Yet zeal must be guided by God, not by personal anger. John 2 records Jesus' disciples remembering, The zeal of thine house hath eaten me up. Today, ask God for zeal that honors Him. Let your passion for His holiness be guided by His Spirit and His word.
S: Eleazar married a daughter of Putiel, and she bore Phinehas, later known for his zeal for God. The priestly line reached three generations of faithful service. Ask God for a godly zeal for His honor, guided by His Spirit and His word.
P: Zeal for God. Exodus 6:25: 'she bare him Phinehas.' Phinehas became known for zeal for God's holiness. Ask God for a passion that honors Him today.

== 6:26
T: These are that Aaron and Moses
R: 'These are that Aaron and Moses, to whom the LORD said, Bring out the children of Israel from the land of Egypt according to their armies.' The genealogy concludes by pointing back to its purpose: These are the Aaron and Moses whom the LORD commanded to bring Israel out of Egypt. After listing many names, Scripture spotlights the two men God chose. Moses and Aaron came from an ordinary family line with both faithful and failing members. They were not chosen because of a perfect pedigree. They were chosen because God called them. Their authority came from the LORD's command. Notice the phrase according to their armies. Israel would leave Egypt not as a mob of escaping slaves but as an organized people, like an army. God brings order and dignity to His people. First Corinthians 14 says, Let all things be done decently and in order. Today, remember that your calling comes from God, not from your background. He calls ordinary people and gives them His authority.
S: The genealogy concludes by naming Aaron and Moses as the ones the LORD told to bring Israel out of Egypt according to their armies. They were chosen not for a perfect pedigree but because God called them. Remember that your calling comes from God, not from your background.
P: Called by God. Exodus 6:26: 'These are that Aaron and Moses, to whom the LORD said, Bring out the children of Israel.' God calls ordinary people. Your background doesn't limit Him.

== 6:27
T: These are they which spake
R: 'These are they which spake to Pharaoh king of Egypt, to bring out the children of Israel from Egypt: these are that Moses and Aaron.' The text repeats it: these are the men who spoke to Pharaoh, these are Moses and Aaron. The emphasis underscores their role. Two ordinary brothers stood before the king of Egypt and spoke God's word. The repetition also humbles human pride. Pharaoh was a mighty king, yet Scripture spends more words identifying Moses and Aaron than Pharaoh. God's servants, not earthly rulers, are the focus of the story. Faithful speakers of God's word matter in God's eyes. Moses and Aaron were known as those who spoke. Their mission was to carry God's message into the halls of power. Acts 4 records that the rulers marked Peter and John, that they had been with Jesus. Today, consider what you are known for. Be known as someone who speaks God's truth faithfully, even before the powerful.

== 6:28
T: On the day the LORD spoke
R: 'And it came to pass on the day when the LORD spake unto Moses in the land of Egypt.' After the genealogy, the story resumes. The writer returns to the day when the LORD spoke to Moses in Egypt. It is a reminder that God's word came to Moses not only in the wilderness but in the land of bondage itself. God speaks in hard places. The burning bush was in the quiet desert. But here, God spoke in Egypt, in the land of oppression and opposition. His voice is not limited to peaceful retreats. Some of God's clearest words come to us in our most difficult circumstances. Joseph heard God in prison. Daniel heard God in Babylon. John received the Revelation as an exile on Patmos. Psalm 139 says, If I make my bed in hell, behold, thou art there. Today, listen for God's voice right where you are. He does not wait for your circumstances to improve before He speaks. He meets you in your Egypt.
S: The story resumes on the day the LORD spoke to Moses in the land of Egypt. God's word came not only in the quiet desert but in the place of bondage. Listen for God right where you are, for He speaks in hard places too.

== 6:29
T: Speak all that I say
R: 'That the LORD spake unto Moses, saying, I am the LORD: speak thou unto Pharaoh king of Egypt all that I say unto thee.' God again said, I am the LORD, and commanded Moses to speak to Pharaoh all that He said. Moses' job was not to invent a message but to faithfully deliver God's words, all of them. The command emphasizes completeness: all that I say. Messengers are tempted to soften hard words or leave out uncomfortable parts. But Moses was to speak everything God commanded, without editing. Faithful messengers must resist the temptation to adjust God's word to their audience. Acts 20 records Paul saying, I have not shunned to declare unto you all the counsel of God. Jeremiah 1 says, whatsoever I command thee thou shalt speak. And again the message rests on I am the LORD. Moses' confidence came from God's identity, not his own. Today, be faithful with God's word. Share it fully and honestly, trusting that the LORD stands behind it.
S: The LORD said again, I am the LORD, and commanded Moses to speak to Pharaoh all that He said. Moses was to deliver God's message completely without softening it. Be faithful with God's word, sharing it fully and trusting the LORD who stands behind it.
P: All that I say. Exodus 6:29: 'speak thou unto Pharaoh king of Egypt all that I say unto thee.' Don't edit God's word. Share it fully and honestly today.

== 6:30
T: How shall Pharaoh hearken?
R: 'And Moses said before the LORD, Behold, I am of uncircumcised lips, and how shall Pharaoh hearken unto me?' Moses repeated his objection: I am of uncircumcised lips. How will Pharaoh listen to me? The chapter ends with Moses still struggling with his sense of inadequacy. God's answer will come in the next chapter. Moses' repeated doubt is very human. Even after encounters with God, signs, and promises, he still wrestled with insecurity. Faith is not always a straight line. We grow, then doubt, then grow again. Moses would become one of the greatest leaders in history. Yet here he struggled. God was patient with him. He did not replace Moses for doubting. Hebrews 3 says, Moses verily was faithful in all his house. The man who doubted became the man known for faithfulness. Today, if you keep wrestling with the same insecurity, do not despair. God is patient. He is still shaping you. Your doubts do not disqualify you from becoming faithful.
S: Moses repeated before the LORD that he was of uncircumcised lips and asked how Pharaoh would listen. Even after many signs, he still wrestled with insecurity, and God remained patient. Do not despair over repeated doubts, for God is still shaping you into a faithful servant.
P: Still wrestling. Exodus 6:30: 'Behold, I am of uncircumcised lips, and how shall Pharaoh hearken unto me?' Moses doubted again, yet became faithful. God is patient with you too.
