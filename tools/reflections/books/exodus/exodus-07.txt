# Exodus 7: Aaron's rod; the river turned to blood

== 7:1
T: Made as God to Pharaoh
R: 'And the LORD said unto Moses, See, I have made thee a god to Pharaoh: and Aaron thy brother shall be thy prophet.' God answered Moses' fear with a stunning statement: I have made you as God to Pharaoh. Moses would speak with divine authority. Pharaoh, who claimed to be a god, would face a man carrying the authority of the true God. Aaron would be Moses' prophet, his spokesman. Moses felt weak, but God clothed him with authority. Our sense of inadequacy does not limit the authority God gives. When God sends us, we go in His name. Pharaoh was not truly dealing with Moses. He was dealing with the God Moses represented. Jesus said in Luke 10, He that heareth you heareth me. When believers speak God's truth, they carry His authority, not their own. Today, do not underestimate the authority God gives you. You represent Him. Speak and act as one sent by the living God.
S: God told Moses that He had made him as God to Pharaoh, with Aaron as his prophet. Though Moses felt weak, he would carry divine authority before a king who claimed to be a god. Remember that when God sends you, you go with His authority, not your own.
P: Clothed with authority. Exodus 7:1: 'See, I have made thee a god to Pharaoh.' Moses felt weak, but God gave him authority. You represent the living God today.

== 7:2
T: Speak all I command
R: 'Thou shalt speak all that I command thee: and Aaron thy brother shall speak unto Pharaoh, that he send the children of Israel out of his land.' God laid out the order. Moses would speak all that God commanded. Aaron would deliver it to Pharaoh. The goal was clear: that Pharaoh would send Israel out of his land. The chain of communication was simple. God speaks; Moses receives; Aaron proclaims. Each had a role, but the message originated with God. No one was free to add personal opinions. Faithful ministry begins with listening. Moses had to hear from God before he could pass on anything. Many people want to speak for God without first listening to Him. Jesus said in John 12, I have not spoken of myself; but the Father which sent me, he gave me a commandment, what I should say. Today, listen before you speak. Let God's word shape your message. Then share it faithfully, without adding or subtracting.
S: God told Moses to speak all He commanded, and Aaron would speak to Pharaoh to send Israel out. The message began with God and passed faithfully through His servants. Listen to God before you speak, and share His word faithfully.
P: Listen before you speak. Exodus 7:2: 'Thou shalt speak all that I command thee.' Faithful ministry starts with hearing from God. Let His word shape your message today.

== 7:3
T: Signs multiplied
R: 'And I will harden Pharaoh's heart, and multiply my signs and my wonders in the land of Egypt.' God told Moses again that Pharaoh's heart would be hardened. But Pharaoh's resistance would not defeat God's plan. Instead, it would become the occasion for God to multiply His signs and wonders in Egypt. Human resistance becomes a stage for God's glory. The harder Pharaoh resisted, the more clearly God's power would be displayed. Each refusal led to another sign. What looked like obstacles became opportunities for God to reveal Himself. Romans 9 quotes God's word to Pharaoh: that I might shew my power in thee. God is not threatened by opposition. He uses it. Sometimes God allows resistance to continue so that His deliverance will be unmistakable. When it finally comes, no one can doubt who did it. Today, if you face stubborn resistance, remember that God may be setting the stage for a greater display of His power. Watch for His wonders.
S: God said He would harden Pharaoh's heart and multiply His signs and wonders in Egypt. Pharaoh's resistance would become the stage for God's power. When you face stubborn resistance, watch for God to display His power even more clearly.
P: Resistance becomes a stage. Exodus 7:3: 'I will… multiply my signs and my wonders in the land of Egypt.' God uses opposition to display His power. Watch for His wonders.

== 7:4
T: Mine armies
R: 'But Pharaoh shall not hearken unto you, that I may lay my hand upon Egypt, and bring forth mine armies, and my people the children of Israel, out of the land of Egypt by great judgments.' God said Pharaoh would not listen. Then God would lay His hand on Egypt and bring out His armies, His people, by great judgments. Notice that God called the enslaved Israelites mine armies. To Pharaoh, they were slaves. To God, they were His hosts. This is a powerful reversal. Israel saw themselves as broken and enslaved. God saw them as His organized, dignified people. God's view of us is often very different from how we see ourselves or how the world sees us. First Peter 2 calls believers a chosen generation, a royal priesthood. Today, see yourself as God sees you. You are not defined by your bondage or your past. You are part of His people, His army.
S: God said Pharaoh would not listen, so He would lay His hand on Egypt and bring out His armies, His people, by great judgments. To Pharaoh they were slaves, but God called them His armies. See yourself as God sees you, not as your bondage or past defines you.
P: Slaves to Pharaoh, armies to God. Exodus 7:4: 'bring forth mine armies, and my people the children of Israel.' God sees you differently than the world does. You are His.

God explained a purpose of the plagues: The Egyptians shall know that I am the LORD. Pharaoh had asked, Who is the LORD? Now God would answer him. Even the Egyptians would come to know who the true God is. God's acts are meant to reveal Himself, even to outsiders. The exodus was not only for Israel. God's saving work is a witness to the watching world. When God delivers His people, others take notice. Psalm 126 says, Then said they among the heathen, The LORD hath done great things for them. Today, let God's work in your life be a testimony. Others are watching, and God wants them to know Him too.

== 7:6
T: So did they
R: 'And Moses and Aaron did as the LORD commanded them, so did they.' After all his objections, Moses obeyed. Moses and Aaron did exactly as the LORD commanded. The verse emphasizes it twice: they did as commanded; so did they. The time for arguing was over. The time for obedience had come. This simple verse marks a turning point. Moses had questioned, doubted, and objected. But in the end, he obeyed. God is patient with our struggles, but He calls us to eventual obedience. Real faith is not the absence of questions but the decision to obey despite them. Jesus told a parable in Matthew 21 about two sons. One said he would not go to work, but afterward repented and went. Obedience after struggle still counts. Moses' later reputation was built on verses like this one. Hebrews 3 says Moses was faithful in all his house. Today, whatever you have been wrestling with, move toward obedience. Questions can be honest, but the goal is to do as the LORD commands.
S: After all his objections, Moses and Aaron did as the LORD commanded them. The time for arguing gave way to obedience. Whatever you have been wrestling with, move toward obeying what God has commanded.

Their greatest work began at an age when most people expect to retire. God was just getting started with them. God is not limited by age. He goes before us and gives us what we need for each step. Notice that God spoke to both Moses and Aaron. They received the same instructions so they could work in unity. Partnership works best when everyone hears the same direction. This short verse also reminds us of the importance of listening before acting. Moses and Aaron did not rush into Pharaoh's court with their own plans. They waited for God's word. Psalm 32 says, I will instruct thee and teach thee in the way which thou shalt go. Today, before facing a challenge, take time to hear from God. Let Him prepare you for what lies ahead.

== 7:11
T: Counterfeit power
R: 'Then Pharaoh also called the wise men and the sorcerers: now the magicians of Egypt, they also did in like manner with their enchantments.' Pharaoh summoned his wise men and sorcerers. The magicians of Egypt imitated the sign with their secret arts. Their rods also seemed to become serpents. Pharaoh found a way to dismiss God's sign. Evil can imitate the works of God. Not every supernatural sign comes from Him. Second Timothy 3 names two of these magicians, Jannes and Jambres, as examples of those who resist the truth. Second Thessalonians 2 warns of lying wonders. Counterfeits exist to confuse and to give people excuses not to believe. Pharaoh was happy to find an alternative explanation. People who do not want to believe will grasp at any reason to dismiss God. Discernment is essential. First John 4 says, try the spirits whether they are of God. Today, do not be impressed by every display of power. Test everything by God's word. Counterfeits may look similar, but they are not the same.
S: Pharaoh called his wise men and sorcerers, and the magicians imitated the sign with their enchantments. Evil can counterfeit God's works to give people excuses not to believe. Test every display of power by God's word and do not be misled by imitations.

Evil may seem powerful for a time. It may mimic God's works and confuse people. But in the end, God's power swallows up every rival. == 7:23
T: He did not set his heart
R: 'And Pharaoh turned and went into his house, neither did he set his heart to this also.' Pharaoh simply turned and went home. He did not take the plague to heart. While his nation suffered, he walked away and ignored it. His indifference was as striking as his stubbornness. To set one's heart is to pay attention, to take something seriously. Pharaoh refused to consider what God was saying. He shut the door on the message. Indifference is a quiet but powerful form of rebellion. Many people today do not openly oppose God. They simply do not set their hearts to Him. They walk away and go on with life. Hebrews 2 asks, How shall we escape, if we neglect so great salvation? Neglect can be as dangerous as defiance. Haggai 1 says, Consider your ways. God calls us to stop and pay attention. Today, do not walk away from what God is showing you. Set your heart to it. Take His word seriously and respond.
S: Pharaoh turned and went into his house and did not set his heart to the plague. While his nation suffered, he walked away in indifference. Do not walk away from what God is showing you, but set your heart to it and respond.
P: Don't walk away. Exodus 7:23: 'Pharaoh turned and went into his house, neither did he set his heart to this.' Indifference is quiet rebellion. Set your heart to God's word today.

== 7:24
T: Digging for water
R: 'And all the Egyptians digged round about the river for water to drink; for they could not drink of the water of the river.' The Egyptians dug along the riverbanks, searching for drinkable water. The people suffered while their king ignored the crisis. They labored to find what they had once taken for granted. Ordinary people often bear the cost of their leaders' pride. The Egyptians had not directly refused God, yet they suffered the consequences of Pharaoh's choices. Sin rarely affects only the sinner. Its effects ripple outward to families, communities, and nations. When our usual sources fail, we are forced to search. Sometimes hardship drives us to seek what we truly need. Isaiah 55 says, Ho, every one that thirsteth, come ye to the waters. God offers living water freely. Psalm 63 says, my soul thirsteth for thee… in a dry and thirsty land, where no water is. Today, if you are digging for something to satisfy your thirst, turn to God. He alone can satisfy.
S: All the Egyptians dug around the river for water because they could not drink from it. Ordinary people bore the cost of their king's pride, laboring for what they once took for granted. When your usual sources fail, turn to God, who alone can satisfy your thirst.
P: Digging for water. Exodus 7:24: 'all the Egyptians digged round about the river for water to drink.' When usual sources fail, come to the One who gives living water.

== 7:25
T: Seven days
R: 'And seven days were fulfilled, after that the LORD had smitten the river.' Seven days passed after the LORD struck the river. A full week of suffering went by, and still Pharaoh did not relent. The plague lasted long enough to make its point clearly. Notice that the verse says the LORD had smitten the river. Though Aaron lifted the rod, the plague was God's act. Scripture makes clear who was really at work. Seven days also gave Pharaoh time to reflect and repent. God did not rush to the next plague. He gave space for a change of heart. But Pharaoh did not use that time well. God often gives us time between His warnings. Those times are gifts, opportunities to turn. Second Peter 3 says God is longsuffering to us-ward, not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance. Today, consider how you are using the time God gives you. Do not waste His patience. Use it to turn your heart toward Him.
S: Seven days were fulfilled after the LORD smote the river, yet Pharaoh did not relent. God gave a full week for reflection before the next plague. Use the time God gives you wisely, turning your heart toward Him rather than wasting His patience.
P: Time to turn. Exodus 7:25: 'seven days were fulfilled, after that the LORD had smitten the river.' God gave Pharaoh time to repent. Don't waste His patience. Turn to Him today.
