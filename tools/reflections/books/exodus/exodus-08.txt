# Exodus 8: Frogs, lice and flies

== 8:1
T: That they may serve me
R: 'And the LORD spake unto Moses, Go unto Pharaoh, and say unto him, Thus saith the LORD, Let my people go, that they may serve me.' Once again God sent Moses with the same message: Let My people go, that they may serve Me. The demand had not changed. God's purpose remained steady through every plague. Notice how often God repeats the reason for freedom: that they may serve me. Israel was not being freed to live for themselves. They were being freed to worship and serve the God who saved them. Every person serves someone. Israel would either serve Pharaoh or serve the LORD. Joshua 24 says, choose you this day whom ye will serve. Jesus said in Matthew 6, No man can serve two masters. The question is not whether we will serve but whom. Today, consider whom you are really serving. God has set you free so that you may serve Him with a willing heart.
S: God sent Moses again to tell Pharaoh, Let my people go, that they may serve me. The purpose of Israel's freedom was to serve the God who saved them. Consider whom you are really serving, for God frees you to serve Him willingly.
P: Free to serve God. Exodus 8:1: 'Thus saith the LORD, Let my people go, that they may serve me.' Everyone serves someone. Choose today to serve the LORD.

== 8:2
T: If thou refuse
R: 'And if thou refuse to let them go, behold, I will smite all thy borders with frogs.' God gave Pharaoh a clear choice and a clear warning. If you refuse, I will strike your whole territory with frogs. Pharaoh was told the consequence before it came. God's warnings are acts of mercy. He tells us what will happen so that we can choose differently. Pharaoh had the opportunity to avoid the plague entirely. The frogs would come only if he refused. God does not delight in judgment. Ezekiel 33 says, I have no pleasure in the death of the wicked; but that the wicked turn from his way and live. Every warning is an invitation to turn back. The frog was also associated with an Egyptian goddess of fertility. God would show that even Egypt's symbols of life were under His control. Today, take God's warnings seriously. They are given in love, so that you may choose the way of life.
S: God warned Pharaoh that if he refused to let Israel go, He would strike all his borders with frogs. The warning was an act of mercy, giving Pharaoh a chance to choose differently. Take God's warnings seriously, for they are given in love.
P: Warnings are mercy. Exodus 8:2: 'if thou refuse to let them go, behold, I will smite all thy borders with frogs.' God warns so we can turn. Heed His voice today.

== 8:3
T: Into thy bedchamber
R: 'And the river shall bring forth frogs abundantly, which shall go up and come into thine house, and into thy bedchamber, and upon thy bed… and into thine ovens, and into thy kneadingtroughs.' God described the plague in vivid detail. Frogs would swarm out of the river into houses, bedrooms, beds, ovens, and kneading bowls. No space would be private or protected. The frogs would invade every part of daily life. Sin works this way. When we refuse God, the consequences do not stay in one area. They spread into our homes, our rest, our work, and our relationships. Pharaoh thought he could keep God out of his palace. But the frogs came even into his bedchamber. Nothing is hidden from God. Hebrews 4 says, all things are naked and opened unto the eyes of him with whom we have to do. Yet this also means God's grace can reach every area of life. Today, invite God into every room of your life, before resistance brings unwelcome consequences.
S: God said frogs would come from the river into Pharaoh's house, bedchamber, bed, ovens and kneading troughs. No part of daily life would be protected. Invite God into every area of your life, for nothing is hidden from Him.
P: Every room of your life. Exodus 8:3: 'frogs… shall go up and come into thine house, and into thy bedchamber.' Nothing is hidden from God. Invite Him into every area today.

== 8:4
T: On thee and thy people
R: 'And the frogs shall come up both on thee, and upon thy people, and upon all thy servants.' The frogs would come upon Pharaoh himself, on his people, and on all his servants. No one would be exempt, not even the king. Rank and power offered no protection. Pharaoh had positioned himself above everyone. Yet before God, he was as vulnerable as the lowest servant. God's judgments humble the proud. Acts 10 says, God is no respecter of persons. Earthly status means nothing in His presence. The king would scrape frogs off his own body like everyone else. James 4 says, God resisteth the proud, but giveth grace unto the humble. Pride builds walls of privilege, but God can break through every wall. This is a warning to all who think their position protects them from accountability. Today, remember that before God, everyone stands on level ground. Walk humbly. Your position does not exempt you from His standards, and it does not separate you from your need for His grace.
S: God said the frogs would come upon Pharaoh, his people and all his servants. Not even the king would be exempt, for rank offers no protection before God. Walk humbly, remembering that everyone stands on level ground before Him.
P: Level ground before God. Exodus 8:4: 'the frogs shall come up both on thee, and upon thy people.' Even the king wasn't exempt. Walk humbly before God today.

== 8:5
T: Stretch forth thine hand
R: 'And the LORD spake unto Moses, Say unto Aaron, Stretch forth thine hand with thy rod over the streams, over the rivers, and over the ponds, and cause frogs to come up upon the land of Egypt.' God gave Moses the command, and Moses passed it to Aaron. Aaron was to stretch his rod over all the waters and bring frogs upon Egypt. The order of authority was clear: God to Moses, Moses to Aaron, Aaron to the waters. God works through order and delegation. Moses did not do everything himself. He involved Aaron, sharing the work. Healthy leadership delegates. Later, Jethro would advise Moses to share his burden with others. Each person had a part, but the power came from God. Aaron's outstretched hand was simply obedience. The frogs were God's doing. First Corinthians 12 says, there are diversities of operations, but it is the same God which worketh all in all. Today, do your part faithfully, and let others do theirs. Remember that the results come from God.
S: God told Moses to have Aaron stretch his rod over the waters and bring frogs upon Egypt. The work passed in order from God to Moses to Aaron, with the power coming from God. Do your part faithfully, let others do theirs, and remember that results come from God.
P: Shared work, God's power. Exodus 8:5: 'Say unto Aaron, Stretch forth thine hand with thy rod.' Each had a role; God gave the power. Do your part faithfully today.

== 8:6
T: The land was covered
R: 'And Aaron stretched out his hand over the waters of Egypt; and the frogs came up, and covered the land of Egypt.' Aaron obeyed, and frogs covered the land. What God said happened immediately. Egypt was overrun with frogs. The creatures associated with fertility and life became a smothering plague. God turned a symbol of blessing into a burden. Too much of anything, even something good, can become oppressive. The Egyptians revered the frog goddess Heqet. Now they had more frogs than they could bear. Their false god's symbol had become their torment. God's word accomplishes what He sends it to do. Isaiah 55 says, it shall accomplish that which I please, and it shall prosper in the thing whereto I sent it. When Aaron obeyed, God's word went into effect. Today, trust the power of God's word. What He speaks happens. And examine whether anything you have exalted in your life has become a burden rather than a blessing.
S: Aaron stretched his hand over the waters, and frogs came up and covered the land of Egypt. The symbol of a fertility goddess became a smothering plague. Trust the power of God's word, and examine whether anything you have exalted has become a burden.
P: God's word accomplishes its purpose. Exodus 8:6: 'the frogs came up, and covered the land of Egypt.' What God speaks happens. Trust His word today.

== 8:7
T: More frogs
R: 'And the magicians did so with their enchantments, and brought up frogs upon the land of Egypt.' The magicians again imitated the plague. They used their secret arts to bring up even more frogs. They proved they had some power, but only to make things worse. They could not remove a single frog. This is the futility of false power. It can add to the problem but never solve it. The magicians were trying to show they were equal to Moses. Instead, they deepened their own nation's misery. Worldly solutions often multiply our troubles. Sin and pride offer imitations of power that leave us worse off. Jeremiah 2 says, my people have committed two evils… forsaken me… and hewed them out cisterns, broken cisterns. False sources promise help but deliver more pain. Only God could take the frogs away, and soon Pharaoh would admit it. Today, beware of solutions that only add to your problems. Turn to the One who can truly remove what burdens you.
S: The magicians used their enchantments to bring up more frogs, proving only that they could make things worse. False power can add to problems but never remove them. Beware of solutions that multiply your troubles and turn to the God who can truly help.
P: Adding to the problem. Exodus 8:7: 'the magicians did so with their enchantments, and brought up frogs.' They couldn't remove one. Turn to God, who truly helps.

== 8:8
T: Intreat the LORD
R: 'Then Pharaoh called for Moses and Aaron, and said, Intreat the LORD, that he may take away the frogs from me, and from my people; and I will let the people go, that they may do sacrifice unto the LORD.' For the first time, Pharaoh acknowledged the LORD. He asked Moses to pray that the LORD would remove the frogs, and he promised to let Israel go. The king who once said, I know not the LORD, now asked for prayer to Him. Suffering can open closed doors in the heart. Yet his request was driven by discomfort, not repentance. He wanted relief, not relationship. Many people turn to God in crisis, only to forget Him when the crisis passes. Psalm 78 says, When he slew them, then they sought him… Nevertheless they did flatter him with their mouth. Today, examine why you seek God. Do you only call on Him for relief? Seek Him for who He is, not only for what He can remove.
S: Pharaoh called Moses and Aaron and asked them to intreat the LORD to remove the frogs, promising to let Israel go. He acknowledged the LORD for the first time, but sought relief rather than repentance. Seek God for who He is, not only for what He can remove.
P: Relief or relationship? Exodus 8:8: 'Intreat the LORD, that he may take away the frogs from me.' Pharaoh wanted relief, not God. Seek Him for who He is today.

== 8:9
T: Glory over me
R: 'And Moses said unto Pharaoh, Glory over me: when shall I intreat for thee, and for thy servants, and for thy people, to destroy the frogs from thee and thy houses, that they may remain in the river only?' Moses responded graciously. He invited Pharaoh to set the time: you have the honor of deciding when I should pray. Moses gave Pharaoh control over the timing so that when it happened exactly as requested, there would be no doubt who did it. Moses showed confidence in God. He was so sure God would answer that he let Pharaoh choose the time. This was bold faith. Moses was not hedging or making vague promises. Notice also Moses' courtesy. He did not gloat over Pharaoh's humiliation. He treated the king with respect, even offering him the honor of choosing. Romans 12 says, Recompense to no man evil for evil. Today, show grace even to those who have opposed you. And pray with confident faith, trusting that God answers.
S: Moses graciously let Pharaoh choose when he should pray for the frogs to be removed. His confidence in God was so strong that he let Pharaoh set the time, and he showed courtesy rather than gloating. Pray with confident faith and show grace even to those who oppose you.

== 8:10
T: To morrow
R: 'And he said, To morrow. And he said, Be it according to thy word: that thou mayest know that there is none like unto the LORD our God.' Pharaoh chose tomorrow. Strangely, he chose to endure one more night with the frogs. Moses agreed: It will be as you say, so that you may know there is none like the LORD our God. Why would anyone choose to wait another day for relief? Perhaps Pharaoh hoped the frogs would leave on their own so he would not have to credit God. Delay is often a sign of a heart unwilling to fully surrender. Many people say tomorrow when God offers help today. They postpone repentance, prayer, or obedience. Second Corinthians 6 says, behold, now is the accepted time; behold, now is the day of salvation. Moses' response points to God's uniqueness: there is none like unto the LORD our God. Today, do not say tomorrow to what God offers now. Respond to Him today.
S: Pharaoh chose tomorrow for the frogs to be removed, and Moses agreed so that he would know there is none like the LORD. Pharaoh chose another night of misery rather than immediate relief. Do not say tomorrow to what God offers now.

== 8:11
T: In the river only
R: 'And the frogs shall depart from thee, and from thy houses, and from thy servants, and from thy people; they shall remain in the river only.' Moses promised that the frogs would leave the houses, servants, and people. They would remain only in the river, where they belonged. God would restore order. God's judgments are measured and controlled. He sets boundaries even on His plagues. The frogs would return to their proper place. God is not chaotic. He brings order out of disorder. This also shows that God can reverse what He has done. The same God who sent the frogs could remove them. Our troubles are under His authority. They can come only as far as He allows, and they leave when He commands. Job 38 records God saying to the sea, Hitherto shalt thou come, but no further. Today, trust that God has authority over the troubles in your life. He sets their limits. And when the time comes, He can send them back to where they belong.

Keep your word, and let your prayers be fervent and sincere.

In crisis, we make promises to God. When relief comes, we forget them. Ecclesiastes 5 says, When thou vowest a vow unto God, defer not to pay it. Pharaoh's broken promise reveals that his heart had never really changed. He wanted relief, not God. Respite is a gift from God. It is meant to lead us toward gratitude and obedience, not back to rebellion. Romans 2 says the goodness of God leadeth thee to repentance. Today, remember the promises you made to God in hard times. Keep them now that relief has come. Let God's kindness lead you to deeper obedience.

Isaiah 46 says, I am God, and there is none like me. Notice the plain statement: but they could not. Human wisdom and false power run out. God's power never does. The Hebrew word can also mean redemption. God was not only separating Israel; He was redeeming them. And the sign would come tomorrow. God draws a line between those who belong to Him and those who do not. This division is not based on merit but on God's choice and redemption. Israel was protected not because they were better but because God had claimed them. Throughout Scripture, God separates His people for Himself. Second Corinthians 6 says, come out from among them, and be ye separate, saith the Lord. Jesus spoke of separating the sheep from the goats. Yet this division also has a redemptive purpose. God set Israel apart so that through them all nations would be blessed. Today, rejoice that God has redeemed you and set you apart. And live as one who belongs to Him.

== 8:24
T: The land was corrupted
R: 'And the LORD did so; and there came a grievous swarm of flies into the house of Pharaoh, and into his servants' houses, and into all the land of Egypt: the land was corrupted by reason of the swarm of flies.' The LORD did what He said. A grievous swarm of flies came into Pharaoh's palace, his servants' houses, and all Egypt. The land was ruined by them. This time, notice that Aaron did not stretch out a rod. The LORD did so. God acted directly. The word corrupted means ruined or destroyed. Flies brought disease and filth. Sin always corrupts. It ruins what God made good. Romans 8 speaks of creation groaning under the bondage of corruption. Yet God promises restoration. The same God who sent the plague would one day redeem creation itself. Today, remember that God keeps His word, both His warnings and His promises. Turn from what corrupts, and trust Him to restore.

== 8:25
T: Sacrifice in the land
R: 'And Pharaoh called for Moses and for Aaron, and said, Go ye, sacrifice to your God in the land.' Pharaoh offered a compromise. You may sacrifice to your God, but do it here, in the land of Egypt. He was willing to allow worship, as long as Israel stayed under his control. This is the first of several compromises Pharaoh would offer. Each one would allow partial obedience. The enemy often does this. He does not always demand that we stop worshiping God. He just wants us to stay in Egypt. Worship God, but do not leave the old life. Serve God, but stay under my control. This is a subtle but dangerous offer. God did not want worship mixed with bondage. He wanted His people completely free. Jesus said in Matthew 6, No man can serve two masters. Revelation 3 warns against being lukewarm. Today, beware of compromises that let you worship God while staying in Egypt. God calls you to full freedom and full devotion.
S: Pharaoh told Moses and Aaron they could sacrifice to their God, but only within Egypt. He offered worship without freedom, keeping Israel under his control. Beware of compromises that let you worship God while staying in bondage, for God calls you to full devotion.
P: Worship, but stay in Egypt? Exodus 8:25: 'Go ye, sacrifice to your God in the land.' Pharaoh offered a compromise. God calls you to full freedom and full devotion.

== 8:26
T: It is not meet
R: 'And Moses said, It is not meet so to do; for we shall sacrifice the abomination of the Egyptians to the LORD our God: lo, shall we sacrifice the abomination of the Egyptians before their eyes, and will they not stone us?' Moses refused the compromise. It is not right, he said. Our sacrifices would be offensive to the Egyptians. If we sacrifice animals they consider sacred, they will stone us. Worship in Egypt was not possible. Moses saw clearly that true worship required separation. The Egyptians held certain animals sacred. Israel's sacrifices would provoke violence. Worshiping God fully could not happen in Pharaoh's territory. Some forms of obedience are simply incompatible with remaining in the world's system. First John 2 says, Love not the world, neither the things that are in the world. True faith sometimes offends the surrounding culture. Today, recognize that full devotion to God may require separation from things that conflict with Him. Do not accept a compromise that makes true worship impossible.
S: Moses refused to sacrifice in Egypt, explaining that their offerings would be an abomination to the Egyptians and provoke violence. True worship required separation from Pharaoh's territory. Do not accept compromises that make full devotion to God impossible.

== 8:27
T: As he shall command us
R: 'We will go three days' journey into the wilderness, and sacrifice to the LORD our God, as he shall command us.' Moses restated the requirement. We will go three days into the wilderness and sacrifice as God commands. He would not negotiate God's terms. Worship would be on God's conditions, not Pharaoh's. Notice the phrase as he shall command us. Moses did not even know all the details yet. But he knew that God, not Pharaoh, would set the terms. True worship follows God's instructions. Many people want to worship God on their own terms. They decide what is acceptable, how much to give, and when to obey. But God defines how He is to be worshiped. John 4 says, God is a Spirit: and they that worship him must worship him in spirit and in truth. Moses refused to let anyone else set the terms of worship. Today, worship God as He commands, not as convenience suggests. Let His word, not the world, define your devotion.

== 8:32
T: At this time also
R: 'And Pharaoh hardened his heart at this time also, neither would he let the people go.' Once again, Pharaoh hardened his heart. At this time also, he refused to let the people go. The cycle repeated: plague, plea, prayer, relief, and then refusal. Pharaoh learned nothing from his experiences. Each hardening made the next easier. Pharaoh was establishing a pattern. His heart became more stubborn with every refusal. This is how habits of sin develop. Each time we say no to God, it becomes easier to say no again. Hebrews 3 warns, lest any of you be hardened through the deceitfulness of sin. The phrase at this time also is heartbreaking. Pharaoh had been given another chance, and he wasted it again. How many chances would he receive? God is patient, but there is a point when patterns become permanent. Today, break the cycle of refusal. If God has been speaking to you about something repeatedly, do not harden your heart again. Respond this time.
S: Pharaoh hardened his heart at this time also and would not let the people go. The cycle of plague, plea, relief and refusal repeated, and each refusal made the next easier. Break the cycle of refusal by responding to God this time.
P: Break the cycle. Exodus 8:32: 'Pharaoh hardened his heart at this time also.' Each refusal makes the next easier. If God keeps speaking to you, respond this time.
