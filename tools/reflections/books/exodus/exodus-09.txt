# Exodus 9: Murrain, boils and hail

== 9:1
T: The God of the Hebrews
R: 'Then the LORD said unto Moses, Go in unto Pharaoh, and tell him, Thus saith the LORD God of the Hebrews, Let my people go, that they may serve me.' God sent Moses back yet again with the same message. This time God identified Himself as the LORD God of the Hebrews. He tied His name to a despised, enslaved people. The God of all the earth was not ashamed to be called the God of slaves. This is the humility of God. He identifies with the lowly. Hebrews 11 says, God is not ashamed to be called their God. The world honored Pharaoh, but God aligned Himself with the oppressed. Jesus would do the same, eating with sinners and touching outcasts. God's repeated message also shows His persistence. He does not give up on His purposes. Today, rejoice that God is not ashamed to be called your God. He identifies with you, whatever your status in the world's eyes.
S: God sent Moses again to Pharaoh with the message, Thus saith the LORD God of the Hebrews, Let my people go. The God of all the earth tied His name to an enslaved people. Rejoice that God is not ashamed to be called your God.
P: Not ashamed of you. Exodus 9:1: 'Thus saith the LORD God of the Hebrews, Let my people go.' God tied His name to slaves. He's not ashamed to be called your God.

== 9:2
T: Wilt hold them still
R: 'For if thou refuse to let them go, and wilt hold them still.' God described Pharaoh's sin precisely: refusing to let go and holding them still. Pharaoh was clinging to what was not his. Israel belonged to God, but Pharaoh would not loosen his grip. Holding on to what God has claimed brings trouble. Pharaoh's stubborn grip would cost him dearly. We can do the same with things God asks us to release: a grudge, a habit, a relationship, control over our future. The longer we hold, the more it costs. Jesus said in Luke 17, Whosoever shall seek to save his life shall lose it. Letting go is often the path to life. Pharaoh thought holding Israel kept him strong. In reality, it was destroying his kingdom. What we refuse to release can end up ruling us. Today, ask God what you are holding still that He has asked you to release. Open your hands. Trust Him with what you have been clinging to.
S: God warned Pharaoh about refusing to let Israel go and holding them still. Pharaoh clung to what belonged to God, and his grip would cost him dearly. Ask God what you are holding that He has asked you to release, and open your hands.
P: Open your hands. Exodus 9:2: 'if thou refuse to let them go, and wilt hold them still.' Clinging to what God claims brings trouble. Release it to Him today.

== 9:3
T: The hand of the LORD
R: 'Behold, the hand of the LORD is upon thy cattle which is in the field, upon the horses, upon the asses, upon the camels, upon the oxen, and upon the sheep: there shall be a very grievous murrain.' God announced the fifth plague: a severe disease on Egypt's livestock. Horses, donkeys, camels, oxen, and sheep would be struck. This was Egypt's wealth, transport, and food. Egypt also worshiped gods in animal form, such as the bull Apis. God's hand reached into the heart of Egypt's economy and religion. Notice the phrase the hand of the LORD. Earlier, the magicians called the lice the finger of God. Now God's whole hand was moving. The plagues were increasing in force. Our security can be shaken quickly. Egypt's wealth was in its herds, yet they could vanish in a day. First Timothy 6 warns not to trust in uncertain riches, but in the living God. Today, remember that everything you own is under God's hand. Place your trust in Him, not in possessions that can disappear.
S: God announced a very grievous murrain on Egypt's livestock, the nation's wealth and the form of some of its gods. The hand of the LORD was now moving with increasing force. Place your trust in the living God rather than in possessions that can disappear.
P: Uncertain riches. Exodus 9:3: 'the hand of the LORD is upon thy cattle which is in the field.' Egypt's wealth could vanish in a day. Trust the living God, not possessions.

== 9:4
T: Nothing shall die
R: 'And the LORD shall sever between the cattle of Israel and the cattle of Egypt: and there shall nothing die of all that is the children's of Israel.' God promised to make a distinction again. Egypt's livestock would die, but nothing belonging to Israel would die. God's protection extended even to His people's animals. God cares about every part of His people's lives. Their livestock were their livelihood. God protected not just their bodies but their means of living. Matthew 6 says your heavenly Father knows what you need. He is concerned with the practical details of our lives. This distinction was also a clear sign. If disease struck randomly, Israel's animals would also die. But God drew a line around His people. Psalm 121 says, The LORD shall preserve thee from all evil. Today, trust that God's care reaches the practical areas of your life: your work, your provision, your home. Nothing is too small for His protection.
S: God promised to sever between the cattle of Israel and of Egypt so that nothing belonging to Israel would die. His protection extended to His people's livelihood. Trust that God's care reaches every practical area of your life.
P: God cares about the details. Exodus 9:4: 'there shall nothing die of all that is the children's of Israel.' He protects your livelihood too. Nothing is too small for His care.

== 9:5
T: A set time
R: 'And the LORD appointed a set time, saying, To morrow the LORD shall do this thing in the land.' God set a specific time: tomorrow. By naming the day, God gave Pharaoh one more opportunity to repent and removed any doubt about who caused the plague. Egyptians who believed could also bring their animals to safety. God's timing is deliberate. He appoints set times for His works. Ecclesiastes 3 says, To every thing there is a season, and a time to every purpose under the heaven. Nothing God does is random or accidental. The appointed time also showed God's control. Pharaoh's magicians could not predict or prevent the plague. Only the true God could announce in advance what He would do. Isaiah 46 says, Declaring the end from the beginning. God's prophetic word demonstrates His sovereignty. Today, trust God's timing in your life. He has appointed set times for His works. What He has planned will happen exactly when He intends.
S: The LORD appointed a set time, announcing that tomorrow He would do this thing in the land. Naming the day showed His control and gave one more opportunity to respond. Trust God's timing, for He has appointed set times for His works.

== 9:6
T: Not one died
R: 'And the LORD did that thing on the morrow, and all the cattle of Egypt died: but of the cattle of the children of Israel died not one.' On the next day, God did exactly what He said. Egypt's livestock died, but not one of Israel's animals perished. The distinction was perfect. God kept His word to the letter. When God makes a promise to protect, He keeps it fully. Not one of Israel's animals died. This precision shows that God is not careless. He knows exactly what belongs to His people. John 10 records Jesus saying of His sheep, they shall never perish, neither shall any man pluck them out of my hand. Not one is lost. God's protection is complete for those who are His. The contrast is striking. All of Egypt's cattle died; none of Israel's. The difference was not luck but God's covenant. Today, rest in the completeness of God's care. He keeps His promises precisely. Not one of those who belong to Him will be lost.

== 9:7
T: Pharaoh sent to see
R: 'And Pharaoh sent, and, behold, there was not one of the cattle of the Israelites dead. And the heart of Pharaoh was hardened, and he did not let the people go.' Pharaoh sent someone to check. Sure enough, not one Israelite animal had died. The evidence was undeniable. God had made a clear distinction. Yet Pharaoh's heart remained hard, and he did not let the people go. Pharaoh investigated, confirmed the truth, and still refused. His problem was not a lack of information. He knew what had happened. He simply would not submit. Many people examine the evidence for God and remain unmoved. Romans 1 says that God's power and divinity are clearly seen, so that they are without excuse. Knowledge without surrender does not save. James 2 says, the devils also believe, and tremble. Belief must lead to obedience. Today, if you have seen evidence of God's work, do not merely acknowledge it. Let it move you to surrender and obedience.

== 9:8
T: Ashes of the furnace
R: 'And the LORD said unto Moses and unto Aaron, Take to you handfuls of ashes of the furnace, and let Moses sprinkle it toward the heaven in the sight of Pharaoh.' God told Moses and Aaron to take handfuls of soot from a furnace and throw it into the air before Pharaoh. This plague would come without warning. The furnace was likely a kiln, perhaps the very kind Israel used to make bricks for Egypt. There is poetic justice here. The furnaces of Israel's forced labor would become the source of Egypt's suffering. Deuteronomy 4 calls Egypt the iron furnace from which God brought Israel. What had been a place of oppression now became an instrument of judgment. God sees the places of our suffering. He does not forget the furnaces in which His people have labored. Galatians 6 says, whatsoever a man soweth, that shall he also reap. Today, trust that God remembers your hard places. He sees what you have endured, and He knows how to bring justice.
S: God told Moses and Aaron to take handfuls of ashes from the furnace and sprinkle them toward heaven before Pharaoh. The furnaces of Israel's forced labor would become the source of Egypt's judgment. Trust that God remembers the hard places you have endured.
P: God remembers the furnace. Exodus 9:8: 'Take to you handfuls of ashes of the furnace.' The place of Israel's oppression became the source of judgment. God sees what you've endured.

== 9:9
T: Boils upon man and beast
R: 'And it shall become small dust in all the land of Egypt, and shall be a boil breaking forth with blains upon man, and upon beast, throughout all the land of Egypt.' The ashes would become fine dust across Egypt and cause painful boils and sores on people and animals. For the first time, the plagues would directly attack human bodies. The judgment was becoming personal. Egypt worshiped gods of healing and medicine. Yet none of them could protect against this plague. God showed that health and healing belong to Him alone. Exodus 15 would later reveal Him as I am the LORD that healeth thee. The plagues were escalating. First water, then frogs, then insects, then livestock, and now human bodies. Each refusal brought something closer. When we persist in rebellion, consequences tend to draw nearer. Proverbs 13 says, the way of transgressors is hard. Today, recognize that God holds authority over health and healing. Seek Him as your healer, and do not wait until consequences touch you personally to turn to Him.
S: The ashes would become dust across Egypt, causing boils breaking forth with sores on man and beast. For the first time, the plagues struck human bodies, showing that health belongs to God alone. Seek God as your healer and turn to Him before consequences draw closer.
P: The LORD who heals. Exodus 9:9: 'a boil breaking forth with blains upon man, and upon beast.' Egypt's healing gods were powerless. Seek the true Healer today.

Moses and Aaron stood before Pharaoh and threw the ashes toward heaven. The boils broke out immediately on people and animals. The sign was performed in Pharaoh's presence. Moses threw the ashes toward heaven, perhaps showing that the judgment came from above. It was not magic or human trickery. It came from the God of heaven. Their obedience was again immediate and complete. They took, they stood, they sprinkled. Each action followed God's command precisely. Faithfulness is often made up of simple, obedient steps. Moses did not need to understand how ashes could cause boils. He simply did what God said. Proverbs 3 says, lean not unto thine own understanding. Today, take the simple steps of obedience God asks of you. You do not need to understand everything. Trust Him, and act.

== 9:11

They were covered in boils like everyone else. The men who had opposed Moses with their secret arts were now humiliated and incapacitated. Their opposition collapsed. This is the last time the magicians appear in the plague narrative. They had imitated some plagues, failed at the lice, and now could not even stand. False power is eventually exposed. Those who oppose God may seem strong for a time, but they cannot stand forever. Psalm 1 says, the ungodly shall not stand in the judgment. Notice that the magicians suffered the same plague as everyone else. Their arts could not protect even themselves. Second Timothy 3 says of such men, their folly shall be manifest unto all men. Today, take courage. Those who oppose God's purposes will not stand. Stand firm in Him, for His people will remain when every rival falls.

== 9:12
T: The LORD hardened
R: 'And the LORD hardened the heart of Pharaoh, and he hearkened not unto them; as the LORD had spoken unto Moses.' For the first time in the plague narrative, Scripture says plainly that the LORD hardened Pharaoh's heart. Before this, Pharaoh hardened his own heart. Now, after repeated refusals, God confirmed Pharaoh in the stubbornness he had chosen. There is a sobering principle here. God gives people over to the choices they persist in making. Romans 1 says three times that God gave them up. Pharaoh had been given many chances. He refused each one. Now God sealed what Pharaoh had chosen. This should not make us fear that God arbitrarily hardens hearts. Rather, it warns us not to keep refusing Him. There may come a point when the opportunity to turn is gone. Hebrews 3 says, To day if ye will hear his voice, harden not your hearts. Today, keep your heart responsive to God. Do not play with repeated refusal. Turn to Him while His voice is still calling.
S: The LORD hardened the heart of Pharaoh, and he would not listen, as the LORD had said. After Pharaoh repeatedly hardened his own heart, God confirmed him in his chosen stubbornness. Keep your heart responsive to God and turn to Him while He is still calling.

== 9:15

He could have stretched out His hand and wiped Pharaoh and Egypt from the earth with pestilence. He had the power to end it all instantly. Yet He had not done so. This verse reveals God's restraint. He could have destroyed Egypt completely, but He held back. Every plague so far had been limited. God's judgments were measured, not because He lacked power, but because He chose mercy. Lamentations 3 says, It is of the LORD'S mercies that we are not consumed. Every day we live is a gift of God's patience. We do not receive what we deserve. Psalm 103 says, He hath not dealt with us after our sins. Today, thank God for His restrained mercy. He has the power to judge, yet He gives time and opportunity. Do not take His patience for granted.

God can use even opposition to spread His fame. Nothing, not even evil, falls outside His sovereign purposes. Today, trust that God is working for the glory of His name, even through circumstances that seem to oppose Him.
