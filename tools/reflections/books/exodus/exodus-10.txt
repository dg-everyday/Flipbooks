# Exodus 10: Locusts and darkness

== 10:1
T: That I might shew my signs
R: 'And the LORD said unto Moses, Go in unto Pharaoh: for I have hardened his heart, and the heart of his servants, that I might shew these my signs before him.' God sent Moses back to Pharaoh and explained that He had hardened Pharaoh's heart and his servants' hearts. The purpose was to display His signs. God was not losing the contest; He was directing it. Moses might have wondered why the plagues kept going. God reassured him that everything was under control. Pharaoh's resistance was not frustrating God's plan. It was serving it. Sometimes we wonder why difficulties continue. God may be allowing them to reveal more of His power. Romans 8 says, all things work together for good to them that love God. The longer the battle, the clearer the victory. Today, if you are weary of a long struggle, take heart. God is not losing. He is revealing Himself through it, and His purpose will be accomplished.
S: God sent Moses back to Pharaoh, explaining that He had hardened Pharaoh's heart to show His signs. Pharaoh's resistance was serving God's plan, not frustrating it. If you are weary of a long struggle, take heart that God is revealing Himself through it.
P: God is not losing. Exodus 10:1: 'I have hardened his heart… that I might shew these my signs before him.' Long struggles can reveal more of His power. Take heart today.

== 10:2
T: Tell thy son
R: 'And that thou mayest tell in the ears of thy son, and of thy son's son, what things I have wrought in Egypt, and my signs which I have done among them; that ye may know how that I am the LORD.' God gave another purpose for the plagues: so that Israel could tell their children and grandchildren what God had done in Egypt. The plagues would become stories passed from generation to generation. God's works are meant to be remembered and retold. Faith is passed down through stories of God's faithfulness. Israel would celebrate Passover every year, retelling the exodus. Psalm 78 says, We will not hide them from their children, shewing to the generation to come the praises of the LORD. Our children need to hear what God has done. Your testimony of God's faithfulness can shape the faith of your children and grandchildren. Today, tell someone younger what God has done for you. Do not let His works be forgotten. Pass on the story.
S: God said the plagues would let Israel tell their sons and grandsons what He had done in Egypt, so they would know He is the LORD. God's works are meant to be remembered and retold across generations. Tell someone younger today what God has done for you.
P: Tell the next generation. Exodus 10:2: 'that thou mayest tell in the ears of thy son, and of thy son's son, what things I have wrought.' Share God's faithfulness with someone younger today.

== 10:3
T: How long?
R: 'And Moses and Aaron came in unto Pharaoh, and said unto him, Thus saith the LORD God of the Hebrews, How long wilt thou refuse to humble thyself before me? let my people go, that they may serve me.' God asked Pharaoh: How long will you refuse to humble yourself before Me? The real issue was finally named. Pharaoh's problem was pride. He would not humble himself before God. Every plague was an invitation to humility. God was not merely asking Pharaoh to release slaves. He was calling him to bow. Pride keeps us from receiving what God offers. First Peter 5 says, Humble yourselves therefore under the mighty hand of God, that he may exalt you in due time. Notice the question how long. God's patience was great, but it was not endless. Each refusal brought Pharaoh closer to destruction. Today, ask yourself whether there is an area where you are refusing to humble yourself before God. How long will you resist? Bow before Him now.
S: Moses and Aaron asked Pharaoh in God's name how long he would refuse to humble himself before the LORD. The root of his resistance was pride. Ask yourself where you are refusing to humble yourself before God, and bow before Him now.
P: How long will you refuse? Exodus 10:3: 'How long wilt thou refuse to humble thyself before me?' Pride was Pharaoh's real problem. Humble yourself before God today.

== 10:4
T: Locusts tomorrow
R: 'Else, if thou refuse to let my people go, behold, to morrow will I bring the locusts into thy coast.' God warned of the eighth plague: locusts. If Pharaoh refused, locusts would come tomorrow. Once again, God gave warning and time. Locusts were among the most feared disasters in the ancient world. A swarm could devour every green thing in hours. Coming after the hail, it would destroy whatever was left. God's warnings increased in severity. Each plague came closer to Egypt's survival. Joel 2 later describes a locust plague as a picture of the day of the LORD, calling people to rend your heart, and not your garments. Locusts in Scripture often symbolize judgment that strips everything away. Yet Joel 2 also promises, I will restore to you the years that the locust hath eaten. God can restore what judgment has destroyed when people turn to Him. Today, respond to God's warnings while there is still time. And trust that He can restore what has been lost.
S: God warned that if Pharaoh refused, He would bring locusts into his land tomorrow. Coming after the hail, they would strip away what remained. Respond to God's warnings while there is time and trust Him to restore what has been lost.
P: Respond while there's time. Exodus 10:4: 'behold, to morrow will I bring the locusts into thy coast.' God warns before He acts. He can restore what the locust has eaten.

== 10:5
T: The residue eaten
R: 'And they shall cover the face of the earth, that one cannot be able to see the earth: and they shall eat the residue of that which is escaped, which remaineth unto you from the hail.' God described the locusts. They would cover the ground so thickly that the earth could not be seen. They would eat whatever the hail had left and every tree growing in the fields. Nothing would remain. The wheat that survived the hail would now be devoured. What God had spared in mercy would be lost because of continued refusal. Mercy ignored leads to greater loss. Instead of turning to God, he hardened his heart. Now even the remnant would be taken. Romans 2 warns that hardness and an impenitent heart treasure up wrath. When God spares something in our lives, it is an invitation to turn. If we ignore it, we may lose even what remained. Today, value what God has preserved for you. Do not take His mercy for granted.
S: God said the locusts would cover the ground and eat everything the hail had left. What God had spared in mercy would be lost because Pharaoh kept refusing. Value what God has preserved for you and do not take His mercy for granted.
P: Mercy ignored. Exodus 10:5: 'they shall eat the residue of that which is escaped… from the hail.' What God spared would now be lost. Don't take His mercy for granted.

== 10:6
T: He turned and went out
R: 'And they shall fill thy houses… which neither thy fathers, nor thy fathers' fathers have seen, since the day that they were upon the earth unto this day. And he turned himself, and went out from Pharaoh.' The locusts would fill every house, a disaster no previous generation had seen. Then Moses turned and left. He did not wait for Pharaoh's response. He had delivered the message, and he walked out. Moses' departure was significant. He had grown bold. He no longer needed Pharaoh's approval or reaction. His job was to deliver God's word, not to manage Pharaoh's response. Faithful messengers deliver the truth and leave the results to God. Ezekiel 2 says, thou shalt speak my words unto them, whether they will hear, or whether they will forbear. We are responsible for faithfulness, not for how others respond. Moses walked out with confidence. Today, speak God's truth faithfully and leave the results with Him. You do not have to control how others respond.

== 10:7
T: Knowest thou not?
R: 'And Pharaoh's servants said unto him, How long shall this man be a snare unto us? let the men go, that they may serve the LORD their God: knowest thou not yet that Egypt is destroyed?' Pharaoh's own servants pleaded with him. How long will this man be a trap to us? Let them go! Do you not realize Egypt is ruined? Even Pharaoh's officials saw what he refused to see. Pride blinds. Everyone around Pharaoh could see the destruction, but he could not. Sometimes those close to us see our blind spots more clearly than we do. Proverbs 12 says, he that hearkeneth unto counsel is wise. Pharaoh had wise counsel, but he ignored it. His pride cost his entire nation. Notice also the question: knowest thou not yet? How much destruction does it take before we see? Today, listen to those who love you when they point out your blind spots. Do not let pride keep you from seeing what is clear to others.
S: Pharaoh's servants asked how long Moses would be a snare and urged him to let Israel go, asking whether he did not know Egypt was destroyed. Everyone could see the ruin except the proud king. Listen to those who love you when they point out your blind spots.

== 10:8
T: Who shall go?
R: 'And Moses and Aaron were brought again unto Pharaoh: and he said unto them, Go, serve the LORD your God: but who are they that shall go?' Pharaoh called Moses and Aaron back. He seemed to give permission: Go, serve the LORD. But then he asked: Who exactly will go? Pharaoh was looking for another compromise. He wanted to set limits on God's demand. This is the third compromise. First, worship in the land. Second, do not go far. Now, only some may go. The enemy often negotiates this way. He allows some of our life to belong to God, but not all. He wants to keep part of us in Egypt. Romans 12 calls us to present our bodies a living sacrifice, our whole selves. Deuteronomy 6 says, love the LORD thy God with all thine heart. Today, examine whether you are giving God all of you or only part. He calls for complete devotion, not a negotiated portion.
S: Pharaoh called Moses and Aaron back and told them to go serve the LORD, but asked who exactly would go. He was negotiating for partial obedience once again. Give God all of yourself, not a negotiated portion.
P: All or part? Exodus 10:8: 'Go, serve the LORD your God: but who are they that shall go?' Pharaoh bargained for pieces. God calls for complete devotion. Give Him all of you.

== 10:9
T: Young and old
R: 'And Moses said, We will go with our young and with our old, with our sons and with our daughters, with our flocks and with our herds will we go; for we must hold a feast unto the LORD.' Moses answered firmly: We will all go. Young and old, sons and daughters, flocks and herds. Everyone and everything. We must hold a feast unto the LORD. Moses would not leave anyone behind. Worship is for the whole family. Moses insisted that children and the elderly come too. No one is too young or too old to be part of God's celebration. Joel 2 calls for gathering the elders, the children, and those that suck the breasts. Notice the joy in Moses' words: a feast. Worship was not a burden but a celebration. All of life belongs to God. Today, include your whole family in worship. Bring the young and the old. Let your worship be a joyful feast that involves every part of your life.
S: Moses declared that they would go with young and old, sons and daughters, flocks and herds, to hold a feast unto the LORD. Worship was for the whole family and all they owned. Include your whole family in joyful worship and offer every part of your life to God.

== 10:10
T: Evil is before you
R: 'And he said unto them, Let the LORD be so with you, as I will let you go, and your little ones: look to it; for evil is before you.' Pharaoh responded with sarcasm. The LORD had better be with you if I ever let you go with your children! Then he accused them: you have evil intentions. He assumed they planned to escape for good. Pharaoh mocked God's name and refused to let the children go. Notice that Pharaoh especially resisted letting the little ones go. He knew that if the children stayed, the parents would return. Children were his leverage. The enemy has always targeted the next generation. If he can keep the children, he can keep the future. Protecting children spiritually is a vital part of faith. Jesus said in Mark 10, Suffer the little children to come unto me. Today, fight for the spiritual freedom of the children in your life. Do not leave them behind in Egypt.

R: 'Not so: go now ye that are men, and serve the LORD; for that ye did desire. And they were driven out from Pharaoh's presence.' Pharaoh refused. Only the men may go. Then Moses and Aaron were driven out of his presence. Pharaoh would not allow families to worship together. And he expelled God's messengers with contempt. Pharaoh twisted Moses' request, claiming the men were all they wanted. He tried to divide families, separating men from wives and children. Division is a classic strategy against God's people. The enemy still works to separate generations and to pull family members apart. Ephesians 6 encourages fathers to bring up their children in the nurture and admonition of the Lord. Families are meant to follow God together. Notice too that Moses and Aaron were driven out. Faithfulness can lead to rejection. Today, resist anything that divides your family from following God together. And do not be discouraged by rejection when you stand for what is right.

God follows through on His warnings. Pharaoh had been told what would happen. He chose to refuse. Now the consequence came exactly as announced. God is not bluffing when He warns. God's servants are involved in His work, even in judgment. They act in obedience, and God brings the result. Today, take God's word seriously in all its parts. Moses stretched out his rod, and the LORD brought an east wind that blew all day and all night. By morning, the wind had carried the locusts into Egypt. God used natural means to accomplish His supernatural purpose. God controls the forces of nature. The wind obeyed Him. Psalm 104 says He maketh the winds his messengers. Notice the timing. The locusts covered the whole land. It was a swarm unlike any before or after. Nothing in Egypt's history compared to it, and nothing would ever match it again. God's judgments on Egypt were unique. They were not ordinary disasters but extraordinary acts of God. This uniqueness marked them as divine. The phrase neither after them shall be such shows that God was doing something unrepeatable. The exodus was a singular event in history. It would become the foundation of Israel's identity and faith. Likewise, the cross and resurrection of Jesus are unique, unrepeatable acts. == 10:16
T: In haste
R: 'Then Pharaoh called for Moses and Aaron in haste; and he said, I have sinned against the LORD your God, and against you.' Pharaoh called for Moses and Aaron in a hurry. He confessed: I have sinned against the LORD your God and against you. This confession went further than before, acknowledging sin against both God and people. Notice that Pharaoh still said your God. He did not call the LORD his own God. His confession acknowledged God's power but not God's lordship over his life. True repentance claims God personally. Thomas did not merely say Jesus is Lord. He said in John 20, My Lord and my God. Pharaoh's haste also reveals his motive. He wanted immediate relief. His urgency was about the locusts, not about his relationship with God. Yet his confession did contain truth. He had sinned against God and against Israel. Today, make your confession personal. Do not speak of God as someone else's God. Say, my Lord and my God, and turn to Him fully.
S: Pharaoh hastily called Moses and Aaron and confessed that he had sinned against the LORD their God and against them. But he still called the LORD your God, not his own. Make your confession personal and turn to God as my Lord and my God.

== 10:17

R: 'Now therefore forgive, I pray thee, my sin only this once, and intreat the LORD your God, that he may take away from me this death only.' Pharaoh begged for forgiveness: just this once. And he asked Moses to pray that God would take away this death. He recognized the locusts as a deadly threat. But his request was for relief, not for a changed life. Only this once is a revealing phrase. Pharaoh had already broken several promises. Now he asked for one more chance. He did not promise to change; he only asked for relief. Many of us have prayed this way. Just this once, Lord. Get me out of this, and then I will do better. But God desires more than one-time rescues. He wants transformed hearts. Yet God's forgiveness is not limited to once. Jesus told Peter to forgive seventy times seven. God's mercy is abundant. But it calls for genuine repentance. Today, do not bargain with God for one more rescue. Ask Him for a transformed heart.

== 10:23
T: Light in their dwellings
R: 'They saw not one another, neither rose any from his place for three days: but all the children of Israel had light in their dwellings.' The darkness was so thick that the Egyptians could not see each other or move for three days. But all the Israelites had light in their homes. In the same land, at the same time, God's people lived in light. This is one of the most beautiful contrasts in the plagues. Darkness everywhere, yet light where God's people lived. == 10:29
T: Thou hast spoken well
R: 'And Moses said, Thou hast spoken well, I will see thy face again no more.' Moses agreed. You have spoken well. I will not see your face again. The conversation was over. Pharaoh had made his final choice, and Moses accepted it. Moses' response was calm and firm. He did not plead or argue. He accepted Pharaoh's decision. There comes a time when the warnings have been given and the choice is made. The time for persuasion had passed. Moses' words carried a solemn finality. Today, do not presume that God's invitations will always be available. Respond while His voice is still calling. Now is the time.
