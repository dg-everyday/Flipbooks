# Exodus 11: The final plague announced

== 11:1
T: One plague more
R: 'And the LORD said unto Moses, Yet will I bring one plague more upon Pharaoh, and upon Egypt; afterwards he will let you go hence: when he shall let you go, he shall surely thrust you out hence altogether.' God told Moses that one more plague would come. After it, Pharaoh would not merely let Israel go; he would drive them out completely. The long struggle was almost over. After nine plagues, the end was near. Long battles do end. Moses had faced Pharaoh again and again. Now God revealed that the final blow was coming. What seemed endless had a limit set by God. Psalm 30 says, weeping may endure for a night, but joy cometh in the morning. Notice how complete the deliverance would be: altogether. Not partial freedom, not negotiated terms, but total release. Today, if you are in a long struggle, take heart. God knows how many plagues remain. Your deliverance has a set time, and when it comes, it will be complete.
S: God told Moses that He would bring one plague more, and afterward Pharaoh would thrust Israel out altogether. The long struggle had a limit set by God, and the deliverance would be complete. If you are in a long struggle, take heart that God knows when it will end.
P: One more. Exodus 11:1: 'Yet will I bring one plague more upon Pharaoh… he shall surely thrust you out hence altogether.' Long battles end. God's deliverance will be complete.

== 11:2
T: Ask your neighbour
R: 'Speak now in the ears of the people, and let every man borrow of his neighbour, and every woman of her neighbour, jewels of silver, and jewels of gold.' God told Moses to instruct the people to ask their Egyptian neighbors for silver and gold. This fulfilled God's promise that Israel would not leave empty-handed. After generations of unpaid labor, they would receive wages at last. God prepares provision before we need it. Israel was about to begin a long journey. They would need resources, and God arranged for them before they left. Notice the word neighbour. Despite the plagues, ordinary Egyptians and Israelites had relationships. Not every Egyptian was hostile. God would use these relationships to provide for His people. Genesis 15 had promised Abraham, afterward shall they come out with great substance. God keeps every detail of His word. Philippians 4 says, my God shall supply all your need. Today, trust God to provide what you need for the journey ahead. He prepares provision before you set out.
S: God told Moses to have the people ask their Egyptian neighbors for jewels of silver and gold. After generations of unpaid labor, God was providing for the journey ahead as He had promised Abraham. Trust God to prepare provision for the road before you set out.
P: Provision for the journey. Exodus 11:2: 'let every man borrow of his neighbour… jewels of silver, and jewels of gold.' God prepares what you need before you set out. Trust Him today.

== 11:3
T: Favour and greatness
R: 'And the LORD gave the people favour in the sight of the Egyptians. Moreover the man Moses was very great in the land of Egypt, in the sight of Pharaoh's servants, and in the sight of the people.' God gave Israel favor with the Egyptians. And Moses was highly respected throughout Egypt, by Pharaoh's officials and the people. Though Pharaoh hated him, the nation honored him. Favor comes from God. The Egyptians had every reason to resent Israel after the plagues. Yet God turned their hearts. Proverbs 16 says, When a man's ways please the LORD, he maketh even his enemies to be at peace with him. Moses' reputation had changed dramatically. The fugitive who fled forty years earlier was now very great in Egypt. God had vindicated His servant. First Peter 5 says God will exalt you in due time. Moses did not seek this greatness. It came from faithful obedience. Today, trust God with your reputation. Focus on faithfulness, and let Him give favor and honor in His time.
S: The LORD gave Israel favor with the Egyptians, and Moses was very great in the sight of Pharaoh's servants and the people. God turned hearts and vindicated His faithful servant. Focus on faithfulness and trust God to give favor and honor in His time.
P: Favor from God. Exodus 11:3: 'the LORD gave the people favour… Moreover the man Moses was very great in the land of Egypt.' Be faithful, and let God handle your reputation.
H: #DailyGrace #Exodus113 #Favor #Faithfulness #Vindication #BibleVerse

== 11:4
T: About midnight
R: 'And Moses said, Thus saith the LORD, About midnight will I go out into the midst of Egypt.' Moses announced the final plague. About midnight, the LORD Himself would go out into the midst of Egypt. This time, there would be no rod and no outstretched hand. God would act personally. Midnight is the darkest hour. It was the time when Egypt would be asleep and unaware. God's judgment would come suddenly. Jesus spoke of His return in similar terms. Matthew 25 says, at midnight there was a cry made, Behold, the bridegroom cometh. Judgment and salvation often come when least expected. Notice the phrase I will go out. The LORD Himself would pass through Egypt. This was not a distant act. God was personally involved in both judgment and deliverance. For Israel, His coming meant freedom. For Egypt, it meant judgment. Today, be ready. God's timing may be sudden. Live each day prepared to meet Him.
S: Moses announced that about midnight the LORD Himself would go out into the midst of Egypt. The final plague would come suddenly, with God acting personally in judgment and deliverance. Live each day ready, for God's timing may be sudden.
P: About midnight. Exodus 11:4: 'About midnight will I go out into the midst of Egypt.' God would act suddenly and personally. Live each day ready to meet Him.

== 11:5
T: All the firstborn
R: 'And all the firstborn in the land of Egypt shall die, from the firstborn of Pharaoh that sitteth upon his throne, even unto the firstborn of the maidservant that is behind the mill; and all the firstborn of beasts.' The final plague was the most severe. Every firstborn in Egypt would die, from Pharaoh's son to the son of the lowest slave girl, and even the firstborn of animals. No household would be exempt. God had warned Pharaoh from the beginning: if you refuse to let My son go, I will slay your firstborn. Egypt had killed Israel's sons. Now judgment reached Egypt's sons. This plague shows the seriousness of sin and the reality of judgment. Romans 6 says, the wages of sin is death. Yet it also prepares for the great provision God would make: the Passover lamb. God would provide a way for death to pass over. Today, take sin seriously, and turn to the Lamb God has provided.
S: Moses announced that every firstborn in Egypt would die, from Pharaoh's son to the maidservant's son and the firstborn of beasts. Pharaoh's stubbornness brought judgment on every home, as God had warned. Take sin seriously and turn to the Lamb God has provided.
P: The seriousness of sin. Exodus 11:5: 'all the firstborn in the land of Egypt shall die.' Judgment is real, but God provided a Lamb. Turn to Jesus, the Lamb of God, today.

== 11:6
T: A great cry
R: 'And there shall be a great cry throughout all the land of Egypt, such as there was none like it, nor shall be like it any more.' There would be a loud cry throughout Egypt, worse than any before or after. The grief would be universal and overwhelming. Every household would mourn. This is the tragic result of hardened hearts. Years earlier, Israel's cry had gone up to God from their bondage. Now Egypt would cry. The oppressors would experience the sorrow they had inflicted. God's justice is real. Yet even here, we should not rejoice in others' pain. Ezekiel 33 says God has no pleasure in the death of the wicked. The great cry in Egypt was a tragedy, not a triumph to celebrate lightly. Proverbs 24 says, Rejoice not when thine enemy falleth. Today, take seriously the cost of rebellion against God. And let your heart grieve for those who suffer the consequences of sin, even your enemies. Pray that many will turn before judgment comes.
S: Moses said there would be a great cry throughout Egypt, such as there had never been nor would be again. The oppressors would experience the sorrow they had inflicted, yet God takes no pleasure in such grief. Take seriously the cost of rebellion and pray for others to turn before judgment.
P: A great cry. Exodus 11:6: 'there shall be a great cry throughout all the land of Egypt.' God takes no pleasure in judgment. Grieve for the lost and pray for them today.

== 11:7
T: Not a dog shall move his tongue
R: 'But against any of the children of Israel shall not a dog move his tongue, against man or beast: that ye may know how that the LORD doth put a difference between the Egyptians and Israel.' While Egypt cried out, Israel would be completely at peace. Not even a dog would bark at them. The contrast would be total: sorrow in Egypt, silence and safety in Israel. God makes a difference between His people and the world. This difference was not based on Israel's righteousness but on God's covenant and the blood of the Passover lamb. Those under God's protection are secure. Psalm 4 says, I will both lay me down in peace, and sleep: for thou, LORD, only makest me dwell in safety. God's peace would be complete. Today, rest in God's protection. He knows those who are His, and He makes a difference for them. In Christ, you are kept in His peace.
S: Moses declared that not even a dog would move his tongue against Israel, so they would know the LORD makes a difference between Egypt and Israel. While Egypt cried out, God's people would be at complete peace. Rest in God's protection, for in Christ you are kept in His peace.
P: Perfect peace. Exodus 11:7: 'against any of the children of Israel shall not a dog move his tongue.' God makes a difference for His people. Rest in His protection today.

== 11:8
T: In a great anger
R: 'And all these thy servants shall come down unto me, and bow down themselves unto me, saying, Get thee out, and all the people that follow thee: and after that I will go out. And he went out from Pharaoh in a great anger.' Moses declared that Pharaoh's officials would come and bow before him, begging Israel to leave. Then Moses left Pharaoh in great anger. Moses' anger was not personal revenge. He was angry at Pharaoh's stubbornness, which would now bring death to every Egyptian home. There is a righteous anger that grieves over unnecessary suffering caused by sin. Jesus also showed anger. Mark 3 says He looked round about on them with anger, being grieved for the hardness of their hearts. Yet Ephesians 4 warns, Be ye angry, and sin not. Righteous anger must be controlled and directed. Today, if you feel anger at injustice, let it be righteous and controlled. Grieve over hardness of heart, but leave judgment to God.
S: Moses declared that Pharaoh's servants would bow and beg Israel to leave, and then he went out from Pharaoh in a great anger. His anger grieved over the hardness that would bring death to every Egyptian home. Let your anger at injustice be righteous and controlled, leaving judgment to God.
P: Righteous anger. Exodus 11:8: 'And he went out from Pharaoh in a great anger.' Moses grieved over hardness of heart. Be angry, and sin not. Leave judgment to God.

== 11:9
T: Wonders multiplied
R: 'And the LORD said unto Moses, Pharaoh shall not hearken unto you; that my wonders may be multiplied in the land of Egypt.' God reminded Moses that Pharaoh would not listen, and that this would lead to the multiplication of God's wonders. Even after all the plagues, Pharaoh's refusal was not surprising to God. It was part of the plan. This verse is a summary of the plague narrative. Pharaoh's resistance provided the stage for God's power. Each refusal led to another wonder. In the end, Pharaoh's rebellion displayed God's glory more fully than his obedience would have. God's purposes cannot be defeated. Even human rebellion ends up serving His plans. Psalm 76 says, Surely the wrath of man shall praise thee. This should comfort us when we face opposition. God is not frustrated by it. He uses it. Today, trust that God is multiplying His wonders even through the resistance you face. His glory will shine through it all.
S: God reminded Moses that Pharaoh would not listen so that His wonders might be multiplied in Egypt. Pharaoh's resistance became the stage for God's power and glory. Trust that God is displaying His wonders even through the resistance you face.
P: Wonders multiplied. Exodus 11:9: 'Pharaoh shall not hearken unto you; that my wonders may be multiplied.' Opposition doesn't frustrate God. He uses it for His glory.

== 11:10
T: All these wonders
R: 'And Moses and Aaron did all these wonders before Pharaoh: and the LORD hardened Pharaoh's heart, so that he would not let the children of Israel go out of his land.' This verse summarizes the whole plague cycle. Moses and Aaron performed all these wonders before Pharaoh. And the LORD hardened Pharaoh's heart so that he would not let Israel go. The stage was set for the final act. Moses and Aaron had been faithful. They did all that God commanded. Their faithfulness did not depend on results. Pharaoh's refusal did not make their obedience a failure. First Corinthians 4 says, it is required in stewards, that a man be found faithful. God measures success by faithfulness, not by outcomes we control. Moses could not change Pharaoh's heart. But he could obey God. And God would accomplish the deliverance. Today, measure your life by faithfulness, not by visible results. Do all that God asks, and trust Him with the outcome. Your faithful obedience is never wasted.
S: Moses and Aaron did all these wonders before Pharaoh, and the LORD hardened Pharaoh's heart so he would not let Israel go. Their faithfulness did not depend on results, for they could not change Pharaoh's heart. Measure your life by faithfulness and trust God with the outcome.
P: Faithfulness, not results. Exodus 11:10: 'Moses and Aaron did all these wonders before Pharaoh.' They obeyed fully and left the results to God. Your faithful obedience is never wasted.
