# Exodus 12:1-26: The Passover instituted

== 12:1
T: God speaks in Egypt
R: 'And the LORD spake unto Moses and Aaron in the land of Egypt, saying.' Before the final plague, God gave Moses and Aaron detailed instructions. Notice where He spoke: in the land of Egypt. Israel's first great feast was given while they were still in bondage. Deliverance was announced before it came. God prepares His people for freedom while they are still captive. He speaks in the place of bondage about the life beyond it. Israel was told how to celebrate their rescue before it happened. This is the nature of faith. Hebrews 11 says, faith is the substance of things hoped for. We live now in light of what God has promised. Even in our own Egypts, God speaks of freedom and calls us to prepare. Today, listen for God's word in your hard place. He may be preparing you for a deliverance you cannot yet see. Live in light of His promises.
S: The LORD spoke to Moses and Aaron in the land of Egypt, giving instructions for a feast before deliverance came. God prepares His people for freedom while they are still in bondage. Listen for God's word in your hard place and live in light of His promises.
P: Prepared for freedom. Exodus 12:1: 'the LORD spake unto Moses and Aaron in the land of Egypt.' God speaks of deliverance before it comes. Live in light of His promises today.

== 12:2
T: The beginning of months
R: 'This month shall be unto you the beginning of months: it shall be the first month of the year to you.' God reset Israel's calendar. The month of their deliverance would become the first month of the year. Their history as God's free people would be counted from this moment. Everything before was bondage; everything after was a new beginning. Redemption changes how we count time. When God saves us, life begins anew. Second Corinthians 5 says, if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature: old things are passed away; behold, all things are become new. Many believers mark their spiritual birthday as a new beginning. God did not let Israel's identity be defined by Egypt. Their story would be measured from the moment of salvation. Lamentations 3 says His mercies are new every morning. Today, remember that in Christ your life has a new beginning. Your past does not define you. Count your life from the day God set you free.
S: God declared that the month of deliverance would become the beginning of months for Israel. Their history as a free people would be counted from the moment of salvation. Remember that in Christ your life has a new beginning not defined by your past.
P: A new beginning. Exodus 12:2: 'This month shall be unto you the beginning of months.' Redemption resets everything. In Christ, all things become new!

== 12:3
T: A lamb for an house
R: 'Speak ye unto all the congregation of Israel, saying, In the tenth day of this month they shall take to them every man a lamb, according to the house of their fathers, a lamb for an house.' On the tenth day of the month, every household was to choose a lamb. This is the first mention of the Passover lamb. Each family would need a lamb for its own protection. Salvation was to be applied household by household. Notice the progression in Scripture. In Genesis 22, Isaac asked, where is the lamb? Here, there is a lamb for each house. In John 1, John the Baptist declares, Behold the Lamb of God, which taketh away the sin of the world. God's provision grows from one family to the whole world. Jesus is our Passover Lamb. First Corinthians 5 says, Christ our passover is sacrificed for us. Today, make sure Christ is the Lamb for your house. Pray for each member of your household to know Him.
S: God told Israel that on the tenth day each household should take a lamb. Salvation would be applied house by house, pointing ahead to Christ, the Lamb of God. Make sure Christ is the Lamb for your house and pray for each member of your household.
P: A lamb for every house. Exodus 12:3: 'they shall take to them every man a lamb… a lamb for an house.' Christ is our Passover Lamb. Pray for your household to know Him.

== 12:4
T: Share with your neighbour
R: 'And if the household be too little for the lamb, let him and his neighbour next unto his house take it according to the number of the souls; every man according to his eating shall make your count for the lamb.' If a household was too small for a whole lamb, they were to share with their nearest neighbor. The lamb was to be divided according to the number of people. No one was to be left out because their household was small. God's provision is meant to be shared. Small families and single people were included by joining with neighbors. The Passover created community. No one had to celebrate alone. Notice the phrase every man according to his eating. God's provision was measured to each person's need. The lamb was enough for everyone who shared it. Christ is sufficient for all who come to Him. Acts 2 describes believers breaking bread from house to house. Today, invite others to share in God's provision. Make sure no one in your community is left alone.
S: If a household was too small for a lamb, they were to share it with their neighbor according to the number of people. No one was left out, and God's provision created community. Invite others to share in God's provision so no one is left alone.
P: No one left out. Exodus 12:4: 'let him and his neighbour next unto his house take it.' God's provision is meant to be shared. Invite someone into community today.

== 12:5
T: Without blemish
R: 'Your lamb shall be without blemish, a male of the first year: ye shall take it out from the sheep, or from the goats.' The lamb had to be perfect: without blemish, a male in its first year. Only the best was acceptable. A flawed animal could not serve as a substitute. This requirement points directly to Christ. First Peter 1 says we were redeemed with the precious blood of Christ, as of a lamb without blemish and without spot. Jesus lived a sinless life. He alone could be the perfect substitute. Hebrews 4 says He was in all points tempted like as we are, yet without sin. Only a spotless sacrifice could take the place of sinners. Our own goodness could never qualify. We are all blemished. But Christ, the unblemished Lamb, took our place. Today, thank God for the perfect Lamb He provided. Your salvation rests not on your perfection but on His.
S: God required that the lamb be without blemish, a male of the first year. Only a perfect substitute was acceptable, pointing to Christ, the spotless Lamb. Thank God that your salvation rests not on your perfection but on Christ's.
P: The spotless Lamb. Exodus 12:5: 'Your lamb shall be without blemish.' Jesus is the perfect Lamb who took your place. Rest in His perfection, not yours.

== 12:6
T: Kept until the fourteenth
R: 'And ye shall keep it up until the fourteenth day of the same month: and the whole assembly of the congregation of Israel shall kill it in the evening.' The lamb was chosen on the tenth day and kept until the fourteenth. For four days, families lived with the lamb, observing it closely. Then the whole congregation would kill it at evening. During these days, any blemish would become obvious. The family would also grow attached to the lamb, feeling the cost of its death. Jesus too was examined before His death. He entered Jerusalem days before Passover, and religious leaders questioned Him. Pilate said in John 19, I find no fault in him. The Lamb was tested and found perfect. Notice also that the whole assembly killed it. Every family participated. Our sins also put Christ on the cross. Isaiah 53 says, the LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all. Today, consider the cost of your redemption. The Lamb was examined, found faultless, and given for you.
S: The lamb was kept from the tenth to the fourteenth day and then killed by the whole congregation at evening. Like Jesus before His death, it was examined and found without fault. Consider the cost of your redemption, for the faultless Lamb was given for you.

== 12:7
T: Blood on the doorposts
R: 'And they shall take of the blood, and strike it on the two side posts and on the upper door post of the houses, wherein they shall eat it.' Each family was to put the lamb's blood on the sides and top of their doorframe. The blood marked the house. It was not enough to kill the lamb; its blood had to be applied. This is a crucial truth. Christ's death is sufficient for all, but it must be personally received. A lamb killed but whose blood was not applied would not protect the household. Faith applies the blood of Christ to our lives. Romans 3 speaks of propitiation through faith in his blood. Many have noticed the shape: blood on the top and two sides of the door. It suggests the shape of a cross. The way into the house of safety was marked by blood. Today, make sure Christ's sacrifice is applied to your life. Receive Him personally by faith.
S: Families were to strike the lamb's blood on the side posts and upper door post of their houses. Killing the lamb was not enough; its blood had to be applied. Make sure Christ's sacrifice is applied to your life by personal faith.

== 12:8
T: Roast, unleavened, bitter
R: 'And they shall eat the flesh in that night, roast with fire, and unleavened bread; and with bitter herbs they shall eat it.' The lamb was to be roasted and eaten that night with unleavened bread and bitter herbs. Each element carried meaning. The roasted lamb pictured sacrifice. The unleavened bread pictured haste and purity. The bitter herbs recalled the bitterness of slavery. The lamb was not only killed; it was eaten. Those protected by the blood were also nourished by the lamb. Jesus said in John 6, I am the bread of life… Whoso eateth my flesh, and drinketh my blood, hath eternal life. We are saved by Christ's death and sustained by His life. The bitter herbs remind us that remembering our bondage makes deliverance sweeter. We should never forget what Christ saved us from. Today, feed on Christ. Let Him nourish you, and remember with gratitude the bitterness from which He has set you free.

== 12:9
T: Roasted whole
R: 'Eat not of it raw, nor sodden at all with water, but roast with fire; his head with his legs, and with the purtenance thereof.' The lamb could not be eaten raw or boiled. It had to be roasted whole over fire, including its head, legs, and inner parts. Nothing was to be divided or diluted. Fire in Scripture often represents judgment. The lamb was roasted, bearing the full heat of the fire. Christ also bore the full judgment for sin. It was not diluted or softened. He endured God's wrath completely on our behalf. Isaiah 53 says, it pleased the LORD to bruise him. The lamb was also kept whole. It was not broken apart. Christ is received fully, not in pieces. We cannot take part of Him and leave the rest. He is Savior and Lord together. Today, receive Christ completely. And thank Him for bearing the full fire of judgment so that you would never have to.
S: The lamb was not to be eaten raw or boiled but roasted whole with fire. It pictures Christ bearing the full fire of judgment and being received completely. Receive Christ completely and thank Him for bearing judgment in your place.

== 12:10
T: Nothing remaining
R: 'And ye shall let nothing of it remain until the morning; and that which remaineth of it until the morning ye shall burn with fire.' Nothing of the lamb was to be left until morning. Whatever remained was to be burned. The Passover was a one-night meal, fully consumed. This instruction emphasized urgency and completeness. The lamb was not to become ordinary leftovers. It was holy, set apart for this night of deliverance. Its purpose was fulfilled in one decisive event. This also points to Christ. His sacrifice was once for all. Hebrews 10 says, we are sanctified through the offering of the body of Jesus Christ once for all. It does not need to be repeated or added to. Nothing was left over because nothing more was needed. Today, rest in the completeness of Christ's sacrifice. He did everything necessary for your salvation in one decisive act. Nothing remains to be paid.
S: Nothing of the lamb was to remain until morning, and any remainder was to be burned. The Passover was a holy, one-night meal, fully consumed in one decisive event. Rest in the completeness of Christ's sacrifice, offered once for all.

== 12:11
T: Ready to go
R: 'And thus shall ye eat it; with your loins girded, your shoes on your feet, and your staff in your hand; and ye shall eat it in haste: it is the LORD'S passover.' Israel was to eat the Passover dressed for travel: robes tucked in, sandals on, staff in hand. They would eat in haste, ready to leave at a moment's notice. It is the LORD's Passover. Salvation is meant to move us. Israel was not to linger at the table. They were to be ready to leave Egypt behind. Those who are redeemed are pilgrims, ready to follow God wherever He leads. Luke 12 says, Let your loins be girded about, and your lights burning. First Peter 1 says, gird up the loins of your mind. Believers live in readiness, not settled in the world. Notice too that it is the LORD's Passover. It belongs to Him. Today, live ready to follow God. Hold this world loosely, and be prepared to move when He calls.

== 12:12
T: Against all the gods of Egypt
R: 'For I will pass through the land of Egypt this night, and will smite all the firstborn in the land of Egypt, both man and beast; and against all the gods of Egypt I will execute judgment: I am the LORD.' God declared that He would pass through Egypt and strike the firstborn. And He would execute judgment against all the gods of Egypt. The final plague was not only against Pharaoh; it was against every false god. Egypt had many gods. Pharaoh himself was considered divine, and his firstborn was heir to that divinity. By striking the firstborn, God exposed the emptiness of Egypt's entire religious system. None of its gods could protect it. Every idol eventually falls before the true God. Isaiah 46 says of idols, they cannot save. Colossians 2 says Christ triumphed over principalities and powers. Today, examine whether any false god has claimed a place in your heart. Wealth, success, approval, or comfort cannot save. Only the LORD can.

== 12:13
T: When I see the blood
R: 'And the blood shall be to you for a token upon the houses where ye are: and when I see the blood, I will pass over you, and the plague shall not be upon you to destroy you, when I smite the land of Egypt.' This is the heart of Passover. The blood would be a sign. When God saw the blood, He would pass over that house. The plague would not destroy those inside. Notice that safety did not depend on the family's worthiness or feelings. It depended on the blood. An Israelite family could be fearful and unsure, yet safe because of the blood. An Egyptian family could be confident, yet unprotected. Our security rests on Christ's blood, not on our emotions or performance. Romans 5 says, being now justified by his blood, we shall be saved from wrath through him. Hebrews 9 says Christ entered heaven by his own blood, having obtained eternal redemption. Today, rest in the blood of Christ. God sees it, and judgment passes over you.

God commanded that this day be a memorial. Israel was to keep it as a feast to the LORD for all generations, forever. Deliverance must be remembered. We are prone to forget. Even great acts of God can fade from memory. So God built remembrance into Israel's calendar. Every year, they would retell the story. Jesus instituted a similar memorial. At the Last Supper, during Passover, He said in Luke 22, this do in remembrance of me. The Lord's Supper is the Christian Passover, remembering the Lamb who was slain for us. Notice that it is called a feast. Remembering God's deliverance is a celebration. Today, make time to remember what God has done for you. Celebrate His salvation with gratitude and joy.

== 12:15
T: Put away leaven
R: 'Seven days shall ye eat unleavened bread; even the first day ye shall put away leaven out of your houses: for whosoever eateth leavened bread from the first day until the seventh day, that soul shall be cut off from Israel.' For seven days, Israel was to eat bread without yeast. On the first day, they were to remove all leaven from their homes. Eating leaven during the feast was a serious offense. Leaven in Scripture often pictures sin. A little yeast spreads through the whole lump of dough. First Corinthians 5 says, Purge out therefore the old leaven… For even Christ our passover is sacrificed for us. Paul connects the removal of leaven with the sacrifice of Christ. Because we are redeemed, we put sin out of our lives. Holiness requires attention. We must search our hearts and remove what does not belong. Today, ask God to show you any leaven in your life. Put it away, because Christ your Passover has been sacrificed for you.

== 12:16
T: An holy convocation
R: 'And in the first day there shall be an holy convocation, and in the seventh day there shall be an holy convocation to you; no manner of work shall be done in them, save that which every man must eat.' The first and seventh days of the feast were holy gatherings. No work was allowed except preparing food. God's people were to gather, rest, and worship together. A convocation is a calling together. God calls His people to assemble. Worship is not only private; it is communal. Hebrews 10 says, Not forsaking the assembling of ourselves together. These days were also days of rest. Israel, who had labored without rest in Egypt, would now have holy days without work. Freedom brought rest. God's gift of rest is a sign of redemption. Matthew 11 records Jesus saying, Come unto me… and I will give you rest. Today, gather with God's people for worship. And receive the rest that God offers to those He has redeemed.

== 12:17
T: This selfsame day
R: 'And ye shall observe the feast of unleavened bread; for in this selfsame day have I brought your armies out of the land of Egypt: therefore shall ye observe this day in your generations by an ordinance for ever.' God explained the reason for the feast: on this very day I brought your armies out of Egypt. The feast commemorated a real, historical event. God's salvation happened in time and space. Biblical faith is rooted in history. God did not save Israel in a myth but on a specific day. The same is true of Christ's death and resurrection. First Corinthians 15 says Christ died and rose again the third day according to the scriptures. Our faith rests on real events. Notice again the phrase your armies. God saw Israel as organized hosts, not fleeing slaves. Redemption gives dignity. Today, rejoice that your faith rests on real acts of God in history. Celebrate the day of your salvation and the day of Christ's victory.

== 12:18
T: From evening to evening
R: 'In the first month, on the fourteenth day of the month at even, ye shall eat unleavened bread, until the one and twentieth day of the month at even.' God set the exact dates for the feast: from the evening of the fourteenth day to the evening of the twenty-first. The timing was precise. Every Israelite would know when to begin and when to end. God cares about order and timing. His appointed feasts were not random. They followed a careful pattern. Centuries later, Jesus would die at Passover time, fulfilling this feast precisely. Galatians 4 says, when the fulness of the time was come, God sent forth his Son. The days began at evening, which is how Israel counted time. Genesis 1 says, the evening and the morning were the first day. Darkness came first, then light. Redemption follows the same pattern: the darkness of Egypt's judgment, then the light of freedom. Today, trust God's timing. He keeps His appointed times precisely, and darkness always gives way to His light.

Both stranger and native were held to the same holiness. Foreigners were not excluded from God's people; they were invited into the same covenant. God welcomes outsiders, and He calls everyone to the same purity. Romans 3 says, there is no difference: For all have sinned. And Romans 10 says, there is no difference between the Jew and the Greek: for the same Lord over all is rich unto all that call upon him. Today, rejoice that God's welcome and God's standards are for everyone. All may come, and all are called to holiness.

Protection was found only under the blood. Those who wandered out would not be safe. Abiding matters. Jesus said in John 15, Abide in me. Today, stay under the protection of Christ's blood. == 12:23
T: The LORD will pass over
R: 'For the LORD will pass through to smite the Egyptians; and when he seeth the blood upon the lintel, and on the two side posts, the LORD will pass over the door, and will not suffer the destroyer to come in unto your houses to smite you.' The LORD would pass through Egypt. When He saw the blood, He would pass over the door and not allow the destroyer to enter. The LORD Himself would guard the houses marked by blood. The word pass over can also suggest hovering protectively over the door. Isaiah 31 uses similar language: passing over he will preserve it. God did not merely avoid the house; He guarded it. This is what Christ does for us. He stands between us and judgment. Romans 8 says, Who is he that condemneth? It is Christ that died. No destroyer can enter where God stands guard. Today, rest in the security of God's protection. Through Christ's blood, the LORD Himself guards your life.

== 12:24
T: For ever

Israel was not to remember the exodus only while it was fresh. They were to remember it forever. The passing of time does not reduce the importance of what God has done. Notice to thee and to thy sons. Faith is meant to be passed down. Parents had a responsibility to keep the ordinance and teach it to their children. Deuteronomy 6 says, thou shalt teach them diligently unto thy children. Christian families also have practices of remembrance: communion, Bible reading, prayer, and celebrating what God has done. == 12:25

== 12:26
T: What mean ye?

Their questions would open the door to teaching. God designed the Passover to provoke curiosity in the next generation. Children's questions are opportunities. God expected them to ask, and He wanted parents ready to answer. Rituals and practices are meant to lead to conversations about God. When children see us pray, read Scripture, or take communion, they will ask why. These moments are precious. First Peter 3 says, be ready always to give an answer to every man that asketh you a reason of the hope that is in you. This applies especially to our children. Faith is best passed on through honest conversation about what God has done. Today, welcome the questions of children. Be ready to explain what your faith means and why it matters. Their questions are invitations to share God's story.
