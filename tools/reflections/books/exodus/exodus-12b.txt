# Exodus 12:27-51: The night of deliverance

== 12:27
T: Delivered our houses
R: 'That ye shall say, It is the sacrifice of the LORD'S passover, who passed over the houses of the children of Israel in Egypt, when he smote the Egyptians, and delivered our houses. And the people bowed the head and worshipped.' God gave parents the answer for their children: It is the sacrifice of the LORD's Passover. He passed over our houses and delivered us. When the people heard this, they bowed their heads and worshiped. Notice the word our. Parents were to tell the story as their own. Even generations later, they would say, He delivered our houses. Faith is personal and shared. Each generation joins the story as participants, not spectators. The people's response is striking. Before the deliverance even happened, they worshiped. They believed God's word enough to bow in gratitude ahead of time. Hebrews 11 says, faith is… the evidence of things not seen. Today, tell God's story as your own. He has delivered your house. Bow your heart in worship, even before you see every promise fulfilled.
S: God told parents to explain that it is the sacrifice of the LORD's passover, who passed over and delivered their houses, and the people bowed and worshiped. They told the story as their own and worshiped before the deliverance came. Tell God's story as your own and worship Him in faith.
P: He delivered our houses. Exodus 12:27: 'It is the sacrifice of the LORD'S passover… And the people bowed the head and worshipped.' Make God's story your own. Worship Him today.

== 12:28
T: So did they
R: 'And the children of Israel went away, and did as the LORD had commanded Moses and Aaron, so did they.' The Israelites went and did exactly as God commanded. They chose lambs, killed them, applied the blood, and prepared the meal. Their obedience was complete. The emphasis is repeated: so did they. This was faith in action. The people had no proof that the blood would protect them. They had only God's word. Yet they obeyed fully. James 2 says, faith without works is dead. Their faith was shown in obedience. Imagine the scene: thousands of families putting blood on their doors on the same night. It might have looked strange to their Egyptian neighbors. But they obeyed anyway. Obedience sometimes looks foolish to the world. First Corinthians 1 says, the preaching of the cross is to them that perish foolishness. Today, obey God's word fully, even when it looks strange to others. Faith that trusts God's word acts on it.
S: The children of Israel went and did as the LORD had commanded Moses and Aaron. With only God's word as assurance, they obeyed fully, even if it looked strange. Obey God's word fully today, for faith that trusts Him acts on what He says.
P: Faith in action. Exodus 12:28: 'the children of Israel went away, and did as the LORD had commanded.' Blood on doors looked strange, but they obeyed. Act on God's word today.

== 12:29
T: At midnight
R: 'And it came to pass, that at midnight the LORD smote all the firstborn in the land of Egypt, from the firstborn of Pharaoh that sat on his throne unto the firstborn of the captive that was in the dungeon; and all the firstborn of cattle.' At midnight, just as God had said, the LORD struck every firstborn in Egypt. From Pharaoh's palace to the prisoner in the dungeon, no Egyptian household escaped. The final judgment fell. This verse should be read with great solemnity. God's word was fulfilled exactly. He had warned for months. Now judgment came at the appointed hour. Notice the range: from the throne to the dungeon. All stand equal before God. Hebrews 9 says, it is appointed unto men once to die, but after this the judgment. Only one thing made the difference that night: the blood. Not status, wealth, or power. Today, remember that God's word will be fulfilled. Make sure you are sheltered under the blood of the Lamb.
S: At midnight the LORD smote all the firstborn in Egypt, from Pharaoh's son to the captive's son in the dungeon. God's word was fulfilled exactly, and only the blood made the difference. Make sure you are sheltered under the blood of the Lamb.
P: At midnight. Exodus 12:29: 'at midnight the LORD smote all the firstborn in the land of Egypt.' Only the blood made the difference. Shelter under the Lamb's blood today.

== 12:30
T: Not a house without one dead
R: 'And Pharaoh rose up in the night, he, and all his servants, and all the Egyptians; and there was a great cry in Egypt; for there was not a house where there was not one dead.' Pharaoh rose in the night, along with all his servants and all Egypt. A great cry went up across the land, for there was not one house without someone dead. The sorrow God had warned of came to pass. This is a heartbreaking verse. The cry of Egypt echoed the cry Israel had raised for generations. Sin's consequences spread far beyond the sinner. Pharaoh's pride cost every family in Egypt. Leaders who harden their hearts bring suffering on those they lead. This should move us to pray for leaders and to grieve over the effects of sin. Romans 6 says, the wages of sin is death. Today, take sin seriously and pray for those in authority. And thank God for the Lamb whose blood protects from judgment.
S: Pharaoh, his servants and all Egypt rose in the night with a great cry, for there was not a house without one dead. The sorrow came from one ruler's stubborn refusal. Take sin seriously, pray for those in authority, and thank God for the protecting blood of the Lamb.
P: A great cry. Exodus 12:30: 'there was not a house where there was not one dead.' Sin's consequences spread far. Pray for leaders and thank God for the Lamb today.

== 12:31
T: Rise up and go
R: 'And he called for Moses and Aaron by night, and said, Rise up, and get you forth from among my people, both ye and the children of Israel; and go, serve the LORD, as ye have said.' That very night, Pharaoh summoned Moses and Aaron. Rise up! Get out! Go serve the LORD, as you have said. The man who had said, see my face no more, now begged them to leave. His resistance was finally broken. Notice the phrase as ye have said. Pharaoh gave in completely. All his compromises were gone. No conditions, no limits. God's word through Moses had prevailed. What Pharaoh refused for months, he now commanded in one night. God's deliverance often comes suddenly after long waiting. Israel had waited four hundred years and endured ten plagues. Then, in a single night, everything changed. Isaiah 60 says, I the LORD will hasten it in his time. Today, if you have waited long for God's deliverance, do not lose hope. When His time comes, change can happen overnight.
S: That night Pharaoh called Moses and Aaron and told them to rise up and go serve the LORD, as they had said. His resistance was broken completely, with no more conditions. If you have waited long for deliverance, do not lose hope, for God can change things overnight.
P: Overnight change. Exodus 12:31: 'Rise up, and get you forth… and go, serve the LORD, as ye have said.' After 400 years, freedom came in one night. Don't lose hope.

== 12:32
T: Bless me also
R: 'Also take your flocks and your herds, as ye have said, and be gone; and bless me also.' Pharaoh added: Take your flocks and herds as you asked, and be gone. Then he asked something surprising: Bless me also. The proud king who had defied God now asked for a blessing from God's servants. Pharaoh's request shows how completely he had been humbled. He needed the blessing of the people he had oppressed. Sooner or later, everyone recognizes their need for God's blessing. Yet Pharaoh's request was still shallow. He wanted blessing without submission. His heart would soon harden again. Many people want God's blessing without God's lordship. They want benefits without relationship. True blessing comes from surrender, not from requests made in desperation. Numbers 6 shows the priestly blessing: The LORD bless thee, and keep thee. Today, seek God's blessing through submission to Him. Do not settle for wanting His gifts without wanting Him.
S: Pharaoh told Moses to take the flocks and herds and be gone, and asked them to bless him also. The proud king had been humbled enough to seek a blessing, yet still without true submission. Seek God's blessing through surrender to Him, not just His gifts.

They had seen enough to know that resistance was futile. Sometimes God brings people to the end of themselves so that they will let go of what they should never have held. Today, trust that God can reverse your situation. Those who oppose you today may one day urge you forward.

== 12:34
T: Dough before it was leavened
R: 'And the people took their dough before it was leavened, their kneadingtroughs being bound up in their clothes upon their shoulders.' The Israelites left so quickly that they took their bread dough before it had risen. They wrapped their kneading bowls in their clothes and carried them on their shoulders. There was no time to wait. This detail explains the unleavened bread of the feast. Israel ate unleavened bread because God's deliverance came so fast. They did not have time to let the dough rise. God's timing sometimes requires us to move immediately. When God opens the door, we must be ready to go. Delay could have cost them. Luke 17 warns of the days of Lot, when people hesitated. Israel was ready because they obeyed the instruction to eat with shoes on and staff in hand. Preparation enabled quick obedience. Today, be ready to respond to God quickly. When He opens a door, walk through it. Do not let unfinished things hold you back from His deliverance.

== 12:35

They asked the Egyptians for silver, gold, and clothing. They obeyed even this unusual instruction. God's word had prepared them, and they acted on it. This fulfilled God's promise to Abraham and His word at the burning bush. Every detail God spoke came true. Israel would not leave as beggars but with wealth. Notice that their obedience extended to small details. They did not only obey the dramatic commands like applying blood. They also followed the practical instruction to ask their neighbors. Faithfulness includes the small things. Luke 16 says, He that is faithful in that which is least is faithful also in much. God's promises often require our participation. Israel had to ask; God gave them favor. Today, obey God in the small, practical details. He works through ordinary obedience to fulfill great promises.

The LORD gave Israel favor, and the Egyptians gave them whatever they asked. So Israel plundered Egypt. Not by war or force, but by God's favor. Slaves walked out with the wealth of their masters. This is God's justice. Israel had labored without pay for generations. Now they received what was owed to them. God sees unpaid labor and unjust treatment, and He makes things right. James 5 warns those who withhold wages: the cries of them which have reaped are entered into the ears of the Lord. Notice that it was the LORD who gave favor. Israel did not earn it. God moved Egyptian hearts. Proverbs 21 says the king's heart is in the hand of the LORD. Today, trust God with the injustices you have suffered. He knows what is owed to you, and He is able to restore.

About six hundred thousand men walked, plus women and children. The family of seventy that came into Egypt had become a great multitude. God's promise of multiplication was fulfilled. Think of the journey from Genesis to this moment. Abraham was promised descendants like the stars. Jacob went down to Egypt with seventy people. Now a nation walked out. Leaving the place of bondage is the beginning of the journey. Second Corinthians 5 says, old things are passed away. Today, celebrate God's faithfulness over generations. These were people of other nations, including Egyptians, who chose to join God's people. They had seen God's power and decided to follow Him. God's salvation was already reaching beyond Israel. This is a beautiful glimpse of God's heart for the nations. The plagues had been a witness. Some Egyptians believed and left everything to go with Israel. God's people have always included outsiders who come to faith. Ruth the Moabitess, Rahab of Jericho, and many others would join. Revelation 7 describes a great multitude… of all nations, and kindreds, and people, and tongues worshiping God. The mixed multitude also brought challenges later, as Numbers 11 shows. Welcoming outsiders requires discipleship. Yet God's invitation is open to all. Today, rejoice that God's salvation reaches all people. Welcome those who are new to faith, and help them grow.

== 12:43
T: The ordinance of the passover
R: 'And the LORD said unto Moses and Aaron, This is the ordinance of the passover: There shall no stranger eat thereof.' God gave rules for who could eat the Passover. No foreigner, meaning one outside the covenant, could eat it. The Passover belonged to those in covenant with God. This was not about ethnicity but covenant relationship. As the following verses show, foreigners could join by entering the covenant. The meal was for those who belonged to God. Participation required commitment. The same principle applies to the Lord's Supper. First Corinthians 11 warns against eating unworthily and calls believers to examine themselves. Communion is for those who belong to Christ. It is not a ritual for outsiders but a meal for the family. Yet the door is always open. Anyone can enter the covenant through faith. Today, consider your relationship with God. Are you in covenant with Him through Christ? If so, come to His table with gratitude and self-examination.
S: God told Moses and Aaron that no stranger outside the covenant should eat the Passover. The meal belonged to those in covenant relationship with God, though anyone could enter it. Come to the Lord's table as one who belongs to Christ, with gratitude and self-examination.
P: A meal for the family. Exodus 12:43: 'This is the ordinance of the passover: There shall no stranger eat thereof.' Come to Christ's table as one who belongs to Him.

== 12:49
T: One law
R: 'One law shall be to him that is homeborn, and unto the stranger that sojourneth among you.' God declared one law for both native Israelites and foreigners living among them. There would be no double standard. Everyone in the covenant community was treated equally under God's law. This was remarkable in the ancient world. Most nations had different rules for citizens and outsiders. Foreigners were often exploited. But God demanded equal justice. Leviticus 19 says, the stranger that dwelleth with you shall be unto you as one born among you, and thou shalt love him as thyself. God cares about fairness. He does not show favoritism. Acts 10 says, God is no respecter of persons. His people must reflect His justice. Treating outsiders fairly is a mark of God's people. Today, examine how you treat those who are different from you. Do you apply the same standards to everyone? Let God's one law shape your sense of fairness and justice.

== 12:50
T: Thus did all
R: 'Thus did all the children of Israel; as the LORD commanded Moses and Aaron, so did they.' All the children of Israel did exactly as the LORD had commanded. The whole nation obeyed. This verse repeats an earlier statement, emphasizing that Israel's obedience was complete and unified. This was a high point for Israel. Sadly, their obedience would not always be so complete. In the wilderness, they would complain and rebel. But on this night, they obeyed fully. And God delivered them. Moments of united obedience are precious. When God's people obey together, His power is displayed. Acts 2 describes the early church: they were all with one accord in one place. Unity in obedience brings blessing. Notice the phrase thus did all. Not some, but all. Every household participated. Today, pray for unity in obedience among God's people. And do your part. Let it be said of your life: as the LORD commanded, so did they.

== 12:51
T: Brought out by their armies
R: 'And it came to pass the selfsame day, that the LORD did bring the children of Israel out of the land of Egypt by their armies.' On that very day, the LORD brought Israel out of Egypt in orderly ranks, by their armies. The great deliverance was accomplished. After four hundred thirty years, ten plagues, and one terrible night, Israel was free. Notice who did it: the LORD did bring. Moses was the leader, but God was the deliverer. Israel did not fight their way out. They walked out because God set them free. Salvation is God's work. Notice also the phrase by their armies. They left in order, with dignity. Former slaves marched out like an organized army. God transforms the identity of those He saves. Psalm 105 says, He brought them forth also with silver and gold: and there was not one feeble person among their tribes. Today, celebrate the God who saves. He brings His people out with dignity, order, and power. Your freedom is His gift.
