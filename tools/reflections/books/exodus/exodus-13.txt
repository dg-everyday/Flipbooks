# Exodus 13: The firstborn set apart; the pillar of cloud and fire

== 13:1
T: The LORD spoke after deliverance
R: 'And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying.' Right after the exodus, God spoke to Moses again. Israel had just been freed. Now God had instructions for their new life. Deliverance was not the end of God's speaking; it was the beginning of a new relationship. God does not save us and then fall silent. He continues to guide, teach, and shape His people. Salvation is the start of a life of listening. Many think of faith as a single moment of rescue. But God intends an ongoing conversation. John 10 says, My sheep hear my voice, and I know them, and they follow me. The freed Israelites would need God's word for every step ahead. They knew how to be slaves, but they did not yet know how to be God's people. He would teach them. Today, keep listening to God after your moments of deliverance. He has more to say. Let His word shape your new life in Him.
S: Right after the exodus, the LORD spoke to Moses again with instructions for Israel's new life. Deliverance was the beginning of an ongoing relationship, not the end of God's speaking. Keep listening to God after He rescues you and let His word shape your new life.
P: God keeps speaking. Exodus 13:1: 'And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying.' Salvation starts a life of listening. Let God's word shape your new life today.

== 13:2
T: It is mine
R: 'Sanctify unto me all the firstborn, whatsoever openeth the womb among the children of Israel, both of man and of beast: it is mine.' God claimed every firstborn in Israel, both human and animal. Sanctify them unto Me; they are Mine. Because God spared Israel's firstborn on Passover night, they belonged to Him in a special way. Redemption creates ownership. The firstborn were alive because of the blood of the lamb. Their lives were a gift from God, and therefore they belonged to Him. This principle applies to every believer. First Corinthians 6 says, ye are not your own? For ye are bought with a price: therefore glorify God in your body. Notice the simple phrase it is mine. God claims what He has redeemed. This is not harsh possession but loving relationship. Isaiah 43 says, I have redeemed thee, I have called thee by thy name; thou art mine. Today, remember that you belong to God. He has redeemed you. Offer your life to Him gladly as the one who is His.
S: God told Moses to sanctify every firstborn of man and beast, declaring, It is mine. Because He spared Israel's firstborn, they belonged to Him in a special way. Remember that God has redeemed you and offer your life gladly as His.
P: It is mine. Exodus 13:2: 'Sanctify unto me all the firstborn… it is mine.' God claims what He redeems. You belong to Him. Offer Him your life today.

== 13:3
T: Remember this day
R: 'And Moses said unto the people, Remember this day, in which ye came out from Egypt, out of the house of bondage; for by strength of hand the LORD brought you out from this place.' Moses called the people to remember this day: the day they came out of the house of bondage. And he reminded them who did it. By strength of hand the LORD brought you out. Remembering is a spiritual discipline. The human heart forgets quickly. Within weeks, Israel would complain in the wilderness, forgetting God's power. Moses knew they needed to remember. Remembering God's past faithfulness strengthens faith for present challenges. Psalm 103 says, Bless the LORD, O my soul, and forget not all his benefits. Notice also that Moses called Egypt the house of bondage. He named it honestly. Israel should never romanticize Egypt. Later they would long for its food, forgetting its chains. Today, remember the day God set you free. Name your old bondage honestly. And recall with gratitude the strong hand that brought you out.
S: Moses told the people to remember the day they came out of Egypt, the house of bondage, by the LORD's strong hand. Remembering God's faithfulness strengthens faith and guards against romanticizing the past. Remember the day God set you free and thank Him for His strong hand.
P: Remember this day. Exodus 13:3: 'Remember this day, in which ye came out from Egypt, out of the house of bondage.' Don't romanticize your old chains. Remember God's strong hand.

== 13:4
T: The month Abib
R: 'This day came ye out in the month Abib.' Moses named the month: Abib. The word means fresh ears of grain, the season of spring when new crops appear. Israel's deliverance came in a season of new life. The timing was symbolic. Spring is the season of renewal. After winter, life returns. Israel's freedom came when the fields were turning green. God's redemption brings new life. Centuries later, Jesus would rise from the dead in this same season, during Passover. The resurrection brought new life in spring. First Corinthians 15 calls Christ the firstfruits of them that slept. Naming the month also made the memory specific. Israel was not to remember a vague event but a real day in a real month. Faith is rooted in specific moments of God's work. Today, recall the specific moments when God has worked in your life. And rejoice that in Christ, every season of redemption is a season of new life.
S: Moses told Israel they came out in the month Abib, the season of fresh ears of grain. Their deliverance came in a season of new life, the same season in which Jesus would rise. Recall the specific moments God has worked in your life and rejoice in His new life.
P: A season of new life. Exodus 13:4: 'This day came ye out in the month Abib.' Freedom came in spring, the same season Jesus rose. God brings new life!

== 13:5
T: Keep this service there
R: 'And it shall be when the LORD shall bring thee into the land of the Canaanites… which he sware unto thy fathers to give thee, a land flowing with milk and honey, that thou shalt keep this service in this month.' Moses looked ahead to the promised land. When God brings you into the land flowing with milk and honey, keep this service. Israel was to remember their deliverance even in times of abundance. Prosperity can make us forget. Deuteronomy 8 warns, when thou hast eaten and art full… Then thine heart be lifted up, and thou forget the LORD thy God. The Passover was a safeguard against forgetting. Every year, in the midst of blessing, they would remember the bondage God saved them from. Notice the phrase which he sware unto thy fathers. The land was a gift based on God's oath, not Israel's merit. Today, keep remembering God in seasons of blessing. Do not let abundance make you forget the One who gave it.
S: Moses told Israel that when God brought them into the land flowing with milk and honey, they should keep this service. Remembrance would guard them against forgetting God in prosperity. Keep remembering God in seasons of blessing and do not let abundance make you forget Him.
P: Remember in times of plenty. Exodus 13:5: 'a land flowing with milk and honey, that thou shalt keep this service.' Don't let blessing make you forget the Giver.

== 13:6
T: A feast to the LORD
R: 'Seven days thou shalt eat unleavened bread, and in the seventh day shall be a feast to the LORD.' For seven days, Israel would eat unleavened bread. On the seventh day there would be a feast to the LORD. The week would end in celebration. Remembrance was not meant to be gloomy. It was a feast. Seven in Scripture often represents completeness. The full week of unleavened bread pictured a complete life of purity. Then came the feast on the seventh day, a celebration unto the LORD. Holiness leads to joy. We put away sin, not to live grimly, but to celebrate God more fully. Psalm 16 says, in thy presence is fulness of joy. Nehemiah 8 says, the joy of the LORD is your strength. God's people are a celebrating people. Our worship should include gladness. Today, remember that holiness and joy go together. Put away what does not belong, and celebrate the God who saved you.
S: Israel was to eat unleavened bread for seven days, ending on the seventh day with a feast to the LORD. Remembrance and holiness led to celebration. Put away what does not belong and celebrate with joy the God who saved you.
P: Holiness leads to joy. Exodus 13:6: 'in the seventh day shall be a feast to the LORD.' Putting away sin leads to celebration. Rejoice in the God who saved you today.

== 13:7
T: No leaven seen
R: 'Unleavened bread shall be eaten seven days; and there shall no leavened bread be seen with thee, neither shall there be leaven seen with thee in all thy quarters.' During the feast, no leaven was to be seen anywhere in Israel's territory. Not just avoided in eating, but not even present. The removal was to be complete and visible. Notice the phrase seen with thee. God wanted leaven not only gone from their plates but from their homes and borders. Holiness is thorough. It is not enough to avoid sin in public while keeping it nearby. Many people try to keep sin within reach, just in case. But God calls us to remove it completely. First Thessalonians 5 says, Abstain from all appearance of evil. Romans 13 says, make not provision for the flesh. Leaving temptation nearby makes falling easier. Removing it entirely protects us. Today, examine whether you are keeping any sin within reach. Remove it from your quarters. Make no provision for it.
S: During the feast no leavened bread or leaven was to be seen in all Israel's quarters. Removal was to be complete, not just avoidance at the table. Examine whether you are keeping sin within reach and remove it completely.
P: Out of sight, out of reach. Exodus 13:7: 'there shall no leavened bread be seen with thee… in all thy quarters.' Don't keep sin within reach. Make no provision for it.
H: #DailyGrace #Exodus137 #Holiness #Temptation #Purity #BibleVerse

== 13:8
T: What the LORD did for me
R: 'And thou shalt shew thy son in that day, saying, This is done because of that which the LORD did unto me when I came forth out of Egypt.' Parents were to explain to their sons: we do this because of what the LORD did for me when I came out of Egypt. Notice the personal language: for me. Each generation would tell the story as its own. Even Israelites born centuries later would say, the LORD did this for me. The exodus was not just ancient history; it was personal testimony. This is how faith is passed on. Not as dry facts but as personal story. When we tell our children what God has done for us, faith becomes real to them. Psalm 66 says, Come and hear, all ye that fear God, and I will declare what he hath done for my soul. Your testimony matters. Today, tell someone, especially a child, what the LORD has done for you. Make it personal. Let them hear God's faithfulness in your own words.
S: Parents were to tell their sons that they kept the feast because of what the LORD did for me when I came out of Egypt. Each generation was to tell the story as personal testimony. Tell a child today what the LORD has done for you, in your own words.
P: What God did for me. Exodus 13:8: 'This is done because of that which the LORD did unto me.' Faith passes on through personal testimony. Share your story with someone today.

== 13:9
T: A sign on thine hand
R: 'And it shall be for a sign unto thee upon thine hand, and for a memorial between thine eyes, that the LORD'S law may be in thy mouth: for with a strong hand hath the LORD brought thee out of Egypt.' The feast would be like a sign on the hand and a reminder between the eyes. The purpose was that the LORD's law would be in their mouths. Remembrance would shape their actions, thoughts, and words. The hand represents what we do. The eyes represent what we see and think about. The mouth represents what we say. Remembering His salvation should affect our deeds, our focus, and our speech. Colossians 3 says, whatsoever ye do in word or deed, do all in the name of the Lord Jesus. Today, let God's salvation shape your hands, your eyes, and your mouth. Let what He has done for you show in what you do, think, and say.

== 13:10
T: From year to year
R: 'Thou shalt therefore keep this ordinance in his season from year to year.' Israel was to keep the Passover in its season, year after year. It was not a one-time event but a rhythm of remembrance. Every spring, the story would be retold. Spiritual rhythms help faith endure. Regular practices keep God's work fresh in our minds. Weekly worship, daily prayer, annual celebrations like Easter and Christmas all serve this purpose. They anchor our faith in repeated remembrance. Without rhythms, faith can drift. We get busy and forget. But regular practices pull us back to what matters most. Notice the phrase in his season. There is a right time for each remembrance. Ecclesiastes 3 says, To every thing there is a season. Today, build healthy spiritual rhythms into your life. Make time regularly to remember what God has done. Let year-to-year practices anchor your faith for the long journey.
S: Israel was to keep the Passover ordinance in its season from year to year. Regular rhythms of remembrance would keep God's work fresh and help faith endure. Build healthy spiritual rhythms into your life to anchor your faith.

== 13:11
T: As he sware
R: 'And it shall be when the LORD shall bring thee into the land of the Canaanites, as he sware unto thee and to thy fathers, and shall give it thee.' Moses again spoke of the future: when the LORD brings you into Canaan, as He swore to you and your fathers, and gives it to you. God's oath was the foundation of Israel's future. Notice the phrase unto thee and to thy fathers. The promise was made to the patriarchs, but it also belonged to this generation. God's promises are personal to each generation that receives them. Also notice and shall give it thee. The land was a gift. Israel would have to fight battles, but ultimately the land would be God's gift, not their achievement. Hebrews 6 says God confirmed His promise by an oath, so that we might have a strong consolation. God's oath is unbreakable. Today, rest on God's promises as personally yours. What He swore to His people throughout history, He offers to you in Christ.
S: Moses spoke of when the LORD would bring Israel into Canaan, as He swore to them and their fathers, and give it to them. God's oath made the promise personal to each generation and secured it as a gift. Rest on God's promises as personally yours in Christ.

== 13:12
T: Set apart unto the LORD
R: 'That thou shalt set apart unto the LORD all that openeth the matrix, and every firstling that cometh of a beast which thou hast; the males shall be the LORD'S.' In the land, Israel was to set apart every firstborn male to the LORD, including the firstborn of their animals. The first belonged to God. The principle of firstfruits runs through Scripture. The first and best belongs to God. Giving Him the first is an act of trust. It says we believe more will come, and that He is the source. Proverbs 3 says, Honour the LORD with thy substance, and with the firstfruits of all thine increase. Giving God our leftovers says something very different from giving Him our first. Our time, money, energy, and attention can all be offered first to God. Matthew 6 says, seek ye first the kingdom of God. Today, consider what you give God first. Offer Him the beginning of your day, the first portion of your income, and the first place in your heart.
S: Israel was to set apart to the LORD every firstborn male of people and animals. The first belonged to God as an act of trust in Him as the source. Give God your first and best, not your leftovers.
P: The first belongs to God. Exodus 13:12: 'thou shalt set apart unto the LORD all that openeth the matrix.' Give God your first, not your leftovers. Seek Him first today.

== 13:13
T: Redeem with a lamb
R: 'And every firstling of an ass thou shalt redeem with a lamb; and if thou wilt not redeem it, then thou shalt break his neck: and all the firstborn of man among thy children shalt thou redeem.' A firstborn donkey was unclean and could not be sacrificed. So it had to be redeemed with a lamb. If not redeemed, it had to die. And every firstborn son was to be redeemed as well. This pictures our condition. We too are unfit to offer ourselves to God. We need a substitute. Without redemption, death is the result. But a lamb can take our place. Every firstborn son also needed redemption. Luke 2 records that Mary and Joseph brought Jesus to the temple to present him to the Lord, as the law required. Jesus, though needing no redemption, fulfilled the law. Then He became the Lamb who redeems us. Today, thank God that a Lamb has redeemed you. Without Him, you would perish. Through Him, you live.
S: A firstborn donkey had to be redeemed with a lamb or else its neck broken, and every firstborn son had to be redeemed. Redemption by a substitute was the only alternative to death. Thank God that Christ the Lamb has redeemed you.
P: Redeemed or destroyed. Exodus 13:13: 'every firstling of an ass thou shalt redeem with a lamb.' No third option. Thank God that the Lamb has redeemed you!

God designed Israel's practices to raise questions. The answers always pointed back to God's saving work. Notice how short the answer is. A child's question does not need a long lecture. It needs a clear, heartfelt answer about what God has done. Proverbs 22 says, Train up a child in the way he should go. Second Timothy 3 says Timothy knew the holy scriptures from a child. Today, answer the spiritual questions of children simply and sincerely. Point them to what God has done. Their questions are seeds you can water.

This shows how deeply the exodus shaped Israel's identity. Salvation is not a single event to be filed away. It shapes everything. Second Corinthians 5 says, if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature. Today, let God's redemption shape every part of your life. Your very existence as a believer is a testimony to the Lamb who saved you.

First Corinthians 10 says God will not suffer you to be tempted above that ye are able. God's longer paths are acts of love. Today, if God is leading you a longer way than expected, trust Him. He knows what you are ready for. The long way may be protecting you.

== 13:18

R: 'But God led the people about, through the way of the wilderness of the Red sea: and the children of Israel went up harnessed out of the land of Egypt.' God led them the roundabout way, through the wilderness toward the Red Sea. And Israel left Egypt prepared for battle. They were armed and organized, though not yet ready to fight. The wilderness route would have its own challenges. But it would be the place where God taught them to trust Him. Manna, water from the rock, and the law at Sinai all happened in the wilderness. The detour was God's classroom. Notice the word harnessed, meaning arranged in ranks. They were equipped, but God still protected them from fighting too soon. Character and trust take time to develop. Deuteronomy 8 says God led them in the wilderness to humble thee, and to prove thee. Today, if you are in a wilderness season, recognize it as God's classroom. He is preparing you through the long way.
S: God led the people about through the wilderness toward the Red Sea, and they went up arranged in ranks out of Egypt. The wilderness would become God's classroom for teaching them trust. If you are in a wilderness season, recognize it as God's place of preparation.

== 13:19

Moses took Joseph's bones with him. Joseph had made Israel swear to carry his remains out of Egypt when God visited them. Centuries later, the promise was kept. Joseph's faith was finally rewarded. This is a beautiful moment of fulfilled faith. Joseph believed God would visit His people, even though he died long before it happened. His bones waited in a coffin for generations. Now they were carried toward the promised land. Hebrews 11 highlights this: By faith Joseph… gave commandment concerning his bones. Joseph's faith outlived him. His request became a testimony to generations. Every Israelite who carried or saw his coffin was reminded that God keeps His promises. Today, live with a faith that looks forward. Your trust in God's promises can inspire others long after you are gone.

== 13:22

His guiding presence remained constant throughout the journey. Even when Israel grumbled and rebelled, the pillar stayed. This is remarkable faithfulness. Israel would complain many times in the wilderness. They would doubt, rebel, and even worship a golden calf. Yet God did not take away His presence. Nehemiah 9 recalls this: thou in thy manifold mercies forsookest them not in the wilderness: the pillar of the cloud departed not from them by day. God's faithfulness is greater than our failures. He does not abandon His people when they stumble. Hebrews 13 says, I will never leave thee, nor forsake thee. Today, take comfort in God's constant presence. Even on your worst days, He has not taken away His guidance. He is still before you, leading you home.
