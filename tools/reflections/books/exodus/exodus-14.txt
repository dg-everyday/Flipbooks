# Exodus 14: Crossing the Red Sea

== 14:1
T: Fresh direction
R: 'And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying.' At the edge of the wilderness, God spoke again. Israel had followed the pillar of cloud, and now God had specific new directions. The journey would require step-by-step guidance. God does not give us the whole map at once. He leads one step at a time. Israel had to keep listening, because each stage brought new instructions. Psalm 119 says, Thy word is a lamp unto my feet. A lamp lights the next step, not the whole road. This keeps us dependent on God. If we knew everything in advance, we might stop listening. Instead, God invites daily dependence. Proverbs 3 says, In all thy ways acknowledge him, and he shall direct thy paths. Today, listen for God's next instruction. You do not need to see the whole journey. Trust Him for the next step.
S: At the edge of the wilderness, the LORD spoke to Moses again with fresh directions. God leads His people one step at a time, keeping them dependent on Him. Listen for God's next instruction and trust Him for the next step.
P: One step at a time. Exodus 14:1: 'And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying.' God's word is a lamp for the next step, not the whole road. Trust Him today.

== 14:2
T: Turn and encamp by the sea
R: 'Speak unto the children of Israel, that they turn and encamp… between Migdol and the sea… before it shall ye encamp by the sea.' God gave a strange command. Turn back and camp by the sea. This put Israel in a place where they would be trapped between the water and the desert. It made no military sense. Sometimes God leads us into places that look like dead ends. Israel's position seemed foolish. Why camp with your back to the sea? But God had a plan they could not see. He was setting the stage for the greatest miracle of their journey. Isaiah 55 says, my thoughts are not your thoughts, neither are your ways my ways. Obedience sometimes means following instructions that make no sense to us. Today, if God has led you to a place that looks like a dead end, trust Him. He may be preparing a way where there seems to be none.
S: God told Israel to turn back and encamp by the sea, a place that would leave them trapped. It made no military sense, but God was setting the stage for a great miracle. If God has led you to what looks like a dead end, trust that He is preparing a way.
P: Led to a dead end? Exodus 14:2: 'before it shall ye encamp by the sea.' It looked foolish, but God had a plan. He may be preparing a way where there seems none.

== 14:3
T: Entangled in the land
R: 'For Pharaoh will say of the children of Israel, They are entangled in the land, the wilderness hath shut them in.' God explained His strategy. Pharaoh would see Israel camped by the sea and conclude that they were lost and trapped. He would think the wilderness had shut them in. What Pharaoh would see as weakness was actually God's setup. Appearances can deceive. To Pharaoh, Israel looked confused and vulnerable. In reality, they were exactly where God wanted them. Our enemies may look at our situation and think we are finished. But God sees what they do not. First Corinthians 1 says, God hath chosen the foolish things of the world to confound the wise. What looks like being trapped may be the place of God's greatest victory. Today, do not judge your situation by how it looks to others. If you are where God led you, you are not lost, even if it seems so.
S: God said Pharaoh would think Israel was entangled in the land and shut in by the wilderness. What looked like weakness was God's setup for victory. Do not judge your situation by appearances, for if God led you there, you are not lost.
P: Trapped or positioned? Exodus 14:3: 'They are entangled in the land, the wilderness hath shut them in.' Pharaoh saw weakness; God saw victory. You're exactly where He wants you.

== 14:4
T: I will be honoured
R: 'And I will harden Pharaoh's heart, that he shall follow after them; and I will be honoured upon Pharaoh, and upon all his host; that the Egyptians may know that I am the LORD. And they did so.' God revealed His purpose. Pharaoh would pursue, and God would gain honor through Pharaoh and his army. Egypt would know that He is the LORD. And Israel obeyed, camping where God said. God's glory was the goal. The final confrontation at the sea would display His power more than any plague. Notice the short phrase at the end: And they did so. Israel obeyed even though they did not understand. They camped in a vulnerable place because God said to. Obedience often comes before understanding. John 13 records Jesus saying, What I do thou knowest not now; but thou shalt know hereafter. Today, obey God even when His plan is unclear. His purpose is to reveal His glory, and your obedience is part of it.
S: God said He would harden Pharaoh's heart so he would pursue, and God would be honored over Pharaoh and his host. Israel obeyed and camped where God said, though they did not understand. Obey God even when His plan is unclear, for His purpose is His glory.

== 14:5
T: Why have we done this?
R: 'And it was told the king of Egypt that the people fled: and the heart of Pharaoh and of his servants was turned against the people, and they said, Why have we done this, that we have let Israel go from serving us?' When Pharaoh heard that Israel had truly left, he and his servants changed their minds. Why did we let our slaves go? Their grief turned quickly to regret over lost labor. Sin's hold is slow to release. Pharaoh had just lost his firstborn. Yet within days, he regretted letting Israel go. Notice what they missed: serving us. Egypt valued Israel only as a labor force. The enemy does not easily let go of what he once held. Galatians 5 says, Stand fast therefore in the liberty wherewith Christ hath made us free, and be not entangled again. Today, expect old bondages to try to pull you back. Stand firm in the freedom God has given you.
S: When Pharaoh learned that Israel had fled, he and his servants regretted letting their slaves go. Even after all the plagues, they still saw Israel only as labor. Expect old bondages to try to pull you back, and stand firm in the freedom Christ gives.

== 14:6
T: He made ready his chariot
R: 'And he made ready his chariot, and took his people with him.' Pharaoh prepared his chariot and gathered his army. He would personally lead the pursuit. The king who had begged Israel to leave was now chasing them. Pharaoh's change of heart was swift. His grief and fear gave way to anger and pride. He could not stand to lose. Hardened hearts often return to old patterns quickly. Pharaoh had seen ten plagues and lost his son. Yet he went back to fighting God. This shows how deeply pride can take root. Proverbs 26 says, As a dog returneth to his vomit, so a fool returneth to his folly. Yet God had foretold this. Pharaoh's pursuit was walking straight into God's plan. Today, beware of returning to old patterns after God has dealt with them. And trust that even when enemies pursue, God is already ahead of them.
S: Pharaoh made ready his chariot and took his army to pursue Israel personally. His grief gave way to pride, and he returned to fighting God as foretold. Beware of returning to old patterns, and trust that God is already ahead of those who pursue you.

== 14:7
T: Six hundred chosen chariots
R: 'And he took six hundred chosen chariots, and all the chariots of Egypt, and captains over every one of them.' Pharaoh assembled six hundred of his best chariots, plus all the other chariots of Egypt, each with officers. This was the most advanced military force in the world. Against unarmed former slaves, it seemed overwhelming. Chariots were the tanks of the ancient world. Israel had nothing to match them. Humanly, the situation was hopeless. But God's people do not measure their strength by human weapons. Psalm 20 says, Some trust in chariots, and some in horses: but we will remember the name of the LORD our God. The chariots that seemed invincible would soon be at the bottom of the sea. Earthly power always looks impressive until it meets God. Today, if you face what seems like an overwhelming force, remember the name of the LORD. He is greater than every chariot arrayed against you.
S: Pharaoh took six hundred chosen chariots and all the chariots of Egypt with captains over them. Against unarmed former slaves, the force seemed overwhelming. When you face what seems overwhelming, remember the name of the LORD, who is greater than every chariot.

== 14:8
T: With an high hand
R: 'And the LORD hardened the heart of Pharaoh king of Egypt, and he pursued after the children of Israel: and the children of Israel went out with an high hand.' Pharaoh pursued Israel. But Israel had gone out with a high hand, meaning boldly and confidently. They left Egypt not as fugitives sneaking away, but openly, in triumph. A high hand suggests a raised fist of victory or a hand lifted in confidence. Israel walked out of Egypt with their heads held high. This is how God's people should walk. Not in fear or shame, but with confidence in the God who saved them. Second Corinthians 2 says, thanks be unto God, which always causeth us to triumph in Christ. Yet notice the contrast. Israel left boldly, but they would soon panic when they saw the chariots. Confidence must be rooted in God, not in circumstances. Today, walk with a high hand. Your confidence comes from the God who has set you free.
S: The LORD hardened Pharaoh's heart and he pursued, but the children of Israel went out with an high hand, boldly and in triumph. They left not as fugitives but as people led by God. Walk today with confidence rooted in the God who set you free.

== 14:9
T: Overtaken by the sea
R: 'But the Egyptians pursued after them, all the horses and chariots of Pharaoh, and his horsemen, and his army, and overtook them encamping by the sea.' The Egyptian army caught up with Israel camped by the sea. Horses, chariots, horsemen, and soldiers closed in. Israel was trapped. Behind them was the army; before them was the sea. This is the moment of crisis. Everything seemed lost. Israel had nowhere to go. Many believers have known moments like this: trapped between impossible circumstances with no visible way out. Yet this was the very place God had chosen. He had led them here deliberately. The crisis was not a mistake but a setup for deliverance. Second Corinthians 1 records Paul saying they had the sentence of death in themselves, that we should not trust in ourselves, but in God which raiseth the dead. Today, if you feel trapped between impossible circumstances, remember that God may have led you here to show His power. Your crisis is His opportunity.
S: The Egyptian army with all Pharaoh's horses and chariots overtook Israel encamped by the sea. Trapped between army and water, they had nowhere to go, yet God had led them there. If you feel trapped, remember that your crisis may be God's opportunity to show His power.

== 14:10

Israel looked up and saw the Egyptians marching toward them. They were terrified. And they cried out to the LORD. Their confidence vanished the moment they saw the army. Fear is a natural response to danger. Israel was not wrong to feel afraid. The danger was real. What matters is what we do with our fear. Israel cried out to the LORD. That was the right first response. Psalm 56 says, What time I am afraid, I will trust in thee. Fear can drive us toward God or away from Him. Notice that they lifted up their eyes, but they looked at the Egyptians. Where we look shapes how we feel. Hebrews 12 says, Looking unto Jesus. Today, when fear rises, cry out to God. And lift your eyes beyond the threat to the One who is greater.
S: When Pharaoh drew near, Israel saw the Egyptians marching after them and were sore afraid, so they cried out to the LORD. Fear is natural, but what matters is where it drives us. When fear rises, cry out to God and lift your eyes to the One who is greater.

After crying to God, the people turned on Moses with bitter sarcasm. Were there no graves in Egypt that you brought us here to die? Why did you bring us out? Fear quickly turned to blame. Panic distorts memory and judgment. Now they accused Moses of leading them to death. They forgot the plagues, the Passover, and God's promises. Fear can make us forget everything God has done. The sarcasm is painful. Leaders often absorb the fear of those they lead. Psalm 106 says, Our fathers understood not thy wonders in Egypt; they remembered not the multitude of thy mercies. Today, when fear tempts you to blame, pause and remember God's faithfulness. Do not let panic erase what He has done.

It would have been better to stay slaves than to die in the wilderness, they said. In their fear, bondage looked better than the unknown. This is a tragic moment. Today, resist the pull to return to familiar bondage. Freedom with God is better than security without Him, even when the path is frightening.

Prayer is essential, but it should not become a substitute for doing what God has said. Notice the direction: forward. Toward the sea. Toward the impossible. == 14:23
T: Into the sea after them
R: 'And the Egyptians pursued, and went in after them to the midst of the sea, even all Pharaoh's horses, his chariots, and his horsemen.' The Egyptians followed Israel into the sea. All of Pharaoh's horses, chariots, and horsemen charged onto the seabed after them. They were walking into God's trap. Pride blinds people to danger. The Egyptians saw the sea divided by God's power, yet they rushed in anyway. They assumed the path that protected Israel would also serve them. But God's way of salvation for His people was a way of judgment for His enemies. What saves the faithful can destroy the defiant.
