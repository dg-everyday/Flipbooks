# Exodus 15: The song of Moses; Marah and Elim

== 15:1
T: I will sing unto the LORD
R: 'Then sang Moses and the children of Israel this song unto the LORD, and spake, saying, I will sing unto the LORD, for he hath triumphed gloriously: the horse and his rider hath he thrown into the sea.' After the crossing, Moses and Israel burst into song. This is the first recorded song in the Bible. It celebrates God's glorious triumph over Egypt's army. Deliverance naturally leads to worship. When God acts, His people sing. Notice that the song is addressed unto the LORD. It is not about Israel's bravery but about God's victory. Revelation 15 says the redeemed in heaven sing the song of Moses the servant of God, and the song of the Lamb. Our salvation also calls for song. Psalm 40 says, he hath put a new song in my mouth, even praise unto our God. Today, sing to the LORD. Whatever He has delivered you from, let your heart respond with praise. He has triumphed gloriously.
S: After crossing the sea, Moses and Israel sang to the LORD, celebrating that He had triumphed gloriously and thrown horse and rider into the sea. This first recorded song in Scripture shows that deliverance leads to worship. Sing to the LORD today for all He has delivered you from.
P: Sing unto the LORD! Exodus 15:1: 'I will sing unto the LORD, for he hath triumphed gloriously.' Deliverance leads to worship. Let your heart sing praise today.

== 15:2
T: My strength and song
R: 'The LORD is my strength and song, and he is become my salvation: he is my God, and I will prepare him an habitation; my father's God, and I will exalt him.' The song becomes deeply personal. The LORD is my strength, my song, my salvation, my God. Israel did not only celebrate what God did; they declared who God is to them. Notice the progression. God is my strength in weakness, my song in celebration, and my salvation in danger. He is everything we need. Isaiah 12 and Psalm 118 repeat these same words, showing how central this confession became. Also notice my father's God. Israel connected their experience to their ancestors' faith. The God of Abraham had become their own God. Faith must become personal. It is not enough to know about our parents' God. We must be able to say, he is my God. Today, make this confession your own. The LORD is your strength, your song, and your salvation.
S: Israel sang that the LORD is my strength and song, my salvation and my God, and my father's God whom I will exalt. The God of their ancestors had become personally theirs. Make this confession your own: the LORD is your strength, song and salvation.
P: My strength and song. Exodus 15:2: 'The LORD is my strength and song, and he is become my salvation.' Make it personal. He is your God too. Praise Him today.

== 15:3
T: A man of war
R: 'The LORD is a man of war: the LORD is his name.' Israel praised God as a warrior. The LORD is a man of war. He fought for His people when they could not fight for themselves. And His name is the LORD, the covenant-keeping God. This image may surprise us. We often think of God as gentle. But Scripture also shows Him as a mighty warrior who defends His people and defeats evil. Both are true. He is gentle with the broken and fierce against oppression. Psalm 24 says, The LORD strong and mighty, the LORD mighty in battle. Revelation 19 pictures Christ riding a white horse, judging and making war in righteousness. God's warrior nature is good news for the oppressed. He does not stand by while evil wins. He fights. Today, take comfort that the LORD is a warrior on your behalf. He fights against what oppresses you. His name is the LORD, and He never loses.
S: Israel sang that the LORD is a man of war and that the LORD is His name. He is both gentle with the broken and fierce against oppression. Take comfort that the LORD fights on your behalf against what oppresses you.

== 15:4
T: Cast into the sea
R: 'Pharaoh's chariots and his host hath he cast into the sea: his chosen captains also are drowned in the Red sea.' The song recounts the victory: Pharaoh's chariots and army were thrown into the sea. His best officers drowned. Egypt's military elite was gone. The song names what God defeated. Pharaoh's chariots, his host, his chosen captains. These were the symbols of Egypt's power and pride. All of them were swallowed by the sea. Praise is strengthened by specifics. Israel did not sing vaguely. They named exactly what God had done. When we thank God, it helps to be specific. What exactly has He delivered you from? What enemies has He defeated in your life? Psalm 136 recounts God's specific acts: To him which overthrew Pharaoh and his host in the Red sea: for his mercy endureth for ever. Today, praise God specifically. Name what He has done. Let your gratitude be detailed and personal.

== 15:5
T: Sank as a stone
R: 'The depths have covered them: they sank into the bottom as a stone.' The Egyptians sank to the bottom like a stone. The depths covered them completely. There would be no rising, no return, no further threat. The image of sinking like a stone emphasizes how complete the defeat was. Stones do not float back up. The enemy that had seemed so powerful was now gone forever. This is how God deals with what opposes His people. He does not leave enemies half-defeated. Nehemiah 9 recalls this moment: their persecutors thou threwest into the deeps, as a stone into the mighty waters. The image is also a picture of how God handles our sin. Micah 7 says He will cast all their sins into the depths of the sea. Our sins do not float back to accuse us. They are gone. Today, rejoice that what God defeats stays defeated. Your forgiven sins have sunk like a stone. They will not rise again.
S: Israel sang that the depths covered the Egyptians and they sank to the bottom as a stone. The defeat was complete, with no return or further threat. Rejoice that your forgiven sins have sunk like a stone and will not rise again.

== 15:6
T: Thy right hand

The right hand was the symbol of strength and skill. God's strength was on full display at the sea. The repetition of thy right hand, O LORD emphasizes the point. This victory came from God's hand, not Israel's. Twice they credited God's right hand. Psalm 98 says, his right hand, and his holy arm, hath gotten him the victory. In the New Testament, Jesus is seated at the right hand of God. Hebrews 1 says He sat down on the right hand of the Majesty on high. The place of power belongs to Christ. God's right hand also holds and protects His people. Isaiah 41 says, I will uphold thee with the right hand of my righteousness. Today, rest in the strength of God's right hand. It defeats your enemies and upholds you.

== 15:7
T: The greatness of thine excellency
R: 'And in the greatness of thine excellency thou hast overthrown them that rose up against thee: thou sentest forth thy wrath, which consumed them as stubble.' The song declares that God's excellency overthrew those who rose up against Him. His wrath consumed them like fire burning dry stubble. Notice the phrase rose up against thee. Pharaoh had not only opposed Israel; he had opposed God. To fight God's people is to fight God Himself. And no one can stand against Him. Stubble burns quickly and completely. The enemy that seemed so strong was as easily consumed as dry straw. Human power, however impressive, is fragile before God. Isaiah 40 says, all flesh is grass. Malachi 4 says the proud shall be stubble. God's excellency is His supreme greatness. Nothing compares to Him. Today, remember that those who rise up against God cannot stand. And humble yourself before His greatness rather than resisting it.

== 15:8
T: The floods stood upright
R: 'And with the blast of thy nostrils the waters were gathered together, the floods stood upright as an heap, and the depths were congealed in the heart of the sea.' The song describes the miracle poetically. With a blast from God's nostrils, the waters piled up. The floods stood upright like a heap. The depths became solid in the heart of the sea. Poetry captures what prose cannot. The wind that parted the sea is described as the breath of God. What seemed like a natural force was God's own breath. Creation responds instantly to Him. Genesis 2 says God breathed into Adam the breath of life. Here God's breath gathers the waters. The same breath creates and delivers. Psalm 33 says, By the word of the LORD were the heavens made; and all the host of them by the breath of his mouth. Today, marvel at the power of God's breath. The One who stood the waters upright can certainly handle what overwhelms you.
S: Israel sang that with the blast of God's nostrils the waters were gathered, the floods stood upright as a heap, and the depths congealed. == 15:9
T: The enemy said
R: 'The enemy said, I will pursue, I will overtake, I will divide the spoil; my lust shall be satisfied upon them; I will draw my sword, my hand shall destroy them.' The song quotes the enemy's boast. I will pursue. I will overtake. I will divide the spoil. I will satisfy myself. I will destroy them. Six times the enemy said I will. Pride is full of I will. Pharaoh's army was confident of victory. They imagined the plunder they would take. But they had left God out of their plans. Their boasting was about to be silenced. Isaiah 14 records a similar boast from the king of Babylon: I will ascend… I will exalt my throne… I will be like the most High. And then it says, Yet thou shalt be brought down. James 4 says, ye ought to say, If the Lord will. Today, beware of the proud I will that leaves God out. And take comfort that every enemy boast against God's people will be silenced.
S: Israel's song quotes the enemy's boast, repeating I will pursue, overtake, divide the spoil and destroy them. Proud plans that left God out were about to be silenced. Beware of a proud I will that leaves God out, and trust that every boast against God's people will fail.

== 15:10

The sea covered them. They sank like lead in the mighty waters. The contrast is striking. The enemy made long, boastful plans. God answered with a single breath. All of Pharaoh's strategy was undone in a moment. Human pride is easily overwhelmed by divine power. Psalm 2 says, He that sitteth in the heavens shall laugh. God is not threatened by our enemies' boasts. Lead is heavy and sinks fast. The image emphasizes how swiftly and completely the enemy was defeated. There was no struggle, no escape. Today, do not be intimidated by boastful threats against you. One breath from God can undo every plan of the enemy. Trust Him, and let Him answer for you.

== 15:11
T: Who is like unto thee?
R: 'Who is like unto thee, O LORD, among the gods? who is like thee, glorious in holiness, fearful in praises, doing wonders?' The song reaches its high point with a question: Who is like You, O LORD, among the gods? The answer is clear: no one. God is glorious in holiness, awesome in praises, doing wonders. This question answered Pharaoh's earlier one. Pharaoh had asked, Who is the LORD? Now Israel declared that there is none like Him. Egypt's gods had been humiliated. The LORD alone is incomparable. Notice the three descriptions. Glorious in holiness: He is pure and set apart. Fearful in praises: He is worthy of awe. Doing wonders: He acts with power. Psalm 86 says, Among the gods there is none like unto thee, O Lord; neither are there any works like unto thy works. Today, worship the incomparable God. Let nothing in your life compete with Him. There is none like Him.

== 15:12
T: The earth swallowed them
R: 'Thou stretchedst out thy right hand, the earth swallowed them.' God stretched out His right hand, and the earth swallowed the Egyptians. The sea is described as the earth, meaning the depths below. The enemies disappeared into the ground beneath the waters. God's outstretched hand is a repeated image in Exodus. Moses stretched out his hand over the sea, but it was really God's hand at work. Human obedience and divine power worked together, but the power was God's. The swallowing is final. What the earth swallows does not return. Numbers 16 later records the earth opening to swallow Korah and his rebels. God has power over the very ground beneath our feet. All creation serves Him. This is both comforting and sobering. God protects His people with all of creation, and He judges His enemies with it too. Today, trust the God whose hand commands the earth and sea. Nothing in creation is outside His authority.
S: Israel sang that when God stretched out His right hand, the earth swallowed the enemy. God's outstretched hand commands all of creation for protection and judgment. Trust the God whose hand commands the earth and sea.

== 15:13
T: In thy mercy hast led
R: 'Thou in thy mercy hast led forth the people which thou hast redeemed: thou hast guided them in thy strength unto thy holy habitation.' The song turns from judgment to mercy. God led His redeemed people forth in mercy. He guided them by His strength toward His holy dwelling. Israel's journey was led by God's love. The word mercy here is the Hebrew word for steadfast, covenant love. God's loyal love was the reason for Israel's deliverance. They were not saved because they were good, but because God was faithful. Notice the two sides of God's character in this song. His power defeated Egypt. His mercy led Israel. Strength and love work together. Psalm 23 says, he leadeth me in the paths of righteousness for his name's sake. The destination was God's holy habitation, His dwelling place. God leads His people toward Himself. Today, follow God's leading with confidence. He leads you in love, guides you with strength, and is bringing you home to Himself.

The people of Philistia would tremble at the news. Israel's deliverance would become a testimony across the region. God's works are heard far and wide. What happened at the Red Sea did not stay at the Red Sea. News spread. Forty years later, Rahab in Jericho said in Joshua 2, we have heard how the LORD dried up the water of the Red sea. God's reputation went before His people. Our testimonies also travel. When God works in our lives, others hear. Some respond with fear, others with faith. Rahab heard the same news and chose to trust Israel's God. The same report produced terror in some and faith in others. Today, let God's work in your life become a testimony. Others are listening. R: 'For the horse of Pharaoh went in with his chariots and with his horsemen into the sea, and the LORD brought again the waters of the sea upon them; but the children of Israel went on dry land in the midst of the sea.' After the song, Scripture summarizes the event once more. Pharaoh's horses, chariots, and horsemen went into the sea, and God brought the waters back on them. But Israel walked on dry land. The repetition is intentional. The word but marks the dividing line between perishing and being saved. This pattern runs through all of Scripture. John 3 says, he that believeth on him is not condemned: but he that believeth not is condemned already. The difference is God's grace received by faith. Israel walked on dry ground not because they were stronger, but because God made a way. Today, give thanks that God made a way for you. Where others were overwhelmed, He brought you through on dry ground.

After the celebration, Moses led Israel into the wilderness of Shur. For three days they traveled and found no water. The joy of the Red Sea gave way to the hardship of the desert. Mountaintop experiences are often followed by valleys. Israel had just witnessed one of history's greatest miracles. Three days later, they faced thirst. Victory does not mean the end of challenges. Deuteronomy 8 says God led them in the wilderness to prove thee, to know what was in thine heart. Testing reveals the depth of faith. == 15:23
T: Marah
R: 'And when they came to Marah, they could not drink of the waters of Marah, for they were bitter: therefore the name of it was called Marah.' Finally they found water at Marah. But it was bitter and undrinkable. The name Marah means bitter. After three days of thirst, they found water they could not drink. This is a painful disappointment. Hope rose when they saw water, then fell when they tasted it. Life brings such moments: answers that seem to come, only to disappoint. Bitterness can come from circumstances, and it can also take root in our hearts. Naomi said in Ruth 1, call me Mara: for the Almighty hath dealt very bitterly with me. Bitter experiences are real. Today, if you face bitter waters, bring them to God. Do not let bitter circumstances make your heart bitter.

== 15:24

Israel had seen the Red Sea part, yet they doubted God could provide water. Their need was real. Thirst in the desert is a matter of life and death. Their question was legitimate. First Corinthians 10 warns against murmuring, as some of them also murmured. Complaining reveals a lack of trust. Today, notice when you are tempted to grumble. Turn your complaint into prayer. == 15:25
T: A tree made the waters sweet

God provided, and He used the moment to test and teach His people. Moses' response models what to do with bitterness: cry to God. And God's answer was a tree. Many Christians have seen in this a picture of the cross. First Peter 2 says Christ bare our sins in his own body on the tree. The cross transforms the bitterness of life into sweetness. Notice also that God proved them there. Hard places are testing grounds. Today, when life is bitter, cry to the LORD. Let the cross of Christ transform your bitter waters into sweetness.

== 15:27
T: Twelve wells at Elim
R: 'And they came to Elim, where were twelve wells of water, and threescore and ten palm trees: and they encamped there by the waters.' After Marah came Elim. Twelve springs of water and seventy palm trees. A beautiful oasis. Israel camped there by the waters. After bitterness came refreshment. God does not leave us in Marah forever. He leads us to Elim. Seasons of testing are followed by seasons of rest. Psalm 23 says, He maketh me to lie down in green pastures: he leadeth me beside the still waters. Notice the numbers. Twelve wells, perhaps one for each tribe. Seventy palms, matching the seventy who came into Egypt. God's provision was abundant and fitted to His people. There was enough for everyone. Elim was not far from Marah. Sometimes relief is closer than we think. Today, if you are in a bitter season, take heart. Elim may be just ahead. God knows how to lead you to rest and refreshment.
