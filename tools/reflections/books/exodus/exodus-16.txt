# Exodus 16: Manna and quail; the Sabbath

== 16:1
T: The wilderness of Sin
R: 'And they took their journey from Elim, and all the congregation of the children of Israel came unto the wilderness of Sin, which is between Elim and Sinai, on the fifteenth day of the second month after their departing out of the land of Egypt.' Israel left the oasis of Elim and entered the wilderness of Sin, between Elim and Sinai. It had been one month since they left Egypt. We cannot stay at the oasis forever. Elim was a gift, but the journey had to continue. God refreshes us so that we can keep moving toward His purposes. Notice the precise date: the fifteenth day of the second month. Scripture records the journey carefully. God was with them on every specific day. Psalm 37 says, The steps of a good man are ordered by the LORD. Today, if you must leave a place of rest for a harder road, trust God for the journey. He refreshed you for a reason. Keep moving forward with Him.
S: Israel journeyed from Elim into the wilderness of Sin, one month after leaving Egypt. The comfort of the oasis gave way to another barren stretch, but the journey had to continue. Trust God when you must leave a place of rest, for He refreshed you to keep moving forward.
P: Leaving the oasis. Exodus 16:1: 'they took their journey from Elim… unto the wilderness of Sin.' God refreshes us so we can keep moving. Trust Him for the next stretch.

== 16:2
T: The whole congregation murmured
R: 'And the whole congregation of the children of Israel murmured against Moses and Aaron in the wilderness.' The entire congregation grumbled against Moses and Aaron. Not a few complainers, but the whole community. Hunger had set in, and frustration spread through the camp. Complaining is contagious. When a few start grumbling, others join. Soon the whole community is caught up in negativity. Numbers 11 shows the same pattern later. Murmuring spreads like a disease. Notice that they murmured against Moses and Aaron. They blamed their leaders. But the leaders were only following God. Ultimately, their complaint was against God Himself. Jude says some are murmurers, complainers, walking after their own lusts. A complaining spirit damages community and dishonors God. Philippians 2 says, Do all things without murmurings and disputings: That ye may be blameless. Today, guard against contagious complaining. When grumbling spreads around you, choose gratitude instead. Your thankful spirit can help turn the tide.
S: The whole congregation of Israel murmured against Moses and Aaron in the wilderness. Complaining spread through the camp and was ultimately against God. Guard against contagious complaining and choose gratitude instead.
P: Complaining spreads. Exodus 16:2: 'the whole congregation of the children of Israel murmured.' Grumbling is contagious. Choose gratitude, and help turn the tide today.

== 16:3
T: The flesh pots of Egypt
R: 'And the children of Israel said unto them, Would to God we had died by the hand of the LORD in the land of Egypt, when we sat by the flesh pots.' The people wished they had died in Egypt, where they sat by pots of meat and ate bread until full. They accused Moses of bringing them out to starve. Their memories of Egypt had become strangely sweet. Hunger rewrites history. Israel remembered the flesh pots but forgot the whips. They recalled full stomachs but not the slaughter of their sons. Nostalgia for bondage is a dangerous illusion. Many people romanticize their past life before Christ. They remember the pleasures of sin but forget its chains. Hebrews 11 says Moses chose rather to suffer affliction with the people of God, than to enjoy the pleasures of sin for a season. Today, do not let present hardship make your old bondage look good. Remember the chains, not just the flesh pots.
S: The people wished they had died in Egypt by the flesh pots where they ate bread to the full, and accused Moses of bringing them out to starve. Hunger made them romanticize bondage and forget its chains. Do not let present hardship make your old bondage look good.
P: Remember the chains. Exodus 16:3: 'when we sat by the flesh pots, and when we did eat bread to the full.' Hunger rewrote their history. Don't romanticize your old bondage.

T: Bread from heaven
R: 'Then said the LORD unto Moses, Behold, I will rain bread from heaven for you; and the people shall go out and gather a certain rate every day, that I may prove them, whether they will walk in my law, or no.' God answered their complaint with grace. I will rain bread from heaven for you. Each day they would gather a daily portion. This would also test whether they would follow God's instructions. God responded to grumbling with provision. He did not punish them; He fed them. Psalm 78 says He opened the doors of heaven, And had rained down manna upon them to eat. Notice the daily rhythm. They could gather only enough for each day. Jesus taught us to pray, Give us this day our daily bread. And He said in John 6, I am the bread of life. He is the true bread from heaven. Today, trust God for your daily provision. He gives what you need one day at a time.
S: God told Moses that He would rain bread from heaven, and the people would gather a daily portion to test whether they would walk in His law. He answered grumbling with gracious provision. Trust God for your daily bread, for He provides one day at a time.

== 16:5
T: Twice as much on the sixth day
R: 'And it shall come to pass, that on the sixth day they shall prepare that which they bring in; and it shall be twice as much as they gather daily.' On the sixth day, the people would gather twice as much. God was preparing them for the Sabbath, a day of rest when no manna would fall. He provided extra in advance so they could rest. God builds rest into His provision. He gave a double portion on the sixth day so that the seventh could be free. The former slaves had known no rest in Egypt. Now God provided enough so they could stop. Hebrews 4 speaks of a rest that remains for the people of God. God cares about our need for rest. He provides so that we do not have to labor without ceasing. Today, trust God enough to rest. He knows how to provide what you need so that you can stop and be refreshed in Him.
S: God said that on the sixth day the people would gather twice as much, preparing for the Sabbath. He built rest into His provision for former slaves who had known no rest. Trust God enough to rest, for He provides so that you can stop and be refreshed.

== 16:6
T: At even ye shall know
R: 'And Moses and Aaron said unto all the children of Israel, At even, then ye shall know that the LORD hath brought you out from the land of Egypt.' Moses and Aaron told the people that by evening they would know it was the LORD who brought them out of Egypt. The people had blamed Moses. Soon they would see that God was the one in charge. The people had said, ye have brought us forth. Moses corrected them: the LORD brought you out. Every provision would point back to God. Leaders must constantly redirect attention from themselves to God. Moses did not want credit or blame. He wanted the people to know God. John the Baptist said in John 3, He must increase, but I must decrease. Good leaders point beyond themselves. Today, when people look to you for answers, point them to God. Let your life direct attention to Him, not to yourself.
S: Moses and Aaron told Israel that by evening they would know the LORD had brought them out of Egypt. They redirected the people from blaming them to recognizing God. Point people to God rather than to yourself, letting your life direct attention to Him.

== 16:7
T: Ye shall see the glory
R: 'And in the morning, then ye shall see the glory of the LORD; for that he heareth your murmurings against the LORD: and what are we, that ye murmur against us?' In the morning, they would see the glory of the LORD. God had heard their murmuring. And Moses asked, Who are we, that you complain against us? Their complaints were really against God. Remarkably, God would show His glory in response to grumbling. He did not withdraw; He revealed Himself. His glory would appear in provision. This is grace beyond what we deserve. Notice that God heard their murmurings. Nothing we say escapes God. Our complaints reach Him, even when we direct them at others. Matthew 12 says, every idle word that men shall speak, they shall give account thereof. Moses' question, what are we, shows humility. He knew he was only a servant. Today, remember that God hears every word. Let your words honor Him. And thank Him that He shows His glory even to those who do not deserve it.

== 16:8
T: Not against us
R: 'And Moses said, This shall be, when the LORD shall give you in the evening flesh to eat, and in the morning bread to the full… your murmurings are not against us, but against the LORD.' Moses explained that God would give meat in the evening and bread in the morning. Then he made it clear: your complaints are not against us, but against the LORD. Moses repeated this truth for emphasis. When we complain about our circumstances, we are ultimately questioning God's goodness. Grumbling about leaders, situations, or provision is really grumbling about the One who is in control. This should make us careful. Complaining is not a harmless habit. It reflects distrust of God's care. Numbers 14 records God saying, How long will this evil congregation murmur against me? Yet God still promised to provide. Meat in the evening, bread in the morning. His faithfulness outweighed their complaints. Today, examine your complaints. Recognize that they are directed at God. Replace them with trust in His goodness.
S: Moses said the LORD would give meat in the evening and bread in the morning, and that their murmurings were not against Moses and Aaron but against the LORD. Complaining ultimately questions God's goodness. Examine your complaints and replace them with trust in His care.

== 16:9
T: Come near before the LORD
R: 'And Moses spake unto Aaron, Say unto all the congregation of the children of Israel, Come near before the LORD: for he hath heard your murmurings.' Moses told Aaron to call the people: Come near before the LORD, for He has heard your murmurings. Instead of pushing them away, God invited the complainers to come close. This is surprising grace. The people had grumbled against God. Yet God's response was an invitation to draw near. He did not reject them. He called them into His presence. God's heart is to draw people near, even when they have failed. James 4 says, Draw nigh to God, and he will draw nigh to you. Hebrews 4 says, Let us therefore come boldly unto the throne of grace. Our failures do not disqualify us from God's presence. They are reasons to come near and find grace. Today, if you have complained or doubted, do not stay away from God. Come near. He has heard you, and He invites you into His presence.
S: Moses told Aaron to call the congregation to come near before the LORD, for He had heard their murmurings. God responded to complainers with an invitation to draw close. If you have complained or doubted, do not stay away from God but come near to find grace.

Proverbs praises the ant that prepares. But it warns against anxious hoarding that replaces trust in God. Today, trust God for tomorrow. Receive today's provision with gratitude, and leave tomorrow in His hands.

== 16:26
T: Six days and the seventh
R: 'Six days ye shall gather it; but on the seventh day, which is the sabbath, in it there shall be none.' Moses established the pattern: gather for six days, but on the seventh day, the Sabbath, there will be none. Work and rest each had their place. The rhythm was set. Life with God has a rhythm. There is a time to work and a time to rest. Both are part of His design. Work is not a curse, and rest is not laziness. Together they form a healthy pattern. Exodus 20 would later make this a commandment: Six days shalt thou labour… But the seventh day is the sabbath of the LORD thy God. God modeled this in creation. Our bodies and souls need this rhythm. Constant work without rest leads to exhaustion. Constant rest without work leads to emptiness. Ecclesiastes 3 says, To every thing there is a season. Today, honor God's rhythm in your life. Work diligently, and rest faithfully. Both are acts of worship.
S: Moses said they would gather for six days, but on the seventh day, the Sabbath, there would be none. Work and rest each had their place in God's rhythm. Honor God's rhythm by working diligently and resting faithfully, both as acts of worship.

== 16:36
T: An omer
R: 'Now an omer is the tenth part of an ephah.' The chapter ends with a simple note: an omer is a tenth of an ephah. It is a measurement explanation for later readers. Yet even this small detail reminds us that Scripture was written for future generations. The writer wanted readers to understand. An omer was a modest portion, about what one person needed for a day. God's daily provision was measured, not extravagant. It was enough. God gives what we need, not always what we want. Proverbs 30 prays, give me neither poverty nor riches; feed me with food convenient for me. There is wisdom in being content with enough. Notice the tenth part. The number recalls the tithe, the tenth given back to God. Daily provision and faithful giving are connected in God's economy. Today, thank God for enough. Be content with His daily portion. And remember that He measures out exactly what you need for each day.
S: The chapter closes by explaining that an omer is the tenth part of an ephah, a modest daily portion. God's provision was measured to be enough, not extravagant. Thank God for enough and be content with His daily portion.
