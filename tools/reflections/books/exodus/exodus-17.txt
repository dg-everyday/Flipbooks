# Exodus 17: Water from the rock; victory over Amalek

== 17:1
T: No water at Rephidim
R: 'And all the congregation of the children of Israel journeyed from the wilderness of Sin… according to the commandment of the LORD, and pitched in Rephidim: and there was no water for the people to drink.' Israel moved on as the LORD commanded and camped at Rephidim. But there was no water. They had followed God's direction exactly, yet they arrived at a place of need. Obedience does not guarantee comfort. Israel went where God told them, and they still faced thirst. Being in God's will does not mean being free from hardship. Sometimes God leads us into places of need to teach us dependence. Notice the phrase according to the commandment of the LORD. This was not a wrong turn. God led them there on purpose. James 1 says, the trying of your faith worketh patience. Today, if you are in a place of need despite following God, do not assume you missed His will. He may have led you there to show His provision in a new way.
S: Israel journeyed as the LORD commanded and camped at Rephidim, but there was no water to drink. They had obeyed exactly, yet arrived at a place of need. If you face need while following God, trust that He may have led you there to reveal His provision.
P: Obedient but thirsty. Exodus 17:1: 'according to the commandment of the LORD… and there was no water.' Following God doesn't mean no hardship. He may be about to show His provision.

== 17:2
T: Why tempt the LORD?
R: 'Wherefore the people did chide with Moses, and said, Give us water that we may drink. And Moses said unto them, Why chide ye with me? wherefore do ye tempt the LORD?' The people argued with Moses and demanded water. Moses answered, Why do you quarrel with me? Why do you test the LORD? He saw that their complaint was really a challenge to God. To tempt the LORD means to test Him, demanding that He prove Himself. Israel had seen the plagues, the sea, the manna, and the quail. Yet they still questioned whether God would provide. Their demand was not a humble request but an accusation. There is a difference between bringing needs to God in faith and demanding that He prove Himself. Jesus refused to tempt God. In Matthew 4 He said, Thou shalt not tempt the Lord thy God. Faith asks humbly. Unbelief demands proof. Today, bring your needs to God humbly. Do not test Him with demands. Trust Him based on what He has already shown you.
S: The people quarreled with Moses and demanded water, and he asked why they chided him and tempted the LORD. Their demand challenged God to prove Himself despite all He had already done. Bring your needs to God humbly in faith rather than testing Him with demands.
P: Ask, don't demand. Exodus 17:2: 'Why chide ye with me? wherefore do ye tempt the LORD?' Faith asks humbly; unbelief demands proof. Trust God based on what He's shown you.

== 17:3
T: To kill us with thirst?
R: 'And the people thirsted there for water; and the people murmured against Moses, and said, Wherefore is this that thou hast brought us up out of Egypt, to kill us and our children and our cattle with thirst?' The people's thirst grew, and so did their anger. They accused Moses of bringing them out of Egypt to kill them, their children, and their animals. Fear for their families fueled their desperation. Their thirst was real. Watching children suffer is painful, and parents' fears are understandable. Scripture does not dismiss their need. But their response was to accuse, not to pray. Notice how quickly they assumed the worst. They did not ask, How will God provide? They asked, Why are you trying to kill us? Fear assumes evil intent. Faith assumes God's goodness. First Corinthians 13 says love believeth all things, hopeth all things. Today, when needs press hard, resist assuming the worst about God. He has not brought you this far to abandon you. Turn your fear into prayer.
S: As their thirst grew, the people accused Moses of bringing them from Egypt to kill them, their children and their cattle. Their fear was real, but it assumed the worst instead of turning to prayer. Resist assuming the worst about God and turn your fears into prayer.
P: Don't assume the worst. Exodus 17:3: 'Wherefore is this that thou hast brought us up out of Egypt, to kill us?' God hasn't brought you this far to abandon you. Pray.

== 17:4
T: What shall I do?
R: 'And Moses cried unto the LORD, saying, What shall I do unto this people? they be almost ready to stone me.' Moses cried out to God: What should I do with these people? They are about to stone me. The leader was in danger. He did not know what to do. So he took the problem straight to God. Moses modeled the right response. The people murmured; Moses prayed. He brought his honest fear and confusion to God. He did not try to solve it alone or retaliate. Leadership can be lonely and frightening. Moses was carrying the anger of an entire nation. Yet he turned to the One who could help. Psalm 55 says, Cast thy burden upon the LORD, and he shall sustain thee. Notice his question: What shall I do? Moses did not presume to know. He asked for direction. Today, if you feel overwhelmed by the people or problems in your care, cry out to God. Ask Him what to do. He will guide you.
S: Moses cried to the LORD, asking what he should do with the people who were almost ready to stone him. He brought his honest fear and confusion to God instead of retaliating. When you feel overwhelmed by people or problems, cry out to God and ask what to do.
P: What shall I do? Exodus 17:4: 'Moses cried unto the LORD, saying, What shall I do unto this people?' The people murmured; Moses prayed. Bring your burdens to God today.

== 17:5
T: Go on before the people
R: 'And the LORD said unto Moses, Go on before the people, and take with thee of the elders of Israel; and thy rod, wherewith thou smotest the river, take in thine hand, and go.' God answered Moses. Go ahead of the people. Take some elders with you. Take the rod you used to strike the Nile. And go. God gave clear, practical direction. Notice that God told Moses to go before the people. The people wanted to stone him, but God told him to lead. Leadership does not retreat in the face of hostility. It moves forward under God's direction. The rod was a reminder of God's past power. It had turned the Nile to blood. Now it would bring water. God reminded Moses of what He had already done. Past faithfulness builds confidence for present challenges. Also note the elders. Moses was not to act alone. Witnesses would see God's provision. Today, step forward in the direction God gives. Remember His past faithfulness. And invite others to witness His work.
S: God told Moses to go before the people with some elders and take the rod he used on the river. Moses was to lead forward, remembering God's past power. Step forward in God's direction, remember His faithfulness, and invite others to witness His work.
P: Lead forward. Exodus 17:5: 'Go on before the people… and thy rod, wherewith thou smotest the river, take in thine hand.' Remember God's past power and move forward.

== 17:6
T: Smite the rock
R: 'Behold, I will stand before thee there upon the rock in Horeb; and thou shalt smite the rock, and there shall come water out of it, that the people may drink. And Moses did so in the sight of the elders of Israel.' God promised to stand before Moses on the rock at Horeb. Moses would strike the rock, and water would flow out for the people. Moses obeyed, and the elders saw it. This is a powerful picture of Christ. First Corinthians 10 says, they drank of that spiritual Rock that followed them: and that Rock was Christ. The rock was struck, and life-giving water flowed. Christ was struck for us on the cross, and from Him flows living water. Jesus said in John 7, If any man thirst, let him come unto me, and drink. Notice also that God stood upon the rock. His presence was there. Today, come to Christ, the Rock who was struck for you. Drink deeply of the living water that flows from Him.
S: God promised to stand before Moses on the rock in Horeb, and when Moses struck it, water came out for the people. Paul says that Rock was Christ, struck so that living water might flow. Come to Christ, the Rock struck for you, and drink deeply of His living water.
P: Water from the Rock. Exodus 17:6: 'thou shalt smite the rock, and there shall come water out of it.' That Rock was Christ, struck for you. Come and drink today.

== 17:7
T: Is the LORD among us?
R: 'And he called the name of the place Massah, and Meribah, because of the chiding of the children of Israel, and because they tempted the LORD, saying, Is the LORD among us, or not?' Moses named the place Massah, meaning testing, and Meribah, meaning quarreling. The names recorded Israel's failure. They had asked, Is the LORD among us, or not? That question revealed their doubt. The cloud was over them. Manna fell every morning. Yet they asked whether God was with them. When hardship comes, we are tempted to ask the same question. Does God care? Is He really here? The names Massah and Meribah became warnings. Psalm 95 says, Harden not your heart, as in the provocation, and as in the day of temptation in the wilderness. Hebrews 3 repeats this warning. Doubting God's presence despite clear evidence is dangerous. Yet even here, God provided water. His presence was real whether they believed it or not. Today, when you wonder if God is with you, remember His faithfulness. He is among you.
S: Moses named the place Massah and Meribah because Israel quarreled and tested the LORD, asking whether He was among them. They doubted God's presence despite clear evidence. When you wonder if God is with you, remember His faithfulness, for He is among you.
P: Is the LORD among us? Exodus 17:7. They doubted while manna fell and the cloud led them. Don't harden your heart. God is with you, even in hardship.

== 17:8
T: Then came Amalek
R: 'Then came Amalek, and fought with Israel in Rephidim.' Right after the water from the rock, Amalek attacked Israel. Deuteronomy 25 tells us that Amalek struck the weak and weary at the back of the line. It was a cowardly attack on the vulnerable. New blessings are sometimes followed by new battles. Israel had just received water. Now they faced their first military enemy. The Christian life includes both provision and conflict. Ephesians 6 says, we wrestle not against flesh and blood. Amalek's tactic is worth noticing. They targeted the weakest, those lagging behind. The enemy often attacks us when we are tired, discouraged, or isolated. First Peter 5 warns that the devil walketh about, seeking whom he may devour. Stay close to the community. Do not lag behind alone. Today, be alert. After seasons of blessing, be ready for spiritual battles. And watch over those who are weary, lest they become easy targets.
S: Then Amalek came and fought with Israel at Rephidim, attacking the weak and weary at the rear. New blessing was followed by a new battle. Be alert after seasons of blessing, and watch over those who are weary.
P: After blessing, battle. Exodus 17:8: 'Then came Amalek, and fought with Israel.' Amalek attacked the weary. Stay alert, and watch over those lagging behind.

== 17:9
T: Choose us out men
R: 'And Moses said unto Joshua, Choose us out men, and go out, fight with Amalek: to morrow I will stand on the top of the hill with the rod of God in mine hand.' Moses gave Joshua his first assignment. Choose men and fight Amalek. Meanwhile, Moses would stand on the hilltop with the rod of God. This is the first time Joshua appears in Scripture. Joshua would one day lead Israel into Canaan. Here, his training began. Moses delegated the fighting to Joshua while he took a different role: standing before God with the rod. Both roles mattered. One fought in the valley; the other interceded on the hill. Spiritual battles require both action and prayer. Some fight on the front lines; others pray. Neither is complete without the other. Notice the rod of God. Moses trusted not in the army's strength but in God's power. Today, recognize your role in God's work. Whether you fight in the valley or pray on the hill, you are needed.
S: Moses told Joshua to choose men and fight Amalek while he stood on the hilltop with the rod of God. Joshua's training began as both action and intercession were needed. Recognize your role in God's work, whether fighting in the valley or praying on the hill.
P: Valley and hill. Exodus 17:9: 'fight with Amalek: to morrow I will stand on the top of the hill with the rod of God.' Action and prayer both matter. Find your role today.

== 17:10
T: Moses, Aaron, and Hur
R: 'So Joshua did as Moses had said to him, and fought with Amalek: and Moses, Aaron, and Hur went up to the top of the hill.' Joshua obeyed and fought Amalek. Moses, Aaron, and Hur climbed to the top of the hill. The battle had two fronts: physical combat below and spiritual support above. Joshua's obedience is notable. He did as Moses said. He did not question or delay. Faithful service begins with obedience to those God has placed over us. Notice that Moses did not go up alone. Aaron and Hur went with him. Even the great leader needed companions. Hur appears rarely in Scripture, but his presence here was crucial, as the next verses show. Ministry is a team effort. Ecclesiastes 4 says, Two are better than one… For if they fall, the one will lift up his fellow. Today, value those who stand with you. And be willing to stand with others, even in supporting roles that seem small.
S: Joshua did as Moses said and fought Amalek, while Moses, Aaron and Hur went up to the top of the hill. Joshua obeyed promptly, and Moses did not go up alone. Value those who stand with you, and be willing to support others in roles that seem small.
P: Not alone. Exodus 17:10: 'Moses, Aaron, and Hur went up to the top of the hill.' Even Moses needed companions. Stand with someone today.

== 17:11
T: When Moses held up his hand
R: 'And it came to pass, when Moses held up his hand, that Israel prevailed: and when he let down his hand, Amalek prevailed.' A remarkable pattern emerged. When Moses held up his hands, Israel won. When he lowered them, Amalek gained ground. The battle in the valley depended on what happened on the hill. Raised hands were a posture of prayer and dependence on God. The outcome of the battle was connected to Moses' intercession. This teaches the power of prayer. What happens in prayer affects what happens on the battlefield. James 5 says, The effectual fervent prayer of a righteous man availeth much. Our prayers matter more than we realize. They are connected to victories we may never see. Notice also that the rod of God was in Moses' hand. The raised rod declared that the battle belonged to God. First Timothy 2 says, I will therefore that men pray every where, lifting up holy hands. Today, lift up your hands in prayer. Your intercession matters in the battles others are fighting.
S: When Moses held up his hand, Israel prevailed, and when he let it down, Amalek prevailed. The battle in the valley was connected to intercession on the hill. Lift up your hands in prayer, for your intercession matters in the battles others fight.
P: Prayer affects the battle. Exodus 17:11: 'when Moses held up his hand, that Israel prevailed.' Your prayers matter more than you know. Lift your hands today.

== 17:12
T: Aaron and Hur stayed up his hands
R: 'But Moses' hands were heavy; and they took a stone, and put it under him, and he sat thereon; and Aaron and Hur stayed up his hands, the one on the one side, and the other on the other side; and his hands were steady until the going down of the sun.' Moses' arms grew tired. So Aaron and Hur sat him on a stone and held up his hands, one on each side. His hands stayed steady until sunset. Moses was a great leader, but he could not keep his hands raised alone. He needed help. Aaron and Hur provided it. Every leader grows weary. We need others to hold up our hands. Galatians 6 says, Bear ye one another's burdens. Notice that Aaron and Hur did not take Moses' place. They supported him in his role. Their help made his steadiness possible. Today, find someone whose hands are heavy and hold them up. Support your leaders, pray for them, and help them stand.

== 17:13
T: Joshua discomfited Amalek
R: 'And Joshua discomfited Amalek and his people with the edge of the sword.' Joshua defeated Amalek with the sword. The battle was won. Joshua fought in the valley, Moses interceded on the hill, and Aaron and Hur supported Moses. Everyone's role contributed to victory. Victory came through cooperation. Joshua's sword, Moses' prayer, and Aaron and Hur's support all worked together. No one could have won alone. This is how God's work is accomplished. The body of Christ functions this way. First Corinthians 12 says, the body is not one member, but many. Each person's contribution matters. Notice that Joshua fought with real weapons. Prayer did not replace action. God used both. Nehemiah 4 records the builders working with one hand and holding a weapon with the other, while also praying. Faith and effort go together. Today, celebrate victories as team accomplishments. Thank those who fought, those who prayed, and those who supported. God works through all of us together.

== 17:14
T: Write this for a memorial
R: 'And the LORD said unto Moses, Write this for a memorial in a book, and rehearse it in the ears of Joshua: for I will utterly put out the remembrance of Amalek from under heaven.' God told Moses to write the victory in a book as a memorial and to repeat it to Joshua. This is the first mention in Scripture of writing something down. God wanted the event preserved and remembered. God's acts were not to be forgotten. Scripture itself exists because God commanded His servants to write. Romans 15 says, whatsoever things were written aforetime were written for our learning. Notice that Moses was to rehearse it in Joshua's ears. Joshua would lead the next generation. He needed to remember how God gave victory. Future leaders need to know the stories of God's faithfulness. God also declared judgment on Amalek. Today, write down what God has done for you. Keep a record of His faithfulness. Share it with those who will lead after you.

== 17:15
T: The LORD my banner
R: 'And Moses built an altar, and called the name of it Jehovah-nissi.' Moses built an altar and named it Jehovah-nissi, which means the LORD is my banner. A banner was a flag or standard raised in battle. Armies rallied around it. Moses declared that God Himself was Israel's banner. Victory belonged to God. Moses did not build a monument to Joshua's skill or his own prayers. He built an altar to the LORD. The credit went where it belonged. This is a new name for God. The LORD is not only our healer and provider but our banner. We rally around Him. Under His banner, we fight and win. Psalm 20 says, in the name of our God we will set up our banners. Song of Solomon 2 says, his banner over me was love. God's banner over His people is both victory and love. Today, lift up the LORD as your banner. Rally around Him in every battle. Give Him the credit for every victory.
S: Moses built an altar and called it Jehovah-nissi, the LORD is my banner. He gave God the credit for the victory and declared Him the standard His people rally around. Lift up the LORD as your banner and give Him the credit for every victory.
P: The LORD my banner. Exodus 17:15: 'Moses built an altar, and called the name of it Jehovah-nissi.' Rally around God in every battle and give Him the credit for every win.

== 17:16
T: From generation to generation
R: 'For he said, Because the LORD hath sworn that the LORD will have war with Amalek from generation to generation.' Moses explained the altar's name. The LORD had sworn to wage war against Amalek from generation to generation. This battle was not a one-time event. The conflict would continue through many years. Some battles are long. Amalek would trouble Israel for centuries, through the days of the judges, Saul, David, and even into the book of Esther, where Haman the Agagite descended from Amalek's royal line. The enemy that attacked the weak would face God's continual opposition. Spiritual warfare also continues across generations. The enemy of our souls does not give up after one defeat. Ephesians 6 calls us to put on the whole armour of God. We must remain vigilant. Yet notice the promise. The LORD will have war. God Himself fights this battle. We are not alone. Today, stay alert in the ongoing spiritual battle. And take courage that the LORD fights for His people from generation to generation.
S: Moses explained that the LORD had sworn to have war with Amalek from generation to generation. Some battles are long, but God Himself fights them on behalf of His people. Stay alert in the ongoing spiritual battle and take courage that the LORD fights for you.
P: God fights through generations. Exodus 17:16: 'the LORD will have war with Amalek from generation to generation.' Some battles are long, but God fights for you. Stay alert.
