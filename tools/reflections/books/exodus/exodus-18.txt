# Exodus 18: Jethro's visit and counsel

== 18:1
T: Jethro heard
R: 'When Jethro, the priest of Midian, Moses' father in law, heard of all that God had done for Moses, and for Israel his people, and that the LORD had brought Israel out of Egypt.' News of the exodus reached Jethro, Moses' father-in-law, in Midian. He heard all that God had done for Moses and for Israel. The testimony of God's deliverance was spreading beyond Israel. God's works become news. What He did in Egypt did not stay hidden. People in other nations heard about it. Testimony travels. Jethro was a priest of Midian, not an Israelite. Yet he was drawn by what he heard. God's acts reach people outside the community of faith and draw them to Him. Psalm 96 says, Declare his glory among the heathen, his wonders among all people. Our stories of God's faithfulness can reach people we never expected. Today, share what God has done for you. Your testimony may reach someone outside the church and draw them toward Him.
S: Jethro, priest of Midian and Moses' father-in-law, heard of all God had done for Moses and Israel in bringing them out of Egypt. The testimony of God's deliverance was spreading beyond Israel. Share what God has done for you, for your testimony may draw others to Him.
P: Testimony travels. Exodus 18:1: 'When Jethro… heard of all that God had done for Moses, and for Israel.' God's works become news. Share your story today.

== 18:2
T: Zipporah returned
R: 'Then Jethro, Moses' father in law, took Zipporah, Moses' wife, after he had sent her back.' Jethro brought Zipporah, Moses' wife, back to him. At some point, Moses had sent her back to her father's home, perhaps for safety during the confrontation with Pharaoh. Now the family would be reunited. Ministry can strain families. Moses' calling had taken him away from his wife and children for a season. This verse quietly acknowledges that cost. Leaders sometimes make sacrifices that affect those closest to them. Yet God restores. The family that had been separated was brought back together. Jethro played an important role in that restoration. Malachi 4 speaks of turning the heart of the fathers to the children. God cares about families. He does not call us to neglect them but to honor them. First Timothy 5 says, if any provide not for his own… he hath denied the faith. Today, consider the impact of your responsibilities on your family. Pray for restoration where there has been strain. Invest in those closest to you.
S: Jethro brought Zipporah, Moses' wife, back to him after Moses had sent her home. The verse quietly acknowledges the strain ministry can place on families and God's work of restoration. Consider how your responsibilities affect your family and invest in those closest to you.

== 18:3
T: Gershom
R: 'And her two sons; of which the name of the one was Gershom; for he said, I have been an alien in a strange land.' Jethro also brought Moses' two sons. The first was Gershom, named because Moses had been an alien in a strange land. The name recalled Moses' years of exile in Midian. Names in Scripture often tell stories. Gershom's name was a reminder of a hard season, when Moses felt like a foreigner without a home. We are strangers in this world. First Peter 2 calls us strangers and pilgrims. Yet the reunion recorded here shows that God brings exiles home. Moses had been a stranger, but now he was leading God's people. His alien years had become preparation. Hebrews 11 says the patriarchs confessed that they were strangers and pilgrims on the earth. They looked for a better country. Today, if you feel like a stranger, remember that God uses exile to prepare us. And He is bringing you home.
S: Jethro also brought Moses' two sons, the first named Gershom because Moses had been an alien in a strange land. His years of exile had become preparation for leadership. If you feel like a stranger, remember that God uses exile to prepare you and is bringing you home.

== 18:4
T: Eliezer
R: 'And the name of the other was Eliezer; for the God of my father, said he, was mine help, and delivered me from the sword of Pharaoh.' The second son was Eliezer, meaning my God is help. Moses named him because the God of his father had helped him and delivered him from Pharaoh's sword. While Gershom recalled exile, Eliezer recalled rescue. The two names together tell Moses' story: a stranger in exile, but helped by God. Both are true of many believers. We may feel like strangers, but God is our help. Notice the phrase the God of my father. Moses connected his rescue to the faith of his family. God had been faithful through generations. Psalm 121 says, My help cometh from the LORD, which made heaven and earth. Psalm 46 says, God is our refuge and strength, a very present help in trouble. Today, remember that God is your help. Whatever threatens you, He has delivered before, and He will deliver again.

Jethro brought Moses' wife and sons to him in the wilderness, where Israel was camped at the mountain of God. This was the same mountain where Moses had seen the burning bush. God had brought him full circle. At the burning bush, God had promised, ye shall serve God upon this mountain. Now Moses was there with all Israel. God's promise had been fulfilled. The sign God gave Moses at the beginning had come true. God keeps His word precisely. Moses had doubted, objected, and struggled. Yet here he was, exactly where God said he would be. Numbers 23 says, hath he said, and shall he not do it? Today, remember the promises God has made to you. He brought Moses back to the mountain. He will bring you to the place He has promised.

Jethro's action shows love for both his daughter and his son-in-law. He wanted the family whole. Today, be someone who brings people together. Take initiative to reconnect families and friends. Numbers 11 later records Moses saying, I am not able to bear all this people alone. Today, examine whether you have taken on responsibilities that belong to others too. You are important, but you are not indispensable. God has other servants.
