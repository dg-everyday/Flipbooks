# Exodus 19: Israel at Mount Sinai

== 19:1
T: The third month
R: 'In the third month, when the children of Israel were gone forth out of the land of Egypt, the same day came they into the wilderness of Sinai.' Three months after leaving Egypt, Israel arrived at the wilderness of Sinai. This was a turning point. They had been delivered; now they would be formed into God's covenant people. Deliverance was the beginning, not the end. God brought Israel out of Egypt so He could bring them to Himself. Sinai would be the place of covenant, law, and relationship. Salvation leads to transformation. Notice the precise timing. Scripture records exactly when they arrived. God's work happens in real history. Jewish tradition connects this arrival with the giving of the law, later celebrated at Pentecost. In Acts 2, the Holy Spirit came at Pentecost, writing God's law on hearts. Today, remember that God saves you to bring you into relationship with Himself. Your deliverance is the start of a journey of transformation.
S: Three months after leaving Egypt, Israel came into the wilderness of Sinai. Deliverance was the beginning, and Sinai would be the place of covenant and relationship. Remember that God saves you to bring you into relationship and transformation.
P: Saved for relationship. Exodus 19:1: 'In the third month… came they into the wilderness of Sinai.' Deliverance was the beginning. God saves you to bring you to Himself.

== 19:2
T: Camped before the mount
R: 'For they were departed from Rephidim, and were come to the desert of Sinai, and had pitched in the wilderness; and there Israel camped before the mount.' Israel left Rephidim, entered the desert of Sinai, and camped before the mountain. Notice the singular: Israel camped. The many tribes had become one people, gathered before God. There is significance in camping before the mount. Israel stopped traveling and settled before God's mountain. Sometimes we need to stop moving and simply wait before God. Sinai was not a place to rush through. Psalm 27 says, Wait on the LORD: be of good courage. Waiting before God prepares us to hear Him. This was the same mountain where Moses had met God at the burning bush. God had promised, ye shall serve God upon this mountain. Now it was coming true. Today, take time to camp before God. Stop rushing, and wait in His presence. Let Him speak to you.
S: Israel departed from Rephidim, came to the desert of Sinai and camped before the mount. The people stopped traveling and settled before God's mountain as one. Take time to stop rushing and wait in God's presence so He can speak to you.
P: Camp before God. Exodus 19:2: 'there Israel camped before the mount.' Stop rushing. Wait in His presence and let Him speak to you today.

== 19:3
T: The LORD called from the mountain
R: 'And Moses went up unto God, and the LORD called unto him out of the mountain, saying, Thus shalt thou say to the house of Jacob, and tell the children of Israel.' Moses climbed the mountain toward God. The LORD called to him and gave him a message for the house of Jacob, the children of Israel. God had something important to say to His people. Moses went up to God before God spoke. He took the initiative to draw near. James 4 says, Draw nigh to God, and he will draw nigh to you. Moses' climb was an act of seeking. Notice the two names: house of Jacob and children of Israel. Jacob was the struggling, scheming ancestor. Israel was the name God gave after transforming him. The nation carried both identities. God speaks to us as we are and calls us toward who we can become. Today, go up to meet God. Seek Him intentionally. He has words for you, and He calls you toward transformation.
S: Moses went up to God, and the LORD called to him from the mountain with a message for the house of Jacob and the children of Israel. Moses drew near, and God spoke to a people who carried both names. Seek God intentionally, for He has words for you and calls you toward transformation.
P: Draw near to God. Exodus 19:3: 'Moses went up unto God, and the LORD called unto him out of the mountain.' Seek Him, and He will speak to you today.

== 19:4
T: On eagles' wings
R: 'Ye have seen what I did unto the Egyptians, and how I bare you on eagles' wings, and brought you unto myself.' God reminded Israel of what they had witnessed. You saw what I did to Egypt. I carried you on eagles' wings. I brought you to Myself. These are some of the most tender words in the Old Testament. An eagle carries its young on its wings, lifting them to safety and teaching them to fly. God described His deliverance this way. He did not merely drag Israel out of Egypt. He carried them tenderly. Deuteronomy 32 uses the same image: As an eagle stirreth up her nest… beareth them on her wings. Notice the destination: unto myself. God's goal was not just freedom from Egypt but closeness with Him. Isaiah 40 says, they shall mount up with wings as eagles. Today, remember that God has carried you. He lifts you when you cannot fly. And He is bringing you to Himself.
S: God reminded Israel that they had seen what He did to Egypt and how He bore them on eagles' wings and brought them to Himself. His deliverance was tender, with closeness to Him as its goal. Remember that God has carried you and is bringing you to Himself.
P: On eagles' wings. Exodus 19:4: 'how I bare you on eagles' wings, and brought you unto myself.' God carries you tenderly, and His goal is closeness with you.

== 19:5
T: A peculiar treasure
R: 'Now therefore, if ye will obey my voice indeed, and keep my covenant, then ye shall be a peculiar treasure unto me above all people: for all the earth is mine.' God offered a covenant. If you obey My voice and keep My covenant, you will be My special treasure among all peoples. For all the earth is Mine. Peculiar treasure means a special possession, something cherished. God owns the whole earth, yet He chose Israel as His own treasure. They were valued not because they were great but because He loved them. Deuteronomy 7 says the LORD did not choose them because they were more in number, but because the LORD loved you. First Peter 2 applies this language to believers: a peculiar people. In Christ, we are God's treasured possession. Notice the call to obedience. Relationship with God includes responding to His voice. Love and obedience go together. Today, rejoice that you are God's treasure. And respond to His love by listening to His voice.
S: God promised that if Israel would obey His voice and keep His covenant, they would be His peculiar treasure above all people, for all the earth is His. He valued them because He loved them. Rejoice that in Christ you are God's treasure and respond by listening to His voice.

== 19:6
T: A kingdom of priests
R: 'And ye shall be unto me a kingdom of priests, and an holy nation. These are the words which thou shalt speak unto the children of Israel.' God declared Israel's calling: You will be a kingdom of priests and a holy nation. The whole nation would represent God to the world and bring the world to God. Priests stand between God and people. Israel's calling was to be a priestly nation, showing the world who God is. Their holiness would make them distinct, and their distinctness would make God known. This calling is renewed in Christ. First Peter 2 says, ye are a chosen generation, a royal priesthood, an holy nation. Revelation 1 says Christ hath made us kings and priests unto God. Every believer has a priestly role. We pray for others, worship God, and represent Him to the world. Today, embrace your calling as a priest of God. Live a holy life that points others to Him, and bring their needs before Him in prayer.

He set before them everything God had said. He did not hide or change any part of God's offer. The covenant was presented clearly. Moses was a faithful messenger. He delivered all of God's words. The people deserved to hear the full offer before responding. God's covenant was not imposed secretly; it was presented openly. Notice the phrase before their faces. Moses spoke directly and honestly. He looked them in the eye. Faithful communication of God's word is clear and complete. == 19:8
T: All that the LORD hath spoken
R: 'And all the people answered together, and said, All that the LORD hath spoken we will do. And Moses returned the words of the people unto the LORD.' The people responded together: Everything the LORD has said, we will do. Moses brought their answer back to God. Israel committed themselves to the covenant. Within weeks they would make a golden calf. Their words were sincere, but their hearts were not yet strong enough to keep them. Many of us make bold promises to God. We mean them. But we often overestimate our ability to keep them. Peter said in Matthew 26, Though I should die with thee, yet will I not deny thee. That same night he denied Jesus three times. Our commitment matters, but our strength comes from God. Philippians 2 says, it is God which worketh in you both to will and to do. Today, commit yourself to God sincerely. But depend on His grace to keep your promises, not your own strength.

== 19:9
T: That the people may hear
R: 'And the LORD said unto Moses, Lo, I come unto thee in a thick cloud, that the people may hear when I speak with thee, and believe thee for ever.' God told Moses He would come in a thick cloud. The people would hear God speak with Moses and trust him forever. God was confirming Moses' authority before the whole nation. The thick cloud both revealed and concealed. God would be present, but His full glory would be veiled. Human eyes cannot bear God's unveiled glory. The cloud protected the people even as it showed God's presence. God wanted the people to hear His voice directly. This would establish Moses' credibility. They would know that Moses' words came from God. Faith grows when we hear God for ourselves. Romans 10 says, faith cometh by hearing, and hearing by the word of God. Today, listen for God's voice in His word. Let Him confirm His truth to your heart, so that your faith rests on His voice, not merely on human messengers.

We do not earn God's presence, but we can prepare our hearts to receive Him. Psalm 24 asks, Who shall ascend into the hill of the LORD?… He that hath clean hands, and a pure heart. In Christ, we are made clean. First John 1 says, the blood of Jesus Christ his Son cleanseth us from all sin. Today, prepare your heart to meet God. Confess what needs cleansing. On the third day, the LORD would come down in the sight of all the people on Mount Sinai. They had a set time to prepare for God's appearing. The third day is significant throughout Scripture. Abraham reached Moriah on the third day. Jonah was in the fish three days. Most importantly, Jesus rose on the third day. The third day often marks God's decisive action. Notice the call to be ready. God was coming, and the people needed to prepare. Jesus also calls us to readiness. Matthew 24 says, be ye also ready: for in such an hour as ye think not the Son of man cometh. God will come again. == 19:12
T: Set bounds
R: 'And thou shalt set bounds unto the people round about, saying, Take heed to yourselves, that ye go not up into the mount, or touch the border of it: whosoever toucheth the mount shall be surely put to death.' God told Moses to set boundaries around the mountain. The people must not go up or even touch its edge. Anyone who did would die. God's holiness required boundaries. These boundaries were not cruel; they were protective. God's holiness is so intense that unprepared sinners cannot survive direct contact with it. The boundary kept the people safe while still allowing them to witness God's presence. Hebrews 12 contrasts Sinai with what believers have in Christ. We have not come to the mount that might be touched… But ye are come unto mount Sion… and to Jesus the mediator of the new covenant. Through Christ, the barrier is removed. We can draw near with confidence. Today, honor God's holiness. And thank Jesus, who has opened the way for you to approach God without fear.

One day a trumpet will call God's people into His presence forever. Hebrews 10 says, Let us draw near with a true heart. In Christ, the invitation stands. Today, approach God at His invitation. == 19:23

R: 'And Moses said unto the LORD, The people cannot come up to mount Sinai: for thou chargedst us, saying, Set bounds about the mount, and sanctify it.' Moses responded that the people could not come up, because God had already commanded them to set boundaries. Moses had done what God asked. He was confident the people would stay back. The boundaries were in place. He reminded God of what had been done. Yet God would insist on the warning anyway, as the next verse shows. Sometimes we think we have done enough. == 19:24

== 19:25
T: Moses spake unto them
R: 'So Moses went down unto the people, and spake unto them.' Moses went down to the people and told them what God had said. The chapter ends with Moses faithfully carrying God's message. Next would come the Ten Commandments. Moses had climbed the mountain several times in this chapter. Up and down, up and down. Each time he carried God's word faithfully. Faithful service often involves repetitive, tiring work. Moses did not complain. He went where God sent him and said what God told him. His faithfulness in the ordinary rhythms of ministry made him ready for extraordinary moments. Notice the simplicity: he went down and spoke. Ministry is often this simple. Receive God's word, then share it. Second Timothy 4 says, Preach the word; be instant in season, out of season. Today, be faithful in the ordinary rhythms of your service.
