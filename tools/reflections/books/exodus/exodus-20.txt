# Exodus 20: The Ten Commandments

== 20:1
T: God spake all these words
R: 'And God spake all these words, saying.' God Himself spoke the Ten Commandments. Not through Moses this time, but directly to the people from the mountain. These words came straight from the mouth of God. The law was not human invention. It was God's own word. Its authority came from Him. The Ten Commandments are not suggestions or cultural customs. They reflect the character and will of the living God. Deuteronomy 5 recalls this moment: These words the LORD spake unto all your assembly in the mount out of the midst of the fire. Notice the phrase all these words. God gave the complete law. He did not leave His people guessing about what He expects. He spoke clearly. Psalm 19 says, The law of the LORD is perfect, converting the soul. God's word is a gift that shows us how to live. Today, receive God's word as His own voice. Read it with reverence, knowing that it comes from Him.
S: God spoke all the words of the Ten Commandments directly to His people. The law came from His own mouth and reflects His character and will. Receive God's word as His own voice and read it with reverence.
P: God's own words. Exodus 20:1: 'And God spake all these words, saying.' The law came straight from God. Read His word with reverence today.

== 20:2
T: Grace before law
R: 'I am the LORD thy God, which have brought thee out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of bondage.' Before giving a single command, God reminded Israel who He is and what He had done. I am the LORD your God. I brought you out of slavery. Grace came before law. This order is vital. God did not say, keep these laws and I will save you. He said, I have saved you; now live like My people. The commandments were a response to redemption, not a way to earn it. This is the pattern of the gospel. Ephesians 2 says we are saved by grace through faith, not of works. Then verse 10 says we are created in Christ Jesus unto good works. Obedience flows from salvation, not toward it. Notice also thy God. The law was personal. God related to each person. Today, remember that God saved you first. Let your obedience flow from gratitude, not from fear or a desire to earn His love.
S: Before giving any command, God declared that He is the LORD who brought Israel out of Egypt and the house of bondage. Grace came before law, and obedience was a response to redemption. Let your obedience flow from gratitude for what God has already done.
P: Grace first. Exodus 20:2: 'I am the LORD thy God, which have brought thee out of the land of Egypt.' Salvation comes before law. Obey from gratitude, not to earn His love.

== 20:3
T: No other gods
R: 'Thou shalt have no other gods before me.' The first commandment is foundational: no other gods before Me. God must have first place. Nothing may compete with Him for our devotion and trust. Israel lived surrounded by many gods. Egypt had hundreds. Canaan had Baal and Asherah. God demanded exclusive loyalty. He would not share His people's hearts with rivals. Today, our gods may not be statues. But anything we love, trust, or serve more than God becomes an idol. Money, career, relationships, comfort, or even ourselves can take God's place. Jesus said in Matthew 22 that the first and great commandment is to love the Lord thy God with all thy heart. First John 5 says, Little children, keep yourselves from idols. Today, examine your heart. What competes with God for first place? Put Him back at the center. He alone deserves your highest loyalty.
S: The first commandment declares, Thou shalt have no other gods before me. God demands exclusive loyalty and first place in His people's hearts. Examine what competes with God for first place and put Him back at the center.
P: God first. Exodus 20:3: 'Thou shalt have no other gods before me.' What competes with God in your heart? Put Him back at the center today.

== 20:4
T: No graven image
R: 'Thou shalt not make unto thee any graven image, or any likeness of any thing that is in heaven above, or that is in the earth beneath, or that is in the water under the earth.' The second commandment forbids making images to represent God or anything in creation as objects of worship. He is greater than anything we can make. When we shape God into our own image, we create a god who fits our preferences. But the true God is not made by human hands. Acts 17 says, we ought not to think that the Godhead is like unto gold, or silver, or stone, graven by art and man's device. We can also create mental images of God that are false. These distortions are also idols. Romans 1 warns against changing the glory of God into an image. Today, worship God as He has revealed Himself in Scripture and in Christ, not as you imagine Him to be.
S: The second commandment forbids making any graven image or likeness to worship. God cannot be captured or controlled by anything we make, whether in metal or in our minds. Worship God as He has revealed Himself in Scripture and in Christ.
P: No images. Exodus 20:4: 'Thou shalt not make unto thee any graven image.' Don't shape God into what you prefer. Worship Him as He truly is.

== 20:5
T: A jealous God
R: 'Thou shalt not bow down thyself to them, nor serve them: for I the LORD thy God am a jealous God, visiting the iniquity of the fathers upon the children unto the third and fourth generation of them that hate me.' God forbade bowing to idols because He is a jealous God. It is the righteous passion of a husband for his wife's faithfulness. The phrase visiting the iniquity speaks of how sin affects generations. Children often suffer the consequences of parents' sins and may follow the same patterns. Notice it applies to them that hate me. Yet each generation can choose differently. Ezekiel 18 says the son shall not bear the iniquity of the father if he turns to God. God's jealousy protects His people from destruction. Second Corinthians 11 says, I am jealous over you with godly jealousy. Today, recognize that God's jealousy is love. And break unhealthy generational patterns by turning fully to Him.
S: God forbade bowing to idols because He is a jealous God, and sin's effects can pass through generations of those who hate Him. His jealousy is the righteous passion of love, and each generation can turn back to Him. Recognize God's jealousy as love and break harmful patterns by turning fully to Him.
P: Jealous love. Exodus 20:5: 'I the LORD thy God am a jealous God.' His jealousy is love, not pettiness. Break harmful generational patterns by turning to Him today.

== 20:6
T: Mercy unto thousands
R: 'And shewing mercy unto thousands of them that love me, and keep my commandments.' God continued: I show mercy to thousands of those who love Me and keep My commandments. Compare this with the previous verse. Iniquity is visited to the third and fourth generation. Mercy extends to thousands. God's mercy vastly outweighs His judgment. This is one of the most hopeful verses in the law. Sin's effects are real but limited. Mercy is overwhelming. Many understand thousands to mean a thousand generations. God's love ripples forward far beyond the effects of sin. Notice who receives this mercy: them that love me, and keep my commandments. Love and obedience go together. Jesus said in John 14, If ye love me, keep my commandments. Obedience is the natural expression of love. Psalm 103 says, the mercy of the LORD is from everlasting to everlasting upon them that fear him. Today, rejoice that God's mercy outlasts every curse. Love Him and obey Him, and your faithfulness can bless generations to come.
S: God promised to show mercy to thousands of those who love Him and keep His commandments. His mercy vastly outweighs judgment, rippling forward far beyond sin's effects. Love and obey God, trusting that your faithfulness can bless generations to come.
P: Mercy to thousands. Exodus 20:6: 'shewing mercy unto thousands of them that love me.' God's mercy far outweighs judgment. Your faithfulness can bless generations.

== 20:7
T: Not in vain
R: 'Thou shalt not take the name of the LORD thy God in vain; for the LORD will not hold him guiltless that taketh his name in vain.' The third commandment protects God's name. Do not take it in vain, meaning do not use it emptily, falsely, or carelessly. God will hold accountable those who misuse His name. It includes swearing false oaths in God's name, using His name to justify evil, and speaking of Him carelessly. To take His name in vain is to treat it as worthless. God's name represents His character and reputation. Misusing it dishonors Him. As believers, we also carry His name. We are called Christians. If we live in ways that contradict His character, we take His name in vain. Romans 2 says, the name of God is blasphemed among the Gentiles through you. Jesus taught us to pray, Hallowed be thy name. Today, honor God's name in your words and actions. Let your life reflect well on the One whose name you bear.
S: The third commandment forbids taking the name of the LORD in vain, for He will not hold guiltless those who misuse it. This includes careless speech, false oaths and living in ways that dishonor the name we bear. Honor God's name in your words and actions.
P: Honor His name. Exodus 20:7: 'Thou shalt not take the name of the LORD thy God in vain.' You bear His name. Let your words and life honor Him today.

== 20:8
T: Remember the sabbath
R: 'Remember the sabbath day, to keep it holy.' The fourth commandment calls God's people to remember the Sabbath and keep it holy. It is the only commandment that begins with remember. The Sabbath had already been introduced with the manna. Now it became part of the covenant. Remember suggests we are prone to forget. Life's demands can crowd out rest and worship. God commands us to deliberately remember. The Sabbath does not happen by accident. We must choose it. To keep it holy means to set it apart. It is different from other days. It is dedicated to God, to rest, and to worship. Mark 2 says the sabbath was made for man. It is God's gift for our good. Hebrews 4 speaks of a rest that remains for God's people, fulfilled in Christ. Today, deliberately set apart time for rest and worship. Do not let the demands of life crowd out what God has made holy.
S: The fourth commandment calls God's people to remember the Sabbath day and keep it holy. It is set apart for rest and worship, and remembering it requires deliberate choice. Deliberately set apart time for rest and worship this week.
P: Remember to rest. Exodus 20:8: 'Remember the sabbath day, to keep it holy.' Rest doesn't happen by accident. Set apart time for God this week.

== 20:9
T: Six days of labour
R: 'Six days shalt thou labour, and do all thy work.' God commanded six days of work. Work is not a curse but part of God's design. Adam was given work in Eden before the fall. Labor is honorable. Notice that the Sabbath commandment includes work. God did not command only rest; He commanded diligent labor too. Six days of work give meaning to the seventh day of rest. Both are part of a healthy life. Colossians 3 says, whatsoever ye do, do it heartily, as to the Lord. Work done for God is worship. Second Thessalonians 3 says, if any would not work, neither should he eat. Laziness is not godliness. The phrase do all thy work suggests completing tasks. Six days are enough to accomplish what we need. Trusting God includes believing that six days of faithful work are sufficient. Today, work diligently as unto the Lord. And trust that the time He gives is enough to do what He requires.
S: God commanded six days of labor in which to do all one's work. Work is part of God's good design and gives meaning to rest. Work diligently as unto the Lord and trust that the time He gives is enough.
P: Work is worship. Exodus 20:9: 'Six days shalt thou labour, and do all thy work.' God designed work as good. Do it heartily as unto the Lord today.

== 20:10
T: Rest for all
R: 'But the seventh day is the sabbath of the LORD thy God: in it thou shalt not do any work, thou, nor thy son, nor thy daughter, thy manservant, nor thy maidservant, nor thy cattle, nor thy stranger that is within thy gates.' The seventh day belongs to the LORD. No one was to work: not the head of the house, not children, not servants, not animals, not foreigners. Rest was for everyone. In the ancient world, servants and animals rarely rested. But God's law extended rest to all. The Sabbath was a gift of dignity. Israel had been slaves who never rested. Now they were commanded to give rest to their own servants. Deuteronomy 5 connects this to their history: remember that thou wast a servant in the land of Egypt. God's people should never treat others the way they were once treated. Today, make sure those who depend on you also receive rest. Treat workers, family, and even animals with the dignity God gives.
S: The seventh day is the Sabbath of the LORD, and no one was to work, including children, servants, animals and strangers. God extended rest and dignity to every member of the household. Make sure those who depend on you also receive the rest God intends.

== 20:11
T: God rested
R: 'For in six days the LORD made heaven and earth, the sea, and all that in them is, and rested the seventh day: wherefore the LORD blessed the sabbath day, and hallowed it.' God explained the reason for the Sabbath. He made everything in six days and rested on the seventh. So He blessed and sanctified the Sabbath. Rest is rooted in God's own example. God did not rest because He was tired. Isaiah 40 says He fainteth not, neither is weary. He rested to establish a pattern, to delight in His creation, and to set apart a holy day. When we rest, we imitate God. Notice that God blessed the Sabbath. It is not only a day of ceasing but a day of blessing. Rest brings refreshment, perspective, and renewal. Genesis 2 records the same truth. The Sabbath is built into creation itself. Our bodies and souls are designed for this rhythm. Today, follow God's example. Rest not just because you are tired, but to enjoy His blessing and delight in His work.
S: God explained that He made heaven and earth in six days and rested the seventh, so He blessed and hallowed the Sabbath. Rest is rooted in God's own example and built into creation. Follow God's example and rest to enjoy His blessing and delight in His work.
P: Rest like God. Exodus 20:11: 'the LORD… rested the seventh day: wherefore the LORD blessed the sabbath day.' Rest is built into creation. Enjoy His blessing today.

== 20:12
T: Honour thy parents
R: 'Honour thy father and thy mother: that thy days may be long upon the land which the LORD thy God giveth thee.' The fifth commandment turns to human relationships. Honor your father and mother. This commandment comes with a promise: long life in the land God gives. Ephesians 6 calls it the first commandment with promise. Honoring parents means respecting, valuing, and caring for them. It does not always mean agreeing with them or obeying them as adults. But it means treating them with dignity. This includes caring for aging parents. Jesus rebuked the Pharisees in Mark 7 for finding excuses not to support their parents. Honor is practical. The family is the foundation of society. When families are healthy, communities thrive. Some people have had painful relationships with parents. Honor in those cases may look different, but it still means treating them with dignity and forgiving where possible. Today, honor your parents in a practical way. Call them, thank them, or care for their needs.
S: The fifth commandment says, Honour thy father and thy mother, with the promise of long days in the land. Honor means respecting, valuing and practically caring for parents. Honor your parents in a practical way today.

== 20:13
T: Thou shalt not kill
R: 'Thou shalt not kill.' The sixth commandment protects human life. Do not murder. Every person is made in the image of God, and taking innocent life is a grave offense against Him. Genesis 9 says, Whoso sheddeth man's blood… for in the image of God made he man. Human life is sacred because it reflects God. Murder attacks not only a person but God's image. Jesus deepened this commandment. In Matthew 5 He said, whosoever is angry with his brother without a cause shall be in danger of the judgment. Murder begins in the heart with anger, contempt, and hatred. First John 3 says, Whosoever hateth his brother is a murderer. This commandment also calls us to protect life. Not killing is the minimum. Valuing, defending, and nurturing life is the fuller obedience. We honor God by caring for the vulnerable, the unborn, the elderly, and the sick. Today, examine your heart for anger or contempt. Replace them with love. And actively value every life as bearing God's image.
S: The sixth commandment says, Thou shalt not kill, protecting human life made in God's image. Jesus taught that murder begins in the heart with anger and contempt. Examine your heart for anger and actively value every life as bearing God's image.
P: Life is sacred. Exodus 20:13: 'Thou shalt not kill.' Every person bears God's image. Guard your heart against anger, and value every life today.

== 20:14
T: Faithfulness in marriage
R: 'Thou shalt not commit adultery.' The seventh commandment protects marriage. Do not commit adultery. Marriage is a covenant between husband and wife, and breaking it is a serious sin against God and one's spouse. God designed marriage as a lifelong union. Genesis 2 says, they shall be one flesh. Adultery tears apart what God has joined. It devastates trust, families, and children. Jesus again went deeper. In Matthew 5 He said, whosoever looketh on a woman to lust after her hath committed adultery with her already in his heart. Faithfulness begins in the mind. What we allow ourselves to dwell on shapes what we eventually do. Marriage also pictures God's relationship with His people. Ephesians 5 compares it to Christ and the church. Unfaithfulness in marriage reflects unfaithfulness to God. Hebrews 13 says, Marriage is honourable in all, and the bed undefiled. Today, guard your heart and your marriage. If you are married, invest in faithfulness. If you are single, honor God with purity. Guard your thoughts as well as your actions.
S: The seventh commandment says, Thou shalt not commit adultery, protecting the covenant of marriage. Jesus taught that faithfulness begins in the heart and mind. Guard your heart and your marriage, honoring God with purity in thought and action.
P: Faithful in heart and action. Exodus 20:14: 'Thou shalt not commit adultery.' Faithfulness begins in the mind. Guard your heart and honor God with purity today.

== 20:15
T: Thou shalt not steal
R: 'Thou shalt not steal.' The eighth commandment protects property. Do not steal. What belongs to others is theirs, and taking it without right violates both them and God. Stealing takes many forms. Shoplifting and robbery are obvious. But cheating on taxes, wasting an employer's time, not paying debts, or taking credit for others' work are also theft. Malachi 3 even speaks of robbing God by withholding tithes. Behind this commandment is a call to trust God's provision. We steal when we believe God has not given us enough. Contentment is the antidote. Hebrews 13 says, be content with such things as ye have. Ephesians 4 gives the positive version: Let him that stole steal no more: but rather let him labour… that he may have to give to him that needeth. The goal is not only to stop taking but to start giving. Today, examine whether you are taking anything that is not yours. Practice honesty and generosity instead.
S: The eighth commandment says, Thou shalt not steal, protecting what belongs to others. Theft takes many forms and often comes from distrust of God's provision. Practice honesty and contentment, and move from taking to giving.
P: From taking to giving. Exodus 20:15: 'Thou shalt not steal.' Theft comes in many forms. Practice honesty, contentment, and generous giving in every area of life today.

== 20:16
T: No false witness
R: 'Thou shalt not bear false witness against thy neighbour.' The ninth commandment protects truth. Do not give false testimony against your neighbor. Lies destroy reputations, relationships, and justice. God is a God of truth. In its first sense, this commandment applies to courts. False testimony could lead to unjust punishment, even death. But it extends to all speech. Gossip, slander, exaggeration, and half-truths can all damage our neighbor. Proverbs 6 lists a false witness that speaketh lies among the things God hates. Jesus is the truth, according to John 14. Satan is called the father of lies in John 8. When we lie, we reflect the enemy rather than Christ. Ephesians 4 says, putting away lying, speak every man truth with his neighbour. Truthfulness builds trust and community. Notice the word neighbour. Our words affect real people. Today, guard your tongue. Speak truthfully about others. Refuse to spread gossip or damage anyone's reputation with careless words.
S: The ninth commandment says, Thou shalt not bear false witness against thy neighbour. Lies, gossip and slander damage reputations and reflect the enemy rather than Christ. Guard your tongue and speak truthfully about others.
P: Speak truth. Exodus 20:16: 'Thou shalt not bear false witness against thy neighbour.' Words affect real people. Refuse gossip and speak truth in love today.

== 20:17
T: Thou shalt not covet
R: 'Thou shalt not covet thy neighbour's house, thou shalt not covet thy neighbour's wife, nor his manservant, nor his maidservant, nor his ox, nor his ass, nor any thing that is thy neighbour's.' The tenth commandment addresses the heart. Do not covet anything that belongs to your neighbor. While the other commandments mostly govern actions, this one governs desire. Coveting is the root of many sins. Theft, adultery, and even murder often begin with wanting what belongs to someone else. James 4 says, ye lust, and have not: ye kill, and desire to have. Paul said in Romans 7 that he would not have known sin, except the law had said, Thou shalt not covet. The antidote to coveting is contentment and gratitude. Philippians 4 says, I have learned, in whatsoever state I am, therewith to be content. Thankfulness for what we have crowds out desire for what others have. Today, notice when you envy others. Turn that envy into gratitude for what God has given you.
S: The tenth commandment forbids coveting anything that belongs to one's neighbor. It addresses the heart, where many sins begin with wanting what others have. Notice envy in your heart and turn it into gratitude for what God has given you.
P: Guard your desires. Exodus 20:17: 'Thou shalt not covet… any thing that is thy neighbour's.' Envy is the root of many sins. Turn it into gratitude today.

== 20:18
T: They stood afar off
R: 'And all the people saw the thunderings, and the lightnings, and the noise of the trumpet, and the mountain smoking: and when the people saw it, they removed, and stood afar off.' The people saw the thunder, lightning, trumpet, and smoking mountain. They pulled back and stood at a distance. God's holy presence was overwhelming. Fear drove them away. God's majesty is terrifying to sinful people. When we truly see God's holiness, we recognize our unworthiness. Isaiah cried, Woe is me! for I am undone. Yet distance from God is not the solution. Sinful people cannot stand in God's presence. They need a mediator. Hebrews 12 contrasts Sinai's terror with the grace of Christ. Ye are not come unto the mount that might be touched… But ye are come… to Jesus the mediator of the new covenant. Through Christ, we who were afar off are made nigh. Ephesians 2 says so. Today, do not stand afar off from God. Come near through Jesus, who makes the way open.
S: The people saw the thunderings, lightnings, trumpet and smoking mountain, and they pulled back and stood afar off. God's holiness exposed their need for a mediator. Do not stand afar off from God but come near through Jesus.

== 20:19
T: Speak thou with us
R: 'And they said unto Moses, Speak thou with us, and we will hear: but let not God speak with us, lest we die.' The people begged Moses to speak to them instead of God. We will listen to you, they said, but do not let God speak to us, or we will die. They wanted a mediator. This request reveals humanity's need. We cannot bear God's direct presence in our sinful state. We need someone to stand between us and God. Moses filled that role for Israel. Deuteronomy 18 records God's response: I will raise them up a Prophet from among their brethren, like unto thee. Acts 3 identifies this Prophet as Jesus. He is the ultimate mediator. Through Him, God speaks to us without destroying us. Hebrews 1 says God hath in these last days spoken unto us by his Son. Jesus is God's final word, spoken in a way we can receive. Today, thank God for sending Jesus as the mediator you need. Through Him, you can hear God's voice and live.
S: The people asked Moses to speak to them instead of God, for they feared they would die. Their request revealed humanity's need for a mediator, fulfilled in Jesus, the Prophet like Moses. Thank God for Jesus, through whom you can hear God's voice and live.

== 20:20
T: Fear not, yet fear
R: 'And Moses said unto the people, Fear not: for God is come to prove you, and that his fear may be before your faces, that ye sin not.' Moses told the people, Do not be afraid. God has come to test you, so that the fear of Him will keep you from sinning. There is a fear to avoid and a fear to embrace. Fear not, Moses said, yet God wanted His fear before them. The difference is between terror and reverence. Terror drives us away from God. Reverence draws us to obey Him. He wanted them to honor Him so deeply that they would avoid sin. The fear of the LORD is not cowering dread. Proverbs 9 says, The fear of the LORD is the beginning of wisdom. It is awe that shapes behavior. First John 4 says perfect love casteth out fear. Love removes terror, but deepens reverence. Today, let go of fearful dread toward God. But cultivate holy reverence that keeps you from sin.

== 20:21

The contrast is striking. Most kept away; one man approached. Notice where God was: in the thick darkness. God's presence was hidden, mysterious, beyond full understanding. Moses walked into that mystery. Faith sometimes means approaching God when we cannot see clearly. Many of us experience seasons when God seems hidden. We cannot understand His ways. Yet God is present even in the darkness. Psalm 18 says, He made darkness his secret place. Psalm 97 says, Clouds and darkness are round about him. Moses did not wait for perfect clarity. He drew near. Hebrews 10 says, Let us draw near with a true heart in full assurance of faith. Today, draw near to God even when you cannot see clearly. He is present in the darkness, and He welcomes those who come.

== 20:22

R: 'And the LORD said unto Moses, Thus thou shalt say unto the children of Israel, Ye have seen that I have talked with you from heaven.' God gave Moses a message: You have seen that I spoke to you from heaven. The people had experienced God's voice directly. That experience would shape how they worshiped. Israel had not seen God's form. They had heard His voice. This was the basis for the instructions that followed about worship. God revealed Himself through His word, not through an image. That is why the next verse forbids idols. Deuteronomy 4 makes this explicit: ye heard the voice of the words, but saw no similitude. Our faith is also grounded in God's word. We have not seen God's face, but we have His word. Second Peter 1 calls it a more sure word of prophecy. Today, ground your faith in what God has spoken. His word from heaven is enough to guide your worship and your life.

== 20:23
T: Not gods of silver
R: 'Ye shall not make with me gods of silver, neither shall ye make unto you gods of gold.' God warned against making gods of silver or gold to worship alongside Him. Israel had just received gold and silver from Egypt. The temptation would be real. In fact, they would soon make a golden calf. Notice the phrase with me. The danger was not only replacing God but adding other things alongside Him. Many people do not abandon God entirely. They simply add other objects of trust. God plus money. God plus success. God plus comfort. But God refuses to share His throne. Silver and gold are especially tempting idols. Wealth promises security and power. Jesus said in Matthew 6, Ye cannot serve God and mammon. == 20:24
T: Where I record my name

God gave simple instructions for worship. Make an altar of earth. Offer sacrifices on it. And wherever I cause My name to be remembered, I will come to you and bless you. The altar was simple, made of ordinary dirt. God did not require elaborate structures. Worship was about the heart and the sacrifice, not the impressiveness of the building. Notice God's promise: I will come unto thee, and I will bless thee. Where God's name is honored, He shows up. His presence is not tied to grand locations but to places where He is genuinely worshiped. Wherever you honor His name, He promises to come and bless you.

== 20:25
T: Not hewn stone
R: 'And if thou wilt make me an altar of stone, thou shalt not build it of hewn stone: for if thou lift up thy tool upon it, thou hast polluted it.' If Israel made a stone altar, they must not use cut stones. Using tools on the stones would defile the altar. The stones were to remain as God made them. Human craftsmanship might draw attention to the builder rather than to God. It might tempt people to make the altar into an artistic object of pride. God wanted worship focused on Him, not on human achievement. Our salvation is also not built by human effort. We cannot shape or improve the way to God. Ephesians 2 says salvation is not of works, lest any man should boast. The cross was God's work. We receive it as it is. Isaiah 64 says, all our righteousnesses are as filthy rags. Today, come to God on His terms, not by your own effort. Let your worship point to His work, not yours.
S: God said that a stone altar must not be built of hewn stone, for using a tool on it would pollute it. Worship was to focus on God's work, not on human craftsmanship or pride. Come to God on His terms and let your worship point to His work, not yours.
P: God's work, not ours. Exodus 20:25: 'thou shalt not build it of hewn stone.' Worship isn't about our achievement. Salvation is God's work. Come on His terms today.

== 20:26
T: No steps to the altar
R: 'Neither shalt thou go up by steps unto mine altar, that thy nakedness be not discovered thereon.' God instructed that the altar should not have steps. Climbing steps in the robes of that time could expose a person's nakedness. God cared about modesty and dignity in worship. This shows God's attention to detail in worship. Pagan worship sometimes included immodest or sexual practices. God set His worship apart. It was to be pure, dignified, and focused on Him. Modesty in worship honors God and protects others. It keeps attention on God rather than on ourselves. First Timothy 2 speaks of modest apparel in worship. Later, priests were given linen garments to cover them, according to Exodus 28. Ultimately, our true covering comes from God. Since the fall, humanity has needed covering. Isaiah 61 says, he hath clothed me with the garments of salvation. In Christ, we are clothed in His righteousness. Today, approach God with dignity and reverence. And rejoice that He has covered your shame with Christ's righteousness.
S: God instructed that His altar have no steps, so that nakedness would not be exposed in worship. He set His worship apart as pure and dignified, unlike pagan practices. Approach God with reverence and rejoice that He has covered your shame with Christ's righteousness.
P: Covered by grace. Exodus 20:26: 'Neither shalt thou go up by steps unto mine altar.' God cares about dignity in worship. In Christ, your shame is covered.
