# Exodus 21: Laws on servants, injury and responsibility

== 21:1
T: These are the judgments
R: 'Now these are the judgments which thou shalt set before them.' After the Ten Commandments, God gave detailed laws for daily life. These judgments would guide Israel in matters of work, family, injury, and property. God cared about the practical details of community life. Faith is not only about worship but about how we treat one another. God's law addressed real situations: disputes, accidents, debts, and responsibilities. Holiness reaches into every part of life. Many of these laws reflect an ancient culture very different from ours. Yet they reveal timeless principles: justice, mercy, protection of the vulnerable, and responsibility. Micah 6 says, what doth the LORD require of thee, but to do justly, and to love mercy. Today, remember that God cares about how you live in ordinary matters. Your work, your relationships, and your responsibilities are all places to honor Him.
S: After the Ten Commandments, God gave judgments for Israel's daily life in matters of work, family, injury and property. Faith reaches into how we treat one another in ordinary matters. Honor God in your work, relationships and responsibilities.
P: Faith in daily life. Exodus 21:1: 'Now these are the judgments which thou shalt set before them.' God cares how you live in ordinary matters. Honor Him in them today.

== 21:2
T: Free in the seventh year
R: 'If thou buy an Hebrew servant, six years he shall serve: and in the seventh he shall go out free for nothing.' The first law addressed servants. A Hebrew who sold himself into service, often because of debt, would serve six years. In the seventh year, he would go free without paying anything. This law limited servitude. Unlike slavery in Egypt, Hebrew service had an end date. No Israelite would be trapped in permanent bondage. God built freedom into the system. Notice that the first law after the commandments concerned servants. God, who had just freed Israel from slavery, protected the vulnerable first. Deuteronomy 15 adds that the master should send the servant away with generous provisions. This pattern of release points to the gospel. Luke 4 records Jesus saying He came to preach deliverance to the captives. Today, thank God for the freedom He gives. And consider how you can help release others from burdens that trap them.
S: God's first law addressed Hebrew servants, who would serve six years and go free in the seventh without payment. God built freedom into Israel's system, protecting the vulnerable first. Thank God for your freedom and consider how you can help release others from burdens.
P: Freedom built in. Exodus 21:2: 'six years he shall serve: and in the seventh he shall go out free for nothing.' God protects the vulnerable. Help someone find freedom today.

== 21:3
T: Families protected
R: 'If he came in by himself, he shall go out by himself: if he were married, then his wife shall go out with him.' If a servant came alone, he would leave alone. But if he was married when he came, his wife would go free with him. Marriages were protected. The servant's family was not to be divided by his debt. God cared about keeping families together. A man's financial troubles should not cost him his wife. Even in hard circumstances, the bond of marriage was respected. This principle reflects God's high view of marriage. Genesis 2 says a man shall cleave unto his wife. Matthew 19 adds, What therefore God hath joined together, let not man put asunder. Economic pressure should not tear families apart. Today, pray for families under financial stress. And look for ways to protect and support marriages in your community, especially in hard times.
S: A servant who came alone would leave alone, but if he came married, his wife would go free with him. God protected marriages from being divided by debt. Pray for families under financial stress and support marriages in hard times.
P: Families protected. Exodus 21:3: 'if he were married, then his wife shall go out with him.' Debt shouldn't divide families. Pray for and support struggling marriages today.

== 21:4
T: A difficult provision
R: 'If his master have given him a wife, and she have born him sons or daughters; the wife and her children shall be her master's, and he shall go out by himself.' If the master gave the servant a wife during his service, the wife and children remained with the master when the servant left. This law reflects the hard realities of an ancient economic system. The wife was likely a servant with her own longer term of service. Scripture often addresses broken human systems by limiting harm rather than immediately abolishing them. Jesus said in Matthew 19 that some laws were given because of the hardness of your hearts. Yet this law sets up the choice in the next verses. The servant could choose to stay with his family. Love could bind him where law could not. Today, recognize that God's law works within a broken world, pointing toward His perfect design. And trust Him with situations where families face painful choices.
S: If the master gave a servant a wife, she and the children stayed with the master when the servant left. The law regulated a hard ancient system and set up a choice for love to bind the family. Trust God with situations where families face painful choices.

== 21:5
T: I love my master
R: 'And if the servant shall plainly say, I love my master, my wife, and my children; I will not go out free.' A servant might say plainly, I love my master, my wife, and my children. I do not want to go free. Love could lead him to choose lifelong service. This is a remarkable verse. Freedom was offered, but love chose to stay. The servant was not forced. He chose service out of love for his master and family. This pictures the believer's relationship with Christ. We are set free from sin, yet we choose to serve Christ out of love. Romans 6 says, being then made free from sin, ye became the servants of righteousness. Paul often called himself a servant of Jesus Christ. Love transforms service into joy. Galatians 5 says, by love serve one another. Today, choose to serve Christ out of love, not obligation. He has set you free. Let love bind you to Him willingly.
S: A servant could plainly say that he loved his master, wife and children and did not want to go free. Love led him to choose lifelong service, picturing the believer's willing devotion to Christ. Choose to serve Christ out of love, not obligation.

== 21:6
T: The bored ear
R: 'Then his master shall bring him unto the judges; he shall also bring him to the door, or unto the door post; and his master shall bore his ear through with an aul; and he shall serve him for ever.' If the servant chose to stay, a public ceremony marked his decision. At the doorpost, his ear was pierced with an awl. This permanent mark showed he was a servant by choice for life. The ear is significant. It represents listening and obedience. A pierced ear declared, I will listen to my master's voice forever. Psalm 40 says, mine ears hast thou opened… I delight to do thy will, O my God. Hebrews 10 applies this psalm to Jesus. Christ Himself chose to be the obedient servant of the Father. Philippians 2 says He took upon him the form of a servant. Notice that the ceremony was public, before judges. Commitment was declared openly. Today, let your ear be open to God. Commit yourself publicly and willingly to listen and obey Him.

== 21:7
T: Protection for daughters
R: 'And if a man sell his daughter to be a maidservant, she shall not go out as the menservants do.' This law addressed a poor father selling his daughter as a maidservant. She would not simply be released like male servants. The following verses explain that she was protected with special rights, often leading to marriage. This verse reflects deep poverty. A father might sell his daughter to survive. It was a tragic situation. But God's law did not ignore her. It gave her additional protections that male servants did not need. Notice that God's law consistently looked out for the most vulnerable. Women in the ancient world had few rights. Here, God built in safeguards. The goal was to secure her future, not exploit her. Psalm 68 says God is a father of the fatherless. God sees vulnerable women and girls. Today, pray for women and girls trapped by poverty around the world. And support efforts to protect them from exploitation.

== 21:8
T: Dealt deceitfully
R: 'If she please not her master, who hath betrothed her to himself, then shall he let her be redeemed: to sell her unto a strange nation he shall have no power, seeing he hath dealt deceitfully with her.' If the master who intended to marry her changed his mind, he had to let her be redeemed. He could not sell her to foreigners. By breaking his commitment, he had dealt deceitfully with her. God held the master accountable. He could not discard her or sell her abroad. Breaking a promise to her was called deceit. God takes our commitments seriously, especially to the vulnerable. Notice the word redeemed. Her family could buy her back. Redemption was always available. This theme of redemption runs through all of Scripture. Even in hard situations, God provides a way back. Ruth 4 shows a kinsman redeemer restoring a vulnerable woman. Christ is our ultimate Redeemer. Today, keep your commitments, especially to those who depend on you. And rejoice that God always provides a way of redemption.

== 21:9
T: As a daughter
R: 'And if he have betrothed her unto his son, he shall deal with her after the manner of daughters.' If the master arranged for her to marry his son, he must treat her as a daughter. She would have full family status. Her position changed from servant to family member. This law elevated her dignity. She was not to be treated as property but as a daughter. Marriage brought her into the family with all its rights and protections. This pictures what God does for us. We come as servants, but He makes us His children. Galatians 4 says, Wherefore thou art no more a servant, but a son. First John 3 says, Behold, what manner of love the Father hath bestowed upon us, that we should be called the sons of God. In God's family, we receive full status, not partial acceptance. Today, rejoice that God treats you as His child. Your place in His family is secure, with all the rights and love of a son or daughter.

== 21:10
T: Not diminished
R: 'If he take him another wife; her food, her raiment, and her duty of marriage, shall he not diminish.' If the man took another wife, he could not reduce the first wife's food, clothing, or marital rights. Her needs had to be met fully. She could not be neglected or set aside. This law protected women in a culture that permitted multiple wives. While Scripture's ideal is one man and one woman, as Genesis 2 shows, God's law limited the harm of polygamy. The first wife could not be cast off or starved. Notice the three areas protected: food, clothing, and marital intimacy. These represent physical provision and emotional care. Marriage involves meeting both. First Timothy 5 says those who do not provide for their own have denied the faith. Ephesians 5 calls husbands to nourish and cherish their wives. Today, if you are married, consider whether you are fully caring for your spouse. Provide faithfully, both physically and emotionally. Never let love be diminished.

== 21:12
T: Life for life
R: 'He that smiteth a man, so that he die, shall be surely put to death.' Whoever strikes someone and kills him must be put to death. This law reflects the seriousness of murder. Human life is so valuable that taking it deliberately required the ultimate penalty. This principle goes back to Genesis 9: Whoso sheddeth man's blood, by man shall his blood be shed: for in the image of God made he man. The severity of the penalty reflects the value of the victim. Life is precious because it bears God's image. But the underlying principle remains: human life is sacred. Murder is an attack on God's image. Justice must take it seriously. Yet the gospel adds another dimension. Jesus died for murderers too. Paul, who consented to Stephen's death, received grace. First Timothy 1 says Christ came to save sinners; of whom I am chief. Today, value every human life as sacred. And remember that God's grace reaches even the worst of sinners who repent.

== 21:13
T: A place to flee
R: 'And if a man lie not in wait, but God deliver him into his hand; then I will appoint thee a place whither he shall flee.' God distinguished between murder and accidental killing. If someone did not plan to kill, but a death happened by accident, God would provide a place to flee. These later became the cities of refuge. Intent matters to God. He does not treat accidents the same as deliberate murder. Justice must consider the heart and circumstances. The cities of refuge, described in Numbers 35, protected those who killed unintentionally from revenge. This shows God's careful justice. He provided mercy for those who did not deserve the full penalty. Hebrews 6 speaks of believers who have fled for refuge to lay hold upon the hope set before us. Christ is our city of refuge. In Him, we find safety from judgment. Today, practice careful justice. Consider intentions and circumstances before condemning. And run to Christ, your refuge, where you are safe.

This law strikes at the heart of slavery based on kidnapping. Joseph had been sold by his brothers. Israel had been enslaved in Egypt. First Timothy 1 lists menstealers among the lawless and ungodly. Human trafficking remains a terrible evil today. Millions are kidnapped, sold, and exploited. God's law condemns it absolutely. Every person bears God's image and has dignity that cannot be bought or sold. Proverbs 31 says, Open thy mouth for the dumb in the cause of all such as are appointed to destruction. Today, pray for victims of human trafficking. Support efforts to rescue them and bring justice to those who exploit them.

== 21:21
T: A hard verse
R: 'Notwithstanding, if he continue a day or two, he shall not be punished: for he is his money.' If the servant survived a day or two before dying, the master would not be punished in the same way, since the servant was his money. It reflects an ancient economic reality where servants represented financial investment. The law assumed the master did not intend to kill, since he would lose his own property. We should read such verses honestly. God's law worked within a fallen world, limiting harm rather than immediately abolishing every unjust structure. Over time, Scripture's revelation moved toward full recognition of human dignity. Galatians 3 says, there is neither bond nor free… for ye are all one in Christ Jesus. Philemon shows Paul urging a master to receive his runaway servant as a brother beloved. The trajectory of Scripture leads to freedom and equality in Christ. Today, read difficult passages with honesty and trust. God's full revelation in Christ declares the equal worth of every person.

== 21:23
T: If any mischief follow
R: 'And if any mischief follow, then thou shalt give life for life.' If further harm followed, the penalty matched the injury. If a life was lost, the penalty was life for life. Justice was proportional. This begins the famous principle of proportional justice. Punishment should match the crime, not exceed it. This principle protected against excessive revenge. In a world where feuds escalated, God set limits. Notice that the life of the woman or child was valued equally. If mischief followed, meaning serious harm or death, full justice applied. God did not treat these lives as less important. This law reflects God's justice. He does not overlook harm, and He does not demand more than is fair.
