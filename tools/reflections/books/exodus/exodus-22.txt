# Exodus 22: Restitution, compassion and holiness

== 22:1
T: Restore fivefold
R: 'If a man shall steal an ox, or a sheep, and kill it, or sell it; he shall restore five oxen for an ox, and four sheep for a sheep.' A thief who stole and killed or sold an ox had to repay five oxen. For a sheep, he paid four. Restitution went far beyond the value of what was taken. Theft was costly. The extra repayment discouraged stealing and compensated the victim generously. An ox was a working animal, so the loss was greater. The law took both value and livelihood into account. Notice that God's response to theft was restoration, not just punishment. The victim was made more than whole. Zacchaeus echoed this in Luke 19 when he promised to restore fourfold. True repentance goes beyond the minimum. It seeks to bless those we have wronged. Today, if you have taken anything unjustly, make it right generously. Let your repentance be more than a bare return.
S: A thief who stole and killed or sold an ox had to restore five oxen, and four sheep for a sheep. God's response to theft was generous restitution that left the victim more than whole. If you have taken anything unjustly, make it right generously.
P: Generous restitution. Exodus 22:1: 'he shall restore five oxen for an ox, and four sheep for a sheep.' True repentance goes beyond the minimum. Make things right generously.

== 22:2
T: Defending the home
R: 'If a thief be found breaking up, and be smitten that he die, there shall no blood be shed for him.' If a thief broke into a house at night and was struck and killed, the homeowner was not guilty. In the darkness, he could not know the intruder's intent. Defending one's household was permitted. God's law recognized the right to protect one's family. A homeowner surprised in the dark faced a real threat. He could not stop to determine whether the thief was armed or intended violence. Yet this law was carefully limited, as the next verse shows. The right of defense was not unlimited. Context mattered. Nehemiah 4 records the people working with weapons ready to protect their families. Protection of the vulnerable in our care is a responsibility. Today, take seriously your responsibility to protect those in your home. And trust God, who is the ultimate guardian of your household. Psalm 127 says, Except the LORD keep the city, the watchman waketh but in vain.
S: If a thief was caught breaking in and was struck so that he died, no blood guilt fell on the defender. God's law recognized the right to protect one's household in a dangerous moment. Take seriously your responsibility to protect those in your care and trust God as the ultimate guardian.
P: Protecting your home. Exodus 22:2: 'If a thief be found breaking up, and be smitten that he die, there shall no blood be shed for him.' Guard your household and trust God as its keeper.

== 22:3
T: In daylight, restraint
R: 'If the sun be risen upon him, there shall be blood shed for him; for he should make full restitution; if he have nothing, then he shall be sold for his theft.' But if the thief was killed after sunrise, the homeowner was guilty. In daylight, one could see the situation clearly. A thief could be caught and made to repay. Killing him was no longer justified. This distinction is wise. Darkness justified defensive force because the threat was unknown. Daylight demanded restraint because other options existed. The thief deserved punishment, but not death. His life still had value. God's law balanced protection with restraint. Even a thief was not to be killed needlessly. Justice called for restitution, not revenge. If the thief had nothing, he would work to repay. Romans 12 says, Recompense to no man evil for evil. Ephesians 4 says the thief should labour, working with his hands. Today, when wronged, choose restraint and justice over revenge. Even those who harm us bear God's image.
S: If the thief was killed after sunrise, the defender was guilty, for the thief should make restitution or be sold to repay. Daylight called for restraint because other options existed. When wronged, choose restraint and justice over revenge.
P: Restraint in the light. Exodus 22:3: 'If the sun be risen upon him, there shall be blood shed for him.' Justice seeks restitution, not revenge. Choose restraint today.

== 22:4
T: Restore double
R: 'If the theft be certainly found in his hand alive, whether it be ox, or ass, or sheep; he shall restore double.' If the stolen animal was found alive in the thief's possession, he repaid double. This was less than fivefold, since the animal could be returned. But it was still more than the original value. The law was proportional. A thief who had not yet sold or killed the animal paid less than one who had. The punishment fit the extent of the wrong. Notice that even in the lesser case, the thief paid double. Stealing always cost more than it gained. Proverbs 6 says that a thief, if he be found, shall restore sevenfold. Sin never pays in the end. It promises gain but brings loss. Galatians 6 says, whatsoever a man soweth, that shall he also reap. Today, remember that dishonest gain always costs more than it is worth. Choose integrity, even when shortcuts seem appealing.
S: If the stolen animal was found alive with the thief, he had to restore double. The law was proportional, yet stealing always cost more than it gained. Remember that dishonest gain costs more than it is worth and choose integrity.
P: Sin never pays. Exodus 22:4: 'If the theft be certainly found in his hand alive… he shall restore double.' Dishonest gain always costs more. Choose integrity today.

== 22:5
T: Of the best
R: 'If a man shall cause a field or vineyard to be eaten, and shall put in his beast, and shall feed in another man's field; of the best of his own field, and of the best of his own vineyard, shall he make restitution.' If someone let his animal graze in another man's field or vineyard, he had to repay from the best of his own field and vineyard. Not average produce, but his best. Restitution was to be generous. Notice the phrase of the best. When we wrong someone, we should not repay grudgingly with leftovers. We should give our best. Genuine repentance is generous, not stingy. This also reflects respect for neighbors' property. Letting animals graze where they did not belong was taking advantage. God's law called for honoring boundaries. Proverbs 22 says, Remove not the ancient landmark. When we cross boundaries and harm others, we must make it right fully and generously. Today, respect the boundaries of others. And when you wrong someone, offer your best in making it right.

== 22:6
T: He that kindled the fire
R: 'If fire break out, and catch in thorns, so that the stacks of corn, or the standing corn, or the field, be consumed therewith; he that kindled the fire shall surely make restitution.' If someone started a fire that spread through thorns and burned another's grain or field, the one who kindled it had to make restitution. Even if he did not intend the damage, he was responsible. A small spark can destroy a whole field. The person who started it bears responsibility for what follows. This principle applies to our words and actions. James 3 says, Behold, how great a matter a little fire kindleth! And the tongue is a fire. We are responsible for the fires we start, even if we did not intend the damage. Proverbs 26 says, Where no wood is, there the fire goeth out: so where there is no talebearer, the strife ceaseth. Today, be careful with sparks. Guard your words. And if you have started a fire, take responsibility for the damage.

== 22:7
T: Entrusted goods
R: 'If a man shall deliver unto his neighbour money or stuff to keep, and it be stolen out of the man's house; if the thief be found, let him pay double.' If someone entrusted money or goods to a neighbor, and they were stolen, the thief had to pay double if caught. Trust between neighbors was protected by law. Entrusting goods to a neighbor was an act of trust. God's law honored that trust and protected both parties. The thief who violated it paid heavily. When people can rely on one another, relationships flourish. When trust is broken, everything suffers. First Corinthians 4 says, it is required in stewards, that a man be found faithful. Those entrusted with others' property must guard it carefully. Luke 16 says, if ye have not been faithful in that which is another man's, who shall give you that which is your own? Today, be faithful with what others have entrusted to you. Guard their trust as carefully as you would your own possessions.

If the thief was not found, the neighbor who held the goods had to appear before the judges. They would determine whether he had taken the goods himself. Suspicion was investigated fairly. This law protected both parties. The owner could seek justice. The keeper had a chance to prove his innocence. Accusations should be investigated, not ignored or immediately believed. Deuteronomy 19 says, at the mouth of two witnesses… shall the matter be established. Justice requires evidence and fairness. Notice that the keeper had to give account. Those entrusted with responsibility must be willing to be examined. Integrity welcomes scrutiny. Second Corinthians 8 records Paul taking care that no one could blame him in handling money. Today, be willing to give account for what you manage. And when accusations arise, seek fair process rather than rushing to judgment.

For every kind of dispute over property, whether animals, clothing, or lost items, both sides were to bring their case before the judges. Whoever the judges found guilty would pay double. Disputes were resolved through proper process. God provided a system for resolving conflicts. They could appeal to impartial judges. Notice the phrase the cause of both parties. == 22:13
T: Bring it for witness
R: 'If it be torn in pieces, then let him bring it for witness, and he shall not make good that which was torn.' If a wild animal killed the entrusted animal, the keeper could bring the remains as evidence. Then he would not have to repay. Evidence of an unavoidable loss cleared him. This law distinguished between negligence and unavoidable tragedy. A keeper could not prevent a lion or bear from attacking. If he could show the evidence, he was not held responsible. Fairness considered what was within his control. Jacob referred to this practice in Genesis 31, complaining that Laban made him bear the loss of animals torn by beasts. Jacob had been treated unfairly. God's law protected keepers from such injustice. We should also be fair in assigning blame. Not every loss is someone's fault. Sometimes tragedy simply happens. Today, be fair when losses occur. Distinguish between carelessness and unavoidable misfortune. Do not blame others for what they could not control.
