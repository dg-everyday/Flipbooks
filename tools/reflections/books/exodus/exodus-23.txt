# Exodus 23: Justice, rest, feasts and the promise of the Angel

== 23:1
T: No false report
R: 'Thou shalt not raise a false report: put not thine hand with the wicked to be an unrighteous witness.' God forbade spreading false reports or joining with the wicked to give false testimony. Rumors and lies were to have no place among His people. A false report can destroy a person's life. Once spread, it is hard to recall. Joining with others to spread falsehood multiplies the damage. God calls His people to guard their words. Proverbs 10 says, he that uttereth a slander, is a fool. Notice the phrase put not thine hand with the wicked. Sometimes we do not start a lie, but we help spread it. Passing along gossip or agreeing with false accusations makes us partners in the wrong. Ephesians 4 says, Let no corrupt communication proceed out of your mouth. Today, refuse to spread rumors. Check facts before repeating anything. Do not lend your voice to falsehood, even when others do.
S: God forbade raising a false report or joining the wicked as an unrighteous witness. Spreading or agreeing with lies makes us partners in the harm. Refuse to spread rumors and do not lend your voice to falsehood.
P: Don't spread it. Exodus 23:1: 'Thou shalt not raise a false report.' Passing along gossip makes you a partner in harm. Check facts and guard your words today.

== 23:2
T: Not with the multitude
R: 'Thou shalt not follow a multitude to do evil; neither shalt thou speak in a cause to decline after many to wrest judgment.' God warned against following the crowd into wrongdoing. Do not go along with the majority when they pervert justice. Doing what is right sometimes means standing alone. Peer pressure is powerful. When everyone is doing something, it feels right. But the majority can be wrong. Popular opinion is not the measure of truth. Israel would face this often. At the golden calf, the crowd demanded an idol. At Kadesh, the majority refused to enter the land. Only a few stood firm. Romans 12 says, be not conformed to this world. Matthew 7 warns that wide is the gate… that leadeth to destruction, and many there be which go in thereat. Courage means doing right even when it is unpopular. Today, resist the pull of the crowd when it leads away from God. Stand for truth, even if you stand alone.
S: God warned His people not to follow a multitude to do evil or side with the many to pervert justice. The majority can be wrong, and doing right sometimes means standing alone. Resist the crowd when it leads away from God and stand for truth.
P: Not with the crowd. Exodus 23:2: 'Thou shalt not follow a multitude to do evil.' The majority can be wrong. Stand for truth, even if you stand alone today.

== 23:3
T: No favoritism
R: 'Neither shalt thou countenance a poor man in his cause.' God commanded that a poor person should not be favored in court simply because he is poor. Justice must be impartial, even toward the vulnerable. This verse balances the many laws protecting the poor. God cares deeply for the poor. But justice cannot be bent in their favor. Truth matters more than sympathy. A poor person who is guilty is still guilty. Leviticus 19 says, thou shalt not respect the person of the poor, nor honour the person of the mighty: but in righteousness shalt thou judge. Justice must be blind to status, whether rich or poor. This protects the integrity of justice for everyone. If courts favored the poor unfairly, trust would erode. God's justice is perfectly fair. James 2 warns against partiality in any direction. Today, practice fairness without favoritism. Care for the poor, but do not twist truth for anyone. Let justice be impartial.
S: God commanded that a poor man not be favored in his cause simply because he is poor. Justice must be impartial, neither bent for the mighty nor for the vulnerable. Care for the poor, but practice fairness without twisting truth for anyone.
P: Impartial justice. Exodus 23:3: 'Neither shalt thou countenance a poor man in his cause.' Care for the poor, but justice must be fair to all. Practice impartiality today.

== 23:4
T: Your enemy's ox
R: 'If thou meet thine enemy's ox or his ass going astray, thou shalt surely bring it back to him again.' If you find your enemy's ox or donkey wandering loose, you must return it to him. God commanded kindness even toward enemies. Personal conflict did not excuse neglect. This is remarkable. Long before Jesus said love your enemies, God's law commanded practical kindness toward them. Returning a lost animal was helping someone you disliked. Enmity was no excuse for withholding good. Jesus expanded this in Matthew 5: Love your enemies, bless them that curse you, do good to them that hate you. Proverbs 25 says, If thine enemy be hungry, give him bread to eat. Kindness to enemies can soften hearts and break cycles of hostility. Romans 12 says, overcome evil with good. Today, look for a practical way to do good to someone who has wronged you. Return what is theirs. Show kindness that surprises them.
S: God commanded that if you meet your enemy's ox or donkey going astray, you must bring it back to him. Practical kindness was required even toward enemies. Look for a practical way to do good to someone who has wronged you.
P: Kindness to enemies. Exodus 23:4: 'If thou meet thine enemy's ox… thou shalt surely bring it back to him.' Do good to someone who has wronged you today.

== 23:5
T: Help him that hateth thee
R: 'If thou see the ass of him that hateth thee lying under his burden, and wouldest forbear to help him, thou shalt surely help with him.' If you see the donkey of someone who hates you collapsed under its load, you might be tempted not to help. But you must help him. God commanded working alongside an enemy to relieve a burden. Notice the honesty: and wouldest forbear to help him. God acknowledged the natural reluctance. We do not want to help those who hate us. Yet God commanded it anyway. Feelings do not excuse failing to do good. The phrase help with him is beautiful. It means working together. Helping an enemy's animal required standing beside the enemy himself. Shared work can begin to heal hostility. Luke 6 says, do good to them which hate you. Today, help someone who has been hostile to you. Work alongside them. Let your kindness open a door to reconciliation.
S: God commanded that if you see the donkey of one who hates you lying under its burden, you must help him, even if you would rather not. Working alongside an enemy can begin to heal hostility. Help someone who has been hostile to you and let kindness open a door to reconciliation.
P: Help your enemy. Exodus 23:5: 'If thou see the ass of him that hateth thee lying under his burden… thou shalt surely help with him.' Work alongside them. Kindness heals.

== 23:6
T: Justice for the poor
R: 'Thou shalt not wrest the judgment of thy poor in his cause.' God commanded that justice not be twisted against the poor. Their cases must be judged fairly. Their poverty must not make them easy targets for injustice. This complements verse 3. The poor must not be favored unfairly, but neither may they be denied justice. Often the poor lack resources to defend themselves. They cannot hire advocates or pay bribes. God's law protected them. Notice the phrase thy poor. God calls them your poor, people you are responsible for. They are not strangers but members of your community. Proverbs 31 says, plead the cause of the poor and needy. Isaiah 1 says, relieve the oppressed, judge the fatherless, plead for the widow. God's people are to be advocates for those without power. Today, stand up for justice for the poor. Speak for those who cannot speak for themselves. Ensure that poverty does not make anyone a victim of injustice.
S: God commanded that the judgment of the poor not be wrested in their cause. Those without resources must not become easy targets for injustice. Stand up for justice for the poor and speak for those who cannot speak for themselves.
P: Justice for the poor. Exodus 23:6: 'Thou shalt not wrest the judgment of thy poor in his cause.' Speak up for those without power today.

== 23:7
T: Far from a false matter
R: 'Keep thee far from a false matter; and the innocent and righteous slay thou not: for I will not justify the wicked.' Stay far away from false charges, God said. Do not put the innocent to death. For I will not acquit the guilty. God's justice would ultimately prevail. Notice keep thee far. Not just avoid falsehood, but stay far from it. Do not get close to lies or deceptive schemes. Distance from sin is wise. God's promise is sobering: I will not justify the wicked. Even if human courts fail, God will not. Those who condemn the innocent will answer to Him. Yet the gospel adds something astonishing. Romans 4 speaks of God who justifieth the ungodly through faith in Christ. God does not overlook sin, but He justifies sinners who trust Jesus, because Christ bore their guilt. Today, keep far from falsehood. And rejoice that the God who will not excuse wickedness has made a way for sinners to be justified through Christ.
S: God commanded His people to keep far from a false matter and not slay the innocent, for He will not justify the wicked. Even if human courts fail, God's justice will prevail, yet in Christ He justifies sinners who believe. Keep far from falsehood and rejoice in justification through Christ.
P: Keep far from lies. Exodus 23:7: 'Keep thee far from a false matter… for I will not justify the wicked.' God's justice prevails, yet in Christ He justifies sinners.

== 23:8
T: The gift blindeth
R: 'And thou shalt take no gift: for the gift blindeth the wise, and perverteth the words of the righteous.' God forbade taking bribes. A bribe blinds even wise people and twists the words of the righteous. Money corrupts judgment. This is a powerful insight into human nature. Even good and wise people can be corrupted by gifts. A bribe does not just influence decisions; it blinds us to the truth. We begin to see what benefits us rather than what is right. Proverbs 17 says, A wicked man taketh a gift out of the bosom to pervert the ways of judgment. Bribery destroys justice and trust. Today, bribery may take subtle forms: favors, flattery, or benefits that make us look the other way. Anything that buys our silence or loyalty compromises integrity. First Timothy 6 says the love of money is the root of all evil. Today, refuse anything that might blind your judgment. Keep your integrity unbought, and let truth guide your decisions.
S: God forbade taking a gift or bribe, for it blinds the wise and perverts the words of the righteous. Even good people can be corrupted when gifts cloud their judgment. Refuse anything that might blind your judgment and keep your integrity unbought.
P: Unbought integrity. Exodus 23:8: 'the gift blindeth the wise, and perverteth the words of the righteous.' Refuse anything that would blind your judgment today.

== 23:9
T: Ye know the heart of a stranger
R: 'Also thou shalt not oppress a stranger: for ye know the heart of a stranger, seeing ye were strangers in the land of Egypt.' God repeated the command not to oppress foreigners. And He added a moving reason: you know the heart of a stranger. Israel knew from experience what it felt like to be an outsider. The phrase ye know the heart of a stranger is deeply personal. Israel had felt the loneliness, vulnerability, and fear of living in a foreign land. God wanted that memory to produce empathy. Our past pain can become a source of compassion. Those who have suffered can understand others who suffer. Second Corinthians 1 says God comforts us so that we may comfort others. Hebrews 13 says, Be not forgetful to entertain strangers. Today, let your own experiences of being an outsider make you kind to newcomers. You know their heart. Welcome them as you would have wanted to be welcomed.
S: God repeated that Israel must not oppress a stranger, for they knew the heart of a stranger from their time in Egypt. Their past pain was to become a source of empathy. Let your own experiences of being an outsider make you kind to newcomers.
P: You know their heart. Exodus 23:9: 'ye know the heart of a stranger, seeing ye were strangers.' Let your past pain become compassion. Welcome a newcomer today.

== 23:10
T: Six years of sowing
R: 'And six years thou shalt sow thy land, and shalt gather in the fruits thereof.' God gave Israel six years to sow their land and gather its harvest. Work and harvest were good gifts. But this verse sets up a pattern of rest, as the next verse shows. God established rhythms not only for weeks but for years. Six years of work, then rest. The same pattern of six and one that governed the week also governed the land. God's design for rest extends beyond individuals to creation itself. Diligent work is honored. For six years, Israel was to farm faithfully and reap the rewards of labor. Proverbs 12 says, He that tilleth his land shall be satisfied with bread. But work is not the whole of life. God also built rest into time. Today, work diligently in your season of labor. And trust that God's rhythms include both work and rest, both sowing and refreshment.
S: God told Israel to sow their land and gather its fruits for six years. He established a rhythm of work and rest not only for weeks but for years. Work diligently in your season of labor and trust God's rhythm of work and rest.
P: Seasons of sowing. Exodus 23:10: 'six years thou shalt sow thy land, and shalt gather in the fruits thereof.' God honors diligent work. Trust His rhythm of work and rest.

== 23:11
T: Rest for the land
R: 'But the seventh year thou shalt let it rest and lie still; that the poor of thy people may eat: and what they leave the beasts of the field shall eat.' In the seventh year, the land was to rest. Whatever grew on its own would feed the poor and the wild animals. The same applied to vineyards and olive groves. The land's rest blessed the needy. This sabbatical year was an act of faith. Israel had to trust God to provide enough in the sixth year to last through the seventh. It was also an act of generosity. The poor could gather freely what grew. Notice how rest and generosity are connected. When the owner stopped working the land, the poor were fed. Our rest can make room for others' blessing. Leviticus 25 adds that God would command His blessing in the sixth year. Today, consider how your rest might bless others. Trust God enough to let things lie still. And let your margins become provision for those in need.
S: In the seventh year the land was to rest and lie still so the poor could eat what grew, and the beasts what remained. The sabbatical year required faith and became generosity to the needy. Trust God enough to rest, and let your margins become provision for others.
P: Rest that feeds others. Exodus 23:11: 'the seventh year thou shalt let it rest… that the poor of thy people may eat.' Your margins can bless the needy. Trust God and give.

== 23:12
T: That they may be refreshed
R: 'Six days thou shalt do thy work, and on the seventh day thou shalt rest: that thine ox and thine ass may rest, and the son of thy handmaid, and the stranger, may be refreshed.' God repeated the weekly Sabbath command, emphasizing its benefit for others: so that animals may rest, and the servant's son and the foreigner may be refreshed. The Sabbath was a gift to everyone. Notice the word refreshed. The Hebrew means to catch one's breath. God wanted the most vulnerable workers to have time to breathe. Rest was not a luxury for the rich but a right for all. This shows God's compassion for workers. Servants, foreigners, and even animals needed rest. Those with power were responsible to provide it. Deuteronomy 5 connects this to Israel's slavery. Remember how you never rested in Egypt. Do not deny rest to others. Today, ensure that those who work for you or with you have time to rest. Give others the gift of catching their breath.
S: God repeated that on the seventh day all should rest so that animals, the servant's son and the stranger may be refreshed. The Sabbath gave the most vulnerable workers time to catch their breath. Ensure that those who work for or with you have time to rest.

God told Israel to be careful in everything He had said. And they were not even to mention the names of other gods. False gods were not to be part of their conversation. To be circumspect means to be watchful and careful. Israel would live among nations with many gods. They needed to guard their hearts and their speech. Even speaking casually of false gods could lead to familiarity and then to worship. What we talk about shapes what we think about. What we think about shapes what we love. Psalm 16 says, their drink offerings of blood will I not offer, nor take up their names into my lips. Philippians 4 says, whatsoever things are pure… think on these things. Today, be careful about what fills your mind and conversation. Guard your words, and let them honor the one true God.

== 23:14
T: Three times a year
R: 'Three times thou shalt keep a feast unto me in the year.' God commanded Israel to celebrate three feasts to Him every year. Worship was not only weekly but seasonal. Regular celebrations would mark the rhythm of Israel's life. Notice the word feast. These were celebrations, not burdens. God wanted His people to gather joyfully, remembering His goodness. Faith includes festivity. God is not a joyless taskmaster. He commands His people to celebrate. Deuteronomy 16 says, thou shalt rejoice in thy feast. Joy is commanded. These feasts also brought the nation together. Families traveled, met, worshiped, and celebrated as one people. Shared celebration builds community. Today, find reasons to celebrate God's goodness. Mark special times of the year with worship and joy. And gather with God's people to rejoice in what He has done.

== 23:15
T: None shall appear empty
R: 'Thou shalt keep the feast of unleavened bread… in the time appointed of the month Abib; for in it thou camest out from Egypt: and none shall appear before me empty.' The first feast was Unleavened Bread, in the month Abib, remembering the exodus. And God added: no one shall appear before Me empty-handed. Worship included giving. Notice the connection between remembering salvation and bringing an offering. When we remember what God has done, we respond with gifts. Gratitude naturally overflows into generosity. Appearing empty would suggest forgetfulness or ingratitude. God had given Israel freedom, provision, and a future. Coming with nothing would not reflect that. Deuteronomy 16 says, Every man shall give as he is able, according to the blessing of the LORD thy God. Giving was proportional to blessing. Second Corinthians 9 says, God loveth a cheerful giver. Today, when you come to worship, come with something to give. Bring your thanks, your praise, your resources. Let gratitude for salvation overflow in generosity.

== 23:16
T: Harvest and ingathering
R: 'And the feast of harvest, the firstfruits of thy labours, which thou hast sown in the field: and the feast of ingathering, which is in the end of the year, when thou hast gathered in thy labours out of the field.' The other two feasts were the Feast of Harvest, celebrating the firstfruits, and the Feast of Ingathering at the end of the year, celebrating the completed harvest. God's people celebrated both the beginning and the end of the harvest. Gratitude was to mark every stage. At the first sign of harvest, they thanked God. When everything was gathered, they thanked Him again. These feasts later became Pentecost and Tabernacles. Acts 2 records the Spirit coming at Pentecost, the feast of firstfruits. Three thousand were saved, the first harvest of the church. God fulfilled these feasts in Christ and His people. Psalm 65 says, Thou crownest the year with thy goodness. Today, thank God at every stage. Celebrate beginnings with gratitude, and celebrate completions with praise. His goodness crowns your whole year.

== 23:18
T: Pure sacrifice
R: 'Thou shalt not offer the blood of my sacrifice with leavened bread; neither shall the fat of my sacrifice remain until the morning.' God gave two instructions about sacrifices. Do not offer the blood with anything leavened. And do not let the fat remain until morning. Sacrifices were to be pure and prompt. Leaven often represents sin and corruption in Scripture. Mixing it with sacrificial blood would defile the offering. God's sacrifices were to be holy and unmixed. The fat belonged to God and was to be burned promptly. Letting it sit until morning showed carelessness. Worship deserves our full attention, not delay. Notice the phrase my sacrifice. The offering belonged to God. It was not to be handled casually. These instructions point to Christ, the pure sacrifice without sin. Hebrews 9 says Christ offered himself without spot to God. Today, offer your worship to God with purity and promptness. Do not mix it with sin or neglect it with delay.
S: God commanded that His sacrifice not be offered with leavened bread and that its fat not remain until morning. Sacrifices were to be pure and prompt, pointing to Christ's spotless offering. Offer your worship with purity and promptness.

== 23:19
T: The first of the firstfruits
R: 'The first of the firstfruits of thy land thou shalt bring into the house of the LORD thy God. Thou shalt not seethe a kid in his mother's milk.' The very first of the firstfruits were to be brought to God's house. God deserved the first and best. And Israel was not to boil a young goat in its mother's milk. Notice the phrase first of the firstfruits. Not just the first, but the very first and best of the first. God's portion came off the top. This reflects His worth. The prohibition on boiling a kid in its mother's milk is mysterious. It may have been a pagan ritual. It may also reflect tenderness, refusing to use the mother's nourishment to cook her young. Either way, it shows that God's people were to be distinct and compassionate. Proverbs 3 says, Honour the LORD with… the firstfruits of all thine increase. Today, give God the very best of your first. And let compassion and distinctiveness mark even the small details of your life.

== 23:23
T: I will cut them off
R: 'For mine Angel shall go before thee, and bring thee in unto the Amorites, and the Hittites, and the Perizzites, and the Canaanites, and the Hivites, and the Jebusites: and I will cut them off.' God's Angel would lead Israel into the land of the Canaanite nations. And God Himself would cut them off. The land would be given by God's power. Israel would face many nations.
