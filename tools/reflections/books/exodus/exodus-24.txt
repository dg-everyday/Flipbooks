# Exodus 24: The covenant confirmed; Moses on the mount

== 24:1
T: Worship afar off
R: 'And he said unto Moses, Come up unto the LORD, thou, and Aaron, Nadab, and Abihu, and seventy of the elders of Israel; and worship ye afar off.' God invited Moses, Aaron, Aaron's sons Nadab and Abihu, and seventy elders to come up the mountain. But they were to worship from a distance. Access to God was granted, but with limits. This invitation was an honor. Leaders of Israel were called closer to God's presence. Yet they still had to keep their distance. Holiness set boundaries even for leaders. Notice Nadab and Abihu. They were among the privileged few invited near. Yet later, in Leviticus 10, they died for offering strange fire. Privilege does not guarantee faithfulness. Being close to holy things does not make us holy. Hebrews 10 shows the contrast in Christ: Having therefore, brethren, boldness to enter into the holiest by the blood of Jesus. Through Christ, we draw near. Today, value your access to God through Jesus. And remember that privilege calls for faithfulness, not presumption.
S: God invited Moses, Aaron, Nadab, Abihu and seventy elders to come up and worship afar off. Even leaders had limits, and some privileged ones later failed. Value your access to God through Jesus and let privilege call you to faithfulness.
P: Privilege and faithfulness. Exodus 24:1: 'Come up unto the LORD… and worship ye afar off.' Through Jesus we draw near. Let your access lead to faithfulness, not presumption.

== 24:2
T: Moses alone
R: 'And Moses alone shall come near the LORD: but they shall not come nigh; neither shall the people go up with him.' Only Moses could come near the LORD. The others had to stay back, and the people could not come up at all. Moses stood alone as the mediator between God and Israel. This verse highlights the unique role of a mediator. Moses alone could approach. He represented the people before God and brought God's word back to them. Leadership sometimes means standing alone. Moses bore a unique responsibility. He went where others could not go. This foreshadows Christ. First Timothy 2 says, there is one mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus. Only Jesus can bring us fully into God's presence. Hebrews 9 says He entered in once into the holy place. Yet through Him, we now can come near. Today, thank Jesus for standing in the gap. He alone could approach God fully, and He opened the way for you.
S: Moses alone was to come near the LORD, while the others stayed back and the people did not go up. He stood as the unique mediator between God and Israel, foreshadowing Christ. Thank Jesus, the one mediator, who opened the way for you to come near.
P: One mediator. Exodus 24:2: 'Moses alone shall come near the LORD.' Moses foreshadowed Christ, the one mediator. Through Jesus, you can come near today.

== 24:3
T: With one voice
R: 'And Moses came and told the people all the words of the LORD, and all the judgments: and all the people answered with one voice, and said, All the words which the LORD hath said will we do.' Moses told the people everything God had said, all the words and judgments. The people answered with one voice: All that the LORD has said, we will do. The nation committed itself to the covenant. Their unity was beautiful. With one voice, they promised obedience. But as before, their commitment would soon be tested. Notice that Moses told them everything. The people knew what they were agreeing to. Informed commitment is important. God does not want blind agreement but understanding obedience. Luke 14 speaks of counting the cost before building. Acts 4 says the believers were of one heart and of one soul. But unity must be sustained by grace. Today, commit yourself to God's word with understanding. And depend on His grace to keep your promises.
S: Moses told the people all the words of the LORD, and they answered with one voice that they would do all He said. Their united commitment was informed but would soon be tested. Commit yourself to God's word with understanding and depend on His grace to keep your promises.
P: One voice. Exodus 24:3: 'all the people answered with one voice… All the words which the LORD hath said will we do.' Commit with understanding, and rely on His grace.

== 24:4
T: Moses wrote
R: 'And Moses wrote all the words of the LORD, and rose up early in the morning, and builded an altar under the hill, and twelve pillars, according to the twelve tribes of Israel.' Moses wrote down all the LORD's words. Early the next morning, he built an altar at the foot of the mountain and set up twelve pillars, one for each tribe. The covenant was being formalized. Writing God's words preserved them. Written words endured. This is how Scripture came to be. God's servants recorded His words for future generations. Second Timothy 3 says, All scripture is given by inspiration of God. The twelve pillars represented every tribe. No one was left out. The covenant included all Israel. Each tribe stood before God as part of His people. Notice that Moses rose early. Diligence marked his service. He did not delay in carrying out God's work. Today, value the written word of God. Read it, study it, and treasure it. It preserves God's voice for every generation.
S: Moses wrote all the words of the LORD, rose early, and built an altar with twelve pillars for the twelve tribes. Writing preserved God's words, and the pillars showed every tribe included in the covenant. Value the written word of God and treasure it for every generation.
P: God's word written. Exodus 24:4: 'Moses wrote all the words of the LORD.' Scripture preserves God's voice. Read and treasure His word today.
H: #DailyGrace #Exodus244 #Scripture #Covenant #Diligence #BibleVerse

== 24:5
T: Burnt and peace offerings
R: 'And he sent young men of the children of Israel, which offered burnt offerings, and sacrificed peace offerings of oxen unto the LORD.' Moses sent young men to offer burnt offerings and peace offerings of oxen to the LORD. Sacrifice was part of making the covenant. Before the priesthood was established, young men performed this service. Two kinds of offerings were made. Burnt offerings were completely consumed, representing total dedication to God. Peace offerings were partly shared in a meal, representing fellowship with God. Covenant involved both surrender and communion. Our relationship with God includes both. We offer ourselves wholly to Him. And we enjoy fellowship with Him. Romans 12 says, present your bodies a living sacrifice. First John 1 says, truly our fellowship is with the Father, and with his Son Jesus Christ. Notice also the young men. God used the young in His service. First Timothy 4 says, Let no man despise thy youth. Today, offer yourself fully to God. And enjoy the fellowship He offers through Christ.
S: Young men of Israel offered burnt offerings and peace offerings of oxen to the LORD. The offerings represented total dedication and joyful fellowship with God. Offer yourself fully to God and enjoy the fellowship He offers through Christ.
P: Surrender and fellowship. Exodus 24:5: 'offered burnt offerings, and sacrificed peace offerings… unto the LORD.' Give yourself fully to God and enjoy fellowship with Him.
H: #DailyGrace #Exodus245 #Sacrifice #Fellowship #Dedication #BibleVerse

== 24:6
T: Half the blood
R: 'And Moses took half of the blood, and put it in basons; and half of the blood he sprinkled on the altar.' Moses divided the blood. Half he put in basins. The other half he sprinkled on the altar. The blood would bind both parties of the covenant: God and the people. The altar represented God. Sprinkling blood on it showed God's commitment to the covenant. The other half would be sprinkled on the people. Both sides were bound by blood. Blood in Scripture represents life. Leviticus 17 says, the life of the flesh is in the blood. A blood covenant was a life-and-death commitment. It was the most serious kind of agreement. This points to the new covenant in Christ's blood. Hebrews 9 says, without shedding of blood is no remission. Christ's blood binds us to God in an unbreakable covenant. Today, remember the seriousness of covenant with God. It was sealed in blood. And thank God that Christ's blood has sealed your covenant with Him forever.
S: Moses put half the blood in basins and sprinkled half on the altar. The blood bound both God and the people in a life-and-death covenant, pointing to Christ's blood. Thank God that Christ's blood has sealed your covenant with Him forever.
P: Sealed in blood. Exodus 24:6: 'half of the blood he sprinkled on the altar.' Covenant with God is serious. Christ's blood seals yours forever.

== 24:7
T: The book of the covenant
R: 'And he took the book of the covenant, and read in the audience of the people: and they said, All that the LORD hath said will we do, and be obedient.' Moses read the book of the covenant aloud to the people. Again they responded: All that the LORD has said we will do, and we will obey. This was their third declaration of commitment. Notice that Moses read the written word publicly. The people heard exactly what they were agreeing to. God's word was not hidden. It was read openly to all. Public reading of Scripture is important. Nehemiah 8 describes Ezra reading the law to the people from morning until midday. First Timothy 4 says, give attendance to reading. Hearing God's word together shapes a community. The people added and be obedient. Hearing must lead to doing. James 1 says, be ye doers of the word, and not hearers only. Today, listen to God's word with the intention to obey. Let hearing lead to doing.
S: Moses read the book of the covenant to the people, and they said they would do all the LORD said and be obedient. Public reading of God's word prepared them to respond with commitment. Listen to God's word with the intention to obey, letting hearing lead to doing.
P: Hear and obey. Exodus 24:7: 'All that the LORD hath said will we do, and be obedient.' Be a doer of the word, not a hearer only. Let hearing lead to action today.

== 24:8
T: The blood of the covenant
R: 'And Moses took the blood, and sprinkled it on the people, and said, Behold the blood of the covenant, which the LORD hath made with you concerning all these words.' Moses sprinkled the remaining blood on the people and declared: This is the blood of the covenant that the LORD has made with you. The covenant was sealed. God and Israel were bound together. These words echo through Scripture. At the Last Supper, Jesus took the cup and said in Matthew 26, this is my blood of the new testament, which is shed for many for the remission of sins. He deliberately echoed Moses. The new covenant is sealed with Christ's own blood. Hebrews 9 says Christ is the mediator of the new testament, by means of death. His blood does what animal blood could not. Hebrews 10 says it cleanses completely. Today, thank God for the blood of the new covenant. Every time you take communion, remember that Christ's blood binds you to God forever.
S: Moses sprinkled the blood on the people and declared, Behold the blood of the covenant, which the LORD hath made with you. Jesus echoed these words at the Last Supper when He established the new covenant in His blood. Thank God for the blood of the new covenant that binds you to Him forever.
P: The blood of the covenant. Exodus 24:8: 'Behold the blood of the covenant.' Jesus said, This is my blood of the new testament. His blood binds you to God forever.

== 24:9
T: Then went up
R: 'Then went up Moses, and Aaron, Nadab, and Abihu, and seventy of the elders of Israel.' After the covenant was sealed with blood, Moses, Aaron, Nadab, Abihu, and seventy elders went up the mountain. The blood made the way for them to approach. Notice the order. First the covenant was sealed with blood. Then the leaders went up. Access to God followed the shedding of blood. Without the sacrifice, they could not approach. This pattern is fulfilled in Christ. Hebrews 10 says we have boldness to enter into the holiest by the blood of Jesus. His blood opens the way. We do not approach God on our own merit but through the covenant blood. Notice also the seventy elders. Representatives of the whole nation went up. The people were represented before God through their leaders. Today, approach God through the blood of Christ. It is the only way into His presence. And thank Him that the way is now open.
S: After the covenant was sealed with blood, Moses, Aaron, Nadab, Abihu and seventy elders went up the mountain. Access to God followed the shedding of blood. Approach God through the blood of Christ, the only way into His presence.
P: Blood opens the way. Exodus 24:9: 'Then went up Moses, and Aaron… and seventy of the elders.' After the covenant blood, they approached. Through Christ's blood, the way is open.

== 24:10
T: They saw the God of Israel
R: 'And they saw the God of Israel: and there was under his feet as it were a paved work of a sapphire stone, and as it were the body of heaven in his clearness.' They saw the God of Israel. Under His feet was something like a pavement of sapphire, as clear and bright as the sky itself. It was a vision of breathtaking beauty. Human beings saw a vision of God. They did not see His full glory, which no one can see and live. But they saw something real. Notice what they described: what was under His feet. They could not look directly at God's face. They saw only the base of His presence, and even that was overwhelmingly beautiful. Ezekiel 1 describes a similar vision of sapphire. God's beauty is beyond description. Psalm 27 says, to behold the beauty of the LORD. Today, meditate on the beauty of God. Let worship fill you with awe at His glory.
S: They saw the God of Israel, and under His feet was something like a paved work of sapphire, clear as the sky itself. Even the base of His presence was overwhelmingly beautiful. Meditate on the beauty of God and let worship fill you with awe.
P: The beauty of God. Exodus 24:10: 'there was under his feet as it were a paved work of a sapphire stone.' Even glimpses of God are breathtaking. Worship Him in awe today.

== 24:11
T: They saw God, and did eat
R: 'And upon the nobles of the children of Israel he laid not his hand: also they saw God, and did eat and drink.' God did not strike the leaders who saw Him. They saw God, and they ate and drank. A covenant meal took place in God's presence. Those who should have died lived and feasted. This is grace. Sinful people saw God and were not destroyed. The covenant blood protected them. They were not only spared; they were welcomed to a meal. A meal in Scripture represents fellowship and peace. God shared a meal with them, confirming the covenant relationship. This anticipates the Lord's Supper. Believers eat and drink in God's presence, remembering Christ's sacrifice. It also anticipates the marriage supper of the Lamb in Revelation 19. One day, God's people will feast with Him forever. Isaiah 25 speaks of a feast of fat things prepared on God's mountain. Today, rejoice that through Christ, you are not only spared but welcomed to God's table. Feast on His grace.
S: God did not lay His hand on the nobles of Israel; they saw God and ate and drank. Sinful people were spared and welcomed to a covenant meal in His presence. Rejoice that through Christ you are welcomed to God's table, and feast on His grace.
P: Welcomed to the table. Exodus 24:11: 'they saw God, and did eat and drink.' Not only spared but welcomed to a meal! Through Christ, you're invited to His table.

== 24:12
T: Tables of stone
R: 'And the LORD said unto Moses, Come up to me into the mount, and be there: and I will give thee tables of stone, and a law, and commandments which I have written; that thou mayest teach them.' God called Moses higher. Come up to Me and stay there. I will give you stone tablets with the law and commandments I have written, so you can teach the people. Notice the phrase and be there. God did not just want Moses to visit. He wanted him to stay, to remain in His presence. God wrote the commandments Himself. They were not human ideas. They came from God's own hand. God's moral law does not change. The purpose was teaching. Moses would receive God's word in order to share it. Second Corinthians 3 contrasts tables of stone with fleshy tables of the heart. In the new covenant, God writes His law on our hearts. Today, spend time abiding with God. Let Him write His word on your heart. Then share it with others.
S: God called Moses to come up and stay on the mount to receive stone tablets with the law He had written so Moses could teach the people. Abiding with God came before teaching for Him. Spend time abiding with God and let Him write His word on your heart.
P: Come up and be there. Exodus 24:12: 'Come up to me into the mount, and be there.' Abide with God before serving Him. Let Him write His word on your heart.

== 24:13
T: Joshua went too
R: 'And Moses rose up, and his minister Joshua: and Moses went up into the mount of God.' Moses rose and went up the mountain of God with Joshua, his assistant. Joshua accompanied Moses partway. The young man was being trained by being near his mentor. Joshua is called Moses' minister, meaning his servant or assistant. He was learning by serving. He saw Moses' faith up close. He went where Moses went. Mentoring happens through proximity. Joshua did not learn only from lectures but from walking alongside Moses. He observed how Moses related to God. This prepared him to lead Israel into Canaan. Second Timothy 2 says, the things that thou hast heard of me… commit thou to faithful men. Paul mentored Timothy the same way. Today, if you are learning, stay close to godly mentors. Serve them and learn from their example. If you are mature in faith, invite someone younger to walk with you.
S: Moses rose up with his minister Joshua and went up into the mount of God. Joshua learned by serving and staying close to his mentor. Stay close to godly mentors, or invite someone younger to walk with you in faith.
P: Learning by walking together. Exodus 24:13: 'Moses rose up, and his minister Joshua.' Mentoring happens through proximity. Walk with someone in faith today.

== 24:14
T: Tarry ye here
R: 'And he said unto the elders, Tarry ye here for us, until we come again unto you: and, behold, Aaron and Hur are with you: if any man have any matters to do, let him come unto them.' Before going up, Moses told the elders to wait for him. He left Aaron and Hur in charge. Anyone with a dispute could go to them. Moses ensured that leadership continued in his absence. Good leaders prepare for their absence. Moses did not leave a vacuum. He appointed others to handle matters while he was gone. Sadly, while Moses was away, the people would pressure Aaron to make a golden calf. Delegated leadership needs strength and integrity. Aaron would fail this test. Notice the phrase tarry ye here… until we come again. The people were asked to wait. Their impatience would lead to sin. Psalm 27 says, Wait on the LORD. Today, prepare others to carry responsibility when you are away. And when you are asked to wait, wait faithfully.
S: Moses told the elders to tarry until he returned and left Aaron and Hur in charge of any matters. He prepared for his absence, though the people would soon struggle to wait. Prepare others to carry responsibility, and when you are asked to wait, wait faithfully.
P: Wait faithfully. Exodus 24:14: 'Tarry ye here for us, until we come again unto you.' Waiting is hard, but impatience leads to sin. Wait on the LORD today.

== 24:15
T: A cloud covered the mount
R: 'And Moses went up into the mount, and a cloud covered the mount.' Moses climbed the mountain, and a cloud covered it. God's presence enveloped Sinai. Moses was entering the cloud of God's glory. The cloud represented God's presence. It was the same cloud that led Israel through the wilderness. Now it covered the mountain where God would speak with Moses. Clouds both reveal and conceal. They show that God is present while hiding His full glory. Moses walked into mystery. Sometimes God's presence feels like a cloud. We cannot see clearly. Yet He is there. At the transfiguration, Matthew 17 says, a bright cloud overshadowed them: and behold a voice out of the cloud. God still speaks from the cloud. First Thessalonians 4 says believers will meet the Lord in the clouds. Today, if your path feels cloudy, remember that God is often present in the cloud. Walk forward in faith, trusting that He is near.
S: Moses went up into the mount, and a cloud covered it. God's presence enveloped Sinai, revealing and concealing His glory. If your path feels cloudy, remember that God is often present in the cloud and walk forward in faith.
P: God in the cloud. Exodus 24:15: 'Moses went up into the mount, and a cloud covered the mount.' When things feel cloudy, God is near. Walk forward in faith today.

== 24:16
T: Six days of waiting
R: 'And the glory of the LORD abode upon mount Sinai, and the cloud covered it six days: and the seventh day he called unto Moses out of the midst of the cloud.' The glory of the LORD rested on Sinai, covered by the cloud for six days. On the seventh day, God called Moses from within the cloud. Moses waited six days before God spoke. Waiting was part of the encounter. Moses did not rush into God's presence. He waited in the cloud for six days. Preparation and patience preceded revelation. God spoke on the seventh day, the day of completion and rest. God works on His timeline, not ours. Isaiah 40 says, they that wait upon the LORD shall renew their strength. Waiting is not wasted time. It prepares the heart to hear. But some encounters require patient waiting. Psalm 62 says, My soul, wait thou only upon God. Today, if God seems silent, keep waiting. He will speak in His time. The waiting prepares you to hear.
S: The glory of the LORD abode on Sinai, and after six days covered by the cloud, God called Moses on the seventh day. Moses waited patiently before God spoke. If God seems silent, keep waiting, for the waiting prepares you to hear Him.
P: Wait for His voice. Exodus 24:16: 'the cloud covered it six days: and the seventh day he called unto Moses.' Waiting isn't wasted. God will speak in His time.

== 24:17
T: Like devouring fire
R: 'And the sight of the glory of the LORD was like devouring fire on the top of the mount in the eyes of the children of Israel.' To the Israelites below, the glory of the LORD on the mountaintop looked like a consuming fire. What Moses entered, they saw as blazing flames. God's glory was both beautiful and terrifying. Fire represents God's holiness. It purifies and consumes. Hebrews 12 says, our God is a consuming fire. God's holiness cannot coexist with sin. To the sinful, His glory looks dangerous. Notice that Moses walked into this fire. What looked like destruction from below was communion for Moses. The same glory that terrified the people welcomed the mediator. This shows the difference a mediator makes. Through Christ, God's fire purifies rather than destroys us. Malachi 3 says He is like a refiner's fire. He burns away impurity and leaves us refined. Today, do not fear God's holiness if you are in Christ. Let His fire purify you and draw you closer.
S: To the Israelites below, the glory of the LORD on the mountaintop looked like devouring fire. Moses walked into that fire as mediator and found communion. Through Christ, let God's holy fire purify you and draw you closer.
P: Consuming fire. Exodus 24:17: 'the glory of the LORD was like devouring fire.' God's holiness purifies. In Christ, His fire refines rather than destroys you.

== 24:18
T: Forty days and forty nights
R: 'And Moses went into the midst of the cloud, and gat him up into the mount: and Moses was in the mount forty days and forty nights.' Moses entered the cloud and went up the mountain. He stayed there forty days and forty nights. During this time, God gave him detailed instructions for the tabernacle and its worship. Forty days is significant in Scripture. The flood rains lasted forty days. Elijah traveled forty days to Horeb. Jesus fasted forty days in the wilderness. Moses spent extended time with God. Deep encounter requires time. Many of us want brief moments with God, but transformation often comes through extended seeking. Notice that Moses went into the midst of the cloud. He entered fully into God's presence. Jeremiah 29 says, ye shall seek me, and find me, when ye shall search for me with all your heart. Today, set aside extended time to be with God. Go into the midst of His presence. Let Him meet you there.
S: Moses went into the midst of the cloud and stayed on the mount forty days and forty nights. Extended time with God prepared him to receive the tabernacle instructions. Set aside extended time to seek God wholeheartedly and let Him meet you.
P: Extended time with God. Exodus 24:18: 'Moses was in the mount forty days and forty nights.' Deep encounters require time. Seek Him with all your heart today.
