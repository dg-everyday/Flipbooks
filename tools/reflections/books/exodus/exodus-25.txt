# Exodus 25: Offerings for the sanctuary; the ark, table and lampstand

== 25:1
T: God speaks of His dwelling
R: 'And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying.' On the mountain, God began to give Moses instructions for the tabernacle. The next several chapters describe in detail the place where God would dwell among His people. This was a new phase of the covenant. God had rescued Israel and given them His law. Now He would come to live among them. The God of Sinai would not stay on the mountain. He would travel with His people. This shows God's desire for closeness. He did not want to be distant. He wanted a dwelling in the middle of the camp. John 1 says, the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us. The word dwelt literally means tabernacled. Jesus is the fulfillment of the tabernacle. Today, rejoice that God desires to dwell with His people. He has come near in Christ, and He dwells in you by His Spirit.
S: On the mountain, the LORD began to give Moses instructions for the tabernacle where He would dwell among Israel. God desired closeness, not distance, which is fulfilled in Christ who tabernacled among us. Rejoice that God desires to dwell with you through Christ and His Spirit.
P: God wants to dwell with you. Exodus 25:1: 'And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying.' The tabernacle instructions begin. In Jesus, God came to dwell among us.

== 25:2
T: Willingly with his heart
R: 'Speak unto the children of Israel, that they bring me an offering: of every man that giveth it willingly with his heart ye shall take my offering.' God asked for an offering to build the tabernacle. But He accepted only what was given willingly from the heart. No one was forced to give. Generosity had to come freely. God does not want reluctant gifts. He values the heart behind the offering more than the amount. Second Corinthians 9 says, Every man according as he purposeth in his heart, so let him give; not grudgingly, or of necessity: for God loveth a cheerful giver. Willing giving reflects gratitude. Israel had been rescued, fed, and protected. Their gifts could express thanks for all God had done. Exodus 35 later records that the people gave so generously that Moses had to tell them to stop. When hearts are moved, generosity overflows. Today, give to God willingly. Let your offerings flow from a grateful heart, not from obligation.
S: God asked Israel for an offering for the sanctuary, accepting only what each person gave willingly with his heart. God values the willing heart behind a gift more than its size. Give to God willingly from a grateful heart, not from obligation.
P: A willing heart. Exodus 25:2: 'of every man that giveth it willingly with his heart ye shall take my offering.' God loves a cheerful giver. Give gratefully today.

== 25:3
T: Gold, silver, and brass
R: 'And this is the offering which ye shall take of them; gold, and silver, and brass.' God listed the materials needed: gold, silver, and bronze. These were precious metals, much of it likely from the Egyptians. Wealth received at the exodus would now build God's dwelling. God redeems resources. The gold that once belonged to Egypt would now glorify God. What had been the spoil of oppressors became material for worship. Each metal had a place. Gold was used closest to God's presence. Silver formed the foundations. Bronze was used in the outer court. Every material had value and purpose. In God's house, there are different roles, but all contribute. First Corinthians 12 says, the members should have the same care one for another. Today, consider what resources God has placed in your hands. Whether they seem like gold or bronze, offer them for His purposes. Everything can be redeemed for His glory.
S: God listed gold, silver and brass as offerings for the sanctuary, much of it likely from Egypt's spoil. Resources once held by oppressors were redeemed for worship, each with its own place. Offer the resources God has given you, whatever their value, for His purposes.
P: Redeemed resources. Exodus 25:3: 'gold, and silver, and brass.' Egypt's spoil built God's house. Offer what's in your hands for His glory today.

== 25:4
T: Blue, purple, and scarlet
R: 'And blue, and purple, and scarlet, and fine linen, and goats hair.' God asked for blue, purple, and scarlet yarn, fine linen, and goat hair. These would become the curtains and garments of the tabernacle. God's dwelling would be beautiful. Each color carried meaning for many readers. Blue suggested heaven. Purple was the color of royalty. Scarlet recalled blood and sacrifice. Together they formed a rich picture. Fine linen represented purity and righteousness. Revelation 19 says fine linen is the righteousness of saints. Goat hair was humbler material, used for outer coverings. Notice that both costly and common materials were used. God's house included the fine and the plain. Both had their place. God values beauty. He did not design a drab tent but a place of color and craftsmanship. Psalm 96 says, worship the LORD in the beauty of holiness. Today, appreciate beauty as God's gift. And remember that both fine and humble offerings have a place in His house.
S: God asked for blue, purple and scarlet, fine linen and goats' hair for the tabernacle. His dwelling was to be beautiful, using both costly and humble materials. Appreciate beauty as God's gift and know that both fine and humble offerings have a place in His house.
P: Beauty in God's house. Exodus 25:4: 'blue, and purple, and scarlet, and fine linen.' God values beauty, and every offering has a place. Worship in the beauty of holiness.

== 25:5
T: Ordinary materials
R: 'And rams' skins dyed red, and badgers' skins, and shittim wood.' God requested rams' skins dyed red, durable leather coverings, and acacia wood. These were practical materials. They would form the frames and outer coverings of the tabernacle. Not everything in God's house glittered. The acacia wood, called shittim wood, grew in the desert. It was hard and durable, resistant to decay. Much of the tabernacle's structure was made from it, then overlaid with gold. Many see in this a picture of Christ: true humanity, like desert wood, covered with divine glory. The outer coverings were plain. From the outside, the tabernacle looked ordinary. The beauty was inside. Isaiah 53 says the Messiah had no form nor comeliness. His glory was hidden beneath a humble exterior. Today, do not judge by outward appearance. Ordinary materials in God's hands become part of His dwelling. Value the humble gifts and the hidden glory.
S: God requested rams' skins dyed red, badgers' skins and shittim wood for the tabernacle. Durable, ordinary materials formed its structure and plain outer coverings, while its beauty was hidden inside. Do not judge by outward appearance, for God uses ordinary things in His dwelling.
P: Hidden glory. Exodus 25:5: 'rams' skins dyed red, and badgers' skins, and shittim wood.' Plain outside, glorious inside. Don't judge by appearances. God uses the ordinary.

== 25:6
T: Oil, spices, and incense
R: 'Oil for the light, spices for anointing oil, and for sweet incense.' God asked for oil for the lamps, spices for the anointing oil, and fragrant incense. These would provide light, consecration, and a sweet aroma in God's house. Each element has spiritual meaning. Oil for light kept the lampstand burning. Oil in Scripture often represents the Holy Spirit. He gives light to God's people. Zechariah 4 links oil with God's Spirit. Anointing oil set apart priests and objects for holy use. Today, believers are anointed by the Spirit. First John 2 says, ye have an unction from the Holy One. Incense represented prayer. Psalm 141 says, Let my prayer be set forth before thee as incense. Revelation 8 describes the prayers of saints rising like incense. Today, ask God to fill you with the oil of His Spirit. Let your life give light, be set apart for Him, and rise to Him as a sweet aroma of prayer.
S: God asked for oil for the light, spices for anointing oil and sweet incense. These pictured the Spirit's light, consecration for holy use and prayer rising to God. Ask God to fill you with His Spirit and let your prayers rise to Him as incense.

== 25:7
T: Precious stones
R: 'Onyx stones, and stones to be set in the ephod, and in the breastplate.' God asked for onyx and other precious stones for the high priest's ephod and breastpiece. These stones would bear the names of Israel's tribes. The high priest would carry the people on his shoulders and heart. The stones were not mere decoration. Exodus 28 explains that the tribes' names were engraved on them. When the high priest entered God's presence, he carried the people with him. They were precious to God, like jewels. This beautifully pictures Christ, our High Priest. He carries us before the Father. Hebrews 7 says he ever liveth to make intercession for them. We are precious to Him. Malachi 3 says, they shall be mine… in that day when I make up my jewels. God sees His people as treasured jewels. Today, rejoice that Jesus carries your name before God. You are precious to Him, like a jewel set on His heart.

== 25:8
T: That I may dwell among them
R: 'And let them make me a sanctuary; that I may dwell among them.' This is the heart of the tabernacle. God said, let them make Me a sanctuary so that I may dwell among them. The purpose of everything was God's presence with His people. Since Eden, humanity had been separated from God's presence. Now God was coming back to live among His people. The tabernacle was a portable Eden, a place where God and people could meet. This theme runs through all of Scripture. The tabernacle led to the temple. The temple pointed to Christ, God with us. Now believers are God's temple. First Corinthians 3 says, ye are the temple of God, and… the Spirit of God dwelleth in you. And Revelation 21 promises, the tabernacle of God is with men, and he will dwell with them. Today, rejoice that God wants to dwell with you. Make room in your heart and life for His presence.
S: God said, Let them make me a sanctuary, that I may dwell among them. The tabernacle's purpose was God's presence with His people, a theme fulfilled in Christ, in believers and in the new creation. Rejoice that God wants to dwell with you and make room for His presence.
P: God with us. Exodus 25:8: 'let them make me a sanctuary; that I may dwell among them.' God's desire is to dwell with you. Make room for His presence today.

== 25:9
T: After the pattern
R: 'According to all that I shew thee, after the pattern of the tabernacle, and the pattern of all the instruments thereof, even so shall ye make it.' God told Moses to build everything exactly according to the pattern He would show him. The tabernacle was not to be designed by human creativity. It was to follow God's blueprint. Hebrews 8 says the tabernacle was the example and shadow of heavenly things. Moses saw a heavenly reality and built an earthly copy. The details mattered because they reflected something real. This teaches that worship must follow God's pattern. We do not invent our own way to God. He shows us the way. John 14 records Jesus saying, I am the way, the truth, and the life: no man cometh unto the Father, but by me. Notice the phrase according to all. Not most, but all. Obedience to God's design was complete. Today, follow God's pattern in your worship and life. Let His word, not human preference, shape how you approach Him.
S: God told Moses to make the tabernacle and its instruments exactly after the pattern He would show him. The earthly tabernacle copied a heavenly reality, and worship had to follow God's design. Follow God's pattern in your worship and life rather than human preference.
P: God's pattern. Exodus 25:9: 'after the pattern of the tabernacle… even so shall ye make it.' We don't invent our way to God. Follow His design today.

== 25:10
T: The ark
R: 'And they shall make an ark of shittim wood.' The first item God described was the ark, a chest of acacia wood about four feet long and over two feet wide and high. It would be the most sacred object in the tabernacle. God began from the inside out. Before describing walls or curtains, He described the ark, which would sit in the Most Holy Place. The center mattered most. The ark would hold God's covenant and serve as His throne on earth. Notice that it was relatively small. God's presence did not require a massive structure. A simple chest became the meeting place between heaven and earth. Many see the ark as a picture of Christ, in whom God and humanity meet. Colossians 2 says, in him dwelleth all the fulness of the Godhead bodily. Today, put first things first. Let God's presence be at the center of your life, and build everything else around Him.
S: God told Moses to make an ark of shittim wood about two and a half cubits long and a cubit and a half wide and high. He began from the center, with the most sacred object where heaven and earth would meet. Let God's presence be at the center of your life and build everything around Him.
P: Start at the center. Exodus 25:10: 'they shall make an ark of shittim wood.' God began with the heart of the tabernacle. Put Him at the center of your life today.

The ark was to be covered with pure gold, inside and outside. A gold molding would run around it. The wooden chest would be completely clothed in gold. Notice within and without. The ark was not gold only on the outside, like a disguise. It was gold through and through. Inner and outer matched. This speaks of integrity. God desires purity not only in public but in private. Many people present a polished exterior while hiding a different interior. Jesus warned the Pharisees in Matthew 23 that they cleaned the outside of the cup while the inside was full of extortion. God wants consistency. Psalm 51 says, thou desirest truth in the inward parts. The crown of gold suggests royalty. The ark was God's throne. Today, let your inner life match your outer life. Pursue integrity that is golden within and without.

== 25:12
T: Four rings
R: 'And thou shalt cast four rings of gold for it, and put them in the four corners thereof; and two rings shall be in the one side of it, and two rings in the other side of it.' Four gold rings were to be attached to the ark's corners, two on each side. These rings would hold poles for carrying the ark. The ark was designed to travel. God's presence was not fixed to one place. The ark would move with Israel through the wilderness. Wherever they went, God's throne went with them. God is not confined to buildings. He goes with His people. Matthew 28 says, lo, I am with you alway. Notice also that the rings were part of the ark's design. Mobility was built in from the beginning. He knew His people would not stay in one place. Hebrews 13 says, here have we no continuing city. Today, rejoice that God travels with you. Wherever life takes you, His presence goes along.

== 25:13
T: Staves of acacia and gold
R: 'And thou shalt make staves of shittim wood, and overlay them with gold.' Poles of acacia wood, overlaid with gold, were made for carrying the ark. Even the carrying poles matched the ark in material and beauty. Every part of the ark was holy. The poles might seem like a minor detail. But they were made with the same care as the ark itself. Nothing connected to God's presence was treated carelessly. Every detail reflected His holiness. The poles also had a practical purpose. Later, in Second Samuel 6, David tried to move the ark on a cart instead of with poles. Uzzah touched the ark and died. God's instructions about carrying His presence mattered. Following God's way is not legalism; it is reverence. When we ignore His instructions, even with good intentions, we risk serious consequences. Today, handle holy things with care. Pay attention to God's instructions, even in details that seem small.

A life ready for God's leading does not need long preparation to respond. Notice also that the poles prevented anyone from needing to touch the ark directly. They provided a safe way to carry holy things. God's instructions protect us. Today, keep yourself ready to follow God. Do not become so settled that you cannot move when He calls. == 25:17
T: The mercy seat
R: 'And thou shalt make a mercy seat of pure gold: two cubits and a half shall be the length thereof, and a cubit and a half the breadth thereof.' God commanded a mercy seat of pure gold to cover the ark. It matched the ark's length and width. This lid would be the most important place in the entire tabernacle. The mercy seat sat over the law. The law inside the ark testified against Israel's sins. But the mercy seat covered it. On the Day of Atonement, blood would be sprinkled on the mercy seat. Mercy covered judgment. The Greek word for mercy seat is used in Romans 3, where Christ is called a propitiation, a mercy seat, through faith in his blood. Jesus is our mercy seat. His blood covers the law's condemnation. First John 2 says, he is the propitiation for our sins. Notice that it was pure gold, no wood. Mercy is wholly divine. Today, rejoice that mercy covers your sin. Through Christ, God's judgment is covered by His grace.
S: God commanded a mercy seat of pure gold to cover the ark, where blood would later be sprinkled. Mercy covered the law that testified against sin, pointing to Christ our propitiation. Rejoice that through Christ, mercy covers your sin.

== 25:18
T: Two cherubims
R: 'And thou shalt make two cherubims of gold, of beaten work shalt thou make them, in the two ends of the mercy seat.' God commanded two cherubim of hammered gold on each end of the mercy seat. These angelic figures would guard God's throne. They were made of beaten work, shaped by hammering. Cherubim first appear in Genesis 3, guarding the way to the tree of life after Adam and Eve were expelled from Eden. They represented God's holiness barring sinners from His presence. But here, the cherubim guard the mercy seat, where atonement would happen. Now they guard not a closed door but a place of mercy. Notice beaten work. The gold was hammered into shape. Beauty came through pressure. Many see this as a picture of how God shapes His people through trials. Romans 5 says tribulation worketh patience. Today, remember that God's holiness and mercy meet at the mercy seat. And trust that He is shaping you, even through hard blows, into something beautiful.
S: God commanded two cherubims of beaten gold on the ends of the mercy seat. Once guarding Eden's closed way, cherubim now guarded the place of mercy, and their beauty came through hammering. Trust that God's holiness and mercy meet in Christ, and that He shapes you through trials.
P: Shaped by pressure. Exodus 25:18: 'two cherubims of gold, of beaten work.' Beauty came through hammering. God is shaping you through trials into something beautiful.

God commanded a table of acacia wood for the Holy Place. It would hold the bread of the Presence. A table in God's house signified fellowship and provision. Tables in Scripture are places of fellowship. Sharing a meal created relationship. God placed a table in His dwelling, signaling His desire to share life with His people. Believers gather around it to remember Christ and enjoy fellowship. Revelation 3 records Jesus saying, I will come in to him, and will sup with him. Today, accept God's invitation to His table. == 25:24
T: Crowned with gold
R: 'And thou shalt overlay it with pure gold, and make thereto a crown of gold round about.' The table was overlaid with pure gold and given a gold molding around it. Like the ark, it combined acacia wood with gold. It was both humble and glorious. The pattern repeats. Wood overlaid with gold appears throughout the tabernacle. Many see this as a picture of Christ's two natures: true humanity and true divinity. He was fully human, like the wood. He was fully God, like the gold. Philippians 2 describes Christ as being in the form of God, yet made in the likeness of men. The crown of gold suggests royalty. The table belonged to a King. God's provision comes from a royal source. He is not a poor host but the King of kings. When He provides, He provides richly. Philippians 4 says, my God shall supply all your need according to his riches in glory. Today, trust that you dine at a King's table. His provision is rich and abundant.

Notice the double crown, on the table and on the border. God's house was crafted with care in every detail. Today, trust that God keeps what is His secure. == 25:33
