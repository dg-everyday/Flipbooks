# Exodus 27: The altar, the court and the oil for the lamp

== 27:1
T: The altar foursquare
R: 'And thou shalt make an altar of shittim wood, five cubits long, and five cubits broad; the altar shall be foursquare: and the height thereof shall be three cubits.' God commanded an altar of acacia wood, square in shape, about seven and a half feet on each side and four and a half feet high. This would be the altar of burnt offering in the courtyard. Before anyone could approach God's presence, they encountered the altar. Sacrifice came first. The altar stood between the entrance and the tabernacle. This pictures the cross. Jesus' sacrifice is the first thing we must encounter on the way to God. Hebrews 13 says, We have an altar. Christ's cross is our altar. Notice foursquare. The altar was equal on all sides. Some see this as a picture of the gospel reaching to all four corners of the earth. Today, remember that the way to God begins at the cross. There is no other entrance.
S: God commanded a foursquare altar of shittim wood, five cubits long and broad and three cubits high. It was the first thing met on entering the court, showing that sacrifice came before access to God. Remember that the way to God begins at the cross.
P: Sacrifice first. Exodus 27:1: 'thou shalt make an altar of shittim wood… the altar shall be foursquare.' The altar came first. The way to God begins at the cross.

== 27:2
T: The horns of the altar
R: 'And thou shalt make the horns of it upon the four corners thereof: his horns shall be of the same: and thou shalt overlay it with brass.' The altar had horns on its four corners, made from the same piece. It was overlaid with bronze. The horns would become a place of refuge and atonement. Horns in Scripture represent strength and power. On the altar, they were also places where blood was applied for atonement, as Leviticus 4 describes. Strength and sacrifice met at the altar's corners. People sometimes fled to the altar and grasped its horns for protection. First Kings 1 records Adonijah doing this. The altar became a place of mercy for those seeking refuge. Psalm 118 says, bind the sacrifice with cords, even unto the horns of the altar. Christ is the horn of salvation. Luke 1 says God hath raised up an horn of salvation for us. Today, run to the cross as your place of refuge. Grasp it, and find mercy and strength there.
S: The altar had horns on its four corners, of one piece with it, and was overlaid with brass. The horns represented strength and became places of atonement and refuge. Run to the cross as your refuge and find mercy and strength there.
P: The horns of the altar. Exodus 27:2: 'thou shalt make the horns of it upon the four corners thereof.' A place of strength and refuge. Run to the cross and find mercy today.

== 27:3
T: Vessels of brass
R: 'And thou shalt make his pans to receive his ashes, and his shovels, and his basons, and his fleshhooks, and his firepans: all the vessels thereof thou shalt make of brass.' The altar's tools were all made of bronze: pans for ashes, shovels, basins, meat forks, and firepans. These were practical tools for the work of sacrifice. They were used daily. Sacrifice was messy, hard work. Ashes had to be removed. Blood was collected. Meat was handled. The tools reflected the reality of what happened at the altar. Bronze in the tabernacle is associated with judgment and the outer court. The altar was where sin was judged through sacrifice. Its tools were made of the same material. Notice the pans to receive his ashes. After the fire, ashes remained. Even the remains of sacrifice were handled with care. Leviticus 6 says the ashes were carried to a clean place. Today, remember that Christ's sacrifice was real and costly. And handle the things of God with care, even the humble tasks.
S: The altar's pans for ashes, shovels, basins, fleshhooks and firepans were all made of brass. They were practical tools for the real and costly work of sacrifice. Remember the cost of Christ's sacrifice and handle even humble tasks of God with care.
P: Humble tools, holy work. Exodus 27:3: 'all the vessels thereof thou shalt make of brass.' Sacrifice was real and costly. Handle even humble tasks for God with care today.

== 27:4
T: A grate of network
R: 'And thou shalt make for it a grate of network of brass; and upon the net shalt thou make four brasen rings in the four corners thereof.' A bronze grate, like a net, was made for the altar, with four bronze rings at its corners. The grate held the burning sacrifice. Air could flow through it, keeping the fire hot. The grate was where the sacrifice rested in the fire. It bore the full heat of the burning offering. It held what was being consumed. This pictures Christ bearing the fire of judgment. Isaiah 53 says, it pleased the LORD to bruise him. He took the full heat of God's wrath against sin. The fire that should have consumed us consumed Him instead. Notice the network design. It allowed the fire to breathe. The sacrifice was fully consumed. Christ's sacrifice was complete. Nothing was held back. Today, thank Jesus for bearing the fire you deserved. He took the full heat of judgment so that you could be free.
S: A grate of brass network with four rings at its corners was made for the altar. It held the sacrifice in the full heat of the fire, picturing Christ bearing judgment completely. Thank Jesus for bearing the fire you deserved so that you could be free.

== 27:6
T: Staves for the altar
R: 'And thou shalt make staves for the altar, staves of shittim wood, and overlay them with brass.' Poles of acacia wood overlaid with bronze were made for carrying the altar. Like the ark and table, the altar was portable. It would travel with Israel. The altar of sacrifice went wherever the people went. Every time they camped, the altar was set up. Sacrifice was not left behind. It accompanied them through the wilderness. Our need for Christ's sacrifice goes with us everywhere. We never outgrow the cross. Wherever we are in our journey, we depend on His sacrifice. Galatians 6 says, God forbid that I should glory, save in the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ. Notice that the altar's poles were bronze, not gold. They matched the altar. Each part of the tabernacle had consistent materials. Today, carry the message of the cross wherever you go. Never leave it behind. Every stage of your journey depends on Christ's sacrifice.

The poles were put through the rings on both sides of the altar so it could be carried. The Levites would bear the altar on their shoulders. The place of sacrifice was carried by people. Bearing the altar was a physical burden. It required strength and cooperation. The Levites shared the load. The altar of sacrifice was carried through teamwork. Following Christ involves bearing His cross. It is not always easy. Yet we do not carry it alone. Galatians 6 says, Bear ye one another's burdens, and so fulfil the law of Christ. == 27:8
T: Hollow with boards
R: 'Hollow with boards shalt thou make it: as it was shewed thee in the mount, so shall they make it.' The altar was to be hollow, made of boards, as God had shown Moses on the mountain. Once again, God emphasized following His pattern. The altar was hollow, perhaps to be filled with earth or stones when it was set up. Exodus 20 had commanded altars of earth. The hollow frame could be filled at each campsite, connecting the altar to the ground wherever they were. Notice the repeated phrase as it was shewed thee in the mount. Moses had to follow exactly what God showed him. The altar of sacrifice especially had to be built God's way. The way of salvation is not something we design. God has shown us the way. Acts 4 says, there is none other name under heaven given among men, whereby we must be saved. Today, approach God the way He has shown, through Christ alone. Do not invent your own way.

== 27:9
T: The court
R: 'And thou shalt make the court of the tabernacle: for the south side southward there shall be hangings for the court of fine twined linen of an hundred cubits long for one side.' God described the courtyard around the tabernacle. Its south side would have curtains of fine linen, one hundred cubits long, about one hundred fifty feet. The court enclosed the sacred space. The courtyard created a boundary. Inside was holy ground; outside was the camp. The white linen walls separated God's dwelling from ordinary life. Fine linen in Scripture represents righteousness. Revelation 19 says, the fine linen is the righteousness of saints. The court was surrounded by righteousness. To enter, one had to come through the gate. Holiness marked the boundary. Yet the linen walls were only about seven and a half feet high. They did not hide the tabernacle completely. People could see the cloud of God's presence above it. Today, honor the boundaries that keep God's things holy. And rejoice that through Christ's righteousness, you are welcomed inside.
S: God told Moses to make a court of fine twined linen around the tabernacle, one hundred cubits long on the south side. The white linen walls marked a boundary of holiness and righteousness around God's dwelling. Honor what is holy and rejoice that through Christ's righteousness you are welcomed inside.
P: Surrounded by righteousness. Exodus 27:9: 'hangings for the court of fine twined linen.' Holiness marked the boundary. Through Christ's righteousness, you're welcomed in.

== 27:10
T: Silver hooks and fillets
R: 'And the twenty pillars thereof and their twenty sockets shall be of brass; the hooks of the pillars and their fillets shall be of silver.' The court's south side had twenty pillars standing in bronze sockets. But their hooks and bands were silver. Bronze below, silver above. The court combined two metals. The foundations were bronze, recalling judgment and the altar. The tops were silver, recalling redemption. The pillars stood on judgment but were crowned with redemption. This pictures the gospel. Christ bore judgment for sin, and from that comes redemption. Romans 3 says we are justified freely by his grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus. Judgment and redemption are both part of salvation. Notice that silver held the curtains. What hung around God's court was supported by redemption. Everything was held up by the price of ransom. Today, remember that your life is held up by redemption. Christ bore judgment, and now His redemption crowns your life.

The west side of the court was fifty cubits wide with ten pillars and ten sockets. This was the back of the court, behind the tabernacle. The west side was the farthest from the entrance. This suggests hope and new beginnings. In Eden, Genesis 3 says, God placed cherubim at the east of the garden. Humanity had been driven out eastward. Christ is the dawn that brings us back to God. Luke 1 speaks of the dayspring from on high. Today, face toward God's dawn. He invites you back into His presence.
