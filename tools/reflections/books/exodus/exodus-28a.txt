# Exodus 28:1-22: Garments for the priesthood

== 28:1
T: Called to minister
R: 'And take thou unto thee Aaron thy brother, and his sons with him, from among the children of Israel, that he may minister unto me in the priest's office.' God chose Aaron and his sons to serve as priests. They were taken from among the people to minister to God on the people's behalf. Priesthood began with God's call. Aaron did not appoint himself. God chose him. Hebrews 5 says, no man taketh this honour unto himself, but he that is called of God, as was Aaron. Ministry begins with God's initiative. Notice from among the children of Israel. The priests were ordinary Israelites, set apart for a special task. They shared the people's weaknesses. Hebrews 5 says the high priest can have compassion on the ignorant, for that he himself also is compassed with infirmity. Christ is our great High Priest, chosen by God. And First Peter 2 calls all believers a royal priesthood. Today, recognize that God calls His people to serve Him. Respond to His call with humility and gratitude.
S: God told Moses to take Aaron and his sons from among Israel to minister to Him in the priest's office. Priesthood began with God's call, and the priests were ordinary people set apart. Recognize that God calls His people to serve and respond with humility and gratitude.
P: Called by God. Exodus 28:1: 'that he may minister unto me in the priest's office.' Aaron didn't appoint himself. God calls His people to serve. Respond humbly today.

== 28:2
T: For glory and for beauty
R: 'And thou shalt make holy garments for Aaron thy brother for glory and for beauty.' God commanded holy garments for Aaron, made for glory and for beauty. The priest's clothing was to reflect the honor of his office and the beauty of God's service. Notice the purpose: glory and beauty. God cares about beauty. Worship was not meant to be drab. The priest's appearance honored the God he served. These garments also pictured spiritual realities. Isaiah 61 speaks of the garments of salvation and the robe of righteousness. God clothes His people with beauty. Notice the word holy. The garments were set apart. They were not ordinary clothes. They were worn only for sacred service. Christ, our High Priest, is clothed in glory. Revelation 1 describes Him clothed with a garment down to the foot. And He clothes us in His righteousness. Today, rejoice that God clothes you with garments of salvation. Wear them with gratitude, and let your life reflect His glory and beauty.
S: God commanded holy garments for Aaron for glory and for beauty. The priest's clothing honored God's service and pictured the beauty with which God clothes His people. Rejoice that God clothes you with garments of salvation and let your life reflect His glory.
P: Clothed in beauty. Exodus 28:2: 'holy garments for Aaron thy brother for glory and for beauty.' God clothes His people with salvation. Reflect His glory today.

== 28:3
T: Wise hearted
R: 'And thou shalt speak unto all that are wise hearted, whom I have filled with the spirit of wisdom, that they may make Aaron's garments to consecrate him.' God told Moses to speak to the skilled craftsmen whom He had filled with wisdom. They would make Aaron's garments. Their skill came from God's Spirit. Notice whom I have filled with the spirit of wisdom. Artistic and practical skill is a gift from God. Craftsmanship is not merely human talent; it is spiritual endowment. This is the first time Scripture speaks of the Spirit filling people for a task. And it is for artistic work. God values creativity and skill. Those who sew, build, design, and create can serve God with their gifts. Colossians 3 says, whatsoever ye do, do it heartily, as to the Lord. Notice also wise hearted. Wisdom begins in the heart. Skill flows from a heart shaped by God. Today, recognize that your skills are gifts from God. Use them for His glory, whatever they are.
S: God told Moses to speak to the wise hearted craftsmen He had filled with the spirit of wisdom to make Aaron's garments. Skill and creativity were gifts of God's Spirit. Recognize your skills as gifts from God and use them for His glory.
P: Skills are gifts. Exodus 28:3: 'whom I have filled with the spirit of wisdom.' God fills people with creativity and skill. Use yours for His glory today.

== 28:4
T: The priestly garments
R: 'And these are the garments which they shall make; a breastplate, and an ephod, and a robe, and a broidered coat, a mitre, and a girdle.' God listed the garments: a breastpiece, an ephod, a robe, a woven tunic, a turban, and a sash. Each piece had a purpose. Together they formed the complete attire of the high priest. Every piece mattered. The priest could not serve with only some of the garments. He needed the full set. Ephesians 6 describes the whole armour of God for believers. We need every piece: truth, righteousness, the gospel, faith, salvation, and the Word. Notice that the garments were made for Aaron and his sons. The calling extended to his family. Ministry often touches the whole household. The garments also pictured Christ, our perfect High Priest. Every aspect of His priestly work was complete. Hebrews 7 says He is able to save them to the uttermost. Today, put on all that God provides. Do not leave any part of His spiritual covering behind.
S: God listed the priestly garments: a breastplate, an ephod, a robe, a broidered coat, a mitre and a girdle. Every piece mattered, and the priest needed the full set to serve. Put on all that God provides and leave no part of His covering behind.

== 28:5
T: The same materials
R: 'And they shall take gold, and blue, and purple, and scarlet, and fine linen.' The craftsmen would use gold, blue, purple, scarlet, and fine linen for the garments. These were the same materials used in the tabernacle's curtains and veil. The priest's clothing matched God's dwelling. The priest was dressed in the colors of the tabernacle. He belonged to God's house. His appearance showed that he served in God's presence. Those who serve God reflect His house. As believers, we carry the marks of the One we belong to. Second Corinthians 3 says we are changed into the same image. Our lives should reflect the character of the God we serve. Gold represented divinity, blue heaven, purple royalty, scarlet sacrifice, and linen purity. The priest wore them all. Christ embodies them perfectly: divine, heavenly, royal, sacrificial, and pure. Today, let your life reflect the God you serve. Wear His character as the priest wore the colors of His house.

== 28:6
T: The ephod
R: 'And they shall make the ephod of gold, of blue, and of purple, of scarlet, and fine twined linen, with cunning work.' The ephod was a vest-like garment made of gold thread and colored yarns, skillfully woven. It was worn over the robe and held the breastpiece. It was central to the high priest's attire. Gold thread was woven into the fabric. Exodus 39 says they beat the gold into thin plates and cut it into wires to work it in. Gold became part of the cloth itself. Divine glory was woven into the priest's clothing. This pictures what God does in His people. He weaves His glory into our lives. Not just on the surface, but into the fabric of who we are. Second Peter 1 says we are made partakers of the divine nature. Notice the phrase cunning work, meaning skilled work. Excellence marked God's service. Nothing was careless. Today, let God weave His character into the fabric of your life. And offer Him excellence in all you do.
S: The ephod was made of gold, blue, purple, scarlet and fine twined linen with skilled work. Gold thread was woven into the fabric itself, picturing God weaving His glory into His people. Let God weave His character into your life and offer Him excellence.
P: Woven with glory. Exodus 28:6: 'the ephod of gold, of blue, and of purple… with cunning work.' Gold was woven into the fabric. Let God weave His character into you.

Isaiah 9 says of the Messiah, the government shall be upon his shoulder. Christ carries responsibility for His people. Luke 15 describes the shepherd who finds the lost sheep and layeth it on his shoulders, rejoicing. Jesus carries us. Today, let Christ carry you on His shoulders. And let your heart be whole and undivided as you serve and carry others.

A girdle or belt in Scripture represents readiness for service. Luke 12 says, Let your loins be girded about. The priest was ready to serve.
