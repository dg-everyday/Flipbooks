# Exodus 28:23-43: The breastplate fastened; robe, plate and linen garments

== 28:23
T: Two rings of gold
R: 'And thou shalt make upon the breastplate two rings of gold, and shalt put the two rings on the two ends of the breastplate.' Two gold rings were attached to the upper corners of the breastpiece. These rings would receive the gold chains that connected it to the shoulder pieces. Every connection was carefully planned. The rings were small, but they mattered. Without them, the chains had nothing to hold. The breastpiece would hang loose. Small connections create stability. Our relationship with God also has connecting points. Prayer, worship, Scripture, and obedience are like rings that hold us close to Him. Neglecting them weakens the bond. James 4 says, Draw nigh to God, and he will draw nigh to you. Notice again the gold. Even the rings were precious. The places where we connect with God are holy. Today, strengthen the small connecting points of your faith. Daily prayer, time in God's word, and simple obedience keep you securely connected to Him.
S: Two gold rings were put on the upper ends of the breastplate to receive the chains. Small connections kept the breastplate secure. Strengthen the small connecting points of your faith, like prayer, Scripture and obedience.
P: Small connections matter. Exodus 28:23: 'thou shalt make upon the breastplate two rings of gold.' Daily prayer and Scripture keep you connected to God. Strengthen them today.

== 28:24
T: Chains in the rings
R: 'And thou shalt put the two wreathen chains of gold in the two rings which are on the ends of the breastplate.' The braided gold chains were placed in the rings at the corners of the breastpiece. Now the breastpiece could be fastened to the shoulders. The connection was taking shape. Step by step, each part was joined. Chains in rings, rings on the breastpiece. Nothing was left disconnected. God's work in our lives is also step by step. He connects one thing to another. Philippians 1 says, he which hath begun a good work in you will perform it until the day of Jesus Christ. Growth happens piece by piece. Notice that the chains were braided. Multiple strands made them strong. Our faith grows stronger when it is woven with others' faith, with Scripture, and with experience. Single strands break. Braided cords hold. Today, trust God's step-by-step work in your life. Each connection He makes strengthens you. Be patient with the process.
S: The two braided chains of gold were put in the two rings on the ends of the breastplate. Step by step, each part was joined until nothing was left disconnected. Trust God's step-by-step work in your life and be patient with the process.
P: Step by step. Exodus 28:24: 'put the two wreathen chains of gold in the two rings.' God works piece by piece. Trust His process in your life today.

== 28:25
T: Fastened to the shoulders
R: 'And the other two ends of the two wreathen chains thou shalt fasten in the two ouches, and put them on the shoulderpieces of the ephod before it.' The other ends of the chains were fastened to the gold settings on the shoulder pieces. The breastpiece now hung from the shoulders. Heart and shoulders were connected. This connection unified the priest's attire. The names on his shoulders and the names on his heart were linked. Strength and love were bound together. When Christ carries us, He carries us with both strength and love. He does not merely bear our weight; He holds us close to His heart. Isaiah 40 says, He shall feed his flock like a shepherd: he shall gather the lambs with his arm, and carry them in his bosom. Notice before it, meaning in front. The breastpiece was visible. The priest's love for the people was not hidden. Today, rejoice that Christ carries you with both strength and love. He holds you on His shoulders and close to His heart.
S: The other ends of the braided chains were fastened to the settings on the ephod's shoulderpieces. The breastplate now hung from the shoulders, joining strength and love. Rejoice that Christ carries you with both strength and love, on His shoulders and near His heart.

Two more gold rings were attached to the lower corners of the breastpiece, on the inside edge facing the ephod. These would secure the bottom of the breastpiece. Not only the top but the bottom had to be fastened. It needed to be held at the bottom too. Stability required connection at every point. Our faith also needs to be secure at every level. Ephesians 3 prays that we may be rooted and grounded in love. Notice the word inward. These rings were on the inner side, hidden from view. Hidden connections matter. The private life of faith holds the public life steady. Matthew 6 says, thy Father which seeth in secret shall reward thee openly. Today, tend to your hidden spiritual life. What is unseen holds what is seen in place.

== 28:27
T: Above the girdle
R: 'And two other rings of gold thou shalt make, and shalt put them on the two sides of the ephod underneath, toward the forepart thereof, over against the other coupling thereof, above the curious girdle of the ephod.' Two more gold rings were placed on the front of the ephod, just above the sash. These would align with the lower rings of the breastpiece. Precise placement was required. Notice the detail: over against the other coupling. The rings had to line up exactly. Alignment mattered. Our lives need to be aligned with God's will. When we are out of alignment, things do not fit. Proverbs 3 says, In all thy ways acknowledge him, and he shall direct thy paths. Alignment with God brings order. Notice also above the curious girdle. The breastpiece rested just above the belt of service. Love and service were positioned together. Today, examine whether your life is aligned with God. Make adjustments where needed so that your heart and service fit His design.
S: Two more gold rings were placed on the front of the ephod, aligned with the breastplate's rings just above the girdle. Precise alignment was needed for everything to fit. Examine whether your life is aligned with God and adjust so that your heart and service fit His design.

== 28:28
T: Not loosed
R: 'And they shall bind the breastplate by the rings thereof unto the rings of the ephod with a lace of blue.' The breastpiece was tied to the ephod with a blue cord so that it would not come loose. It would stay firmly in place over the priest's heart. Notice the purpose: that the breastplate be not loosed. God made sure the names on the priest's heart could not fall away. His people were bound securely. The cord was blue, the color of heaven. Heaven itself held the breastpiece in place. What binds us to God is from above. Our security rests not on our grip but on His. John 6 records Jesus saying, him that cometh to me I will in no wise cast out. Second Timothy 2 says, If we believe not, yet he abideth faithful. God's hold on us is secure. Today, rest in the truth that you will not be loosed from God's heart. He binds you to Himself with heavenly cords.
S: The breastplate was bound to the ephod with a lace of blue so that it would not be loosed. Israel's names were held securely over the priest's heart by a heavenly cord. Rest in the truth that you will not be loosed from God's heart.
P: Never loosed. Exodus 28:28: 'that the breastplate be not loosed from the ephod.' A heavenly cord held it fast. God holds you securely. He will never let you go.

== 28:29
T: Upon his heart
R: 'And Aaron shall bear the names of the children of Israel in the breastplate of judgment upon his heart, when he goeth in unto the holy place, for a memorial before the LORD continually.' Aaron would carry Israel's names on his heart whenever he entered the Holy Place. They would be a continual memorial before the LORD. The people were carried both on his shoulders and over his heart. Shoulders speak of strength; the heart speaks of love. Aaron bore the people with both. This is the heart of priestly ministry: carrying people before God with love. Christ, our High Priest, does this perfectly. Hebrews 4 says we have an high priest which can be touched with the feeling of our infirmities. He carries us with compassion. Notice continually. Not occasionally, but always. Every time Aaron entered God's presence, Israel came with him. Today, rest in the truth that Jesus carries you on His heart continually. And let your prayers carry others before God with love.

== 28:30
T: Urim and Thummim
R: 'And thou shalt put in the breastplate of judgment the Urim and the Thummim; and they shall be upon Aaron's heart, when he goeth in before the LORD.' The Urim and Thummim were placed in the breastpiece. These mysterious objects were used to seek God's decisions. They rested on Aaron's heart as he came before the LORD. We do not know exactly what the Urim and Thummim were. The names mean lights and perfections. They were used to discern God's will in difficult matters. Notice where they were kept: on the priest's heart. Seeking God's guidance was connected to the heart. God reveals His will to those who seek Him with their whole heart. Today, we do not use Urim and Thummim. We have God's word and Spirit. Psalm 119 says, Thy word is a lamp unto my feet. John 16 says the Spirit will guide you into all truth. Today, seek God's guidance through His word and Spirit. Bring your decisions to Him with a sincere heart.

== 28:32
T: That it be not rent
R: 'And there shall be an hole in the top of it, in the midst thereof: it shall have a binding of woven work round about the hole of it, as it were the hole of an habergeon, that it be not rent.' The robe had an opening for the head, reinforced with a woven collar like a coat of armor, so it would not tear. This detail becomes significant in the Gospels. At Jesus' trial, Matthew 26 says the high priest rent his clothes, violating this command. And John 19 records that Jesus' own coat was without seam, woven from the top throughout. Jesus, the true High Priest, wore a seamless garment that was not torn. Notice the reinforcement around the opening. God anticipates where we might tear and provides strength there. Second Corinthians 12 says, my strength is made perfect in weakness. Today, trust God to strengthen your weak points. And rejoice in Christ, the true High Priest whose garment remained whole.
S: The robe had an opening at the top with a woven binding like a coat of armor so that it would not be rent. The high priest's robe was to remain whole, and Jesus' seamless coat was not torn. Trust God to strengthen your weak points and rejoice in Christ, the true High Priest.

== 28:33
T: Pomegranates and bells
R: 'And beneath upon the hem of it thou shalt make pomegranates of blue, and of purple, and of scarlet, round about the hem thereof; and bells of gold between them round about.' Around the hem of the robe were pomegranates of blue, purple, and scarlet yarn, with golden bells between them. Fruit and sound decorated the bottom of the priest's robe. Pomegranates are full of seeds. They represent fruitfulness. Bells represent sound, testimony, and announcement. Together they suggest a life that bears fruit and makes a sound for God. Some see in this the fruit and gifts of the Spirit. Galatians 5 lists the fruit of the Spirit. First Corinthians 12 lists spiritual gifts. Both are needed. Gifts without fruit can be like sounding brass, as First Corinthians 13 warns. Fruit gives gifts their meaning. Notice that they alternated: bell, pomegranate, bell, pomegranate. Balance between testimony and character. Today, let your life bear fruit and make a sound for God. Let your words and your character work together.
S: Around the hem of the robe were pomegranates of blue, purple and scarlet with golden bells between them. Fruit and sound alternated, picturing a life of character and testimony. Let your life bear fruit and make a sound for God, with words and character working together.
P: Fruit and sound. Exodus 28:33: 'pomegranates… and bells of gold between them.' Let your words and your character work together for God today.

== 28:34

Every bell was matched by a pomegranate. Testimony was always paired with fruit. Words were never separated from character. Many Christians are strong in one and weak in the other. And Romans 10 says, how shall they hear without a preacher? Words and works together make a full witness. Notice round about. The pattern continued without interruption. Consistency matters. Our witness should be steady, not occasional. Today, pair your words with your life. Let your testimony be matched by fruit, consistently and all the way around.

The plate was continually on Aaron's forehead. Christ's mediation is continual. Today, rejoice that your imperfect worship is accepted through Christ. He bears what is lacking and presents you holy before God.

In Genesis 3, after sin, Adam and Eve realized their nakedness and tried to cover themselves. God made them garments. Ever since, humanity has needed covering before God. Our sin exposes us. Revelation 3 counsels buying white raiment, that the shame of thy nakedness do not appear. Christ provides that covering. Isaiah 61 says, he hath covered me with the robe of righteousness. Today, thank God that Christ covers your shame. Serve Him with dignity and purity, clothed in His righteousness.

== 28:43
T: That they bear not iniquity
R: 'And they shall be upon Aaron, and upon his sons, when they come in unto the tabernacle of the congregation, or when they come near unto the altar to minister in the holy place; that they bear not iniquity, and die.' The priests had to wear these garments whenever they entered the tabernacle or approached the altar. Otherwise they would incur guilt and die. It was a lasting statute. Approaching God required proper covering. The priests could not come as they pleased. They had to be clothed according to God's instructions. We cannot approach a holy God on our own terms. We must come clothed in what He provides. Matthew 22 tells of a man at a wedding feast without a wedding garment who was cast out. God provides the garment. Galatians 3 says, as many of you as have been baptized into Christ have put on Christ. Today, approach God clothed in Christ. Do not come on your own terms, but in the righteousness He provides.
S: Aaron and his sons had to wear these garments when they entered the tabernacle or approached the altar, lest they bear iniquity and die. Approaching God required the covering He provided. Approach God clothed in Christ, not on your own terms.
P: Clothed in Christ. Exodus 28:43: 'that they bear not iniquity, and die.' We can't approach God on our own terms. Come clothed in the righteousness Christ provides.
