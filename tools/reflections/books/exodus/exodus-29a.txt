# Exodus 29:1-23: The consecration of the priests

== 29:1
T: To hallow them
R: 'And this is the thing that thou shalt do unto them to hallow them, to minister unto me in the priest's office: Take one young bullock, and two rams without blemish.' God described how to consecrate Aaron and his sons for priestly service. It began with sacrifices: a young bull and two rams without defect. Holiness required sacrifice. The priests could not simply decide to serve. They had to be made holy first. And holiness came through sacrifice. Even those who would offer sacrifices for others needed sacrifices for themselves. Hebrews 7 notes that earthly priests had to offer first for their own sins. But Christ needed no sacrifice for Himself. He was holy, harmless, undefiled. Notice without blemish. Only perfect animals were acceptable. This points to Christ, the Lamb without blemish. Today, remember that holiness is a gift made possible by sacrifice. You are set apart for God's service because Christ was offered for you.
S: God described how to hallow Aaron and his sons for the priest's office, beginning with a young bullock and two rams without blemish. Even priests needed sacrifice before they could serve. Remember that you are set apart for God's service because Christ was offered for you.
P: Set apart by sacrifice. Exodus 29:1: 'to hallow them… Take one young bullock, and two rams without blemish.' Holiness comes through sacrifice. Christ was offered for you.

== 29:2
T: Unleavened bread
R: 'And unleavened bread, and cakes unleavened tempered with oil, and wafers unleavened anointed with oil: of wheaten flour shalt thou make them.' Along with the animals, bread was prepared: unleavened bread, cakes mixed with oil, and wafers spread with oil, all from fine wheat flour. These would be part of the consecration offerings. The bread had no leaven, which often represents sin. It was pure. And it was mixed with oil, which often represents the Spirit. Purity and the Spirit belonged together in the priests' consecration. Fine flour was carefully ground. It was the best quality. Offerings to God were not careless. They required preparation and effort. Jesus is the bread of life, without sin and full of the Spirit. Luke 4 says Jesus was full of the Holy Ghost. He fulfilled what the bread pictured. Today, offer God your best. Let your life be free of the leaven of sin and filled with the oil of His Spirit.
S: Unleavened bread, cakes tempered with oil and wafers anointed with oil were made from fine wheat flour for the consecration. The bread pictured purity joined with the Spirit, fulfilled in Christ. Offer God your best, free of sin's leaven and filled with His Spirit.

== 29:3
T: One basket
R: 'And thou shalt put them into one basket, and bring them in the basket, with the bullock and the two rams.' All the bread was placed in one basket and brought with the bull and the two rams. Everything for the consecration was gathered together. Nothing was forgotten. Preparation mattered. Moses gathered everything before beginning. The bread, the bull, and the rams were all ready. Consecration was not rushed or careless. Notice one basket. The various offerings were kept together. Unity marked even the preparation. Everything belonged to one act of dedication. Our offerings to God should also be complete. We do not give God scattered pieces of our lives. We gather everything and bring it to Him together. Romans 12 says, present your bodies a living sacrifice. The whole self, not fragments. Today, gather your whole life and bring it to God. Do not hold back pieces. Offer Him everything in one complete act of dedication.
S: All the bread was put into one basket and brought with the bullock and the two rams. Everything for the consecration was gathered and prepared together. Gather your whole life and bring it to God in one complete act of dedication.

== 29:4
T: Washed with water
R: 'And Aaron and his sons thou shalt bring unto the door of the tabernacle of the congregation, and shalt wash them with water.' Moses was to bring Aaron and his sons to the entrance of the tabernacle and wash them with water. Before they were clothed, they were cleansed. Washing came first. Cleansing preceded clothing. The priests could not put holy garments on unwashed bodies. They had to be made clean first. Holiness begins with cleansing. Notice that Moses washed them. They did not wash themselves. Cleansing came from another. This pictures God's work in us. Titus 3 says, he saved us, by the washing of regeneration. We cannot cleanse ourselves from sin. God washes us. Jesus washed His disciples' feet in John 13 and told Peter, If I wash thee not, thou hast no part with me. Being washed by Christ is essential. Today, receive God's cleansing. Let Him wash you before you serve Him. You are made clean not by your effort but by His grace.
S: Aaron and his sons were brought to the door of the tabernacle and washed with water by Moses. Cleansing came before clothing, and it came from another. Receive God's cleansing, for you are made clean by His grace, not your effort.
P: Washed first. Exodus 29:4: 'thou… shalt wash them with water.' Cleansing came before clothing. Let Christ wash you clean before you serve Him today.

== 29:5
T: Clothed in the garments
R: 'And thou shalt take the garments, and put upon Aaron the coat.' After washing, Aaron was dressed in his garments: the tunic, the robe, the ephod, the breastpiece, and the sash. He was clothed for service. Again, notice that Moses dressed Aaron. Aaron did not dress himself. The garments were given to him and placed on him. This pictures how God clothes us. Isaiah 61 says, he hath clothed me with the garments of salvation. We do not earn righteousness or create it. God puts it on us. Galatians 3 says, as many of you as have been baptized into Christ have put on Christ. Notice the order: washed, then clothed. Cleansing came first, then garments of glory. Second Corinthians 5 says, if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature. The old is washed away, and new garments are given. Today, thank God for clothing you in Christ. Your righteousness is His gift, placed on you by grace.

== 29:6
T: The holy crown
R: 'And thou shalt put the mitre upon his head, and put the holy crown upon the mitre.' Moses placed the turban on Aaron's head and attached the holy crown, the gold plate engraved HOLINESS TO THE LORD. The high priest was crowned with holiness. Notice the phrase holy crown. Aaron was not a king, but he wore a crown of holiness. His authority came from being set apart for God. This pictures Christ, who is both King and Priest. Zechariah 6 speaks of one who shall be a priest upon his throne. Hebrews 7 describes Christ as a priest after the order of Melchisedec, who was both king and priest. Jesus wears the crown of holiness perfectly. Believers also receive crowns. Second Timothy 4 speaks of a crown of righteousness. James 1 speaks of the crown of life. Revelation 4 shows the elders casting their crowns before the throne. Today, honor Christ as your King and Priest. And live as one crowned with His holiness.

Second Corinthians 1 says, he which… hath anointed us, is God. Today, ask God to pour His Spirit on you generously. Let His anointing empower you for the service He has called you to.

The priesthood extended beyond Aaron to his family. The sons were brought. They did not come on their own initiative. Moses brought them forward. This pictures God's gracious calling. We do not approach God by ourselves. He brings us. John 6 says, No man can come to me, except the Father which hath sent me draw him. God draws us near. The sons received coats, simpler than Aaron's garments but still holy. Each had a place. Hebrews 2 speaks of Christ bringing many sons unto glory. As Aaron's sons shared in his priesthood, believers share in Christ's priesthood. First Peter 2 calls us an holy priesthood. Today, rejoice that God has brought you near. You did not earn your place. He drew you and clothed you for His service.

== 29:9
T: A perpetual statute
R: 'And thou shalt gird them with girdles, Aaron and his sons, and put the bonnets on them: and the priest's office shall be theirs for a perpetual statute: and thou shalt consecrate Aaron and his sons.' Moses wrapped sashes on Aaron and his sons and put caps on the sons. The priesthood would belong to them as a lasting statute. They were consecrated to serve. Notice perpetual statute. The priesthood was to continue through generations. Yet the Aaronic priesthood had limits. Hebrews 7 explains that it could not make anything perfect. A better priesthood was needed. Christ is that better priest. Hebrews 7 says He hath an unchangeable priesthood because he continueth ever. His priesthood never ends. Notice the word consecrate. In Hebrew, it means to fill the hands. The priests' hands were filled with offerings and service. Their hands were dedicated to God. Today, offer your hands to God. Let them be filled with His work. And rest in Christ's unchanging priesthood on your behalf.

== 29:10
T: Hands on the bullock
R: 'And thou shalt cause a bullock to be brought before the tabernacle of the congregation: and Aaron and his sons shall put their hands upon the head of the bullock.' The bull was brought before the tabernacle. Aaron and his sons laid their hands on its head. This act identified them with the animal. Their sin was symbolically transferred to it. It said, this animal takes my place. The priests acknowledged their own sin. Even those called to serve needed atonement. This is the principle of substitution. Isaiah 53 says, the LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all. Christ took our place. Our sins were transferred to Him. Second Corinthians 5 says, he hath made him to be sin for us, who knew no sin. Notice that the priests did this personally. Each laid his own hands. Faith in Christ's sacrifice is personal. Today, lay your hands, by faith, on Christ your sacrifice. Acknowledge your sin, and trust that He took your place.
S: The bullock was brought before the tabernacle, and Aaron and his sons laid their hands on its head. The act identified them with the animal as a substitute bearing their sin. By faith, lay your sin on Christ your sacrifice and trust that He took your place.
P: He took my place. Exodus 29:10: 'Aaron and his sons shall put their hands upon the head of the bullock.' Substitution. Christ bore your sin. Trust Him personally today.

God witnessed the substitute's death. Atonement is ultimately directed toward God. The location mattered too: at the door. The sacrifice happened at the entrance. Access to God's presence required passing through death. Hebrews 9 says, without shedding of blood is no remission. Christ also died before the LORD. He offered Himself to the Father. Hebrews 9 says Christ through the eternal Spirit offered himself without spot to God. His death was the door into God's presence. Today, remember the cost of your access to God. A death occurred at the door. Christ died so that you could enter. Approach God with gratitude for that sacrifice.

The kidneys especially were seen as the seat of feeling. Offering them symbolized giving God one's innermost being. Psalm 51 says, thou desirest truth in the inward parts. God wants our inner life, not just outward actions. Today, give God your best and your innermost self. Do not offer Him only the surface. Let Him have the deepest parts of your heart.

Hebrews 13 continues: Let us go forth therefore unto him without the camp, bearing his reproach. Today, thank Jesus for bearing your sin outside the camp. This filled their hands, which is what consecration literally means. The choicest portions show that God deserves our best. Not leftovers, but our strength and richest gifts. == 29:23
T: Bread from the basket
R: 'And one loaf of bread, and one cake of oiled bread, and one wafer out of the basket of the unleavened bread that is before the LORD.' One loaf of bread, one cake made with oil, and one wafer were taken from the basket of unleavened bread before the LORD. These would be added to the consecration offering. Bread joined the sacrifice. Bread represented daily provision and labor. Offering it to God acknowledged that sustenance came from Him. It also showed dedication of daily work to God. Notice three types of bread: plain, oiled, and anointed. Variety was offered. All parts of daily life could be given to God. The bread was before the LORD. It was in God's presence before it was offered. Everything we have comes from His presence and returns to Him. First Chronicles 29 says, all things come of thee, and of thine own have we given thee. Today, offer your daily bread and daily work to God. Acknowledge that every provision comes from Him and belongs to Him.
