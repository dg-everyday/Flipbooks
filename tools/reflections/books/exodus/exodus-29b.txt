# Exodus 29:24-46: The wave offering, the daily sacrifice and God's dwelling

== 29:24
T: Filled hands
R: 'And thou shalt put all in the hands of Aaron, and in the hands of his sons; and shalt wave them for a wave offering before the LORD.' Moses placed the offerings in the hands of Aaron and his sons. They waved them before the LORD as a wave offering. Their hands were literally filled with offerings. This is the meaning of consecration: filling the hands. The priests' hands held what belonged to God. Their service began with hands full of offerings. The waving motion presented the offering to God and then received it back. It acknowledged that everything belonged to Him. Our hands are also meant to be filled with God's work. What we hold, we hold for Him. Our possessions, talents, and time are offerings in our hands. First Peter 4 says, As every man hath received the gift, even so minister the same one to another. Today, consider what fills your hands. Present it to God. Let your hands be filled with offerings for Him.
S: Moses put the offerings into the hands of Aaron and his sons, and they waved them before the LORD. Consecration meant filling the hands with what belonged to God. Present to God what fills your hands and let them be filled with offerings for Him.
P: Hands filled for God. Exodus 29:24: 'thou shalt put all in the hands of Aaron… and shalt wave them.' What fills your hands? Present it to God today.

== 29:25
T: Received and burned
R: 'And thou shalt receive them of their hands, and burn them upon the altar for a burnt offering, for a sweet savour before the LORD: it is an offering made by fire unto the LORD.' After the waving, Moses took the offerings from their hands and burned them on the altar as a pleasing aroma to the LORD. What filled their hands went up to God. The offerings were placed in their hands, waved, then taken and burned. The priests did not keep what they held. Consecration involves releasing what we hold. We receive gifts from God, and we give them back. This is the rhythm of faithful stewardship. Job 1 says, the LORD gave, and the LORD hath taken away; blessed be the name of the LORD. Holding loosely and offering freely pleases God. Notice sweet savour again. What is given up to God becomes a pleasing aroma. Surrender delights Him. Today, release what you hold to God. Let your offerings rise to Him as a sweet savour.
S: Moses received the offerings from their hands and burned them on the altar as a sweet savour to the LORD. The priests gave up completely what they had held. Release what you hold to God and let your offerings rise to Him as a pleasing aroma.

== 29:26
T: Thy part
R: 'And thou shalt take the breast of the ram of Aaron's consecration, and wave it for a wave offering before the LORD: and it shall be thy part.' Moses took the breast of the consecration ram and waved it before the LORD. Then it became Moses' portion. God provided for His servant from the offering. Notice it shall be thy part. Moses, who performed the consecration, received a share. Those who serve God are provided for through His offerings. First Corinthians 9 says, they which wait at the altar are partakers with the altar. God takes care of those who serve Him. Some see in this the love and affection that God shares with His servants. Notice that the breast was waved before the LORD first. It was presented to God before it was received. Everything we receive comes through God's hands. Today, trust God to provide for you as you serve Him. And remember that everything you receive was first His.
S: Moses waved the breast of the ram of consecration before the LORD, and it became his portion. God provided for His servant from what was first presented to Him. Trust God to provide for you as you serve Him, remembering that everything you receive was first His.

== 29:27
T: Wave and heave
R: 'And thou shalt sanctify the breast of the wave offering, and the shoulder of the heave offering, which is waved, and which is heaved up, of the ram of the consecration.' The breast of the wave offering and the thigh of the heave offering were to be set apart. One was waved before the LORD; the other was lifted up. Both were sanctified. The wave offering moved horizontally, perhaps toward the altar and back. The heave offering moved vertically, lifted up toward heaven. Together they pictured offering in every direction. Worship has both vertical and horizontal dimensions. We lift our offerings up to God. And we extend them outward to others. Matthew 22 records the two great commandments: love God and love your neighbor. Notice sanctify. These portions were made holy. They were set apart for God's purposes. Today, offer your life both upward to God and outward to others. Let your worship and your service be holy, lifted and extended in love.

== 29:28

The people would provide them from their peace offerings. God established a system of support for those who served Him. The priests did not own land. Numbers 18 says God Himself was their inheritance. They lived from the offerings of the people. This principle continues in the church. First Timothy 5 says, The labourer is worthy of his reward. Those who devote themselves to ministry deserve support. Galatians 6 says, Let him that is taught in the word communicate unto him that teacheth in all good things. Notice from the children of Israel. The people provided for the priests. Supporting God's servants was a shared responsibility. Today, consider how you support those who serve in ministry. Your gifts help sustain God's work. And if you serve, trust Him to provide through His people.

== 29:29
T: Garments passed down
R: 'And the holy garments of Aaron shall be his sons' after him, to be anointed therein, and to be consecrated in them.' Aaron's holy garments would be passed to his sons after him. Each new high priest would be anointed and ordained in them. The priesthood would continue through generations. The garments outlived Aaron. They were passed down, carrying the office forward. Each new priest wore what his father had worn. Continuity marked God's service. This reminds us of the importance of passing faith to the next generation. Second Timothy 1 speaks of the unfeigned faith which dwelt first in thy grandmother Lois, and thy mother Eunice. Faith was passed down like holy garments. Yet each new priest needed his own anointing. The garments were inherited, but consecration was personal. We can inherit a heritage of faith, but each person must respond to God. Today, value the faith passed down to you. And pass it on to others, praying that each generation will receive its own anointing from God.

Today, if you are beginning something new, take time to prepare. Do not rush. Let God consecrate you fully before you step into new service.

Any meat or bread left over until morning was to be burned. It could not be eaten later because it was holy. Holy food was not to be treated as ordinary leftovers. This instruction protected the sacredness of the offering. Holy things could not become common. Whatever remained was given entirely to God. There is a principle here about reverence. Holy things should not be treated casually. We should not let sacred moments become ordinary through familiarity. Hebrews 12 warns against being like Esau, a profane person who traded something holy for a meal. Notice until the morning. The holy meal belonged to a specific time. Some moments with God are for that moment. They cannot be preserved and repeated at will. Today, treat holy things with reverence. Do not let familiarity make the sacred common.

== 29:46
T: That I may dwell among them
R: 'And they shall know that I am the LORD their God, that brought them forth out of the land of Egypt, that I may dwell among them: I am the LORD their God.' Israel would know that the LORD is their God, who brought them out of Egypt so that He could dwell among them. The exodus had a purpose: God's presence with His people. Notice the purpose clause: that I may dwell among them. God did not rescue Israel merely to free them from slavery. He rescued them so He could live with them. This is true for us too. God did not save us only from something. He saved us for something: life with Him. Ephesians 2 says we are builded together for an habitation of God through the Spirit. The chapter ends with I am the LORD their God. Today, remember why God saved you: to dwell with you. Live in the joy of His presence. He is the LORD your God.
S: God said Israel would know that He is the LORD who brought them out of Egypt so that He might dwell among them. The purpose of the exodus was God's presence with His people. Remember that God saved you to dwell with you and live in the joy of His presence.
