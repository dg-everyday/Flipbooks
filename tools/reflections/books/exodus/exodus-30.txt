# Exodus 30: The altar of incense, the ransom, the laver, the oil and the incense

== 30:1
T: An altar for incense
R: 'And thou shalt make an altar to burn incense upon: of shittim wood shalt thou make it.' God commanded a second altar, this one for burning incense. It would stand inside the Holy Place. Unlike the bronze altar outside, no animals would be sacrificed here. Only fragrant incense would rise. The two altars belong together. The bronze altar dealt with sin through sacrifice. The golden altar sent up prayer like incense. Atonement comes first; then communion. Psalm 141 says, Let my prayer be set forth before thee as incense. Revelation 8 shows the prayers of the saints rising with incense before God's throne. Prayer is a sweet fragrance to Him. Today, come to God in prayer. Because Christ's sacrifice has dealt with your sin, your prayers can rise to Him like incense.
S: God commanded an altar of shittim wood to burn incense inside the Holy Place. After the bronze altar dealt with sin, the golden altar sent up incense, picturing prayer. Come to God in prayer, for through Christ's sacrifice your prayers rise to Him like incense.
P: Prayer as incense. Exodus 30:1: 'thou shalt make an altar to burn incense upon.' Sacrifice dealt with sin; now prayer rises to God. Let your prayers rise today.

== 30:2
T: Small but significant
R: 'A cubit shall be the length thereof, and a cubit the breadth thereof; foursquare shall it be: and two cubits shall be the height thereof: the horns thereof shall be of the same.' The incense altar was small: one cubit square and two cubits high, with horns on its corners. It was much smaller than the bronze altar outside. Yet it stood close to God's throne. Size does not determine importance. The incense altar was small, but it stood just outside the veil, nearest to the mercy seat. Prayer may seem small, but it brings us closest to God. James 5 says, The effectual fervent prayer of a righteous man availeth much. The horns again speak of power. Prayer is not weak. It carries God's strength. Today, do not underestimate prayer. Though it may seem small, it brings you near to God's throne and carries His power.
S: The incense altar was one cubit square and two cubits high, with horns of the same piece. Though small, it stood nearest the mercy seat, and its horns spoke of power. Do not underestimate prayer, for it brings you near God's throne and carries His power.
P: Small but powerful. Exodus 30:2: 'A cubit shall be the length thereof… the horns thereof shall be of the same.' Prayer may seem small, but it brings you near to God.

The incense altar was overlaid with pure gold on its top, sides, and horns, with a gold molding around it. Everything about it shone. The place of prayer was glorious. The outer altar was bronze; the inner altar was gold. As one moved closer to God, the materials became more precious. Prayer belongs in the golden place, near God's glory. The crown of gold reminds us that prayer approaches a King. Hebrews 4 says, Let us therefore come boldly unto the throne of grace. We pray to the King of kings, and our prayers are welcomed in His royal presence. Today, value prayer as a golden privilege. You approach the throne of the King whenever you pray.

T: Rings and staves
R: 'And two golden rings shalt thou make to it under the crown of it, by the two corners thereof… and they shall be for places for the staves to bear it withal.' Two gold rings were placed under the molding for carrying poles. The incense altar was portable like the other furniture. Prayer would travel with Israel through the wilderness. Wherever Israel camped, the altar of incense was set up. Prayer was never left behind. It went everywhere with God's people. Our prayer life also travels with us. We can pray anywhere: at work, at home, on the road. First Thessalonians 5 says, Pray without ceasing. Prayer is not confined to a building. Daniel prayed in Babylon, and Jonah prayed from the belly of the fish. Today, carry prayer with you everywhere. Let it be a constant companion on your journey.

== 30:9
T: No strange incense
R: 'Ye shall offer no strange incense thereon, nor burnt sacrifice, nor meat offering; neither shall ye pour drink offering thereon.' No unauthorized incense, burnt offering, grain offering, or drink offering was to be placed on this altar. It was for prayer alone, using only the incense God prescribed. Strange incense meant incense not made according to God's recipe. Leviticus 10 records Nadab and Abihu offering strange fire and dying. Worship must follow God's way, not our invention. This applies to prayer. True prayer comes to God on His terms, through Christ, in sincerity. John 4 says, they that worship him must worship him in spirit and in truth. Prayer is not a magic formula or a manipulation. It is humble communion. Today, pray sincerely and in truth. Come to God through Christ, not with strange fire of your own making.
S: God forbade offering strange incense, burnt sacrifice, grain or drink offering on the incense altar. It was for prayer alone, made God's way. Pray sincerely and in truth, coming to God through Christ rather than on your own terms.

== 30:10

Our prayers, too, are touched by imperfection. We pray with mixed motives and distracted hearts. Even our prayers need cleansing. Only through atoning blood can they be accepted. This is why we pray in Jesus' name. John 14 records Jesus saying, whatsoever ye shall ask in my name, that will I do. His blood makes our prayers acceptable. Hebrews 10 says we draw near by the blood of Jesus. Today, thank God that your imperfect prayers are made acceptable through Christ's blood. Pray in His name with confidence.

== 30:12
T: A ransom for his soul
R: 'When thou takest the sum of the children of Israel after their number, then shall they give every man a ransom for his soul unto the LORD, when thou numberest them; that there be no plague among them.' When Israel was counted, every man had to pay a ransom for his life to the LORD. This would prevent a plague. Each person owed a ransom. Being counted among God's people required redemption. No one could be numbered without a ransom. Every life belonged to God and needed to be redeemed. Second Samuel 24 records a plague when David numbered the people improperly. Counting without ransom brought judgment. Christ is our ransom. Mark 10 says the Son of man came… to give his life a ransom for many. We are counted among God's people because He paid for us. Today, rejoice that you are numbered among God's people. Your ransom has been paid in full by Christ.

== 30:13
T: Half a shekel
R: 'This they shall give, every one that passeth among them that are numbered, half a shekel after the shekel of the sanctuary… an half shekel shall be the offering of the LORD.' The ransom was half a shekel, measured by the sanctuary standard. Everyone who was counted paid this amount. It was a modest, fixed price. The amount was small enough for anyone to pay. God did not set an impossible price. Yet it was required of everyone. No one was exempt. Notice after the shekel of the sanctuary. God's measure was the standard, not human measures. The price was set by Him. Matthew 17 records Jesus paying the temple tax, a later form of this ransom, with a coin from a fish's mouth. The Son paid for Himself and for Peter. Today, rejoice that God set the price of your redemption, and that Christ paid it fully on your behalf.
S: The ransom was half a shekel by the sanctuary standard, paid by everyone who was numbered. God set a modest fixed price required of all. Rejoice that God set the price of your redemption and Christ paid it fully for you.

== 30:34
T: Sweet spices for incense
R: 'And the LORD said unto Moses, Take unto thee sweet spices, stacte, and onycha, and galbanum; these sweet spices with pure frankincense: of each shall there be a like weight.' God gave the recipe for the holy incense: stacte, onycha, galbanum, and pure frankincense, in equal amounts. These spices would create a unique fragrance for the incense altar. Notice of each… a like weight. Equal portions. No single spice dominated. Balance created the perfect fragrance. Galbanum is interesting. On its own, it has a bitter, unpleasant smell. Yet blended with the others, it enhanced the fragrance. Some see in this how even bitter experiences, offered to God, become part of a sweet aroma. Frankincense was one of the gifts brought to Jesus in Matthew 2. Today, offer all your experiences to God, the sweet and the bitter. In His hands, they blend into a fragrance that pleases Him.

The people were forbidden to make this incense for themselves. It was holy for the LORD alone. Its fragrance belonged to God's worship. Notice to yourselves. The temptation was to use God's things for personal pleasure. The incense smelled wonderful. People might want it for their homes. But it belonged to God. This guards against self-centered spirituality. Worship is for God, not primarily for our own enjoyment. Prayer is communion with Him, not a technique for feeling good. Matthew 6 warns against praying to be seen by men. Today, keep your worship focused on God. Let your prayers be for Him, not for your own image or pleasure.
