# Exodus 31: Gifted craftsmen; the Sabbath sign; the tables of stone

== 31:1
T: God speaks of the builders
R: 'And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying.' After giving the full design of the tabernacle, God spoke to Moses about who would build it. Plans need people. God had a design, and now He named the builders. God cares not only about what is built but who builds it. He does not leave His work to chance. He chooses and equips specific people for specific tasks. This is still true. God calls people to serve in His kingdom, each with a role. Ephesians 2 says we are his workmanship, created in Christ Jesus unto good works, which God hath before ordained. God prepared the work and prepares the workers. Notice that God took the initiative. He did not wait for volunteers. He named the builders Himself. Today, consider that God has prepared good works for you. He has a role for you in His building project. Listen for His call.
S: After giving the tabernacle's design, the LORD spoke to Moses about who would build it. God chooses and equips specific people for specific tasks in His work. Consider that God has prepared good works for you and listen for His call.
P: God chooses the builders. Exodus 31:1: 'And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying.' God prepared the work and the workers. He has a role for you. Listen for His call.

== 31:2
T: Called by name
R: 'See, I have called by name Bezaleel the son of Uri, the son of Hur, of the tribe of Judah.' God said, See, I have called by name Bezalel, son of Uri, son of Hur, of the tribe of Judah. God personally chose this craftsman. He knew his name, his family, and his tribe. Notice called by name. God's call is personal. He did not call a nameless worker. He called Bezalel specifically. God knows each of us individually and calls us personally. Bezalel's name means in the shadow of God. He worked under God's covering. His grandfather Hur was the one who held up Moses' hands in battle. Faithful families produce faithful servants. Isaiah 43 says, I have called thee by thy name; thou art mine. Today, rejoice that God knows your name. His call on your life is personal, and He equips those He calls.
S: God told Moses that He had called by name Bezaleel, son of Uri, son of Hur, of the tribe of Judah. God's call was personal and specific, and Bezaleel came from a faithful family. Rejoice that God knows your name and calls you personally.
P: Called by name. Exodus 31:2: 'See, I have called by name Bezaleel.' God's call is personal. He knows your name and equips you for His work.

== 31:3
T: Filled with the spirit of God
R: 'And I have filled him with the spirit of God, in wisdom, and in understanding, and in knowledge, and in all manner of workmanship.' God filled Bezalel with His Spirit, giving him wisdom, understanding, knowledge, and skill in all kinds of craftsmanship. His abilities came from God. This is the first time Scripture says someone was filled with the Spirit of God. And it was for craftsmanship. The Spirit empowered artistic and practical work. Notice the four gifts: wisdom, understanding, knowledge, and workmanship. Wisdom for decisions, understanding for insight, knowledge for skill, and workmanship for doing. All came from God. Our skills are not merely natural talents. They are gifts from God's Spirit. James 1 says every good gift and every perfect gift is from above. Today, thank God for the abilities He has given you. Use them for His glory, knowing they come from His Spirit.
S: God filled Bezaleel with His Spirit in wisdom, understanding, knowledge and all manner of workmanship. This first mention of a Spirit-filled person concerned skilled craftsmanship. Thank God for the abilities He has given you and use them for His glory.
P: Spirit-filled skill. Exodus 31:3: 'I have filled him with the spirit of God, in wisdom… and in all manner of workmanship.' Your skills are God's gifts. Use them for Him.

== 31:4
T: Cunning works
R: 'To devise cunning works, to work in gold, and in silver, and in brass.' Bezalel would design artistic works and craft them in gold, silver, and bronze. He was both designer and maker. His creativity served God's purposes. Notice devise cunning works. Bezalel did not only follow instructions. He used creativity to design. God's Spirit inspires imagination. Creativity is part of being made in God's image. God is the ultimate Creator. When we create beautiful things, we reflect Him. Genesis 1 shows God creating with beauty and order. Artists, designers, and builders can serve God with their creativity. The tabernacle was beautiful because God valued beauty. Philippians 4 says, whatsoever things are lovely… think on these things. Today, use your creativity for God. Whether you design, build, write, or make, let your work reflect the beauty of the Creator.
S: Bezaleel was gifted to devise cunning works and to craft in gold, silver and brass. God's Spirit inspired creativity and design as well as skill. Use your creativity for God and let your work reflect the beauty of the Creator.
P: Creativity for God. Exodus 31:4: 'To devise cunning works, to work in gold, and in silver, and in brass.' Your creativity reflects your Creator. Use it for His glory.

== 31:5
T: All manner of workmanship
R: 'And in cutting of stones, to set them, and in carving of timber, to work in all manner of workmanship.' Bezalel was also skilled in cutting and setting stones and carving wood. He could work in every kind of craft. His gifts were broad and varied. God gave Bezalel many skills. Metal, stone, wood: he could work with all of them. The tabernacle required diverse abilities, and God provided them in one gifted man. God equips people for the tasks He assigns. When He calls someone to a work, He provides what is needed. Second Corinthians 9 says God is able to make all grace abound toward you; that ye… may abound to every good work. Notice all manner of workmanship. No task was outside the scope of God's gifting. Today, trust that God will equip you for what He calls you to do. If He has given you a task, He will provide the skills you need.
S: Bezaleel was also gifted in cutting and setting stones, carving timber and all manner of workmanship. God equipped him with broad skills for the tasks He assigned. Trust that God will equip you for whatever He calls you to do.
P: Equipped for the task. Exodus 31:5: 'in cutting of stones… and in carving of timber, to work in all manner of workmanship.' God equips those He calls. Trust Him today.

== 31:6
T: Aholiab and the wise hearted
R: 'And I, behold, I have given with him Aholiab, the son of Ahisamach, of the tribe of Dan: and in the hearts of all that are wise hearted I have put wisdom, that they may make all that I have commanded thee.' God appointed Oholiab from the tribe of Dan to work with Bezalel. And He gave wisdom to all the skilled workers. The tabernacle would be built by a team. Notice the tribes. Bezalel was from Judah, a prominent tribe. Oholiab was from Dan, one of the lesser tribes. God brought them together. His work unites people from different backgrounds. No one built the tabernacle alone. Bezalel led, but Oholiab assisted, and many others contributed. God placed wisdom in all their hearts. First Corinthians 12 says the body is not one member, but many. God's work is a team effort. Today, value the people God has placed beside you in His work. Different backgrounds, different gifts, one purpose.
S: God appointed Aholiab of Dan to work with Bezaleel of Judah and put wisdom in the hearts of all the wise hearted. The tabernacle was built by a team from different tribes. Value the people God has placed beside you, with different gifts and one purpose.
P: A team effort. Exodus 31:6: 'I have given with him Aholiab… and in the hearts of all that are wise hearted I have put wisdom.' God unites different people for His work.

== 31:7
T: The tabernacle and the ark
R: 'The tabernacle of the congregation, and the ark of the testimony, and the mercy seat that is thereupon, and all the furniture of the tabernacle.' God listed what the craftsmen would make: the tabernacle, the ark, the mercy seat, and all the furniture. They would build everything God had designed. The list starts with the most important items. The tabernacle as a whole, then the ark and mercy seat at its heart. The craftsmen's work centered on God's presence and mercy. All our work should center on what matters most to God. Whatever we build, we should keep His presence and mercy at the heart. Colossians 3 says, whatsoever ye do, do it heartily, as to the Lord. Notice all the furniture. Nothing was left out. Every item mattered. Today, center your work on what matters to God. Let His presence and mercy be at the heart of everything you build.
S: The craftsmen would make the tabernacle, the ark of the testimony, the mercy seat and all the furniture. Their work centered on God's presence and mercy. Center your work on what matters most to God, keeping His presence and mercy at the heart.

== 31:8
T: Table, candlestick, incense
R: 'And the table and his furniture, and the pure candlestick with all his furniture, and the altar of incense.' The list continued with the table, the pure lampstand, and the altar of incense. Each item and its utensils would be made by these gifted workers. Notice the pure candlestick. The lampstand was made of pure gold, giving pure light. Purity in what gives light matters. Our witness shines best when our lives are pure. The table provided bread, the lampstand provided light, and the incense altar provided prayer. Provision, illumination, and communion. These are the elements of a healthy spiritual life. Each requires attention. Jesus is the bread of life, the light of the world, and our intercessor. Everything in the Holy Place points to Him. Today, attend to your spiritual provision, light, and prayer. Feed on God's word, walk in His light, and commune with Him in prayer.
S: The craftsmen would make the table, the pure candlestick and the altar of incense with their furniture. Bread, light and prayer formed the heart of the Holy Place, all pointing to Christ. Feed on God's word, walk in His light and commune with Him in prayer today.

== 31:9
T: Altar and laver
R: 'And the altar of burnt offering with all his furniture, and the laver and his foot.' The craftsmen would also make the bronze altar and the laver. The outer court's furnishings were just as important as those inside. Every part of the tabernacle needed skilled work. The altar and laver were in the courtyard, seen by everyone. They dealt with sacrifice and cleansing, the first steps toward God. They required the same skilled craftsmanship as the inner furniture. Every stage of approaching God matters. The beginning of faith, where sin is dealt with and cleansing happens, is as important as deeper communion. We never outgrow the cross or our need for cleansing. Galatians 6 says, God forbid that I should glory, save in the cross. Today, value the foundation of your faith. Christ's sacrifice and cleansing remain essential at every stage of your journey.
S: The craftsmen would also make the altar of burnt offering and the laver with their furnishings. The outer court's work of sacrifice and cleansing mattered as much as the inner furniture. Value the foundation of your faith, for Christ's sacrifice and cleansing remain essential at every stage.
P: The foundation matters. Exodus 31:9: 'the altar of burnt offering… and the laver and his foot.' We never outgrow the cross. Value Christ's sacrifice at every stage.

== 31:10
T: Garments of service
R: 'And the cloths of service, and the holy garments for Aaron the priest, and the garments of his sons, to minister in the priest's office.' The craftsmen would make the woven garments: holy garments for Aaron and his sons to serve as priests. Textile work was as important as metalwork. Weavers and seamstresses were part of God's building project. Their skill in fabric was valued alongside skill in gold. Every craft had a place. This affirms the value of all kinds of work. Some might think only certain skills are spiritual. But God values all faithful work done for Him. Proverbs 31 praises a woman who works with her hands. Acts 9 honors Dorcas for making garments for widows. Notice holy garments. Even clothing was set apart for God's service. Today, value every kind of skill offered to God. Whatever your work, it can be holy when done for Him.
S: The craftsmen would also make the cloths of service and the holy garments for Aaron and his sons. Textile work was valued as highly as metalwork in God's building. Value every kind of skill offered to God, for any work can be holy when done for Him.

== 31:11
T: According to all
R: 'And the anointing oil, and sweet incense for the holy place: according to all that I have commanded thee shall they do.' The craftsmen would also make the anointing oil and the fragrant incense. Everything was to be made according to all that God commanded. Perfumers and spice-makers had a role too. Notice the final instruction: according to all that I have commanded thee. Creativity had to serve obedience. The craftsmen were gifted, but they followed God's design. Their skills served His purposes, not their own ideas. Gifted people can be tempted to do things their own way. But true service follows God's direction. First Samuel 15 says, to obey is better than sacrifice. Skill without obedience misses the point. Today, use your gifts in obedience to God. Let your creativity serve His design, not your own preferences.
S: The craftsmen would also make the anointing oil and sweet incense, doing according to all God commanded. Their gifts served obedience to God's design rather than their own ideas. Use your gifts in obedience to God and let your creativity serve His purposes.

== 31:12
T: Then about the Sabbath
R: 'And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying.' After describing the builders, God spoke to Moses about the Sabbath. Right after work instructions came rest instructions. The order is significant. The craftsmen were about to begin an important and exciting project. Yet God reminded them that even holy work must pause for the Sabbath. Building the tabernacle did not override the rhythm of rest. Even good work can become all-consuming. We can become so busy serving God that we neglect resting in Him. Luke 10 records Martha busy with serving, while Mary sat at Jesus' feet. Jesus said Mary had chosen the good part. Today, remember that rest is part of God's design, even in the middle of important work. Do not let even good work crowd out your rest in Him.
S: After describing the builders, the LORD spoke to Moses about the Sabbath. Even the holy work of building the tabernacle had to pause for rest. Do not let even good work for God crowd out your rest in Him.
P: Rest even from good work. Exodus 31:12: 'And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying.' After work came Sabbath. Don't let even ministry crowd out rest in God.

== 31:13
T: A sign between me and you
R: 'Speak thou also unto the children of Israel, saying, Verily my sabbaths ye shall keep: for it is a sign between me and you throughout your generations; that ye may know that I am the LORD that doth sanctify you.' God told Israel to keep His Sabbaths. It was a sign between God and them for all generations, so they would know that He is the LORD who sanctifies them. The Sabbath was a covenant sign. It marked Israel as God's people. Other nations worked seven days. Israel rested on the seventh, showing that they belonged to the LORD. Notice that I am the LORD that doth sanctify you. Rest reminded Israel that holiness comes from God, not from endless effort. By stopping work, they acknowledged that God makes them holy. Hebrews 4 says, he that is entered into his rest, he also hath ceased from his own works. Today, rest in the truth that God sanctifies you. Your holiness is His work. Let rest remind you that you belong to Him.
S: God told Israel to keep His Sabbaths as a sign between Him and them, so they would know He is the LORD who sanctifies them. Rest marked them as His and reminded them that holiness comes from God. Rest in the truth that God sanctifies you and let rest remind you that you belong to Him.
P: A sign of belonging. Exodus 31:13: 'it is a sign between me and you… that I am the LORD that doth sanctify you.' Rest reminds you that God makes you holy.

== 31:14
T: Holy unto you
R: 'Ye shall keep the sabbath therefore; for it is holy unto you: every one that defileth it shall surely be put to death.' The Sabbath was holy to Israel. Anyone who defiled it by working would be put to death and cut off. The penalty shows how seriously God viewed this covenant sign. The severity may surprise us. But breaking the Sabbath was breaking the covenant sign. It was declaring independence from God. It said, I do not need to stop; I depend on my own work. Notice holy unto you. The Sabbath was holy for Israel's sake. It was a gift for them. Rejecting it was rejecting God's gift. Mark 2 records Jesus saying, The sabbath was made for man. Under the new covenant, believers are not bound by these penalties. Colossians 2 shows the Sabbath fulfilled in Christ. Yet the principle of rest remains a gift. Today, do not reject God's gift of rest. Trust Him enough to stop, and receive the holiness of rest as His gift.
S: The Sabbath was holy to Israel, and anyone who defiled it by working faced death. Breaking it declared independence from God and rejected His gift of rest. Though fulfilled in Christ, rest remains God's gift, so trust Him enough to stop and receive it.
P: Don't reject the gift. Exodus 31:14: 'Ye shall keep the sabbath therefore; for it is holy unto you.' Rest is God's gift. Trust Him enough to stop today.
H: #DailyGrace #Exodus3114 #Sabbath #Rest #Trust #BibleVerse

== 31:15
T: Sabbath of rest
R: 'Six days may work be done; but in the seventh is the sabbath of rest, holy to the LORD: whosoever doeth any work in the sabbath day, he shall surely be put to death.' Work could be done for six days, but the seventh was a Sabbath of complete rest, holy to the LORD. Again God emphasized the penalty for working on it. Notice sabbath of rest. The Hebrew suggests complete rest, a rest of rest. Not partial rest, but full ceasing. This was rest at its deepest level. Many of us rarely experience true rest. We may stop physical work but keep our minds busy with worry. God invites us into deep rest, body and soul. Matthew 11 records Jesus saying, Come unto me… and I will give you rest… ye shall find rest unto your souls. Notice also holy to the LORD. Rest is not selfish. It is an act of worship. Today, seek deep rest in God, not just a pause from work. Let your soul find rest in Him.
S: Work could be done six days, but the seventh was a Sabbath of complete rest, holy to the LORD. God invited His people into deep rest as an act of worship. Seek deep rest in God for both body and soul, not just a pause from work.
P: Deep rest. Exodus 31:15: 'in the seventh is the sabbath of rest, holy to the LORD.' Not just a pause, but soul-deep rest. Come to Jesus and find rest today.

== 31:16
T: A perpetual covenant
R: 'Wherefore the children of Israel shall keep the sabbath, to observe the sabbath throughout their generations, for a perpetual covenant.' Israel was to keep the Sabbath throughout their generations as a lasting covenant. It was not a temporary arrangement but a permanent sign. Notice throughout their generations. The Sabbath was meant to be passed down. Each generation would learn to rest. Children would grow up in a rhythm of work and worship. Healthy rhythms shape families and communities. When parents model rest and worship, children learn what matters. Deuteronomy 6 calls parents to teach God's ways diligently. Notice also perpetual covenant. The Sabbath was tied to God's covenant with Israel. It expressed their ongoing relationship. Hebrews 4 says there remaineth therefore a rest to the people of God. The Sabbath points to an eternal rest. Today, build rhythms of rest and worship into your family's life. Pass them on to the next generation.
S: Israel was to keep the Sabbath throughout their generations as a perpetual covenant. It was a lasting sign meant to be passed down, pointing to God's eternal rest. Build rhythms of rest and worship into your family's life and pass them on.

== 31:17
T: He rested, and was refreshed
R: 'It is a sign between me and the children of Israel for ever: for in six days the LORD made heaven and earth, and on the seventh day he rested, and was refreshed.' The Sabbath was a lasting sign, because God created heaven and earth in six days, then rested on the seventh and was refreshed. Rest was rooted in God's own example. Notice and was refreshed. This is a striking phrase. God does not grow tired, yet Scripture says He was refreshed. It suggests delight and satisfaction. God paused to enjoy what He had made. Rest is not only recovery from exhaustion. It is delight in what is good. Genesis 1 says God saw every thing that he had made, and, behold, it was very good. The Sabbath invites us to delight in God's goodness. Isaiah 58 says, call the sabbath a delight. Today, rest not only to recover but to delight. Pause to enjoy God and His good gifts. Let rest refresh your soul.
S: The Sabbath was a sign forever because God made heaven and earth in six days, rested on the seventh and was refreshed. Rest is rooted in God's own delight in His good work. Rest not only to recover but to delight in God and His good gifts.
P: Rest and be refreshed. Exodus 31:17: 'on the seventh day he rested, and was refreshed.' Rest isn't only recovery; it's delight. Enjoy God's goodness today.

== 31:18
T: Written with the finger of God
R: 'And he gave unto Moses, when he had made an end of communing with him upon mount Sinai, two tables of testimony, tables of stone, written with the finger of God.' When God finished speaking with Moses on Sinai, He gave him two stone tablets of the covenant, written with the finger of God. Notice written with the finger of God. These tablets were not written by Moses. God Himself inscribed them. Notice also communing. Moses had been in fellowship with God, not merely receiving instructions. The law flowed from relationship. Jesus said in Luke 11, if I with the finger of God cast out devils, no doubt the kingdom of God is come upon you. The same finger that wrote the law worked through Christ. Second Corinthians 3 says the new covenant is written not in tables of stone, but in fleshy tables of the heart. Today, let God write His word on your heart. Spend time in communion with Him, and let His finger shape your life.
S: When God finished communing with Moses on Sinai, He gave him two tables of stone written with the finger of God. The law came directly from God's hand after forty days of fellowship. Let God write His word on your heart through time spent in communion with Him.
P: The finger of God. Exodus 31:18: 'two tables of testimony, tables of stone, written with the finger of God.' Let God write His word on your heart today.
