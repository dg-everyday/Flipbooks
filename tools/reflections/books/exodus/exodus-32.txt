# Exodus 32: The golden calf and Moses' intercession

== 32:1
T: Moses delayed
R: 'And when the people saw that Moses delayed to come down out of the mount, the people gathered themselves together unto Aaron, and said unto him, Up, make us gods, which shall go before us.' Moses had been on the mountain for forty days. The people grew impatient. They gathered around Aaron and demanded, Make us gods to lead us. They said they did not know what had happened to Moses. Impatience opened the door to idolatry. The people could not wait. When God seemed silent and their leader absent, they looked for something visible to trust. Waiting is one of the hardest tests of faith. When God's timing feels slow, we are tempted to create our own solutions. Abraham and Sarah did this with Hagar. Saul did it when he offered sacrifice instead of waiting for Samuel. Isaiah 40 says, they that wait upon the LORD shall renew their strength. Today, resist the pull to fill God's silence with substitutes. Wait patiently. He has not forgotten you.
S: When Moses delayed on the mount, the people gathered to Aaron and demanded that he make them gods to go before them. Impatience in God's silence opened the door to idolatry. Resist filling God's silence with substitutes and wait patiently for Him.
P: Don't fill the silence with idols. Exodus 32:1: 'when the people saw that Moses delayed… make us gods.' Impatience leads astray. Wait on the LORD today.

== 32:2
T: Break off the earrings
R: 'And Aaron said unto them, Break off the golden earrings, which are in the ears of your wives, of your sons, and of your daughters, and bring them unto me.' Aaron gave in. He told the people to take off their gold earrings and bring them to him. The leader left in charge did not resist the crowd. Aaron's failure is sobering. He had stood with Moses before Pharaoh. He had seen the plagues and the Red Sea. He had been on the mountain and seen God. Yet under pressure, he gave the people what they wanted. Leadership requires courage to say no. Aaron chose popularity over faithfulness. Proverbs 29 says, The fear of man bringeth a snare. Notice the gold came from Egypt. God had given it to them for the tabernacle. Now it was turned toward an idol. God's gifts can be misused. Today, have the courage to say no when others pressure you toward wrong. And use God's gifts for His purposes, not for idols.
S: Aaron told the people to break off the golden earrings of their wives, sons and daughters and bring them to him. The leader gave in to pressure, and gold meant for God's house was turned toward an idol. Have the courage to say no to wrong and use God's gifts for His purposes.
P: Courage to say no. Exodus 32:2: 'Break off the golden earrings… and bring them unto me.' Aaron gave in to the crowd. Have courage to stand firm today.

== 32:3
T: All the people gave
R: 'And all the people brake off the golden earrings which were in their ears, and brought them unto Aaron.' Everyone took off their gold earrings and brought them to Aaron. The response was immediate and complete. They gave generously, but for the wrong purpose. The people were eager to give, but they gave to an idol. Generosity is not automatically good. It depends on what we give to. People often give enthusiastically to things that do not honor God. Notice the contrast with chapter 35, when they would give for the tabernacle. The same people, the same gold, but a different purpose. One act was idolatry; the other was worship. What we invest in reveals our hearts. Matthew 6 says, where your treasure is, there will your heart be also. Today, examine where your resources go. Are you investing in what honors God, or in substitutes that compete with Him?
S: All the people broke off their golden earrings and brought them to Aaron. They gave generously and immediately, but for an idol. Examine where your resources go, for what you invest in reveals your heart.

== 32:4
T: These be thy gods
R: 'And he received them at their hand, and fashioned it with a graving tool, after he had made it a molten calf: and they said, These be thy gods, O Israel, which brought thee up out of the land of Egypt.' Aaron melted the gold and shaped it into a calf. The people declared, These are your gods, O Israel, who brought you out of Egypt. They gave credit for their salvation to a statue. Weeks earlier, they had heard God say, I am the LORD thy God, which have brought thee out of the land of Egypt. Now they credited a golden calf. Idolatry always steals God's glory. It gives credit to created things for what only God has done. Romans 1 says they changed the glory of the uncorruptible God into an image. Many today do the same, giving credit for their blessings to money, effort, or luck. Today, give God the credit He deserves. Do not let anything steal the glory for what He has done.
S: Aaron fashioned the gold into a molten calf, and the people declared, These be thy gods, O Israel, which brought thee up out of Egypt. They gave credit for their salvation to a statue. Give God the credit He deserves and let nothing steal His glory.

== 32:5
T: A feast to the LORD
R: 'And when Aaron saw it, he built an altar before it; and Aaron made proclamation, and said, To morrow is a feast to the LORD.' Aaron built an altar before the calf and announced, Tomorrow will be a feast to the LORD. He mixed the worship of God with the worship of an idol. He tried to combine the two. This is syncretism: blending true worship with false. Aaron did not abandon God's name. He used it. But he attached it to a golden calf. He tried to have both. Many people try to worship God on their own terms. They keep God's name but add their own idols. They want God plus something else. Second Kings 17 describes people who feared the LORD, and served their own gods. God rejects this mixture. Notice that Aaron called it a feast to the LORD. Using God's name did not make the worship acceptable. Today, examine your worship. Do not mix devotion to God with devotion to idols. Worship Him alone, on His terms.
S: Aaron built an altar before the calf and proclaimed, To morrow is a feast to the LORD. He tried to blend true worship with idolatry while keeping God's name. Worship God alone on His terms, without mixing in devotion to idols.
P: No mixing. Exodus 32:5: 'he built an altar before it… To morrow is a feast to the LORD.' Using God's name doesn't make false worship right. Worship Him alone.

== 32:6
T: Rose up to play
R: 'And they rose up early on the morrow, and offered burnt offerings, and brought peace offerings; and the people sat down to eat and to drink, and rose up to play.' The next day the people rose early, offered sacrifices, and then sat down to eat and drink. Then they rose up to play, a phrase suggesting wild revelry. Worship turned into indulgence. Notice they rose up early. They were zealous. They offered burnt offerings and peace offerings. On the surface, it looked like worship. But it was directed at an idol and ended in indulgence. Idolatry often leads to moral collapse. When we worship false gods, our behavior follows. First Corinthians 10 quotes this verse: The people sat down to eat and drink, and rose up to play. Paul warns, Neither be ye idolaters. What begins as misdirected worship ends in loss of self-control. Today, guard your worship. True worship leads to holiness. False worship leads to indulgence. Let your devotion produce godly fruit.
S: The people rose early, offered sacrifices to the calf, sat down to eat and drink, and rose up to play. Misdirected zeal turned into indulgence and moral collapse. Guard your worship, for true worship leads to holiness while false worship leads to indulgence.

== 32:7
T: Thy people have corrupted themselves
R: 'And the LORD said unto Moses, Go, get thee down; for thy people, which thou broughtest out of the land of Egypt, have corrupted themselves.' God told Moses to go down quickly. Your people, whom you brought out of Egypt, have corrupted themselves. God saw everything happening in the camp. Notice the shift in language. God called them thy people, Moses' people, not My people. And He said Moses brought them out. It is as if God was distancing Himself from them. Sin damages relationship. The word corrupted means ruined or spoiled. In just forty days, the people had ruined themselves. Corruption can happen quickly. Notice that God saw it all from the mountain. Nothing was hidden from Him. Hebrews 4 says, all things are naked and opened unto the eyes of him with whom we have to do. Today, remember that God sees everything. And recognize how quickly sin can corrupt. Guard your heart diligently.

== 32:8
T: Turned aside quickly
R: 'They have turned aside quickly out of the way which I commanded them: they have made them a molten calf, and have worshipped it.' God described what they had done. They turned aside quickly from His commands. They made a calf, worshiped it, sacrificed to it, and credited it with their deliverance. Notice the word quickly. Only weeks after hearing God's voice and promising to obey, they abandoned Him. How quickly we can forget. The commandment against idols was fresh in their ears. Yet they broke it almost immediately. Their promises of obedience had been sincere but shallow. Galatians 1 records Paul's amazement: I marvel that ye are so soon removed. Quick departure from truth happens in every generation. Hebrews 2 says, we ought to give the more earnest heed… lest at any time we should let them slip. Today, hold firmly to what God has taught you. Do not let it slip away quickly. Stay in the way He has commanded.

== 32:9
T: A stiffnecked people
R: 'And the LORD said unto Moses, I have seen this people, and, behold, it is a stiffnecked people.' God said, I have seen this people. They are stiff-necked. The image is of an ox that refuses to bend its neck to the yoke. Israel was stubborn and resistant to God's guidance. Stiff-necked describes stubbornness. An ox with a stiff neck would not turn when the farmer guided it. Israel would not bend to God's direction. They insisted on their own way. This description would follow Israel throughout Scripture. Stephen used it in Acts 7: Ye stiffnecked and uncircumcised in heart and ears, ye do always resist the Holy Ghost. Stubbornness is a serious spiritual problem. It resists God's correction and guidance. Psalm 32 says, Be ye not as the horse, or as the mule… whose mouth must be held in with bit and bridle. Today, examine whether you are stiff-necked in any area. Bend your neck to God's yoke. Matthew 11 says His yoke is easy.
S: The LORD said He had seen the people and that they were stiffnecked. Like an ox refusing the yoke, they resisted God's guidance and insisted on their own way. Examine any stubbornness in your heart and bend your neck to God's easy yoke.
P: Don't be stiffnecked. Exodus 32:9: 'I have seen this people, and, behold, it is a stiffnecked people.' Bend to God's guidance. His yoke is easy. Yield to Him today.

== 32:10
T: Let me alone
R: 'Now therefore let me alone, that my wrath may wax hot against them, and that I may consume them: and I will make of thee a great nation.' God said to Moses, Leave Me alone, so that My anger may burn against them and I may destroy them. Then I will make you into a great nation. God was testing Moses' heart. This verse reveals something remarkable. God said, let me alone, as if Moses' intercession could restrain Him. God invited Moses to stand in the gap. The offer to Moses was a test. Would he accept personal greatness at the people's expense? Or would he intercede for them? Moses' response would reveal his heart. God takes prayer seriously. Ezekiel 22 says, I sought for a man among them, that should… stand in the gap before me for the land, that I should not destroy it. God looks for intercessors. Today, stand in the gap for others in prayer. Do not seek your own advancement at their expense. Intercede for those who need mercy.
S: God told Moses to let Him alone so His wrath might consume the people, offering to make Moses a great nation instead. The offer tested whether Moses would choose personal greatness or intercede for the people. Stand in the gap for others in prayer rather than seeking your own advancement.

== 32:11
T: Moses besought the LORD
R: 'And Moses besought the LORD his God, and said, LORD, why doth thy wrath wax hot against thy people, which thou hast brought forth out of the land of Egypt with great power, and with a mighty hand?' Moses pleaded with the LORD. Why should Your anger burn against Your people, whom You brought out of Egypt with great power? Moses refused the offer of greatness and interceded. Notice Moses turned the language back. God had said thy people. Moses said thy people, meaning God's people. You brought them out, not me. Moses reminded God of His own investment in Israel. Effective intercession appeals to God's nature and promises. Moses did not argue that Israel deserved mercy. He argued from who God is. Psalm 106 says, he said that he would destroy them, had not Moses his chosen stood before him in the breach. Today, intercede by appealing to God's character. Pray for others not on the basis of their worthiness but on the basis of His mercy.
S: Moses besought the LORD, asking why His wrath should burn against His own people whom He brought out of Egypt with great power. Moses refused personal greatness and appealed to God's character and past acts. Intercede for others by appealing to God's mercy, not their worthiness.
P: Appeal to God's character. Exodus 32:11: 'LORD, why doth thy wrath wax hot against thy people?' Moses pleaded on God's mercy, not their merit. Pray that way today.

== 32:14
T: The LORD repented
R: 'And the LORD repented of the evil which he thought to do unto his people.' The LORD relented from the disaster He had spoken of. Moses' prayer was heard. The people were spared from destruction. This is one of the most remarkable verses about prayer. Intercession made a difference. God responded to Moses' plea. The language of repenting describes God's change of action, not a change in His character. God's character never changes. He is always merciful. Moses appealed to that unchanging mercy, and God acted according to it. Prayer does not manipulate God. It aligns with His merciful nature. James 5 says, The effectual fervent prayer of a righteous man availeth much. Jonah 3 records God relenting when Nineveh repented. God responds to prayer and repentance. Today, believe that your prayers matter. Intercede for others with confidence. God hears and responds to those who stand in the gap.

Notice graven. Engraved, not painted. Permanent. God's word does not fade. Isaiah 40 says, the word of our God shall stand for ever. Today, treat God's word as His own voice. Read it with reverence, knowing it comes from His hand and will stand forever.

== 32:23
T: Repeating their words
R: 'For they said unto me, Make us gods, which shall go before us: for as for this Moses, the man that brought us up out of the land of Egypt, we wot not what is become of him.'
