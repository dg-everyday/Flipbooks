# Exodus 33: God's presence and Moses' request to see His glory

== 33:1
T: Depart and go up
R: 'And the LORD said unto Moses, Depart, and go up hence, thou and the people which thou hast brought up out of the land of Egypt, unto the land which I sware unto Abraham, to Isaac, and to Jacob.' God told Moses to leave Sinai and lead the people to the land He promised the patriarchs. Despite the golden calf, God's promise still stood. He would keep His word. Notice God's faithfulness. Israel had broken the covenant, yet God still pointed them toward the promised land. His oath to Abraham did not depend on Israel's perfection. Yet notice also the distance in God's words: the people which thou hast brought up. God still referred to them as Moses' people. Sin had created distance. Second Timothy 2 says, If we believe not, yet he abideth faithful: he cannot deny himself. God's promises rest on His character, not ours. Today, rejoice that God's faithfulness outlasts your failures. His promises still stand, even when you have stumbled.
S: God told Moses to depart from Sinai with the people and go up to the land He swore to Abraham, Isaac and Jacob. Despite the golden calf, God's promise still stood, though sin had created distance. Rejoice that God's faithfulness outlasts your failures and His promises still stand.
P: His promise still stands. Exodus 33:1: 'unto the land which I sware unto Abraham, to Isaac, and to Jacob.' After the golden calf, God remained faithful. He's faithful to you too.

== 33:2
T: An angel before thee
R: 'And I will send an angel before thee; and I will drive out the Canaanite, the Amorite, and the Hittite, and the Perizzite, the Hivite, and the Jebusite.' God promised to send an angel ahead of Israel and drive out the nations of Canaan. They would still receive the land. God's power would still go before them. God would still fight for Israel. The angel would lead, and the enemies would be driven out. The external blessings remained. But as the next verse shows, something important was missing. It is possible to receive God's blessings without enjoying His presence. Israel would get the land, protection, and victory. But would they have God Himself? This is a searching question. We can enjoy God's gifts while being distant from the Giver. Psalm 106 says, he gave them their request; but sent leanness into their soul. Today, ask yourself whether you desire God's blessings or God Himself. Seek the Giver above His gifts.
S: God promised to send an angel before Israel and drive out the nations of Canaan. The blessings of land and victory remained, but something more important was at stake. Ask whether you desire God's blessings or God Himself, and seek the Giver above His gifts.
P: Gifts or the Giver? Exodus 33:2: 'I will send an angel before thee; and I will drive out the Canaanite.' The blessings remained. But do you want God Himself? Seek Him today.

== 33:3
T: I will not go up in the midst
R: 'Unto a land flowing with milk and honey: for I will not go up in the midst of thee; for thou art a stiffnecked people: lest I consume thee in the way.' God said He would give them the land flowing with milk and honey, but He would not go with them Himself. They were stiff-necked, and His holy presence might destroy them on the way. They would have the land without the LORD. This was a devastating announcement. The land was good, but without God's presence it would be empty. The promise of milk and honey meant little if God was absent. Notice the reason: lest I consume thee. God's holiness in the midst of an unrepentant people would be dangerous. His distance was actually mercy. Yet His absence would be a terrible loss. Psalm 73 says, Whom have I in heaven but thee? and there is none upon earth that I desire beside thee. Today, recognize that nothing is good without God's presence. Treasure His nearness above every earthly blessing.
S: God said He would give Israel the land flowing with milk and honey but would not go up in their midst, lest He consume them as a stiffnecked people. A good land without God's presence would be a terrible loss. Recognize that nothing is good without God and treasure His nearness above every blessing.
P: Land without the LORD? Exodus 33:3: 'I will not go up in the midst of thee.' Milk and honey mean nothing without God. Treasure His presence above all.

== 33:4
T: They mourned
R: 'And when the people heard these evil tidings, they mourned: and no man did put on him his ornaments.' When the people heard that God would not go with them, they mourned. No one put on jewelry. They understood the seriousness of losing God's presence. This mourning was a sign of hope. The people recognized what they were losing. They did not celebrate the promise of land. They grieved the absence of God. Their priorities were finally right. Godly sorrow is valuable. Second Corinthians 7 says, godly sorrow worketh repentance to salvation. Recognizing our loss can lead us back to God. Notice that they removed their ornaments. These may have been the same kind of jewelry used for the golden calf. Removing them showed humility and repentance. James 4 says, Be afflicted, and mourn, and weep… Humble yourselves in the sight of the Lord. Today, if you sense distance from God, let it move you to godly sorrow. Mourn what is lost, and return to Him.
S: When the people heard that God would not go with them, they mourned and no one put on ornaments. Their grief showed they valued God's presence more than the land. If you sense distance from God, let it move you to godly sorrow and return to Him.
P: Godly sorrow. Exodus 33:4: 'when the people heard these evil tidings, they mourned.' They grieved losing God's presence. Let distance from God lead you back to Him.

== 33:5
T: Put off thy ornaments
R: 'For the LORD had said unto Moses, Say unto the children of Israel, Ye are a stiffnecked people… therefore now put off thy ornaments from thee, that I may know what to do unto thee.' God told Moses to tell Israel to remove their ornaments. Then He would decide what to do with them. Their response would shape what came next. God invited repentance. By removing their ornaments, they would show humility. God was giving them an opportunity. Notice that I may know what to do unto thee. God's response depended on their repentance. This does not mean God was uncertain. It shows that He responds to the hearts of His people. Jeremiah 18 says, If that nation… turn from their evil, I will repent of the evil that I thought to do unto them. Repentance changes outcomes. Joel 2 says, rend your heart, and not your garments. Today, respond to God's invitation to repent. Remove whatever stands between you and Him. He waits to see your heart turn.
S: God told Moses to have Israel put off their ornaments so that He would know what to do with them. He invited repentance and responded to their hearts. Respond to God's invitation to repent and remove whatever stands between you and Him.
P: An invitation to repent. Exodus 33:5: 'put off thy ornaments from thee, that I may know what to do unto thee.' Repentance changes things. Turn your heart to God today.

== 33:6
T: Stripped at Horeb
R: 'And the children of Israel stripped themselves of their ornaments by the mount Horeb.' The Israelites removed their ornaments at Mount Horeb. They obeyed God's instruction. Their repentance was visible. Notice by the mount Horeb. At the very mountain where they had received the covenant and broken it, they now humbled themselves. The place of failure became the place of repentance. Repentance often happens where we failed. We return to the scene of our sin and turn back to God there. Peter, who denied Jesus by a fire, was restored by Jesus beside a fire in John 21. The people obeyed promptly. They did not argue or delay. Genuine repentance acts. It does not merely feel sorry; it changes behavior. Matthew 3 says, Bring forth therefore fruits meet for repentance. Today, let your repentance be real and visible. Return to where you failed, and there turn back to God.
S: The children of Israel stripped themselves of their ornaments by Mount Horeb. At the place where they had broken the covenant, they humbled themselves in visible repentance. Let your repentance be real and visible, turning back to God where you have failed.

And it came to pass, that every one which sought the LORD went out unto the tabernacle.' Moses pitched a tent outside the camp, far away, and called it the tent of meeting. Anyone who sought the LORD went out to it. Meeting God required going outside the camp. The camp had been defiled by idolatry. God's presence was now outside it. Jeremiah 29 says, ye shall seek me, and find me, when ye shall search for me with all your heart. Hebrews 13 says, Let us go forth therefore unto him without the camp. Today, seek the LORD intentionally. Leave behind what distracts you, and go out to meet Him.

They honored what Moses was doing, even if they did not go themselves. A visible life of devotion inspires others. Matthew 5 says, Let your light so shine before men. Today, remember that others watch your walk with God. Let your devotion be visible and inspiring.

== 33:11
T: Face to face as a friend
R: 'And the LORD spake unto Moses face to face, as a man speaketh unto his friend. And he turned again into the camp: but his servant Joshua, the son of Nun, a young man, departed not out of the tabernacle.' The LORD spoke with Moses face to face, as a man speaks with his friend. Then Moses returned to the camp. But Joshua, his young assistant, stayed in the tent. These are some of the most remarkable words in Scripture. God spoke with Moses as a friend. Not as master to slave or king to subject, but as friend to friend. James 2 says Abraham was called the Friend of God. Jesus said in John 15, I have called you friends. Friendship with God is possible. Notice Joshua. He stayed in the tent. He lingered in God's presence. This young man was learning to love being near God. It prepared him to lead Israel later. Today, rejoice that God invites you into friendship. And like Joshua, linger in His presence. Do not rush away.

== 33:23
T: My back parts
R: 'And I will take away mine hand, and thou shalt see my back parts: but my face shall not be seen.' After God passed by, He would remove His hand, and Moses would see His back, but not His face. Moses would see the afterglow of God's glory. Seeing God's back means seeing where He has been. Moses saw the trail of glory God left behind. Often we see God most clearly in hindsight. Looking back, we recognize His hand in our lives. Moses' request was granted, but within limits. He saw enough to be transformed, but not more than he could bear. God gives us what we can handle. First Corinthians 13 says, now we see through a glass, darkly; but then face to face. Our current view is partial. One day it will be complete. Revelation 22 promises, they shall see his face. Today, look back and see the trail of God's glory in your life. And look forward to the day when you will see Him face to face.
S: God said He would take away His hand so Moses could see His back parts, but not His face. Moses saw the trail of God's glory, enough to be transformed, while full sight awaits. Look back to see God's glory in your life and look forward to seeing Him face to face.
