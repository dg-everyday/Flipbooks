# Exodus 34: The covenant renewed; Moses' shining face

== 34:1
T: Tables like the first
R: 'And the LORD said unto Moses, Hew thee two tables of stone like unto the first: and I will write upon these tables the words that were in the first tables, which thou brakest.' God told Moses to cut two new stone tablets like the first ones. God would write the same words again. The broken covenant would be restored. This is a verse of grace. Israel had broken the covenant. Moses had broken the tablets. Yet God offered to write again. He did not abandon His people after their failure. God is the God of second chances. Jonah received a second call. Peter was restored after his denial. God gives new beginnings. Notice that Moses had to cut the tablets this time. Restoration involved human participation. Yet God still wrote the words. Lamentations 3 says His compassions fail not. Today, if you have broken something with God, know that He offers to write again. Bring Him your fresh tablets. He restores what sin has broken.
S: God told Moses to hew two new tables of stone like the first, and He would write the same words on them. After Israel broke the covenant, God offered restoration and a new beginning. If you have broken something with God, bring Him your fresh tablets, for He restores.
P: God writes again. Exodus 34:1: 'Hew thee two tables of stone like unto the first: and I will write upon these tables.' God restores what sin broke. He offers new beginnings.

== 34:2
T: Be ready in the morning
R: 'And be ready in the morning, and come up in the morning unto mount Sinai, and present thyself there to me in the top of the mount.' God told Moses to be ready in the morning, to climb Sinai, and to present himself to God at the top. A new appointment with God was set. Notice the repeated morning. Moses was to meet God at the start of the day. Fresh beginnings come with fresh mornings. Lamentations 3 says God's mercies are new every morning. Notice also present thyself. Moses was to offer himself to God. Before receiving God's word, he gave himself. Romans 12 says, present your bodies a living sacrifice. Preparation mattered. Be ready meant Moses had to prepare his heart before climbing. Meeting God is not casual. Today, be ready to meet God. Prepare your heart, present yourself to Him, and seek Him in the morning.
S: God told Moses to be ready in the morning and come up to the top of Sinai to present himself there. Fresh mornings brought a fresh meeting with God, requiring prepared hearts. Be ready to meet God, presenting yourself to Him early in the day.
P: Ready in the morning. Exodus 34:2: 'be ready in the morning… and present thyself there to me.' Prepare your heart and present yourself to God early today.

== 34:3
T: No man with thee
R: 'And no man shall come up with thee, neither let any man be seen throughout all the mount; neither let the flocks nor herds feed before that mount.' No one else was to come with Moses. No one was even to be seen on the mountain. Not even animals could graze near it. Moses was to meet God alone. Some encounters with God are meant to be solitary. Moses needed to be alone with God. No distractions, no companions, no interruptions. Jesus also sought solitude. Luke 5 says he withdrew himself into the wilderness, and prayed. Matthew 6 says, when thou prayest, enter into thy closet, and when thou hast shut thy door, pray to thy Father which is in secret. Private prayer is essential. Notice the complete separation. Even the animals were kept away. Nothing was to distract from the encounter. Today, find time to be alone with God. Shut the door on distractions. Some of your deepest encounters with Him will happen in solitude.
S: No one was to come up with Moses or be seen on the mount, and not even flocks were to graze near it. Moses was to meet God alone without distractions. Find time to be alone with God, shutting the door on distractions.

== 34:4
T: Moses rose up early
R: 'And he hewed two tables of stone like unto the first; and Moses rose up early in the morning, and went up unto mount Sinai, as the LORD had commanded him, and took in his hand the two tables of stone.' Moses cut two tablets, rose early, and climbed Sinai as God commanded. He carried the blank tablets in his hands. He came ready to receive God's word. Notice the blank tablets. Moses came with empty stones, waiting for God to write. He brought readiness to receive. Our approach to God should be similar. We come with open hearts, ready for Him to write on them. Second Corinthians 3 speaks of God writing on fleshy tables of the heart. Notice also rose up early. Moses' diligence continued. Even after the failure of the golden calf, he was eager to meet God again. Today, come to God with an open heart, ready for Him to write. And do not let past failures keep you from seeking Him eagerly.
S: Moses hewed two tables like the first, rose early and went up Sinai with the blank stones in his hand, as God commanded. He came with open readiness to receive God's word. Come to God with an open heart, ready for Him to write, and do not let past failures stop your seeking.

== 34:6
T: Merciful and gracious
R: 'And the LORD passed by before him, and proclaimed, The LORD, The LORD God, merciful and gracious, longsuffering, and abundant in goodness and truth.' As God passed by, He proclaimed: The LORD, the LORD God, merciful and gracious, slow to anger, and abounding in goodness and truth. This is God's own description of Himself. This verse is one of the most important in the Bible. It is quoted or echoed many times: in Numbers, Nehemiah, Psalms, Joel, Jonah. It became Israel's creed about God's character. Notice the order. Mercy and grace come first. After the golden calf, God led with compassion. Longsuffering means slow to anger. God is patient with His people. Abundant in goodness and truth means His love and faithfulness overflow. Psalm 103 echoes this: The LORD is merciful and gracious, slow to anger, and plenteous in mercy. Today, meditate on God's self-description. He is merciful, gracious, patient, and abundantly good. Let this shape how you see Him.
S: As the LORD passed by, He proclaimed Himself merciful and gracious, longsuffering and abundant in goodness and truth. After the golden calf, God described Himself first by mercy and grace. Meditate on God's own description of Himself and let it shape how you see Him.
P: Who God is. Exodus 34:6: 'The LORD, The LORD God, merciful and gracious, longsuffering, and abundant in goodness and truth.' Let His character shape how you see Him.

== 34:7
T: Forgiving iniquity
R: 'Keeping mercy for thousands, forgiving iniquity and transgression and sin, and that will by no means clear the guilty.' God continued: He keeps mercy for thousands and forgives iniquity, transgression, and sin. Yet He will not leave the guilty unpunished. Mercy and justice stand together in God's character. Notice three words for wrongdoing: iniquity, transgression, and sin. God forgives every kind. Iniquity is twistedness. Transgression is rebellion. Sin is missing the mark. God's forgiveness covers them all. Yet God does not clear the guilty. How can He forgive and also punish? The answer is the cross. Romans 3 says God set forth Christ to be a propitiation, that he might be just, and the justifier of him which believeth in Jesus. At the cross, guilt was punished in Christ, and sinners were forgiven. Notice mercy for thousands. His mercy reaches far beyond judgment. Today, rejoice that God forgives every kind of sin. And thank Him that justice and mercy meet perfectly at the cross.
S: God declared that He keeps mercy for thousands and forgives iniquity, transgression and sin, yet will by no means clear the guilty. Mercy and justice stand together, perfectly reconciled at the cross. Rejoice that God forgives every kind of sin and thank Him that justice and mercy meet in Christ.
P: Forgiving all sin. Exodus 34:7: 'Keeping mercy for thousands, forgiving iniquity and transgression and sin.' At the cross, justice and mercy meet. You are forgiven!

== 34:9
T: Pardon our iniquity
R: 'And he said, If now I have found grace in thy sight, O Lord, let my Lord, I pray thee, go among us; for it is a stiffnecked people; and pardon our iniquity and our sin, and take us for thine inheritance.' Moses prayed: If I have found favor, Lord, please go with us. Yes, we are stiff-necked, but forgive our sin and take us as Your own. Moses identified with the people's sin. Notice our iniquity. Moses did not say their sin. He included himself. Notice the reasoning: for it is a stiffnecked people. Moses used their stubbornness as a reason God should go with them. They needed His presence precisely because they were weak. We need God most because we are flawed. Notice take us for thine inheritance. Moses asked God to claim them as His own possession. Ephesians 1 says believers are God's inheritance. Today, ask God to go with you because you need Him. Confess honestly, and ask Him to claim you as His own.
S: Moses prayed that the Lord would go among them, though they were stiffnecked, pardon their iniquity and take them as His inheritance. He included himself in the people's sin and asked for God's presence because they needed it. Ask God to go with you because you need Him, and confess honestly.

Personal vigilance was required. Israel could not assume they were immune to temptation. The golden calf had just proven how easily they fell. A snare is a hidden trap. Compromises often look harmless at first. A small agreement, a friendly alliance. But they can lead to spiritual disaster. First Corinthians 10 says, let him that thinketh he standeth take heed lest he fall. Overconfidence leads to falling. Second Corinthians 6 warns against being unequally yoked. Today, take heed to yourself. Watch for compromises that could become snares. Do not assume you are beyond temptation.
