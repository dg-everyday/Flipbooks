# Exodus 35: The Sabbath, the willing offering and the gifted builders

== 35:1
T: That ye should do them
R: 'And Moses gathered all the congregation of the children of Israel together, and said unto them, These are the words which the LORD hath commanded, that ye should do them.' Moses gathered the whole community and said, These are the things the LORD has commanded you to do. After the covenant was renewed, it was time to act. The building of the tabernacle was about to begin. Notice that ye should do them. God's words were meant to be obeyed, not just heard. Israel had received detailed instructions. Now they had to carry them out. James 1 says, be ye doers of the word, and not hearers only. Notice also all the congregation. Everyone was included. The work of building God's dwelling belonged to the whole community. Every person had a part. Today, move from hearing to doing. Take what God has said and put it into practice. And remember that His work involves all His people together.
S: Moses gathered the whole congregation and declared the words the LORD had commanded them to do. After the covenant was renewed, it was time for everyone to act. Move from hearing God's word to doing it, remembering that His work involves all His people.
P: Hear and do. Exodus 35:1: 'These are the words which the LORD hath commanded, that ye should do them.' God's word is meant to be obeyed. Put it into practice today.

== 35:2
T: Rest first
R: 'Six days shall work be done, but on the seventh day there shall be to you an holy day, a sabbath of rest to the LORD.' Before giving the building instructions, Moses reminded the people about the Sabbath. Six days for work, the seventh for holy rest to the LORD. Even building God's house must pause for the Sabbath. Notice the order. Before the work began, Moses spoke about rest. Rest framed the work. God's priorities put worship and rest before even holy labor. This is a lesson for those who serve God. Ministry can become all-consuming. We can work so hard for God that we neglect resting in Him. Mark 6 records Jesus telling His disciples, Come ye yourselves apart into a desert place, and rest a while. Even Jesus' disciples needed rest from ministry. Today, put rest in its proper place. Let it frame your work, not be squeezed in afterward. Rest in God before working for Him.
S: Before giving the building instructions, Moses reminded Israel of the Sabbath, six days of work and a holy day of rest to the LORD. Rest framed even the holy work of building God's house. Put rest in its proper place and rest in God before working for Him.
P: Rest frames the work. Exodus 35:2: 'on the seventh day there shall be to you an holy day, a sabbath of rest to the LORD.' Rest in God before you work for Him.

== 35:3
T: No fire on the Sabbath
R: 'Ye shall kindle no fire throughout your habitations upon the sabbath day.' God commanded that no fires be kindled in their homes on the Sabbath. This included fires for work or cooking. The Sabbath was to be a day of complete rest. In that time, kindling a fire required effort. It was work. The Sabbath was meant to free people from the ordinary labors of life. Even daily chores paused. This command also applied to the craftsmen. Metalwork for the tabernacle required fire. Even this holy work had to stop. No exception was made for religious projects. Rest is not laziness. It is trust. When we stop, we declare that God sustains us, not our constant effort. Isaiah 30 says, in quietness and in confidence shall be your strength. Today, find ways to truly stop and rest. Let go of the constant labor, even good labor, and trust God to sustain you.
S: God commanded that no fire be kindled in any home on the Sabbath day. Even the metalwork for the tabernacle had to pause for complete rest. Find ways to truly stop and rest, trusting God to sustain you.
P: Truly stop. Exodus 35:3: 'Ye shall kindle no fire throughout your habitations upon the sabbath day.' Even holy work paused. Trust God enough to truly rest.
H: #DailyGrace #Exodus353 #Sabbath #Rest #Trust #BibleVerse

== 35:4
T: The thing the LORD commanded
R: 'And Moses spake unto all the congregation of the children of Israel, saying, This is the thing which the LORD commanded, saying.' Moses addressed the whole congregation again: This is what the LORD has commanded. Now came the call for offerings to build the tabernacle. Notice which the LORD commanded. Moses was clear that this request came from God. He was not raising funds for his own project. He was conveying God's will. Ministry leaders must be careful to distinguish God's call from their own ideas. When we ask others to give or serve, we should be sure it reflects God's purposes. Moses spoke with authority because he was faithful to God's word. Second Corinthians 4 says, we preach not ourselves, but Christ Jesus the Lord. Notice all the congregation again. The invitation was open to everyone. Today, when God calls you to give or serve, recognize that it comes from Him. And if you lead others, make sure your requests reflect His purposes, not your own.
S: Moses told the whole congregation, This is the thing which the LORD commanded, before calling for offerings. He made clear the request came from God, not from his own ideas. Recognize God's call when He asks you to give or serve, and lead others according to His purposes.
P: God's call, not ours. Exodus 35:4: 'This is the thing which the LORD commanded.' Moses conveyed God's will, not his own. Respond to God's call today.

== 35:5
T: A willing heart
R: 'Take ye from among you an offering unto the LORD: whosoever is of a willing heart, let him bring it, an offering of the LORD; gold, and silver, and brass.' Moses asked for an offering for the LORD from everyone with a willing heart: gold, silver, and bronze. Giving was voluntary, but it had to come from the heart. Notice whosoever is of a willing heart. God did not want forced giving. He wanted gifts that flowed from love and gratitude. Second Corinthians 9 says, not grudgingly, or of necessity: for God loveth a cheerful giver. The gold here was the same kind of gold used for the golden calf. Now it would build God's dwelling. The same resources can be used for idolatry or worship. The difference is the heart. Notice also from among you. The resources were already among the people. God had provided them. Now He invited them to give back. Today, examine your heart as you give. Let your giving be willing and joyful, flowing from gratitude.
S: Moses asked for an offering to the LORD from everyone with a willing heart: gold, silver and brass. God wanted gifts flowing from love, and the same gold once used for an idol could now build His dwelling. Let your giving be willing and joyful, flowing from a grateful heart.
P: A willing heart. Exodus 35:5: 'whosoever is of a willing heart, let him bring it.' God loves a cheerful giver. Give joyfully from a grateful heart today.

== 35:24
T: Silver, brass, and wood
R: 'Every one that did offer an offering of silver and brass brought the LORD'S offering: and every man, with whom was found shittim wood for any work of the service, brought it.' Those who offered silver or bronze brought it as the LORD's offering. And everyone who had acacia wood for the work brought it. Every resource was gathered. Notice the LORD'S offering. Once given, the gifts belonged to God. They were no longer the givers' property. They were dedicated to His service. Notice also for any work of the service. The wood would be used wherever needed. Givers did not control exactly how their gifts were used. They trusted the work to God's purposes. Generous giving releases control. We give, and we trust God and His appointed leaders to use it well. Luke 6 says, Give, and it shall be given unto you. Today, give generously and trust God with how your gifts are used. Release them to His purposes.
S: Everyone who offered silver and brass brought it as the LORD's offering, and those with shittim wood brought it for any work of the service. Once given, the gifts belonged to God and were used as needed. Give generously and trust God with how your gifts are used.
P: Release it to God. Exodus 35:24: 'brought the LORD'S offering… for any work of the service.' Once given, it's God's. Give generously and trust Him with the rest.

== 35:25
T: Wise hearted women spun
R: 'And all the women that were wise hearted did spin with their hands, and brought that which they had spun, both of blue, and of purple, and of scarlet, and of fine linen.' All the skilled women spun yarn with their hands and brought blue, purple, scarlet, and fine linen. They contributed both materials and labor. Their skill became part of God's dwelling. Notice did spin with their hands. These women did not just give materials. They gave their work, their time, and their skill. Their hands were part of building God's house. Women's skilled work is honored here. Proverbs 31 praises the woman who layeth her hands to the spindle. God values the work of women's hands. Notice wise hearted again. Skill came from a heart shaped by wisdom. Their craftsmanship was an act of worship. Today, offer the work of your hands to God. Whatever your skill, it can be part of building His kingdom.
S: All the wise hearted women spun with their hands and brought blue, purple, scarlet and fine linen. They gave their time, labor and skill, which became part of God's dwelling. Offer the work of your hands to God, for your skill can help build His kingdom.

== 35:26

It was not glamorous, but it was essential. Notice that spinning goat hair was less prestigious than spinning fine linen. Yet the women did it willingly. Their hearts were stirred to do humble work. Willingness to do humble tasks reflects a servant's heart. Jesus washed His disciples' feet, a servant's task, in John 13. He said, I have given you an example. Notice whose heart stirred them up in wisdom. Even humble work was guided by wisdom and inspired by the heart. It was not menial in God's eyes. Today, embrace humble service. Do the unglamorous tasks with a willing heart. In God's house, nothing done in love is beneath us.

== 35:27

R: 'And the rulers brought onyx stones, and stones to be set, for the ephod, and for the breastplate.' The leaders brought onyx and precious stones for the ephod and breastpiece. Those with the most resources gave the most valuable gifts. Leaders set an example of generosity. Notice the rulers gave. Leadership involves leading in generosity. Notice that they gave the stones for the high priest. These stones would bear the names of the tribes. Leaders contributed to the representation of the whole people before God. Today, if you lead in any way, lead in generosity. Model the giving and service you hope others will practice.

== 35:29
T: A willing offering
R: 'The children of Israel brought a willing offering unto the LORD, every man and woman, whose heart made them willing to bring for all manner of work.' The Israelites brought a freewill offering to the LORD. Every man and woman whose heart made them willing gave for the work God commanded. Generosity flowed throughout the community. This verse summarizes the giving. It was willing, it was from both men and women, and it came from the heart. The whole community participated. Notice the repeated word willing. It appears again and again in this chapter. God emphasized that the tabernacle was built by willing hearts, not forced labor. This is the opposite of Egypt. In Egypt, Israel built with forced labor for Pharaoh. Now they built willingly for God. Freedom transformed their work. Romans 6 says, being then made free from sin, ye became the servants of righteousness. Today, serve God willingly. Let your freedom in Christ make your service joyful, not forced.
S: The children of Israel, every man and woman whose heart made them willing, brought a willing offering to the LORD for all the work. Unlike forced labor in Egypt, they now built freely for God. Serve God willingly and let your freedom in Christ make your service joyful.
P: Willing, not forced. Exodus 35:29: 'The children of Israel brought a willing offering unto the LORD.' Once slaves, now free givers. Serve God joyfully today.

Ephesians 4 says, unto every one of us is given grace according to the measure of the gift of Christ. Every gift is from grace. Today, thank God for the wisdom He places in your heart.
