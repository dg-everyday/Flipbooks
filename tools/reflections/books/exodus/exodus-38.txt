# Exodus 38: The altar, laver and court made; the sum of the materials

== 38:1
T: The altar of burnt offering
R: 'And he made the altar of burnt offering of shittim wood: five cubits was the length thereof, and five cubits the breadth thereof; it was foursquare.' Bezalel made the altar of burnt offering from acacia wood. The altar stood in the courtyard, the first object encountered after entering the gate. Before anyone could approach God, they faced the place of sacrifice. Atonement came first. This is the order of the gospel. We cannot come to God without first coming to the cross. Hebrews 13 says, We have an altar. Christ's cross is our altar. His sacrifice opens the way. Notice foursquare. The altar was equal on all sides. Some see this as a picture of the gospel's reach to every direction. Christ died for people from every nation. Revelation 5 says He redeemed us to God by thy blood out of every kindred, and tongue, and people, and nation. Today, come to God through the cross. It is the first step and the only way.
S: Bezaleel made the altar of burnt offering of shittim wood, five cubits long and broad and foursquare. It was the first object met in the court, showing that atonement came first. Come to God through the cross, the first step and the only way.
P: The cross first. Exodus 38:1: 'he made the altar of burnt offering of shittim wood… it was foursquare.' Before approaching God, we come to the cross. Start there today.

== 38:2
T: Overlaid with brass
R: 'And he made the horns thereof on the four corners of it; the horns thereof were of the same: and he overlaid it with brass.' Horns were made on the altar's four corners, of one piece with it. The altar was overlaid with bronze. It was built to withstand fire. Notice brass, or bronze. Gold was used inside, near God's presence. Bronze was used outside, at the place of sacrifice. Bronze could withstand intense heat. The altar would burn constantly. Bronze in Scripture often represents judgment. The altar was where sin was judged through sacrifice. The fire of God's justice fell on the offering. Christ bore that fire for us. Isaiah 53 says, he was wounded for our transgressions, he was bruised for our iniquities. The judgment that should have fallen on us fell on Him. The horns represented strength. At the place of judgment, there was also refuge and power. Today, thank Christ for bearing the fire of judgment. At His cross, you find both justice satisfied and refuge offered.
S: Bezaleel made horns on the altar's four corners of the same piece and overlaid it with brass. Bronze withstood fire and pictured judgment borne at the place of sacrifice, as Christ bore it for us. Thank Christ for bearing the fire of judgment, and find refuge at His cross.
P: He bore the fire. Exodus 38:2: 'he overlaid it with brass.' Bronze withstood the flames of judgment. Christ bore that fire for you. Find refuge at His cross.

== 38:3
T: All the vessels of brass
R: 'And he made all the vessels of the altar, the pots, and the shovels, and the basons, and the fleshhooks, and the firepans: all the vessels thereof made he of brass.' Bezalel made all the altar's utensils of bronze: pots, shovels, basins, meat forks, and firepans. These tools would be used daily in the work of sacrifice. Notice the practical tools. Sacrifice involved real work: removing ashes, collecting blood, handling meat, carrying fire. The tools reflected the reality of what happened at the altar. Christ's sacrifice was also real and costly. It was not symbolic only. He truly suffered and died. Hebrews 9 says Christ was once offered to bear the sins of many. Notice that all the vessels matched the altar. Everything connected to sacrifice was bronze. Consistency marked the design. Today, remember that Christ's sacrifice was real and costly. Do not take it lightly. And serve faithfully, even in the humble, practical tasks of God's work.
S: Bezaleel made all the altar's vessels of brass, its pots, shovels, basons, fleshhooks and firepans. These practical tools reflected the real and costly work of sacrifice. Remember that Christ's sacrifice was real and costly, and serve faithfully even in humble tasks.

== 38:4
T: The brasen grate
R: 'And he made for the altar a brasen grate of network under the compass thereof beneath unto the midst of it.' Bezalel made a bronze grate of network for the altar, placed beneath the ledge, halfway up. The sacrifice would rest on this grate in the fire. Notice unto the midst of it. The grate was at the center of the altar. The sacrifice rested at the heart of the fire. It bore the full heat. This pictures Christ at the center of God's plan, bearing the full weight of judgment. He did not take partial punishment. He bore it all. First Peter 2 says, who his own self bare our sins in his own body on the tree. The network design let air flow, keeping the fire hot. The sacrifice was completely consumed. Nothing was held back. Christ's sacrifice was complete. Today, rest in the completeness of Christ's sacrifice. He bore all your sin. Nothing remains for you to pay.
S: Bezaleel made a brasen grate of network for the altar, reaching to the midst of it. The sacrifice rested at the center and bore the full heat, picturing Christ bearing all our sin. Rest in the completeness of Christ's sacrifice, for nothing remains for you to pay.

Unlike the gold-covered poles for the inner furniture, these were bronze. They matched the altar they carried. Notice the consistency of materials. The inner furniture had gold poles. The outer altar had bronze poles. Each matched its function and location. God's design was orderly. This reminds us that God assigns different roles in different places. Some serve in highly visible ways; others serve in the outer court of practical needs. Both matter. First Corinthians 12 says, the members should have the same care one for another. Notice the bronze. The poles that carried the altar of sacrifice were themselves associated with judgment. Those who carry the message of the cross bear something weighty. Today, carry the message of Christ's sacrifice with seriousness and joy. It is weighty, but it brings life.

== 38:9
T: The court of fine linen
R: 'And he made the court: on the south side southward the hangings of the court were of fine twined linen, an hundred cubits.' Bezalel made the courtyard. The south side had curtains of fine linen, one hundred cubits long. The boundary of God's dwelling was formed. Notice fine twined linen. The courtyard walls were white linen, representing righteousness and purity. Revelation 19 says fine linen is the righteousness of saints. God's dwelling was surrounded by purity. Anyone approaching saw a wall of white. It declared that God is holy. Only through the proper entrance could one come in. Holiness defined the boundary. Psalm 93 says, holiness becometh thine house, O LORD, for ever. Yet the walls were not so high that God's presence was hidden. The cloud above was visible to all. God is holy but not hidden. Today, honor God's holiness. And rejoice that through Christ's righteousness, you may enter His courts.
