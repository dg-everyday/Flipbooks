# Exodus 39:1-21: The priestly garments made

== 39:1
T: As the LORD commanded
R: 'And of the blue, and purple, and scarlet, they made cloths of service, to do service in the holy place, and made the holy garments for Aaron; as the LORD commanded Moses.' The workers used blue, purple, and scarlet yarn to make the woven garments for service and the holy garments for Aaron. They did it as the LORD commanded Moses. This phrase will be repeated throughout the chapter. Notice as the LORD commanded Moses. This refrain appears again and again in chapter 39. It emphasizes complete obedience. Every garment was made exactly according to God's word. After the failure of the golden calf, this repeated obedience is beautiful. The people who had turned aside now followed God's instructions precisely. Restoration was shown through faithful action. First Samuel 15 says, to obey is better than sacrifice. God values obedience above impressive gifts. Today, let your life echo this refrain. Do things as the LORD commands. Let obedience be the pattern of your restored life.
S: The workers made the cloths of service and the holy garments for Aaron as the LORD commanded Moses. This refrain of complete obedience runs through the chapter, showing a restored people following God's word. Let as the LORD commanded be the pattern of your life.
P: As the LORD commanded. Exodus 39:1: 'made the holy garments for Aaron; as the LORD commanded Moses.' After failure, faithful obedience. Let this refrain describe your life.

== 39:2
T: The ephod made
R: 'And he made the ephod of gold, blue, and purple, and scarlet, and fine twined linen.' The ephod was made of gold, blue, purple, scarlet, and fine twisted linen. The high priest's central garment was complete. It combined all the precious materials. The ephod united the colors of the tabernacle. Gold, blue, purple, scarlet, and white were woven together. The high priest wore the colors of God's dwelling. He belonged to the sanctuary. Christ, our High Priest, embodies every quality these colors represent. Divine like gold, heavenly like blue, royal like purple, sacrificial like scarlet, pure like linen. Hebrews 7 says such an high priest became us, who is holy, harmless, undefiled. He is everything we need in a priest. Notice he made. Skilled hands crafted the garment. Faithful work honored God's design. Today, rejoice that Christ is the perfect High Priest. Every quality you need, He embodies fully.
S: The ephod was made of gold, blue, purple, scarlet and fine twined linen, uniting the tabernacle's colors. The high priest wore what pictured Christ's divine, heavenly, royal, sacrificial and pure character. Rejoice that Christ is the perfect High Priest who embodies every quality you need.

== 39:3
T: Gold beaten into wires
R: 'And they did beat the gold into thin plates, and cut it into wires, to work it in the blue, and in the purple, and in the scarlet, and in the fine linen, with cunning work.' The workers hammered gold into thin sheets and cut it into threads. They wove the gold into the blue, purple, scarlet, and fine linen. Gold does not naturally become thread. It had to be beaten thin and cut into fine strands. Then it was woven into fabric. Gold became part of the cloth itself. This pictures God weaving His glory into our lives. Not as an external decoration, but woven into the fabric of who we are. Second Peter 1 says we are partakers of the divine nature. Notice did beat the gold. Gold was hammered before it was woven. Pressure prepared it. God sometimes shapes us through hard seasons so His glory can be woven in. Today, trust God's shaping process. He is weaving His glory into the fabric of your life.
S: The workers beat gold into thin plates, cut it into wires and wove it into the blue, purple, scarlet and fine linen with skill. Gold became part of the fabric itself after being hammered thin. Trust God's shaping, for He is weaving His glory into the fabric of your life.
P: Glory woven in. Exodus 39:3: 'they did beat the gold into thin plates, and cut it into wires, to work it in.' God weaves His glory into who you are. Trust His process.

== 39:4
T: Shoulderpieces coupled
R: 'They made shoulderpieces for it, to couple it together: by the two edges was it coupled together.' The workers made shoulder pieces to join the ephod's front and back. The two edges were coupled together. The garment was unified. Shoulders carry burdens. The shoulder pieces would hold the stones bearing Israel's names. The priest would carry the people on his shoulders. Christ carries His people. Luke 15 describes the shepherd who finds the lost sheep and layeth it on his shoulders, rejoicing. Isaiah 46 says, I have made, and I will bear; even I will carry, and will deliver you. Notice coupled together. The front and back were joined. The garment was one. A priest with a divided heart cannot carry others well. Today, rest on Christ's shoulders. He carries you with strength and joy. And let your heart be whole as you carry others in prayer.

The girdle, or belt, represents readiness for service. A girded priest was prepared to work. Jesus girded Himself with a towel in John 13 and washed the disciples' feet. True greatness serves. == 39:6

R: 'And they wrought onyx stones inclosed in ouches of gold, graven, as signets are graven, with the names of the children of Israel.' The workers set onyx stones in gold settings and engraved them like seals with the names of Israel's sons. Every name was carved permanently. A signet was a personal seal of ownership. Engraving names like a signet meant God claimed them. They were His, marked permanently. Isaiah 49 says, Behold, I have graven thee upon the palms of my hands. God's people are engraved, not written in fading ink. Notice inclosed in ouches of gold. The stones were held securely in gold. They would not fall. God holds His people firmly. John 10 says, neither shall any man pluck them out of my hand. Today, rejoice that your name is engraved before God. You are sealed as His own, held securely in His hands.

R: 'It was foursquare; they made the breastplate double: a span was the length thereof, and a span the breadth thereof, being doubled.' The extra layer added strength. It could hold precious stones and the Urim and Thummim securely. What it carried was too valuable to risk. Love must be strong to carry others well.
