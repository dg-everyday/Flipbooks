# Exodus 39:22-43: The robe, the holy crown, and the work finished and blessed

== 39:22
T: Woven all of blue
R: 'And he made the robe of the ephod of woven work, all of blue.' The robe worn under the ephod was woven entirely of blue. It was one solid color, the color of heaven. The high priest was clothed in heavenly blue. Notice all of blue. Not mixed with other colors. The robe was wholly devoted to one color. It declared that the priest belonged entirely to heaven's service. Undivided devotion is God's desire. James 1 warns that a double minded man is unstable in all his ways. Matthew 6 says, No man can serve two masters. The robe of blue pictured a heart fully given to God. Notice woven work. The robe was woven as one piece. John 19 records that Jesus' coat was without seam, woven from the top throughout. Christ, our High Priest, wore a seamless garment. Today, let your devotion be undivided. Belong wholly to God, as the blue robe belonged wholly to heaven.
S: The robe of the ephod was made of woven work, all of blue. It was wholly devoted to heaven's color, picturing undivided devotion. Let your devotion to God be undivided and belong wholly to Him.
P: Wholly His. Exodus 39:22: 'he made the robe of the ephod of woven work, all of blue.' One color, undivided. Give God your whole heart today.

== 39:23
T: That it should not rend
R: 'And there was an hole in the midst of the robe, as the hole of an habergeon, with a band round about the hole, that it should not rend.' The robe had an opening for the head, reinforced with a woven band like a coat of mail, so it would not tear. The high priest's robe was to remain whole. Notice that it should not rend. The robe was protected from tearing. Its integrity mattered. Weak points were reinforced. At Jesus' trial, Matthew 26 says the high priest Caiaphas rent his clothes. He tore what was meant to stay whole. Meanwhile, John 19 records that Jesus' seamless coat was not torn. The soldiers cast lots for it. Jesus, the true High Priest, kept His garment whole. Notice the reinforcement around the opening. God strengthens our weak places. Second Corinthians 12 says His strength is made perfect in weakness. Today, trust God to reinforce your weak points. And rejoice in Christ, the true High Priest, whose integrity remained whole.
S: The robe had an opening reinforced with a band like an habergeon so that it would not rend. The high priest's robe was protected at its weak point, and Jesus' seamless coat was not torn. Trust God to strengthen your weak places and rejoice in Christ, the true High Priest.
P: Not torn. Exodus 39:23: 'with a band round about the hole, that it should not rend.' God reinforces weak places. Christ's seamless coat stayed whole. Trust Him today.
H: #DailyGrace #Exodus3923 #Strength #Integrity #Christ #BibleVerse

== 39:24
T: Pomegranates on the hem
R: 'And they made upon the hems of the robe pomegranates of blue, and purple, and scarlet, and twined linen.' Pomegranates of blue, purple, scarlet, and twisted linen were made on the robe's hem. They decorated the bottom of the priest's garment. Fruit adorned his steps. Pomegranates are full of seeds. They represent fruitfulness and abundance. The priest's robe was edged with fruit. His ministry was meant to bear fruit. Jesus said in John 15, Herein is my Father glorified, that ye bear much fruit. Fruitfulness is a mark of true discipleship. Galatians 5 lists the fruit of the Spirit: love, joy, peace, longsuffering, gentleness, goodness, faith, meekness, temperance. Notice on the hems. The fruit was at the bottom, near the feet. Fruitfulness follows our steps. Where we walk, fruit should appear. Today, let your life bear fruit. Let love, joy, and peace follow your steps wherever you go.
S: Pomegranates of blue, purple, scarlet and twined linen were made on the hems of the robe. Fruit adorned the priest's steps, picturing a fruitful ministry. Let your life bear the fruit of the Spirit wherever you walk.

R: 'And they made bells of pure gold, and put the bells between the pomegranates upon the hem of the robe, round about between the pomegranates.' Bells of pure gold were made and placed between the pomegranates on the hem. Fruit and sound alternated around the robe. The priest's movements would be heard. Bells made sound. As the priest moved, they rang. His service was audible. People outside could hear that he was ministering. The bells were between the pomegranates. Fruit and sound together. Some see the bells as testimony and the pomegranates as fruit. Words and works must go together. James 2 says, faith without works is dead. And First John 3 says, let us not love in word, neither in tongue; but in deed and in truth. Notice pure gold. The bells were precious. Our testimony for God is precious. Today, let your words and works go together. Let your testimony ring true because it is backed by fruit.

Notice for Aaron, and for his sons. Both the high priest and the ordinary priests wore linen. Righteousness was required of all. Every believer is clothed in Christ's righteousness. Today, rejoice that you are clothed in the fine linen of Christ's righteousness. It is the foundation of your standing before God.
