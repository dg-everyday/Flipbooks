# Genesis 3: The fall

== 3:1
T: The first temptation
R: 'Now the serpent was more subtil than any beast of the field which the LORD God had made.' Temptation rarely announces itself honestly. It comes subtly, with a question rather than a command: 'Yea, hath God said?' The serpent did not begin by denying God outright. He began by planting doubt about God's word and twisting it. God had said they could eat freely of every tree but one. The serpent made it sound as if God had forbidden every tree. This is still the enemy's strategy. He questions what God has said, exaggerates God's restrictions, and makes us wonder whether God is really good. Jesus called him the father of lies. The best defense is to know clearly what God has actually said. When Jesus was tempted in the wilderness, He answered every lie with, It is written. Today, fill your mind with God's word, so that when doubt whispers, you can answer with the truth.
S: The serpent came with a subtle question that cast doubt on God's word. Temptation often begins by twisting what God has said and questioning His goodness. Know God's word well so you can answer lies with truth.
P: Temptation starts with a question. Genesis 3:1: 'Yea, hath God said, Ye shall not eat of every tree of the garden?' Know God's word so you can stand firm against doubt.

== 3:2
T: Answering the tempter
R: 'And the woman said unto the serpent, We may eat of the fruit of the trees of the garden.' Eve began well. She corrected the serpent, affirming that God had given them freedom to eat from the trees. Yet notice that she was now in conversation with the tempter. Sometimes the danger is not in the first answer but in staying to talk. Eve engaged in a dialogue with one who meant her harm, and the longer she listened, the more the lie took hold. Scripture teaches us to resist the devil and to flee temptation, not to negotiate with it. Joseph did not debate with Potiphar's wife; he ran. Some battles are won by simply refusing to stay in the conversation. Is there a temptation you keep entertaining, turning it over in your mind, telling yourself you can handle it? Today, do not linger. State the truth, and then walk away. Guard your heart by refusing to keep the door open.
S: Eve answered the serpent truthfully but remained in conversation with him. Temptation gains ground when we linger and keep listening. Speak the truth and then walk away from what tempts you.
P: Don't stay in conversation with temptation. Genesis 3:2: 'We may eat of the fruit of the trees of the garden.' Eve knew the truth, but she kept listening. Speak truth, then walk away.

== 3:3
T: Adding to God's word
R: Eve continued: 'God hath said, Ye shall not eat of it, neither shall ye touch it, lest ye die.' God had told Adam not to eat of the tree, but He had said nothing about touching it. Somewhere along the way, the command had been altered. A small change perhaps, but a significant one. When we add to God's word, we often make Him seem harsher than He is. And when we subtract from it, we make sin seem less serious. Both are dangerous. Eve's addition made God's command sound burdensome, which played right into the serpent's hands. It is important to know exactly what God has said, no more and no less. Rules invented by people can become a heavy yoke that God never placed on us, while His true commands get lost. Today, read God's word carefully. Let it speak for itself. Ask the Holy Spirit to help you understand it rightly and to hold it faithfully.
S: Eve added to God's command, saying they could not even touch the tree. Adding to or taking from God's word distorts our view of Him. Read Scripture carefully and hold it faithfully today.
P: Know exactly what God has said. Genesis 3:3: 'Ye shall not eat of it, neither shall ye touch it, lest ye die.' God never said don't touch. Read His word carefully today.

== 3:4
T: The first lie
R: 'And the serpent said unto the woman, Ye shall not surely die.' Now the serpent moved from questioning God's word to flatly contradicting it. God had said, Thou shalt surely die. The serpent said the opposite. This is the first lie recorded in Scripture, and it is still one of the most common: sin has no real consequences. You can disobey God and be just fine. The world repeats this lie in countless forms. It promises freedom without cost and pleasure without regret. But God's word is true, and the wages of sin is death. The consequences may not be immediate, but they are real. Thankfully, the same Bible that warns of death also offers life. The gift of God is eternal life through Jesus Christ our Lord. Today, when you hear the voice that says it will not matter, remember who first spoke those words. Trust what God has said over every contradicting voice.
S: The serpent directly contradicted God by saying they would not surely die. The lie that sin has no consequences is still common today. Trust God's word above every voice that contradicts it.
P: The oldest lie: 'Ye shall not surely die.' Genesis 3:4 shows the enemy contradicting God. Sin always has consequences, but Jesus offers life. Trust God's word today.

== 3:5
T: The lie that God holds out
R: The serpent went further: 'For God doth know that in the day ye eat thereof, then your eyes shall be opened, and ye shall be as gods, knowing good and evil.' The lie was that God was holding out on them, keeping something good for Himself. The serpent painted God as jealous and restrictive, and offered something irresistible: to be like God. This is the root of all sin, the desire to be our own god, to define good and evil for ourselves. The irony is that Adam and Eve were already made in God's image. They did not need to grab at godlikeness; they already reflected Him. Jesus, by contrast, though He was God, did not grasp at equality with God but humbled Himself. Pride says, I will rise up. Humility says, I will trust. Today, examine your heart for places where you want to be in control. Surrender them to the God who is good and withholds no good thing.
S: The serpent claimed God was holding out on them and promised they would be as gods. The desire to be our own god is the root of sin. Surrender control to the God who withholds no good thing.

== 3:6
T: How sin takes hold
R: 'And when the woman saw that the tree was good for food, and that it was pleasant to the eyes, and a tree to be desired to make one wise, she took of the fruit thereof, and did eat, and gave also unto her husband with her; and he did eat.' Notice the progression: she saw, she desired, she took, she ate, she gave. Sin follows the same path today. The apostle John describes it as the lust of the flesh, the lust of the eyes, and the pride of life. Notice too that Adam was with her. He stood by silently and then joined in. Sin is rarely a solitary act; it spreads. The good news is that the pattern can be broken at any point. You can look away, refuse the desire, keep your hand back. The earlier you stop, the easier it is. Today, ask God to help you recognize the first step of temptation, and to give you strength to turn before it grows.
S: Eve saw the fruit, desired it, took it, ate it and gave some to Adam. Sin follows a progression that can be broken at any step. Ask God to help you turn away at the first sign of temptation.
P: Sin has a pattern: see, desire, take. Genesis 3:6: 'she took of the fruit thereof, and did eat, and gave also unto her husband with her.' Turn away early today.

== 3:7
T: Covering our shame
R: 'And the eyes of them both were opened, and they knew that they were naked; and they sewed fig leaves together, and made themselves aprons.' Their eyes were opened, as the serpent promised, but not to glory. They saw their shame. The first result of sin was not wisdom but exposure, and their first instinct was to cover up. Fig leaves were a human attempt to deal with shame, and they were flimsy and inadequate. We still reach for fig leaves today. We cover our guilt with excuses, busyness, achievements, religion, or distractions. But no amount of self-made covering can truly remove shame. Only God can provide a covering that works, and later in this chapter He does. Ultimately He provided it in Christ, whose righteousness covers all who trust in Him. What fig leaves have you been sewing? Today, lay them down. Bring your shame to God honestly. He offers a covering far better than anything you can make.
S: After they sinned, Adam and Eve saw their nakedness and covered themselves with fig leaves. Our human attempts to hide shame are flimsy and cannot truly remove guilt. Lay down your fig leaves and receive the covering God provides in Christ.

== 3:8
T: Hiding from God
R: 'And they heard the voice of the LORD God walking in the garden in the cool of the day: and Adam and his wife hid themselves from the presence of the LORD God amongst the trees of the garden.' This verse hints at something beautiful that had been lost. It seems God walked with them in the garden, enjoying fellowship in the cool of the evening. But now, the sound of His approach brought fear instead of joy. They hid among the very trees He had given them. Sin always drives us away from God. It makes us want to avoid prayer, skip church, and keep our distance from His word. Yet hiding never works. God sees through every tree. The good news is that He still comes looking for us. If you have been avoiding God because of guilt, know that He is not walking toward you in anger but in love. Stop hiding. Step out and meet Him.
S: When Adam and Eve heard God walking in the garden, they hid from His presence. Sin drives us away from the God who wants fellowship with us. Stop hiding today and come back into His presence.

== 3:9
T: God comes seeking
R: 'And the LORD God called unto Adam, and said unto him, Where art thou?' God knew exactly where Adam was. This question was not for God's information but for Adam's sake. It was an invitation to come out of hiding and be honest. It is also the first evidence of grace after the fall. Before any judgment was spoken, God came seeking. He did not wait for Adam to find his way back; He called out first. This is the story of the whole Bible. God pursues people who are running from Him. Jesus said He came to seek and to save that which was lost. He is the shepherd who leaves the ninety-nine to find the one. Perhaps you hear that question today: Where are you? Where are you spiritually, emotionally, in your walk with Him? Answer honestly. God is not asking to shame you. He is calling you home. Come out from behind the trees and answer Him.
S: God called to Adam with a question, not because He did not know, but to invite him out of hiding. Before any judgment, God came seeking. Answer His call honestly and come home to Him today.

== 3:10
T: Fear replaces fellowship
R: Adam answered, 'I heard thy voice in the garden, and I was afraid, because I was naked; and I hid myself.' Fear is the first emotion Adam names after the fall. Before, God's voice was a delight. Now it was a terror. Sin changed how Adam felt about God, though God Himself had not changed. That is still true. When we live in guilt, we begin to see God as someone to fear and avoid. But Scripture says that perfect love casts out fear. Through Jesus, the barrier of guilt is removed, and we can approach God with confidence instead of dread. Notice that Adam's answer was partly honest. He admitted his fear and his hiding, even though he did not yet confess his sin. Honesty is the first step back. Today, tell God how you really feel. If you are afraid, say so. If you have been hiding, admit it. He can handle your honesty, and His love can drive out your fear.
S: Adam admitted that he was afraid and had hidden himself because he was naked. Sin turned fellowship with God into fear. Be honest with God today and let His love drive out your fear.

== 3:11
T: An invitation to confess
R: 'And he said, Who told thee that thou wast naked? Hast thou eaten of the tree, whereof I commanded thee that thou shouldest not eat?' God asked two questions, both designed to lead Adam to confession. God already knew the answers. He was giving Adam the opportunity to own what he had done. This is how God deals with His children. He does not simply expose us; He invites us to come clean. Confession is not about informing God of something He does not know. It is about agreeing with Him about our sin. John writes that if we confess our sins, He is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness. Confession opens the door to forgiveness. Is there something God has been gently asking you about? A question He keeps bringing to your mind? Do not deflect. Answer Him truthfully. His questions are invitations to freedom, and His forgiveness is waiting on the other side.

== 3:12
T: The blame game
R: 'And the man said, The woman whom thou gavest to be with me, she gave me of the tree, and I did eat.' Instead of confessing, Adam shifted the blame. First to the woman, and then, subtly, to God Himself: the woman whom thou gavest me. Only at the very end did he admit, I did eat. Blame-shifting is as old as sin. We blame our upbringing, our circumstances, our spouse, our boss, or even God for the choices we make. It feels safer than taking responsibility, but it keeps us stuck. As long as someone else is at fault, we cannot be healed. The path to freedom always runs through honest ownership. David prayed, I acknowledge my transgression: and my sin is ever before me. That is the posture God blesses. Today, notice any place where you have been pointing the finger. Take responsibility for your part. Grace meets us not in our excuses but in our honesty.
S: Adam blamed the woman and even God for his choice to eat. Blame-shifting keeps us stuck and prevents healing. Take honest responsibility for your choices and receive God's grace.

== 3:13
T: Deceived but responsible
R: 'And the LORD God said unto the woman, What is this that thou hast done? And the woman said, The serpent beguiled me, and I did eat.' Eve followed Adam's lead and pointed to the serpent. Her words contained truth; she had been deceived. Paul later confirms that the serpent beguiled Eve through his subtlety. Yet being deceived did not erase her responsibility. She still chose to eat. We live in a world full of deception, and much of it is skillful. But God holds us responsible to seek truth and to obey what He has said. The good news is that He also provides what we need to resist deception: His word, His Spirit, and the counsel of other believers. Notice again that Eve ends with the same words as Adam: I did eat. Both finally admitted the act. That small admission was a beginning. Today, ask God to protect you from deception and to give you a teachable heart that quickly admits wrong.
S: Eve said the serpent had deceived her, and she ate. Being deceived does not remove our responsibility to obey God. Ask Him for discernment and a heart that quickly admits wrong.

== 3:14

R: 'And the LORD God said unto the serpent, Because thou hast done this, thou art cursed above all cattle, and above every beast of the field; upon thy belly shalt thou go, and dust shalt thou eat all the days of thy life.' God did not question the serpent as He did Adam and Eve. There was no invitation to confess, only judgment. The deceiver who sought to lift humanity up in pride was himself brought low, condemned to crawl in the dust. Evil does not have the last word in God's world. Those who oppose Him will be humbled. This verse reminds us that God sees every deception and will judge it. When you see evil seem to prosper, remember that its end is already decided. The serpent may strike, but he is a defeated foe. Revelation describes that old serpent being cast down forever. Take courage today. The enemy of your soul is under God's judgment, and his time is short.

== 3:15
T: The first promise of the Savior
R: In the middle of judgment, God spoke the first promise of the gospel: 'And I will put enmity between thee and the woman, and between thy seed and her seed; it shall bruise thy head, and thou shalt bruise his heel.' A descendant of the woman would come and crush the serpent's head, though He Himself would be wounded. This is the first glimmer of Jesus in Scripture. On the cross, the serpent struck the Savior's heel, but through that very suffering, Christ crushed the power of sin, death, and the devil. Notice that God made this promise before He announced the consequences to Adam and Eve. Hope came before the curse was spoken. That is the heart of God. He did not wait to plan our rescue. From the moment we fell, He promised to save us. Whatever failure you are facing today, remember that God answers sin with a promise. The Savior came, and the serpent's head is crushed.

== 3:16
T: Pain in a broken world
R: 'Unto the woman he said, I will greatly multiply thy sorrow and thy conception; in sorrow thou shalt bring forth children; and thy desire shall be to thy husband, and he shall rule over thee.' Sin brought pain into the most precious parts of life. Childbirth, meant to be pure joy, would now come with sorrow. Marriage, designed as harmonious partnership, would now know tension and struggle for control. This is not how God made it to be. The brokenness we experience in families and relationships is a result of the fall, not God's original design. Yet God does not leave us there. In Christ, husbands are called to love their wives as Christ loved the church, and wives and husbands are called to serve one another. Grace begins to restore what sin damaged. And every child born, despite the pain, is a sign that life goes on under God's mercy. Today, pray for healing in your relationships where sin has left its mark.

== 3:17
T: Toil under the curse
R: To Adam God said, 'Because thou hast hearkened unto the voice of thy wife, and hast eaten of the tree… cursed is the ground for thy sake; in sorrow shalt thou eat of it all the days of thy life.' Adam's sin was listening to another voice above God's voice. The result was that work, once a joyful calling, would now be marked by frustration. Notice that God cursed the ground, not Adam himself. Even in judgment there was restraint. Still, the effects are familiar to all of us: work that feels futile, effort that does not seem to pay off, labor that wears us down. Paul writes that the whole creation groans, waiting to be set free. Yet he also says that our labor is not in vain in the Lord. When we work for Christ, our efforts carry eternal value. Today, if your work feels heavy, offer it to God. He sees your labor, and He gives it meaning beyond the struggle.

== 3:18
T: Thorns and the crown
R: 'Thorns also and thistles shall it bring forth to thee; and thou shalt eat the herb of the field.' The ground that once yielded only good things would now produce thorns. Thorns became a symbol of the curse, of everything that hurts, frustrates, and resists our efforts. Yet consider this: when Jesus went to the cross, soldiers twisted thorns into a crown and pressed it on His head. The very symbol of the curse was placed on the Savior. Galatians tells us that Christ redeemed us from the curse, being made a curse for us. He wore our thorns so that we could receive His blessing. That does not mean the thorns of this life disappear immediately. We still face difficulties, pain, and frustration. But they no longer have the final word. One day the curse will be lifted entirely. Today, whatever thorns you are dealing with, remember the One who wore them for you. He understands your pain.

Death entered the world through sin, and every human life now ends in the grave. The body made from dust returns to dust. It is wise to remember our mortality. Moses prayed, So teach us to number our days, that we may apply our hearts unto wisdom. Life is short, and our time is precious. Yet for those who trust in Christ, dust is not the end. Jesus rose from the dead as the firstfruits of a great resurrection. Paul writes that what is sown in corruption is raised in incorruption. The same God who formed Adam from dust can raise us from it again. Today, live with eternity in view. Spend your days on what will last, and rest in the hope of resurrection.

Yet he gave his wife a name full of life. The name Eve is connected to the Hebrew word for living. It seems Adam believed the promise God had spoken, that her seed would one day crush the serpent. In the shadow of death, Adam chose hope. That is what faith looks like. It does not deny the reality of loss, but it holds on to God's promise. Faith names the future according to what God has said, not according to present circumstances. You may be facing something that feels like death today: a loss, a disappointment, a diagnosis. == 3:21

Adam and Eve had tried to cover themselves with fig leaves, but God replaced their flimsy coverings with garments He made Himself. To make coats of skins, an animal had to die. For the first time, blood was shed to cover human shame. This points forward to the whole system of sacrifice and ultimately to Jesus, the Lamb of God, whose death covers our sin. Isaiah says He has clothed me with the garments of salvation, He hath covered me with the robe of righteousness. We cannot clothe ourselves before God; only He can do it, and it costs a life. Today, thank God that He has not left you in your fig leaves. Through Christ, He has clothed you with His righteousness. You can stand before Him, covered by His grace.

But consider what living forever in a fallen state would mean: endless life in sin, pain, and separation from God, with no hope of redemption. Barring the way to the tree of life was an act of mercy. God would not allow humanity to be locked forever in brokenness. He had a better plan, one that would lead through death to resurrection. The tree of life was not destroyed. It reappears in Revelation, in the city of God, where its leaves are for the healing of the nations. What we lost in Eden will be restored in Christ. Sometimes God's no is His mercy. == 3:23
T: Sent out, not forgotten
R: 'Therefore the LORD God sent him forth from the garden of Eden, to till the ground from whence he was taken.' Adam left paradise. The consequences of sin were real, and the garden was closed to him. Yet notice that God did not send him away without purpose. He sent him forth to work the ground. Life outside Eden was harder, but it was not meaningless. God remained with Adam and Eve, and the story continues with children, worship, and the unfolding of God's promise. Many people live with the consequences of past choices. Some doors have closed, and some losses cannot be undone. But consequences are not the end of your story. God is still at work. He gives purpose even in the place where your choices have led you. The prodigal son eventually came home, and the father ran to meet him. Today, do not let regret paralyze you. Take up the work God gives you right where you are.

== 3:24
T: The way to life reopened
R: 'So he drove out the man; and he placed at the east of the garden of Eden Cherubim, and a flaming sword which turned every way, to keep the way of the tree of life.' The way back to Eden was guarded. No human effort could force a way past the cherubim and the flaming sword. Humanity was shut out from the presence of God and the tree of life. Later, cherubim were woven into the veil of the tabernacle and temple, guarding the way into the Most Holy Place. Year after year, that veil reminded Israel that the way to God was closed. But when Jesus died on the cross, the veil of the temple was torn in two from top to bottom. The way was opened. Jesus said, I am the way, the truth, and the life. Through Him, we have access to God again. Today, rejoice that the flaming sword no longer bars your way. Jesus has opened the door to God's presence.
