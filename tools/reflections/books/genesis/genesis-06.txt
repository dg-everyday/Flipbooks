# Genesis 6: Corruption and the call to build the ark

== 6:1
T: Growth without godliness
R: 'And it came to pass, when men began to multiply on the face of the earth, and daughters were born unto them.' Humanity was fulfilling God's command to be fruitful and multiply. The earth was filling with people. On the surface, this looks like blessing. Yet the verses that follow show that while the population grew, wickedness grew even faster. More people did not mean more godliness. Growth by itself is not a sign of God's approval. A church, a business, or a nation can grow in numbers while declining in faithfulness. God is more interested in the condition of hearts than in the size of crowds. Jesus warned that in the last days, as it was in the days of Noah, people would go on with ordinary life, eating and drinking and marrying, unaware of coming judgment. Today, do not measure success merely by size or outward growth. Ask whether your life, your family, and your church are growing in holiness and love for God.
S: As humanity multiplied on the earth, wickedness multiplied as well. Outward growth is not the same as spiritual health. Measure your life today by growth in holiness, not just in size or success.
P: Growth isn't always godliness. Genesis 6:1: 'when men began to multiply on the face of the earth.' Wickedness grew with them. Seek growth in holiness today.

== 6:2
T: Choosing by sight alone
R: 'That the sons of God saw the daughters of men that they were fair; and they took them wives of all which they chose.' Bible teachers have long debated who the sons of God were here. Some believe they were angelic beings; many others believe they were men from the godly line of Seth who married women from the ungodly line of Cain. Whatever the precise meaning, the pattern is clear and familiar. They saw, they found attractive, and they took whomever they chose. It echoes Eve, who saw that the fruit was pleasant to the eyes and took it. Choices were driven by appearance and desire rather than by God's will. Decisions about relationships are among the most important we make. When we choose based only on outward attraction and personal desire, without seeking God's wisdom, we can be led far from Him. Paul urges believers not to be unequally yoked. Today, whatever choices you face, seek God's guidance first. Look beyond what is pleasing to the eye to what pleases Him.
S: The sons of God chose wives based on appearance and their own desires. When choices are driven only by sight and desire, they can lead us away from God. Seek God's wisdom first in every important decision.
P: Not every attractive choice is a wise one. Genesis 6:2: 'they took them wives of all which they chose.' Seek God's guidance before following your desires.

== 6:3
T: God's patience has limits
R: 'And the LORD said, My spirit shall not always strive with man, for that he also is flesh: yet his days shall be an hundred and twenty years.' God's Spirit had been striving with humanity, pleading, convicting, and restraining. But God declared that this would not go on forever. Many understand the hundred and twenty years as a period of grace before the flood, a final window for repentance. God is patient, but His patience has a purpose and a limit. He waits so that people might turn to Him. Peter writes that the longsuffering of God waited in the days of Noah, while the ark was a preparing. The Holy Spirit still strives with hearts today, convicting of sin and drawing people to Christ. If you sense Him tugging at your heart, do not harden yourself. Hebrews urges, To day if ye will hear his voice, harden not your hearts. God's patience is a gift, not a guarantee of unlimited time. Respond to Him while He is calling.
S: God declared that His Spirit would not strive with humanity forever, giving a period of grace before judgment. His patience is generous but has a purpose and a limit. Respond today when His Spirit speaks to your heart.
P: God is patient, but His patience has a purpose. Genesis 6:3: 'My spirit shall not always strive with man.' If He's calling you today, don't wait. Respond now.

== 6:4
T: Renown without righteousness
R: 'There were giants in the earth in those days… the same became mighty men which were of old, men of renown.' The world before the flood had its heroes: powerful, famous, and celebrated. They were men of renown, literally men of name. Yet these famous figures are not remembered for righteousness. Their fame did not save them from judgment. The world has always been impressed by strength, power, and celebrity. We tend to admire those who are mighty and well known. But God measures greatness differently. In the very next chapters, the man God chose was not one of these mighty men, but Noah, a righteous man who walked with God. Jesus said that whoever wants to be great must be the servant of all. Seeking a name for ourselves leads nowhere lasting. Seeking to honor God's name leads to eternal reward. Today, do not chase renown. Pursue righteousness, humility, and faithfulness. God sees and honors what the world often ignores.
S: The world before the flood had mighty men of renown, but their fame did not make them righteous. God measures greatness by faithfulness, not power or celebrity. Pursue righteousness rather than renown today.
P: Fame doesn't impress God. Genesis 6:4 mentions 'mighty men which were of old, men of renown.' But God chose humble, faithful Noah. Seek His approval, not the world's.

== 6:5
T: The heart of the problem
R: 'And GOD saw that the wickedness of man was great in the earth, and that every imagination of the thoughts of his heart was only evil continually.' This is one of the darkest descriptions of humanity in the Bible. Not just some actions, but every imagination of the thoughts of the heart was only evil, and continually so. God looked beneath the surface to the heart. Sin is not merely a matter of outward behavior; it begins inside, in our thoughts and desires. Jeremiah later wrote, The heart is deceitful above all things, and desperately wicked. Jesus taught that out of the heart proceed evil thoughts and every kind of sin. This is why external reforms are never enough. We need new hearts. God promised through Ezekiel, A new heart also will I give you, and a new spirit will I put within you. That is exactly what He does through Christ. Today, ask God to search your heart, and to renew your thoughts by His Spirit.
S: God saw that human wickedness was great and that every thought of the heart was only evil continually. Sin begins in the heart, not just in outward actions. Ask God to search your heart and give you a new one through Christ.
P: Sin starts in the heart. Genesis 6:5: 'every imagination of the thoughts of his heart was only evil continually.' We need new hearts, and God gives them in Christ.

== 6:6
T: God's grief over sin
R: 'And it repented the LORD that he had made man on the earth, and it grieved him at his heart.' This verse reveals something profound about God: He feels grief. Sin is not merely a broken rule to Him; it is a wound to His heart. The word repented here does not mean that God made a mistake or changed His mind like a fickle person. It expresses deep sorrow at what humanity had become. God is not a distant, unfeeling force. He loves His creation and is pained when it turns to evil. Ephesians warns us, And grieve not the holy Spirit of God. Our sin affects God personally. That truth should humble us, but it should also comfort us. A God who grieves over sin is a God who loves deeply. His grief is the flip side of His love. Jesus wept over Jerusalem because He longed to gather its children. Today, consider how your choices affect the heart of God. Let His love move you to turn from sin.
S: God was grieved in His heart over the wickedness of humanity. Sin is not just a broken rule but a wound to His loving heart. Let His grief over sin move you to turn toward Him in love.
P: Our sin grieves God's heart. Genesis 6:6: 'it grieved him at his heart.' He's not a distant force but a loving Father. Let His love draw you back to Him today.

== 6:7
T: The certainty of judgment
R: 'And the LORD said, I will destroy man whom I have created from the face of the earth; both man, and beast, and the creeping thing, and the fowls of the air.' God's grief led to a decision. Judgment was coming. This is a hard verse, and we should not soften it. God is holy, and He will not tolerate evil forever. A God who never judged evil would not be good; He would be indifferent to injustice and suffering. The flood shows that God takes sin seriously. Yet even here, God's judgment was not hasty. He had waited patiently, His Spirit had striven, and He would still provide a way of escape. The New Testament points to the flood as a warning of the final judgment to come. Peter writes that the heavens and the earth are reserved unto fire against the day of judgment. But he also says God is not willing that any should perish. Today, take sin seriously, and run to the refuge God provides in Christ.
S: God announced that He would bring judgment on the earth because of its wickedness. A holy God takes sin seriously and will not tolerate evil forever. Run today to the refuge He provides in Christ.
P: God's judgment is real, and so is His rescue. Genesis 6:7: 'I will destroy man whom I have created from the face of the earth.' Take sin seriously and run to Christ.

== 6:8
T: But Noah found grace
R: 'But Noah found grace in the eyes of the LORD.' After the darkest verses in the chapter comes a single word that changes everything: But. In the midst of universal corruption and coming judgment, one man found grace. This is the first time the word grace appears in the Bible. Notice that Noah found grace; he did not earn it. Grace is God's undeserved favor. Noah was a righteous man, as the next verse says, but his righteousness flowed from the grace he received, not the other way around. Every story of salvation begins here. We are not saved because we are better than others, but because God shows us grace. Paul writes, For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God. No matter how dark the world around you becomes, God's grace can still find you. Today, thank Him for the grace that found you when you did not deserve it.
S: In the midst of universal wickedness, Noah found grace in the eyes of the LORD. This is the first mention of grace in the Bible, God's undeserved favor. Thank God today for the grace that found you.
P: One word changes everything: grace. Genesis 6:8: 'But Noah found grace in the eyes of the LORD.' In a dark world, God's grace can still find you!

== 6:9
T: Righteous in a corrupt age
R: 'These are the generations of Noah: Noah was a just man and perfect in his generations, and Noah walked with God.' Noah lived in the most corrupt generation in history, yet he was just and blameless among his contemporaries. The word perfect here does not mean sinless but complete, wholehearted, and of integrity. Like Enoch before him, Noah walked with God. It is possible to live faithfully even when everyone around you is going the other way. Noah did not use his surroundings as an excuse. He did not say, Everyone else is doing it. He chose to walk with God when it was unpopular and lonely. Paul urges believers to be blameless and harmless, the sons of God, without rebuke, in the midst of a crooked and perverse nation, among whom ye shine as lights in the world. Your workplace, school, or community may not honor God, but you can. Today, walk with God even if you walk alone.
S: Noah was just and blameless in a corrupt generation, and he walked with God. It is possible to live faithfully even when everyone else goes the other way. Walk with God today, even if you walk alone.
P: You can be faithful in a faithless world. Genesis 6:9: 'Noah was a just man and perfect in his generations, and Noah walked with God.' Walk with Him today.

== 6:10
T: A father's faith shelters his family
R: 'And Noah begat three sons, Shem, Ham, and Japheth.' The names of Noah's sons are repeated here at the start of his story because they would share in his deliverance. Noah's faithfulness affected his whole family. When the flood came, his sons and their wives were saved with him. A godly parent's life has a profound effect on children. This does not mean faith is automatically transferred; each person must believe for himself or herself. But a parent who walks with God creates an environment where faith can grow and where God's protection is sought for the whole household. Later, when the Philippian jailer asked how to be saved, Paul said, Believe on the Lord Jesus Christ, and thou shalt be saved, and thy house. Faith often spreads through households. If you are a parent, your walk with God matters deeply for your children. Today, pray specifically for each member of your family by name, asking God to draw them to Himself.
S: Noah's three sons would share in their father's deliverance from the flood. A parent's faithful walk with God deeply affects the whole household. Pray today for each member of your family by name.

== 6:11
T: Corruption and violence
R: 'The earth also was corrupt before God, and the earth was filled with violence.' Two words describe the world before the flood: corrupt and violent. Corruption is the inward decay of what was good; violence is its outward expression. When people turn away from God, society eventually fills with cruelty and harm. Notice the phrase before God. The corruption was not hidden from Him. Everything happened in His sight. This is a sobering picture, but it also explains much about our own world. When we see violence in the news, it reflects the same root problem: human hearts turned away from God. Laws and policies can restrain evil, but only God can transform hearts. Christians are called to be peacemakers in a violent world. Jesus said, Blessed are the peacemakers: for they shall be called the children of God. Today, pray for peace in places torn by violence. And look for ways to bring God's peace into your own relationships and community.
S: The earth was corrupt before God and filled with violence. When hearts turn from God, society fills with cruelty and harm. Pray for peace today and be a peacemaker in your own community.

== 6:12
T: God sees everything
R: 'And God looked upon the earth, and, behold, it was corrupt; for all flesh had corrupted his way upon the earth.' In chapter one, God looked at His creation and saw that it was very good. Now He looks again and sees corruption. The same divine gaze that once delighted now grieves. The contrast is heartbreaking. What God made good, humanity had ruined. All flesh had corrupted its way. That phrase suggests that people had departed from the path God intended. We were made to walk in His ways, but we chose our own. God sees everything clearly, both the good and the corrupt. Nothing escapes His notice. Hebrews says, Neither is there any creature that is not manifest in his sight. That can be frightening if we are hiding sin, but comforting if we are walking in honesty. Today, live as one who is seen by God. Ask Him to show you any way in which you have wandered from His path, and return to it.
S: God looked at the earth and saw that all flesh had corrupted its way. What He once called very good had been ruined by sin. Live today as one seen by God and return to His path.

God spoke directly to Noah and told him what He was about to do. Before judgment came, God warned the one who walked with Him. Amos writes, Surely the Lord GOD will do nothing, but he revealeth his secret unto his servants the prophets. God shares His heart with those who walk closely with Him. Jesus told His disciples, Henceforth I call you not servants… but I have called you friends; for all things that I have heard of my Father I have made known unto you. Noah was God's friend, and God let him in on His plans. Intimacy with God brings understanding. When we walk with Him, read His word, and listen in prayer, we begin to see the world as He sees it. Today, draw near to God. He delights to share His heart with those who seek Him.

== 6:14
T: God provides the way of escape
R: 'Make thee an ark of gopher wood; rooms shalt thou make in the ark, and shalt pitch it within and without with pitch.' Along with the announcement of judgment came the provision of salvation. God did not simply tell Noah what would happen; He told him what to do. The ark was God's design for rescue. Interestingly, the Hebrew word for pitch is related to the word later used for atonement, meaning to cover. The ark was covered inside and out, sealed against the waters of judgment. This is a beautiful picture of Christ. Just as the ark protected those inside from the flood, Christ shelters those who are in Him from the judgment of sin. Peter later connects the ark to salvation through Jesus. There was only one ark and only one door. Today there is only one Savior. Jesus said, I am the door: by me if any man enter in, he shall be saved. Today, rest in the safety of being in Christ, our ark of salvation.
S: God gave Noah instructions to build an ark sealed with pitch inside and out. The ark is a picture of Christ, who shelters us from judgment. Rest today in the safety of being in Christ.
P: God always provides a way of escape. Genesis 6:14: 'Make thee an ark of gopher wood.' Jesus is our ark of salvation. Rest in Him today.

== 6:15
T: God's detailed design
R: 'And this is the fashion which thou shalt make it of: The length of the ark shall be three hundred cubits, the breadth of it fifty cubits, and the height of it thirty cubits.' God gave Noah precise measurements. The ark was about four hundred fifty feet long, seventy-five feet wide, and forty-five feet high. Its proportions have been praised by shipbuilders for stability. Noah did not have to invent his own plan for salvation. God provided the blueprint. This reminds us that salvation is God's design, not ours. We do not get to make up our own way to God, building whatever seems right to us. God has given the plan, and it is revealed in His word. It also reminds us that God cares about details. He is not vague or careless. If He gave exact measurements for a boat, He certainly cares about the details of your life. Today, trust God's design rather than your own. Follow His instructions, and trust that He has thought of everything.
S: God gave Noah precise measurements for building the ark. Salvation is God's design, and He cares about every detail. Trust His plan and follow His instructions today.
P: God's plans are precise. Genesis 6:15: 'The length of the ark shall be three hundred cubits.' He cares about every detail, including the details of your life.

== 6:16
T: Light and a door
R: 'A window shalt thou make to the ark, and in a cubit shalt thou finish it above; and the door of the ark shalt thou set in the side thereof; with lower, second, and third stories shalt thou make it.' Two features stand out: a window for light, and a single door. Even in the ark, during the darkest days of judgment, God provided light. And there was only one way in. Both point to Jesus. He is the light of the world, and He is the door. Those who come through Him find safety and light even in the midst of the storm. The ark was a place of refuge and provision, not despair. In Christ, there is room for all who will come. Jesus said, In my Father's house are many mansions. Today, if you are walking through a storm, remember that in Christ you have both a door of safety and a window of light. You are not alone in the dark.

== 6:17
T: The seriousness of judgment
R: 'And, behold, I, even I, do bring a flood of waters upon the earth, to destroy all flesh, wherein is the breath of life, from under heaven; and every thing that is in the earth shall die.' God emphasized that He Himself would bring the flood: I, even I. This was not a natural disaster that happened to strike. It was a deliberate act of divine judgment. Many are uncomfortable with a God who judges, but the Bible presents judgment as an expression of God's holiness and justice. Evil must be dealt with. A world where evil is never judged would be a world without hope for the oppressed. Yet the God who judges is also the God who saves. The same God who sent the flood provided the ark. The same God who will judge the world sent His Son to bear judgment for us. John writes that God sent not His Son into the world to condemn the world, but that the world through Him might be saved.
S: God declared that He Himself would bring the flood to judge the earth. Judgment is an expression of His holiness and justice. The God who judges is also the God who provides salvation through His Son.

== 6:18
T: The first covenant
R: 'But with thee will I establish my covenant; and thou shalt come into the ark, thou, and thy sons, and thy wife, and thy sons' wives with thee.' Here is another gracious But. In the middle of announcing judgment, God promised a covenant. This is the first time the word covenant appears in the Bible. A covenant is a binding promise, a commitment God makes and keeps. God committed Himself to Noah and his family. He would bring them through the flood. Covenant is one of the great themes of Scripture. God made covenants with Noah, Abraham, Moses, and David, each one moving toward the new covenant in Jesus' blood. At the Last Supper, Jesus said, This cup is the new testament in my blood. God is a covenant-keeping God. His promises are not casual words but binding commitments. Today, take hold of the promises God has made to you in Christ. He has bound Himself to keep them, and He will never break His word.
S: God promised to establish His covenant with Noah and bring his family safely into the ark. This is the first covenant in the Bible, a binding promise God would keep. Take hold of His promises in Christ today.

== 6:19
T: God preserves life
R: 'And of every living thing of all flesh, two of every sort shalt thou bring into the ark, to keep them alive with thee; they shall be male and female.' God's plan of rescue included not just Noah's family but the animals as well. Two of every sort would be brought in to keep them alive. God cares for all of His creation. He did not intend to start over from nothing but to preserve what He had made and carry it into a new beginning. This reveals God's heart for restoration rather than abandonment. Romans 8 tells us that creation itself will be delivered from the bondage of corruption into the glorious liberty of the children of God. God's plan of redemption is cosmic in scope. He is not only saving individual souls but restoring His whole creation. Noah's ark was a small picture of that great renewal. Today, rejoice that God's purposes include the whole world He made. Care for His creation as one who shares His heart for it.
S: God told Noah to bring two of every kind of animal into the ark to keep them alive. His plan of rescue included all of creation. Rejoice that God's purposes of restoration extend to the whole world He made.

== 6:20
T: God brings them in
R: 'Of fowls after their kind, and of cattle after their kind, of every creeping thing of the earth after his kind, two of every sort shall come unto thee, to keep them alive.' Notice the wording: two of every sort shall come unto thee. Noah did not have to hunt down and capture every animal on earth. God would bring them to him. This is a beautiful detail. God gave Noah a task that seemed impossible, but He also took responsibility for the parts Noah could not do. Noah built the ark; God gathered the animals. That is often how God works with us. He asks us to do what we can and trusts Himself with what we cannot. Paul writes, Work out your own salvation with fear and trembling. For it is God which worketh in you both to will and to do of his good pleasure. Today, do your part faithfully and leave the impossible to God. He is able to bring what you cannot gather.

== 6:21
T: Preparation by faith
R: 'And take thou unto thee of all food that is eaten, and thou shalt gather it to thee; and it shall be for food for thee, and for them.' God told Noah to store up food for his family and all the animals. This was practical preparation for a long stay in the ark. Faith does not mean passivity. Noah trusted God completely, yet he also gathered food and made careful preparations. Faith and wise planning go together. Proverbs commends the ant, which provides her meat in the summer, and gathereth her food in the harvest. God provides, but He often provides through our diligent work and planning. At the same time, Noah's preparation was itself an act of faith. He gathered food for a flood no one else believed was coming. Every sack he stored was a declaration that he believed God's word. Today, prepare wisely for what God has called you to. Let your practical choices reflect your trust in His promises.
S: God told Noah to gather food for his family and all the animals. Faith and wise preparation go hand in hand. Prepare wisely today for what God has called you to.

== 6:22
T: Complete obedience
R: 'Thus did Noah; according to all that God commanded him, so did he.' This simple verse is one of the greatest compliments in Scripture. Noah obeyed God fully. Not partially, not selectively, but according to all that God commanded. Building the ark took many years. It meant enduring ridicule from neighbors and doing something that made no sense to the world. Yet Noah persisted. Hebrews celebrates his faith: By faith Noah, being warned of God of things not seen as yet, moved with fear, prepared an ark to the saving of his house. True faith shows itself in obedience. James writes that faith without works is dead. Noah's faith was proven by the hammer in his hand and the wood he cut day after day. What has God asked you to do? Perhaps He has spoken through His word, through prayer, or through wise counsel. Today, follow Noah's example. Do according to all that God has commanded you, and trust Him with the outcome.
S: Noah did everything God commanded him. His faith was proven by years of faithful obedience. Obey God fully today and trust Him with the outcome.
