# Genesis 7: The flood

== 7:1
T: Come into the ark
R: 'And the LORD said unto Noah, Come thou and all thy house into the ark; for thee have I seen righteous before me in this generation.' After years of building, the moment arrived. God's invitation was simple: Come. Notice that God did not say, Go into the ark, as if sending Noah away alone. He said, Come, as if God Himself were inside, waiting to receive him. That small word captures the heart of the gospel. Jesus says, Come unto me. Revelation closes with the Spirit and the bride saying, Come. God's invitation is personal, warm, and urgent. It also included Noah's whole house. The righteousness of one man became a shelter for his family. God saw Noah, even in a generation that had forgotten Him. He sees you too. Whatever storm is approaching in your life, hear His invitation today. Come into the safety He provides. Bring your household with you in prayer. The door is open, and He is waiting.
S: God invited Noah and his household to come into the ark. His invitation was personal, warm and urgent. Hear God's invitation today to come into the safety He provides.

== 7:2
T: Provision for worship
R: 'Of every clean beast thou shalt take to thee by sevens, the male and his female: and of beasts that are not clean by two, the male and his female.' Here we learn a detail not mentioned earlier. Clean animals were to be taken in greater numbers, by sevens. Why? After the flood, Noah would offer sacrifices to God, and clean animals were used for worship. God was planning ahead for worship even before the rain began. Before the judgment fell, God was already making provision for thanksgiving afterward. That says something wonderful about God's intentions. His aim was not destruction for its own sake but a renewed world where He would be worshiped. God always has worship in view. He saves us so that we might glorify Him. Peter writes that we are called to show forth the praises of Him who called us out of darkness. Today, remember that you were saved for worship. Even in the storm, God is preparing you to praise Him on the other side.
S: God told Noah to take clean animals by sevens, providing for worship after the flood. God was already planning for thanksgiving before the rain began. Remember today that you were saved so that you might worship Him.

== 7:3
T: Keeping seed alive
R: 'Of fowls also of the air by sevens, the male and the female; to keep seed alive upon the face of all the earth.' God's purpose was clear: to keep seed alive. Judgment was coming, but God was preserving life for the future. The word seed recalls the promise of Genesis 3:15, the seed of the woman who would crush the serpent. Through all the upheaval of the flood, God was keeping alive both the animals and the human line through which the Savior would come. God never loses sight of His long-term purposes. Even when it looks like everything is being swept away, He is protecting what matters most. He preserves a remnant, a seed, a future. Isaiah speaks of a holy seed that remains like the stump of a tree after it is cut down. If your life feels like it is being shaken, trust that God is keeping something alive. His purposes for you will survive the storm. What He has planted, He will protect.
S: God told Noah to bring birds by sevens to keep seed alive on the earth. Even in judgment, God was preserving life and the line of the promised Savior. Trust that God is protecting His purposes for you through every storm.

== 7:4
T: Seven days of grace
R: 'For yet seven days, and I will cause it to rain upon the earth forty days and forty nights; and every living substance that I have made will I destroy from off the face of the earth.' God gave one final week. Seven more days before the rain began. Even at the very end, God's mercy delayed judgment. Those seven days were a last opportunity for anyone who would listen. Noah, whom Peter calls a preacher of righteousness, may have used them to warn others. But no one else came. This verse reminds us of the urgency of the gospel. God's patience is real, but it will not last forever. Paul writes, behold, now is the accepted time; behold, now is the day of salvation. There is a final moment for every person and for the world. If you have been delaying your response to God, do not wait. And if you know Christ, use the time you have to share His invitation with others. The door is still open today.

== 7:5
T: Obedience again
R: 'And Noah did according unto all that the LORD commanded him.' This statement echoes the end of chapter six. Noah's obedience was not a single moment but a consistent pattern. He obeyed when building the ark, and he obeyed again when it was time to enter it. Faith is not a one-time decision but a lifelong walk of obedience. Some people obey God in one season and then drift in the next. Noah's life shows steady faithfulness from beginning to end. Notice also that this time the verse uses the name the LORD, God's personal covenant name. Noah's obedience flowed from relationship. He was not merely following rules; he was responding to the God he knew and loved. Jesus said, If ye love me, keep my commandments. Obedience is the natural expression of love. Today, let your obedience flow from your relationship with God. Not grudgingly, but gladly, because you know Him and trust Him. Keep obeying, one step at a time.
S: Noah again did everything the LORD commanded him. His obedience was a consistent pattern flowing from his relationship with God. Let your obedience today flow from love and trust in Him.

== 7:6
T: Never too old
R: 'And Noah was six hundred years old when the flood of waters was upon the earth.' Six hundred years old! Noah faced the greatest challenge of his life in what we might think of as old age. His most significant work of faith came late. Scripture often shows God using people in their later years. Abraham became a father at a hundred. Moses led Israel out of Egypt at eighty. Anna the prophetess was a widow of great age when she recognized the infant Jesus. You are never too old to be used by God. Age brings wisdom, experience, and perspective that younger people may lack. Psalm 92 says the righteous shall still bring forth fruit in old age. If you are older, do not think your season of usefulness has passed. God may have something significant still ahead for you. And if you are younger, honor and learn from those who have walked with God for many years. Their faith is a treasure.
S: Noah was six hundred years old when the flood came. God often does significant work through people in their later years. You are never too old to be used by God.

After years of building and waiting, they stepped inside the refuge God had provided. Knowing about the ark was not enough; they had to go in. Many people know about Jesus. They may admire Him, attend church, or respect Christianity. But salvation requires more than knowledge; it requires entering in, trusting Christ personally. Noah's neighbors had watched the ark being built for years. They knew about it. But they did not enter. Today, the invitation stands. It is not enough to know about the refuge; you must be in it. Paul writes that there is now no condemnation to them which are in Christ Jesus. If you have never personally trusted Christ, step into the ark today. And if you have, thank God that you are safely inside.

== 7:8
T: Clean and unclean together
R: 'Of clean beasts, and of beasts that are not clean, and of fowls, and of every thing that creepeth upon the earth.' Both clean and unclean animals came into the ark. All were preserved together. In the ark there was room for creatures of every kind, the noble and the lowly, the useful and the strange. There is a picture of grace here. God's refuge is not only for the respectable. In the New Testament, Peter received a vision of a great sheet filled with clean and unclean animals, and God taught him not to call any person common or unclean. The gospel was for Gentiles as well as Jews, for sinners of every background. The church is meant to be like the ark, a place where people of all kinds find refuge together in Christ. Today, ask yourself whether you have written off any person or group as beyond God's reach. In God's ark, there is room for everyone He calls.

== 7:9
T: As God had commanded
R: 'There went in two and two unto Noah into the ark, the male and the female, as God had commanded Noah.' Picture the scene: animals coming in pairs, walking calmly into the ark. Lions and lambs, elephants and mice, entering together in an orderly procession. This was clearly the hand of God at work. The phrase as God had commanded appears again, emphasizing that everything happened exactly according to God's word. When God commands, creation responds. The animals obeyed their Creator's direction without understanding why. They simply came. Sometimes we struggle to obey because we want to understand everything first. But faith often means moving in obedience before we see the full picture. Proverbs says, Trust in the LORD with all thine heart; and lean not unto thine own understanding. If animals can follow God's leading by instinct, how much more should we, who know Him, follow His word? Today, take a step of obedience even if you do not fully understand. He knows the way.

== 7:10
T: God's word comes true
R: 'And it came to pass after seven days, that the waters of the flood were upon the earth.' Exactly as God said, after seven days the flood began. For more than a century, people may have mocked Noah's warnings. Perhaps they said it had never rained like that before, so it never would. But God's word came to pass precisely as He had spoken. This is one of the clearest lessons of the flood. God's word is reliable. What He says will happen. Peter warns that in the last days scoffers will come, saying, Where is the promise of his coming? for since the fathers fell asleep, all things continue as they were. But Peter reminds them of the flood. God's delays are not denials. His word stands. That is both a warning and an encouragement. A warning to take His judgments seriously, and an encouragement to trust His promises completely. Every promise God has made to you in Christ will come to pass, right on time.
S: After seven days the flood came, exactly as God had said. God's word is always reliable, even when people doubt it. Take His warnings seriously and trust His promises completely.

== 7:11
T: A day in history
R: 'In the six hundredth year of Noah's life, in the second month, the seventeenth day of the month, the same day were all the fountains of the great deep broken up, and the windows of heaven were opened.' Scripture gives the exact date. This was not a myth but a real event in history, dated by Noah's life. On that day, waters burst from below and poured from above. Creation itself seemed to undo the separation of waters God had established on the second day. The world was returning to the watery chaos of the beginning. Yet God was in control of every drop. The same God who opened the windows of heaven for judgment would later open them for blessing. Malachi records God's promise to open the windows of heaven, and pour you out a blessing. God has power over all of creation. The forces that terrify us are under His command. Today, whatever floods are breaking loose around you, remember that God holds the keys to the windows of heaven.

== 7:12
T: Forty days
R: 'And the rain was upon the earth forty days and forty nights.' Forty days of relentless rain. In Scripture, the number forty often marks a period of testing, trial, or preparation. Moses was on Mount Sinai forty days. Israel wandered forty years in the wilderness. Elijah journeyed forty days to Horeb. Jesus fasted forty days in the wilderness and was tempted by the devil. These periods were difficult, but they were not endless, and they led to something new. The rain did not fall forever. It had a limit set by God. If you are in a season of testing right now, take heart. It has a beginning, and it will have an end. God sets the boundaries of your trials. Paul writes that our light affliction, which is but for a moment, worketh for us a far more exceeding and eternal weight of glory. Hold on through your forty days. God is using them to prepare you for what comes next.
S: The rain fell for forty days and forty nights. In Scripture, forty often marks a season of testing that leads to something new. Take heart that your season of trial has limits set by God.

Jesus said of His sheep, I give unto them eternal life; and they shall never perish, neither shall any man pluck them out of my hand. Today, rest in God's perfect timing. He is never late. The flood was not the end of creation but its preservation through judgment toward a new start. The repeated words are almost like a roll call, every kind accounted for. God remembers what He has made. Nothing slips through the cracks of His attention. Every bird of every sort was included. This reminds us that God's care is comprehensive. Jesus said, Are not five sparrows sold for two farthings, and not one of them is forgotten before God? If He remembered every kind of creature in the ark, He will not forget you. The phrase the breath of life takes us back to Genesis 2, when God breathed into Adam's nostrils and he became a living soul. Life is God's gift, and in the ark He was protecting it. Everything inside the ark had breath because God gave it. Life is precious to God. He is the author of it, and He cares about preserving it. This truth shapes how we view every living thing, and especially every human life. Each breath is a gift from Him. Job said, The Spirit of God hath made me, and the breath of the Almighty hath given me life. When we understand that our breath comes from God, we become more grateful and more careful with the life He has given. Today, thank God for the breath in your lungs. == 7:16
T: The LORD shut him in
R: 'And they that went in, went in male and female of all flesh, as God had commanded him: and the LORD shut him in.' What a tender and powerful phrase: the LORD shut him in. Noah did not close the door himself. God did. That meant two things. First, Noah was completely secure. No wave could break through a door God had sealed. Second, the time for entering had ended. God's hand closed the door of opportunity. In Christ, we are secure in the same way. Paul writes that we are sealed with the Holy Spirit. Our salvation does not depend on our ability to hold the door shut; God holds it. Jesus told a parable of ten virgins, and when the bridegroom came, those who were ready went in, and the door was shut. There is security for those inside and urgency for those outside. Today, rest in the safety of God's sealing hand. You are shut in with Him, safe from the storm.

The flood that destroyed the world bore up those who were in God's refuge. The higher the waters rose, the higher the ark was lifted. This is a powerful picture of how God sustains His people through trials. The storms that threaten to destroy us can become the very means by which God lifts us closer to Him. Isaiah says, When thou passest through the waters, I will be with thee; and through the rivers, they shall not overflow thee. The waters were real, and they were rising, but they could not sink what God was keeping. If the floodwaters are rising in your life, remember the ark. In Christ, you will not be overwhelmed. You may even find that the very trials you fear lift you higher in faith and dependence on Him.

== 7:18
T: Floating on the waters
R: 'And the waters prevailed, and were increased greatly upon the earth; and the ark went upon the face of the waters.' The waters prevailed over the earth. But the ark went upon the face of the waters. Notice the contrast. The flood conquered everything except the ark. The ark did not have a rudder, a sail, or an engine. It simply floated, carried by the waters wherever God directed. Noah did not steer. He trusted. Sometimes life feels like that: we cannot control the direction, and we have no rudder in our hands. But if we are in the vessel God provided, we are safe even when we cannot steer. The waters may prevail over everything else, but they cannot prevail over God's people. Jesus said of His church, the gates of hell shall not prevail against it. Today, if you feel adrift without control, remember that you do not need to steer when God is carrying you. Rest in His keeping.

There was no mountain tall enough to escape the waters of God's judgment. People might have climbed to the highest peaks, hoping to find safety, but no human refuge was high enough. The only safe place was the ark. This verse reminds us that we cannot escape God's judgment through our own efforts. Our achievements, our goodness, our religious activities are like mountains we climb, hoping they will be high enough. But they are not. Only the refuge God provides can save us. Paul writes, Not by works of righteousness which we have done, but according to his mercy he saved us. Today, stop climbing mountains of self-effort. Find your safety in Christ alone. He is the only refuge high enough, and He is enough. Rest in His mercy, not in your merits.

Corrie ten Boom used to say that God throws our sins into the deepest sea and puts up a sign that says, No fishing allowed. Today, thank God that His grace is deeper than your sin.

Everything was destroyed except for one small group: Noah and those with him in the ark. The phrase Noah only remained alive is striking. Of all the people on earth, only eight survived. It was not because they were stronger or smarter. It was because they were in the ark. Salvation was a matter of location. The question was not how good you were, but where you were when the flood came. The same is true spiritually. Paul repeatedly uses the phrase in Christ. Those who are in Christ are safe from judgment. If any man be in Christ, he is a new creature. Today, ask yourself the only question that matters: Am I in Christ? If you are, rejoice. == 7:24
T: The long wait
R: 'And the waters prevailed upon the earth an hundred and fifty days.' One hundred fifty days is about five months. That is a long time to wait inside a boat, surrounded by water, with no land in sight. The rain had stopped after forty days, but the waters remained. Noah and his family had to wait, day after day, not knowing exactly when the waters would recede. Waiting is one of the hardest parts of faith. We often want God to act immediately. But many of His most important works involve long periods of waiting. Abraham waited twenty-five years for Isaac. Joseph waited years in prison. The disciples waited in the upper room for the Spirit. Isaiah says, they that wait upon the LORD shall renew their strength. Waiting is not wasted time. It is time when God is working, even if we cannot see it. Today, if you are in a long season of waiting, keep trusting. God has not forgotten you.
