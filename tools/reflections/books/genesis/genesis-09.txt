# Genesis 9: The covenant of the rainbow

== 9:1
T: A blessing renewed
R: 'And God blessed Noah and his sons, and said unto them, Be fruitful, and multiply, and replenish the earth.' Once again we hear the words first spoken to Adam and Eve. After the flood, God blessed humanity afresh with the same commission: fill the earth. God's purposes for humanity did not change because of sin and judgment. His design was still for people to flourish, to build families, and to spread across the world as His image-bearers. This is a reminder that God's grace is greater than our failures. Humanity had failed spectacularly, yet God started again with blessing. He does the same for us. When we fall, He does not cancel His purposes for our lives. He restores, renews, and recommissions. Peter denied Jesus three times, yet Jesus restored him and told him, Feed my sheep. Today, if you feel your failures have disqualified you, hear God's blessing again. His purposes for you still stand. Go forward in His grace.
S: God blessed Noah and his sons with the same commission He gave Adam and Eve. His purposes for humanity were not cancelled by sin and judgment. Hear His renewed blessing over your life today and go forward in grace.
P: God's purposes outlast our failures. Genesis 9:1: 'And God blessed Noah and his sons, and said unto them, Be fruitful, and multiply.' His blessing over you still stands.

== 9:2
T: Responsibility over creation
R: 'And the fear of you and the dread of you shall be upon every beast of the earth… into your hand are they delivered.' After the flood, the relationship between humans and animals changed. Animals would now fear people. The harmony of Eden was broken, and creation reflected the effects of sin. Yet God also said that the animals were delivered into human hands. This is a statement of responsibility. Authority always comes with accountability. We are stewards of creation, not owners. Proverbs says, A righteous man regardeth the life of his beast. How we treat animals and the natural world reflects our understanding of God's ownership of all things. The fear animals have of humans is a reminder that the world is not as it should be. But Isaiah looks forward to a day when the wolf shall dwell with the lamb, and creation will be restored to peace. Today, care for God's creatures with kindness and wisdom, as one entrusted with something that belongs to Him.
S: After the flood, animals would fear humanity, yet they were delivered into human hands. Authority over creation comes with the responsibility of stewardship. Care for God's creatures with kindness and wisdom today.
P: Authority means responsibility. Genesis 9:2: 'into your hand are they delivered.' We are stewards of God's creation, not owners. Care for it wisely.

== 9:3
T: God's generous provision
R: 'Every moving thing that liveth shall be meat for you; even as the green herb have I given you all things.' God expanded humanity's diet after the flood. Before, plants had been given for food. Now animals were included as well. The key phrase is have I given you all things. Everything we eat is a gift from God. Whether bread or vegetables or meat, it comes from His generous hand. In the New Testament, Paul writes that every creature of God is good, and nothing to be refused, if it be received with thanksgiving: for it is sanctified by the word of God and prayer. That is why Christians have a long tradition of giving thanks before meals. It is not a ritual to rush through but a moment to remember the Giver. Food is a daily reminder of God's provision. Today, at each meal, pause and genuinely thank God. Recognize that the food on your table is a gift from His hand.
S: After the flood, God gave humanity animals as well as plants for food. Everything we eat is a gift from His generous hand. Pause at each meal today to give genuine thanks to the Giver.
P: Every meal is a gift. Genesis 9:3: 'even as the green herb have I given you all things.' Pause and thank God for your food today.

== 9:4
T: Life is in the blood
R: 'But flesh with the life thereof, which is the blood thereof, shall ye not eat.' Along with permission came a limit. Blood was not to be eaten because the life is in the blood. This command taught reverence for life. Even when taking an animal's life for food, people were to acknowledge that life belongs to God. This principle runs throughout Scripture. Leviticus explains, For the life of the flesh is in the blood: and I have given it to you upon the altar to make an atonement for your souls. Blood represents life given, and it became central to the sacrificial system. All of this pointed forward to Jesus. Hebrews says that without shedding of blood is no remission. Jesus gave His life, His blood, for our forgiveness. At the Last Supper He said, This is my blood of the new testament, which is shed for many for the remission of sins. Today, remember the preciousness of life, and give thanks for the precious blood of Christ shed for you.
S: God forbade eating blood because the life is in the blood. This principle taught reverence for life and pointed forward to atonement through sacrifice. Give thanks today for the precious blood of Christ shed for you.
P: Life is precious to God. Genesis 9:4: 'flesh with the life thereof, which is the blood thereof, shall ye not eat.' Thank God for the precious blood of Jesus.

== 9:5
T: God values every life
R: 'And surely your blood of your lives will I require; at the hand of every beast will I require it, and at the hand of man; at the hand of every man's brother will I require the life of man.' God declared that He would hold accountable anyone who takes human life. Human life is so valuable to Him that He personally requires an accounting for it. This echoes the story of Cain and Abel, where Abel's blood cried out from the ground. Notice the phrase every man's brother. Every human being is a brother or sister to every other. We are one human family, all descended from Noah, all made by the same God. This verse is a foundation for justice and for protecting the vulnerable. God sees violence and will call it to account. Today, let this truth shape how you see others. Every person you meet is someone whose life God values supremely. Treat them with dignity, protect the vulnerable, and stand for justice.
S: God declared that He would require an accounting for every human life that is taken. Every person is precious to Him and part of one human family. Treat others with dignity and stand for justice today.
P: Every life matters to God. Genesis 9:5: 'at the hand of every man's brother will I require the life of man.' Treat everyone as precious today.

== 9:6
T: The image of God protected
R: 'Whoso sheddeth man's blood, by man shall his blood be shed: for in the image of God made he man.' Here God established the principle of justice in human society. Murder was to be punished because it is an attack on the image of God. The reason given is crucial: for in the image of God made he man. Even after the fall and the flood, humanity still bears God's image. That image gives every person dignity and worth. To harm a human being is to strike at something that reflects God Himself. This verse lays a foundation for government and justice. Paul later writes that rulers are God's ministers to restrain evil. But it also shapes our everyday interactions. James notes that with the same tongue we bless God and curse men, which are made after the similitude of God. If every person bears God's image, then our words and actions toward them matter deeply. Today, honor the image of God in everyone you meet.
S: God established justice for murder because humanity is made in His image. Even after the fall, every person bears God's image and has dignity and worth. Honor the image of God in everyone you meet today.
P: Every person bears God's image. Genesis 9:6: 'for in the image of God made he man.' Let that shape how you treat people today.

== 9:7
T: Abundant life
R: 'And you, be ye fruitful, and multiply; bring forth abundantly in the earth, and multiply therein.' God repeated His blessing for emphasis. Twice in this chapter He told Noah's family to be fruitful and multiply. God does not want His people merely to survive; He wants them to flourish. The word abundantly speaks of overflowing life. This blessing applied first to families and population, but it carries a spiritual principle as well. Jesus said, I am come that they might have life, and that they might have it more abundantly. God desires abundance for His people, not necessarily in material wealth, but in love, joy, peace, purpose, and spiritual fruit. Paul prays that believers would be filled with the fruits of righteousness. Some Christians live as though faith were mere survival, just getting by. But God offers more. Today, ask God to make your life fruitful and abundant in the things that matter most. He delights to bless His children with overflowing life.
S: God repeated His blessing for Noah's family to be fruitful and bring forth abundantly. He wants His people not merely to survive but to flourish. Ask Him today for an abundant, fruitful life in Him.

== 9:8
T: God speaks to the family
R: 'And God spake unto Noah, and to his sons with him, saying.' This time God spoke not only to Noah but to his sons as well. The covenant God was about to make would include the whole family and every generation after them. God's promises are not only for individuals but for families and communities. When Peter preached at Pentecost, he said, For the promise is unto you, and to your children, and to all that are afar off. God has always worked through households and generations. This means that our faith is not a private matter. It affects those around us and those who come after us. Noah's sons heard God's voice directly. They would carry His promise to their own children. Today, consider how you can help the younger people in your family hear God's word for themselves. Read Scripture together, pray together, and talk about God's promises. Let the next generation hear Him speak.
S: God spoke to Noah and to his sons together about His covenant. His promises are for families and for generations to come. Help the younger people in your life hear God's word for themselves today.

== 9:9
T: God takes the initiative
R: 'And I, behold, I establish my covenant with you, and with your seed after you.' Notice the emphasis: I, behold, I. God was the one establishing the covenant. Noah did not negotiate it or earn it. God initiated it, and God would keep it. This covenant was one-sided in the sense that its fulfillment depended entirely on God. That is the beauty of grace. Our relationship with God does not begin with our efforts but with His initiative. John writes, We love him, because he first loved us. God always makes the first move. He sought Adam in the garden. He called Abraham out of Ur. He sent His Son into the world. And He has drawn you to Himself. Notice also that the covenant extended to Noah's seed after him. God's faithfulness stretches across generations. Today, rest in the fact that your relationship with God is anchored in His initiative and His commitment, not in your performance. He has bound Himself to you.
S: God emphasized that He Himself was establishing His covenant with Noah and his descendants. Our relationship with God begins with His initiative, not our efforts. Rest today in the God who first loved you and committed Himself to you.
P: God always makes the first move. Genesis 9:9: 'And I, behold, I establish my covenant with you.' His love comes first. Rest in His commitment today.

== 9:10
T: A covenant with all creation
R: God's covenant extended 'with every living creature that is with you, of the fowl, of the cattle, and of every beast of the earth with you; from all that go out of the ark.' This is remarkable. God's promise was not just for humans but for animals too. Every creature that came out of the ark was included in God's covenant. God's care reaches all of His creation. He made promises not only to people who could understand them but to birds and beasts that could not. This shows that God's commitment to His world is complete. He did not make creation only to abandon it. Psalm 145 says, The LORD is good to all: and his tender mercies are over all his works. One day, the whole creation will share in the freedom of God's children, as Paul writes in Romans 8. Today, let God's care for all creatures enlarge your view of His goodness. If He makes covenants with birds and beasts, His love for you is beyond measure.
S: God's covenant included every living creature that came out of the ark. His care and commitment extend to all of His creation. Let His goodness to all creatures deepen your trust in His love for you.

== 9:11
T: Never again
R: 'And I will establish my covenant with you; neither shall all flesh be cut off any more by the waters of a flood; neither shall there any more be a flood to destroy the earth.' God made a clear and unconditional promise: never again would a flood destroy all life on the earth. After such a devastating judgment, this promise brought deep reassurance. Noah's family would not have to live in fear every time it rained. God's word gave them security. God's promises are meant to give us peace. Fear often comes from uncertainty, from not knowing what will happen. But God gives us firm promises to stand on. He has said, I will never leave thee, nor forsake thee. He has said that nothing can separate us from His love in Christ. These promises are as sure as the one He gave Noah. Today, when fear rises, take hold of a promise from God's word. Let His never again become your peace. He keeps every word He speaks.
S: God promised that never again would a flood destroy all life on the earth. His promises give security and peace in place of fear. Take hold of God's promises today when fear rises in your heart.

== 9:12
T: A sign for every generation
R: 'And God said, This is the token of the covenant which I make between me and you and every living creature that is with you, for perpetual generations.' God gave a token, a visible sign, of His covenant. He knew that human beings need reminders. We forget easily. We need signs we can see to help us remember what we cannot see. Throughout Scripture, God gives signs of His covenants: circumcision for Abraham, the Sabbath for Israel, and baptism and the Lord's Supper for the church. Jesus said of the bread and cup, this do in remembrance of me. These signs do not replace faith but strengthen it. They are visible promises. Notice too the phrase for perpetual generations. This sign was not temporary. It would last as long as the earth endures. God's faithfulness spans all of history. Today, thank God for the reminders He has given you of His love. Let them strengthen your faith whenever you see them.
S: God gave a visible token of His covenant that would last for all generations. He knows we need reminders of His unseen promises. Thank Him for the signs of His love that strengthen your faith.

== 9:13
T: The rainbow
R: 'I do set my bow in the cloud, and it shall be for a token of a covenant between me and the earth.' The rainbow is God's signature in the sky. Every time you see one, you are looking at a reminder of His promise. Interestingly, the word for bow is the same as the word for a warrior's bow. Some have suggested that God hung up His bow of war, pointed away from the earth, as a sign of peace. Whether or not that image was intended, the rainbow speaks of mercy after judgment. It appears when sun shines through rain, beauty emerging from the storm. In Revelation, John sees a rainbow round about the throne of God. Even in heaven, the rainbow surrounds God's glory, a permanent reminder of His covenant faithfulness. The next time you see a rainbow, stop and remember. God keeps His promises. His mercy shines through the storms of life. Let every rainbow lead you to worship the faithful God who set it there.
S: God set His bow in the cloud as a sign of His covenant with the earth. The rainbow speaks of mercy after judgment and beauty after the storm. Let every rainbow remind you that God keeps His promises.

== 9:14
T: Beauty in the clouds
R: 'And it shall come to pass, when I bring a cloud over the earth, that the bow shall be seen in the cloud.' The rainbow appears in the cloud. You cannot have a rainbow without clouds and rain. The very thing that might remind Noah's family of the flood became the canvas for God's promise. God placed His sign of hope right in the middle of what could have caused fear. This is a beautiful picture of how God works. He often reveals His faithfulness in the midst of our clouds. The storms of life are where we discover that His promises hold. Without the dark clouds, we would never see the rainbow. Paul learned that God's comfort abounds in our sufferings. He wrote that the God of all comfort comforts us in all our tribulation. If clouds are gathering over your life today, look for the rainbow. God has placed His promise right there in the cloud. His faithfulness shines most brightly against the darkest sky.
S: God promised that when clouds came over the earth, His bow would be seen in them. He often reveals His faithfulness in the middle of our storms. Look for His promise in the clouds you face today.

== 9:15
T: God remembers His covenant
R: 'And I will remember my covenant, which is between me and you and every living creature of all flesh; and the waters shall no more become a flood to destroy all flesh.' Notice who does the remembering: God Himself. The rainbow was not primarily a reminder for people but for God. Of course, God does not forget. But He speaks in human terms to assure us of His commitment. When He sees the rainbow, He remembers His promise. That means our security does not depend on our memory but on His. Even when we forget God's promises, He never forgets them. Even when our faith is weak, His faithfulness remains strong. Paul writes, If we believe not, yet he abideth faithful: he cannot deny himself. This is the foundation of our hope. Our relationship with God rests not on our ability to hold on to Him, but on His commitment to hold on to us. Today, rest in the God who remembers His covenant with you.
S: God said that He Himself would remember His covenant whenever He saw the rainbow. Our security rests on His memory and faithfulness, not ours. Rest in the God who never forgets His promises to you.

== 9:16
T: An everlasting covenant
R: 'And the bow shall be in the cloud; and I will look upon it, that I may remember the everlasting covenant between God and every living creature of all flesh that is upon the earth.' God called this an everlasting covenant. It has no expiration date. Thousands of years later, rainbows still appear, and God's promise still stands. The word everlasting appears many times in Scripture to describe God's covenants and His love. Jeremiah records God saying, I have loved thee with an everlasting love. Hebrews speaks of the blood of the everlasting covenant through which Jesus was raised from the dead. Everything in this world is temporary. Possessions fade, relationships change, and life passes quickly. But God's covenant love is everlasting. It does not change with circumstances or wear out with time. Today, anchor your heart in what is everlasting. When everything around you feels uncertain, remember that God's covenant with you in Christ will never end.

== 9:17
T: God confirms His word
R: 'And God said unto Noah, This is the token of the covenant, which I have established between me and all flesh that is upon the earth.' God repeated the promise once more, as if to make absolutely sure Noah understood. This chapter mentions the covenant again and again. God was not giving a casual assurance; He was emphasizing, confirming, and sealing His word. God knows our need for assurance. After a traumatic experience like the flood, Noah needed to hear God's promise more than once. And God patiently repeated it. Our God is patient with our need for reassurance. When Thomas doubted the resurrection, Jesus came and showed him His wounds. When Gideon asked for a sign, God gave him two. God does not despise our weakness. He strengthens it. Hebrews says that God, willing more abundantly to shew unto the heirs of promise the immutability of his counsel, confirmed it by an oath. Today, if you need reassurance, ask God for it. He delights to confirm His love to His children.

== 9:18
T: A new family of nations
R: 'And the sons of Noah, that went forth of the ark, were Shem, and Ham, and Japheth: and Ham is the father of Canaan.' These three men stepped out of the ark into an empty world. From them would come every nation on earth. Every person alive today traces their ancestry back to one of these three sons. That means humanity is truly one family. Paul declared in Athens that God hath made of one blood all nations of men for to dwell on all the face of the earth. Racism and ethnic hatred deny this basic biblical truth. We are all related, all descended from the same ancestors, all made in the image of the same God. The mention of Canaan here prepares us for the story that follows, but the larger truth is unity. Today, remember that every person you meet is part of your extended human family. Let that shape how you treat people of every background and nation. In Christ, we are brought together as one.
S: Noah's three sons came out of the ark, and from them all nations descend. Humanity is truly one family made of one blood. Treat people of every background as part of your extended family today.

== 9:19
T: The whole earth from one family
R: 'These are the three sons of Noah: and of them was the whole earth overspread.' From three brothers, the entire world was populated. It is astonishing how God can take something small and make it great. A single family became every nation, language, and culture on earth. God often works this way. He takes small beginnings and multiplies them. Jesus compared the kingdom of heaven to a mustard seed, the smallest of seeds, which grows into a great tree. He started His church with twelve ordinary men, and it has spread to every corner of the world. Zechariah asks, For who hath despised the day of small things? If your efforts for God feel small, do not despise them. A small family can fill the earth. A small act of faithfulness can ripple through generations. A small seed of the gospel planted in one heart can spread to many. Today, be faithful in small things. Trust God to multiply them beyond what you can imagine.
S: From Noah's three sons the whole earth was populated. God often takes small beginnings and multiplies them greatly. Be faithful in small things today and trust God to multiply them.

== 9:20
T: A new work
R: 'And Noah began to be an husbandman, and he planted a vineyard.' Noah returned to ordinary work. He became a farmer and planted a vineyard. After the dramatic events of the flood, life settled into normal rhythms. This is a reminder that even after great spiritual experiences, life goes on. We cannot live on mountaintops forever. We must return to the fields and the vineyards, to work and to daily responsibilities. That is where faith is tested and lived out. Planting a vineyard requires patience, since vines take years to produce good fruit. It is an act of hope in the future. Noah was investing in the new world God had given. Yet the story that follows shows that ordinary life also brings its own temptations. Today, embrace your ordinary work as part of God's calling. But also stay alert. Spiritual vigilance is needed not only in crises but in the quiet seasons of everyday life.

== 9:21
T: Even heroes fall
R: 'And he drank of the wine, and was drunken; and he was uncovered within his tent.' This is a shocking turn. Noah, the righteous man who walked with God and obeyed through the flood, became drunk and lay exposed in his tent. Scripture does not hide the failures of its heroes. Even the best of people are still sinners in need of grace. Noah's failure did not come during the crisis but afterward, in a time of peace and plenty. Sometimes we are most vulnerable when life is comfortable. Paul warns, Wherefore let him that thinketh he standeth take heed lest he fall. This verse also reminds us that no human hero can be our savior. Noah saved his family from the flood, but he could not save himself from sin. We need a perfect Savior, and only Jesus fits that description. Today, stay humble. Do not assume past faithfulness guarantees future strength. Depend on God daily, especially in seasons of ease.
S: Noah, a righteous man, became drunk and lay uncovered in his tent. Scripture shows that even heroes of faith are sinners in need of grace. Stay humble and depend on God daily, especially in times of ease.

== 9:22
T: Exposing another's shame
R: 'And Ham, the father of Canaan, saw the nakedness of his father, and told his two brethren without.' Ham saw his father's shameful condition and went out to tell his brothers. Rather than covering his father's shame, he exposed it. Perhaps he mocked or gossiped. His response revealed a lack of honor and love. It is one thing to stumble upon someone's failure; it is another to spread it. Proverbs says, He that covereth a transgression seeketh love; but he that repeateth a matter separateth very friends. When we learn of someone's weakness, we have a choice. We can broadcast it, or we can handle it with discretion and grace. Peter writes, charity shall cover the multitude of sins. That does not mean ignoring sin or enabling it, but refusing to delight in someone else's shame. Today, think about how you talk about others' failures. Choose love over gossip. Treat others' weaknesses as you would want yours to be treated.
S: Ham saw his father's nakedness and told his brothers rather than covering him. Exposing another's shame reveals a lack of love and honor. Choose today to cover others' failures with love rather than spread them.

== 9:23
T: Covering in love
R: 'And Shem and Japheth took a garment, and laid it upon both their shoulders, and went backward, and covered the nakedness of their father.' Shem and Japheth responded very differently from Ham. They took a garment and walked backward so they would not see their father's shame, and they covered him. This is a beautiful picture of love and respect. They did not deny that their father had failed. But they refused to look on his shame or add to it. They protected his dignity. This is how love responds to the failures of others. It does not gloat or expose. It covers and restores. Galatians 6 says, Brethren, if a man be overtaken in a fault, ye which are spiritual, restore such an one in the spirit of meekness. Above all, this reminds us of what God does for us. In Christ, He covers our shame. He does not look on our sin to expose it but to cover it with His righteousness. Today, cover someone with grace.

== 9:24
T: When the truth comes out
R: 'And Noah awoke from his wine, and knew what his younger son had done unto him.' Eventually, Noah woke up and learned what had happened. What is done in secret has a way of coming to light. Ham may have thought his mockery would stay hidden, but Noah found out. Jesus said, For there is nothing covered, that shall not be revealed; neither hid, that shall not be known. This is both a warning and a comfort. A warning, because our hidden sins will one day be exposed. A comfort, because the hidden wrongs done to us will also be brought to light, and God will set things right. Notice that Noah also had to face his own failure when he woke. His drunkenness was the occasion for Ham's sin. Both father and son had to reckon with what had happened. Today, live with integrity as one who knows that God sees all. And if you have something hidden, bring it into the light before God now.

== 9:25
T: Consequences across generations
R: 'And he said, Cursed be Canaan; a servant of servants shall he be unto his brethren.' Noah pronounced a curse on Canaan, Ham's son. This is a difficult verse, and it has sadly been misused throughout history to justify racism and slavery. That misuse is a grave distortion. The curse was specifically on Canaan, not on Ham's other descendants, and it was fulfilled in history when Israel later took the land of the Canaanites because of their great wickedness. It had nothing to do with skin color or race. Scripture insists that all people are made in God's image and that in Christ there is neither Jew nor Greek, bond nor free. The verse does teach that sin's consequences can ripple through generations. Our choices affect our children and grandchildren. Yet the gospel also declares that the curse can be broken in Christ, who became a curse for us. Today, pray that the patterns of sin in your family line would be broken by God's grace.

== 9:26
T: Blessed be the LORD God of Shem
R: 'And he said, Blessed be the LORD God of Shem; and Canaan shall be his servant.' Interestingly, Noah did not bless Shem directly. He blessed the LORD, the God of Shem. The greatest blessing Shem had was that the LORD was his God. Shem's line would carry the knowledge of the true God. From Shem would come Abraham, Israel, David, and ultimately Jesus Christ. The word Semitic comes from Shem's name. Noah's blessing was prophetic: the line of Shem would be the line through which God revealed Himself to the world. The greatest blessing anyone can have is to belong to the LORD. Wealth, health, and success are temporary, but having God as your God is eternal. Psalm 144 says, happy is that people, whose God is the LORD. Today, if the LORD is your God, you are truly blessed. Praise Him for revealing Himself to you. And thank Him that the promise carried through Shem's line reached you in Christ.

== 9:28
T: Life after the storm
R: 'And Noah lived after the flood three hundred and fifty years.' Noah lived another three hundred fifty years after the flood. That is a long time to remember. He had seen the old world destroyed and the new world begin. He watched his grandchildren and great-grandchildren spread across the earth. He likely lived long enough to see the tower of Babel and possibly even into the lifetime of Abraham. Noah's life after the flood reminds us that there is life after the storm. Whatever devastating experience you have gone through, it does not have to define the rest of your life. God can give many fruitful years after a crisis. Joseph lived many years after his trials and saw his family restored. Job was blessed with more after his suffering than before. Today, if you are on the other side of a great storm, embrace the life God still has for you. Your story is not over. There are still years of purpose ahead.

== 9:29
T: A life of faith completed
R: 'And all the days of Noah were nine hundred and fifty years: and he died.' Noah's life ends with the familiar refrain of Genesis 5. Even the man who survived the flood eventually died. Yet Noah's life was not defined by its ending. He is remembered as a man who found grace, walked with God, and obeyed through the most challenging assignment in history. Hebrews includes him in its great hall of faith: By faith Noah… became heir of the righteousness which is by faith. His life was not perfect, as the vineyard episode shows. But it was marked by faith. That is what God honors. Not flawless performance, but faith that trusts and obeys. When your life is summarized, it will not be perfect either. But it can be marked by faith. Today, think about the legacy you want to leave. Like Noah, let your life be remembered not for perfection but for walking with God, finding grace, and trusting His word.
