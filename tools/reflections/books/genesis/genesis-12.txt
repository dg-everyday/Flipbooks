# Genesis 12: The call of Abram

== 12:1
T: The call to go
R: 'Now the LORD had said unto Abram, Get thee out of thy country, and from thy kindred, and from thy father's house, unto a land that I will shew thee.' This is one of the great turning points in the Bible. God called Abram to leave everything familiar: his homeland, his relatives, and his father's household. And notice what God did not give him: a map. He simply said, unto a land that I will shew thee. Abram had to step out without knowing the destination. That is the essence of faith. Hebrews says, By faith Abraham, when he was called to go out into a place which he should after receive for an inheritance, obeyed; and he went out, not knowing whither he went. God still calls people to step out in faith. It may be a call to leave a comfortable place, a familiar habit, or a secure plan. He shows the next step. Today, listen for God's call, and be willing to go where He leads.
S: God called Abram to leave his country, his family and his father's house for a land He would show him. Abram had to step out without knowing the destination. Listen for God's call today and be willing to follow without seeing the whole path.
P: Faith steps out without a map. Genesis 12:1: 'Get thee out of thy country… unto a land that I will shew thee.' Will you follow where God leads today?

== 12:2
T: Blessed to be a blessing
R: God promised Abram, 'I will make of thee a great nation, and I will bless thee, and make thy name great; and thou shalt be a blessing.' What the builders of Babel tried to seize, God freely gave. They said, Let us make us a name. God said to Abram, I will make thy name great. True greatness comes from God, not from human striving. But notice the final phrase: thou shalt be a blessing. God's blessing was never meant to stop with Abram. He was blessed in order to bless others. This is the pattern for all of God's people. We are not blessed to hoard but to share. God gives us gifts, resources, and grace so that they will overflow to others. Paul writes that God is able to make all grace abound toward you, so that you may abound to every good work. Today, count the blessings God has given you. Then ask Him how you can pass them on. You are blessed to be a blessing.
S: God promised to make Abram a great nation, bless him and make his name great, so that he would be a blessing. God's blessing was meant to overflow to others. Ask God today how you can pass on the blessings He has given you.
P: Blessed to be a blessing. Genesis 12:2: 'I will bless thee, and make thy name great; and thou shalt be a blessing.' How will you bless someone today?

== 12:3
T: Blessing for all families
R: 'And I will bless them that bless thee, and curse him that curseth thee: and in thee shall all families of the earth be blessed.' This is one of the most important promises in the entire Bible. Through Abram, all families of the earth would be blessed. After the scattering of the nations at Babel, God announced His plan to bring blessing back to every people. Paul calls this verse the gospel preached beforehand: the Scripture, foreseeing that God would justify the heathen through faith, preached before the gospel unto Abraham. The blessing would come through Abraham's seed, Jesus Christ. Through Him, people from every family, tribe, and nation can be brought into God's blessing. This promise shows that God's heart has always been global. From the very beginning of His covenant with Israel, He had the whole world in view. Today, rejoice that you are included in this promise. And remember that God wants every family on earth to hear the good news.
S: God promised that through Abram all families of the earth would be blessed. Paul calls this promise the gospel preached beforehand, fulfilled in Jesus. Rejoice that you are included and pray for every family to hear the good news.
P: The gospel in Genesis! Genesis 12:3: 'in thee shall all families of the earth be blessed.' Through Jesus, Abraham's seed, God's blessing reaches every nation, including you.

== 12:4
T: Obedience at seventy-five
R: 'So Abram departed, as the LORD had spoken unto him; and Lot went with him: and Abram was seventy and five years old when he departed out of Haran.' Abram's response was simple: he departed. No long debate, no list of conditions. He went as the LORD had spoken. Faith is proven by obedience. And notice his age: seventy-five. Abram was not a young man looking for adventure. He was well settled, with wealth and a household. Yet he left it all at God's word. It is never too late to begin a new chapter of obedience. Some people think their best years of serving God are behind them, or that it is too late to change. Abram's story says otherwise. His greatest journey began in what many would consider retirement age. Caleb asked for a mountain at eighty-five. Moses began leading Israel at eighty. Today, whatever your age, God can call you to something new. Respond like Abram. Depart as the LORD has spoken.
S: Abram departed from Haran at seventy-five, simply obeying as the LORD had spoken. Faith is proven by obedience, and it is never too late to begin. Respond today to whatever God is calling you to, whatever your age.
P: It's never too late to obey God. Genesis 12:4: 'So Abram departed, as the LORD had spoken unto him… Abram was seventy and five years old.' Step out in faith today!

== 12:5
T: Arriving by faith
R: 'And Abram took Sarai his wife, and Lot his brother's son, and all their substance… and they went forth to go into the land of Canaan; and into the land of Canaan they came.' Notice the difference from chapter 11. There, Terah set out for Canaan but stopped in Haran. Here, Abram went forth to go into Canaan, and into the land of Canaan they came. Abram finished what his father started. He did not stop halfway. Abram also brought the souls that they had gotten in Haran. Many have understood this to mean the servants and people who had joined his household, perhaps even those he had influenced toward the true God. Abram did not travel alone. His faith affected many people around him. Your obedience is never just about you. It affects your family, your friends, and everyone connected to you. Today, finish the journey God has given you. Do not stop at Haran. Keep going until you arrive where He has called you.
S: Abram set out for Canaan with his family and household, and unlike his father, he arrived. His obedience affected many people who traveled with him. Finish the journey God has given you, and do not stop halfway.
P: Finish the journey. Genesis 12:5: 'they went forth to go into the land of Canaan; and into the land of Canaan they came.' Don't stop halfway. Keep going with God!

== 12:6
T: Promises in a contested land
R: 'And Abram passed through the land unto the place of Sichem, unto the plain of Moreh. And the Canaanite was then in the land.' Abram arrived in the land God promised, but there was a problem: the Canaanite was then in the land. The land was already occupied. Abram had obeyed, he had arrived, and yet the promise looked far from fulfilled. Other people lived there, built there, and controlled it. This is often how faith works. We step out in obedience and find that the promise is not yet visible. There are obstacles, opposition, and delays. The Canaanites in the land were a test of Abram's faith. Would he trust God's promise even when circumstances seemed to contradict it? Paul says Abraham against hope believed in hope. Faith does not deny the obstacles; it trusts God in spite of them. Today, if you have obeyed God and still see Canaanites in the land, do not lose heart. God's promise is not cancelled by present circumstances.
S: Abram arrived in Canaan, but the Canaanites already occupied the land. Obedience does not always bring immediate fulfillment of God's promises. Trust God's promise even when obstacles remain in view.
P: Obstacles don't cancel God's promises. Genesis 12:6: 'And the Canaanite was then in the land.' Abram trusted God anyway. Keep believing His promises today.

== 12:7
T: An altar of response
R: 'And the LORD appeared unto Abram, and said, Unto thy seed will I give this land: and there builded he an altar unto the LORD, who appeared unto him.' Right when Abram faced the reality of the Canaanites in the land, God appeared to him. God's timing is perfect. He renewed His promise at the very moment Abram needed it most. And Abram responded by building an altar. An altar was a place of worship, sacrifice, and remembrance. It was Abram's way of saying, I believe You. This land belongs to You and to Your promise. Abram built altars wherever he went, marking the land with worship before he ever possessed it. Worship is the right response to God's promises. Even before we see them fulfilled, we can thank Him for His faithfulness. Paul says Abraham was strong in faith, giving glory to God. Today, build an altar of worship in your heart. Thank God for His promises before you see them come to pass.
S: God appeared to Abram and promised to give the land to his seed, and Abram built an altar there. Worship is the right response to God's promises, even before they are fulfilled. Thank God today for His promises before you see them come to pass.
P: Worship before you see the promise fulfilled. Genesis 12:7: 'there builded he an altar unto the LORD, who appeared unto him.' Give God glory in faith today.

== 12:8
T: Tent and altar
R: 'And he removed from thence unto a mountain on the east of Bethel, and pitched his tent… and there he builded an altar unto the LORD, and called upon the name of the LORD.' Two things marked Abram's life wherever he went: a tent and an altar. The tent was temporary, a reminder that he was a pilgrim. The altar was permanent, a sign of his worship and relationship with God. Abram held loosely to earthly things but held tightly to God. That is a beautiful balance for every believer. Our homes, jobs, and possessions are tents, temporary dwellings. But our worship and walk with God are altars that endure. Notice too that Abram called upon the name of the LORD. Prayer was central to his life. He did not merely build structures; he spoke with God. Today, examine your priorities. Are you building permanent tents and temporary altars, or the other way around? Hold the things of this world loosely, and make worship and prayer the center of your life.
S: Wherever Abram went, he pitched a tent and built an altar. He held earthly things loosely while making worship and prayer central to his life. Make worship and prayer the permanent center of your life today.
P: A tent and an altar. Genesis 12:8: 'and pitched his tent… and there he builded an altar unto the LORD, and called upon the name of the LORD.' Hold the world loosely; hold God tightly.

== 12:9
T: Still journeying
R: 'And Abram journeyed, going on still toward the south.' This short verse captures the restless nature of Abram's life. He kept moving, journeying further into the land God had promised. He did not have a permanent home. He was a sojourner, always on the move. Hebrews tells us why: For he looked for a city which hath foundations, whose builder and maker is God. Abram's wandering was not aimless. He was a pilgrim with his eyes on something greater than any earthly land. The Christian life is also a journey. We are going on still, moving forward in faith, growing, learning, and following God's lead. We have not yet arrived. Paul wrote, I press toward the mark for the prize of the high calling of God in Christ Jesus. There is always more of God to know and more of His will to obey. Today, keep journeying. Do not settle into spiritual complacency. Go on still toward the destination God has prepared for you.
S: Abram kept journeying through the land God had promised him. Like him, believers are pilgrims always moving forward toward a heavenly city. Keep journeying in faith today and do not settle into complacency.
P: Keep moving forward with God. Genesis 12:9: 'And Abram journeyed, going on still toward the south.' Press on toward the prize. Don't settle!

== 12:10
T: Famine in the promised land
R: 'And there was a famine in the land: and Abram went down into Egypt to sojourn there; for the famine was grievous in the land.' Abram had obeyed God, arrived in the promised land, and built altars. Then famine struck. The land God promised could not feed him. This is a hard reality of faith. Obedience does not guarantee an easy life. Sometimes, right after we step out in faith, trouble comes. The famine tested Abram's faith. Would he stay and trust God, or would he leave and rely on his own solutions? Abram went down to Egypt. Scripture does not say he asked God first. In moments of crisis, our first instinct is often to solve the problem ourselves. Egypt in Scripture often represents relying on worldly resources instead of God. Isaiah warns, Woe to them that go down to Egypt for help. Today, if you are facing a famine, a season of lack or difficulty, bring it to God first. Seek His direction before running to your own solutions.
S: A grievous famine struck the promised land, and Abram went down to Egypt. Obedience does not guarantee an easy life, and crises test our faith. Seek God's direction first before running to your own solutions.
P: Trouble can come right after obedience. Genesis 12:10: 'And there was a famine in the land: and Abram went down into Egypt.' In your famine, seek God first.

== 12:11
T: When fear takes over
R: 'And it came to pass, when he was come near to enter into Egypt, that he said unto Sarai his wife, Behold now, I know that thou art a fair woman to look upon.' As Abram approached Egypt, fear began to grip him. He knew Sarai was beautiful, and he feared what the Egyptians might do. The man who had boldly left everything at God's command was now anxious and scheming. Abram had trusted God enough to leave Haran, but in Egypt, he trusted his own plans more than God's protection. Fear has a way of making us forget God's promises. God had promised to make Abram a great nation. How could that happen if Abram were killed? Yet in his fear, Abram forgot. This is a warning for all of us. Fear distorts our thinking and can lead us to compromise. Isaiah says, Fear thou not; for I am with thee. Today, when fear rises, remind yourself of God's promises. Let faith, not fear, guide your choices.
S: As Abram neared Egypt, fear gripped him about how the Egyptians might treat him because of Sarai's beauty. Fear can make even people of great faith forget God's promises. When fear rises today, remind yourself of what God has promised.
P: Fear makes us forget God's promises. Genesis 12:11: 'Behold now, I know that thou art a fair woman to look upon.' Abram let fear take over. Let faith guide you today.

== 12:12
T: Imagining the worst
R: Abram reasoned, 'Therefore it shall come to pass, when the Egyptians shall see thee, that they shall say, This is his wife: and they will kill me, but they will save thee alive.' Abram imagined the worst-case scenario and planned around it. He was certain the Egyptians would kill him. His mind ran ahead to disaster. This is how anxiety works. It writes stories about the future that have not happened and may never happen. And then it makes us act on those imaginary stories. Jesus addressed this directly: Take therefore no thought for the morrow: for the morrow shall take thought for the things of itself. Abram's fear was not entirely irrational; the danger was real. But his response left God out of the picture. He calculated without remembering the God who had called and promised to bless him. Today, notice when your mind starts writing worst-case stories. Stop and bring them to God. Replace imagined disasters with His promises. He holds your future.
S: Abram imagined the Egyptians would kill him and take Sarai, and he planned around that fear. Anxiety writes stories about the future and leads us to leave God out. Bring your worst-case thoughts to God and replace them with His promises.
P: Don't let anxiety write your future. Genesis 12:12: 'they will kill me, but they will save thee alive.' Abram imagined the worst. Bring your fears to God today.

== 12:13
T: The cost of a half-truth
R: 'Say, I pray thee, thou art my sister: that it may be well with me for thy sake; and my soul shall live because of thee.' Abram asked Sarai to say she was his sister. It was a half-truth, since she was his half-sister, as we learn later. But it was meant to deceive. And it put Sarai at great risk to protect himself. Half-truths are still lies when they are designed to mislead. Abram's plan was self-protective and placed his wife in danger. This was not love; it was fear. Husbands are called to protect their wives, as Christ loved the church and gave Himself for it. Abram did the opposite. But Scripture is honest about the failures of its heroes so that we will learn from them and see our need for grace. Today, examine your words. Are you using half-truths to protect yourself? Choose honesty, even when it feels risky, and trust God with the outcome.
S: Abram asked Sarai to say she was his sister, a half-truth meant to deceive and protect himself. Half-truths designed to mislead are still lies and often harm others. Choose honesty today and trust God with the outcome.
P: A half-truth is still a lie. Genesis 12:13: 'Say, I pray thee, thou art my sister.' Abram's fear led to deception. Choose honesty today and trust God.

== 12:14
T: When fears come true
R: 'And it came to pass, that, when Abram was come into Egypt, the Egyptians beheld the woman that she was very fair.' Abram's prediction partly came true. The Egyptians did notice Sarai's beauty. In a sense, his fear was justified. But his solution made things worse. Sometimes our fears are based on real dangers. The question is not whether danger exists but how we respond to it. Do we trust God or take matters into our own hands? Abram took control, and it led to a crisis. Had he trusted God, perhaps the story would have unfolded differently. David faced real enemies too, yet he wrote, What time I am afraid, I will trust in thee. Real dangers call for real faith, not deceptive schemes. Proverbs warns, The fear of man bringeth a snare: but whoso putteth his trust in the LORD shall be safe. Today, if you are facing a genuine threat, resist the urge to scheme. Bring it to God in prayer and trust His protection.
S: When Abram entered Egypt, the Egyptians noticed Sarai's great beauty, as he had feared. Even real dangers call for trust in God rather than deceptive schemes. Bring genuine threats to God in prayer and trust His protection today.
P: Real dangers need real faith. Genesis 12:14: 'the Egyptians beheld the woman that she was very fair.' Abram schemed instead of trusting. What time I am afraid, I will trust in God.

== 12:15
T: Consequences for others
R: 'The princes also of Pharaoh saw her, and commended her before Pharaoh: and the woman was taken into Pharaoh's house.' Abram's deception had consequences, and Sarai bore the worst of them. She was taken into Pharaoh's household. The woman through whom God's promised seed would come was now in a foreign king's palace. Abram's attempt to protect himself put God's entire plan at risk. Our sins rarely affect only ourselves. They ripple out and harm the people we love. Abram's fear-driven choice placed his wife in a terrible situation. This verse is painful to read. Yet it also sets the stage for God's intervention. When human schemes fail, God steps in. He would not let His promise be destroyed by Abram's failure. Today, consider how your choices affect those around you. Ask God to protect your loved ones from the consequences of your mistakes. And trust that He is able to intervene even when things seem out of control.
S: Pharaoh's princes praised Sarai, and she was taken into Pharaoh's house. Abram's deception placed his wife and God's promise at risk. Consider how your choices affect others and trust God to intervene when things seem out of control.
P: Our choices affect those we love. Genesis 12:15: 'the woman was taken into Pharaoh's house.' Abram's fear put Sarai at risk. But God was about to step in.

== 12:16
T: Prosperity is not approval
R: 'And he entreated Abram well for her sake: and he had sheep, and oxen, and he asses, and menservants, and maidservants, and she asses, and camels.' Ironically, Abram prospered through his deception. Pharaoh gave him livestock and servants because of Sarai. On the surface, things were going well. He was getting richer. But this wealth came at a terrible cost, his wife's honor and safety. Material prosperity is not always a sign of God's approval. Sometimes people gain wealth through compromise, and it looks like blessing. But gain through dishonesty is hollow. Jesus asked, For what shall it profit a man, if he shall gain the whole world, and lose his own soul? Some of the servants gained here may have included Hagar, the Egyptian maid, who would later bring much trouble into Abram's household. Wealth gained wrongly often brings complications. Today, examine whether any of your gains have come at an unacceptable cost. Choose integrity over profit, and trust God to provide.
S: Pharaoh treated Abram well and gave him livestock and servants because of Sarai. Prosperity gained through compromise is not a sign of God's approval. Choose integrity over profit and trust God to provide.

== 12:17
T: God protects His promise
R: 'And the LORD plagued Pharaoh and his house with great plagues because of Sarai Abram's wife.' Abram had failed, but God had not. The LORD intervened directly, sending plagues on Pharaoh's household to protect Sarai. Notice the phrase Sarai Abram's wife. God reaffirmed what Abram had denied. She was not just his sister; she was his wife, and the mother of the promised seed. God would not let His promise be undone by human weakness. This is a picture of grace. Abram did nothing to deserve this rescue. In fact, he caused the problem. But God's faithfulness does not depend on our faithfulness. Paul writes, If we believe not, yet he abideth faithful. This also foreshadows the later plagues on Egypt when God delivered Israel. God protects His people and His purposes. Today, thank God that His faithfulness is greater than your failures. He is able to protect His promises even when you have stumbled.
S: The LORD sent great plagues on Pharaoh's house to protect Sarai, Abram's wife. God's faithfulness to His promise did not depend on Abram's faithfulness. Thank God today that His faithfulness is greater than your failures.
P: God's faithfulness is greater than our failures. Genesis 12:17: 'the LORD plagued Pharaoh… because of Sarai Abram's wife.' He always protects His promises to His people!

== 12:18
T: Rebuked by an unbeliever
R: 'And Pharaoh called Abram, and said, What is this that thou hast done unto me? why didst thou not tell me that she was thy wife?' What an embarrassing moment. The pagan king rebuked the man of God. Pharaoh's question echoes God's question to Eve in the garden: What is this that thou hast done? Abram, who was meant to be a blessing to the nations, had instead brought a curse on Pharaoh's household. When believers behave dishonestly, the world notices. Sometimes unbelievers have higher standards than God's people. Paul wrote to the Romans that the name of God is blasphemed among the Gentiles through you, when God's people fail to live up to their calling. Our behavior reflects on the God we claim to serve. This is a call to integrity. Peter urges believers to have their conversation honest among the Gentiles, so that others may glorify God by their good works. Today, live in a way that brings honor to God's name. Let your conduct match your faith.
S: Pharaoh rebuked Abram for his deception, asking what he had done. When believers act dishonestly, the world notices and God's name is dishonored. Live today in a way that brings honor to the God you serve.
P: The world is watching. Genesis 12:18: 'What is this that thou hast done unto me?' A pagan king rebuked Abram. Let your conduct honor God today.

== 12:19
T: Truth restores what lies endanger
R: Pharaoh continued, 'Why saidst thou, She is my sister? so I might have taken her to me to wife: now therefore behold thy wife, take her, and go thy way.' Pharaoh spelled out how close disaster had come. Because of Abram's lie, Sarai might have become Pharaoh's wife. But now the truth was out, and Sarai was returned. Truth has a restoring power. Once the deception was exposed, the situation could be set right. Lies create confusion and danger; truth brings clarity and restoration. Jesus said, And ye shall know the truth, and the truth shall make you free. Sometimes we are afraid to tell the truth because of what it might cost us. But living in deception is far more costly. Proverbs says, The lip of truth shall be established for ever: but a lying tongue is but for a moment. Today, if you have been living with a deception, large or small, bring it into the light. Truth may be uncomfortable, but it opens the door to restoration.
S: Pharaoh explained how close he came to taking Sarai as his wife, and then returned her to Abram. Once the truth came out, the situation could be set right. Bring any deception into the light today and let truth restore what lies have endangered.
P: Truth restores; lies endanger. Genesis 12:19: 'now therefore behold thy wife, take her, and go thy way.' The truth set things right. Walk in truth today.

== 12:20
T: Sent away in grace
R: 'And Pharaoh commanded his men concerning him: and they sent him away, and his wife, and all that he had.' Abram left Egypt in a humiliating way, escorted out by Pharaoh's men. Yet he left with his wife and all his possessions. God's grace preserved him despite his failure. This episode is a mixture of shame and mercy. Abram's sin was exposed, but God protected him and his family. He did not lose what God had given him. This is the story of many believers. We stumble, we make foolish choices, and God graciously brings us through. Not without consequences, not without lessons, but with His promises still intact. Psalm 103 says, He hath not dealt with us after our sins; nor rewarded us according to our iniquities. The God who protected Abram in Egypt is the same God who keeps you. Today, learn from Abram's failure. But also rejoice in God's mercy. He does not abandon His children when they fall. He brings them out and sets them back on the path.
S: Pharaoh sent Abram away with his wife and all his possessions. Despite Abram's failure, God's grace preserved him and his family. Learn from his mistake and rejoice that God does not abandon His children when they fall.
P: God's mercy brings us through our failures. Genesis 12:20: 'they sent him away, and his wife, and all that he had.' He hasn't dealt with us after our sins. Praise Him!
