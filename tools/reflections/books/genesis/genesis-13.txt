# Genesis 13: Abram and Lot part ways

== 13:1
T: Coming back up
R: 'And Abram went up out of Egypt, he, and his wife, and all that he had, and Lot with him, into the south.' After his failure in Egypt, Abram went up. The direction matters. Going down to Egypt had been a spiritual descent; now he was going up, back toward the land God had promised. Repentance often involves a change of direction, turning back from where we wandered. Abram did not stay in Egypt to wallow in his shame. He returned. That is what God calls us to do when we fail. The prodigal son came to himself and said, I will arise and go to my father. But God welcomes those who return. He is not waiting to condemn but to restore. Today, if you have wandered somewhere you should not have gone, it is time to go up. Turn around and head back toward God. He is ready to receive you and set you back on the path of His promise.
S: After his failure in Egypt, Abram went up out of Egypt back toward the promised land. Repentance involves turning around and returning to God. If you have wandered, go back up to Him today.
P: It's time to go back up. Genesis 13:1: 'And Abram went up out of Egypt.' After failure, Abram returned. If you've wandered, turn back to God today.

== 13:2
T: Rich in more than goods
R: 'And Abram was very rich in cattle, in silver, and in gold.' Abram returned from Egypt a wealthy man. God had blessed him materially. Scripture does not condemn wealth in itself. Abraham, Job, and David were all rich. But wealth brings responsibilities and temptations. It can draw our hearts away from God or become a tool for His purposes. The next verses show how wealth would test Abram's character, as conflict arose with Lot over their possessions. Paul instructed Timothy, Charge them that are rich in this world, that they be not highminded, nor trust in uncertain riches, but in the living God. The issue is not how much we have but whether we trust in it. Abram's true riches were not his cattle, silver, and gold, but his relationship with God. Today, whatever your financial situation, examine where your trust lies. Are you rich toward God? Use whatever resources you have to honor Him and bless others.
S: Abram returned from Egypt very rich in cattle, silver and gold. Wealth is not wrong in itself, but it brings responsibilities and temptations. Examine where your trust lies and be rich toward God.
P: True riches are found in God. Genesis 13:2: 'And Abram was very rich in cattle, in silver, and in gold.' Wealth tests the heart. Trust in the living God, not uncertain riches.

== 13:3
T: Back to where it began
R: 'And he went on his journeys from the south even to Bethel, unto the place where his tent had been at the beginning, between Bethel and Hai.' Abram returned to the place where he had been at the beginning. He went back to Bethel, where he had first built an altar and called on the LORD. There is something powerful about returning to the beginning, to the place where your faith first came alive. When we drift, the way back often involves remembering where we started. Jesus told the church in Ephesus, Remember therefore from whence thou art fallen, and repent, and do the first works. Sometimes we need to go back to our first love, to the simple faith and devotion we once had. What were the habits, the prayers, the passion that marked your early walk with God? Perhaps it is time to return to them. Today, think back to your Bethel, the place where you first met God. Return there in your heart and renew your devotion to Him.
S: Abram returned to Bethel, the place where he had first built an altar. When we drift, the way back often involves returning to where our faith began. Remember your first love for God and return to it today.

== 13:4
T: Calling on the LORD again
R: Abram came 'unto the place of the altar, which he had made there at the first: and there Abram called on the name of the LORD.' The altar was still there, waiting for him. And Abram did what he had done before: he called on the name of the LORD. Notice there is no mention of an altar in Egypt. In Egypt, Abram schemed; back at Bethel, he prayed. The difference between those two places is the difference between fear and faith. When we are away from God, we rely on ourselves. When we return, we call on Him. The altar represents restored fellowship. God had not moved. Abram had. And when he came back, the place of worship was still there. God's welcome is always waiting. James says, Draw nigh to God, and he will draw nigh to you. Today, return to your place of prayer. If it has been a while, do not let shame keep you away. The altar is still there. Call on the name of the LORD.

== 13:5
T: Blessing by association
R: 'And Lot also, which went with Abram, had flocks, and herds, and tents.' Lot had prospered too. Much of his wealth likely came from being associated with Abram. He shared in the blessings God poured on his uncle. There is a principle here: those who walk with God's people often share in God's blessings. Laban later admitted that he had been blessed because of Jacob. Potiphar's house was blessed because of Joseph. Being near those who walk with God can bring blessing. But Lot's story also shows the limits of borrowed blessing. Lot had flocks and tents, but did he have his own altar? Scripture never mentions Lot building an altar. His prosperity was real, but his faith seems to have been secondhand. Many people grow up around faith without making it their own. Being close to godly people is a gift, but it is not enough. Each of us must know God personally. Today, ask yourself whether your faith is truly your own. Build your own altar.
S: Lot prospered alongside Abram, sharing in the blessings God gave his uncle. Yet borrowed blessing is not the same as personal faith. Make sure today that your faith is truly your own.

== 13:6
T: When blessing brings strain
R: 'And the land was not able to bear them, that they might dwell together: for their substance was great, so that they could not dwell together.' Abram and Lot had been blessed so much that their possessions became a problem. The land could not support all their flocks and herds. Abundance created pressure. It is ironic but true: blessings can create conflict. Families argue over inheritances. Churches divide over growth. Friends drift apart over success. Prosperity tests relationships in ways that hardship sometimes does not. When we have little, we often depend on one another. When we have much, we may compete. Solomon observed, When goods increase, they are increased that eat them. More possessions mean more to manage, more to protect, and more potential for tension. Today, examine whether any of your blessings have become sources of strain in your relationships. Ask God for wisdom to handle abundance in a way that preserves love and peace. People matter more than possessions.
S: Abram and Lot had so many possessions that the land could not support them both. Blessings and abundance can create strain in relationships. Ask God for wisdom to handle abundance in a way that preserves love and peace.
P: Blessings can bring strain. Genesis 13:6: 'their substance was great, so that they could not dwell together.' Remember that people matter more than possessions.

== 13:7
T: Strife before watching eyes
R: 'And there was a strife between the herdmen of Abram's cattle and the herdmen of Lot's cattle: and the Canaanite and the Perizzite dwelled then in the land.' The tension turned into open conflict between the herdsmen. And Scripture adds a significant detail: the Canaanite and the Perizzite dwelled then in the land. The surrounding peoples were watching. Abram's household was supposed to be a witness to the true God, and here they were fighting among themselves. Conflict among believers damages our witness. When Christians quarrel, the world takes notice. Jesus said, By this shall all men know that ye are my disciples, if ye have love one to another. Our unity is a testimony. Our division is a stumbling block. That does not mean believers will never disagree. But how we handle disagreements matters. Today, think about any conflicts in your church, family, or friendships. Remember that others are watching. Ask God to help you handle disagreements in a way that honors Him.
S: Strife broke out between the herdsmen of Abram and Lot while the Canaanites watched. Conflict among believers damages our witness before the world. Handle disagreements today in a way that honors God.
P: The world is watching how we handle conflict. Genesis 13:7: 'there was a strife… and the Canaanite and the Perizzite dwelled then in the land.' Let love be your witness.

== 13:8
T: Peacemaking
R: 'And Abram said unto Lot, Let there be no strife, I pray thee, between me and thee, and between my herdmen and thy herdmen; for we be brethren.' Abram took the initiative to make peace. As the elder and the one to whom God had given the promise, he could have insisted on his rights. Instead, he humbly appealed to Lot: let there be no strife, for we be brethren. Family relationship mattered more to Abram than winning an argument. This is the heart of a peacemaker. Jesus said, Blessed are the peacemakers: for they shall be called the children of God. Peacemaking requires humility, initiative, and a willingness to value relationships over rights. Psalm 133 says, Behold, how good and how pleasant it is for brethren to dwell together in unity! Abram reminded Lot of what they shared. Often, the path to peace begins by remembering what unites us. Today, is there a conflict where you could take the first step toward peace? Do not wait for the other person. Be the peacemaker.
S: Abram took the initiative to make peace with Lot, reminding him that they were brethren. Peacemaking requires humility and valuing relationships above rights. Take the first step toward peace in a conflict today.
P: Be the peacemaker. Genesis 13:8: 'Let there be no strife, I pray thee, between me and thee… for we be brethren.' Don't wait. Take the first step today.

== 13:9
T: Letting the other choose
R: 'Is not the whole land before thee? separate thyself, I pray thee, from me: if thou wilt take the left hand, then I will go to the right; or if thou depart to the right hand, then I will go to the left.' Abram made a remarkably generous offer. He let Lot choose first. As the elder and the one with God's promise, Abram had every right to pick the best land. But he gave up that right for the sake of peace. This reveals Abram's trust in God. He did not need to grasp for the best land because he knew God had promised to give it to him. When we trust God's provision, we can afford to be generous. We do not have to fight for our share. Paul writes, Let nothing be done through strife or vainglory; but in lowliness of mind let each esteem other better than themselves. Today, look for an opportunity to let someone else go first. Trust God to take care of you.
S: Abram let Lot choose first which part of the land he wanted. Because Abram trusted God's promise, he could afford to be generous. Let someone else go first today and trust God to take care of you.
P: Trust frees us to be generous. Genesis 13:9: 'Is not the whole land before thee?' Abram let Lot choose first. Trust God and let someone else go first today.

== 13:10
T: Choosing by appearance
R: 'And Lot lifted up his eyes, and beheld all the plain of Jordan, that it was well watered every where… even as the garden of the LORD, like the land of Egypt.' Lot looked and saw a land that seemed perfect, well watered, lush, like the garden of the LORD. It reminded him of Egypt. Lot made his choice based on what looked best to the eye. But Scripture adds an ominous note: before the LORD destroyed Sodom and Gomorrah. What looked like paradise was headed for destruction. Lot chose based on appearance and economic advantage. He did not seem to consider the spiritual condition of the place. Many choices look attractive on the surface but carry hidden dangers. A job, a relationship, a neighborhood can seem ideal while leading us away from God. Samuel was told, man looketh on the outward appearance, but the LORD looketh on the heart. Today, before making any significant decision, look beyond appearances. Ask God to show you the spiritual realities behind what looks attractive.

== 13:11
T: The direction of a choice
R: 'Then Lot chose him all the plain of Jordan; and Lot journeyed east: and they separated themselves the one from the other.' Lot chose for himself. The phrase chose him emphasizes his self-interest. He took the best land without regard for his uncle who had been so generous. And Lot journeyed east. Throughout Genesis, moving east often signals moving away from God. Adam and Eve were driven east of Eden. Cain went east to Nod. The builders of Babel journeyed from the east. Now Lot went east toward Sodom. His choice was not just geographic; it was spiritual. He separated himself from Abram and from the influence of his godly uncle. The choices we make about where we live, who we associate with, and what we pursue shape our spiritual direction. Small decisions can set us on paths that lead far from God. Today, consider the direction of your choices. Are they moving you toward God or away from Him? Choose the path that keeps you close to Him.

== 13:12
T: Pitching toward Sodom
R: 'Abram dwelled in the land of Canaan, and Lot dwelled in the cities of the plain, and pitched his tent toward Sodom.' Notice the gradual progression. Lot first looked toward the plain, then chose it, then pitched his tent toward Sodom. Later we will find him living inside Sodom and sitting in its gate. Spiritual decline rarely happens all at once. It happens step by step. First we look, then we move closer, then we settle in. Lot did not move into Sodom immediately. He pitched his tent toward it. He positioned himself in its direction. Psalm 1 describes a similar progression: walking in the counsel of the ungodly, standing in the way of sinners, sitting in the seat of the scornful. Each step seems small, but together they lead far. Today, ask yourself which direction your tent is facing. Are you slowly moving toward something that will harm your soul? It is much easier to turn your tent around now than to escape Sodom later.
S: Lot dwelled in the cities of the plain and pitched his tent toward Sodom. Spiritual decline usually happens gradually, one small step at a time. Check which direction your tent is facing today and turn it around if needed.

== 13:13
T: Exceedingly wicked
R: 'But the men of Sodom were wicked and sinners before the LORD exceedingly.' Scripture is clear about the character of Sodom. Its people were wicked, sinners before the LORD exceedingly. This was the place Lot had chosen to move toward. He knew, or should have known, the reputation of the city. Yet the economic opportunity seemed worth the risk. Ezekiel later describes Sodom's sins: pride, fulness of bread, and abundance of idleness, and that she did not strengthen the hand of the poor and needy. They were haughty and committed abomination. Sodom was prosperous but spiritually bankrupt. Its comfort had bred arrogance and cruelty. Many places today are like Sodom: wealthy, comfortable, and far from God. Christians are not called to withdraw from the world, but we are called to be careful about where we put down roots and what we allow to shape us. Today, pray for the cities of our world that are far from God. And guard your heart from being shaped by their values.

== 13:14
T: Lift up your eyes
R: 'And the LORD said unto Abram, after that Lot was separated from him, Lift up now thine eyes, and look from the place where thou art northward, and southward, and eastward, and westward.' After Lot left, God spoke to Abram. Lot had lifted up his eyes and chosen for himself. Now God told Abram to lift up his eyes and see what God was giving him. The contrast is beautiful. Lot chose by sight; Abram received by promise. Abram had given up his right to choose, and God responded by showing him everything. Notice that God spoke after Lot was separated. Sometimes God waits until a difficult parting is complete before He speaks His next word. But in that moment of loss, God came with fresh vision. When we give up something for the sake of peace or obedience, God does not leave us empty. Today, lift up your eyes from your losses. Look at what God has promised you. His vision is bigger than anything you gave up.

== 13:15
T: All the land you see
R: 'For all the land which thou seest, to thee will I give it, and to thy seed for ever.' Abram had let Lot take the best portion, but God promised Abram all of it. Everything he could see in every direction would belong to him and his descendants forever. Abram gave up a part and received the whole. This is how God works with those who trust Him. Jesus said, Give, and it shall be given unto you; good measure, pressed down, and shaken together, and running over. When we release our grip on what we think we deserve, God often gives far more than we imagined. Lot got the plain of Jordan, and it was destroyed. Abram got the promise of God, and it lasted forever. Notice the words for ever. God's promises have eternal significance. What we grasp for ourselves is temporary; what God gives is lasting. Today, release your grip on something you have been clutching. Trust God to give you what is truly best, and what will last forever.

== 13:16
T: As the dust of the earth
R: 'And I will make thy seed as the dust of the earth: so that if a man can number the dust of the earth, then shall thy seed also be numbered.' Abram had no children, and Sarai was barren. Yet God promised descendants as countless as the dust of the earth. This was an impossible promise by any human measure. Later God would compare Abram's descendants to the stars of heaven and the sand of the seashore. The dust of the earth speaks of number, but it also reminds us of humility. We are made from dust. God takes ordinary, humble material and multiplies it beyond counting. Through Jesus, Abraham's spiritual descendants now include believers from every nation. Paul writes, if ye be Christ's, then are ye Abraham's seed, and heirs according to the promise. If you belong to Christ, you are part of the fulfillment of this promise. You are one of those countless descendants. Today, marvel that God's impossible promise to a childless old man included you.

== 13:17
T: Walk through the land
R: 'Arise, walk through the land in the length of it and in the breadth of it; for I will give it unto thee.' God invited Abram to explore the land, to walk through its length and breadth. This was an act of faith. Abram did not yet own any of it. The Canaanites still lived there. But God told him to walk through it as one who would inherit it. Faith walks through God's promises before they are fully realized. It explores, enjoys, and claims what God has said. God later told Joshua, Every place that the sole of your foot shall tread upon, that have I given unto you. In the Christian life, we are invited to walk through God's promises: His peace, His provision, His presence. These are ours in Christ. Paul prays that believers may comprehend the breadth, and length, and depth, and height of Christ's love. Today, arise and walk through God's promises. Explore the length and breadth of what He has given you in Christ.
S: God told Abram to walk through the length and breadth of the land He would give him. Faith explores and claims God's promises before they are fully realized. Walk through the promises God has given you in Christ today.

== 13:18
T: Settling with an altar
R: 'Then Abram removed his tent, and came and dwelt in the plain of Mamre, which is in Hebron, and built there an altar unto the LORD.' Abram moved to Hebron and, once again, built an altar. Wherever Abram settled, worship came first. Lot pitched his tent toward Sodom; Abram pitched his tent near an altar. The contrast between the two men could not be clearer. One oriented his life toward worldly advantage, the other toward God. Hebron would become a significant place in Israel's history. Abraham, Sarah, Isaac, Rebekah, Jacob, and Leah would be buried there. David would first reign as king in Hebron. But it all began with Abram building an altar. Where we set up our lives and what we put at the center matters deeply. Today, look at the center of your home and your schedule. Is there an altar there? Make worship the foundation of wherever God has placed you. Build your life around Him, and He will make it a place of lasting blessing.
S: Abram settled at Mamre in Hebron and built an altar to the LORD there. Unlike Lot, he oriented his life around worship. Make worship the foundation of your home and your life today.
