# Genesis 16: Hagar and the God who sees

== 16:1
T: When waiting grows long
R: 'Now Sarai Abram's wife bare him no children: and she had an handmaid, an Egyptian, whose name was Hagar.' Years had passed since God's promise. Abram had been counted righteous by faith, and the covenant had been sealed. Yet Sarai still had no children. The waiting was painful. And there was Hagar, an Egyptian maid, likely acquired during Abram's ill-fated trip to Egypt. The consequences of past compromises have a way of resurfacing. Hagar's presence would become the occasion for a new mistake. Long waiting is one of the greatest tests of faith. When God's promise seems delayed, we are tempted to take matters into our own hands. We look around for some means to make it happen. Isaiah says, they that wait upon the LORD shall renew their strength. Waiting is not wasted; it is where strength is renewed. Today, if you have been waiting a long time, resist the urge to force the outcome. Keep trusting. God's timing is perfect.
S: Years after God's promise, Sarai still had no children, and she had an Egyptian maid named Hagar. Long waiting tempts us to take matters into our own hands. Keep trusting God's perfect timing rather than forcing the outcome.
P: Waiting is hard, but don't force it. Genesis 16:1: 'Now Sarai Abram's wife bare him no children.' The long wait tested their faith. Keep trusting God's timing today.

== 16:2
T: Running ahead of God
R: 'And Sarai said unto Abram, Behold now, the LORD hath restrained me from bearing: I pray thee, go in unto my maid; it may be that I may obtain children by her. And Abram hearkened to the voice of Sarai.' Sarai proposed a solution that was culturally acceptable at the time. A servant could bear a child on behalf of a barren wife. It seemed reasonable. Sarai even acknowledged that the LORD had restrained her. But instead of waiting on God, she devised her own plan. And Abram listened to her, just as Adam had listened to Eve. The echo is deliberate. Once again, a husband followed his wife's suggestion instead of seeking God. Notice there is no prayer here, no seeking of God's counsel. Running ahead of God often looks reasonable at the time. But human solutions to divine promises create complications. Today, before acting on any plan, even a reasonable one, bring it to God in prayer. Ask Him whether it is His way or merely your own.
S: Sarai suggested that Abram have a child through her maid Hagar, and Abram agreed. They ran ahead of God with a plan that seemed reasonable but did not seek Him. Bring your plans to God in prayer before acting on them.
P: Don't run ahead of God. Genesis 16:2: 'go in unto my maid; it may be that I may obtain children by her.' Their plan seemed reasonable but wasn't God's way. Pray before you act.

== 16:3
T: Ten years of waiting
R: 'And Sarai Abram's wife took Hagar her maid the Egyptian, after Abram had dwelt ten years in the land of Canaan, and gave her to her husband Abram to be his wife.' Ten years. That is how long they had waited since entering Canaan. Ten years of hoping, praying, and watching each month pass without a child. We can understand their frustration. Ten years is a long time to wait for a promise. Yet God's timing was not yet complete. In fact, it would be another fifteen years before Isaac was born. The language of this verse echoes Genesis 3, where Eve took the fruit and gave it to her husband. Taking and giving, outside of God's will, leads to pain. Sometimes the hardest moment to trust God is right after a long period of waiting, when our patience has worn thin. But God's delays are not denials. Habakkuk says, though it tarry, wait for it; because it will surely come. Today, do not give up after years of waiting. God has not forgotten.
S: After ten years in Canaan without a child, Sarai gave Hagar to Abram as a wife. Their patience had worn thin, and they took matters into their own hands. Do not give up after long waiting; God has not forgotten His promise.
P: God's delays aren't denials. Genesis 16:3: 'after Abram had dwelt ten years in the land of Canaan.' Ten years of waiting, and still more to come. Don't give up!

== 16:4
T: Plans that backfire
R: 'And he went in unto Hagar, and she conceived: and when she saw that she had conceived, her mistress was despised in her eyes.' The plan worked, at least on the surface. Hagar conceived. But immediately problems arose. Hagar began to look down on Sarai. The woman who had been a servant now felt superior to her barren mistress. What was meant to solve a problem created a new and painful one. Human schemes often produce results, but they also produce complications. Sarai gained a pregnancy in her household but lost peace in her home. Relationships were strained, and pride entered the picture. Proverbs says, Every way of a man is right in his own eyes: but the LORD pondereth the hearts. Our plans may seem to succeed, but if they are not God's plans, they bring unexpected pain. Today, examine any area where your own solution has created new problems. It is never too late to bring the mess to God and ask Him for wisdom.
S: Hagar conceived, but then she began to despise Sarai. The plan that seemed to solve a problem created new pain in the household. Bring the complications of your own plans to God and ask for His wisdom.
P: Our plans can backfire. Genesis 16:4: 'she conceived: and when she saw that she had conceived, her mistress was despised in her eyes.' Bring the mess to God today.

== 16:5
T: Blaming others
R: 'And Sarai said unto Abram, My wrong be upon thee: I have given my maid into thy bosom… the LORD judge between me and thee.' Sarai turned on Abram. She blamed him for the situation, even though it had been her idea. The blame game that began in Eden continued in Abram's household. When plans fail, our instinct is often to point fingers. Sarai's pain was real. She felt despised and humiliated. But blaming Abram did not solve the problem. It only added strain to their marriage. Notice also that Sarai called on the LORD to judge between them. She brought God into the conflict, but as a weapon rather than as a healer. Sometimes we invoke God's name in our disputes not to seek His wisdom but to prove we are right. James says, the wrath of man worketh not the righteousness of God. Today, if you are in conflict, resist the urge to blame. Take ownership of your part. Bring the situation to God humbly, seeking healing rather than vindication.
S: Sarai blamed Abram for the conflict with Hagar, though it had been her own idea. Blame adds strain to relationships and does not solve problems. Take ownership of your part today and seek healing rather than vindication.
P: Blame doesn't heal. Genesis 16:5: 'My wrong be upon thee.' Sarai blamed Abram for her own plan. Take ownership and seek God's healing today.

== 16:6
T: Passivity and harshness
R: 'But Abram said unto Sarai, Behold, thy maid is in thy hand; do to her as it pleaseth thee. And when Sarai dealt hardly with her, she fled from her face.' Abram did not take responsibility. He passed the problem back to Sarai, saying, do to her as it pleaseth thee. He avoided conflict instead of leading wisely. And Sarai treated Hagar harshly, so harshly that the pregnant servant fled into the wilderness. Everyone in this story made mistakes. Abram was passive. Sarai was harsh. Hagar was proud. And a vulnerable woman ended up alone in the desert. Sin in a household rarely affects just one person. It spreads, wounding everyone. Leadership requires courage to address problems justly, not to avoid them. Kindness requires restraint even when we feel wronged. Paul writes, be ye kind one to another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another. Today, consider whether you have been passive when you should lead, or harsh when you should be kind. Ask God to give you both courage and gentleness.
S: Abram passed responsibility to Sarai, who treated Hagar so harshly that she fled. Passivity and harshness together wounded everyone in the household. Ask God for both the courage to lead and the gentleness to be kind.
P: Passivity and harshness both wound. Genesis 16:6: 'And when Sarai dealt hardly with her, she fled from her face.' Ask God for courage and kindness today.

== 16:7
T: Found in the wilderness
R: 'And the angel of the LORD found her by a fountain of water in the wilderness, by the fountain in the way to Shur.' Hagar was alone, pregnant, and running away. She was an Egyptian servant, an outsider, and seemingly forgotten. But the angel of the LORD found her. This is the first appearance of the angel of the LORD in Scripture, and it is to a runaway slave woman. God sought out the one everyone else had cast aside. Many believe the angel of the LORD was a pre-incarnate appearance of Christ Himself. The Good Shepherd was already seeking the lost. Notice where God found her: by a fountain of water in the wilderness. In the dry place, God met her at a spring. Hagar was heading toward Shur, back toward Egypt. She was going home the only way she knew. But God intercepted her. Today, if you feel alone and overlooked, know that God sees you. He finds people in the wilderness, and He meets them at the spring.
S: The angel of the LORD found Hagar by a spring of water in the wilderness. God sought out a runaway servant whom others had cast aside. If you feel alone today, know that God sees you and comes looking for you.
P: God finds us in the wilderness. Genesis 16:7: 'the angel of the LORD found her by a fountain of water in the wilderness.' You're never too far for Him to find.

== 16:8
T: Where did you come from?
R: 'And he said, Hagar, Sarai's maid, whence camest thou? and whither wilt thou go? And she said, I flee from the face of my mistress Sarai.' The angel called Hagar by name. To Abram and Sarai, she had been my maid and thy maid. But God called her Hagar. He knew her personally. Then He asked two questions: Where have you come from, and where are you going? These are questions for every one of us. Where have I come from? What is my story, my pain, my past? And where am I going? What direction is my life taking? Hagar could answer the first question: she was fleeing. But she had no answer to the second. She was running away without a destination. Many people are like that, fleeing pain without knowing where they are headed. God's questions invite us to stop and reflect. Today, let God ask you these questions. Answer them honestly. He calls you by name and cares about both your past and your future.
S: The angel called Hagar by name and asked where she had come from and where she was going. God knows us personally and cares about our past and our future. Let God ask you these questions today and answer Him honestly.
P: God calls you by name. Genesis 16:8: 'Hagar, Sarai's maid, whence camest thou? and whither wilt thou go?' Where are you coming from and going? Answer Him honestly today.

== 16:9
T: The hard road back
R: 'And the angel of the LORD said unto her, Return to thy mistress, and submit thyself under her hands.' This was a hard command. God told Hagar to go back to the very situation she had fled, to the mistress who had treated her harshly. It would have been natural for Hagar to expect God to rescue her from Sarai entirely. Instead, He sent her back. Sometimes God's answer is not escape but endurance. He does not always remove us from difficult situations; sometimes He sends us back into them with His presence and promise. Hagar would return not as the same woman who fled, but as one who had met God. That changes everything. God did not send her back without hope. In the next verses, He gave her a promise for her future. Peter writes about enduring grief while suffering wrongfully, noting that this is commendable before God. Today, if God is calling you back to a hard place, trust that He goes with you. His presence makes the hard road bearable.
S: The angel told Hagar to return to Sarai and submit to her. Sometimes God's answer is not escape but endurance with His presence. If He calls you back to a hard place, trust that He goes with you.
P: Sometimes God sends us back. Genesis 16:9: 'Return to thy mistress, and submit thyself under her hands.' Not escape, but endurance with His presence. Trust Him in the hard place.

== 16:10
T: A promise for the outsider
R: 'And the angel of the LORD said unto her, I will multiply thy seed exceedingly, that it shall not be numbered for multitude.' God gave Hagar a promise very similar to the one He gave Abram. Her descendants would be too many to count. This servant woman, an Egyptian, an outsider to the covenant family, received a personal promise from God. God's compassion was not limited to Abram's household. He cared for Hagar and her unborn child. This shows the wideness of God's mercy. Even those who seem to be on the margins of God's story are not forgotten. Hagar was a foreigner and a servant, yet God spoke blessing over her. Throughout Scripture, God shows special care for the stranger, the widow, and the oppressed. Deuteronomy says God loveth the stranger. If you feel like an outsider, unworthy of God's attention, take comfort from Hagar's story. God sees you and has a future for you. His blessings reach further than we imagine.
S: God promised Hagar that her descendants would be too many to count. His compassion reached an Egyptian servant outside the covenant family. Take comfort that God sees outsiders and has a future for them.
P: God's mercy reaches the outsider. Genesis 16:10: 'I will multiply thy seed exceedingly.' God blessed Hagar, a servant and a foreigner. He sees you too.

== 16:11
T: God hears
R: 'And the angel of the LORD said unto her, Behold, thou art with child, and shalt bear a son, and shalt call his name Ishmael; because the LORD hath heard thy affliction.' God named Hagar's son before he was born: Ishmael, meaning God hears. Every time Hagar called her son's name, she would remember that God had heard her affliction. Her suffering was not unnoticed. Her cries had reached heaven. This is a profound comfort for anyone in pain. God hears. He hears the cries of the mistreated, the lonely, and the overlooked. Exodus records God saying, I have surely seen the affliction of my people… and have heard their cry. Psalm 34 says, This poor man cried, and the LORD heard him, and saved him out of all his troubles. You may feel that no one understands your pain. But God hears. He knows every tear and every silent prayer. Today, bring your affliction to Him. Let the name Ishmael remind you: God hears, and He cares.
S: God told Hagar to name her son Ishmael, meaning God hears, because He had heard her affliction. Her suffering had not gone unnoticed. Bring your pain to God today, knowing that He hears and cares.

== 16:12
T: An honest future
R: 'And he will be a wild man; his hand will be against every man, and every man's hand against him; and he shall dwell in the presence of all his brethren.' God gave Hagar an honest description of her son's future. Ishmael would be a free-spirited, independent man, living in conflict with others yet surviving among them. This was not a curse but a realistic picture. Ishmael would be strong, untamed, and resilient. God does not always give us rosy predictions. He tells the truth. Yet even in this description, there is a promise of survival and presence. Ishmael would dwell in the presence of all his brethren. He would not be destroyed. God's honesty is a mark of His love. He does not deceive us with false hopes. But He also promises His care through every struggle. Today, ask God to give you an honest view of your situation, and to help you trust Him with whatever the future holds. His truth sets us free.
S: God told Hagar that Ishmael would be a wild, independent man in conflict with others. God gives honest descriptions of the future, not false hopes. Ask Him for an honest view of your situation and trust Him with the future.

== 16:13
T: The God who sees me
R: 'And she called the name of the LORD that spake unto her, Thou God seest me: for she said, Have I also here looked after him that seeth me?' Hagar gave God a name, the only person in Scripture recorded as doing so. She called Him El Roi, the God who sees me. This forgotten servant had been seen. In her loneliness, her pain, and her flight, God had seen her. And she had seen Him. This is one of the most tender moments in Genesis. The God of the universe took notice of a runaway slave in the desert. He is not only the God of the powerful and the prominent. He is the God who sees the overlooked. If you feel invisible today, unnoticed at work, forgotten by friends, overlooked in your family, remember Hagar's name for God. He sees you. Psalm 33 says, The LORD looketh from heaven; he beholdeth all the sons of men. He sees you with eyes of love. You are never invisible to Him.
S: Hagar named the LORD the God who sees me. The God of the universe took notice of a forgotten servant in the desert. If you feel invisible today, remember that God sees you with eyes of love.
P: You are seen. Genesis 16:13: 'Thou God seest me.' Hagar, a forgotten servant, discovered the God who sees. He sees you today too.

== 16:14
T: A well to remember
R: 'Wherefore the well was called Beer-lahai-roi; behold, it is between Kadesh and Bered.' The well where Hagar met God was given a name: Beer-lahai-roi, meaning the well of the Living One who sees me. The place became a memorial. Years later, Isaac would live near this very well. A place of encounter became a lasting landmark. It is good to mark the places where God has met us. Not necessarily physical wells, but moments and places in our lives where we encountered His presence. Remembering these moments strengthens our faith in later trials. Samuel set up a stone called Ebenezer, saying, Hitherto hath the LORD helped us. Do you have a Beer-lahai-roi in your life? A moment when you knew God saw you and spoke to you? Write it down. Tell someone about it. Return to it in your memory when you feel unseen. Today, thank God for the wells in your past where He met you. Let those memories carry you forward.
S: The well where Hagar met God was named Beer-lahai-roi, the well of the Living One who sees me. Places of encounter with God can become lasting memorials. Remember and give thanks for the times God has met you.

== 16:15
T: Promise kept to Hagar
R: 'And Hagar bare Abram a son: and Abram called his son's name, which Hagar bare, Ishmael.' Hagar returned as God had told her, and she gave birth to a son. Abram named him Ishmael, the very name the angel had given. That means Hagar must have told Abram about her encounter with God. Abram accepted the name. Every time the boy's name was spoken in the household, it testified that God hears. God's word to Hagar was fulfilled exactly. Her return to the household was not in vain. The son was born, and the name proclaimed God's care. Even in a situation born out of human impatience, God brought something meaningful. He did not abandon Hagar or Ishmael. God can bring good out of our mistakes. Romans 8:28 says, And we know that all things work together for good to them that love God. Today, if you are living with the results of past mistakes, remember that God can still work in them. He hears, and He is still faithful.

P: God keeps His word. Genesis 16:15: 'Abram called his son's name, which Hagar bare, Ishmael.' The name meant God hears. He still hears you today!

== 16:16
T: Still waiting
R: 'And Abram was fourscore and six years old, when Hagar bare Ishmael to Abram.' Abram was eighty-six when Ishmael was born. He had been in Canaan eleven years. But the son of promise had not yet come. Ishmael was Abram's son, but he was not the son God had promised through Sarai. There would be another thirteen years of silence before God appeared again to Abram. Thirteen years with no recorded word from God. Perhaps Abram assumed Ishmael was the answer to God's promise. Sometimes we settle for our own version of God's plan and think it is complete. But God had something greater in mind. Silence from God does not mean absence. It may mean He is waiting for the right moment to reveal His full plan. Ecclesiastes says, He hath made every thing beautiful in his time. Today, if God seems silent, do not settle for less than His best. Keep waiting, keep trusting, and keep listening. His full promise is still coming.
S: Abram was eighty-six when Ishmael was born, but the son of promise had not yet come. Thirteen years of silence would follow before God spoke again. If God seems silent, keep trusting and do not settle for less than His best.
P: God's silence isn't His absence. Genesis 16:16: 'Abram was fourscore and six years old, when Hagar bare Ishmael.' Thirteen silent years followed. Keep trusting. His promise is coming.
