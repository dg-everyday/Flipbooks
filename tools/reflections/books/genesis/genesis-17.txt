# Genesis 17: Abraham, Sarah and the sign of the covenant

== 17:1
T: The Almighty God
R: 'And when Abram was ninety years old and nine, the LORD appeared to Abram, and said unto him, I am the Almighty God; walk before me, and be thou perfect.' After thirteen years of silence, God appeared again. Abram was now ninety-nine, and Sarai was eighty-nine. Humanly speaking, a child was impossible. That is exactly when God revealed Himself as El Shaddai, the Almighty God. God waited until there was no human possibility left, so that the fulfillment would be clearly His work. Then He gave a call: walk before me, and be thou perfect. Perfect here means wholehearted, blameless, complete in devotion. God was calling Abram to a deeper walk, not perfection in performance but integrity of heart. Our impossibilities are the stage on which God displays His might. Jeremiah prayed, Ah Lord GOD!… there is nothing too hard for thee. Today, bring your impossible situation to the Almighty God. And answer His call to walk before Him wholeheartedly.
S: When Abram was ninety-nine, God appeared as the Almighty God and called him to walk wholeheartedly before Him. God often waits until human possibility is gone so His power is clearly seen. Bring your impossible situation to the Almighty today.
P: Nothing is too hard for the Almighty. Genesis 17:1: 'I am the Almighty God; walk before me, and be thou perfect.' Bring Him your impossible situation today!

== 17:2
T: God's covenant initiative
R: 'And I will make my covenant between me and thee, and will multiply thee exceedingly.' God reaffirmed His covenant with Abram. Notice the repeated I will. God took the initiative. He would make the covenant, and He would multiply Abram exceedingly. After thirteen years of silence, Abram may have wondered whether God's promise still stood. Perhaps he thought Ishmael was all he would get. But God came back with the same promise, now even stronger. God's covenant had not lapsed during the silence. His promises do not expire because time has passed. Even when we have wandered or settled for less, God returns to renew His word. Lamentations says, It is of the LORD'S mercies that we are not consumed, because his compassions fail not. They are new every morning: great is thy faithfulness. Today, if you have been through a long silence, listen for God's renewed promise. His covenant with you in Christ still stands, as strong as the day He made it.
S: God reaffirmed His covenant with Abram after thirteen years of silence. His promises do not expire with the passing of time. Listen for God's renewed promise today, for His covenant with you still stands.
P: God's promises don't expire. Genesis 17:2: 'And I will make my covenant between me and thee, and will multiply thee exceedingly.' After 13 years of silence, God renewed His word. It still stands for you.

== 17:3
T: Face down before God
R: 'And Abram fell on his face: and God talked with him, saying.' Abram's response to God's appearance was to fall on his face. This was a posture of worship, awe, and humility. In the presence of the Almighty, Abram recognized his own smallness. And then, remarkably, God talked with him. The Almighty God, before whom Abram lay prostrate, spoke with him personally. This is the wonder of our relationship with God. He is infinitely great, worthy of our deepest reverence. Yet He speaks with us intimately, as a friend. True worship holds both together: awe at God's majesty and joy in His nearness. Isaiah saw the LORD high and lifted up and cried, Woe is me! Yet God cleansed him and sent him. John fell at the feet of the risen Christ as though dead, and Jesus laid His right hand on him and said, Fear not. Today, approach God with both reverence and confidence. Bow before His greatness, and listen as He speaks with you.
S: Abram fell on his face before the Almighty, and God talked with him. True worship holds together reverence for God's majesty and joy in His nearness. Approach God today with both awe and confidence.
P: Bow before Him, then listen. Genesis 17:3: 'And Abram fell on his face: and God talked with him.' The Almighty speaks with His people. Approach Him with awe and confidence today.

== 17:4
T: Father of many nations
R: 'As for me, behold, my covenant is with thee, and thou shalt be a father of many nations.' God expanded His promise. Abram would not just be the father of one nation but of many nations. This is a stunning promise for a man with only one son, born of a servant. God's vision for Abram was far larger than Abram's own vision. Paul explains that this promise is fulfilled in all who share Abraham's faith. In Romans 4, he writes that Abraham is the father of us all, as it is written, I have made thee a father of many nations. Every believer in Christ, from every nation, is a child of Abraham by faith. That includes you. When God made this promise, He was thinking of you, thousands of years later, coming to faith in Jesus. God's plans are always bigger than we can see. Today, rejoice that you belong to Abraham's great family of faith. And trust that God's vision for your life is larger than your own.
S: God promised that Abram would be the father of many nations. Paul shows this is fulfilled in all who share Abraham's faith, from every nation. Rejoice today that you belong to Abraham's great family of faith.

== 17:5
T: A new name
R: 'Neither shall thy name any more be called Abram, but thy name shall be Abraham; for a father of many nations have I made thee.' God changed Abram's name to Abraham. Abram means exalted father; Abraham means father of a multitude. Imagine introducing yourself as father of a multitude when you have only one son, and your wife is ninety. It would have sounded ridiculous. But every time Abraham said his name, he declared his faith in God's promise. Notice also that God said, have I made thee, as if it were already done. God's word is so sure that He speaks of the future as the past. A new name means a new identity. God does the same for us. In Christ, we receive a new identity. Revelation promises a new name written on a white stone. Paul writes that if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature. Today, live in your new identity. You are not who you were. You are who God says you are.
S: God changed Abram's name to Abraham, father of a multitude, declaring the promise as already done. A new name meant a new identity grounded in God's word. Live today in the new identity God has given you in Christ.

== 17:6
T: Exceedingly fruitful
R: 'And I will make thee exceeding fruitful, and I will make nations of thee, and kings shall come out of thee.' God promised that kings would come from Abraham. This would be fulfilled in the kings of Israel, especially David and his royal line. And ultimately it points to Jesus Christ, the King of kings, born of the line of David and of Abraham. Matthew begins his Gospel, The book of the generation of Jesus Christ, the son of David, the son of Abraham. The phrase exceeding fruitful echoes God's blessing at creation: be fruitful and multiply. Through Abraham, God was renewing His original purpose for humanity. What was lost through sin would be restored through Abraham's seed. God's blessing produces fruit, not just in numbers but in character and influence. Jesus said, Herein is my Father glorified, that ye bear much fruit. Today, ask God to make your life exceedingly fruitful. Abide in Christ, the King who came from Abraham, and let His life bear fruit through you.

== 17:7
T: To be your God
R: 'And I will establish my covenant between me and thee and thy seed after thee in their generations for an everlasting covenant, to be a God unto thee, and to thy seed after thee.' Here is the heart of the covenant: to be a God unto thee. More than land, more than descendants, more than kings, the greatest promise was relationship. God would be Abraham's God and the God of his descendants. This phrase echoes throughout Scripture: I will be their God, and they shall be my people. It is the heartbeat of the Bible's story. Jeremiah promised it in the new covenant. Revelation celebrates its final fulfillment: Behold, the tabernacle of God is with men, and he will dwell with them, and they shall be his people, and God himself shall be with them, and be their God. Everything God does is aimed at this: a people who belong to Him, and He to them. Today, rejoice in the greatest promise of all. God has promised to be your God, forever.
S: The heart of God's covenant was His promise to be a God to Abraham and his descendants. Relationship with God is the greatest promise of all, fulfilled in Christ. Rejoice today that God has promised to be your God forever.

== 17:8
T: Strangers who will inherit
R: 'And I will give unto thee, and to thy seed after thee, the land wherein thou art a stranger, all the land of Canaan, for an everlasting possession; and I will be their God.' God called Abraham a stranger in the land he would inherit. Abraham lived in Canaan, but he did not own it. He was a sojourner, living in tents. Yet God promised that this very land would become an everlasting possession. Hebrews says Abraham sojourned in the land of promise, as in a strange country. He looked beyond the earthly land to a better country, that is, an heavenly. Christians are also strangers in this world, living in it but not belonging to it. Peter calls us strangers and pilgrims. Our true inheritance is still ahead. But just as surely as God gave Canaan to Abraham's descendants, He will give us our eternal inheritance. Today, live as a stranger who knows where home is. Hold this world loosely, and look forward to your everlasting possession in Christ.
S: God promised to give Abraham the land where he was a stranger as an everlasting possession. Like Abraham, believers are strangers in this world with a sure inheritance ahead. Live today as a pilgrim who knows where home is.

== 17:9
T: Our part in the covenant
R: 'And God said unto Abraham, Thou shalt keep my covenant therefore, thou, and thy seed after thee in their generations.' After all of God's promises, He spoke of Abraham's responsibility. Thou shalt keep my covenant. The covenant was initiated by grace, but it called for a faithful response. God's grace is never an excuse for passivity. It is the foundation for obedience. We do not obey to earn God's love; we obey because we have received it. Notice also that the responsibility extended to Abraham's seed in their generations. Faithfulness is not only personal but generational. Parents are called to teach their children to keep God's ways. Moses would later tell Israel to teach God's words diligently unto thy children. The covenant was to be passed down, kept alive in each new generation. Today, consider how you are keeping your covenant with God. And think about how you are helping the next generation to know and follow Him. Grace calls us to faithful response.
S: God told Abraham to keep His covenant, along with his descendants in every generation. God's grace calls for a faithful response of obedience. Keep your covenant with God today and help pass faith to the next generation.
P: Grace calls for a response. Genesis 17:9: 'Thou shalt keep my covenant therefore, thou, and thy seed after thee.' Obey out of love, and pass faith on to the next generation.

== 17:10
T: A sign of belonging
R: 'This is my covenant, which ye shall keep, between me and you and thy seed after thee; Every man child among you shall be circumcised.' God gave a physical sign of the covenant: circumcision. Every male in Abraham's household would carry in his body a mark that he belonged to God's covenant people. It was a constant, personal reminder of God's promises and of their identity as His people. Signs of belonging matter. They mark us as part of something greater than ourselves. In the New Testament, Paul explains that the true meaning of circumcision is inward. He writes, circumcision is that of the heart, in the spirit, and not in the letter. Colossians says believers are circumcised with the circumcision made without hands, in Christ. Baptism now serves as the outward sign of entering the new covenant. Today, remember that you bear the mark of belonging to God. Your identity is not in your achievements but in being His. Live as one who carries His sign.
S: God gave circumcision as the sign of His covenant with Abraham and his descendants. In the New Testament, the true circumcision is of the heart, done by Christ. Live today as one who bears the mark of belonging to God.
P: You belong to God. Genesis 17:10: 'This is my covenant… Every man child among you shall be circumcised.' Today God marks His people with a changed heart in Christ.

== 17:11
T: A token in the flesh
R: 'And ye shall circumcise the flesh of your foreskin; and it shall be a token of the covenant betwixt me and you.' Like the rainbow given to Noah, circumcision was a token, a sign pointing to the covenant. But unlike the rainbow, which was in the sky, this sign was in the flesh. It was personal and permanent. God's covenant was not just an abstract idea; it was marked on the body. This teaches that our relationship with God is meant to affect every part of us, including our physical lives. Paul writes, present your bodies a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable unto God. Our bodies belong to God. How we use them, what we do with them, and how we care for them all matter to Him. The sign of circumcision also involved cutting away, a picture of removing what is unclean. Today, consider how your faith is expressed not only in your thoughts but in your body and actions. Offer every part of yourself to God as a living sacrifice.
S: Circumcision was a token of the covenant marked in the flesh, personal and permanent. Our relationship with God is meant to affect every part of us, including our bodies. Offer every part of yourself to God as a living sacrifice today.
P: Faith affects every part of us. Genesis 17:11: 'it shall be a token of the covenant betwixt me and you.' Present your body as a living sacrifice to God today.

== 17:12
T: Grace for the newborn and the stranger
R: 'And he that is eight days old shall be circumcised among you, every man child in your generations, he that is born in the house, or bought with money of any stranger, which is not of thy seed.' Two details stand out. First, the sign was given to infants at eight days old. Children were included in the covenant community from the very beginning of their lives. God's promises were for families, not just individuals. Second, the covenant sign was extended to servants and strangers in Abraham's household, even those not of his bloodline. From the start, God's covenant was open to outsiders who joined His people. This foreshadows the gospel going to the Gentiles. Paul writes that in Christ there is neither Jew nor Greek. Jesus was Himself circumcised on the eighth day, fulfilling the law for us. Today, thank God that His covenant embraces children and strangers. Pray for the children in your church and family, and welcome outsiders into God's family.

== 17:13
T: An everlasting covenant
R: 'He that is born in thy house, and he that is bought with thy money, must needs be circumcised: and my covenant shall be in your flesh for an everlasting covenant.' God repeated the command for emphasis and called it an everlasting covenant. The sign was not optional. It was a necessary mark of belonging. The phrase everlasting covenant reminds us that God's relationship with His people is not temporary. The outward sign of circumcision would later give way to the new covenant in Christ, but the everlasting covenant of grace that it represented continues forever. God's covenant is not fragile. It does not come and go with our feelings. Hebrews speaks of the blood of the everlasting covenant shed by Jesus, the great shepherd of the sheep. Through His blood, we are brought into a relationship with God that cannot be broken. Today, rest in the permanence of God's covenant with you. It was sealed by Christ's blood, and it will last forever.

== 17:14
T: The seriousness of the covenant
R: 'And the uncircumcised man child whose flesh of his foreskin is not circumcised, that soul shall be cut off from his people; he hath broken my covenant.' This is a sobering verse. Refusing the covenant sign meant being cut off from God's people. It was a rejection of the covenant itself. God's covenant is gracious, but it is not casual. To reject the sign was to reject the God who gave it. This teaches us that our response to God's grace matters. God offers His covenant freely, but we must receive it. Hebrews warns, How shall we escape, if we neglect so great salvation? The gospel is a gracious invitation, but it is also a serious one. Jesus said that whoever believes in Him has eternal life, but whoever rejects Him remains under judgment. There is no neutral ground. Today, take God's covenant seriously. Do not treat His grace lightly. Receive it with gratitude and respond with faithful obedience. Neglecting His gift is the greatest loss.

== 17:15
T: Sarah, a princess
R: 'And God said unto Abraham, As for Sarai thy wife, thou shalt not call her name Sarai, but Sarah shall her name be.' God also gave Sarai a new name. Sarah means princess. Sarah was not a passive bystander in God's plan. She was a central figure in the covenant. The promise would come through her specifically, not through Hagar. God gave her a new identity, honoring her as the mother of the promised line. After years of barrenness, shame, and painful mistakes, God called her princess. He saw her not as she saw herself but as He intended her to be. This is how God sees His children. Our past failures and present struggles do not define us. He calls us by names of honor. Peter writes that we are a chosen generation, a royal priesthood. In Christ, we are children of the King. Today, hear God call you by your new name. You are not defined by your past. You are His beloved, chosen and honored.

Sarah, the barren woman, would be the mother of nations. Kings would come from her. The woman who had felt cursed would be abundantly blessed. God's promise was specific: the son would come from Sarah. Not from a substitute, not through a human workaround. From Sarah herself. God's plan does not need our shortcuts. He fulfills His promises in exactly the way He said He would. Isaiah later addressed Israel, Look unto Abraham your father, and unto Sarah that bare you. Sarah became the honored mother of God's people. Her story is a testimony that God can bring life out of barrenness and honor out of shame. Today, if you feel barren or overlooked, remember Sarah. God's blessing can transform what seems lifeless into something abundantly fruitful.

== 17:17
T: The laughter of disbelief
R: 'Then Abraham fell upon his face, and laughed, and said in his heart, Shall a child be born unto him that is an hundred years old? and shall Sarah, that is ninety years old, bear?' Abraham fell on his face, but this time he laughed. The promise seemed too wonderful, too impossible. A hundred-year-old man and a ninety-year-old woman having a baby? It was laughable. Abraham's laughter seems to mix wonder with disbelief. Even the father of faith struggled to believe this promise. Yet God did not reject him for it. Instead, God told him to name the son Isaac, which means he laughs. God turned Abraham's laughter of doubt into a name of joy. Paul writes that Abraham considered not his own body now dead, when he was about an hundred years old, neither yet the deadness of Sara's womb. His faith grew stronger. Today, if God's promise seems laughable, bring your doubts honestly to Him. He can turn the laughter of disbelief into the laughter of joy.

== 17:18

He prayed that Ishmael might live before God, that he might be blessed and included. This is a father's prayer. Abraham had raised Ishmael for thirteen years. He loved him deeply. Perhaps Abraham thought it would be easier for God simply to bless Ishmael and count him as the promised son. Sometimes we ask God to bless what we already have rather than wait for what He has promised. Yet Abraham's prayer was heard. God would bless Ishmael, though the covenant would come through Isaac. A parent's prayers for a child are precious to God. Job rose early to offer sacrifices for each of his children. Today, pray for your children by name, or for children you care about. Like Abraham, ask that they might live before God. He hears a parent's prayer.

== 17:19

God was firm. Sarah would bear a son, indeed, truly, certainly. God even named the child before he was conceived: Isaac. The covenant would pass through Isaac and his descendants. God gently but clearly corrected Abraham's suggestion. Ishmael would be blessed, but the covenant line was Isaac's. God's plans are specific, and He does not change them to fit our preferences. Sometimes we want God to adjust His plan to match what we have already done. But God's way is always better. Isaac would be the child of promise, born not by human effort but by God's miraculous power. Paul uses Isaac as a picture of all who are born by the Spirit through promise. Today, trust God's plan even when it differs from yours. His way is better than any substitute.

Isaac would be born at this set time in the next year. After twenty-five years of waiting, the promise now had a date. God's timing is precise. He had a set time all along. The years of waiting were not random delays; they were part of His plan. Galatians says, But when the fulness of the time was come, God sent forth his Son. God works according to His appointed times. We often wish we knew God's timeline in advance. Usually we do not. But this verse reminds us that God always has one. Every promise has a set time in His calendar. Habakkuk wrote, For the vision is yet for an appointed time… though it tarry, wait for it. Today, trust that God has a set time for the promises you are waiting on. It will come, right on time.

== 17:22
T: When God finishes speaking
R: 'And he left off talking with him, and God went up from Abraham.' God finished speaking and went up from Abraham. The encounter ended. What a conversation it had been: a new name, a covenant sign, a promised son, and a specific timeline. Now Abraham was left to act on what he had heard. Encounters with God are meant to lead to action. We cannot stay forever in the moment of revelation. Peter wanted to build tents on the mountain of transfiguration, but Jesus led the disciples back down to the valley. Mountaintop experiences are meant to equip us for life in the valley. After God left, Abraham was alone with the word. That is often how faith works. We hear God, and then we must walk in obedience when the sense of His presence fades. Today, think about what God has spoken to you recently, through His word, in prayer, or through others. Now act on it. Faith is proven in what we do after God finishes speaking.

== 17:23
T: The selfsame day
R: 'And Abraham took Ishmael his son, and all that were born in his house… and circumcised the flesh of their foreskin in the selfsame day, as God had said unto him.' Abraham obeyed immediately. In the selfsame day, the very day God spoke, Abraham carried out the command. He did not delay or debate. He did not wait until it was convenient. Prompt obedience is a mark of genuine faith. Psalm 119 says, I made haste, and delayed not to keep thy commandments. Delayed obedience is often disobedience in disguise. When we put off doing what God has said, we often never get around to it. Abraham's immediate response showed that he truly believed. Notice too that he included Ishmael. Even though the covenant would go through Isaac, Abraham brought Ishmael under the covenant sign. He included his whole household. Today, is there something God has asked you to do that you have been putting off? Do it today. Make haste, and do not delay.

== 17:24
T: Never too old to obey
R: 'And Abraham was ninety years old and nine, when he was circumcised in the flesh of his foreskin.' At ninety-nine years old, Abraham underwent a painful procedure in obedience to God. Age did not exempt him. He could have argued that he was too old, that it was unnecessary at his stage of life. But he obeyed. There is no retirement from obedience in God's kingdom. At every age, God calls His people to follow Him faithfully. And sometimes, obedience involves pain. Abraham's circumcision was not comfortable. But he trusted that God's command was good. Obedience that costs something is often the most meaningful. Paul wrote at the end of his life, I have fought a good fight, I have finished my course, I have kept the faith. He obeyed to the very end. Today, whatever your age, ask God what He is calling you to do. Do not let age or discomfort become an excuse. Follow Him faithfully, no matter the cost.

== 17:25
T: Ishmael included
R: 'And Ishmael his son was thirteen years old, when he was circumcised in the flesh of his foreskin.' Ishmael was thirteen when he received the covenant sign. He was old enough to understand what was happening. He had just heard that the covenant would pass through a brother not yet born. Yet he was still circumcised and included in Abraham's household of faith. Ishmael was not cast out of God's care. He bore the sign of the covenant and received God's blessing. Thirteen is an age of transition, between childhood and adulthood. Many traditions mark this age as a time of spiritual responsibility. Ishmael's story reminds us to include young people in the life of faith. They are not too young to understand or to participate. Paul told Timothy, Let no man despise thy youth. Today, encourage a young person in their faith. Include them, teach them, and help them understand that they belong in God's family.

== 17:26
T: Father and son together
R: 'In the selfsame day was Abraham circumcised, and Ishmael his son.' Father and son obeyed together on the same day. Abraham did not ask Ishmael to do something he was not willing to do himself. He led by example. This is a powerful model for parents and leaders. Children learn far more from what they see us do than from what we tell them. When Abraham and Ishmael underwent the covenant sign together, Ishmael saw his father's faith in action. Shared obedience creates shared memories and shared identity. Joshua declared, as for me and my house, we will serve the LORD. Faith that is lived together in a family is powerful. It shapes children in ways that lectures never can. Today, think about how you can practice your faith alongside those you influence. Pray together, serve together, worship together. Let your children or those you mentor see your faith in action. Lead by example.

== 17:27
T: A whole household
R: 'And all the men of his house, born in the house, and bought with money of the stranger, were circumcised with him.' Abraham's entire household followed him in obedience. Servants born in his house and those purchased from foreigners all received the covenant sign. No one was excluded. Abraham's faith shaped the whole community around him. This shows the influence of a faithful leader. When one person walks with God, it affects many others. Abraham's household numbered in the hundreds, and all of them came under the covenant because of his leadership. This also shows the inclusiveness of God's covenant. Foreigners, servants, and outsiders were all included. The covenant community was diverse from the beginning. In the New Testament, the Philippian jailer believed and was baptized, he and all his. Faith often spreads through households and communities. Today, pray for your household and community. Ask God to use your faith to draw those around you into His covenant family.
