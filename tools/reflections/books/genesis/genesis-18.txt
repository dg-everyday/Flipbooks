# Genesis 18: The visitors at Mamre and Abraham's intercession

== 18:1
T: God visits in ordinary moments
R: 'And the LORD appeared unto him in the plains of Mamre: and he sat in the tent door in the heat of the day.' Abraham was doing nothing special. He was sitting at the entrance of his tent during the hottest part of the day, probably resting. It was an ordinary afternoon. And the LORD appeared. God often meets us in ordinary moments. Moses was tending sheep when he saw the burning bush. Gideon was threshing wheat. The disciples were fishing. The woman of Samaria was drawing water. God does not wait for us to be in a religious setting. He comes into the middle of daily life. This means every moment can be holy. The kitchen, the car, the office, and the backyard can all become places where God shows up. Brother Lawrence, a humble cook, learned to practice the presence of God while washing pots. Today, stay alert in your ordinary moments. God may visit you in the heat of the day, right where you are.
S: The LORD appeared to Abraham as he sat in his tent door in the heat of an ordinary day. God often meets us in the everyday moments of life. Stay alert to His presence in the ordinary places of your day.
P: God shows up in ordinary moments. Genesis 18:1: 'the LORD appeared unto him… and he sat in the tent door in the heat of the day.' Watch for Him in your everyday life today.

== 18:2
T: Running to welcome
R: 'And he lift up his eyes and looked, and, lo, three men stood by him: and when he saw them, he ran to meet them from the tent door, and bowed himself toward the ground.' When Abraham saw three strangers, he did not wait for them to approach. He ran to meet them. At ninety-nine years old, in the heat of the day, he ran. And he bowed low in respect. Abraham's eagerness to welcome strangers is a beautiful model of hospitality. He did not know who they were at first. He simply saw travelers in need and responded with enthusiasm. Hebrews alludes to this very story: Be not forgetful to entertain strangers: for thereby some have entertained angels unawares. Hospitality is not about impressive homes or fancy meals. It is about a welcoming heart. Jesus said that when we welcome a stranger, we welcome Him. Today, look for someone you can welcome. A new neighbor, a lonely coworker, a visitor at church. Run to meet them with the warmth of God's love.
S: When Abraham saw three strangers, he ran to meet them and bowed in respect. His eager hospitality is a model praised in the New Testament. Look for someone to welcome warmly today.
P: Run to welcome others. Genesis 18:2: 'when he saw them, he ran to meet them from the tent door.' At 99, Abraham ran! Show hospitality to someone today.

== 18:3
T: Please stay
R: 'And said, My Lord, if now I have found favour in thy sight, pass not away, I pray thee, from thy servant.' Abraham pleaded with his visitors not to pass by. He wanted them to stay. Something in him recognized that these were no ordinary travelers. His words, pass not away, are a prayer we can echo. When God draws near, we should not let the moment slip by. The disciples on the road to Emmaus did the same thing. When the risen Jesus seemed ready to go on, they constrained him, saying, Abide with us: for it is toward evening. And He went in and stayed with them, and they recognized Him in the breaking of bread. God often waits to be invited. Revelation says Jesus stands at the door and knocks. If any man hear my voice, and open the door, I will come in to him. Today, invite God to stay with you. Do not let His presence pass by. Ask Him to abide with you throughout the day.
S: Abraham urged his visitors not to pass by but to stay with him. When God draws near, we should invite Him to remain. Ask the Lord today to abide with you and not pass you by.

== 18:4
T: Refreshing others
R: 'Let a little water, I pray you, be fetched, and wash your feet, and rest yourselves under the tree.' Abraham offered simple but meaningful hospitality: water to wash dusty feet and shade to rest under. In the hot, dry climate of the region, these were great kindnesses. Abraham thought of his guests' comfort first. Foot washing appears again in Scripture in a profound way. On the night before His crucifixion, Jesus took a towel and washed His disciples' feet. He said, If I then, your Lord and Master, have washed your feet; ye also ought to wash one another's feet. Serving others in humble, practical ways is at the heart of Christian love. It does not require grand gestures. A cup of water, a place to rest, a listening ear: these are ways we refresh others. Jesus said that even a cup of cold water given in His name will not lose its reward. Today, look for a simple way to refresh someone who is weary. Small kindnesses matter greatly.
S: Abraham offered his guests water to wash their feet and shade to rest under. Simple, practical service is at the heart of Christian love. Look for a small way to refresh someone who is weary today.

== 18:5
T: Generous understatement
R: 'And I will fetch a morsel of bread, and comfort ye your hearts; after that ye shall pass on: for therefore are ye come to your servant.' Abraham offered a morsel of bread. But as the next verses show, he prepared a lavish feast: fine flour cakes, a choice calf, butter, and milk. He promised little and delivered much. This is the opposite of what many of us do. We often promise much and deliver little. Abraham's humble understatement reveals a generous heart that did not seek credit. Notice too that he spoke of comforting their hearts. Hospitality is not just about food; it is about refreshing the whole person. A meal shared with warmth can lift a weary spirit. Abraham also said, for therefore are ye come to your servant, as if their visit were an honor to him. True hospitality makes guests feel valued, not like a burden. Today, practice generous understatement. Do more than you promise, and make those you serve feel honored.
S: Abraham offered a morsel of bread but then prepared a lavish feast. His humble understatement revealed a generous heart that did not seek credit. Do more than you promise today and make others feel valued.

== 18:6
T: Sharing the work
R: 'And Abraham hastened into the tent unto Sarah, and said, Make ready quickly three measures of fine meal, knead it, and make cakes upon the hearth.' Abraham hurried to Sarah and asked her to prepare bread. Three measures of fine meal was a large amount, more than enough for three guests. This was generous hospitality. Abraham and Sarah worked together. Hospitality often requires teamwork. A welcoming home is usually the work of many hands. Notice the urgency: hastened, quickly. Abraham did not keep his guests waiting long. He served with energy and joy. Paul encourages believers to be given to hospitality, literally to pursue it. Hospitality is not passive; it requires effort, planning, and sometimes sacrifice. Peter writes, Use hospitality one to another without grudging. Serving without complaint is part of the gift. Today, consider how you and those around you can work together to welcome others. Serve with joy, energy, and a generous heart. Hospitality shared is a blessing multiplied.
S: Abraham hurried to Sarah to prepare a generous amount of bread for the guests. Hospitality often requires teamwork, energy and generosity. Work together with others today to welcome and serve with joy.

== 18:7
T: Giving the best
R: 'And Abraham ran unto the herd, and fetcht a calf tender and good, and gave it unto a young man; and he hasted to dress it.' Abraham ran again, this time to the herd, and selected a calf tender and good. He did not give his guests an old or poor animal. He chose the best. The same principle applies to how we serve God and others. We should give our best, not our leftovers. Malachi rebuked Israel for offering blind and lame animals to God. God deserves our best, and so do the people we serve in His name. Abraham did not know yet that one of his guests was the LORD. He gave his best to strangers. That is a powerful lesson. Jesus said, Inasmuch as ye have done it unto one of the least of these my brethren, ye have done it unto me. Today, give your best to someone, whether they can repay you or not.

== 18:8
T: Standing by to serve
R: 'And he took butter, and milk, and the calf which he had dressed, and set it before them; and he stood by them under the tree, and they did eat.' Abraham set the feast before his guests and then stood by them while they ate. He did not sit down to eat with them. He stood ready to serve, attentive to their needs. This posture of service is beautiful. Abraham was a wealthy and powerful man, yet he took the role of a servant. Jesus said, For whether is greater, he that sitteth at meat, or he that serveth? is not he that sitteth at meat? but I am among you as he that serveth. True greatness in God's kingdom is found in serving others. Abraham's attentiveness showed that he cared more about his guests' comfort than his own. In a world that values being served, Christians are called to serve. Today, find a way to stand by someone in service. Be attentive to their needs. Serve without seeking recognition.

== 18:9
T: God asks about Sarah
R: 'And they said unto him, Where is Sarah thy wife? And he said, Behold, in the tent.' The visitors asked about Sarah by name. How did they know her name? This was a sign that these were no ordinary travelers. The LORD knew Sarah. He came not only for Abraham but for her. Sarah had been in the background of much of the story. Promises had been spoken to Abraham, but now God specifically asked for Sarah. She mattered to Him. God's message was meant for her ears too. Throughout Scripture, God seeks out those who are in the background. He noticed Hagar in the wilderness. He would notice Hannah weeping at the tabernacle and Mary in Nazareth. Jesus noticed the widow giving her two mites and the woman who touched His garment. You may feel like you are in the tent, behind the scenes, unnoticed. But God knows your name and asks about you. Today, rest in the truth that you matter to God personally.

== 18:10
T: I will certainly return
R: 'And he said, I will certainly return unto thee according to the time of life; and, lo, Sarah thy wife shall have a son. And Sarah heard it in the tent door, which was behind him.' The LORD repeated the promise, this time within Sarah's hearing. She was listening at the tent door. God wanted her to hear it directly. Notice the word certainly. There was no doubt in God's mind. He would return, and Sarah would have a son. The time of life likely refers to the natural time of pregnancy. In about a year, the promise would be fulfilled. God's promises are certain. His word does not include maybe or perhaps. When God says certainly, we can stake everything on it. Jesus also promised to return: I will come again, and receive you unto myself. Just as certainly as God returned to fulfill His promise to Sarah, Christ will return for His people. Today, rest in God's certain promises. What He says He will do, He will certainly do.

== 18:11
T: When nature says impossible
R: 'Now Abraham and Sarah were old and well stricken in age; and it ceased to be with Sarah after the manner of women.' Scripture states the facts plainly. Abraham and Sarah were old, and Sarah was past the age of childbearing. Biologically, it was impossible. This was not a case of difficulty but of impossibility. God often allows situations to become humanly impossible before He acts. That way, there is no question about who deserves the glory. Paul writes that Abraham considered not his own body now dead, nor the deadness of Sara's womb, but was strong in faith, giving glory to God. The facts were real, but God's power was greater than the facts. Faith does not deny reality. It looks reality in the face and trusts God anyway. Jesus said, With men this is impossible; but with God all things are possible. Today, whatever impossible facts you face, acknowledge them honestly. Then bring them to the God who is greater than every impossibility.
S: Abraham and Sarah were old, and Sarah was past the age of bearing children. God often allows situations to become impossible so that His power is clearly seen. Acknowledge your impossible facts honestly and bring them to God.

== 18:12
T: Laughing inside
R: 'Therefore Sarah laughed within herself, saying, After I am waxed old shall I have pleasure, my lord being old also?' Sarah laughed silently, within herself. It was the laughter of disbelief, perhaps tinged with sadness. After so many years of disappointment, the promise sounded too good to be true. She had stopped hoping long ago. Her laughter was a defense against being hurt again. Many of us know this feeling. After years of unanswered prayers, we stop expecting God to act. We laugh at the idea that things could change. It is not always cynicism; sometimes it is self-protection. We are afraid to hope because hope has disappointed us before. But God heard Sarah's inner laughter. Nothing is hidden from Him. And instead of rejecting her, He answered her doubt with a question that has encouraged believers ever since. Today, if you have stopped hoping, bring your inner laughter and your weary heart to God. He knows your disappointment, and He is still able.

Psalm 139 says, For there is not a word in my tongue, but, lo, O LORD, thou knowest it altogether. God knows our hidden thoughts, our silent doubts, our secret laughter. This can feel unsettling. Notice that God addressed Sarah's doubt directly. He did not ignore it or pretend He had not noticed. He brought it into the open so that it could be answered. Today, be honest with God about your hidden doubts. He already knows them. Let Him answer them with His truth.

This is one of the great questions of Scripture: Is any thing too hard for the LORD? The answer, of course, is no. Nothing is too hard for Him. Jeremiah would later echo these words: Ah Lord GOD! behold, thou hast made the heaven and the earth by thy great power and stretched out arm, and there is nothing too hard for thee. God's power has no limits. Notice also: at the time appointed. God's power works on God's schedule. Today, bring your hardest situation to Him and hear Him ask, Is any thing too hard for me?

== 18:15
T: Fear and denial
R: 'Then Sarah denied, saying, I laughed not; for she was afraid. And he said, Nay; but thou didst laugh.' Sarah was caught, and she was afraid. So she denied it. I did not laugh. But the LORD gently and firmly corrected her: No, you did laugh. Fear often leads us to deny the truth. We cover up our doubts, our mistakes, and our feelings rather than admitting them. Yet God does not let us hide behind denials. He brings the truth into the open, not to shame us but to free us. Notice that God did not withdraw His promise because of Sarah's doubt and denial. She would still have a son. His promise depended on His faithfulness, not on her perfect faith. Hebrews later says that through faith Sarah received strength to conceive. Her faith grew. The God who exposed her laughter also strengthened her belief. Today, stop denying what God already knows. Admit your doubts and fears honestly. He will not cancel His promises because of them.

Abraham walked with them for a while to see them off. This was customary hospitality, but it also became an opportunity for something remarkable. As Abraham walked with the LORD, God would reveal His plans and invite Abraham to intercede. Walking with God opens the door to intimacy. When we spend time with Him, He shares His heart with us. Enoch walked with God. Noah walked with God. Now Abraham walked with the LORD on the road toward Sodom. The disciples walked with Jesus on the road to Emmaus, and their hearts burned within them as He opened the Scriptures. Today, take time to walk with God. It might be a literal walk where you pray as you go, or simply a time of quiet fellowship. Stay with Him a little longer, and let Him share His heart with you.

== 18:17

R: 'And the LORD said, Shall I hide from Abraham that thing which I do.' God asked Himself a question: Should I hide My plans from Abraham? The answer was no. God chose to reveal His intentions to Abraham because of their relationship. Abraham was God's friend. Isaiah calls him Abraham my friend, and James says he was called the Friend of God. Friends share their plans with each other. God was about to bring judgment on Sodom, and He wanted Abraham to know. Jesus said to His disciples, Henceforth I call you not servants; for the servant knoweth not what his lord doeth: but I have called you friends; for all things that I have heard of my Father I have made known unto you. Through Christ, we too are invited into friendship with God. He reveals His heart to us through His word and His Spirit. Today, draw near to God as a friend. Listen for what He wants to share with you. Friendship with God is the greatest privilege of all.

Abraham was not chosen for his own sake alone. He was chosen to be a channel of blessing to the whole world. That calling gave him the right to know God's plans for Sodom, because Sodom was among the nations Abraham was meant to bless. God's blessing always has a purpose beyond the one who receives it. Those who are blessed are meant to bless others. Jesus told His followers, Ye are the light of the world. We are chosen not to hoard God's grace but to share it. Today, remember that your blessings carry responsibility. Ask God how you can be a channel of His blessing to the people and places around you.

== 18:22

He did not leave. He had something to say. This is the posture of an intercessor. Abraham stood between God and the city facing judgment. He did not run away from the hard conversation. He stayed in God's presence to plead for others. Moses would later do the same, standing in the breach for Israel. Ezekiel records God saying, I sought for a man among them, that should make up the hedge, and stand in the gap before me for the land. Intercession is a sacred calling. It means standing before God on behalf of others. Jesus is our great intercessor, who ever liveth to make intercession for us. Today, stand before the LORD for someone else. Stay in His presence a little longer, and bring before Him those who need His mercy.

== 18:23
T: Drawing near to plead
R: 'And Abraham drew near, and said, Wilt thou also destroy the righteous with the wicked?' Abraham drew near to God. Prayer is drawing near. And he asked a bold question about God's justice. Would God destroy the righteous along with the wicked? Abraham was likely thinking of Lot and his family in Sodom. But his question was rooted in his understanding of God's character. He knew God was just, and he appealed to that justice. Bold prayer is not disrespectful when it is grounded in who God is. Abraham was not demanding; he was appealing to God's own nature. Hebrews invites us, Let us therefore come boldly unto the throne of grace, that we may obtain mercy, and find grace to help in time of need. God welcomes bold, honest prayer from His friends. Today, draw near to God with boldness. Bring your concerns for others before Him, appealing to His goodness, justice, and mercy. He delights in the prayers of those who know His heart.

== 18:24
T: Praying for a whole city
R: 'Peradventure there be fifty righteous within the city: wilt thou also destroy and not spare the place for the fifty righteous that are therein?' Abraham asked God to spare the whole city for the sake of fifty righteous people. He did not merely ask God to rescue the righteous; he asked God to spare the entire place for their sake. Abraham's heart was larger than his concern for Lot. He was praying for a whole city. This is a model for intercession. We often pray only for ourselves and our immediate family. But Abraham prayed for a wicked city that was not his own. Jeremiah told the exiles to seek the peace of the city where they lived and pray unto the LORD for it. Our prayers should extend beyond our own walls. God may spare and bless whole communities because of the righteous people in them and the prayers offered on their behalf. Today, pray for your city. Ask God to show mercy to the place where you live.

== 18:25
T: The Judge of all the earth
R: 'That be far from thee to do after this manner, to slay the righteous with the wicked… Shall not the Judge of all the earth do right?' This is one of the great statements of faith in Scripture. Shall not the Judge of all the earth do right? Abraham was confident that God would always act justly. He appealed to God's character as the righteous Judge. This question has comforted believers through the ages. When we do not understand what God is doing, when suffering seems senseless, when injustice seems to prevail, we can hold on to this truth: the Judge of all the earth will do right. God's justice is perfect. Even when we cannot see the full picture, we can trust His character. Deuteronomy says, He is the Rock, his work is perfect: for all his ways are judgment: a God of truth and without iniquity, just and right is he. Today, rest in the justice of God. Whatever you do not understand, trust that He will do right.
S: Abraham asked, Shall not the Judge of all the earth do right? He trusted God's perfect justice. When you do not understand what God is doing, rest in His righteous character.

== 18:26
T: Mercy for the sake of the few
R: 'And the LORD said, If I find in Sodom fifty righteous within the city, then I will spare all the place for their sakes.' God agreed immediately. If fifty righteous people were found, He would spare the entire city. This shows the heart of God. He is not eager to judge. He looks for reasons to show mercy. The presence of righteous people in a place can preserve it. Jesus called His followers the salt of the earth. Salt preserves. The righteous preserve their communities from decay and judgment. This is a sobering responsibility and a wonderful privilege. Your faithfulness matters not only for yourself but for the people around you. Your presence as a believer in your workplace, school, or neighborhood may be holding back darkness. God's willingness to spare many for the sake of a few points ultimately to Christ. For the sake of One righteous man, Jesus, God offers mercy to all who believe. Today, be salt in your community. Your faithfulness matters more than you know.
S: God agreed to spare all of Sodom if He found fifty righteous people there. The presence of the righteous can preserve a whole community. Be salt in your community today, knowing your faithfulness matters.

== 18:27
T: Dust and ashes
R: 'And Abraham answered and said, Behold now, I have taken upon me to speak unto the Lord, which am but dust and ashes.' Abraham continued his intercession, but with deep humility. He recognized that he was but dust and ashes, a frail human speaking to the Almighty. Bold prayer and deep humility go together. Abraham was bold because he knew God's character. He was humble because he knew his own. This balance is essential in prayer. We come boldly because God invites us, but we come humbly because we are creatures before our Creator. Pride has no place in prayer. Jesus told a parable of a Pharisee who prayed proudly and a tax collector who would not lift up so much as his eyes unto heaven, but smote upon his breast, saying, God be merciful to me a sinner. The tax collector went home justified. Today, approach God with both confidence and humility. Remember that you are dust and ashes, yet invited into His presence. That is grace.
S: Abraham continued his intercession, calling himself but dust and ashes. Bold prayer and deep humility belong together. Approach God today with both confidence in His invitation and humility before His greatness.

== 18:28
T: Persistent prayer
R: 'Peradventure there shall lack five of the fifty righteous: wilt thou destroy all the city for lack of five? And he said, If I find there forty and five, I will not destroy it.' Abraham kept asking. What if there were five fewer than fifty? Would God destroy the city for lack of five? And God agreed again. Abraham's persistence did not annoy God. God welcomed it. Jesus taught His disciples to be persistent in prayer. He told of a friend who knocked at midnight and a widow who kept coming to the judge. He said, Ask, and it shall be given you; seek, and ye shall find; knock, and it shall be opened unto you. The verbs in that teaching suggest continuing action: keep asking, keep seeking, keep knocking. Persistent prayer is not nagging God; it is expressing our dependence and our desire. It also shapes our own hearts, deepening our faith and aligning us with God's purposes. Today, do not give up on a prayer you have been praying. Keep asking.
S: Abraham asked God to spare the city for forty-five righteous, and God agreed. God welcomes persistent prayer that expresses our dependence on Him. Keep asking today and do not give up on your prayers.

== 18:29
T: Asking again
R: 'And he spake unto him yet again, and said, Peradventure there shall be forty found there. And he said, I will not do it for forty's sake.' Yet again Abraham spoke. Forty? And God said He would not destroy it for forty's sake. Each time Abraham asked, God agreed. There was no hesitation, no rebuke. Abraham was discovering the depth of God's mercy. The more he asked, the more he learned how willing God was to spare. Prayer is not only about getting answers; it is about discovering God's heart. As we pray, we learn who He is. Abraham might have expected God to resist at some point. Instead, God kept saying yes. His mercy kept stretching. Psalm 103 says, The LORD is merciful and gracious, slow to anger, and plenteous in mercy. Many of us pray small prayers because we underestimate God's mercy. Today, pray bigger prayers. Ask God for more mercy, more grace, more blessing for others. Discover how generous His heart truly is.
S: Abraham asked again for forty, and God agreed not to destroy the city for their sake. Through persistent prayer, Abraham discovered the depth of God's mercy. Pray bigger prayers today and discover how generous God's heart is.

== 18:30
T: Speaking with reverence
R: 'And he said unto him, Oh let not the Lord be angry, and I will speak: Peradventure there shall thirty be found there. And he said, I will not do it, if I find thirty there.' Abraham was aware that he was pushing the boundaries. Oh let not the Lord be angry, he said. He was careful, respectful, and reverent. Yet he kept pleading. Thirty? And God agreed once more. Abraham's sensitivity to God's honor is instructive. He did not take God's patience for granted. He spoke with reverence even as he made his requests. Intimacy with God does not mean casualness. We can be close to God and still deeply respect Him. Jesus taught us to pray, Our Father which art in heaven, Hallowed be thy name. Father expresses closeness; hallowed expresses reverence. Both belong in prayer. Abraham's tone shows a man who knew God was both approachable and holy. Today, as you pray, combine intimacy with reverence. Come close, but come with awe.
S: Abraham asked God not to be angry as he pleaded for thirty, and God agreed again. He combined persistence with deep reverence for God. As you pray today, come close to God but come with awe.

== 18:31
T: Pressing in further
R: 'And he said, Behold now, I have taken upon me to speak unto the Lord: Peradventure there shall be twenty found there. And he said, I will not destroy it for twenty's sake.' Abraham pressed further: twenty. And God's answer remained the same: I will not destroy it. Each step down revealed more of God's patience. Abraham was learning that God's mercy is far greater than human expectations. Notice that God never interrupted Abraham or ended the conversation. He let Abraham keep asking. God is patient with our prayers. He does not tire of hearing from us. Isaiah says, Hast thou not known? hast thou not heard, that the everlasting God… fainteth not, neither is weary? God never grows weary of His children coming to Him. Some people feel they have asked God for the same thing too many times. They worry they are bothering Him. But Abraham's example shows that God welcomes continued prayer. Today, press in further. Bring your requests to God again. He is never weary of hearing you.

== 18:32
T: For the sake of ten
R: 'And he said, Oh let not the Lord be angry, and I will speak yet but this once: Peradventure ten shall be found there. And he said, I will not destroy it for ten's sake.' Abraham made his final request: ten. And God agreed. For the sake of just ten righteous people, God would spare the whole wicked city. Sadly, as the next chapter shows, there were not even ten righteous in Sodom. But God still rescued Lot and his family. God's mercy went even beyond Abraham's request. This is a picture of the gospel. In the end, there was not even one righteous person who could save humanity. Romans says, There is none righteous, no, not one. But God sent One who was righteous, Jesus Christ, and for His sake, God extends mercy to all who believe. Where Abraham stopped at ten, God went all the way to one. Today, rejoice that God's mercy goes further than our prayers. He sent His righteous Son to save us.
S: Abraham asked God to spare Sodom for ten righteous, and God agreed. Though ten were not found, God sent the one righteous Man, Jesus, for the sake of all who believe. Rejoice that God's mercy goes further than our prayers.

== 18:33
T: Returning to your place
R: 'And the LORD went his way, as soon as he had left communing with Abraham: and Abraham returned unto his place.' The conversation ended. The LORD went His way, and Abraham returned home. The word communing describes deep, personal fellowship. Abraham had communed with God, speaking and listening as a friend. Then he went back to his ordinary life. This is the rhythm of the believer: times of intense communion with God followed by a return to daily life. We cannot stay on the mountain forever. But the time we spend with God shapes how we live when we return. Abraham went back to his tent having interceded for a city. He would wake the next morning to see smoke rising from Sodom. His prayer had not saved the city, but it had saved Lot. Prayer always matters, even when the outcome is not what we hoped. Today, after your time with God, return to your place carried by His presence. Trust Him with the results of your prayers.
S: When the LORD finished communing with Abraham, He went His way, and Abraham returned home. Times of deep fellowship with God prepare us for daily life. Return to your daily tasks carried by His presence and trust Him with your prayers.
P: Commune with God, then carry Him into your day. Genesis 18:33: 'the LORD went his way… and Abraham returned unto his place.' Trust Him with your prayers today.
