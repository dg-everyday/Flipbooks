# Genesis 19: The destruction of Sodom and Lot's rescue

== 19:1
T: Sitting in the gate
R: 'And there came two angels to Sodom at even; and Lot sat in the gate of Sodom.' The gate of a city was where leaders and elders sat to conduct business and judge disputes. Lot was not just living in Sodom; he had become part of its establishment. The man who once pitched his tent toward Sodom now sat in its gate. This shows how far Lot had drifted. He had become comfortable in a city whose sin was very grievous. Yet Lot still showed some marks of his upbringing. When he saw the visitors, he rose to meet them and bowed, just as Abraham had done. There was still something of Abraham's hospitality in him. Lot is a picture of a believer who has compromised but not completely lost his way. Peter calls him just Lot, vexed with the filthy conversation of the wicked. He was troubled by Sodom, yet he stayed. Today, ask yourself whether you have grown too comfortable in places that trouble your soul.
S: Lot was sitting in the gate of Sodom, a sign that he had become part of the city's establishment. Though he had drifted far, he still showed hospitality like Abraham. Ask yourself today whether you have grown too comfortable where your soul is troubled.
P: How far have you drifted? Genesis 19:1: 'Lot sat in the gate of Sodom.' From pitching his tent toward it to sitting in its gate. Examine where you've settled today.

== 19:2
T: Offering protection
R: 'And he said, Behold now, my lords, turn in, I pray you, into your servant's house, and tarry all night, and wash your feet… And they said, Nay; but we will abide in the street all night.' Lot urged the visitors to stay in his house. He seemed to know how dangerous Sodom's streets were at night. His invitation was not just hospitality; it was protection. The angels initially declined, saying they would stay in the street. Perhaps they were testing Lot's heart, or perhaps they intended to see the city's wickedness for themselves. Lot's urgency reveals that he knew the evil of the place where he lived. He was not blind to it. Yet he had chosen to raise his family there. Sometimes we see the dangers around us clearly but still remain in them. Lot protected strangers but had not protected his own household from Sodom's influence. Today, consider whether you are guarding those closest to you from the influences you know are harmful. Protection begins at home.
S: Lot urged the visitors to stay in his house, knowing the danger of Sodom's streets at night. He saw the city's evil yet had chosen to raise his family there. Consider whether you are guarding those closest to you from harmful influences.
P: Protection begins at home. Genesis 19:2: 'turn in, I pray you, into your servant's house, and tarry all night.' Lot knew the danger. Guard those closest to you today.

== 19:3
T: Persistent hospitality
R: 'And he pressed upon them greatly; and they turned in unto him, and entered into his house; and he made them a feast, and did bake unleavened bread, and they did eat.' Lot insisted, and the visitors agreed to stay. He prepared a feast with unleavened bread, bread made quickly without waiting for yeast to rise. This detail anticipates the Passover, when Israel would eat unleavened bread on the night of deliverance from Egypt. That night in Sodom was also a night of deliverance for Lot. Lot's persistence in hospitality was commendable. Even in a corrupt city, he maintained this virtue. Sometimes, even when our lives are compromised in some areas, God honors the good we still do. Lot's hospitality opened the door for his rescue. The visitors he welcomed would be the ones who pulled him out of Sodom. Hebrews reminds us that some have entertained angels unawares. Today, continue to practice kindness and hospitality, even in difficult environments. You never know how God may use your welcome.
S: Lot pressed the visitors to stay and made them a feast with unleavened bread. His hospitality opened the door for his own rescue. Keep practicing kindness and hospitality, for God may use your welcome in unexpected ways.
P: Hospitality opens doors. Genesis 19:3: 'he made them a feast, and did bake unleavened bread, and they did eat.' The guests Lot welcomed would rescue him. Show kindness today.

== 19:4
T: A city fully corrupted
R: 'But before they lay down, the men of the city, even the men of Sodom, compassed the house round, both old and young, all the people from every quarter.' The men of Sodom surrounded Lot's house. Scripture emphasizes that it was everyone: old and young, from every quarter of the city. The corruption was total. There was no segment of society untouched by it. This explains why not even ten righteous could be found. When sin becomes normal across an entire community, it reaches every generation. The young had learned from the old, and the old had abandoned all restraint. Romans 1 describes a similar progression, where people who reject God are given over to their own desires, and their wickedness grows ever worse. This verse is a sobering warning about where unchecked sin leads, not only for individuals but for whole cultures. Today, pray for your community and its young people. Ask God to raise up voices of righteousness and to protect the next generation from the corruption that surrounds them.
S: The men of Sodom, both old and young from every quarter, surrounded Lot's house. The corruption of the city had reached every generation. Pray today for your community and especially for its young people.
P: When sin becomes normal, it reaches every generation. Genesis 19:4: 'both old and young, all the people from every quarter.' Pray for your community and its youth today.

== 19:5
T: The depth of depravity
R: 'And they called unto Lot, and said unto him, Where are the men which came in to thee this night? bring them out unto us, that we may know them.' The men of Sodom demanded that Lot hand over his guests so they could abuse them. This was violent and predatory. The sacred duty of hospitality, protecting guests, was being trampled. Sodom's sin was not only immorality but violence, cruelty, and a complete disregard for the vulnerable. Jude refers to Sodom and Gomorrah as an example of those who gave themselves over to fornication and going after strange flesh, set forth as a warning. Ezekiel also notes their pride and their neglect of the poor. Sodom's wickedness was many-layered. This verse is hard to read, but Scripture includes it to show the reality of sin's destructiveness. Sin is not harmless. It degrades people and leads to cruelty. Today, thank God for His grace that rescues us from the power of sin. And pray for those who are victims of violence and abuse.

== 19:6
T: Standing at the door
R: 'And Lot went out at the door unto them, and shut the door after him.' Lot showed courage. He went outside to face the mob and shut the door behind him to protect his guests. He placed himself between the danger and those he was sheltering. There is something admirable here. Whatever Lot's failures, in this moment he stood at the door. He tried to protect the vulnerable. Standing in the gap for others takes courage. It often means facing hostility and risk. Lot was one man against a crowd. Yet he did not hand over his guests immediately. Jesus called Himself the door of the sheep. A good shepherd stands at the door to protect the flock, even at the cost of his life. Today, think about who in your life needs protection. Are there vulnerable people you can stand up for? It may cost you something, but standing at the door for others reflects the heart of Christ.
S: Lot went out to face the mob and shut the door behind him to protect his guests. Standing between danger and the vulnerable takes courage. Think today about who you can stand up for and protect.

== 19:7
T: Calling wickedness by its name
R: 'And said, I pray you, brethren, do not so wickedly.' Lot appealed to the men, calling their intentions what they were: wickedness. He did not soften it or excuse it. Yet notice that he called them brethren. Lot had grown so close to the men of Sodom that he considered them his brothers. His identification with them is telling. He had made his home among them and accepted them as his people. Yet he still recognized that what they wanted was wicked. Lot is a tragic figure, caught between two worlds. He knew right from wrong, but he had compromised so much that his voice carried no authority. When we compromise with the world, we lose our moral influence. People do not listen to someone who has blended in. Paul urges believers to be blameless and harmless, the sons of God, without rebuke. Today, call sin by its name in your own life first. Then live in such a way that your words carry weight.
S: Lot pleaded with the men of Sodom not to act so wickedly, yet he called them brethren. His compromise with the city had robbed his words of authority. Call sin by its name and live so that your words carry weight.

== 19:8
T: A terrible offer
R: 'Behold now, I have two daughters which have not known man; let me, I pray you, bring them out unto you, and do ye to them as is good in your eyes: only unto these men do nothing.' This is one of the most disturbing verses in the Bible. To protect his guests, Lot offered his own daughters to the mob. Scripture records it but does not approve of it. It shows how deeply Sodom had distorted Lot's values. A father who should have protected his children was willing to sacrifice them. This is what compromise can do. Living in a corrupt environment gradually warps our moral judgment until we consider unthinkable things. Lot was trying to do a right thing, protecting his guests, by doing a terrible wrong. God does not call us to choose between evils but to trust Him. Today, pray for children who are not protected by those meant to care for them. And ask God to guard your own heart from the slow distortion that compromise brings.
S: Lot offered his own daughters to the mob to protect his guests, a terrible act Scripture records without approval. Living in a corrupt environment had distorted his moral judgment. Pray for unprotected children and ask God to guard your heart from compromise.

== 19:9
T: Rejected as a judge
R: 'And they said, Stand back. And they said again, This one fellow came in to sojourn, and he will needs be a judge: now will we deal worse with thee, than with them.' The men of Sodom turned on Lot. Despite his years among them and his place in the gate, they still saw him as an outsider, a sojourner. And they resented him for trying to judge them. Lot had tried to fit in, but he never truly belonged. The world will never fully accept those who carry any trace of God's values. Jesus told His disciples, If ye were of the world, the world would love his own: but because ye are not of the world… therefore the world hateth you. Lot had compromised much to fit in, yet when he took a stand, they rejected him. Compromise does not buy acceptance. It only costs our integrity. Today, remember that you were not made to blend into the world. Be faithful to God, even if it means being seen as an outsider.

== 19:10
T: Pulled to safety
R: 'But the men put forth their hand, and pulled Lot into the house to them, and shut to the door.' Just as the mob pressed in to break the door, the angels reached out and pulled Lot inside. They shut the door, sealing Lot in safety. Lot could not save himself. He had tried, and it had gone terribly wrong. But divine hands reached out and rescued him. This is a picture of salvation. We cannot save ourselves from the forces that threaten us. But God reaches out His hand and pulls us to safety. Psalm 18 says, He sent from above, he took me, he drew me out of many waters. He delivered me from my strong enemy. Notice the parallel to Noah, when the LORD shut him in the ark. Here, too, the door was shut by heavenly hands. Today, if you feel overwhelmed and unable to save yourself, cry out to God. His hand is not too short to save. He will pull you in.

== 19:11
T: Blinded and wearied
R: 'And they smote the men that were at the door of the house with blindness, both small and great: so that they wearied themselves to find the door.' The angels struck the mob with blindness. Remarkably, even blinded, the men kept trying to find the door. They wearied themselves in the attempt. Their determination to do evil did not stop even under divine judgment. This is a sobering picture of hardened hearts. Even when God intervenes, some people keep pressing on in sin. Paul writes that the god of this world hath blinded the minds of them which believe not. Sin is exhausting. The men of Sodom wearied themselves pursuing what would destroy them. Isaiah asks, Wherefore do ye spend money for that which is not bread? and your labour for that which satisfieth not? Today, pray for those who are spiritually blind. Ask God to open their eyes. And if you are wearied from chasing what does not satisfy, stop. Come to Christ, who gives true rest.

The angels asked Lot about his family. God's mercy extended beyond Lot to anyone connected to him. Bring them out. God did not want any of Lot's family to perish. This reveals God's heart for households. Salvation often spreads through families. Paul told the Philippian jailer, Believe on the Lord Jesus Christ, and thou shalt be saved, and thy house. God gives believers a special opportunity to reach their families with the message of rescue. Lot was now responsible to warn his loved ones. This is a sobering responsibility. Do the people closest to you know about God's offer of salvation? Have you told them? Today, think of family members who do not yet know Christ. Pray for them by name. == 19:13
T: Judgment is coming
R: 'For we will destroy this place, because the cry of them is waxen great before the face of the LORD; and the LORD hath sent us to destroy it.' The angels made their mission plain. Sodom would be destroyed. The cry against it had grown so great that judgment could no longer be delayed. God had been patient, and Abraham had interceded, but there were not even ten righteous. Judgment was certain. It is uncomfortable to think about God's judgment. But Scripture is clear that God will judge sin. Paul told the Athenians that God hath appointed a day, in the which he will judge the world in righteousness by that man whom he hath ordained. That man is Jesus. Yet the same Jesus who will judge is the One who offers rescue. The same angels who announced judgment also brought Lot out. God always provides a way of escape before judgment falls. Today, take God's warnings seriously. And rejoice that in Christ there is refuge from the judgment to come.

== 19:14
T: When warnings are mocked
R: 'And Lot went out, and spake unto his sons in law… and said, Up, get you out of this place; for the LORD will destroy this city. But he seemed as one that mocked unto his sons in law.' Lot obeyed and went to warn his future sons-in-law. But they thought he was joking. His warning seemed ridiculous to them. Why did they not take him seriously? Perhaps because Lot had lived so much like the people of Sodom that his sudden urgency seemed strange. His life had not matched the message. This is a sobering lesson. Our credibility depends on our consistency. If we live like the world, people will not take our warnings seriously. Jesus said that people would be eating, drinking, marrying, and giving in marriage right up until judgment came, unaware. Many still treat the gospel as a joke. Today, let your life give weight to your words. Live consistently with your faith, so that when you speak of God, others will listen.

== 19:15

Morning came, and the angels urged Lot to hurry. There was no time to lose. The phrase lest thou be consumed in the iniquity of the city is striking. Staying meant sharing Sodom's fate. Proximity to sin is dangerous. Lot needed to get out, and he needed to do it quickly. Revelation contains a similar call: Come out of her, my people, that ye be not partakers of her sins, and that ye receive not of her plagues. God calls His people to separate from what He is judging. There are moments when we must act with urgency. Lingering can be fatal. When God calls us away from sin, the time to respond is now, not later. Today, if God is calling you away from something, do not delay. Arise and go. His urgency is an expression of His love.

== 19:16

R: 'And while he lingered, the men laid hold upon his hand, and upon the hand of his wife, and upon the hand of his two daughters; the LORD being merciful unto him: and they brought him forth, and set him without the city.' Even with judgment imminent, Lot lingered. He hesitated. Perhaps he was thinking of his possessions or his position. But God did not leave him there. The angels took him by the hand and led him out. Scripture gives the reason: the LORD being merciful unto him. Lot did not deserve rescue. He hesitated when he should have run. But God's mercy pulled him out. This is grace. God does not wait for us to be perfectly willing. He takes us by the hand. Many of us linger when God calls. But God in His mercy keeps reaching for us. Today, thank God for the times He has taken your hand and pulled you out when you were lingering. His mercy is greater than your hesitation.

== 19:17
T: Do not look back
R: 'Escape for thy life; look not behind thee, neither stay thou in all the plain; escape to the mountain, lest thou be consumed.' The command was urgent and clear: escape, do not look back, do not stop in the plain, go to the mountain. God's instructions for deliverance often require decisive, forward motion. Looking back was dangerous, not just physically but spiritually. It would reveal a heart still attached to what God was judging. Jesus said, No man, having put his hand to the plough, and looking back, is fit for the kingdom of God. Paul wrote, forgetting those things which are behind, and reaching forth unto those things which are before, I press toward the mark. When God delivers us from something, we must not keep longing for it. Nostalgia for sin can pull us back. Today, if God has delivered you from a past way of life, do not look back with longing. Keep your eyes forward. Run toward the mountain of His presence and His future for you.

Lot acknowledged God's grace and mercy in saving his life. Yet in the same breath, he expressed fear that he would die in the mountain. It is a strange mixture. He had just experienced a miraculous rescue, yet he did not trust God to protect him in the next step. We often do the same. If God saved us from Sodom, can He not keep us safe in the mountain? Paul reasoned, He that spared not his own Son, but delivered him up for us all, how shall he not with him also freely give us all things? God's past grace is a guarantee of future care. Today, let your memory of God's mercy strengthen your trust for the next step. The God who rescued you will keep you.

I cannot do any thing till thou be come thither. Judgment was held back until Lot was safe. God would not destroy Sodom while Lot was still within reach of its destruction. This shows God's protective care for His people. It does not sweep up the righteous with the wicked. He waits until His people are safe. The city was named Zoar, meaning little, a lasting reminder of Lot's plea. Today, take comfort that God knows how to deliver His people. In a world heading toward judgment, those who belong to Him are kept safe by His hand.

== 19:23
T: Sunrise and judgment
R: 'The sun was risen upon the earth when Lot entered into Zoar.' The sun rose as Lot reached safety. It was a new day. For Lot, it was a day of rescue. For Sodom, it was a day of destruction. The same sunrise brought very different outcomes depending on where people were. This is a sobering truth about the Day of the Lord. Malachi speaks of a day that shall burn as an oven for the wicked, but unto you that fear my name shall the Sun of righteousness arise with healing in his wings. The same day brings judgment to some and healing to others. Where we stand when that day dawns makes all the difference. Lot was in Zoar, safe. The people of Sodom were still in the city. The rising sun is also a picture of new beginnings. Lot's old life was over. Today, make sure you are standing in the safety of Christ. And let every sunrise remind you that God's mercies are new every morning.
