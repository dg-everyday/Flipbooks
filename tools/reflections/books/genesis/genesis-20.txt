# Genesis 20: Abraham and Abimelech

== 20:1
T: Moving on
R: 'And Abraham journeyed from thence toward the south country, and dwelled between Kadesh and Shur, and sojourned in Gerar.' After the destruction of Sodom, Abraham moved south. Perhaps the smoking ruins were too painful to look at. Perhaps the land had been affected. Whatever the reason, Abraham continued his life as a sojourner, moving from place to place. Seasons of life change, and sometimes we must move on from places that hold painful memories. Yet wherever Abraham went, he was still within God's care. The promise traveled with him. Gerar was Philistine territory, and it would bring a new test. New places often bring new challenges and reveal old weaknesses. The same struggles we thought we had overcome can resurface in new settings. Today, if you are entering a new season or place, ask God to go with you. And ask Him to guard you against old patterns of fear and sin that might return in unfamiliar territory.
S: After Sodom's destruction, Abraham journeyed south and sojourned in Gerar. New places can bring new tests and reveal old weaknesses. Ask God to go with you into new seasons and guard you against old patterns.
P: New places bring new tests. Genesis 20:1: 'Abraham journeyed from thence toward the south country… and sojourned in Gerar.' Ask God to guard you against old weaknesses today.

== 20:2
T: The same old fear
R: 'And Abraham said of Sarah his wife, She is my sister: and Abimelech king of Gerar sent, and took Sarah.' Incredibly, Abraham repeated the same deception he had used in Egypt more than twenty years earlier. Again he said, She is my sister. And again Sarah was taken into a king's household. This time it was even more serious. God had just promised that Sarah would bear Isaac within the year. Abraham's fear put the promise at risk once more. How could the father of faith make the same mistake twice? Because old fears and habits die hard. Even mature believers can fall back into familiar sins under pressure. The writer of Hebrews speaks of the sin which doth so easily beset us. We all have patterns we are prone to. The key is recognizing them and depending on God's strength, not our own. Today, identify the fear-driven patterns you tend to repeat. Bring them to God honestly and ask Him for victory.
S: Abraham repeated his old deception, calling Sarah his sister, and Abimelech took her. Even mature believers can fall back into familiar sins under pressure. Identify your recurring patterns and ask God for victory over them.
P: Old fears die hard. Genesis 20:2: 'Abraham said of Sarah his wife, She is my sister.' He repeated the same mistake twice. Bring your recurring struggles to God today.

== 20:3
T: God intervenes in a dream
R: 'But God came to Abimelech in a dream by night, and said to him, Behold, thou art but a dead man, for the woman which thou hast taken; for she is a man's wife.' God stepped in. He spoke directly to Abimelech, a Philistine king, in a dream. The warning was stark: you are a dead man because of this woman. God protected Sarah and the promise. Notice that God spoke to someone outside the covenant family. He is not limited to working through His own people. He can speak to kings, rulers, and unbelievers to accomplish His purposes. Proverbs says, The king's heart is in the hand of the LORD, as the rivers of water: he turneth it whithersoever he will. When God's people fail, God Himself intervenes to protect His plan. Abraham's failure did not stop God's faithfulness. Today, take comfort that God can work through anyone, even those who do not know Him. And trust that He guards His promises even when you stumble.
S: God came to Abimelech in a dream and warned him that he was a dead man for taking another man's wife. God protected Sarah and His promise by speaking to a foreign king. Trust that God guards His promises even when you stumble.
P: God protects His promises. Genesis 20:3: 'God came to Abimelech in a dream by night.' Even when Abraham failed, God stepped in. He's faithful when we're not.

== 20:4
T: A pagan's plea
R: 'But Abimelech had not come near her: and he said, Lord, wilt thou slay also a righteous nation?' Abimelech had not touched Sarah. And he responded to God with a question that echoes Abraham's own prayer for Sodom: wilt thou slay also a righteous nation? It is striking that this Philistine king appealed to God's justice. He seemed to know that God would not punish the innocent with the guilty. There is irony here. Abraham had assumed that there was no fear of God in Gerar. Yet Abimelech spoke to God with reverence and an understanding of His justice. Sometimes we underestimate the spiritual sensitivity of people outside the church. God is at work in places and people we might not expect. Today, be careful not to assume the worst about others. God may already be speaking to people you think are far from Him. Approach them with respect, and be open to how God is working in their lives.
S: Abimelech had not touched Sarah, and he appealed to God's justice for his nation. The king Abraham feared showed reverence for God. Do not assume the worst about others, for God may already be at work in their lives.
P: Don't underestimate how God is working in others. Genesis 20:4: 'Lord, wilt thou slay also a righteous nation?' A pagan king appealed to God's justice. Be open today.

== 20:5
T: Integrity of heart
R: Abimelech said, 'Said he not unto me, She is my sister? and she, even she herself said, He is my brother: in the integrity of my heart and innocency of my hands have I done this.' Abimelech defended himself. He had acted on the information he was given. Both Abraham and Sarah had told him they were siblings. He had acted with integrity of heart and innocent hands. This is a humbling moment for Abraham. A pagan king could claim integrity in this situation, but Abraham could not. Abraham's deception had led an innocent man into danger. When believers act dishonestly, they can cause others to stumble unknowingly. Our lies do not only affect us; they can trap others in situations they never intended. Psalm 24 asks who may ascend the hill of the LORD, and answers, He that hath clean hands, and a pure heart. Today, examine whether your words have led anyone into confusion or harm. Commit to speaking the truth so that others can act with clear consciences.
S: Abimelech explained that he had acted with integrity because both Abraham and Sarah said they were siblings. Abraham's deception had led an innocent man into danger. Speak the truth so that others can act with clear consciences.
P: Our lies can trap others. Genesis 20:5: 'in the integrity of my heart and innocency of my hands have I done this.' A pagan king had more integrity than Abraham here. Speak truth today.

== 20:6
T: God restrains from sin
R: 'And God said unto him in a dream, Yea, I know that thou didst this in the integrity of thy heart; for I also withheld thee from sinning against me: therefore suffered I thee not to touch her.' God acknowledged Abimelech's integrity. But He also revealed something remarkable: I withheld thee from sinning against me. God had actively prevented Abimelech from sinning. This shows God's restraining grace. Many times, God keeps us from sin without our even knowing it. He closes doors, creates obstacles, and redirects our steps. We may never realize how many times God has held us back from disaster. Notice also that God called it sinning against me. All sin is ultimately against God. David said, Against thee, thee only, have I sinned. Today, thank God for the times He has restrained you from sin, even when you were unaware. And pray as Jesus taught: lead us not into temptation, but deliver us from evil. His restraining grace is a gift.
S: God told Abimelech that He had withheld him from sinning by not allowing him to touch Sarah. God often restrains us from sin without our knowing it. Thank Him today for His restraining grace and ask Him to keep you from temptation.
P: God holds us back from sin more than we know. Genesis 20:6: 'I also withheld thee from sinning against me.' Thank Him for His restraining grace today.

== 20:7
T: A flawed prophet who prays
R: 'Now therefore restore the man his wife; for he is a prophet, and he shall pray for thee, and thou shalt live.' God called Abraham a prophet, the first time this word appears in the Bible. And God told Abimelech that Abraham would pray for him. This is surprising. Abraham had just lied and caused this whole crisis. Yet God still identified him as His prophet and gave him a role in Abimelech's healing. God's calling on Abraham's life had not been revoked by his failure. Romans says, For the gifts and calling of God are without repentance. God does not discard His servants when they stumble. He restores them and continues to use them. This is both humbling and encouraging. Abraham had no grounds for pride; his prayer would be needed to fix a problem he had caused. Today, if you have failed, do not assume God is finished with you. He may still use you to bless others, even in the aftermath of your mistakes.
S: God called Abraham a prophet and said he would pray for Abimelech. Despite Abraham's failure, God's calling on his life remained. Do not assume God is finished with you after you have failed.
P: God doesn't discard His servants when they fail. Genesis 20:7: 'he is a prophet, and he shall pray for thee.' Despite Abraham's lie, God still used him. He can still use you.

== 20:8
T: Responding to God's warning
R: 'Therefore Abimelech rose early in the morning, and called all his servants, and told all these things in their ears: and the men were sore afraid.' Abimelech took God's warning seriously. He rose early, gathered his servants, and told them everything. And they were filled with fear. This pagan king responded to God's word more promptly than many believers do. He did not delay or dismiss the dream. He acted immediately. The fear of God is the beginning of wisdom. Abimelech's household trembled at God's warning. That kind of reverence is often missing today. We hear God's word and treat it lightly. Abimelech's response is a rebuke and an example. When God speaks, the right response is to take it seriously and act on it quickly. Jonah's message to Nineveh produced a similar response: the people believed God and repented. Today, take God's word seriously. When He warns or corrects you, respond quickly, as Abimelech did, with reverence and obedience.
S: Abimelech rose early, told his servants about God's warning, and they were greatly afraid. This pagan king responded promptly and reverently to God's word. When God warns or corrects you, respond quickly with reverence and obedience.
P: Take God's word seriously. Genesis 20:8: 'Abimelech rose early in the morning… and the men were sore afraid.' He acted immediately on God's warning. Will you?

== 20:9
T: Rebuked again
R: 'Then Abimelech called Abraham, and said unto him, What hast thou done unto us? and what have I offended thee, that thou hast brought on me and on my kingdom a great sin?' For the second time, Abraham was rebuked by a pagan king. Abimelech's question echoes Pharaoh's: What have you done? Abraham had brought guilt and danger on an entire kingdom. His deception had consequences far beyond himself. Abimelech called it a great sin. He recognized the seriousness of adultery, even though he had been spared from committing it. Again, a so-called outsider showed moral clarity that the man of God lacked in this moment. It is painful when unbelievers have to correct believers. But sometimes God uses such moments to humble us. Proverbs says, Faithful are the wounds of a friend. Even from unexpected sources, correction can be a gift. Today, if someone has rebuked you, even someone outside the faith, consider whether God may be speaking through them. Receive correction with humility.
S: Abimelech rebuked Abraham for bringing a great sin on his kingdom. For the second time, a pagan king corrected the man of God. Receive correction with humility, even from unexpected sources.
P: Correction can come from unexpected places. Genesis 20:9: 'What hast thou done unto us?' Abraham was rebuked again by a king. Receive correction humbly today.

== 20:10
T: What were you thinking?
R: 'And Abimelech said unto Abraham, What sawest thou, that thou hast done this thing?' Abimelech pressed further. What did you see? What made you think you needed to do this? It was a fair question. Abraham had acted on assumptions, not facts. He had not seen anything that justified his deception. He had imagined danger and responded with a lie. Many of our sins come from false assumptions. We assume the worst about people or situations and act accordingly. We protect ourselves from imagined threats. Abimelech's question invites us to examine our motives. Why do we do what we do? What are we seeing, or imagining, that leads us to compromise? Jesus warned against judging by appearances. Paul urges us to think on things that are true. Today, examine an area where you may be acting on assumptions rather than truth. Ask God to show you reality as He sees it. Fear-based assumptions often lead us into unnecessary sin.
S: Abimelech asked Abraham what he had seen that made him act this way. Abraham had acted on assumptions rather than facts. Examine whether you are acting on fearful assumptions rather than truth today.
P: Are you acting on assumptions? Genesis 20:10: 'What sawest thou, that thou hast done this thing?' Abraham imagined danger and lied. Ask God to show you the truth today.

== 20:11
T: Underestimating others
R: 'And Abraham said, Because I thought, Surely the fear of God is not in this place; and they will slay me for my wife's sake.' Abraham finally explained his reasoning. He thought there was no fear of God in Gerar. He assumed the people were godless and would kill him. But he was wrong. Abimelech feared God more than Abraham expected. Abraham's assumption was a form of spiritual pride. He thought he was the only one who knew God, and that others were wicked. This kind of thinking can creep into the hearts of believers. We assume that people outside our circle have no conscience, no fear of God, no moral sense. But God's common grace is at work in many hearts. Paul writes that even Gentiles show the work of the law written in their hearts. Today, guard against spiritual pride. Do not assume others are godless. Treat them with respect, and look for evidence of God's grace in their lives.
S: Abraham explained that he thought there was no fear of God in Gerar. His assumption was wrong and reflected a kind of spiritual pride. Guard against assuming others are godless and look for God's grace in their lives.
P: Don't assume others are godless. Genesis 20:11: 'Surely the fear of God is not in this place.' Abraham was wrong. Look for God's grace in others today.

== 20:12
T: Technically true, still wrong
R: 'And yet indeed she is my sister; she is the daughter of my father, but not the daughter of my mother; and she became my wife.' Abraham defended himself by pointing out that Sarah really was his half-sister. Technically, he had not lied. But this was a defense of a half-truth. The purpose of his words was to deceive, and it worked. Abimelech believed she was available for marriage. Technically true statements used to mislead are still dishonest. God cares not only about the literal accuracy of our words but about their intent. Jesus said, let your communication be, Yea, yea; Nay, nay. Our words should be straightforward and honest, not carefully crafted to create false impressions. Many people rationalize their deceptions with technicalities. But God sees the heart. Today, examine whether you have been using technically true statements to mislead. Commit to plain honesty. Let your words reflect the truth fully, not just partially.
S: Abraham defended himself by saying Sarah really was his half-sister. But a technically true statement used to deceive is still dishonest. Commit today to plain honesty that reflects the whole truth.
P: Technically true can still be dishonest. Genesis 20:12: 'And yet indeed she is my sister.' Abraham's half-truth still deceived. Let your yes be yes today.

== 20:13
T: A long-standing habit
R: 'And it came to pass, when God caused me to wander from my father's house, that I said unto her, This is thy kindness which thou shalt shew unto me; at every place whither we shall come, say of me, He is my brother.' Abraham revealed that this deception was a long-standing agreement. From the time they left home, he had asked Sarah to call him her brother wherever they went. This was not a momentary lapse; it was a habit built over decades. Some sins become so routine that we stop seeing them as sins. They become part of how we operate. Abraham even called it kindness. He had reframed his fear-driven deception as an act of love. Habits of sin can be deeply rooted and cleverly disguised. Paul urges us to put off the old man with his deeds. Today, ask God to reveal any long-standing habits of sin you have come to accept. Let Him break patterns you have grown used to.
S: Abraham revealed that he had asked Sarah to call him her brother everywhere they went since leaving home. His deception was a long-standing habit he had come to call kindness. Ask God to reveal and break habits of sin you have grown used to.

== 20:14
T: Generosity from the wronged
R: 'And Abimelech took sheep, and oxen, and menservants, and womenservants, and gave them unto Abraham, and restored him Sarah his wife.' Abimelech returned Sarah and gave Abraham generous gifts. The one who had been wronged showed generosity to the one who wronged him. This is remarkable. Abimelech could have punished Abraham or sent him away empty-handed. Instead, he gave gifts. Perhaps he wanted to make peace and avoid further divine displeasure. Whatever his motives, his response was gracious. It is humbling when those we have wronged respond with kindness. It shames us more than anger would. Proverbs says, If thine enemy be hungry, give him bread to eat… For thou shalt heap coals of fire upon his head. Abimelech's generosity was like coals of fire for Abraham. Today, consider how you respond to those who wrong you. Could you respond with unexpected generosity? Kindness to those who hurt us reflects the heart of Christ, who loved us while we were yet sinners.

== 20:15
T: An open invitation
R: 'And Abimelech said, Behold, my land is before thee: dwell where it pleaseth thee.' Abimelech went even further. He invited Abraham to live anywhere he wanted in his land. Instead of expelling him as Pharaoh had done, Abimelech offered welcome. This echoes Abraham's own words to Lot: Is not the whole land before thee? Now a Philistine king offered the same generosity to Abraham. God's protection over Abraham was evident. Despite Abraham's failure, God turned the situation into one of blessing and favor. God has a way of turning our mistakes into opportunities when we are walking under His covenant. That does not excuse sin, but it shows His grace. Romans 8:28 reminds us that God works all things together for good to them that love Him. Today, thank God for the times He has turned your failures into unexpected blessings. And extend generous welcome to others, as Abimelech did, opening doors rather than closing them.

== 20:16
T: Vindicated
R: 'And unto Sarah he said, Behold, I have given thy brother a thousand pieces of silver: behold, he is to thee a covering of the eyes, unto all that are with thee, and with all other: thus she was reproved.' Abimelech gave a thousand pieces of silver as a public sign that Sarah was innocent. The phrase a covering of the eyes likely means a vindication of her honor before everyone. No one could accuse her of wrongdoing. Notice too Abimelech's pointed choice of words: thy brother. Perhaps there was a gentle rebuke in it. Sarah had gone along with the deception. Yet her honor was restored. God cares about the reputation of His people, especially those who have been put in compromising situations by others. He vindicates the innocent. Isaiah promises, No weapon that is formed against thee shall prosper; and every tongue that shall rise against thee in judgment thou shalt condemn. Today, if your reputation has been harmed, trust God to vindicate you in His time.

== 20:17
T: Prayer that heals
R: 'So Abraham prayed unto God: and God healed Abimelech, and his wife, and his maidservants; and they bare children.' Abraham prayed, and God healed. This is the first time in Scripture that prayer is directly linked to healing. Abraham, who had caused the problem, now became the channel of its solution. His prayer brought restoration to Abimelech's household. There is something beautiful about this. God did not set Abraham aside because of his sin. He used him to bring healing. Notice also the poignancy: Abraham prayed for Abimelech's household to bear children, while Sarah still had no child. Abraham prayed for others to receive what he himself was still waiting for. That is selfless intercession. James writes, pray one for another, that ye may be healed. Today, pray for someone else's healing, even if you are still waiting for your own breakthrough. God honors those who intercede for others. And He may use your prayers to bring healing where you least expect it.
S: Abraham prayed, and God healed Abimelech's household so they could bear children. Abraham prayed for others to receive what he himself was still waiting for. Pray for someone else's healing today, even while you wait for your own.
P: Pray for others, even while you wait. Genesis 20:17: 'So Abraham prayed unto God: and God healed Abimelech.' He prayed for children while still waiting himself. Intercede today.

== 20:18
T: God guards the promise
R: 'For the LORD had fast closed up all the wombs of the house of Abimelech, because of Sarah Abraham's wife.' This verse explains what had been happening. God had closed the wombs of Abimelech's household as a sign of His displeasure and as protection for Sarah. Notice the final phrase: Sarah Abraham's wife. God did not call her Abraham's sister. He called her what she was: Abraham's wife. God was guarding the truth and guarding the promise. Within months, Sarah would conceive Isaac. God made sure there could be no question about who the father was. The promised child had to be clearly Abraham's son. God's attention to detail in protecting His promises is remarkable. He orchestrated circumstances to ensure that His word would be fulfilled exactly. Today, trust that God is guarding His promises to you with the same care. Even when you fail, He works behind the scenes to make sure His purposes are fulfilled. His faithfulness covers what your faithfulness cannot.
S: God had closed the wombs of Abimelech's household because of Sarah, Abraham's wife. He guarded the truth and protected the coming birth of Isaac. Trust that God guards His promises to you with the same careful attention.
P: God guards His promises carefully. Genesis 20:18: 'because of Sarah Abraham's wife.' God called her what she truly was and protected the promise. His faithfulness covers our failures.
