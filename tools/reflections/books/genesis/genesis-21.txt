# Genesis 21: The birth of Isaac

== 21:1
T: As He had said
R: 'And the LORD visited Sarah as he had said, and the LORD did unto Sarah as he had spoken.' After twenty-five years of waiting, the promise came true. Notice the repeated phrases: as he had said, as he had spoken. The emphasis is on God's faithfulness to His word. Every detail happened exactly as God promised. The LORD visited Sarah. That word visited means God came near to act on her behalf. He had not forgotten her during the long years of barrenness. At the right time, He came. This verse is a monument to God's faithfulness. Every promise He makes, He keeps. Joshua said at the end of his life, not one thing hath failed of all the good things which the LORD your God spake concerning you. Today, whatever promise you are waiting for, remember Sarah. God did as He had said. He will do the same for you. His word never fails, though it may take time.
S: The LORD visited Sarah and did exactly as He had promised. After twenty-five years of waiting, God's word came true. Whatever promise you are waiting on, remember that God does as He has said.

== 21:2
T: At the set time
R: 'For Sarah conceived, and bare Abraham a son in his old age, at the set time of which God had spoken to him.' Sarah conceived and gave birth at the set time God had spoken. Not a day too early, not a day too late. God's timing was perfect. In his old age, Abraham finally held his son. The phrase in his old age emphasizes the miracle. This birth was not the result of human ability but of divine power. God waited until it was humanly impossible so that the glory would be His alone. Galatians tells us that in the fulness of the time, God sent forth his Son. God's appointed times are always perfect. We often grow impatient, wondering why He delays. But when the set time comes, we see that His timing was right all along. Today, trust God's set times. He has not forgotten your situation. When His appointed moment arrives, it will be worth the wait. Rest in His perfect timing.
S: Sarah conceived and bore Abraham a son in his old age at the exact time God had promised. God's timing was perfect, and the miracle was clearly His work. Trust God's set times in your own life today.

== 21:3
T: Naming in obedience
R: 'And Abraham called the name of his son that was born unto him, whom Sarah bare to him, Isaac.' Abraham named his son Isaac, just as God had commanded. Isaac means he laughs. Every time Abraham called his son's name, he would remember his own laughter of disbelief turned into the laughter of joy. The name was a testimony. It told the story of God's faithfulness in the face of human doubt. God often gives us reminders of His faithfulness. Names, places, objects, and dates can become memorials of what He has done. Notice also Abraham's obedience. He did not choose his own name for the boy. He used the name God had given. His obedience extended even to this detail. Today, think of the reminders in your life of God's faithfulness. Perhaps it is a verse, a place, or a memory. Treasure these reminders. And let your obedience extend to the small details, as Abraham's did.
S: Abraham named his son Isaac, meaning he laughs, as God had commanded. The name was a lasting testimony of God turning doubt into joy. Treasure the reminders of God's faithfulness in your life today.

== 21:4
T: Faithful in the details
R: 'And Abraham circumcised his son Isaac being eight days old, as God had commanded him.' Abraham did exactly what God had commanded. On the eighth day, Isaac received the sign of the covenant. Abraham did not skip this step in his joy over the birth. He remembered his covenant obligations. Joy over God's blessings should lead to deeper obedience, not complacency. Sometimes, when God answers our prayers, we forget the commitments we made while we were waiting. Abraham did not. He was faithful in the details. The phrase as God had commanded him appears repeatedly in Abraham's story. His obedience was consistent. This is a mark of genuine faith. Jesus said, He that is faithful in that which is least is faithful also in much. Today, consider whether there are any commitments you made to God that you have forgotten in the busyness of life. Return to them. Let God's blessings lead you into deeper faithfulness, not away from it.

== 21:5
T: A hundred years old
R: 'And Abraham was an hundred years old, when his son Isaac was born unto him.' One hundred years old. Scripture records Abraham's age to emphasize the miracle. By any human standard, a man of one hundred fathering a child with a ninety-year-old wife was impossible. Yet God did it. Paul reflects on this in Romans 4, saying Abraham did not consider his own body now dead, but was fully persuaded that, what he had promised, he was able also to perform. Abraham's faith rested not on his circumstances but on God's ability. That is the essence of faith. We look beyond what seems possible to the God for whom all things are possible. Age, health, finances, and circumstances are real, but they are not the final word. God's power is. Today, whatever limitation you face, remember Abraham at one hundred. God is able to do what He has promised. Be fully persuaded of His power.
S: Abraham was one hundred years old when Isaac was born. The miracle showed that God's power is greater than every human limitation. Be fully persuaded today that God is able to do what He has promised.

== 21:8
T: Celebrating milestones
R: 'And the child grew, and was weaned: and Abraham made a great feast the same day that Isaac was weaned.' When Isaac was weaned, probably around two or three years old, Abraham threw a great feast. In the ancient world, surviving infancy was a significant milestone. Abraham celebrated God's faithfulness in bringing Isaac safely through his early years. Celebration is an important part of the life of faith. God commanded Israel to hold feasts to remember His works. Jesus told stories of celebrations when the lost are found. Celebrating milestones helps us remember God's goodness and builds faith for the future. Too often we move from one challenge to the next without pausing to give thanks. Abraham stopped and celebrated. Today, think of a milestone in your life or the life of someone you love. A recovery, an anniversary, a step of growth. Take time to celebrate it and thank God. Gratitude expressed in celebration honors the God who brought you this far.

== 21:9
T: Mockery at the feast
R: 'And Sarah saw the son of Hagar the Egyptian, which she had born unto Abraham, mocking.' In the middle of the celebration, Sarah noticed Ishmael mocking. He was about sixteen or seventeen and seems to have been ridiculing Isaac. Paul later interprets this, saying that he that was born after the flesh persecuted him that was born after the Spirit. The tension between the son of human effort and the son of promise had come to the surface. Joy and conflict often exist side by side. Even in our happiest moments, problems can emerge. The celebration of Isaac's milestone exposed the deep rivalry in Abraham's household. The consequences of Abraham and Sarah's earlier decision to use Hagar were now evident. What we sow in impatience, we may reap in conflict later. Today, if conflict is arising in a season that should be joyful, bring it to God. Ask Him for wisdom to handle it rightly. And remember that the children of promise may face mockery, but God protects His own.

== 21:10
T: A hard demand
R: 'Wherefore she said unto Abraham, Cast out this bondwoman and her son: for the son of this bondwoman shall not be heir with my son, even with Isaac.' Sarah demanded that Hagar and Ishmael be sent away. It was a harsh request, and it hurt Abraham deeply. Yet Paul later uses these words in Galatians to illustrate a spiritual truth. The son of the bondwoman represents relying on human effort and the law; the son of the freewoman represents the promise and grace. Paul writes, Cast out the bondwoman and her son: for the son of the bondwoman shall not be heir with the son of the freewoman. We cannot mix self-effort with grace as the basis of our standing with God. Salvation is by promise, not by works. The inheritance belongs to the children of promise. Today, examine whether you are trying to earn God's favor by your own efforts. Rest instead in the promise. Through faith in Christ, you are a child of the freewoman, an heir of grace.

== 21:24
