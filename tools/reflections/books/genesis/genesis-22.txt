# Genesis 22: The binding of Isaac

== 22:1
T: Here I am
R: 'And it came to pass after these things, that God did tempt Abraham, and said unto him, Abraham: and he said, Behold, here I am.' The word tempt here means to test or prove. God was not enticing Abraham to sin. He was testing the depth of his faith. James tells us that God tempts no man to evil, but He does allow trials that prove and strengthen our faith. When God called his name, Abraham answered, Here I am. These three words express readiness and availability. Abraham did not know what God was about to ask. Yet he made himself available. Samuel would later answer God the same way. Isaiah said, Here am I; send me. The posture of a faithful servant is readiness before knowing the assignment. Many of us want to know what God will ask before we say yes. Abraham said yes first. Today, when God calls your name, answer like Abraham: Here I am. Make yourself available to Him, trusting His heart before you know His plan.
S: God tested Abraham, and when He called his name, Abraham answered, Here I am. His readiness came before he knew what God would ask. Make yourself available to God today, trusting His heart before you know His plan.

== 22:2
T: Your only son
R: 'And he said, Take now thy son, thine only son Isaac, whom thou lovest, and get thee into the land of Moriah; and offer him there for a burnt offering.' This is one of the most difficult commands in all of Scripture. God asked Abraham to offer Isaac, the son of promise, the son he had waited twenty-five years for. Notice how God described him: thy son, thine only son Isaac, whom thou lovest. This is the first time the word love appears in the Bible, and it describes a father's love for his son. The story points forward to another Father and another Son. John 3:16 echoes this verse: For God so loved the world, that he gave his only begotten Son. What God asked of Abraham, God Himself would do. Moriah is the region where Solomon later built the temple, near where Jesus would be crucified. Today, marvel that God did not spare His own Son, but gave Him up for us all.
S: God told Abraham to take his only son Isaac, whom he loved, and offer him on a mountain in Moriah. This is the first mention of love in the Bible, and it points to the Father's love in giving His only Son. Marvel today that God did not spare His own Son for you.
P: Thy son, thine only son, whom thou lovest. Genesis 22:2 points to John 3:16. What God asked of Abraham, He Himself did for us in Jesus. Marvel at His love today.

== 22:3
T: Early obedience
R: 'And Abraham rose up early in the morning, and saddled his ass, and took two of his young men with him, and Isaac his son, and clave the wood for the burnt offering, and rose up, and went unto the place of which God had told him.' Abraham did not argue, delay, or bargain as he had done for Sodom. He rose up early in the morning. His obedience was immediate. Notice the practical details: he saddled the donkey, gathered servants, split the wood. Obedience is often expressed in ordinary tasks. Abraham did each step faithfully, even as his heart must have been breaking. Faith sometimes means taking the next practical step when everything in us wants to stop. Hebrews tells us that Abraham accounted that God was able to raise Isaac up, even from the dead. His obedience was built on trust in God's power. Today, take the next step of obedience God has given you, even if your heart is heavy. Do the practical thing in faith.
S: Abraham rose early, prepared the donkey and the wood, and set out for the place God had told him. His obedience was immediate and expressed in practical steps. Take the next step of obedience today, even if your heart is heavy.

== 22:4
T: Three days of walking
R: 'Then on the third day Abraham lifted up his eyes, and saw the place afar off.' For three days Abraham walked toward Moriah. Three days to think, to pray, to reconsider. Three days of carrying the weight of God's command. Yet he kept walking. This was not an impulsive act but a sustained obedience. Faith that holds steady over days of uncertainty is deep faith. Notice also the significance of the third day. Throughout Scripture, the third day is associated with deliverance and resurrection. Hosea says, in the third day he will raise us up. Jesus rose on the third day. Hebrews says Abraham received Isaac back from the dead in a figure. In Abraham's mind, Isaac was as good as dead for three days, and then he was restored. Today, if you are walking a long road of obedience, keep going. The third day is coming. God's deliverance often arrives just when we reach the place of greatest trial.
S: On the third day Abraham saw the place of sacrifice in the distance. He had walked for three days, holding steady in his obedience. Keep walking your road of faith, for God's third day of deliverance is coming.

== 22:5
T: We will come again
R: 'And Abraham said unto his young men, Abide ye here with the ass; and I and the lad will go yonder and worship, and come again to you.' Notice what Abraham said: I and the lad will go yonder and worship, and come again to you. We will come back. Both of us. Abraham fully intended to obey God and offer Isaac. Yet he also believed they would both return. How? Hebrews explains: Abraham accounted that God was able to raise him up, even from the dead. Abraham believed in resurrection before it had ever been seen. He trusted that God would keep His promise through Isaac, even if it meant raising him from the dead. Notice also that Abraham called this act worship. Worship is not just singing; it is surrendering what is most precious to God. Today, offer your worship as Abraham did, giving God what matters most, trusting Him to keep His promises. Faith believes that what we surrender to God is not lost.

== 22:6
T: The son carries the wood
R: 'And Abraham took the wood of the burnt offering, and laid it upon Isaac his son; and he took the fire in his hand, and a knife; and they went both of them together.' Isaac carried the wood up the mountain for his own sacrifice. Centuries later, on the same range of hills, another Son would carry wood up a hill. John writes that Jesus, bearing his cross went forth into a place called… Golgotha. The father carried the fire and the knife, the instruments of sacrifice. The son carried the wood. And they went both of them together. Father and son walked together in unity toward the place of sacrifice. In the same way, the Father and the Son were united in the plan of redemption. Jesus said, I and my Father are one. The cross was not something forced on an unwilling Son. Father and Son walked together. Today, meditate on the love of the Father and the Son, united in the work of saving you.

== 22:7
T: Where is the lamb?
R: 'And Isaac spake unto Abraham his father, and said, My father: and he said, Here am I, my son. And he said, Behold the fire and the wood: but where is the lamb for a burnt offering?' Isaac asked an innocent and heartbreaking question: where is the lamb? He could see the fire and the wood, but no animal for the sacrifice. Abraham answered his son with the same words he had said to God: Here am I. He was present for both his heavenly Father and his earthly son. Isaac's question echoes through the Old Testament. Every sacrifice, every Passover lamb, every offering at the temple asked the same question: where is the lamb who can truly take away sin? The answer came when John the Baptist saw Jesus and cried out, Behold the Lamb of God, which taketh away the sin of the world. Isaac's question found its answer in Christ. Today, rejoice that the question has been answered. Jesus is the Lamb God provided.

== 22:8
T: God will provide Himself a lamb
R: 'And Abraham said, My son, God will provide himself a lamb for a burnt offering: so they went both of them together.' Abraham's answer is one of the most profound statements of faith in Scripture. God will provide himself a lamb. Abraham did not know how, but he trusted that God would provide. His words were also prophetic in a way he could not have fully understood. God would indeed provide Himself a lamb. On this mountain, He provided a ram. But centuries later, God provided Himself, in the person of His Son, as the Lamb. Notice again: so they went both of them together. Isaac continued walking with his father after hearing the answer. Perhaps he trusted his father as his father trusted God. Today, whatever need you face, hold on to Abraham's words. God will provide. He provided a ram for Abraham, and He provided His Son for the world. He will provide what you need.

== 22:9
T: Laid on the altar
R: 'And they came to the place which God had told him of; and Abraham built an altar there, and laid the wood in order, and bound Isaac his son, and laid him on the altar upon the wood.' They arrived. Abraham built the altar, arranged the wood, bound his son, and laid him on the altar. Notice that Isaac did not resist. He was a young man, likely strong enough to overpower his elderly father. Yet he allowed himself to be bound. This points to Jesus, who was led as a lamb to the slaughter and opened not His mouth. He said, No man taketh it from me, but I lay it down of myself. Isaac's willing submission foreshadows Christ's willing sacrifice. Every step in this verse is an act of complete surrender: Abraham surrendering his son, Isaac surrendering his life. Today, consider what it means to lay your life on the altar before God. Paul urges us to present our bodies as living sacrifices. Surrender fully to Him.

Jesus said, He that loveth son or daughter more than me is not worthy of me. That does not mean we love our families less, but that we love God first. Abraham's willingness reveals a heart that held nothing back from God. Most of us will never face a test like this. But we all face moments where God asks us to release something we love. A dream, a relationship, a plan, a possession. Today, ask yourself if there is anything you are holding back from God. Open your hand. Trust Him with what you love most.

Jesus said, Martha, Martha, and Simon, Simon. It is the voice of One who cares deeply. God did not let the test go one moment too far. He stopped Abraham's hand right on time. And once again, Abraham answered, Here am I. His readiness had not changed from the beginning of the story to the end. He was available to God when the test began, and he was still listening when it ended. Today, listen for God calling your name. == 22:13
T: A substitute provided
R: 'And Abraham lifted up his eyes, and looked, and behold behind him a ram caught in a thicket by his horns: and Abraham went and took the ram, and offered him up for a burnt offering in the stead of his son.' God provided a ram caught in a thicket. Abraham offered it in the stead of his son, in Isaac's place. This is the first clear picture of substitution in Scripture. An innocent animal died so that Isaac could live. This is the heart of the gospel. Jesus died in our place. Peter writes, Who his own self bare our sins in his own body on the tree. Isaiah says, he was wounded for our transgressions… and with his stripes we are healed. The ram was caught by his horns in a thicket, perhaps a picture of Christ crowned with thorns. Isaac walked down the mountain alive because a substitute took his place. We too walk free because Christ took ours. Today, thank God for the Substitute who died in your stead.

== 22:16
T: God swears by Himself
R: 'And said, By myself have I sworn, saith the LORD, for because thou hast done this thing, and hast not withheld thy son, thine only son.' God swore an oath by Himself. There is no one greater to swear by. Hebrews explains: when God made promise to Abraham, because he could swear by no greater, he sware by himself. God bound Himself with the strongest possible commitment. Why did God swear an oath? Hebrews continues: God, willing more abundantly to shew unto the heirs of promise the immutability of his counsel, confirmed it by an oath. The oath was for our assurance, so that we might have strong consolation. God's promises are already certain, but He added an oath so that we would never doubt. Our hope rests on two unchangeable things: God's promise and God's oath. Hebrews calls this hope an anchor of the soul, both sure and stedfast. Today, anchor your soul in God's sworn promise. He has bound Himself to bless you in Christ, and He cannot lie.

== 22:23
T: Rebekah enters the story
R: 'And Bethuel begat Rebekah: these eight Milcah did bear to Nahor, Abraham's brother.' Here Rebekah is introduced for the first time. She would become Isaac's wife and the mother of Jacob and Esau. Her introduction, right after the binding of Isaac, is no accident. God had preserved Isaac's life on Moriah, and now He showed that He had already prepared a wife for him. God's provision is complete. He did not just spare Isaac; He prepared his future. The ram on the mountain was God's provision for that day. Rebekah was God's provision for the years ahead. == 22:24
T: Every child counts
R: 'And his concubine, whose name was Reumah, she bare also Tebah, and Gaham, and Thahash, and Maachah.' The chapter ends with the children of Nahor's concubine, Reumah. These children, born to a woman of lower status, are still named and recorded. Scripture does not overlook them. In God's record, every child matters, regardless of the circumstances of their birth. This short verse is a quiet reminder that God values every person. Society may rank people by status, but God sees each one. The concubine Reumah is named. Her children are named. They are part of the family history God preserved. Jesus welcomed children, touched the untouchable, and honored those society overlooked. Galatians declares that in Christ there is neither bond nor free. Today, remember that no one is insignificant in God's eyes. Look for those whom others may overlook because of their background or status, and treat them as God does: with dignity, value, and love.
