# Genesis 24:1-33: A bride for Isaac

== 24:1
T: Blessed in all things
R: 'And Abraham was old, and well stricken in age: and the LORD had blessed Abraham in all things.' What a summary of a life. Abraham was old, and the LORD had blessed him in all things. His journey had included famine, failure, family conflict, long waiting, and the greatest test imaginable. Yet looking back, the verdict was clear: God had blessed him in all things. Not every moment felt like blessing at the time. But from the vantage point of old age, Abraham could see God's faithfulness woven through every season. Romans 8:28 promises that all things work together for good to them that love God. Sometimes we can only see this looking back. Today, look back over your own life. Trace the ways God has blessed you, even through hard seasons. And trust that one day you too will be able to say that the LORD has blessed you in all things.
S: Abraham was old, and the LORD had blessed him in all things. Looking back, he could see God's faithfulness through every season, even the hard ones. Trace God's blessings in your own life today and trust Him with the rest.
P: Looking back, God blessed in all things. Genesis 24:1: 'the LORD had blessed Abraham in all things.' Even the hard seasons were woven into His goodness. Look back and give thanks today.

== 24:2
T: A trusted servant
R: 'And Abraham said unto his eldest servant of his house, that ruled over all that he had, Put, I pray thee, thy hand under my thigh.' Abraham called his most trusted servant, the one who managed everything he owned. Many believe this was Eliezer of Damascus, mentioned in chapter 15. This servant had proven faithful over many years. Now Abraham entrusted him with the most important task of all: finding a wife for Isaac. Faithfulness in small things leads to greater responsibilities. Jesus said, Well done, thou good and faithful servant: thou hast been faithful over a few things, I will make thee ruler over many things. This servant had earned Abraham's trust through years of reliable service. The gesture of placing his hand under Abraham's thigh was a solemn way of swearing an oath in that culture. Today, be faithful in whatever responsibilities you have now, however small. Faithfulness builds trust, and trust opens the door to greater service.
S: Abraham called his eldest servant, who ruled over all he had, to swear a solemn oath. Years of faithful service had earned the servant this great responsibility. Be faithful in your present responsibilities, for faithfulness opens doors to greater service.
P: Faithfulness builds trust. Genesis 24:2: 'his eldest servant of his house, that ruled over all that he had.' Years of faithful service led to a great task. Be faithful today.

== 24:3
T: Marrying in the faith
R: 'And I will make thee swear by the LORD, the God of heaven, and the God of the earth, that thou shalt not take a wife unto my son of the daughters of the Canaanites.' Abraham was concerned about Isaac's marriage. He did not want his son to marry a Canaanite woman. This was not about ethnicity but about faith. The Canaanites worshiped other gods and practiced great wickedness. Abraham wanted Isaac to marry someone who would share his commitment to the LORD. Marriage profoundly shapes a person's spiritual life. Paul writes, Be ye not unequally yoked together with unbelievers. A spouse can either strengthen or weaken one's faith. Abraham understood this, and he took steps to protect Isaac. Notice that he called on the God of heaven and earth, acknowledging God's sovereignty over this important decision. Today, if you are single, seek God's guidance about marriage. If you are a parent, pray for your children's future spouses. And trust the God of heaven and earth with this important part of life.
S: Abraham made his servant swear not to take a wife for Isaac from the Canaanites. He wanted Isaac to marry someone who shared his faith in the LORD. Seek God's guidance about marriage and pray for the future spouses of your children.
P: Marriage shapes faith. Genesis 24:3: 'thou shalt not take a wife unto my son of the daughters of the Canaanites.' Abraham wanted a spouse who shared Isaac's faith. Pray about this today.

== 24:4
T: Looking to the right place
R: 'But thou shalt go unto my country, and to my kindred, and take a wife unto my son Isaac.' Abraham sent his servant back to his homeland to find a wife among his relatives. He wanted a wife for Isaac who came from a family with at least some knowledge of the true God. Wisdom involves knowing where to look. We are more likely to find what we seek when we look in the right places. If we want godly friendships, we should seek them where godly people gather. If we want to grow spiritually, we should place ourselves where spiritual growth happens. Abraham's instruction was both practical and spiritual. He took deliberate steps to align Isaac's future with God's purposes. Proverbs says, He that walketh with wise men shall be wise. Today, think about where you are looking for what you need, whether friendship, guidance, or support. Look in places where God is honored. Your environment shapes your outcomes.
S: Abraham told his servant to go to his homeland and find a wife for Isaac among his relatives. He wisely directed the search toward people who knew something of the true God. Look for what you need in places where God is honored.

== 24:5
T: Honest questions before commitment
R: 'And the servant said unto him, Peradventure the woman will not be willing to follow me unto this land: must I needs bring thy son again unto the land from whence thou camest?' The servant asked a wise question before taking the oath. What if the woman would not come? Should he take Isaac back to Mesopotamia? The servant wanted to understand the full scope of his commitment before making it. Asking questions before committing is not a lack of faith; it is wisdom. Jesus said that anyone planning to build a tower should first count the cost. The servant wanted to know what he was swearing to. This kind of careful thought honors both the one making the request and the commitment itself. Rash vows can lead to trouble, as Jephthah's story shows. Today, before making important commitments, take time to ask questions. Understand what you are agreeing to. Wise commitment is stronger commitment.
S: Before taking the oath, the servant asked what to do if the woman would not come. Asking honest questions before committing is wisdom, not a lack of faith. Before making important commitments, take time to understand what you are agreeing to.

== 24:6
T: No going back
R: 'And Abraham said unto him, Beware thou that thou bring not my son thither again.' Abraham was firm. Under no circumstances should Isaac go back to Mesopotamia. God had called Abraham out of that land and promised Canaan to his descendants. Going back would mean undoing God's call. Isaac's future was in the land of promise, not in the land Abraham had left. This principle applies to our spiritual lives. When God calls us out of something, we should not go back to it. The Israelites in the wilderness often wanted to return to Egypt. But God had called them forward. Jesus said, No man, having put his hand to the plough, and looking back, is fit for the kingdom of God. Today, consider whether there is anything God has called you away from that you are tempted to return to. Old habits, old relationships, old ways of thinking. Do not go back. Your future is in the land of His promise.
S: Abraham firmly told the servant never to take Isaac back to Mesopotamia. God had called Abraham out of that land, and going back would undo His call. Do not return to what God has called you away from.
P: Don't go back to what God called you out of. Genesis 24:6: 'Beware thou that thou bring not my son thither again.' Your future is in His promise. Keep moving forward.

Abraham expressed complete confidence. The same God who had led him out of his homeland and made promises to him would send an angel ahead of the servant. Abraham's faith was rooted in his history with God. God had been faithful before, so Abraham trusted Him now. Notice the promise: he shall send his angel before thee. God goes ahead of His people. He prepares the way before we arrive. Exodus records God saying, Behold, I send an Angel before thee, to keep thee in the way. Deuteronomy says, the LORD, he it is that doth go before thee. Today, whatever task or journey lies ahead of you, trust that God goes before you. He is already at work in places you have not yet reached, preparing the way.

== 24:8
T: Responsibility and release
R: 'And if the woman will not be willing to follow thee, then thou shalt be clear from this my oath: only bring not my son thither again.' Abraham gave the servant a clear limit to his responsibility. If the woman refused to come, the servant would be released from his oath. He was responsible to try faithfully, but not responsible for the woman's choice. This is a freeing principle. We are responsible for our faithfulness, not for outcomes beyond our control. Paul wrote, I have planted, Apollos watered; but God gave the increase. We plant and water; God gives the growth. We share the gospel; people respond. We cannot force anyone's will. Many believers carry burdens that are not theirs, feeling responsible for others' decisions. But God asks for our faithfulness, not our control. Today, release the outcomes you cannot control into God's hands. Do your part faithfully, and trust Him with the rest.
S: Abraham told the servant he would be released from his oath if the woman refused to come. The servant was responsible for faithful effort, not for her choice. Release outcomes you cannot control into God's hands today.
P: You're responsible for faithfulness, not outcomes. Genesis 24:8: 'if the woman will not be willing… thou shalt be clear from this my oath.' Do your part and trust God.

== 24:9
T: Sealing the commitment
R: 'And the servant put his hand under the thigh of Abraham his master, and sware to him concerning that matter.' The servant took the oath. He committed himself to the task. Having asked his questions and understood the terms, he was ready to act. There is a time for questions and a time for commitment. The servant moved from one to the other with clarity. Once he understood, he did not hesitate. Some people get stuck in endless questions and never commit. Others commit too quickly without understanding. The servant found the right balance. He asked, he understood, and then he committed fully. Ecclesiastes warns, Better is it that thou shouldest not vow, than that thou shouldest vow and not pay. Commitment is serious. But when we have counted the cost, we should commit wholeheartedly. Today, is there something you have been considering for a long time? If you have asked your questions and sought God's guidance, it may be time to commit. Step forward in faith.

== 24:10

The servant set out well equipped. Ten camels and gifts from his master's wealth went with him. He represented Abraham's household and carried its resources. This is a picture of how God sends us. When God gives us a task, He equips us with what we need. We do not go in our own resources but in His. The servant went with his master's goods in his hand. We go with Christ's resources: His Spirit, His word, His authority. Jesus said to His disciples, All power is given unto me in heaven and in earth. Go ye therefore. We go in His power. The journey to Mesopotamia was long, perhaps several weeks. Today, remember that you do not serve God in your own strength. You are equipped with His resources. Go confidently into whatever task He gives.

The servant arrived at the city well at the time when women came to draw water. This was practical wisdom. He positioned himself where he was most likely to meet the woman he was looking for. Faith does not exclude common sense. The servant prayed, as we will see, but he also used good judgment. He went to the right place at the right time. God often works through our wise choices. Proverbs says, The heart of the prudent getteth knowledge. Being in the right place does not guarantee an outcome, but it opens the door for God to work. Many important encounters in Scripture happen at wells: Jacob met Rachel, Moses met Zipporah, and Jesus met the Samaritan woman. Today, combine prayer with practical wisdom. Position yourself where God can use you, and trust Him to work.

== 24:14
T: Looking for character
R: The servant prayed that the right young woman would say, 'Drink, and I will give thy camels drink also.' This was a clever request. The servant was not asking for a random sign but for a test of character. Offering water to a stranger was common courtesy. But volunteering to water ten thirsty camels was extraordinary. A camel can drink many gallons of water after a long journey. Watering ten of them would require many trips to the well. Only a generous, hardworking, kind young woman would make such an offer. The servant was looking for character, not just beauty or social status. God values character above appearance. Samuel was reminded, the LORD looketh on the heart. In choosing friends, partners, and leaders, character matters most. Today, ask God to help you value character in others. And ask Him to develop in you the kind of generous, servant-hearted character that goes beyond what is expected.
S: The servant prayed that the right woman would offer to water his camels as well as give him a drink. He was looking for generosity and character, not just appearance. Value character in others and ask God to develop a servant heart in you.

== 24:17

He took the initiative to approach her and make his request. Prayer is not a substitute for action. After praying, the servant stepped forward in faith. This is the balance of the Christian life. Today, after you pray about something, take the next step of faith. Approach the person, make the call, ask the question. God often reveals His answers as we step forward in obedience.

She was exactly the kind of bride Abraham had hoped for, from his own kindred. Out of all the young women in the city, the first one to come to the well, the one who passed the character test, was from Abraham's family. This was no coincidence. == 24:25
T: Room in the house
R: 'She said moreover unto him, We have both straw and provender enough, and room to lodge in.' Rebekah offered more than the servant asked. He asked if there was room to lodge. She told him there was straw and feed for the camels as well as room for the men. Once again, she exceeded expectations. Her generosity was consistent. It was not a one-time act at the well but part of her character. This verse also beautifully echoes the gospel. There is room in the Father's house. Jesus said, In my Father's house are many mansions. When the prodigal son came home, he found that there was room for him. Unlike the inn at Bethlehem that had no room for Jesus, God's house always has room for those who come. Today, thank God that there is room for you in His house. And open your own home and heart to others, offering hospitality generously as Rebekah did.

== 24:26
T: Worship first
R: 'And the man bowed down his head, and worshipped the LORD.' When the servant realized God had answered his prayer, his first response was worship. He bowed his head right there at the well and worshiped the LORD. Before celebrating with Rebekah, before going to her house, before anything else, he gave thanks to God. This is the right response to answered prayer. Too often we pray urgently but forget to thank God when He answers. Jesus healed ten lepers, but only one returned to give thanks. Jesus asked, Were there not ten cleansed? but where are the nine? The servant was like the one who returned. His heart overflowed with gratitude. Worship should be our first response to God's blessings. Psalm 103 says, Bless the LORD, O my soul, and forget not all his benefits. Today, think of a prayer God has answered recently. Stop and worship Him for it. Do not let His faithfulness go unacknowledged.

== 24:27
T: I being in the way
R: 'Blessed be the LORD God of my master Abraham, who hath not left destitute my master of his mercy and his truth: I being in the way, the LORD led me.' The servant praised God for His mercy and truth, His steadfast love and faithfulness. Then he made a profound statement: I being in the way, the LORD led me. The servant was on the path of obedience, and God guided him. This is a key principle of guidance. God leads those who are already moving in obedience. It is easier to steer a moving ship than one sitting at anchor. The servant had set out, traveled, prayed, and acted. And as he walked in the way, God led him. Proverbs says, In all thy ways acknowledge him, and he shall direct thy paths. Many people wait for guidance before taking any step. But God often guides us as we go. Today, take the next step of obedience you know to take. As you walk in the way, trust the LORD to lead you.

== 24:29
T: Introducing Laban
R: 'And Rebekah had a brother, and his name was Laban: and Laban ran out unto the man, unto the well.' Here we meet Laban, Rebekah's brother. He will play a significant role later in the story of Jacob, where he proves to be a shrewd and manipulative man. Even here, there may be hints of his character. In the next verse, we learn that he ran out after seeing the gold jewelry. People's motives are not always what they seem. Laban ran to the well, but was it hospitality or interest in wealth? Scripture often introduces characters with subtle clues about who they are. Jesus taught, by their fruits ye shall know them. We should be wise and discerning in our relationships, not cynical, but attentive. Today, ask God for discernment in your relationships. Be kind to everyone, but pay attention to the fruit of people's lives. And examine your own motives. Are you serving others for what you can get, or for what you can give?

== 24:30
T: Drawn by gold
R: 'And it came to pass, when he saw the earring and bracelets upon his sister's hands… that he came unto the man; and, behold, he stood by the camels at the well.' Laban's motivation becomes clearer. He came running when he saw the gold on his sister's hands. Wealth attracted his attention. This foreshadows Laban's later greed in his dealings with Jacob. Money has a powerful pull on the human heart. It can make people suddenly hospitable, friendly, and eager, but for the wrong reasons. Paul warns, For the love of money is the root of all evil. Laban's eagerness was not wrong in itself, but his motivation reveals a heart attracted to material gain. Contrast this with Rebekah, who served the stranger before she knew anything about his wealth. Her kindness was genuine; Laban's may have been calculated. Today, examine what draws your attention and motivates your actions. Let your kindness be genuine, not dependent on what others can give you.

== 24:33
T: Mission before comfort
R: 'And there was set meat before him to eat: but he said, I will not eat, until I have told mine errand. And he said, Speak on.' Food was placed before the servant after a long journey. He must have been hungry. But he refused to eat until he had explained why he had come. His mission came before his comfort. This shows remarkable focus and dedication. The servant was not distracted by hospitality or his own needs. He wanted to complete his assignment. Jesus showed the same focus. When His disciples urged Him to eat, He said, My meat is to do the will of him that sent me, and to finish his work. There are times to rest and eat, and there are times when the mission must come first. The servant knew the importance of his errand. Today, consider what assignment God has given you. Is anything distracting you from it? Ask God to give you the focus and dedication to put His purposes first.
