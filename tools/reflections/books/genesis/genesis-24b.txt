# Genesis 24:34-67: Rebekah says, I will go

== 24:34
T: Knowing who you serve
R: 'And he said, I am Abraham's servant.' The servant began his speech with his identity. He did not give his own name. He did not boast of his position or abilities. He simply said, I am Abraham's servant. His identity was defined by whom he served. This is a beautiful model for believers. Paul often began his letters, Paul, a servant of Jesus Christ. Our primary identity is not our job, our accomplishments, or even our name, but whose we are. We belong to Christ. The servant's humility is also striking. He was entrusted with great wealth and responsibility, yet he introduced himself simply as a servant. True greatness does not need to announce itself. Jesus, the greatest of all, said, I am among you as he that serveth. Today, remember whose you are. Let your identity be rooted not in what you do but in the One you serve. And carry that identity with humility.
S: The servant introduced himself simply as Abraham's servant. His identity was defined by whom he served, not by his own name or position. Let your identity today be rooted in belonging to Christ.
P: Know whose you are. Genesis 24:34: 'I am Abraham's servant.' His identity came from whom he served. Yours comes from belonging to Jesus. Live that today.

== 24:35
T: Giving God the credit
R: 'And the LORD hath blessed my master greatly; and he is become great: and he hath given him flocks, and herds, and silver, and gold, and menservants, and maidservants, and camels, and asses.' The servant described Abraham's great wealth, but notice the first words: the LORD hath blessed my master. He did not say Abraham had become rich through his own skill. He gave credit to God. Every good thing Abraham had came from the LORD. This is the right way to speak about blessings. Moses warned Israel not to say, My power and the might of mine hand hath gotten me this wealth. Instead, thou shalt remember the LORD thy God: for it is he that giveth thee power to get wealth. When we talk about our successes, do we give God the credit? James writes, Every good gift and every perfect gift is from above. Today, when you speak about your blessings, give the glory to God. Let your words reflect that everything you have comes from His hand.
S: The servant described Abraham's wealth but began by saying that the LORD had blessed him greatly. He gave God the credit for every good thing. When you speak about your blessings today, give the glory to God.

== 24:36
T: The heir of all things
R: 'And Sarah my master's wife bare a son to my master when she was old: and unto him hath he given all that he hath.' The servant spoke of Isaac, the miracle son born to Sarah in her old age. And he declared that Abraham had given Isaac everything he owned. Isaac was the sole heir. This is a picture of Christ. Hebrews says that God hath appointed his Son heir of all things. Just as Abraham gave everything to Isaac, the Father has given all things into the hands of the Son. And here is the amazing part: through faith in Christ, we become joint heirs with Him. Romans says, if children, then heirs; heirs of God, and joint-heirs with Christ. The servant was inviting Rebekah to share in Isaac's inheritance. The gospel invites us to share in Christ's. Today, marvel that you are an heir of God through Christ. Everything the Father has given the Son is shared with those who belong to Him.
S: The servant explained that Isaac, born to Sarah in old age, was heir to all that Abraham had. This points to Christ, appointed heir of all things. Marvel today that through faith you are a joint heir with Christ.

== 24:37
T: Speaking truthfully
R: 'And my master made me swear, saying, Thou shalt not take a wife to my son of the daughters of the Canaanites, in whose land I dwell.' The servant now recounted his oath to Abraham. He told the family exactly what had happened, including the conditions of his mission. He was transparent about his purpose. Honesty in communication builds trust. The servant did not manipulate the family or hide his intentions. He laid everything out clearly. This is how God's people should communicate. Ephesians says, Wherefore putting away lying, speak every man truth with his neighbour. When we share the gospel, we too should be honest and clear. We do not need to manipulate people or use pressure tactics. We simply tell the truth about who Jesus is and what He has done, and we trust God to work in hearts. Paul wrote that we have renounced the hidden things of dishonesty. Today, speak truthfully and transparently in your conversations. Let your words build trust.
S: The servant told Rebekah's family about the oath Abraham had made him swear. He was transparent and honest about his mission. Speak truthfully and transparently in your conversations today.

== 24:38
T: Back to the family
R: 'But thou shalt go unto my father's house, and to my kindred, and take a wife unto my son.' The servant repeated Abraham's instructions. Abraham wanted Isaac's wife to come from his own family. This was not about pride of ancestry but about faith. Abraham's kindred, though imperfect, had some knowledge of the true God. They were more likely to share his values. Families matter in the transmission of faith. Many people come to know God because of a parent, grandparent, or relative who prayed for them and lived out their faith. God works through family lines. At the same time, faith must become personal. Rebekah would need to choose for herself. Her family background was a starting point, not a guarantee. Today, thank God for any members of your family who have helped you know Him. And pray for family members who do not yet know Christ. God often works through family connections to draw people to Himself.

== 24:41

R: 'Then shalt thou be clear from this my oath, when thou comest to my kindred; and if they give not thee one, thou shalt be clear from my oath.' The servant explained that he would be released from his oath if the family refused. This showed Rebekah's family that they had a real choice. The servant was not there to force anything. Their decision mattered. God respects human choice. He does not force anyone into His kingdom. The gospel is an invitation, not a coercion. It also respects the dignity of those we speak to. Today, when you share your faith or make requests of others, respect their freedom to choose. Present the truth clearly and lovingly, and then trust God with their response. Faithfulness, not results, is what God asks of us.

== 24:55

R: 'And her brother and her mother said, Let the damsel abide with us a few days, at the least ten; after that she shall go.' Rebekah's family wanted her to stay a little longer. Their request was understandable. They were about to say goodbye to a daughter and sister they might never see again. Ten days seemed like a small request. But delays can be dangerous when God has made His will clear. A few days can become weeks, and weeks can become months. Laban would later delay Jacob for twenty years. The family's request, though loving, could have derailed the mission. Sometimes the people who love us most can unintentionally hold us back from God's call. Jesus said, He that loveth father or mother more than me is not worthy of me. This does not mean we disregard family, but that God's call comes first. Today, if God has called you to something, beware of delays, even well-meaning ones. When God says go, it is wise to go.
