# Genesis 25: Abraham's death; Jacob and Esau

== 25:1
T: Life after loss
R: 'Then again Abraham took a wife, and her name was Keturah.' After Sarah's death, Abraham married again. Life continued. Grief had its season, but Abraham did not stop living. He remarried and had more children. There is something hopeful in this verse. Even in old age, after great loss, Abraham embraced new beginnings. Some people feel guilty about moving forward after losing someone they love. But moving forward is not betrayal. It is a recognition that God still has purposes for our lives. Abraham honored Sarah's memory by burying her with care, and he honored God's gift of life by continuing to live fully. Ecclesiastes reminds us there is a time to mourn and a time to dance. Today, if you have experienced loss, know that it is not wrong to embrace new seasons. God's purposes for your life continue. Let Him lead you into whatever new beginnings He has prepared.
S: After Sarah's death, Abraham married again and took a wife named Keturah. He embraced new beginnings even in old age and after great loss. Know that God's purposes continue for you after loss, and let Him lead you into new seasons.
P: Life continues after loss. Genesis 25:1: 'Then again Abraham took a wife, and her name was Keturah.' Moving forward isn't betrayal. God still has purposes for you.

== 25:2
T: Many descendants
R: 'And she bare him Zimran, and Jokshan, and Medan, and Midian, and Ishbak, and Shuah.' Keturah bore Abraham six sons. God's promise to make Abraham the father of many nations was being fulfilled in more ways than one. Among these sons was Midian, whose descendants, the Midianites, would appear often in Israel's history. Moses later found refuge among the Midianites and married the daughter of Jethro, a Midianite priest. Abraham's descendants spread across the region, carrying some memory of their father's God. God's blessing on Abraham overflowed beyond Isaac's line. Many nations traced their ancestry to him. This was a partial fulfillment of the promise that he would be a father of many nations. God's promises often have layers of fulfillment. Today, marvel at the abundance of God's blessing. He did more for Abraham than Abraham could have imagined. And He is able to do the same for you, beyond what you ask or think.
S: Keturah bore Abraham six more sons, including Midian. God's promise to make Abraham a father of many nations overflowed beyond Isaac's line. Marvel today at the abundance of God's blessing, which goes beyond what we imagine.
P: God's blessing overflows. Genesis 25:2: 'she bare him Zimran, and Jokshan, and Medan, and Midian.' Abraham became father of many nations. God does more than we imagine!

== 25:3
T: Woven into the story
R: 'And Jokshan begat Sheba, and Dedan. And the sons of Dedan were Asshurim, and Letushim, and Leummim.' The names Sheba and Dedan appear again, as they did in the Table of Nations. Abraham's descendants intermingled with other peoples in Arabia. Biblical genealogies can be complex, with names repeating across different lines. But the larger point is clear: Abraham's family spread widely. His influence touched many peoples. This reminds us that faith has a way of spreading beyond what we see. The influence of one faithful person can ripple outward to many families and nations. Abraham did not personally evangelize these peoples, but his family carried his story with them. Today, think about how your faith might ripple out to others through your family and relationships. You may never see the full reach of your influence. But God can use your faithfulness to touch people and places far beyond your own circle.
S: Jokshan's sons included Sheba and Dedan, and Abraham's descendants spread widely across the region. The influence of one faithful life can ripple outward to many families. Trust God to use your faithfulness to touch people beyond your own circle.

== 25:4
T: Midian's line
R: 'And the sons of Midian; Ephah, and Epher, and Hanoch, and Abidah, and Eldaah. All these were the children of Keturah.' The sons of Midian are listed. Midian's descendants would have a mixed relationship with Israel. Jethro, Moses' father-in-law, was a Midianite who offered wise counsel and blessed the LORD. But later the Midianites oppressed Israel in the days of Gideon. The same family line produced both friends and enemies of God's people. Every family contains people who choose differently. Faith is not inherited automatically. Each generation, each person, must choose whom they will serve. Isaiah later prophesied that the camels of Midian and Ephah would come to Zion, bringing gold and incense and showing forth the praises of the LORD. Even Midian would one day worship. Today, pray for families that seem divided in faith. God is able to draw even those who have wandered far back to Himself. His mercy reaches every family line.
S: The sons of Midian are listed, a family line that produced both friends and enemies of Israel. Faith is not inherited automatically, and each person must choose. Pray today for families divided in faith, trusting God's mercy to reach them.

== 25:5
T: All to the heir
R: 'And Abraham gave all that he had unto Isaac.' Abraham made his will clear. Everything went to Isaac, the son of promise. This was not favoritism in a petty sense; it was obedience to God's covenant. God had said the promise would come through Isaac, and Abraham honored that. Isaac's inheritance was not something he earned. He received it because of who he was: the son of promise. Our inheritance in Christ works the same way. We do not earn it. We receive it because we are children of God through faith. Galatians says, if ye be Christ's, then are ye Abraham's seed, and heirs according to the promise. Everything the Father has is ours in Christ. Colossians says we are made meet to be partakers of the inheritance of the saints in light. Today, rejoice that you are an heir, not because of your performance but because of God's promise. Live as one who has received everything in Christ.

== 25:6
T: Gifts and wise provision
R: 'But unto the sons of the concubines, which Abraham had, Abraham gave gifts, and sent them away from Isaac his son, while he yet lived, eastward, unto the east country.' Abraham did not neglect his other sons. He gave them gifts and sent them away to the east while he was still alive. This was wise. By settling these matters during his lifetime, Abraham prevented conflict over the inheritance after his death. He protected Isaac's position while providing for his other children. Many families experience painful conflicts over inheritance. Abraham's planning shows wisdom and care. He made his intentions clear and acted on them while he could. Proverbs says, A good man leaveth an inheritance to his children's children. Wise planning is an act of love. It protects relationships and prevents future strife. Today, consider whether there are matters in your life that need wise planning. Do not leave important things unresolved. Act with foresight and care for those who will come after you.

He had been seventy-five when God first called him out of Haran. That means he walked with God in the land of promise for a full century. One hundred years of faith, of altars and tents, of waiting and trusting. That is a long obedience. Abraham's faith was not a brief burst of enthusiasm. It was a lifetime of walking with God through every season. He had his failures, but he kept returning to God. Hebrews says, By faith Abraham… sojourned in the land of promise. His whole life was a pilgrimage of faith. Today, think of your faith as a long journey, not a sprint. Whatever stage of life you are in, God calls you to keep walking with Him. Faithfulness over a lifetime is what builds a legacy.

== 25:8
T: Full of years
R: 'Then Abraham gave up the ghost, and died in a good old age, an old man, and full of years; and was gathered to his people.' Abraham died just as God had promised: in a good old age. He was full of years, satisfied with life. And he was gathered to his people. This phrase suggests more than burial; it hints at reunion beyond death, joining those who had gone before. Abraham's death was peaceful. God had kept His word. Jesus later said that God is the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, and that He is not the God of the dead, but of the living. Abraham is alive with God. For believers, death is not the end but a gathering home. Paul wrote, to depart, and to be with Christ… is far better. Today, if you fear death, remember Abraham. Those who walk with God die full of years and are gathered home. Live with that hope, and let it give you peace.

These two half-brothers had been separated since childhood amid tension and conflict. But at their father's grave, they stood side by side. Death often brings families together. Grief can soften old wounds and remind us of what we share. Whatever divided Isaac and Ishmael, they both loved and honored Abraham. This moment of reconciliation, even if brief, is beautiful. It reminds us that relationships broken by conflict can sometimes be restored. Psalm 133 says, Behold, how good and how pleasant it is for brethren to dwell together in unity! Today, think of a family member from whom you have been estranged. Is there a way to reach out? Do not wait for a funeral to come together. Seek reconciliation now, while there is time. God can heal even long-standing divisions.

The only land he ever owned in Canaan became his resting place. He and Sarah lay together in the land God had promised to their descendants. Their burial was a final act of faith. Even in death, they claimed the promise. They did not ask to be buried in their homeland of Ur. They chose to rest in Canaan because they believed God's word. Hebrews says, These all died in faith, not having received the promises, but having seen them afar off. Abraham and Sarah died believing. Their graves were a declaration that God would keep His promise. Today, let your life and even your death declare your faith in God's promises. Live and die as one who believes that God's word is true and that the best is yet to come.

Her story was part of God's record. God keeps His promises to everyone He makes them to, not only to the main characters of the story. Today, take comfort that God remembers His promises to you, even if you feel like a side character in someone else's story. He sees you, He knows your name, and He is faithful to His word.

== 25:23
T: The elder shall serve the younger
R: 'And the LORD said unto her, Two nations are in thy womb… and the one people shall be stronger than the other people; and the elder shall serve the younger.' God answered Rebekah. Two nations were in her womb, and the elder would serve the younger. This went against the customs of the time, where the firstborn received the greater inheritance. But God's choices are not bound by human tradition. Paul refers to this verse in Romans 9 to show that God's choice is based on His purpose, not on human merit. Before the twins were born or had done anything good or bad, God declared His plan. This reveals God's sovereign grace. He chooses according to His own wisdom, not according to what people deserve. Throughout Scripture, God often chooses the younger, the weaker, or the overlooked: Abel over Cain, Isaac over Ishmael, Jacob over Esau, Joseph over his brothers, David over his brothers.
