# Genesis 26: Isaac and the wells

== 26:1
T: Facing the same trials
R: 'And there was a famine in the land, beside the first famine that was in the days of Abraham. And Isaac went unto Abimelech king of the Philistines unto Gerar.' Isaac faced a famine, just as his father had. The trials of one generation often repeat in the next. Isaac went toward Gerar, perhaps on his way to Egypt, as Abraham had done. We cannot live on our parents' faith. Isaac had heard the stories of Abraham's journey, but now he had to trust God for himself in his own famine. This is true for all of us. The faith of our parents and grandparents is a gift, but it cannot replace our own walk with God. Each of us will face famines, seasons of lack and difficulty, that test whether our faith is real. Today, if you are facing a trial similar to one your family faced before, remember that God was faithful to them. And trust that He will be faithful to you, in your own famine.
S: A famine struck the land in Isaac's day, just as it had in Abraham's. Each generation must face its own trials and trust God personally. When you face a familiar trial, remember God's past faithfulness and trust Him for yourself.
P: Each generation faces its own famine. Genesis 26:1: 'there was a famine in the land, beside the first famine that was in the days of Abraham.' Trust God personally in your trial today.

== 26:2
T: Do not go down to Egypt
R: 'And the LORD appeared unto him, and said, Go not down into Egypt; dwell in the land which I shall tell thee of.' Before Isaac could repeat his father's mistake of going to Egypt, God appeared to him. Go not down into Egypt. God gave clear direction: stay in the land. Abraham had gone to Egypt during famine without asking God, and it brought trouble. God now prevented Isaac from making the same error. God's guidance often comes at critical moments. He speaks to keep us from mistakes. When we are about to make a decision driven by fear or worldly wisdom, God may intervene with a clear word. Isaiah says, thine ears shall hear a word behind thee, saying, This is the way, walk ye in it. Egypt seemed like the logical place to go in a famine. It had food. But God's way was to stay and trust. Today, before making a major decision, listen for God's direction. His guidance may contradict what seems logical, but it is always right.
S: God appeared to Isaac and told him not to go down to Egypt but to stay in the land. He prevented Isaac from repeating his father's mistake. Listen for God's direction before major decisions, even when His way seems less logical.
P: Listen before you go. Genesis 26:2: 'Go not down into Egypt; dwell in the land which I shall tell thee of.' God's way may not seem logical, but it's always right.

== 26:3
T: I will be with thee
R: 'Sojourn in this land, and I will be with thee, and will bless thee; for unto thee, and unto thy seed, I will give all these countries, and I will perform the oath which I sware unto Abraham thy father.' God renewed the covenant with Isaac. The same promises given to Abraham were now given to Isaac. And at the heart of it all was this: I will be with thee. In the middle of famine, God's presence was the greatest provision. When God is with us, we have everything we need. Moses said to God, If thy presence go not with me, carry us not up hence. Jesus promised, lo, I am with you alway, even unto the end of the world. God's presence is the foundation of every blessing. Today, if you are in a time of scarcity, hold on to God's promise: I will be with thee. His presence is enough. He will provide what you need as you stay where He has placed you.
S: God told Isaac to stay in the land and promised to be with him and bless him. In the middle of famine, God's presence was the greatest provision. Hold on to the promise of God's presence in your time of need.
P: God's presence is enough. Genesis 26:3: 'Sojourn in this land, and I will be with thee, and will bless thee.' In famine or plenty, He is with you. Trust Him today.

== 26:4
T: The promise passes on
R: 'And I will make thy seed to multiply as the stars of heaven, and will give unto thy seed all these countries; and in thy seed shall all the nations of the earth be blessed.' God repeated to Isaac the same promises He had made to Abraham: descendants like the stars, the land, and blessing for all nations. The covenant now officially passed to the next generation. This is the beauty of God's covenant. It is not limited to one person or one lifetime. It continues through generations. Isaac received the same promises his father had received. God's faithfulness is generational. Psalm 105 says, He hath remembered his covenant for ever, the word which he commanded to a thousand generations. The promise of blessing for all nations through Abraham's seed would ultimately be fulfilled in Christ. Every generation of believers inherits this promise. Today, rejoice that you are part of a promise that spans thousands of years. God's covenant with Abraham and Isaac reaches all the way to you through Jesus.
S: God repeated to Isaac the promises of countless descendants, the land and blessing for all nations. The covenant passed from Abraham to the next generation. Rejoice that God's covenant promise reaches all the way to you through Jesus.
P: A promise across generations. Genesis 26:4: 'in thy seed shall all the nations of the earth be blessed.' The promise to Abraham passed to Isaac, and in Christ, to you!

== 26:5
T: A father's obedience
R: 'Because that Abraham obeyed my voice, and kept my charge, my commandments, my statutes, and my laws.' God told Isaac that the blessing came partly because of Abraham's obedience. Abraham had obeyed God's voice and kept His commands. His faithfulness had consequences that reached his son. Our obedience affects not only our own lives but the lives of those who come after us. Parents who walk faithfully with God create a heritage of blessing for their children. Exodus speaks of God showing mercy unto thousands of them that love me, and keep my commandments. This does not mean children are saved by their parents' faith. Each must believe for themselves. But a godly heritage is a precious gift. Isaac inherited not just Abraham's wealth but the blessing of his obedience. Today, consider the heritage you are building for those who come after you. Your faithfulness matters beyond your own life. Obey God's voice, knowing that your obedience may bless generations to come.
S: God told Isaac that the blessing was connected to Abraham's obedience to His voice and commands. A parent's faithfulness creates a heritage of blessing for the next generation. Obey God today, knowing that your faithfulness can bless those who come after you.
P: Your obedience blesses generations. Genesis 26:5: 'Because that Abraham obeyed my voice, and kept my charge.' Abraham's faithfulness blessed Isaac. Build a godly heritage today.

== 26:6
T: Staying where God placed him
R: 'And Isaac dwelt in Gerar.' Isaac obeyed. He stayed in Gerar instead of going to Egypt. This simple verse shows his response to God's word. He trusted that God would provide in the land where He had placed him. Staying can be harder than going. When things are difficult, our instinct is often to escape to somewhere that seems better. But sometimes God calls us to stay put and trust Him to provide where we are. Isaac's obedience in staying would lead to great blessing, as the chapter shows. He would sow and reap a hundredfold in the very land of famine. Jeremiah told the exiles to settle in Babylon and seek its peace. Paul learned to be content in whatsoever state he was. Today, if you are tempted to run from a difficult situation, ask God whether He wants you to stay. Sometimes the blessing is found not in escaping but in faithfully remaining where He has placed you.
S: Isaac obeyed God and stayed in Gerar instead of going to Egypt. Staying in a hard place can require more faith than leaving. Ask God whether He wants you to stay where you are and trust Him to provide.
P: Sometimes faith means staying. Genesis 26:6: 'And Isaac dwelt in Gerar.' He trusted God to provide in the famine. Blessing may be where you are today.

== 26:7
T: Repeating a father's sin
R: 'And the men of the place asked him of his wife; and he said, She is my sister: for he feared to say, She is my wife; lest, said he, the men of the place should kill me for Rebekah.' Isaac repeated the exact sin of his father. He lied about his wife, calling her his sister out of fear. Unlike Abraham, Isaac could not even claim a half-truth. Rebekah was his cousin's daughter, not his sister. Children often inherit their parents' weaknesses as well as their strengths. Isaac had learned more from Abraham's fear than from his faith in this moment. Patterns of fear, deception, anger, or addiction can pass from parent to child. But they do not have to continue. In Christ, generational patterns can be broken. Ezekiel declares that each person is responsible for their own sin and can turn to God. Today, identify any patterns you may have inherited from your family that do not honor God. Bring them to Christ. He can break the chains of the past.
S: Isaac lied about Rebekah, calling her his sister out of fear, just as his father had done. Children often inherit their parents' weaknesses as well as strengths. Bring inherited patterns of sin to Christ, who can break them.

== 26:8
T: The truth comes out
R: 'And it came to pass, when he had been there a long time, that Abimelech king of the Philistines looked out at a window, and saw, and, behold, Isaac was sporting with Rebekah his wife.' Isaac's deception lasted a long time, but eventually the truth came out. Abimelech saw Isaac being affectionate with Rebekah in a way that made it clear she was his wife, not his sister. Lies are hard to maintain. Over time, the truth tends to surface. Isaac could not hide his real relationship forever. His love for his wife revealed the truth. Numbers says, be sure your sin will find you out. Jesus said, there is nothing covered, that shall not be revealed. Living a lie is exhausting. It requires constant vigilance. And eventually, something slips. Today, if you are living with a deception, consider bringing it into the light before it comes out on its own. Honesty may be uncomfortable, but it is far better than being caught.
S: After a long time, Abimelech saw Isaac being affectionate with Rebekah and realized she was his wife. Lies are hard to maintain, and the truth tends to surface. Bring any deception into the light before it comes out on its own.

== 26:9
T: Confronted with the truth
R: 'And Abimelech called Isaac, and said, Behold, of a surety she is thy wife: and how saidst thou, She is my sister? And Isaac said unto him, Because I said, Lest I die for her.' Abimelech confronted Isaac directly. Isaac admitted his fear: I thought I would die because of her. At least Isaac was honest when confronted. He did not invent another lie. Admitting the truth, even when caught, is a step toward integrity. Fear drives many of our worst decisions. Isaac's fear of death led him to lie and put his wife at risk. But his fear was unfounded. Abimelech was a reasonable man. Isaac had underestimated him, just as Abraham had. Fear often exaggerates dangers and shrinks our view of God. Psalm 34 says, I sought the LORD, and he heard me, and delivered me from all my fears. Today, examine the fears driving your decisions. Are they realistic? Bring them to God and let Him deliver you from them.
S: Abimelech confronted Isaac, and Isaac admitted he had lied because he feared for his life. Fear exaggerated the danger and led him into deception. Bring your fears to God today and let Him deliver you from them.

== 26:10
T: The ripple effects of a lie
R: 'And Abimelech said, What is this thou hast done unto us? one of the people might lightly have lien with thy wife, and thou shouldest have brought guiltiness upon us.' Abimelech pointed out the danger Isaac's lie had created. Someone could have taken Rebekah, bringing guilt on the whole community. Isaac's deception put others at risk of sinning unknowingly. Our sins rarely affect only ourselves. They create situations where others can stumble. Isaac's lie had the potential to bring guilt on an entire people. This is why honesty matters so much. When we deceive, we distort reality for others and can lead them into wrong without their knowledge. Romans 14 urges us not to put a stumblingblock or an occasion to fall in our brother's way. Our choices should help others walk rightly, not trip them up. Today, consider how your words and actions affect others. Are you creating clarity or confusion? Let your honesty help others make good choices.
S: Abimelech said that Isaac's lie could have led someone to sin and brought guilt on the whole people. Our deceptions can cause others to stumble without knowing it. Let your honesty create clarity that helps others walk rightly.

== 26:11

R: 'And Abimelech charged all his people, saying, He that toucheth this man or his wife shall surely be put to death.' Instead of punishing Isaac, Abimelech protected him. He issued a decree that anyone who harmed Isaac or Rebekah would die. The very danger Isaac feared, death because of his wife, was now prevented by royal command. God protected Isaac despite his failure. Isaac had tried to protect himself through deception, and it failed. Then God protected him through the very king he feared. God's protection does not depend on our cleverness. Psalm 105 says of the patriarchs, He suffered no man to do them wrong: yea, he reproved kings for their sakes; Saying, Touch not mine anointed. God watches over His people even when they stumble. Today, thank God for protecting you in ways you did not deserve. And let His faithfulness lead you to trust Him rather than relying on your own schemes.

This was an extraordinary harvest, especially during a time of scarcity. The reason is clear: the LORD blessed him. Isaac's obedience in staying in the land was rewarded with abundance. When others were struggling, God provided miraculously for Isaac. God can bless us even in difficult economic times. His provision is not limited by circumstances. Isaac sowed in faith, planting seed during a famine when it seemed foolish. And God multiplied it. Jesus spoke of seed that fell on good ground and brought forth a hundredfold. Paul writes, he which soweth bountifully shall reap also bountifully. Today, sow in faith, even in difficult times. Whether it is time, money, love, or service, trust God to multiply what you plant. == 26:13

R: 'And the man waxed great, and went forward, and grew until he became very great.' Isaac's prosperity grew and grew. Scripture uses three phrases: he waxed great, he went forward, and he grew until he became very great. His growth was steady and continuous. God's blessing often works this way, not in one sudden burst but in steady increase over time. Proverbs says, the path of the just is as the shining light, that shineth more and more unto the perfect day. Spiritual growth is similar. We do not become mature overnight. We grow steadily as we walk with God, day by day. Peter urges believers to grow in grace, and in the knowledge of our Lord and Saviour Jesus Christ. Growth requires patience. Today, do not be discouraged if your progress seems slow. God is growing you steadily. Keep walking with Him, and trust that He is bringing you forward step by step.

== 26:14
T: When blessing brings envy
R: 'For he had possession of flocks, and possession of herds, and great store of servants: and the Philistines envied him.' Isaac's success attracted envy. The Philistines saw his flocks, herds, and servants, and they resented him. Blessing does not always bring admiration; sometimes it brings jealousy and hostility. Envy is a destructive emotion. It cannot rejoice in others' success. Proverbs says, envy is the rottenness of the bones. James writes, where envying and strife is, there is confusion and every evil work. The Philistines' envy would soon turn into action against Isaac. When God blesses us, we should be prepared for some to respond with envy. We cannot control their reactions, but we can respond with grace. We should also guard our own hearts against envy toward others. Today, if you notice envy in your heart toward someone else's blessings, confess it. Ask God to help you rejoice with those who rejoice. And if others envy you, respond with kindness and humility.
S: Isaac's great possessions caused the Philistines to envy him. Blessing can provoke jealousy and hostility in others. Guard your heart against envy and respond with grace when others envy you.

== 26:15
T: Stopped wells
R: 'For all the wells which his father's servants had digged in the days of Abraham his father, the Philistines had stopped them, and filled them with earth.' The Philistines had filled Abraham's wells with dirt. In a dry land, wells were lifelines. Stopping them was an act of hostility, cutting off access to water. Envy had turned into sabotage. Spiritually, there are forces that try to stop up our wells, the sources of refreshment and life God has given us. Busyness, distraction, sin, and discouragement can fill our wells with earth until we can no longer draw living water. Jesus offered living water that springs up into everlasting life. Prayer, Scripture, worship, and fellowship are wells through which God refreshes us. When these become clogged, our spiritual life dries up. Today, check your wells. Are they flowing freely, or have they been stopped up? Identify what is blocking your access to God's refreshment, and begin to clear it away.
S: The Philistines had stopped up all the wells Abraham's servants had dug, filling them with earth. Spiritual wells like prayer and Scripture can also become clogged by sin and distraction. Check your spiritual wells today and clear away what blocks God's refreshment.

Despite Isaac's peaceful presence, he was pushed out. Sometimes God's blessing on our lives makes others uncomfortable. They may push us away, not because we have done wrong, but because our success threatens them. Jesus experienced this. Being asked to leave is painful. But Isaac's response, as we will see, was gracious. He did not fight. He moved on. Today, if you are facing rejection because of God's blessing on your life, do not respond with bitterness. Trust that God will lead you to a new place, and continue walking in grace.

Paul asked the Corinthians, Why do ye not rather take wrong? why do ye not rather suffer yourselves to be defrauded? Sometimes letting go is the path to peace. Isaac had been through repeated disputes over wells. Now he went to a place associated with his father's worship. Going up suggests a movement toward God, a spiritual ascent. The psalms of ascent were sung by pilgrims going up to Jerusalem to worship. Psalm 121 begins, I will lift up mine eyes unto the hills, from whence cometh my help.
