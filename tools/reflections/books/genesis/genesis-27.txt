# Genesis 27: Jacob takes the blessing

== 27:1
T: Dim eyes
R: 'And it came to pass, that when Isaac was old, and his eyes were dim, so that he could not see, he called Esau his eldest son… and he said unto him, Behold, here am I.' Isaac was old and nearly blind. His physical blindness becomes a picture of something deeper in this chapter. Isaac seems to have been spiritually unclear as well. God had said the elder would serve the younger, yet Isaac planned to give the blessing to Esau, his favorite. Favoritism can blind us to God's will. Isaac loved Esau's venison and wanted to bless him, regardless of what God had revealed. Esau answered, Here am I, the same words Abraham had spoken to God. But here they were spoken to a father who was about to act against God's word. Today, ask God to open your eyes. Are there areas where your preferences have dimmed your understanding of His will? Let Him give you clear spiritual sight.
S: Isaac was old and his eyes were dim when he called Esau to bless him. His favoritism also seems to have dimmed his understanding of God's revealed will. Ask God today to open your eyes where preferences have clouded your sight.
P: Don't let preferences blind you. Genesis 27:1: 'when Isaac was old, and his eyes were dim, so that he could not see.' Ask God to give you clear spiritual sight today.

== 27:2
T: Not knowing the day
R: 'And he said, Behold now, I am old, I know not the day of my death.' Isaac recognized his mortality. He did not know when he would die. In fact, Isaac would live many more years, perhaps another forty. But he felt the urgency of putting his affairs in order. None of us knows the day of our death. James writes, Whereas ye know not what shall be on the morrow. For what is your life? It is even a vapour, that appeareth for a little time, and then vanisheth away. This uncertainty should not make us anxious but wise. It should lead us to live each day with purpose and to settle important matters while we can. Have we said what needs to be said to those we love? Have we made peace where peace is needed? Most importantly, are we ready to meet God? Today, live with the awareness that your days are in God's hands. Do not put off what matters most.
S: Isaac said he was old and did not know the day of his death. None of us knows when our life will end, and this should make us wise rather than anxious. Settle what matters most today and live ready to meet God.
P: You don't know the day. Genesis 27:2: 'I am old, I know not the day of my death.' Life is a vapour. Don't put off what matters most today.

== 27:3
T: A request shaped by appetite
R: 'Now therefore take, I pray thee, thy weapons, thy quiver and thy bow, and go out to the field, and take me some venison.' Isaac asked Esau to go hunting and bring him venison. It was a simple request, but it reveals what was driving Isaac. Scripture told us earlier that Isaac loved Esau because he did eat of his venison. Now, before giving the most important blessing of his life, Isaac wanted his favorite meal. His appetite seemed to be shaping a spiritual decision. It is easy to let personal preferences and comforts influence decisions that should be guided by God. Isaac was not seeking God about the blessing. He was planning a meal. The danger of mixing our desires with spiritual matters is real. Romans 8 warns that to be carnally minded is death. Today, examine whether your appetites or comforts are influencing decisions that should be guided by God's word. Seek His will above your preferences.
S: Isaac asked Esau to hunt venison for him before he gave the blessing. His love of Esau's venison seemed to be shaping a spiritual decision. Seek God's will above your personal preferences and comforts today.
P: Don't let appetite guide spiritual decisions. Genesis 27:3: 'go out to the field, and take me some venison.' Isaac's favorite meal came before seeking God. Seek Him first today.

== 27:4
T: The weight of a blessing
R: 'And make me savoury meat, such as I love, and bring it to me, that I may eat; that my soul may bless thee before I die.' Isaac intended to give Esau his deathbed blessing. In that culture, a father's final blessing carried enormous weight. It was seen as prophetic and binding. Isaac took it seriously, even if he was misguided about whom to bless. The words that parents and elders speak over children carry power. A blessing can shape identity and direction for a lifetime. Words of encouragement can build up; words of criticism can wound for years. Proverbs says, Death and life are in the power of the tongue. Every parent, grandparent, mentor, and teacher has opportunities to speak blessing. Jesus took children in His arms and blessed them. Today, think about the people you have influence over. Speak words of blessing to them. Tell them what you see in them and what you hope for them. Your words can shape their future.
S: Isaac planned to give Esau a weighty blessing before he died. A parent's words of blessing can shape a child's identity and direction for a lifetime. Speak words of blessing today to those you influence.
P: Your words carry weight. Genesis 27:4: 'that my soul may bless thee before I die.' A blessing can shape a lifetime. Speak blessing over someone today.

== 27:5
T: Overhearing and planning
R: 'And Rebekah heard when Isaac spake to Esau his son. And Esau went to the field to hunt for venison, and to bring it.' Rebekah was listening. She overheard Isaac's plan and immediately began to form her own. She knew God had said the elder would serve the younger. She believed Jacob should receive the blessing. But instead of talking with Isaac or praying, she would choose deception. Rebekah had the right goal but the wrong method. She wanted God's will to happen, but she did not trust God to bring it about His way. We know what God has promised, and we try to make it happen through our own schemes. But God does not need our manipulation. Proverbs says, There are many devices in a man's heart; nevertheless the counsel of the LORD, that shall stand. Today, if you are tempted to manipulate a situation to achieve what you believe is God's will, stop. Trust Him to work it out His way.
S: Rebekah overheard Isaac's plan and began forming her own. She had the right goal but chose deception instead of trusting God. Do not manipulate situations to achieve God's will; trust Him to work it out His way.

== 27:6
T: A divided household
R: 'And Rebekah spake unto Jacob her son, saying, Behold, I heard thy father speak unto Esau thy brother.' Rebekah went to her favorite son. Notice how she described the family: thy father, thy brother. The family was divided into camps. Isaac and Esau on one side, Rebekah and Jacob on the other. Favoritism had split the household. Instead of husband and wife working together, they were working against each other. A family divided by favoritism and secrecy is a breeding ground for conflict. Jesus said, if a house be divided against itself, that house cannot stand. Isaac and Rebekah's marriage had started with such promise. Isaac loved her, and she comforted him. But somewhere along the way, they stopped communicating and started competing. Today, if you are married, examine whether you and your spouse are working together or apart. If there is division in your family, ask God to bring unity. Open communication and shared purpose can heal divided homes.
S: Rebekah went to Jacob and spoke of Isaac and Esau as thy father and thy brother. Favoritism and secrecy had divided the household into competing camps. Ask God to bring unity and open communication to your family today.
P: A house divided cannot stand. Genesis 27:6: 'Rebekah spake unto Jacob her son.' Favoritism had split the family. Pray for unity in your home today.

== 27:7
T: Before the LORD
R: Rebekah reported Isaac's words: 'Bring me venison, and make me savoury meat, that I may eat, and bless thee before the LORD before my death.' Rebekah added a phrase Isaac had not used: before the LORD. Perhaps she wanted to emphasize the seriousness of the blessing. It would be given in God's presence and carry divine weight. Ironically, the plan she was about to propose would involve deceiving her husband in the very presence of the LORD. Everything we do is before the LORD. There is no hidden corner where God does not see. Proverbs says, The eyes of the LORD are in every place, beholding the evil and the good. Rebekah recognized the spiritual weight of the blessing but did not apply that awareness to her own actions. We can do the same, acknowledging God's presence in theory while acting as if He is not watching. Today, live as if every action is before the LORD, because it is. Let that awareness shape your choices.
S: Rebekah emphasized that the blessing would be given before the LORD. Yet she was about to plan a deception in that same presence. Live today aware that every action is done before the LORD.

== 27:8
T: Obeying the wrong voice
R: 'Now therefore, my son, obey my voice according to that which I command thee.' Rebekah told Jacob to obey her voice. Children are rightly taught to obey their parents. But what happens when a parent commands something wrong? Jacob faced that dilemma. His mother was asking him to deceive his father. Obedience to human authority has limits. When human commands contradict God's ways, we must obey God. Peter and the apostles said, We ought to obey God rather than men. Jacob would have done well to say, Mother, I cannot deceive my father. But he went along with the plan. Many people today face pressure from authority figures, employers, family members, or friends, to do something wrong. The pressure can be intense. But the principle remains: God's voice comes first. Today, if someone in authority is pressuring you to compromise, respectfully decline. Choose to obey God's voice above every other voice. He will honor your faithfulness.
S: Rebekah told Jacob to obey her voice and follow her command. When human authority asks us to do wrong, we must obey God first. Respectfully decline any pressure to compromise and choose God's voice above every other.

== 27:9
T: A carefully planned deception
R: 'Go now to the flock, and fetch me from thence two good kids of the goats; and I will make them savoury meat for thy father, such as he loveth.' Rebekah had a detailed plan. She knew exactly what Isaac liked and how to prepare goat meat to taste like venison. Her knowledge of her husband became a tool for deceiving him. Intimacy can be used for good or for harm. Those who know us best can bless us deeply or hurt us deeply. Rebekah used her intimate knowledge of Isaac to manipulate him. This is a betrayal of trust within marriage. Spouses are meant to use their knowledge of each other to serve, protect, and love, not to deceive. Ephesians calls husbands and wives to love and respect one another, as Christ loves the church. Today, think about how you use your knowledge of those closest to you. Do you use it to bless and serve them, or to manipulate? Let intimacy be a channel of love, never of deception.
S: Rebekah planned to prepare goat meat to taste like the venison Isaac loved. She used her intimate knowledge of her husband to deceive him. Use your knowledge of those closest to you to bless and serve them, never to manipulate.
P: Use intimacy to bless, not deceive. Genesis 27:9: 'I will make them savoury meat for thy father, such as he loveth.' Love those closest to you with honesty today.

== 27:10
T: The ends do not justify the means
R: 'And thou shalt bring it to thy father, that he may eat, and that he may bless thee before his death.' Rebekah's goal was clear: Jacob would receive the blessing. And in a sense, she was right. God had said the elder would serve the younger. But the means she chose were deceptive. The ends do not justify the means. God's purposes are never served by sin. Paul addresses this directly in Romans 3, rejecting the idea of doing evil that good may come. God did not need Rebekah's deception to fulfill His promise. He could have worked through Isaac's heart, through circumstances, or through Esau's own choices. Rebekah's scheme would bring consequences: Jacob would have to flee, and she would never see him again. When we try to accomplish God's will through sinful means, we often reap pain. Today, examine whether you are using questionable methods to pursue a good goal. Trust God to accomplish His purposes in His way, through righteous means.
S: Rebekah planned for Jacob to receive the blessing through deception. Even good goals do not justify sinful means, and her scheme brought painful consequences. Trust God to accomplish His purposes through righteous means.
P: The ends don't justify the means. Genesis 27:10: 'that he may bless thee before his death.' Rebekah's goal was right but her method wrong. Trust God's way today.

== 27:11
T: Worried about getting caught
R: 'And Jacob said to Rebekah his mother, Behold, Esau my brother is a hairy man, and I am a smooth man.' Jacob raised an objection. But notice what kind of objection it was. He did not say, This is wrong. He said, This might not work. His concern was practical, not moral. He worried about being caught, not about deceiving his father. This reveals much about Jacob's character at this stage. He was more concerned with consequences than with right and wrong. Many people operate this way. They avoid wrongdoing only when they fear getting caught. But true integrity means doing right even when no one would ever know. Joseph refused Potiphar's wife, saying, how then can I do this great wickedness, and sin against God? His concern was God, not getting caught. Today, examine your motivations. Do you avoid sin because it is wrong, or only because you fear consequences? Ask God to give you a heart that loves righteousness for its own sake.
S: Jacob objected that his smooth skin might give him away, but his concern was getting caught, not doing wrong. True integrity means doing right even when no one would know. Ask God for a heart that loves righteousness for its own sake.

== 27:12
T: Fearing a curse
R: 'My father peradventure will feel me, and I shall seem to him as a deceiver; and I shall bring a curse upon me, and not a blessing.' Jacob's fear went deeper. If caught, he would seem to be a deceiver, which he would be. And he might receive a curse instead of a blessing. Jacob recognized the danger, but again his focus was on outcome, not on righteousness. Yet there was a flicker of conscience here. He used the word deceiver. Somewhere, Jacob knew this was wrong. Our consciences often warn us before we sin. That inner hesitation is a gift from God. When we feel uneasy about a decision, it may be the Holy Spirit warning us. Romans 2 speaks of the conscience bearing witness, accusing or excusing. Jacob felt the warning but did not heed it. Today, pay attention to your conscience. If you feel uneasy about a choice, stop and seek God. Do not override the inner warning. It may save you from much pain.
S: Jacob feared his father would see him as a deceiver and curse him instead of blessing him. His conscience warned him, but he did not heed it. Pay attention to your conscience today and stop when it warns you.

== 27:13
T: Upon me be thy curse
R: 'And his mother said unto him, Upon me be thy curse, my son: only obey my voice, and go fetch me them.' Rebekah dismissed Jacob's concern. Let the curse fall on me, she said. She was willing to take the consequences. It sounds sacrificial, but it was reckless. No one can truly absorb the consequences of another person's sin by declaring it. Jacob would still bear the consequences of his own deception. And Rebekah would bear hers: she would never see her beloved son again. Rebekah's words echo in a strange way the words of a crowd centuries later: His blood be on us, and on our children. Rash words that invite curses rarely end well. Only one person could truly take a curse upon Himself for others. Galatians says, Christ hath redeemed us from the curse of the law, being made a curse for us. Today, be careful with rash promises and reckless words. And thank Jesus, who truly took the curse we deserved upon Himself.
S: Rebekah told Jacob to let any curse fall on her and just obey her voice. Her rash words could not truly absorb the consequences of his sin. Thank Jesus today, who truly took our curse upon Himself.

== 27:14
T: Going along
R: 'And he went, and fetched, and brought them to his mother: and his mother made savoury meat, such as his father loved.' Jacob went along with the plan. He fetched the goats, and Rebekah prepared the meal. Three verbs mark Jacob's compliance: he went, fetched, and brought. Once he silenced his conscience, the actions followed easily. Sin often begins with a decision to go along. We silence our doubts and follow the crowd, or the persuasive voice, or our own desires. Each step makes the next one easier. Psalm 1 describes the progression: walking, standing, sitting in the way of sinners. Jacob had walked into the plan; now he was fully participating. It is far easier to stop at the beginning than to turn back halfway. Today, if you sense you are being drawn into something wrong, stop at the first step. Do not go, fetch, or bring. Say no early, while it is still easy to turn around.
S: Jacob went, fetched the goats and brought them to his mother, fully joining the plan. Once his conscience was silenced, each step came more easily. Stop at the first step when you are drawn toward something wrong.

== 27:15
T: Wearing another's clothes
R: 'And Rebekah took goodly raiment of her eldest son Esau, which were with her in the house, and put them upon Jacob her younger son.' Rebekah dressed Jacob in Esau's best clothes. Jacob was literally pretending to be someone else. He wore his brother's identity like a costume. There is something sad about this picture. Jacob, the chosen son, was trying to receive blessing by pretending to be someone he was not. Yet there is also a gospel echo here. We cannot come to God the Father in our own clothing. Our own righteousness is like filthy rags. But in Christ, we are clothed in His righteousness. Galatians says, as many of you as have been baptized into Christ have put on Christ. The difference is that Jacob's disguise was a deception, while our covering in Christ is a gift of grace, freely given. We do not trick God; He clothes us Himself. Today, rejoice that you do not need to pretend before God. He has clothed you in Christ's righteousness.

== 27:16
T: Covering the truth
R: 'And she put the skins of the kids of the goats upon his hands, and upon the smooth of his neck.' Rebekah covered Jacob's smooth skin with goatskins to imitate Esau's hairy arms and neck. Every detail of the deception was carefully planned. The lengths people will go to deceive can be remarkable. Rebekah thought of everything. But elaborate deceptions often have a way of unraveling, and even when they succeed, they bring bitter fruit. There is irony in the goat skins. Years later, Jacob's own sons would deceive him using a goat. They dipped Joseph's coat in goat's blood to make Jacob believe his son was dead. What Jacob sowed with goatskins, he would reap with a goat's blood. Galatians says, whatsoever a man soweth, that shall he also reap. Today, consider whether any deception in your life might bear bitter fruit later. Choose honesty now. The seeds you sow today will produce a harvest tomorrow.

== 27:19
T: The direct lie

Now Jacob lied directly. I am Esau thy firstborn. There was no ambiguity, no half-truth. It was a flat lie. He also claimed to have done what Isaac asked, another lie. One deception required another. Lies multiply. Once we start deceiving, we often need more lies to support the first. Jacob's lie was especially painful because it was told to his blind, elderly father who trusted him. Taking advantage of someone's vulnerability adds to the wrong. Leviticus says, Thou shalt not curse the deaf, nor put a stumblingblock before the blind. Jacob was exploiting his father's blindness. Today, examine your words. Are you speaking truthfully, especially to those who trust you or are vulnerable? Commit to honesty, even when a lie might seem to bring advantage.

== 27:23
T: Deception succeeds, for now
R: 'And he discerned him not, because his hands were hairy, as his brother Esau's hands: so he blessed him.' The deception worked. Isaac did not recognize Jacob and began to bless him. Sometimes deception succeeds in the short term. Jacob got what he wanted. But short-term success through deception often leads to long-term pain. Jacob would spend twenty years in exile, deceived repeatedly by his uncle Laban. He would live in fear of his brother's revenge. He would later be deceived by his own sons. The blessing he stole by deception came at a great cost. Proverbs says, Bread of deceit is sweet to a man; but afterwards his mouth shall be filled with gravel. What tastes sweet at first can turn bitter. Today, do not be fooled by the short-term success of deception, whether yours or others'. God sees the long game. Choose integrity, even if it seems to cost you now. In the end, honesty is always the better path.

First Corinthians says the Lord will bring to light the hidden things of darkness, and will make manifest the counsels of the hearts. Today, examine your motives honestly. Ask God to purify your heart so that your reasons and your words are fully truthful before Him and others.
