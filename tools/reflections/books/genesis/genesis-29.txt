# Genesis 29: Jacob, Rachel and Leah

== 29:1
T: Walking on with new strength
R: 'Then Jacob went on his journey, and came into the land of the people of the east.' After his encounter with God at Bethel, Jacob continued his journey. The Hebrew literally says Jacob lifted up his feet, suggesting he went on with renewed energy. Something had changed. He was still a fugitive, but now he walked with God's promise ringing in his ears. Encounters with God give us strength for the road ahead. Jacob's circumstances had not changed. He was still alone, still far from home, still uncertain about the future. But he knew God was with him. Isaiah says, they that wait upon the LORD shall renew their strength… they shall run, and not be weary; and they shall walk, and not faint. Today, let your encounters with God give you fresh energy for your journey. Your circumstances may be the same, but God's presence changes how you walk through them. Lift up your feet and go on.
S: After Bethel, Jacob continued his journey with renewed energy. His circumstances had not changed, but he now walked with God's promise. Let your encounters with God give you fresh strength for your journey today.

== 29:2
T: Arriving at the well
R: 'And he looked, and behold a well in the field, and, lo, there were three flocks of sheep lying by it… and a great stone was upon the well's mouth.' Jacob came to a well. Once again in Genesis, a well becomes the place of a significant meeting. Abraham's servant found Rebekah at a well. Now Jacob would find Rachel at a well. God often uses ordinary places to bring about extraordinary meetings. A great stone covered the well's mouth, likely to protect the water. The shepherds were waiting until all the flocks gathered before moving it. Wells were gathering places, centers of community life. They were where people met, shared news, and found refreshment. Jesus met the Samaritan woman at a well and offered her living water. Today, look for the wells in your life, the places where people gather and where God might bring meaningful encounters. Be open to the divine appointments He arranges in ordinary places.
S: Jacob came to a well in the field where flocks were waiting and a great stone covered its mouth. Wells were gathering places where God often arranged significant meetings. Be open today to the divine appointments God arranges in ordinary places.
P: God arranges divine appointments. Genesis 29:2: 'behold a well in the field.' Wells were places of meaningful meetings. Watch for God's appointments today.

== 29:3
T: Working together
R: 'And thither were all the flocks gathered: and they rolled the stone from the well's mouth, and watered the sheep, and put the stone again upon the well's mouth in his place.' The shepherds had a system. They waited until all the flocks gathered, then together they rolled away the heavy stone, watered the sheep, and replaced the stone. Cooperation was necessary because the stone was too heavy for one person. Community requires cooperation. Many tasks in life are too heavy for one person alone. Families, churches, and neighborhoods function best when people work together. Ecclesiastes says, Two are better than one; because they have a good reward for their labour. The body of Christ is designed for cooperation. Paul writes that every part is needed. No one can do everything alone. Today, consider where you might need help or where you could offer help. Do not try to roll heavy stones by yourself. Work together with others in your community and church.
S: The shepherds gathered all the flocks and worked together to roll the heavy stone from the well. Many tasks in life are too heavy for one person alone. Work together with others today, offering and receiving help.

== 29:4
T: Friendly questions
R: 'And Jacob said unto them, My brethren, whence be ye? And they said, Of Haran are we.' Jacob greeted the shepherds warmly, calling them my brethren. He asked where they were from, and they answered Haran. Jacob had arrived at his destination. His friendly approach opened the door to conversation. Kindness and courtesy toward strangers often lead to helpful connections. Jacob did not know these men, but he treated them as brothers. This simple act of friendliness would lead him to the information he needed. Proverbs says, A man that hath friends must shew himself friendly. Our openness to others can open doors we never expected. Many of God's blessings come through people. We find jobs, homes, friends, and opportunities through relationships. Being friendly and approachable is not just good manners; it can be a channel of God's provision. Today, greet the people you meet with warmth. Treat strangers as potential brothers and sisters. You never know what doors your kindness may open.

== 29:5
T: Do you know Laban?
R: 'And he said unto them, Know ye Laban the son of Nahor? And they said, We know him.' Jacob asked about Laban, his mother's brother. The shepherds knew him. Jacob was in the right place. God had guided his steps across many miles to exactly where he needed to be. This is the providence of God at work. Jacob did not have a map or a GPS. He simply traveled in the general direction of Haran. Yet God led him to the very well where Laban's family watered their flocks. Proverbs says, A man's heart deviseth his way: but the LORD directeth his steps. God's guidance is often quiet and unnoticed until we look back and see how precisely He led. Today, trust that God is directing your steps, even when you cannot see the full path. He knows exactly where you need to be. Keep walking in faith, and He will bring you to the right place at the right time.

== 29:6

R: 'And he said unto them, Is he well? And they said, He is well: and, behold, Rachel his daughter cometh with the sheep.' Jacob asked how Laban was doing. And just as the shepherds answered, Rachel appeared with her father's sheep. The timing was perfect. Just as with Abraham's servant, who saw Rebekah before he finished praying, Jacob saw Rachel at the very moment he arrived. God's timing is precise. He orchestrates details we could never arrange ourselves. Rachel's arrival was not a coincidence. It was a divine appointment. == 29:7
T: Questioning the routine
R: 'And he said, Lo, it is yet high day, neither is it time that the cattle should be gathered together: water ye the sheep, and go and feed them.' Jacob questioned the shepherds' routine. It was still the middle of the day. Why were they waiting instead of watering the sheep and taking them back to pasture? Jacob was an experienced shepherd and saw inefficiency. Sometimes we get stuck in routines without asking why. We do things the way they have always been done, even when there might be a better way. Jacob's question challenged the status quo. Fresh eyes can see what long habit hides. At the same time, there may be good reasons for routines that newcomers do not understand. The shepherds had their reasons, as the next verse shows. Wisdom involves both questioning and listening. Today, consider whether any of your routines need examination. Are you doing things out of habit or purpose? Be open to fresh perspectives, and listen to the reasons behind traditions.

== 29:8
T: Waiting for everyone
R: 'And they said, We cannot, until all the flocks be gathered together, and till they roll the stone from the well's mouth; then we water the sheep.' The shepherds explained: they could not water the sheep until all the flocks arrived and they could roll the stone together. It was a matter of cooperation and fairness. Everyone waited so that all could drink together. There is a principle here about community. Sometimes we must wait for others before moving forward. In families, churches, and teams, progress often depends on everyone being ready. Rushing ahead alone can leave others behind. Paul urged the Corinthians to wait for one another when gathering for the Lord's Supper. Patience with others is part of love. First Corinthians says, Charity suffereth long, and is kind. Today, consider whether you need to wait for others in some area of your life. Be patient with those who are slower. Moving forward together is often better than racing ahead alone.
S: The shepherds explained they had to wait until all the flocks gathered before rolling away the stone. Community sometimes requires waiting for others before moving forward. Be patient with others today and value moving forward together.

== 29:9

R: 'And while he yet spake with them, Rachel came with her father's sheep: for she kept them.' Rachel arrived with her father's sheep, for she kept them. She was a shepherdess, responsible for her family's flock. This was hard, physical work. Rachel was not idle; she was diligent and capable. Like Rebekah before her, she was introduced while doing her daily work. God often meets people and calls them in the midst of their work. David was keeping sheep when Samuel came to anoint him. Amos was a herdsman when God called him to prophesy. The disciples were fishing when Jesus called them. Faithful work in ordinary tasks is honored by God. Proverbs 31 praises the woman who works willingly with her hands. Today, do your daily work with diligence and faithfulness. Whether your work is glamorous or ordinary, God sees it. He may meet you, bless you, or call you right in the middle of it.

When Jacob saw Rachel, he did something remarkable. He rolled away the great stone by himself, a task that usually required several shepherds. Then he watered her flock. Love gave Jacob unusual strength. He wanted to serve her and show his care. This is a beautiful picture of love in action. Love moves us to serve, to sacrifice, and to do things we might not otherwise attempt. Jacob did not just admire Rachel from a distance. He acted. He served her practically. First John says, let us not love in word, neither in tongue; but in deed and in truth. Real love is shown through action. Today, think about someone you love. How can you serve them practically? == 29:11
T: Tears of relief
R: 'And Jacob kissed Rachel, and lifted up his voice, and wept.' Jacob kissed Rachel in greeting, as was the custom among relatives, and then he wept aloud. All the emotion of his long journey poured out. He had left home in fear, traveled alone for hundreds of miles, slept on stones, and now he had finally reached family. His tears were tears of relief, joy, and gratitude. Tears are not a sign of weakness. Joseph wept when he saw his brothers. Jesus wept at Lazarus's tomb. David wept freely. Tears express what words cannot. After long seasons of stress or loneliness, it is natural to weep when relief finally comes. Psalm 126 says, They that sow in tears shall reap in joy. Today, if you have been carrying a heavy burden, allow yourself to feel it. And when relief comes, let your tears flow freely. God collects every tear, and He turns mourning into joy.

Family relationships can be complicated, especially when work and money are involved. Mixing family and business requires extra care and honesty. == 29:15
T: Fair wages
R: 'And Laban said unto Jacob, Because thou art my brother, shouldest thou therefore serve me for nought? tell me, what shall thy wages be?' Laban offered to pay Jacob for his work. Just because they were relatives, Jacob should not work for free. On the surface, this was fair and generous. Workers deserve fair wages. Scripture affirms this repeatedly. Deuteronomy says, Thou shalt not muzzle the ox when he treadeth out the corn. Paul writes, The labourer is worthy of his reward. James condemns those who withhold wages from workers. Fair pay honors the dignity of work and the worker. Yet Laban's offer would later be used against Jacob. Laban would change his wages ten times, as Jacob later complained. Words of fairness can mask unfair intentions. Today, if you employ others, pay them fairly and keep your agreements. And if you work for others, do your work faithfully, trusting God to vindicate you if you are treated unjustly.

Today, if you feel overlooked because of your appearance or circumstances, remember Leah. God sees you. He values you. And He may have greater purposes for you than you can imagine. == 29:24

R: 'And Laban gave unto his daughter Leah Zilpah his maid for an handmaid.' Laban gave Leah a maidservant named Zilpah. This was a customary wedding gift.
