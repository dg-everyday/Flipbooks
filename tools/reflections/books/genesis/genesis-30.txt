# Genesis 30: Jacob's children and flocks

== 30:1
T: Envy and desperation
R: 'And when Rachel saw that she bare Jacob no children, Rachel envied her sister; and said unto Jacob, Give me children, or else I die.' Rachel had Jacob's love, but she wanted what Leah had: children. And she envied her sister. Each sister wanted what the other had. Leah wanted love; Rachel wanted children. Envy is never satisfied, because there is always something someone else has that we lack. Rachel's desperation spilled out in a demand: Give me children, or else I die. She placed an impossible burden on Jacob. Envy poisons relationships. It makes us blind to our own blessings and fixated on what others have. James writes, where envying and strife is, there is confusion and every evil work. The cure for envy is gratitude and trust in God. Today, if envy is stirring in your heart, name it before God. Then count your own blessings. Ask Him to help you rejoice in others' good and trust Him with your own longings.
S: Rachel envied her sister because she had no children and demanded that Jacob give her children. Envy blinds us to our own blessings and poisons relationships. Name any envy before God today and count the blessings you already have.
P: Envy is never satisfied. Genesis 30:1: 'Rachel envied her sister; and said unto Jacob, Give me children, or else I die.' Count your blessings and trust God today.

== 30:2
T: Not in God's place
R: 'And Jacob's anger was kindled against Rachel: and he said, Am I in God's stead, who hath withheld from thee the fruit of the womb?' Jacob responded angrily. Am I in God's place? He could not give Rachel children; only God could. His words were true, but his tone was harsh. Rachel needed compassion, and Jacob gave her anger. Contrast this with Isaac, who prayed for Rebekah when she was barren. Jacob did not pray; he got angry. There is truth in Jacob's words. No human being can take God's place. We cannot give what only God can give. But truth spoken without love can wound. Ephesians says, speaking the truth in love. Jacob had the truth but lacked the love. Today, when someone you love is hurting, respond with compassion rather than frustration. Pray for them and with them. Point them to God, but do it gently. And remember that you cannot be God for anyone; you can only lead them to Him.
S: Jacob angrily asked Rachel if he was in God's place, since only God could give her children. His words were true but lacked the compassion she needed. Respond to hurting loved ones with compassion and prayer, gently pointing them to God.
P: Truth needs love. Genesis 30:2: 'Am I in God's stead?' Jacob was right but harsh. Respond to hurting people with compassion and prayer today.

== 30:3
T: Repeating the old pattern
R: 'And she said, Behold my maid Bilhah, go in unto her; and she shall bear upon my knees that I may also have children by her.' Rachel proposed exactly what Sarah had done generations earlier: give her maid to Jacob so she could have children through her. The family pattern repeated. Sarah's plan with Hagar had brought conflict and pain. Now Rachel was walking the same path. Families often repeat patterns, even painful ones. What one generation does, the next often imitates. Rachel did not seem to learn from Sarah's experience. Breaking unhealthy family patterns requires awareness and God's help. We must recognize the patterns, understand where they lead, and choose a different path. Second Corinthians says, if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature. In Christ, we are not bound to repeat the mistakes of the past. Today, identify a family pattern you want to break. Ask God for the wisdom and strength to choose a better way.
S: Rachel proposed giving her maid Bilhah to Jacob, just as Sarah had done with Hagar. Families often repeat patterns, even painful ones. Ask God for wisdom and strength to break unhealthy family patterns.
P: Break the family pattern. Genesis 30:3: 'Behold my maid Bilhah, go in unto her.' Rachel repeated Sarah's mistake. In Christ, you can choose a better way.

== 30:4
T: Going along again
R: 'And she gave him Bilhah her handmaid to wife: and Jacob went in unto her.' Jacob went along with Rachel's plan, just as Abraham had gone along with Sarah's. No prayer is recorded. No seeking of God's guidance. Just compliance. The household became even more complicated. Now Jacob had three wives in practice. Each addition brought more potential for rivalry. Going along with others' plans without seeking God can lead to trouble. Jacob had seen what happened in his grandfather's household. Yet he did not resist. Leaders, especially, bear responsibility for decisions in their households. Passivity can be as damaging as wrong action. Joshua declared, as for me and my house, we will serve the LORD. He took responsibility for his family's direction. Today, if you are in a position of influence in your family or community, do not simply go along. Seek God's guidance before agreeing to plans. Lead with wisdom and prayer.
S: Jacob went along with Rachel's plan and took Bilhah as a wife, without any recorded prayer. Passivity in leadership can be as damaging as wrong action. Seek God's guidance before agreeing to plans that affect your household.
