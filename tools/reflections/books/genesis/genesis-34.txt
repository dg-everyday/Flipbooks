# Genesis 34: Dinah and the violence at Shechem

== 34:1
T: Going out into the world
R: 'And Dinah the daughter of Leah, which she bare unto Jacob, went out to see the daughters of the land.' Dinah, Jacob's daughter, went out to visit the young women of the region. It was a natural desire for a young woman to seek friendship. Yet this visit would lead to tragedy. This chapter is one of the darkest in Genesis. It reminds us that the world can be a dangerous place, especially for the vulnerable. Jacob had settled near Shechem, a Canaanite city, rather than continuing to Bethel as God had directed. Settling near a corrupt culture exposed his family to its dangers. Parents have a responsibility to protect their children and to think carefully about the environments they place them in. Yet we must be careful never to blame victims for what is done to them. The guilt in this story belongs to the one who committed the wrong. Today, pray for the safety of young people in your life. Ask God for wisdom about the environments and influences that shape them.
S: Dinah went out to visit the young women of the land, a natural desire that would lead to tragedy. The world can be dangerous, especially for the vulnerable. Pray today for the safety of the young people in your life and for wisdom to protect them.
P: Pray for the vulnerable. Genesis 34:1: 'Dinah… went out to see the daughters of the land.' The world can be dangerous. Pray for the safety of young people today.

== 34:2
T: A grievous wrong
R: 'And when Shechem the son of Hamor the Hivite, prince of the country, saw her, he took her, and lay with her, and defiled her.' Shechem, a prince of the land, saw Dinah and violated her. Scripture uses three verbs: he took her, lay with her, and defiled her. This was an act of violence and abuse of power. Shechem used his status to take what was not his. Scripture does not soften this. It names the wrong clearly. God sees every act of violence against the vulnerable. He is a God of justice who hears the cries of the oppressed. Psalm 10 says, Thou hast seen it; for thou beholdest mischief and spite… the poor committeth himself unto thee; thou art the helper of the fatherless. If you or someone you love has suffered abuse, know that God sees, and it was not your fault. He grieves with you and offers healing. Today, pray for survivors of abuse. Ask God to bring them healing, justice, and safe people to walk alongside them.
S: Shechem, a prince of the land, violated Dinah, and Scripture names the wrong clearly. God sees every act of violence against the vulnerable and is a God of justice. Pray today for survivors of abuse, that God would bring healing, justice and safe people around them.
P: God sees the wronged. Genesis 34:2 records the grievous wrong done to Dinah. God is a God of justice and healing. Pray for survivors of abuse today.

== 34:3
T: Desire is not love
R: 'And his soul clave unto Dinah the daughter of Jacob, and he loved the damsel, and spake kindly unto the damsel.' After his violent act, Shechem claimed to love Dinah and spoke tenderly to her. But true love does not begin with violation. Shechem's feelings did not erase what he had done. Love that disregards consent and dignity is not love; it is selfish desire. First Corinthians 13 describes true love: it is patient, kind, does not behave itself unseemly, seeketh not her own. Shechem had done the opposite. Kind words after harm do not undo the harm. Many victims of abuse hear tender words from those who hurt them. Those words can confuse and entrap. Scripture helps us see clearly: real love honors, protects, and respects. It never takes by force. Today, ask God to help you recognize the difference between true love and selfish desire. And pray for anyone caught in a relationship where harm is disguised as love.
S: After violating Dinah, Shechem claimed to love her and spoke kindly to her. But love that disregards dignity and consent is not love, and kind words do not undo harm. Pray for anyone caught in a relationship where harm is disguised as love.
P: Real love never takes by force. Genesis 34:3: 'he loved the damsel, and spake kindly unto the damsel.' Kind words don't erase harm. True love honors and protects.

== 34:4
T: Getting what he wants
R: 'And Shechem spake unto his father Hamor, saying, Get me this damsel to wife.' Shechem asked his father to arrange a marriage with Dinah. Get me this damsel. His words reflect entitlement. He wanted her, so he expected to get her. He never asked Dinah what she wanted. He never acknowledged his wrong. Entitlement treats people as objects to be acquired. It focuses on what I want, not on what is right or what the other person needs. This attitude is the opposite of the servant heart Jesus modeled. Philippians says, Look not every man on his own things, but every man also on the things of others. Shechem's demand reveals a heart unchanged by remorse. He wanted to secure what he had taken rather than make it right. Today, examine your heart for entitlement. Do you treat people as means to your ends? Ask God to replace entitlement with humility and genuine care for others.
S: Shechem told his father to get Dinah for him as a wife. His words reflected entitlement, treating her as something to acquire rather than a person to honor. Ask God to replace any entitlement in your heart with humility and genuine care for others.
P: People are not possessions. Genesis 34:4: 'Get me this damsel to wife.' Shechem acted from entitlement. Ask God to replace entitlement with care for others today.

== 34:5
T: A father's silence
R: 'And Jacob heard that he had defiled Dinah his daughter: now his sons were with his cattle in the field: and Jacob held his peace until they were come.' When Jacob heard what had happened to Dinah, he said nothing. He waited for his sons to return. His silence is troubling. Where was his grief? His outrage? His comfort for his daughter? Scripture records no words from Jacob to Dinah. Perhaps he was in shock. Perhaps he did not know what to do. But his passivity left a vacuum that his sons would fill with violence. Silence in the face of injustice can be harmful. Victims need their protectors to speak up. They need to know someone is on their side. Proverbs says, Open thy mouth for the dumb in the cause of all such as are appointed to destruction. Today, if someone in your care has been wronged, do not stay silent. Comfort them, stand with them, and seek justice through right means. Your voice matters.
S: When Jacob heard what had happened to Dinah, he held his peace until his sons came home. His silence left a vacuum that would be filled with violence. When someone in your care is wronged, do not stay silent; comfort them and seek justice rightly.

== 34:6
T: Negotiating instead of repenting
R: 'And Hamor the father of Shechem went out unto Jacob to commune with him.' Hamor came to negotiate with Jacob. Notice what is missing: any apology, any acknowledgment of wrong. Hamor came to make a deal, not to make amends. He treated the situation as a business transaction rather than a moral wrong. When people harm others, true repentance begins with admitting the wrong. Without that, any attempt at resolution is hollow. Hamor wanted to move past the offense without addressing it. Many people try to do this. They want reconciliation without repentance, peace without justice. But real healing requires honesty about what happened. Zacchaeus did not just offer a deal; he acknowledged his wrong. David confessed, I have sinned against the LORD. Today, if you have harmed someone, do not skip to negotiation. Begin with honest acknowledgment and sincere apology. Real restoration starts with repentance.
S: Hamor went out to negotiate with Jacob without any apology or acknowledgment of wrong. He sought resolution without repentance. If you have harmed someone, begin with honest acknowledgment and sincere apology.
P: Repentance comes before resolution. Genesis 34:6: 'Hamor the father of Shechem went out unto Jacob to commune with him.' No apology, just a deal. Real healing starts with honesty.

== 34:7
T: Righteous grief, dangerous anger
R: 'And the sons of Jacob came out of the field when they heard it: and the men were grieved, and they were very wroth, because he had wrought folly in Israel in lying with Jacob's daughter; which thing ought not to be done.' Dinah's brothers were grieved and furious. They rightly recognized that a terrible wrong had been done: a thing which ought not to be done. Their grief was appropriate. Their anger was understandable. Notice the phrase folly in Israel. This is the first time Jacob's family is called Israel as a people. The brothers saw this as an offense against their whole community. Righteous anger has its place. Injustice should provoke us. Yet anger can easily turn from righteous to destructive. Ephesians says, Be ye angry, and sin not. The brothers' anger was justified, but what they would do with it would become sin. Today, if injustice makes you angry, bring that anger to God. Let it motivate you to seek justice through right means, not revenge.
S: Dinah's brothers were grieved and very angry because a wrong had been done that ought not to be done. Their anger was justified, but it would soon turn destructive. Bring your anger at injustice to God and let it lead to right action, not revenge.

== 34:8

He asked the family to give her to Shechem as a wife. Still there was no apology, no recognition that Shechem had done wrong. Hamor focused only on what his son wanted. Some people try to justify wrong actions by pointing to strong feelings. He loves her, so it is acceptable. But feelings do not excuse wrongdoing. Strong desire is not a moral justification. Jeremiah says, The heart is deceitful above all things. Our feelings must be submitted to God's standards. Love that is real accepts accountability. Shechem's longing did not change the fact that he had violated Dinah. Today, be careful not to excuse wrong actions, in yourself or others, because of strong feelings. Submit your desires to God's truth and accept accountability for your choices.
S: Hamor told the brothers that Shechem longed for Dinah and asked them to give her to him. He offered feelings as a reason without any accountability for the wrong. Do not let strong feelings excuse wrong actions; accept accountability before God.

== 34:9
T: An offer to blend in
R: 'And make ye marriages with us, and give your daughters unto us, and take our daughters unto you.' Hamor proposed that the two peoples intermarry. Let our families become one. This would have meant Jacob's family blending into the Canaanite culture. Their distinct identity as God's covenant people would be lost. Abraham and Isaac had been careful to avoid Canaanite marriages. Now Hamor offered full integration. This was a subtle danger. God's people are called to be distinct, not absorbed into surrounding cultures. Not isolated, but distinct in values and faith. Second Corinthians says, Be ye not unequally yoked together with unbelievers… come out from among them, and be ye separate. The temptation to blend in is real. It can feel easier to adopt the culture around us than to remain faithful to God's ways. Today, consider whether you are being pressured to blend in with values that do not honor God. Remain distinct, while still loving those around you.
S: Hamor proposed that the two peoples intermarry and become one, which would have absorbed Jacob's family into Canaanite culture. God calls His people to remain distinct in faith and values. Remain faithful to God's ways today while still loving those around you.

== 34:10
T: Tempting offers
R: 'And ye shall dwell with us: and the land shall be before you; dwell and trade ye therein, and get you possessions therein.' Hamor sweetened the deal. You can live among us, trade freely, and acquire property. It was an attractive economic offer. Prosperity, security, and opportunity. But accepting would have meant compromising with those who had harmed Dinah and absorbing their way of life. Tempting offers often come at moments of vulnerability. The world offers comfort and success in exchange for compromise. Satan offered Jesus all the kingdoms of the world if He would bow down. Jesus refused. Some offers are too costly, even if they bring material gain. Mark asks, For what shall it profit a man, if he shall gain the whole world, and lose his own soul? Today, evaluate the offers in your life. Do any require you to compromise your faith or excuse wrongdoing? Material gain is never worth spiritual loss.

== 34:11
T: Seeking grace without repentance
R: 'And Shechem said unto her father and unto her brethren, Let me find grace in your eyes, and what ye shall say unto me I will give.' Shechem now spoke directly. Let me find grace in your eyes. Ironically, he used the same phrase Jacob had used when seeking reconciliation with Esau. But there was a crucial difference. Jacob came humbly, acknowledging his wrong. Shechem came offering payment without confession. Grace cannot be bought. It must be received with humility and repentance. Shechem thought he could pay his way to forgiveness. Many people approach God this way, thinking they can earn grace through good deeds or offerings. But Ephesians says, by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works. Grace is received by those who humbly admit their need, not by those who try to purchase it. Today, approach God not with payments but with honest repentance. Grace is a gift for the humble.
S: Shechem asked to find grace in the family's eyes and offered to give whatever they asked. He sought grace through payment rather than through repentance. Approach God with honest repentance, for grace is a gift for the humble, not something to buy.

Today, remember that people are not commodities. Honor the dignity of every person, especially the vulnerable. And if you have wronged someone, recognize that some things cannot be fixed with money. == 34:13
T: Answering deceitfully
R: 'And the sons of Jacob answered Shechem and Hamor his father deceitfully, and said, because he had defiled Dinah their sister.' Jacob's sons responded with deception. Scripture says plainly that they answered deceitfully. Their motive was revenge for what had been done to Dinah. The family pattern of deception continued. Jacob had deceived Isaac. Laban had deceived Jacob. Rachel had deceived Laban. Now Jacob's sons deceived Shechem. Deception was being passed down from generation to generation. The brothers had a legitimate grievance. Dinah had been wronged. But they chose a wrong method to address it. Two wrongs do not make a right. Romans 12 says, Recompense to no man evil for evil… avenge not yourselves… Vengeance is mine; I will repay, saith the Lord. Today, if you have been wronged, resist the temptation to respond with deception or revenge. Seek justice through honest means, and trust God to bring ultimate justice.
S: Jacob's sons answered Shechem and Hamor deceitfully because their sister had been wronged. Their grievance was real, but they chose deception and continued the family pattern. If you are wronged, seek justice through honest means and trust God with vengeance.

Deception that looks like an invitation is especially cruel. The brothers used what should have been a sign of covenant relationship with God as a weapon. People who are deceived in God's name may turn away from Him. Paul warns that God's name is blasphemed among the Gentiles because of hypocrisy. Today, let your words of invitation be sincere. Never use spiritual things to manipulate others.

Agree to circumcision, or we will take Dinah and leave. This revealed something important: Dinah was still in Shechem's house. She had not been returned to her family. Shechem was holding her. The brothers' ultimatum added pressure. They knew Shechem wanted Dinah badly enough to agree to almost anything. Ultimatums can be legitimate when protecting the vulnerable. Justice aims to right wrongs and protect the innocent. Vengeance aims to inflict pain. Micah says, what doth the LORD require of thee, but to do justly, and to love mercy, and to walk humbly with thy God? Today, when you seek to right a wrong, examine your motives. Seek justice, not vengeance.

Greed distorts judgment and leads to destruction. The men of Shechem agreed to a painful procedure because they wanted more possessions. Greed made the men of Shechem vulnerable. Today, examine whether greed is influencing your decisions. == 34:31
T: An unanswered question
R: 'And they said, Should he deal with our sister as with an harlot?' The chapter ends with the brothers' defiant question. Should he treat our sister like a prostitute? Their question was a defense of their actions. They felt justified because Dinah had been grievously wronged. And their outrage was not wrong. Dinah deserved to be defended. But their response went far beyond justice. The chapter ends without resolution. No one repents. No one comforts Dinah. The question hangs in the air. Scripture sometimes leaves us with uncomfortable endings. It shows us the brokenness of human responses to evil. Both the crime and the revenge were wrong. Only God can bring true justice and true healing. Isaiah promises a day when God will bind up the brokenhearted and comfort all that mourn. Today, remember that God sees what humans fail to address. He is the defender of the vulnerable and the healer of the wounded. Trust Him with injustices that remain unresolved.
