# Genesis 35: Return to Bethel

== 35:1
T: Go back to Bethel
R: 'And God said unto Jacob, Arise, go up to Bethel, and dwell there: and make there an altar unto God, that appeared unto thee when thou fleddest from the face of Esau thy brother.' After the tragedy at Shechem, God spoke to Jacob: go up to Bethel. Return to the place where you first met Me. Jacob had made a vow at Bethel years before, but he had settled near Shechem instead of going back. Now God called him to return. When life falls apart, God often calls us back to the beginning, to the place of our first encounter with Him. Revelation says to the church in Ephesus, Remember therefore from whence thou art fallen, and repent, and do the first works. Going back to Bethel meant renewing his commitment, fulfilling his vow, and worshiping God afresh. Today, if your spiritual life has drifted, hear God's call to return to Bethel. Go back to your first love. Renew your commitment to Him.
S: After the tragedy at Shechem, God told Jacob to go up to Bethel and build an altar there. God called him back to the place of his first encounter and his unfulfilled vow. If your spiritual life has drifted, hear God's call to return to your first love.
P: Go back to Bethel. Genesis 35:1: 'Arise, go up to Bethel, and dwell there: and make there an altar unto God.' Return to your first love today.

== 35:2
T: Put away strange gods
R: 'Then Jacob said unto his household, and to all that were with him, Put away the strange gods that are among you, and be clean, and change your garments.' Before going to Bethel, Jacob called his household to purify themselves. Put away the strange gods. Rachel's stolen idols, and perhaps others taken from Shechem, had to go. They needed to be clean and change their clothes, symbolizing a fresh start. Returning to God requires leaving behind what competes with Him. We cannot worship God while clinging to idols. Jacob finally led his household in spiritual renewal. This was leadership at its best: calling his family to holiness. Joshua later made the same call: put away the strange gods which are among you, and incline your heart unto the LORD God. Second Corinthians says, let us cleanse ourselves from all filthiness of the flesh and spirit. Today, identify anything in your life that competes with God for your devotion. Put it away. Prepare your heart to meet Him afresh.
S: Jacob told his household to put away their foreign gods, purify themselves and change their garments before going to Bethel. Returning to God requires leaving behind what competes with Him. Identify and put away anything that competes with God for your devotion today.
P: Put away your idols. Genesis 35:2: 'Put away the strange gods that are among you, and be clean.' Return to God with a cleansed heart today.

== 35:3
T: The God who answered
R: 'And let us arise, and go up to Bethel; and I will make there an altar unto God, who answered me in the day of my distress, and was with me in the way which I went.' Jacob explained why they were going: to build an altar to the God who answered him in his distress and was with him on his journey. Jacob's testimony was simple and powerful. God had answered when he was afraid. God had been with him every step. This is the testimony every believer can share. In our distress, God answers. On our journey, He goes with us. Psalm 120 says, In my distress I cried unto the LORD, and he heard me. Jacob was teaching his family that worship flows from remembering God's faithfulness. We worship because of what He has done. Today, remember the times God answered you in distress. Recall how He has been with you. Let those memories lead you to worship and to share your testimony with others.
S: Jacob told his family they would build an altar to the God who answered him in his distress and was with him on the way. Worship flows from remembering God's faithfulness. Remember how God has answered and accompanied you, and let it lead you to worship.
P: The God who answered me! Genesis 35:3: 'God, who answered me in the day of my distress, and was with me in the way which I went.' Remember His faithfulness and worship today.

== 35:4
T: Burying the idols
R: 'And they gave unto Jacob all the strange gods which were in their hand, and all their earrings which were in their ears; and Jacob hid them under the oak which was by Shechem.' The household responded. They gave Jacob their idols and even their earrings, which may have been associated with pagan worship. Jacob buried them under an oak tree near Shechem. The idols were left behind, buried in the place of their shame. True repentance involves decisive action. It is not enough to feel sorry; we must actually get rid of what pulls us away from God. Jacob's family handed over their idols. They did not keep any back. In Acts, new believers in Ephesus burned their books of magic, counting the cost at fifty thousand pieces of silver. Real change costs something. Today, take decisive action against anything that competes with God in your life. Do not just think about it. Hand it over. Bury it. Leave it behind as you go up to meet Him.
S: The household gave Jacob all their foreign gods and earrings, and he buried them under the oak near Shechem. True repentance involves decisive action to leave behind what competes with God. Take decisive action today against anything that pulls you away from Him.
P: Bury the idols. Genesis 35:4: 'Jacob hid them under the oak which was by Shechem.' Real repentance takes action. What do you need to leave behind today?

== 35:5
T: The terror of God protects
R: 'And they journeyed: and the terror of God was upon the cities that were round about them, and they did not pursue after the sons of Jacob.' As they traveled, the terror of God fell on the surrounding cities. No one pursued Jacob's family, even after the violence at Shechem. God protected them. Jacob had feared that the Canaanites would gather and destroy him. But when he obeyed God and purified his household, God's protection surrounded them. Obedience brings protection. When we walk in God's ways, He guards us. This does not mean believers never suffer, but God's hand is upon those who follow Him. Proverbs says, When a man's ways please the LORD, he maketh even his enemies to be at peace with him. Psalm 91 says, He that dwelleth in the secret place of the most High shall abide under the shadow of the Almighty. Today, walk in obedience, and trust God's protection. He is able to restrain those who would harm you.

== 35:6
T: Arriving at Bethel
R: 'So Jacob came to Luz, which is in the land of Canaan, that is, Bethel, he and all the people that were with him.' Jacob arrived at Bethel with all his people. He had finally returned to the place where God first met him. The fugitive who had slept on a stone was now the head of a large household. God had kept every promise. Arriving at Bethel was a fulfillment. Years earlier, Jacob had vowed, If God will be with me… and bring me again to my father's house in peace; then shall the LORD be my God. Now God had done it. Jacob's return to Bethel was a full circle moment. He could see God's faithfulness across all those years. Sometimes we need to return to significant places in our spiritual journey to see how far God has brought us. Today, reflect on where you started with God and how far He has brought you. Let your journey become a testimony of His faithfulness.
S: Jacob came to Bethel with all the people who were with him. The fugitive who once slept on a stone returned as the head of a large household, as God had promised. Reflect today on how far God has brought you since you began.

== 35:7
T: The God of the house of God
R: 'And he built there an altar, and called the place El-beth-el: because there God appeared unto him, when he fled from the face of his brother.' Jacob built an altar and named the place El-beth-el, meaning the God of the house of God. Earlier, he had called the place Bethel, the house of God. Now he focused on the God of that house. Jacob's focus had shifted from the place to the Person. This reflects spiritual growth. It is easy to become attached to places where we have met God. But places are not what matter most. God Himself is what matters. Jesus told the Samaritan woman that true worshippers would worship not in a particular mountain or city but in spirit and in truth. Our faith is not in buildings or locations but in God. Today, examine whether your faith is focused on places, traditions, or experiences, or on God Himself. Let your worship center on the Person of God, not merely on where you meet Him.
S: Jacob built an altar and named the place El-beth-el, the God of the house of God. His focus had shifted from the place to the Person. Let your worship today center on God Himself rather than on places or experiences.

== 35:8
T: Honoring a faithful servant
R: 'But Deborah Rebekah's nurse died, and she was buried beneath Bethel under an oak: and the name of it was called Allon-bachuth.' Deborah, Rebekah's nurse, died at Bethel. She had come with Rebekah from Haran many decades earlier. Now, very old, she died and was buried under an oak. The place was named Allon-bachuth, the oak of weeping. Deborah was not a famous patriarch or matriarch. She was a servant. Yet Scripture records her death and the grief it caused. Her faithful service over a lifetime was honored. God values faithful servants who work behind the scenes. Their names may not be widely known, but He remembers them. Hebrews says, God is not unrighteous to forget your work and labour of love. The tears shed for Deborah show that she was deeply loved. Today, honor the faithful servants in your life: the people who have quietly supported, cared for, and served you. Thank them while you can. And remember that God sees and honors every act of faithful service.
S: Deborah, Rebekah's nurse, died and was buried under an oak called the oak of weeping. Though a servant, her faithful lifetime of care was honored and mourned. Honor and thank the faithful servants in your life today.

Jacob had obeyed, purified his household, and built the altar. Now God met him. Obedience often opens the door to fresh encounters with God. When we return to Him with clean hearts, He meets us with blessing. Jacob had experienced God at Bethel years before, at Mahanaim, and at Peniel. Now he encountered God again. God does not meet us only once. He meets us again and again throughout our lives. Each encounter deepens our relationship with Him. James says, Draw nigh to God, and he will draw nigh to you. When we draw near in obedience, He draws near in blessing. Today, draw near to God. Return to Him with a clean heart. Expect Him to meet you afresh. He delights to bless those who seek Him.

== 35:10
T: Israel confirmed
R: 'And God said unto him, Thy name is Jacob: thy name shall not be called any more Jacob, but Israel shall be thy name: and he called his name Israel.' God confirmed Jacob's new name. At Peniel, the angel had given it. Now God Himself declared it at Bethel. Israel shall be thy name. The confirmation came after Jacob's obedience in putting away idols and returning to Bethel. God reaffirms our identity in Him. Sometimes we receive a word from God, and later He confirms it. God continually reminds us who we are in Him. Jacob had struggled to live up to his new name. At Shechem, he had acted more like Jacob than Israel. But God did not revoke the name. He confirmed it. Second Timothy says, If we believe not, yet he abideth faithful: he cannot deny himself. Today, hear God reaffirm your identity in Christ. Even when you fall short, He does not take back the new name He has given you.

== 35:11
T: God Almighty's promise
R: 'And God said unto him, I am God Almighty: be fruitful and multiply; a nation and a company of nations shall be of thee, and kings shall come out of thy loins.' God revealed Himself to Jacob as God Almighty, El Shaddai. This was the name He had used with Abraham. He repeated the creation blessing: be fruitful and multiply. And He promised that a nation, many nations, and kings would come from Jacob. The promises given to Abraham were now fully passed to Jacob. God's covenant continued through the generations. The God who made promises to Abraham was keeping them through Jacob. Kings would come from Jacob's line, including David and ultimately Jesus, the King of kings. God's promises span centuries. What He says to one generation, He fulfills in many. Today, rest in the faithfulness of God Almighty. His promises to you are as certain as His promises to Jacob. He is able to do everything He has said.

== 35:12
T: The land promised
R: 'And the land which I gave Abraham and Isaac, to thee I will give it, and to thy seed after thee will I give the land.' God promised Jacob the same land He had given Abraham and Isaac. The promise passed down through three generations. And it would continue to Jacob's descendants. God's promises are generational. What He promised Abraham was renewed to Isaac and then to Jacob. Each generation received the promise afresh. This shows that God's faithfulness is not limited to one lifetime. He keeps His word across centuries. Psalm 105 says, He hath remembered his covenant for ever, the word which he commanded to a thousand generations. For believers today, the promise extends further. Hebrews describes the patriarchs as looking for a heavenly country. Our ultimate inheritance is not merely land but eternal life with God. Today, thank God for promises that span generations. And look forward to the inheritance He has prepared for all who belong to Him.

== 35:13
T: God went up
R: 'And God went up from him in the place where he talked with him.' After speaking with Jacob, God went up from him. The encounter ended. This phrase echoes God's departure from Abraham after their conversations. Divine encounters have beginnings and endings. We cannot stay on the mountaintop forever. But the word God speaks stays with us. Jacob would carry this renewed promise for the rest of his life. Notice the phrase where he talked with him. God had talked with Jacob, conversed with him. This was personal communication. Our relationship with God involves conversation. He speaks through His word, and we respond in prayer. Jesus said, My sheep hear my voice. Moments of deep encounter are precious, but they are meant to strengthen us for daily life. Today, treasure the times God has spoken to you. Carry His words into your everyday life. And keep the conversation going through prayer and reading His word.

== 35:14
T: Pouring out an offering
R: 'And Jacob set up a pillar in the place where he talked with him, even a pillar of stone: and he poured a drink offering thereon, and he poured oil thereon.' Jacob set up a stone pillar again, just as he had done at Bethel years before. This time, he poured out both a drink offering and oil. The drink offering is the first mentioned in Scripture. It represented pouring out oneself before God. Worship involves giving. Jacob poured out offerings as an act of devotion. Paul later used this image for his own life: I am ready to be offered, literally poured out as a drink offering. True worship is not just words; it is the offering of our lives. Romans 12 says, present your bodies a living sacrifice. Jacob's pillar and offerings marked his gratitude and commitment. Today, consider what you are pouring out before God. Is it your time, your gifts, your energy? Offer yourself to Him as a drink offering, fully devoted to His purposes.

Places of encounter gain significance over time. Bethel had been the place of his first vision. Now it was the place of renewal and confirmation. Jacob's relationship with God had grown, and Bethel represented that growth. We can return to the same spiritual truths again and again and find them richer each time. The verses we learned as children take on new meaning as we mature. Philippians says, he which hath begun a good work in you will perform it until the day of Jesus Christ. Spiritual highs are often followed by difficult valleys. Jacob had just experienced God's presence and renewed promises. Now he faced one of the greatest sorrows of his life. Jesus Himself was baptized and blessed, then immediately led into the wilderness to be tempted. Second Corinthians says, We are troubled on every side, yet not distressed. Today, if you have experienced a spiritual high followed by hardship, do not be surprised. God is still with you in the valley. The promises given at Bethel remain true on the hard road.

== 35:19

Jacob's beloved wife, the woman he had worked fourteen years to marry, was gone. Her grave was by the roadside. Bethlehem would later become significant. David would be born there. Rachel's grave near Bethlehem became a symbol of grief. Yet from Bethlehem came the Savior who would wipe away every tear. God weaves even our deepest sorrows into His story of redemption. Revelation promises, God shall wipe away all tears from their eyes; and there shall be no more death, neither sorrow, nor crying. Today, if you are grieving, know that God is weaving even your pain into His redemptive story. From the place of sorrow, He brings hope.

== 35:22
T: A son's betrayal
R: 'And it came to pass, when Israel dwelt in that land, that Reuben went and lay with Bilhah his father's concubine: and Israel heard it.' Reuben, Jacob's firstborn, committed a grievous sin by sleeping with Bilhah, his father's concubine. It was an act of betrayal and perhaps an attempt to claim his father's authority. Scripture simply says, and Israel heard it. Jacob's silence here mirrors his silence after Dinah's violation. But this sin would have lasting consequences. On his deathbed, Jacob would strip Reuben of the firstborn's privilege, saying, Unstable as water, thou shalt not excel; because thou wentest up to thy father's bed. Sin has consequences that can affect our future and our legacy. Reuben's moment of passion cost him his birthright. First Chronicles notes that his birthright was given to the sons of Joseph. Today, remember that choices made in a moment can have lasting effects. Guard your heart and your actions. Integrity protects your future.

== 35:23
T: The sons of Leah
R: 'The sons of Leah; Reuben, Jacob's firstborn, and Simeon, and Levi, and Judah, and Issachar, and Zebulun.' Scripture lists Leah's six sons. These would become six of Israel's twelve tribes. Reuben is still called the firstborn, though he would lose that privilege. Simeon and Levi had committed violence at Shechem. Judah would become the royal tribe. Each son had a different story, strengths, and failures. Yet all were part of God's plan. God's people are made up of flawed individuals. None of Jacob's sons was perfect. Some committed serious sins. Yet God used them all to form the nation of Israel. This is encouraging for us. God does not wait for perfect people. He works through imperfect, sometimes deeply flawed people to accomplish His purposes. First Corinthians says, God hath chosen the foolish things of the world to confound the wise. == 35:24
T: The sons of Rachel
R: 'The sons of Rachel; Joseph, and Benjamin.' Rachel had only two sons: Joseph and Benjamin. Yet these two would play enormous roles in Israel's story. Joseph would rise to power in Egypt and save his entire family from famine. Benjamin would be the father of a tribe that produced King Saul and the apostle Paul. Rachel, who had struggled so long with barrenness, gave birth to two of the most significant sons. Numbers are not everything. Rachel had fewer sons than Leah, but her sons had great influence. God measures significance differently than we do. Quality and calling matter more than quantity. Rachel's long wait and her death in childbirth were not in vain. Her sons would bless the world. Today, do not measure your life by quantity alone. God may use what seems small or limited in your life to accomplish great things. Trust Him to make your contributions meaningful, whatever their size.

== 35:25
T: The sons of Bilhah
R: 'And the sons of Bilhah, Rachel's handmaid; Dan, and Naphtali.' Bilhah, Rachel's servant, bore Dan and Naphtali. These became two of Israel's tribes. Bilhah, a woman with little social standing, became a mother of tribes. Her sons were counted equally among the sons of Israel. God's family includes people from every background. The sons of servants were given the same inheritance as the sons of wives. In God's eyes, they were all sons of Israel. This foreshadows the gospel. In Christ, social distinctions do not determine our standing before God. Galatians says, There is neither Jew nor Greek, there is neither bond nor free… for ye are all one in Christ Jesus. Naphtali's territory would later be in Galilee, where Jesus did much of His ministry. Isaiah prophesied that the land of Naphtali would see a great light. Today, rejoice that in God's family, everyone has a place. No background disqualifies you from His grace.
