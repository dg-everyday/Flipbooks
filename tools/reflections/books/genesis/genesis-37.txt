# Genesis 37: Joseph sold into Egypt

== 37:1
T: Dwelling in the land of promise
R: 'And Jacob dwelt in the land wherein his father was a stranger, in the land of Canaan.' Jacob settled in Canaan, the land where his father Isaac had been a stranger. The promise was still not fully realized. Jacob lived in the land but did not yet possess it as his own. Like Abraham and Isaac, he was a sojourner. Yet he dwelt there, trusting God's promise. Living in the land of promise without owning it requires faith. Hebrews 11 says the patriarchs dwelt in tabernacles, looking for a city which hath foundations, whose builder and maker is God. They were content to live as strangers because they knew God's promise was sure. Believers today live in a similar tension. We have received God's promises, but we do not yet see their complete fulfillment. We live in this world as citizens of heaven. Today, live faithfully where God has placed you, trusting that His promises will be fully realized in His time.
S: Jacob dwelt in Canaan, the land where his father had been a stranger. He lived in the land of promise without yet possessing it, trusting God's word. Live faithfully where God has placed you, trusting His promises will be fully realized.
P: Living by faith in God's promises. Genesis 37:1: 'Jacob dwelt in the land wherein his father was a stranger.' Live faithfully today, trusting His promises.

== 37:2
T: A young shepherd
R: 'These are the generations of Jacob. Joseph, being seventeen years old, was feeding the flock with his brethren… and Joseph brought unto his father their evil report.' The story now turns to Joseph, seventeen years old, tending sheep with his brothers. He brought a bad report about them to his father. We do not know if Joseph was right to report his brothers' behavior or whether he was tattling. But this added to the tension in the family. Joseph's story will be one of the most remarkable in Scripture. It begins with an ordinary teenager in a dysfunctional family. Many great stories of faith begin in ordinary, messy circumstances. God does not wait for perfect conditions to work. He takes young, imperfect people and shapes them through trials. First Timothy says, Let no man despise thy youth. God can use young people powerfully. Today, pray for the teenagers and young people in your life. God may be preparing them for remarkable purposes, even through difficulties they face now.
S: Joseph, at seventeen, fed the flock with his brothers and brought a bad report about them to his father. His remarkable story began in an ordinary, troubled family. Pray today for young people, whom God may be preparing for great purposes.
P: Great stories start in ordinary places. Genesis 37:2: 'Joseph, being seventeen years old, was feeding the flock.' God uses young people. Pray for the youth in your life today.

== 37:3
T: The coat of favoritism
R: 'Now Israel loved Joseph more than all his children, because he was the son of his old age: and he made him a coat of many colours.' Jacob loved Joseph more than his other sons. And he showed it openly by giving Joseph a special coat. This coat was a visible symbol of favoritism. Every time the brothers saw it, they were reminded that their father loved Joseph more. Jacob repeated the mistakes of his own parents. Isaac favored Esau; Rebekah favored Jacob. Now Jacob favored Joseph. Patterns of favoritism pass down through generations. Jacob of all people should have known the pain favoritism causes. Yet he did the same thing. Breaking generational patterns requires intentional effort and God's grace. Colossians says, Fathers, provoke not your children to anger, lest they be discouraged. Favoritism provokes. Today, examine whether you show favoritism in your family, workplace, or community. Ask God to help you break unhealthy patterns and love everyone fully.
S: Israel loved Joseph more than his other children and gave him a coat of many colors. Jacob repeated the favoritism that had damaged his own family. Ask God to help you break unhealthy patterns and love everyone fully.

== 37:4
T: Hatred grows
R: 'And when his brethren saw that their father loved him more than all his brethren, they hated him, and could not speak peaceably unto him.' Joseph's brothers saw their father's favoritism, and they hated Joseph for it. They could not even speak kindly to him. The family atmosphere was poisoned. Favoritism breeds resentment. The brothers' hatred was directed at Joseph, but the root cause was their father's unequal love. Joseph did not choose to be favored. Yet he bore the brunt of their resentment. Unresolved resentment grows into hatred. And hatred, as Cain's story showed, can lead to violence. First John says, Whosoever hateth his brother is a murderer. Jesus warned that anger in the heart is the root of murder. The brothers' inability to speak peaceably was a warning sign of worse to come. Today, if you are harboring resentment toward someone, deal with it before it grows. Bring it to God. Ask Him to replace hatred with love and to help you speak peaceably.
S: When the brothers saw their father loved Joseph more, they hated him and could not speak peaceably to him. Resentment from favoritism grew into hatred. Deal with any resentment in your heart before it grows, asking God to replace it with love.
P: Resentment grows into hatred. Genesis 37:4: 'they hated him, and could not speak peaceably unto him.' Deal with resentment before it grows. Ask God for love today.

== 37:5
T: A dream shared unwisely
R: 'And Joseph dreamed a dream, and he told it his brethren: and they hated him yet the more.' Joseph had a dream, and he told his brothers about it. Their hatred increased. Joseph's dream was from God, but sharing it with his brothers at this time only made things worse. Young and perhaps naive, Joseph did not consider how his words would affect them. Not everything God shows us needs to be shared immediately. Wisdom knows when to speak and when to remain silent. Mary, after the shepherds' visit, kept all these things, and pondered them in her heart. Some revelations need time to mature before they are shared. Ecclesiastes says there is a time to keep silence, and a time to speak. Joseph would learn this through suffering. Today, ask God for wisdom about what to share and when. Some things He shows you may be meant to be treasured privately for a season. Speak with sensitivity to how your words will affect others.
S: Joseph had a dream and told his brothers, and they hated him even more. Not everything God shows us needs to be shared immediately or with everyone. Ask God for wisdom about what to share and when.

== 37:6
T: Hear my dream
R: 'And he said unto them, Hear, I pray you, this dream which I have dreamed.' Joseph insisted that his brothers listen to his dream. He seemed eager to share it, perhaps without realizing how it would sound. Young people often share with enthusiasm before they have learned discretion. Joseph's eagerness was not wrong in itself. But it lacked sensitivity. He did not consider that his brothers already resented him. Sharing a dream of his own exaltation only fueled their anger. Maturity involves learning to read our audience. Paul became all things to all men, adapting his approach to his hearers. Joseph would later become a master of discretion, carefully revealing himself to his brothers in Egypt. But at seventeen, he had much to learn. God would teach him through years of hardship. Today, if you are young or newly excited about something God has shown you, ask Him for sensitivity. Consider how your words will be received before you speak.

== 37:7
T: Sheaves bowing
R: 'For, behold, we were binding sheaves in the field, and, lo, my sheaf arose, and also stood upright; and, behold, your sheaves stood round about, and made obeisance to my sheaf.' Joseph described his dream: the brothers were binding grain in the field, and Joseph's sheaf stood up while their sheaves bowed to it. The meaning was obvious. Joseph would rule over his brothers. This dream would come true decades later, in a way no one imagined. During a famine, Joseph's brothers would come to Egypt to buy grain. They would bow before him, not knowing he was their brother. God's revelations often make sense only in hindsight. Joseph could not have known how his dream would be fulfilled. He simply received it. Habakkuk says, the vision is yet for an appointed time… though it tarry, wait for it. Today, if God has given you a vision or promise, hold on to it. Its fulfillment may come in ways you cannot imagine.
S: Joseph dreamed that his brothers' sheaves bowed down to his sheaf. Decades later the dream was fulfilled when they bowed before him in Egypt to buy grain. Hold on to God's promises, even if their fulfillment comes in ways you cannot imagine.

== 37:8
T: Hated for his words
R: 'And his brethren said to him, Shalt thou indeed reign over us? or shalt thou indeed have dominion over us? And they hated him yet the more for his dreams, and for his words.' The brothers mocked Joseph's dream. Shalt thou reign over us? They understood the meaning and rejected it. Their hatred grew because of his dreams and his words. The brothers rejected the idea that their younger brother would rule over them. Ironically, this mirrors the rejection of Jesus. Jesus came to His own, and His own received Him not. In Luke 19, the citizens said, We will not have this man to reign over us. Joseph's brothers rejected the one God had chosen to save them. Yet their rejection could not stop God's plan. In fact, their rejection would become the means by which God's plan unfolded. Acts 2 says Jesus was delivered by the determinate counsel and foreknowledge of God. Today, remember that rejection cannot stop God's purposes. What others reject, God can use for salvation.
S: The brothers mocked Joseph, asking if he would truly reign over them, and hated him more. Their rejection could not stop God's plan, just as Jesus' rejection led to salvation. Remember today that rejection cannot stop God's purposes.

== 37:9
T: The sun, moon and stars
R: 'And he dreamed yet another dream, and told it his brethren, and said, Behold, I have dreamed a dream more; and, behold, the sun and the moon and the eleven stars made obeisance to me.' Joseph had a second dream, even more grand than the first. This time, the sun, moon, and eleven stars bowed to him. The sun and moon represented his parents; the eleven stars, his brothers. The entire family would bow to Joseph. Two dreams confirmed the message. In Scripture, repetition often signals certainty. Pharaoh would later have two dreams with the same meaning, and Joseph would explain that it was doubled because the thing is established by God. God confirmed His plan for Joseph with two dreams. When God repeats something, it is sure. Joseph's second dream, like the first, would come true in God's timing. Today, if God has confirmed something to you more than once, through His word, through prayer, or through others, take it seriously. What God establishes, He will bring to pass.
S: Joseph had a second dream in which the sun, moon and eleven stars bowed to him. Two dreams confirmed that God had established His plan. When God confirms something more than once, take it seriously and trust Him to bring it to pass.
P: When God repeats, it's certain. Genesis 37:9: 'the sun and the moon and the eleven stars made obeisance to me.' Two dreams, one message. What God establishes, He fulfills.

== 37:10
T: A father's rebuke
R: 'And he told it to his father, and to his brethren: and his father rebuked him, and said unto him, What is this dream that thou hast dreamed? Shall I and thy mother and thy brethren indeed come to bow down ourselves to thee to the earth?' Even Jacob rebuked Joseph for this second dream. The idea of his parents bowing to their son seemed outrageous. Jacob's rebuke may have been meant to calm the brothers' anger. Or he may have genuinely found the dream presumptuous. Even those who love us may not understand what God is doing in our lives. Jacob loved Joseph more than anyone, yet he rebuked him. God's plans can seem strange or even offensive to those around us. Jesus' own family thought He was beside Himself. Mary did not fully understand when Jesus said He must be about His Father's business. Today, if those close to you do not understand what God is doing in your life, be patient. Their understanding may come later, as Jacob's eventually did.

== 37:11
T: Envy and pondering
R: 'And his brethren envied him; but his father observed the saying.' Two responses to the same dream. The brothers envied Joseph. But Jacob observed the saying, he kept it in mind and pondered it. Though he had rebuked Joseph, Jacob did not dismiss the dream. Somewhere in his heart, he wondered if it might be from God. Jacob had experienced God's dreams himself at Bethel. He knew God spoke this way. This verse echoes what Luke says about Mary: Mary kept all these things, and pondered them in her heart. There is wisdom in pondering things we do not fully understand. Rather than accepting or rejecting immediately, we can hold them before God and wait for understanding. The brothers' envy closed their hearts; Jacob's pondering kept his heart open. Today, when you encounter something you do not understand, resist both envy and quick dismissal. Ponder it. Hold it before God. Let Him reveal its meaning in time.

It was also the place where Simeon and Levi had massacred the city. The brothers were far from their father's watchful eye. Distance from accountability can be dangerous. When we are far from those who hold us accountable, we may do things we would not do at home. The brothers' hatred, festering at a distance from their father, would soon erupt. Proverbs says, Iron sharpeneth iron. We need others to keep us accountable. Isolation can lead to poor decisions. James says, Confess your faults one to another. Today, consider whether you are staying connected to people who hold you accountable. Do not isolate yourself, especially when you are struggling. Stay close to people who will speak truth into your life.

Joseph responded, Here am I. These words echo Abraham's response to God and Jacob's response to the angel. Joseph was ready and willing to obey his father. Notice the irony. Joseph was sent by his father to his brothers, who hated him. He went willingly, not knowing what awaited him. This foreshadows Jesus, who was sent by His Father to His own people, who would reject Him. Jesus said, I came down from heaven, not to do mine own will, but the will of him that sent me. Joseph's willing obedience set in motion God's plan to save many lives. Today, say Here am I to God. Be willing to go where He sends you, even if the path is uncertain. As soon as the brothers saw Joseph approaching, before he even reached them, they plotted to kill him. Their hatred had grown so deep that the sight of him triggered murderous intent. Joseph was coming to check on their welfare, and they were planning his death. This is a chilling verse. It shows how far unchecked hatred can go. James describes this progression: lust conceives and brings forth sin, and sin, when finished, brings forth death. The brothers' conspiracy foreshadows the religious leaders who conspired to kill Jesus. Matthew says they consulted that they might take Jesus by subtilty, and kill him. Today, guard your heart against hatred. Deal with resentment early, before it grows into something destructive.

== 37:23
T: Stripped of his coat
R: 'And it came to pass, when Joseph was come unto his brethren, that they stript Joseph out of his coat, his coat of many colours that was on him.' When Joseph arrived, his brothers stripped off his coat of many colors. The symbol of their father's favoritism was torn away. They attacked what represented their resentment. Joseph was stripped of his honor, his status, and his protection. Throughout his life, Joseph would be stripped of coats. Here, by his brothers. Later, Potiphar's wife would take his garment. Yet each time, God would clothe him with greater honor. Eventually, Pharaoh would clothe him in fine linen. Jesus too was stripped of His garments before the crucifixion. Soldiers cast lots for His clothing. Yet He was raised and exalted above every name. Philippians 2 says He humbled himself and God highly exalted him.
