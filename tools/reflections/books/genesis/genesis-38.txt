# Genesis 38: Judah and Tamar

== 38:1
T: Going down
R: 'And it came to pass at that time, that Judah went down from his brethren, and turned in to a certain Adullamite, whose name was Hirah.' Right after the sale of Joseph, Judah left his brothers and went down to live among the Canaanites. The phrase went down suggests more than geography. Judah was moving away from his family and spiritually downward. Guilt can drive people away from those who remind them of their wrongdoing. Perhaps Judah could not face his father's grief, knowing he had suggested selling Joseph. He separated himself and settled among people who did not share his family's faith. Spiritual decline often begins with separation. We distance ourselves from those who keep us accountable and settle among influences that pull us further away. Hebrews warns, not forsaking the assembling of ourselves together. Today, if you feel yourself drifting away from God's people because of guilt or shame, do not go down. Turn back. Grace awaits those who return.
S: Right after Joseph was sold, Judah went down from his brothers and settled among the Canaanites. Guilt and separation often begin a spiritual decline. If guilt is pulling you away from God's people, turn back, for grace awaits those who return.
P: Don't let guilt drive you away. Genesis 38:1: 'Judah went down from his brethren.' Separation often starts a decline. Turn back to God and His people today.

== 38:2
T: Choosing without God
R: 'And Judah saw there a daughter of a certain Canaanite, whose name was Shuah; and he took her, and went in unto her.' Judah saw a Canaanite woman and married her. The language is brief: he saw, he took. There is no mention of seeking God or consulting his father. Judah made a major life decision based on sight and desire. This echoes Esau's choices and contrasts sharply with how Abraham found a wife for Isaac. Abraham had prayed and sought God's guidance. Judah simply took what he wanted. Choosing a spouse without seeking God can lead to heartache. The decisions we make in seasons of spiritual drift often have lasting consequences. Judah's family would experience great sorrow in the years ahead. Proverbs 3 says, In all thy ways acknowledge him, and he shall direct thy paths. Today, before making any significant decision, pause and seek God. Do not let desire or circumstance alone guide your choices. Acknowledge Him in all your ways.
S: Judah saw a Canaanite woman and married her without any recorded prayer or counsel. Decisions made in seasons of spiritual drift often bring lasting consequences. Seek God before significant decisions and acknowledge Him in all your ways.
P: Seek God before deciding. Genesis 38:2: 'Judah saw there a daughter of a certain Canaanite… and he took her.' He saw and took without asking God. Acknowledge Him in all your ways today.

== 38:3
T: A son named Er
R: 'And she conceived, and bare a son; and he called his name Er.' Judah's first son was born and named Er. Children are a blessing from God, regardless of the circumstances of their parents' marriage. Er was a new life, a gift. Yet his story would end in tragedy. Every child born into the world carries potential for good or evil. Parents have tremendous influence on the direction their children take. Judah, living among the Canaanites and drifting from God, was not in a strong position to raise godly children. The environment we create for our children matters. Deuteronomy commands parents to teach God's words diligently to their children. Proverbs says, Train up a child in the way he should go. Children absorb the values of the home. Today, if you are raising children or influencing young people, consider the environment you are creating. Is it one where faith can grow? Ask God to help you raise the next generation in His ways.
S: Judah's first son was born and named Er. Every child is a gift, and the environment parents create shapes the direction children take. Ask God to help you create a home where faith can grow for the next generation.

== 38:4
T: Another son
R: 'And she conceived again, and bare a son; and she called his name Onan.' A second son, Onan, was born. Notice that this time, the mother named him. The family was growing. In the midst of Judah's spiritual wandering, God continued to give him children. God's blessings do not stop simply because we drift. He is patient and kind even when we are not walking closely with Him. Romans 2 speaks of the riches of his goodness and forbearance and longsuffering, adding that the goodness of God leadeth thee to repentance. God's continued blessings are meant to draw us back to Him. Judah's children were gifts, but they were also invitations to return to God. Every blessing in our lives, especially when we are far from God, is a reminder of His patient love. Today, look at the blessings in your life. Are they leading you closer to God? Let His goodness draw you to Him in gratitude and repentance.

== 38:5
T: A third son
R: 'And she yet again conceived, and bare a son; and called his name Shelah: and he was at Chezib, when she bare him.' A third son, Shelah, was born. Scripture notes that Judah was at Chezib at the time. Chezib may mean deceitful or false. Some see this as a quiet comment on the direction of Judah's life. Judah was in a place associated with deception. Our surroundings often reflect our spiritual state. When we drift from God, we often find ourselves in places and situations that reflect that drift. The places we spend time, the people we spend time with, and the activities we engage in shape us. First Corinthians says, Be not deceived: evil communications corrupt good manners. Judah was in a period of his life marked by distance from God. But his story was not over. God would eventually bring Judah to repentance and transformation. Today, examine where you are spending your time. Do your surroundings reflect closeness to God or distance from Him?

== 38:6
T: Tamar enters the story
R: 'And Judah took a wife for Er his firstborn, whose name was Tamar.' Judah arranged a marriage for his oldest son Er with a woman named Tamar. Tamar would become one of the most remarkable women in Genesis. She is one of only four women named in Matthew's genealogy of Jesus. Tamar was likely a Canaanite. Yet she would become an ancestor of King David and of Jesus Christ. God's grace reaches outsiders. Tamar's story would be painful and complicated. She would be wronged repeatedly by Judah's family. But God would honor her. Her name would be remembered in the line of the Messiah. God sees the overlooked and the mistreated. He weaves them into His story of redemption. Matthew's genealogy includes Tamar, Rahab, Ruth, and Bathsheba, each with a complicated story. Today, remember that God's grace reaches people of every background. No one is beyond His redemptive purposes. Your story, however complicated, can be part of His plan.
S: Judah took Tamar as a wife for his firstborn son Er. Tamar, likely a Canaanite, would become an ancestor of David and of Jesus. Remember that God's grace reaches every background and can weave complicated stories into His plan.

== 38:7
T: Wicked in God's sight
R: 'And Er, Judah's firstborn, was wicked in the sight of the LORD; and the LORD slew him.' Er was wicked in the sight of the LORD, and God took his life. Scripture does not tell us what Er did, only that it was evil in God's eyes. This is a sobering verse. God sees all our actions and judges them. Er's wickedness was serious enough that God intervened directly. Not every sin brings immediate death. God is patient and merciful. But His patience has limits. Romans 6 says, the wages of sin is death. Er experienced this truth in an immediate way. For all of us, sin leads to death ultimately. That is why we need a Savior. The same verse in Romans continues: but the gift of God is eternal life through Jesus Christ our Lord. Today, take sin seriously. Do not assume God overlooks what is evil in His sight. And thank Him that through Christ, the penalty of sin has been paid for all who believe.

== 38:8
T: Raising up seed
R: 'And Judah said unto Onan, Go in unto thy brother's wife, and marry her, and raise up seed to thy brother.' After Er's death, Judah told Onan to marry Tamar and raise up children for his brother. This was the custom of levirate marriage. If a man died childless, his brother would marry the widow so that the dead brother's name would continue. This custom was later included in the law of Moses. It required the brother to set aside self-interest for the sake of his brother's legacy. This principle of providing for widows and the vulnerable runs throughout Scripture. James says, Pure religion and undefiled before God and the Father is this, To visit the fatherless and widows in their affliction. God cares deeply about those who have lost their protectors. Today, consider the widows and vulnerable people in your community. How can you help care for them? Look for ways to provide support, protection, and dignity to those who have suffered loss.
S: Judah told Onan to marry Tamar and raise up children for his brother according to custom. This practice cared for widows and preserved family lines. Look for ways today to care for widows and vulnerable people in your community.
P: Care for widows. Genesis 38:8: 'raise up seed to thy brother.' God provided for widows and the vulnerable. Pure religion visits widows in their affliction. Help someone today.

== 38:9
T: Selfishness exposed
R: 'And Onan knew that the seed should not be his.' Onan was willing to take Tamar as his wife, but he deliberately refused to give his brother an heir. He knew any child would be counted as his brother's, not his own. So he made sure no child would come. His motive was selfish. A child for Er would reduce Onan's own inheritance. He was unwilling to sacrifice for his brother's legacy. Onan used Tamar while denying her the hope of a child and security in the family. His sin was a betrayal of his brother, of Tamar, and of his family duty. Selfishness that exploits others while avoiding responsibility is deeply wrong. Philippians says, Look not every man on his own things, but every man also on the things of others. Today, examine whether you are taking benefits while avoiding responsibilities. Ask God to free you from selfishness and help you care for others' interests, even when it costs you.
S: Onan knew any child would not be counted as his own, so he deliberately refused to give his brother an heir. He took advantage of Tamar while selfishly avoiding his duty. Ask God to free you from selfishness and help you care for others even when it costs you.
P: Selfishness exploits others. Genesis 38:9: 'Onan knew that the seed should not be his.' He took benefits but refused responsibility. Look to others' interests today.

It was a selfish betrayal of family duty and a cruel exploitation of a vulnerable widow. God cares about how we treat others, especially the vulnerable. Onan's selfishness harmed Tamar and dishonored his brother's memory. God's judgment shows how seriously He takes such injustice. Throughout Scripture, God is the defender of widows and orphans. Psalm 68 calls Him A father of the fatherless, and a judge of the widows. Exodus warns, Ye shall not afflict any widow, or fatherless child. Those who exploit the vulnerable face God's displeasure. Today, examine how you treat those with less power than you. Are you using anyone for your own benefit? God sees, and He defends the vulnerable. Treat others with justice and compassion.

== 38:11
T: A broken promise
R: 'Then said Judah to Tamar his daughter in law, Remain a widow at thy father's house, till Shelah my son be grown: for he said, Lest peradventure he die also, as his brethren did.' Judah told Tamar to wait until his youngest son Shelah grew up. But his real reason was fear. He worried Shelah would die too, as if Tamar were to blame. Judah had no intention of keeping his promise. He sent Tamar away, leaving her in limbo. She was a widow, unable to remarry, waiting for a promise that would never be kept. Judah blamed Tamar for his sons' deaths, when the real cause was their own wickedness. People sometimes blame others for problems they themselves caused. Judah failed to see his sons' sin and his own failure as a father. Instead, he made Tamar the scapegoat. Today, examine whether you are blaming someone else for problems that are not their fault. Keep your promises, and do not leave others in limbo through your fear or neglect.

Loss can either draw us toward God or push us further away. It is a crossroads. Psalm 34 says, The LORD is nigh unto them that are of a broken heart. Today, if you are grieving, let your grief draw you to God rather than away from Him. He is near to the brokenhearted.

== 38:23

R: 'And Judah said, Let her take it to her, lest we be shamed: behold, I sent this kid, and thou hast not found her.' Judah decided to let the matter drop. Let her keep the items, he said, lest we be shamed. His concern was not the wrong he had done but the embarrassment of being found out. He wanted to avoid public shame. Fear of shame can keep us from doing what is right. Judah would rather lose his identity items than face embarrassment. He chose silence over accountability.
