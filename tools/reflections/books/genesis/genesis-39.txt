# Genesis 39: Joseph in Potiphar's house and in prison

== 39:1
T: Brought down
R: 'And Joseph was brought down to Egypt; and Potiphar, an officer of Pharaoh, captain of the guard, an Egyptian, bought him of the hands of the Ishmeelites, which had brought him down thither.' Joseph was brought down to Egypt and bought by Potiphar, captain of Pharaoh's guard. Notice the repeated phrase brought down. Joseph's life was going down: from favored son to pit to slave. He had no choice, no voice, and no apparent future. Yet this was not the end. God was positioning Joseph in exactly the right place. Potiphar was connected to Pharaoh's court. Joseph's downward path was leading him toward the throne. God's ways often involve going down before going up. Jesus humbled Himself, going down to the cross, before God highly exalted Him. Peter says, Humble yourselves therefore under the mighty hand of God, that he may exalt you in due time. Today, if your life seems to be going down, do not lose hope. God may be positioning you for something greater than you can see.
S: Joseph was brought down to Egypt and bought by Potiphar, captain of Pharaoh's guard. His downward path was actually positioning him toward the throne. If your life seems to be going down, trust that God may be positioning you for something greater.
P: Down before up. Genesis 39:1: 'Joseph was brought down to Egypt; and Potiphar… bought him.' God was positioning him for the throne. Don't lose hope in your low season.

== 39:2
T: The LORD was with Joseph
R: 'And the LORD was with Joseph, and he was a prosperous man; and he was in the house of his master the Egyptian.' Here is the key to Joseph's entire story: the LORD was with Joseph. This phrase appears repeatedly in this chapter. Joseph was a slave in a foreign land, yet God was with him. And because God was with him, he prospered. Joseph's circumstances were terrible, but his relationship with God was intact. God's presence does not depend on our circumstances. He is with us in the pit, in slavery, and in prison. Joseph was separated from his family, his home, and his culture, but he was not separated from God. Romans 8 asks, Who shall separate us from the love of Christ? shall tribulation, or distress, or persecution? The answer is no. Nothing can separate us from God's love. Today, whatever your circumstances, know that the LORD is with you. His presence is your greatest asset, even when everything else is taken away.
S: The LORD was with Joseph, and he prospered even as a slave in his Egyptian master's house. God's presence did not depend on Joseph's circumstances. Know today that the LORD is with you, whatever your circumstances.
P: The LORD was with Joseph. Genesis 39:2: 'the LORD was with Joseph, and he was a prosperous man.' Even as a slave, God was with him. He's with you too, wherever you are.

== 39:3
T: Others notice God's hand
R: 'And his master saw that the LORD was with him, and that the LORD made all that he did to prosper in his hand.' Potiphar, an Egyptian official, noticed that the LORD was with Joseph. He saw that everything Joseph did prospered. Joseph's faithfulness and God's blessing were visible even to an outsider. Our lives can be a testimony to others. When we walk with God, people notice. They may not understand everything, but they see something different. Joseph did not preach to Potiphar. His work and character spoke for themselves. Jesus said, Let your light so shine before men, that they may see your good works, and glorify your Father which is in heaven. Peter says believers can win others by their good conversation, their behavior. Today, let your work and character be a testimony. Do your job with excellence and integrity. Let others see God's hand in your life, even if you never say a word.
S: Potiphar saw that the LORD was with Joseph and made everything he did prosper. Joseph's faithfulness and God's blessing were visible even to an outsider. Let your work and character today be a testimony that points others to God.

== 39:4
T: Faithful in service
R: 'And Joseph found grace in his sight, and he served him: and he made him overseer over his house, and all that he had he put into his hand.' Joseph found favor with Potiphar and served him well. Potiphar promoted him to overseer of his entire household. Everything was put into Joseph's hands. Joseph did not sulk about his slavery. He served faithfully. His attitude and work ethic led to promotion. Joseph could have been bitter. He had been betrayed by his brothers and sold as a slave. But he chose to serve with excellence. Faithfulness in small things leads to greater responsibility. Jesus said, thou hast been faithful over a few things, I will make thee ruler over many things. Colossians says, Servants, obey in all things your masters… in singleness of heart, fearing God. Today, whatever your role, serve faithfully. Do not let bitterness about your circumstances affect your work. Serve as unto the Lord, and trust Him with your promotion.
S: Joseph found favor with Potiphar and served him, and Potiphar made him overseer of his whole house. Joseph chose faithful service over bitterness about his slavery. Serve faithfully in your role today, trusting God with your promotion.

== 39:5
T: A blessing to others
R: 'And it came to pass from the time that he had made him overseer in his house… that the LORD blessed the Egyptian's house for Joseph's sake; and the blessing of the LORD was upon all that he had in the house, and in the field.' Because of Joseph, God blessed Potiphar's entire household. His home and fields prospered. This fulfills God's promise to Abraham: in thee shall all families of the earth be blessed. Joseph was a channel of blessing even in a foreign land. God's people are meant to be a blessing wherever they go. Our faithfulness can bring good to our workplaces, neighborhoods, and communities. Jeremiah told the exiles to seek the peace of the city where they lived, for in its peace they would find peace. Laban had been blessed for Jacob's sake; now Potiphar was blessed for Joseph's sake. Today, be a blessing where you are. Let God's favor on your life overflow to those around you, even those who do not share your faith.

== 39:6
T: Fully trusted
R: 'And he left all that he had in Joseph's hand; and he knew not ought he had, save the bread which he did eat. And Joseph was a goodly person, and well favoured.' Potiphar trusted Joseph so completely that he did not concern himself with anything except what he ate. Joseph managed everything. Complete trust had been established. Scripture also notes that Joseph was handsome and well built. This detail sets up the temptation that follows. Success and attractiveness can bring new challenges. With trust and favor come new tests. Joseph's high position and appearance would attract unwanted attention. Often, our greatest temptations come not in times of struggle but in times of success. David's fall came when he was king and at ease. When things are going well, we must remain vigilant. First Corinthians says, let him that thinketh he standeth take heed lest he fall. Today, if you are in a season of success, stay alert. Guard your heart. Success brings its own set of temptations.

== 39:7
T: Temptation comes
R: 'And it came to pass after these things, that his master's wife cast her eyes upon Joseph; and she said, Lie with me.' Potiphar's wife noticed Joseph and tried to seduce him. Her invitation was direct. Temptation had come to Joseph in a powerful form. He was young, far from home, a slave with no family or accountability. And the woman was his master's wife, someone with power over him. Temptation often comes when we are vulnerable. Joseph was isolated, without the support of family or community. No one would know if he gave in. Yet Joseph's response would show his character. First Corinthians says, There hath no temptation taken you but such as is common to man: but God is faithful, who will not suffer you to be tempted above that ye are able; but will with the temptation also make a way to escape. God always provides a way out. Today, if you face temptation, remember that God is faithful. He provides a way of escape. Look for it, and take it.

== 39:8
T: He refused
R: 'But he refused, and said unto his master's wife, Behold, my master wotteth not what is with me in the house, and he hath committed all that he hath to my hand.' Joseph refused. Notice the simple, firm word: But he refused. He did not hesitate or entertain the idea. He said no. And he gave a reason: his master trusted him completely. Joseph would not betray that trust. Saying no to temptation requires clarity about our values. Joseph knew what was right, and he held to it. He thought about the consequences for others: his master's trust, his master's household. Sin is never purely private; it affects others. Joseph's refusal shows that faithfulness in small things builds the strength to resist great temptations. Titus says the grace of God teaches us that, denying ungodliness and worldly lusts, we should live soberly, righteously, and godly. Today, decide in advance what you will say no to. Let your values be clear so that when temptation comes, your answer is ready.
S: Joseph refused Potiphar's wife, saying his master had trusted him with everything. Loyalty and integrity guided his firm and immediate no. Decide in advance what you will refuse so that your answer is ready when temptation comes.
P: But he refused. Genesis 39:8: 'But he refused… my master… hath committed all that he hath to my hand.' Joseph said no firmly. Know your answer before temptation comes.

== 39:9
T: Sin against God
R: 'There is none greater in this house than I; neither hath he kept back any thing from me but thee, because thou art his wife: how then can I do this great wickedness, and sin against God?' Joseph's response reveals the heart of his integrity. He recognized that adultery would be a great wickedness. But most importantly, it would be a sin against God. Joseph's ultimate concern was not getting caught or even betraying Potiphar. It was offending God. This is the foundation of true integrity. When we see sin as an offense against God, not just a broken rule or a risk of consequences, we gain strength to resist. David, after his sin with Bathsheba, prayed, Against thee, thee only, have I sinned. Joseph understood this before he sinned. Notice also that Joseph called it what it was: great wickedness. He did not minimize or rationalize. Today, when temptation comes, ask yourself Joseph's question: How can I do this and sin against God? Let your love for God be your strongest defense.
S: Joseph asked how he could do this great wickedness and sin against God. His ultimate concern was not getting caught but offending God. When temptation comes, let your love for God be your strongest defense.
P: How can I sin against God? Genesis 39:9: 'how then can I do this great wickedness, and sin against God?' Joseph saw sin as an offense to God. Let love for Him guard you today.

== 39:10
T: Day by day
R: 'And it came to pass, as she spake to Joseph day by day, that he hearkened not unto her, to lie by her, or to be with her.' Potiphar's wife did not give up. She pressured Joseph day by day. But he did not listen. He refused not only to sleep with her but even to be with her. Joseph avoided being alone with her. Persistent temptation requires persistent resistance. Some temptations come once and pass. Others return day after day. Joseph had to say no repeatedly. And he wisely avoided situations where he would be vulnerable. He did not test his own strength by staying near temptation. Second Timothy says, Flee also youthful lusts. Wisdom involves not just resisting temptation but avoiding situations that make it easier to fall. Proverbs warns, Remove thy way far from her, and come not nigh the door of her house. Today, if you face a recurring temptation, make a plan to avoid it. Do not put yourself in situations where you are vulnerable. Resist day by day.
S: Potiphar's wife pressured Joseph day by day, but he would not listen or even be with her. Persistent temptation requires persistent resistance and wise avoidance. If you face a recurring temptation, make a plan to avoid situations where you are vulnerable.

First Peter says, Be sober, be vigilant; because your adversary the devil, as a roaring lion, walketh about, seeking whom he may devour. Today, stay alert. He left his garment in her hand and ran out of the house. He ran. Sometimes the best response to temptation is to flee. Joseph did not try to resist while standing still. Joseph's garment was a small price to pay for his purity. Later this garment would be used as false evidence against him. But Joseph chose integrity over safety. Today, if you face temptation, do not linger. Flee. Remove yourself from the situation, whatever it costs. Your integrity is worth more than anything you might leave behind.

Potiphar's wife continued her lie, explaining that Joseph fled when she cried out. She twisted the truth cleverly. Joseph did flee, and he did leave his garment. But she reversed the reason. Lies are most convincing when they contain elements of truth. Satan's temptation of Eve included partial truths. The enemy is a master of twisting facts. Jesus called him the father of lies. Potiphar's wife used real events to tell a false story. This is why discernment is so important. We need to examine not just the facts but how they are being framed. Second Corinthians says Satan himself is transformed into an angel of light. Deception often looks like truth. Today, ask God for discernment. When you hear accusations or stories, look beneath the surface. Seek the whole truth, not just the version someone wants you to believe.

== 39:23
T: The LORD made it prosper
R: 'The keeper of the prison looked not to any thing that was under his hand; because the LORD was with him, and that which he did, the LORD made it to prosper.' The warden did not need to supervise Joseph. He trusted him completely. And the reason is given once more: because the LORD was with him. Whatever Joseph did, the LORD made it succeed. This chapter begins and ends with the same truth: the LORD was with Joseph. In Potiphar's house and in prison, God's presence made the difference. Success is not ultimately about our talents or circumstances. It is about God's presence and blessing. Psalm 1 says the righteous person is like a tree planted by rivers of water… and whatsoever he doeth shall prosper. Joseph's prosperity came from his relationship with God. Today, seek God's presence above all else. Let your success flow from walking with Him. Whatever your circumstances, if the LORD is with you, He can make your work prosper.
