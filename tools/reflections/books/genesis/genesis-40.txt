# Genesis 40: The butler and the baker

== 40:1
T: After these things
R: 'And it came to pass after these things, that the butler of the king of Egypt and his baker had offended their lord the king of Egypt.' Two of Pharaoh's officials, his butler and baker, offended the king. We are not told what they did. But their offense brought them to prison, where Joseph was. God was orchestrating events. The phrase after these things connects this event to Joseph's imprisonment. God was moving pieces into place. Joseph had been unjustly imprisoned; now Pharaoh's officials joined him. It was divine appointment. God often uses other people's troubles to accomplish His purposes in our lives. The butler's offense would eventually lead to Joseph's release. Proverbs says, The king's heart is in the hand of the LORD. God was working through Pharaoh's anger to bring Joseph closer to the throne. Today, trust that God is orchestrating events in your life. People and circumstances that seem random may be part of His plan.
S: Pharaoh's butler and baker offended their master and were brought to the prison where Joseph was. God was orchestrating events to move Joseph closer to the throne. Trust that God is arranging people and circumstances in your life according to His plan.
P: God orchestrates events. Genesis 40:1: 'the butler of the king of Egypt and his baker had offended their lord.' Their trouble brought them to Joseph. God is arranging your story too.
H: #DailyGrace #Genesis401 #Providence #GodsPlan #Timing #BibleVerse

== 40:2
T: The king's anger
R: 'And Pharaoh was wroth against two of his officers, against the chief of the butlers, and against the chief of the bakers.' Pharaoh was angry with his chief butler and chief baker. These were high-ranking officials responsible for the king's food and drink. They had positions of great trust. Yet in a moment, they fell from favor. Positions of power are fragile. Those who serve kings can lose everything when the king is displeased. Proverbs says, The wrath of a king is as messengers of death. Earthly favor is unpredictable and temporary. But God's favor is different. His love is steadfast. Romans 8 assures us that nothing can separate us from the love of God which is in Christ Jesus. We do not have to fear falling out of God's favor because we are secure in Christ. Today, do not place your security in human favor or position. These can change in a moment. Place your security in God's unchanging love.
S: Pharaoh was angry with his chief butler and chief baker, officials of great trust. Positions of human power and favor are fragile and can be lost in a moment. Place your security in God's unchanging love rather than in human favor.
P: Human favor is fragile; God's love is steadfast. Genesis 40:2: 'Pharaoh was wroth against two of his officers.' Rest your security in God's unchanging love today.

== 40:3
T: The same prison
R: 'And he put them in ward in the house of the captain of the guard, into the prison, the place where Joseph was bound.' Pharaoh's officials were placed in the same prison as Joseph. This was God's providence at work. Joseph had been placed in the prison where the king's prisoners were held. Now the king's prisoners arrived. God's timing and placement were precise. Sometimes we wonder why we are where we are. Joseph might have wondered why he was in this particular prison. Now he would see. God had placed him there to meet these officials. Our current location may be preparing us for an encounter we cannot foresee. Esther was told, who knoweth whether thou art come to the kingdom for such a time as this? Today, consider that God may have placed you where you are for a reason. Be faithful and attentive. Someone may come into your life through your current circumstances who will change everything.
S: Pharaoh's officials were put into the same prison where Joseph was bound. God had placed Joseph exactly where he needed to be to meet them. Consider that God may have placed you where you are for a reason, and stay attentive.

== 40:4
T: Serving those in need
R: 'And the captain of the guard charged Joseph with them, and he served them: and they continued a season in ward.' The captain of the guard assigned Joseph to serve the two officials. And Joseph served them. Though he was unjustly imprisoned, he faithfully attended to the needs of others. Joseph did not ask, Why should I serve them when no one is serving me? He did his duty. Serving others while we ourselves are suffering is a mark of mature faith. Jesus washed His disciples' feet on the night He was betrayed. He served even as He faced the cross. Philippians 2 says, Look not every man on his own things, but every man also on the things of others. Joseph's service to these officials would eventually lead to his release. But he did not serve them for that reason. He served because that was who he was. Today, look for someone you can serve, even if you are going through your own difficulties. Service lifts our eyes off ourselves and onto others.

== 40:5
T: Dreams in the night
R: 'And they dreamed a dream both of them, each man his dream in one night, each man according to the interpretation of his dream.' On the same night, both officials had dreams. Each dream had its own meaning. God was speaking through dreams again, as He had to Joseph years before. God often speaks in the night. Jacob dreamed at Bethel. Joseph dreamed of sheaves and stars. Now Pharaoh's officials dreamed in prison. God has many ways of communicating, and dreams were one of them in the ancient world. Job says God speaks in a dream, in a vision of the night, when deep sleep falleth upon men. Though God most clearly speaks to us today through His word and through Jesus Christ, He is still able to get our attention in many ways. Hebrews 1 says God spoke in times past in many ways, but has in these last days spoken unto us by his Son. Today, listen for God. Read His word attentively. He is speaking, and He wants you to hear.

Joseph, though suffering himself, noticed the suffering of others. Compassion begins with noticing. Many people go through life without really seeing those around them. Jesus noticed people: the widow at Nain, the woman who touched His garment, Zacchaeus in the tree. He saw their needs. Romans 12 says, weep with them that weep. To do that, we must first notice who is weeping. Today, pay attention to the people around you. Look at their faces. Notice when someone seems troubled. Your attentiveness may open the door to helping them in ways you cannot yet imagine.

== 40:8
T: Interpretations belong to God
R: 'And they said unto him, We have dreamed a dream, and there is no interpreter of it. And Joseph said unto them, Do not interpretations belong to God? tell me them, I pray you.' The officials explained that they had dreams but no one to interpret them. Joseph's response was humble and confident: Do not interpretations belong to God? He did not claim to have special powers. He pointed to God as the source of understanding. Then he offered to listen. Joseph's humility is striking. He gave God the credit before he even heard the dreams. This is the right posture for anyone with gifts. Our abilities come from God. Daniel later said the same thing to Nebuchadnezzar: there is a God in heaven that revealeth secrets. James says, If any of you lack wisdom, let him ask of God. Today, when you face something you do not understand, look to God for wisdom. And when God gives you insight to share with others, give Him the credit.
S: The officials said they had dreamed but had no interpreter, and Joseph said that interpretations belong to God. He pointed to God as the source of understanding before hearing the dreams. Look to God for wisdom today and give Him credit for any insight He gives.

The butler's dream of a vine would bring news of restoration and life. Vines produce fruit when they are healthy and connected. Branches cut off from the vine wither. Our spiritual life depends on our connection to Christ. Today, examine your connection to Jesus, the true vine. Are you abiding in Him? Stay connected through prayer, His word, and fellowship. Fruitfulness flows from abiding.

Joseph's situation would change suddenly too, though not as quickly as he hoped. Today, if you have been waiting for growth or change, do not lose heart. God can move swiftly when His time comes. Restoration is a beautiful theme in Scripture. God restores what has been lost. The prodigal son was restored to his father's house. Peter was restored after denying Jesus three times. At the Last Supper, Jesus took the cup and said, This cup is the new testament in my blood. Through Christ, we are restored to fellowship with God. Today, if you have failed, know that God is a God of restoration. He can bring you back into His service and His fellowship.

Joseph was forgotten by the butler, but not by God. Today, if you feel forgotten by others, remember that God has engraved you on the palms of His hands. He has not forgotten you.
