# Genesis 41:1-28: Pharaoh's dreams

== 41:1
T: Two full years
R: 'And it came to pass at the end of two full years, that Pharaoh dreamed: and, behold, he stood by the river.' Two full years passed after the butler forgot Joseph. Two more years in prison. Joseph had hoped for quick release, but God had a different timeline. Then Pharaoh dreamed. God's timing is precise. If the butler had remembered Joseph immediately, Joseph might have been released but sent home or forgotten. Instead, God waited until Pharaoh himself needed Joseph. The two-year delay positioned Joseph not merely for release but for promotion. Delays in our lives often serve purposes we cannot see. What feels like God forgetting may be God preparing. Psalm 27 says, Wait on the LORD: be of good courage, and he shall strengthen thine heart. Joseph's two years of waiting were not wasted. They were part of God's perfect timing. Today, if you are in an extended season of waiting, take heart. God has not forgotten you. He is preparing the right moment.
S: Two full years after the butler forgot Joseph, Pharaoh dreamed by the river. The delay positioned Joseph not merely for release but for promotion. If you are in a long season of waiting, take heart that God is preparing the right moment.
P: Delays serve God's purposes. Genesis 41:1: 'at the end of two full years, that Pharaoh dreamed.' Two more years in prison prepared Joseph for the throne. God's timing is perfect.

== 41:2
T: Seven fat cows
R: 'And, behold, there came up out of the river seven well favoured kine and fatfleshed; and they fed in a meadow.' Pharaoh dreamed of seven healthy, fat cows coming up from the Nile and grazing in a meadow. The Nile was Egypt's lifeline. Its annual flooding brought fertility. Fat cows emerging from it represented abundance and prosperity. God was about to reveal the future through this image. Seasons of abundance are gifts from God. James says, Every good gift and every perfect gift is from above. When we experience times of plenty, we should recognize God as the source and be grateful. Yet abundance also brings responsibility. What we do in seasons of plenty prepares us for seasons of scarcity. Proverbs speaks of the ant, which provideth her meat in the summer. Wise people use times of abundance to prepare for harder times. Today, if you are in a season of plenty, thank God for it. And ask Him for wisdom to steward it well, preparing for whatever lies ahead.
S: Pharaoh dreamed of seven healthy, fat cows coming up from the river to graze. Seasons of abundance are gifts from God that carry responsibility. Thank God for seasons of plenty and ask for wisdom to steward them well.
P: Abundance is a gift and a responsibility. Genesis 41:2: 'seven well favoured kine and fatfleshed.' Thank God for plenty and steward it wisely today.

== 41:3
T: Seven lean cows
R: 'And, behold, seven other kine came up after them out of the river, ill favoured and leanfleshed; and stood by the other kine upon the brink of the river.' After the fat cows came seven thin, sickly cows. They stood beside the healthy ones. The contrast was stark. Abundance was followed by scarcity. Life has seasons. Ecclesiastes says there is a time to plant, and a time to pluck up. Seasons of plenty are often followed by seasons of lack. This is not pessimism; it is realism. Wise people prepare for change. The lean cows standing beside the fat ones suggest that hard times can come even while good times are still present. Signs of change may appear before abundance ends. Proverbs says, A prudent man foreseeth the evil, and hideth himself. Today, consider whether you are prepared for changing seasons. Are you living wisely in good times? Ask God for wisdom to recognize seasons and prepare for what may come.
S: Seven thin, sickly cows came up after the fat ones and stood beside them. Seasons of abundance are often followed by seasons of lack. Ask God for wisdom to recognize changing seasons and prepare for what may come.
P: Seasons change. Genesis 41:3: 'seven other kine came up after them… ill favoured and leanfleshed.' Plenty can give way to lack. Prepare wisely today.

== 41:4
T: Devoured
R: 'And the ill favoured and leanfleshed kine did eat up the seven well favoured and fat kine. So Pharaoh awoke.' The thin cows ate the fat cows. This was strange and disturbing. Pharaoh woke up troubled. The image suggested that coming hardship would consume previous abundance. When hard times come, they can quickly use up what was gained in good times. Savings disappear. Resources dwindle. What seemed plentiful suddenly is not enough. This is why preparation matters. Without planning, abundance can be devoured by scarcity. But this dream also contained a hidden mercy. God was warning Pharaoh in advance. He was giving Egypt the chance to prepare. God often warns before hardship comes. He gave Noah 120 years of warning. He sent prophets to Israel before exile. His warnings are acts of mercy. Today, pay attention to God's warnings in your life. They may come through His word, wise counsel, or circumstances. Heed them, and prepare.
S: The thin cows ate up the fat cows, and Pharaoh awoke troubled. The dream warned that hard times would consume previous abundance. Pay attention to God's warnings, which are acts of mercy, and prepare accordingly.
P: God's warnings are mercy. Genesis 41:4: 'the ill favoured and leanfleshed kine did eat up the seven well favoured and fat kine.' God warned Egypt in advance. Heed His warnings today.

== 41:5
T: A second dream
R: 'And he slept and dreamed the second time: and, behold, seven ears of corn came up upon one stalk, rank and good.' Pharaoh fell asleep again and had a second dream. This time, seven full, healthy heads of grain grew on one stalk. The message was the same, but the image was different. God was confirming His message. When God repeats Himself, it is for emphasis and certainty. Joseph had two dreams as a teenager. Now Pharaoh had two dreams. God gave a double witness. Deuteronomy says, at the mouth of two witnesses, or at the mouth of three witnesses, shall the matter be established. God often confirms His word through multiple sources. If God is speaking to you about something, He may confirm it through Scripture, through prayer, through wise counsel, and through circumstances. Today, if you sense God speaking, look for confirmation. And when He confirms His word, take it seriously. Repetition is God's way of saying, Pay attention.
S: Pharaoh slept again and dreamed of seven full, good ears of grain on one stalk. God repeated His message to confirm it. When God confirms His word through multiple witnesses, take it seriously.

== 41:6
T: The east wind
R: 'And, behold, seven thin ears and blasted with the east wind sprung up after them.' After the healthy grain, seven thin, scorched heads appeared, withered by the east wind. In the Middle East, the east wind came from the desert, hot and dry. It could destroy crops in a single day. The east wind appears in Scripture as a symbol of judgment and hardship. Hosea speaks of an east wind coming from the wilderness, drying up springs. Yet God also controlled the east wind. He used an east wind to part the Red Sea. What brings destruction can also bring deliverance when God commands it. God is sovereign over every wind, both the ones that destroy and the ones that deliver. Psalm 135 says He bringeth the wind out of his treasuries. Today, whatever winds are blowing in your life, remember that God controls them. He is sovereign over hardship and blessing alike. Trust Him with the winds you cannot control.
S: After the good grain came seven thin ears scorched by the east wind. The east wind could destroy crops, yet God also used it to part the Red Sea. Trust God, who is sovereign over every wind in your life.

Pharaoh was troubled. He called all of Egypt's magicians and wise men. Egypt was famous for its wisdom and occult knowledge. But none of them could interpret the dreams. Human wisdom has limits. The best minds of Egypt were stumped. Only God could reveal the meaning. This sets the stage for Joseph. When human wisdom fails, God's wisdom shines. First Corinthians says, God hath chosen the foolish things of the world to confound the wise. Daniel faced the same situation in Babylon, when the wise men could not interpret Nebuchadnezzar's dream. God revealed it to Daniel. Today, recognize the limits of human wisdom. When you face problems beyond human understanding, turn to God. His wisdom surpasses all. James says, If any of you lack wisdom, let him ask of God.

He began by confessing: I do remember my faults this day. He acknowledged that he had forgotten Joseph. Late remembering is better than never remembering. The butler's memory, though delayed, came at exactly the right moment. If he had remembered earlier, Joseph might not have been in position for this opportunity. God used even the butler's forgetfulness for His purposes. Notice also the butler's confession. He admitted his faults. Confession often precedes blessing. When we acknowledge our failures, God can work through us. First John says, If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us. Today, if you have forgotten to do something you promised, it is not too late. Remember it now. Confess your failure, and make it right. God can use even your delayed faithfulness.

== 41:28
T: God shows what He is about to do

Twice in this conversation, Joseph emphasized that God was behind the coming events. He wanted Pharaoh to understand that both the plenty and the famine were in God's hands. This does not mean God is the author of sin, but that He is sovereign over all circumstances. Today, trust that God is sovereign over your seasons. Whether in plenty or famine, He is in control.
