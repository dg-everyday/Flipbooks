# Genesis 41:29-57: Joseph over Egypt

== 41:29
T: Great plenty is coming
R: 'Behold, there come seven years of great plenty throughout all the land of Egypt.' Joseph announced seven years of great abundance throughout Egypt. This was a season of blessing that God was sending. It would be a time of overflowing harvests. God is generous. He sends seasons of plenty. Deuteronomy describes God's blessings: Blessed shalt thou be in the city, and blessed shalt thou be in the field. God delights to bless. Yet seasons of plenty are meant to be stewarded, not squandered. Many people waste abundance because they assume it will last forever. The rich fool in Jesus' parable built bigger barns to store his wealth, thinking he had many years of ease ahead. But God said, Thou fool, this night thy soul shall be required of thee. Abundance should lead us to gratitude, generosity, and wise preparation, not complacency. Today, if God has blessed you with plenty, thank Him. Then ask how to use this season wisely, for His purposes and for others.
S: Joseph announced seven years of great plenty throughout all the land of Egypt. Seasons of abundance are God's generous gifts, meant to be stewarded rather than squandered. Thank God for His blessings and ask how to use them wisely for His purposes.
P: Plenty is a gift to steward. Genesis 41:29: 'there come seven years of great plenty throughout all the land of Egypt.' Use abundance with gratitude and wisdom today.

== 41:30
T: Plenty forgotten
R: 'And there shall arise after them seven years of famine; and all the plenty shall be forgotten in the land of Egypt; and the famine shall consume the land.' After the plenty, famine would come. And it would be so severe that the plenty would be forgotten. People would not even remember the good years. Hard seasons can erase our memory of good ones. When we are struggling, it can feel as though things have always been hard. We forget God's past blessings. This is why remembering is so important in Scripture. God repeatedly told Israel to remember what He had done. Deuteronomy 8 says, thou shalt remember all the way which the LORD thy God led thee. Psalm 103 says, forget not all his benefits. Remembering God's faithfulness in the past gives us hope in hard times. Today, write down some of the ways God has blessed you. Keep a record. When hard seasons come, read it. Do not let famine make you forget God's faithfulness.
S: Joseph said seven years of famine would follow and all the plenty would be forgotten. Hard seasons can erase our memory of God's past blessings. Keep a record of God's faithfulness so that hard times do not make you forget.
P: Don't forget God's benefits. Genesis 41:30: 'all the plenty shall be forgotten in the land of Egypt.' Hard times can erase good memories. Write down God's blessings today.

== 41:31
T: Very grievous
R: 'And the plenty shall not be known in the land by reason of that famine following; for it shall be very grievous.' Joseph emphasized how severe the famine would be. Very grievous. The years of abundance would be swallowed up. Joseph did not minimize the coming hardship. Some trials are very grievous. Scripture does not pretend otherwise. Life includes real suffering, deep loss, and severe hardship. The Bible is honest about pain. Job, Lamentations, and many psalms express raw grief. Jesus wept. Paul spoke of being pressed out of measure, above strength, insomuch that we despaired even of life. Yet in all these accounts, God is present. Second Corinthians continues, we had the sentence of death in ourselves, that we should not trust in ourselves, but in God which raiseth the dead. Grievous trials teach us to depend on God. Today, if you are in a very grievous season, know that God sees and understands. Bring your pain to Him honestly. He is the God who raises the dead.
S: Joseph emphasized that the famine would be very grievous and would swallow up the years of plenty. Scripture is honest about severe suffering, yet God is present in it. If you are in a grievous season, bring your pain to the God who raises the dead.
P: Some trials are very grievous. Genesis 41:31: 'for it shall be very grievous.' Scripture is honest about pain. Bring yours to the God who raises the dead today.

== 41:32
T: Established by God
R: 'And for that the dream was doubled unto Pharaoh twice; it is because the thing is established by God, and God will shortly bring it to pass.' Joseph explained why Pharaoh had two dreams. The repetition meant that God had firmly decided this, and it would happen soon. What God establishes is certain. God's purposes cannot be overturned. Isaiah 46 says, My counsel shall stand, and I will do all my pleasure. When God has spoken, the matter is settled. This is both sobering and comforting. Sobering, because God's warnings will come to pass. Comforting, because God's promises will too. Every promise God has made to you in Christ is established. Second Corinthians says, all the promises of God in him are yea, and in him Amen. Nothing can stop them. Today, rest in the certainty of God's word. What He has established will come to pass. His promises to you are as sure as His warning to Pharaoh. Trust Him completely.
S: Joseph explained that Pharaoh's dream was doubled because the matter was established by God and would soon come to pass. What God establishes is certain and cannot be overturned. Rest today in the certainty of God's promises to you in Christ.

== 41:33
T: A wise man for the task
R: 'Now therefore let Pharaoh look out a man discreet and wise, and set him over the land of Egypt.' Joseph did not stop at interpretation. He offered practical advice: find a wise and discerning man to manage the crisis. Joseph moved from revelation to application. Knowing what is coming is not enough. We must also act wisely. Joseph's advice shows practical wisdom. God's revelation leads to responsible action. James says, be ye doers of the word, and not hearers only. Knowledge without action is incomplete. Notice also what Joseph looked for: a man discreet and wise. Character and wisdom were the qualifications. Not wealth, status, or connections. Proverbs says, Wisdom is the principal thing; therefore get wisdom. Leadership in a crisis requires wisdom above all. Today, when God reveals something to you, ask what action He wants you to take. Move from knowing to doing. And seek wisdom, the most important quality for any task.
S: Joseph advised Pharaoh to find a discreet and wise man and set him over Egypt. He moved from revelation to practical action, valuing wisdom above status. When God reveals something to you, ask what action He wants you to take.

== 41:34
T: A practical plan
R: 'Let Pharaoh do this, and let him appoint officers over the land, and take up the fifth part of the land of Egypt in the seven plenteous years.' Joseph proposed a specific plan: appoint officers and collect one-fifth of the harvest during the good years. This was practical, organized, and wise. Faith and planning work together. Joseph trusted God's revelation, and he also made a concrete plan. Some think faith means passively waiting for God to act. But Scripture shows people of faith making wise plans. Nehemiah prayed and planned. Paul planned his missionary journeys. Jesus told a parable about counting the cost before building. Proverbs says, The thoughts of the diligent tend only to plenteousness. Joseph's plan involved saving twenty percent during abundance. This principle of saving for the future is wise for individuals and nations. Today, combine faith with practical planning. Trust God, and also make wise plans for your future. Both honor Him.
S: Joseph proposed appointing officers to collect a fifth of the harvest during the plentiful years. Faith and practical planning work together. Combine trust in God with wise planning for your future today.

== 41:35
T: Gathering in good years
R: 'And let them gather all the food of those good years that come, and lay up corn under the hand of Pharaoh, and let them keep food in the cities.' Joseph advised storing food in the cities during the good years. The time to prepare for famine is during abundance. Waiting until the famine arrives would be too late. This principle applies to many areas of life. We build spiritual strength before trials come. We save money before emergencies. We invest in relationships before we need support. Proverbs 6 says, Go to the ant, thou sluggard; consider her ways, and be wise: Which… Provideth her meat in the summer, and gathereth her food in the harvest. The ant prepares in summer for winter. Spiritually, we should store up God's word in our hearts during calm seasons so that we have it in storms. Psalm 119 says, Thy word have I hid in mine heart. Today, use your good seasons to prepare. Store up God's word, build relationships, and save wisely.

== 41:36
T: That the land perish not
R: 'And that food shall be for store to the land against the seven years of famine, which shall be in the land of Egypt; that the land perish not through the famine.' Joseph's plan had one goal: that the land perish not. The stored food would save lives during the famine. His plan was about preservation and compassion. Good planning protects people. Joseph was not storing food for profit but for survival. His motivation was to keep people alive. This points to God's heart. God does not want people to perish. Peter writes that God is not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance. Jesus came so that whoever believes in Him should not perish but have everlasting life. Joseph's storehouses saved people from physical death. Christ saves people from eternal death. Today, consider how you can use your resources to help others survive and thrive. And thank God that in Christ, He has provided for your eternal survival.
S: Joseph explained that the stored food would keep the land from perishing during the famine. His plan was about preservation and compassion, reflecting God's desire that none should perish. Use your resources to help others and thank God for eternal life in Christ.

When we speak with God's wisdom, it can persuade even unbelievers. Daniel's wisdom impressed Babylonian kings. Paul's reasoning engaged Greek philosophers. God's truth has a way of resonating with people because it reflects reality as God made it. Colossians says, Walk in wisdom toward them that are without. Our wisdom and good conduct can open doors for the gospel. Today, seek God's wisdom in your decisions and conversations. Let your counsel be so sound that even those who do not share your faith recognize its value. Wise words can open hearts.

== 41:38
T: A man with God's Spirit
R: 'And Pharaoh said unto his servants, Can we find such a one as this is, a man in whom the Spirit of God is?' Pharaoh recognized something extraordinary in Joseph. He asked, Can we find anyone like this, a man in whom is the Spirit of God? Even a pagan king could see God's presence in Joseph's life. This is a remarkable testimony. Joseph had been a slave and prisoner for thirteen years. Yet the Spirit of God was evident in him. Hardship had not diminished God's presence; it had refined it. The Spirit of God makes a visible difference in people's lives. Galatians lists the fruit of the Spirit: love, joy, peace, longsuffering, gentleness, goodness, faith, meekness, temperance. When these qualities are present, others notice. Acts 4 says the council saw the boldness of Peter and John and took knowledge of them, that they had been with Jesus. Today, ask God to fill you with His Spirit. Let His presence be so evident in your life that others recognize it.

True wisdom comes from God. James says, If any of you lack wisdom, let him ask of God, that giveth to all men liberally. Joseph's wisdom was not merely natural intelligence. It was God-given insight. Proverbs 2 says, the LORD giveth wisdom: out of his mouth cometh knowledge and understanding. When we seek wisdom from God, He gives generously. Habakkuk 2 says, though it tarry, wait for it; because it will surely come, it will not tarry. Today, if God has given you a promise that seems far from fulfillment, keep trusting. == 41:45
T: A new name and a new family
R: 'And Pharaoh called Joseph's name Zaphnath-paaneah; and he gave him to wife Asenath,' the daughter of a priest of On. 'And Joseph went out over all the land of Egypt.' Pharaoh gave Joseph an Egyptian name, Zaphnath-paaneah, often understood to mean something like revealer of secrets or the God speaks and lives. Joseph also received a wife, Asenath. Our citizenship is in heaven. Today, live faithfully in your culture without losing your identity in Christ. Engage the world while remaining anchored in God.
