# Genesis 43: The second journey with Benjamin

== 43:1
T: The famine continues
R: 'And the famine was sore in the land.' Seven short words describe a heavy reality. The famine did not end. It pressed harder on Jacob's family. The grain they had brought from Egypt would not last forever. Some hardships linger longer than we hoped. We pray, we wait, and the difficulty remains. Yet the continuing famine was moving Jacob toward a decision he had refused to make. Pressure can push us toward the very step God wants us to take. Jacob had said Benjamin would not go. The famine would change his mind. God sometimes allows circumstances to remain hard until we are willing to trust Him. Second Corinthians 4 says, We are troubled on every side, yet not distressed; we are perplexed, but not in despair. Today, if a hard season is dragging on, ask God what step He may be calling you to take. The pressure may be pointing you toward His provision.
S: The famine remained severe in the land and pressed harder on Jacob's family. Lingering hardship was moving Jacob toward a decision he had refused to make. If a hard season drags on, ask God what step He may be calling you to take.
P: Hardship can move us toward God's plan. Genesis 43:1: 'And the famine was sore in the land.' The pressure pushed Jacob toward a needed step. Ask God what yours is today.

== 43:2
T: Go again
R: 'And it came to pass, when they had eaten up the corn which they had brought out of Egypt, their father said unto them, Go again, buy us a little food.' The grain ran out, and Jacob told his sons to go again. He asked only for a little food. He seemed to hope he could avoid the hard condition about Benjamin. We often try to meet our needs without facing the real issue. Jacob wanted food without risking Benjamin. But the governor had made the terms clear. Some problems cannot be solved by avoiding their central demand. God sometimes waits for us to face the very thing we fear. Hebrews 12 says, lift up the hands which hang down, and the feeble knees; And make straight paths for your feet. Going again would require courage. Today, consider whether you are trying to solve a problem while avoiding its heart. Ask God for courage to face what you have been sidestepping.

== 43:3
T: Judah speaks up
R: 'And Judah spake unto him, saying, The man did solemnly protest unto us, saying, Ye shall not see my face, except your brother be with you.' Judah stepped forward and spoke plainly to his father. The governor had been clear: without Benjamin, they would not see his face. Judah did not soften the truth. This is a new Judah. Years earlier, he had suggested selling Joseph. Now he was taking leadership with honesty. Telling hard truths to those we love requires courage. Judah respected his father enough to be honest. Love does not hide reality to avoid discomfort. Proverbs 27 says, Faithful are the wounds of a friend. Honest words, spoken with respect, help people face decisions they would rather avoid. Today, if someone you love needs to hear a hard truth, ask God for the courage and gentleness to speak it. Honest love opens the way forward.
S: Judah reminded his father that the governor had said they would not see his face without their brother. The man who once suggested selling Joseph now led with honesty. Ask God for courage and gentleness to speak hard truths to those you love.

== 43:4
T: Clear conditions
R: 'If thou wilt send our brother with us, we will go down and buy thee food.' Judah laid out the condition clearly. If Benjamin came, they would go. He made the choice plain for his father. Clarity helps people decide. Vague discussions keep us stuck. Judah set the options before Jacob simply. There is wisdom in naming choices clearly, especially in family decisions. God also sets clear choices before us. Deuteronomy 30 says, I have set before you life and death, blessing and cursing: therefore choose life. God does not leave us in confusion about the path of life. He makes it clear in His word. Our part is to choose. Jacob's choice would determine whether his family lived. Today, if you face a decision, ask God to make the options clear. And when He does, choose the path that leads to life.

== 43:5
T: Firm but respectful
R: 'But if thou wilt not send him, we will not go down: for the man said unto us, Ye shall not see my face, except your brother be with you.' Judah was firm. Without Benjamin, they would not go. There was no point. Judah respected his father, but he would not pretend the trip could succeed on Jacob's terms. Sometimes love requires firmness. Giving people what they want is not always loving. Judah would not enable his father's fear at the cost of the family's survival. Firm boundaries, spoken respectfully, can lead to better decisions. Ephesians 4 speaks of speaking the truth in love. Both truth and love were present in Judah's words. He was not harsh, but he was clear. Today, if you need to hold a firm line with someone you love, do it with respect. Firmness and kindness can go together.

== 43:6
T: Why did you tell?
R: 'And Israel said, Wherefore dealt ye so ill with me, as to tell the man whether ye had yet a brother?' Jacob, here called Israel, complained that his sons had told the governor about Benjamin. Why did you treat me so badly? He looked for someone to blame. Frustration often seeks a target. When we face painful choices, we can blame others for putting us there. Jacob was hurting, and his hurt came out as accusation. Yet his sons had simply answered honestly. Blame rarely helps. It adds tension without solving anything. Scripture calls him Israel here, perhaps reminding us that the one who wrestled with God was now wrestling with fear. Even mature believers have moments of weakness. Psalm 103 says, he knoweth our frame; he remembereth that we are dust. Today, if you catch yourself blaming others for a hard situation, pause. Bring your frustration to God instead. He understands your weakness.

The brothers explained. The governor had asked direct questions about their family, and they had answered truthfully. They could not have known he would demand Benjamin. Honest answers sometimes lead to complicated situations. But honesty is still right. The brothers had once lived by lies. Now they were learning to tell the truth, even when it brought difficulty. Notice the governor's question: Is your father yet alive? Joseph was asking about his own father. His heart was longing to know. The brothers did not realize the love behind the questions. Today, choose honesty even when you cannot predict where it will lead. And remember that God's questions in your life often come from a heart of love.

Send the boy with me. He reminded Jacob that the whole family's life was at stake: the brothers, Jacob himself, and the little ones. Judah thought about the children. Good leaders consider those most vulnerable. Judah's appeal was not selfish. He was thinking about the whole family. Holding on to Benjamin might feel safe, but it would mean starvation for everyone. Sometimes what feels safe is actually dangerous. Sometimes the risk of faith is the path to life. Jesus said, whosoever will save his life shall lose it. == 43:9
T: I will be surety
R: 'I will be surety for him; of my hand shalt thou require him: if I bring him not unto thee, and set him before thee, then let me bear the blame for ever.' Judah offered himself as a guarantee for Benjamin. I will be surety for him. If Benjamin did not return, Judah would bear the blame forever. Unlike Reuben, who offered his sons, Judah offered himself. This is a turning point in Judah's life. The brother who sold Joseph now offered his own life for Benjamin. Judah, ancestor of Jesus, offered himself for his brother. Christ, the Lion of Judah, gave Himself for us. Today, thank Jesus for being your surety. He took full responsibility for your debt and bore your blame.

Lingering can be a quiet form of disobedience. Today, consider whether you have been lingering over something God has made clear. Do not let fear keep you waiting. Act now, before more time is lost.

It must have been one of the hardest things he ever did. Releasing what we love is one of the deepest acts of trust. Abraham released Isaac on Mount Moriah. Hannah released Samuel to serve in the temple. Jacob released Benjamin into an uncertain future. In each case, God honored the surrender. Jacob did not know that releasing Benjamin would lead to receiving Joseph back. Surrender often opens the door to restoration we cannot imagine. Jesus said, there is no man that hath left house, or brethren… for my sake, and the gospel's, But he shall receive an hundredfold. Today, if God is asking you to release something or someone you love, trust Him. Your surrender may open the door to blessing you cannot yet see.
