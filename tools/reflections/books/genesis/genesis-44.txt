# Genesis 44: The silver cup and Judah's plea

== 44:1
T: Generous before the test
R: 'And he commanded the steward of his house, saying, Fill the men's sacks with food, as much as they can carry, and put every man's money in his sack's mouth.' Joseph ordered his steward to fill the sacks with as much food as the men could carry and to return their money again. Even as Joseph prepared a final test, he was generous. The brothers would leave with more than they paid for. God's tests often come wrapped in grace. He does not test us to destroy us but to reveal and refine. And even in testing, He provides abundantly. Joseph's generosity shows that his test was not cruelty but love. Lamentations 3 says, though he cause grief, yet will he have compassion according to the multitude of his mercies. God's discipline is always mixed with mercy. Today, if you are in a season of testing, look for God's provision within it. He is generous even when He tests. His heart toward you is kind.
S: Joseph ordered his steward to fill the men's sacks with as much food as they could carry and return their money again. Even as he prepared a final test, he was generous. Look for God's generous provision within your season of testing.

== 44:2
T: The silver cup
R: 'And put my cup, the silver cup, in the sack's mouth of the youngest, and his corn money. And he did according to the word that Joseph had spoken.' Joseph had his silver cup placed in Benjamin's sack. This was the final test. Would the brothers abandon Benjamin to slavery to save themselves, as they had abandoned Joseph? Or would they stand with him? Benjamin was the new favored son, just as Joseph had been. The situation was set up to mirror the past. Would history repeat itself? Tests reveal the heart. This test would show whether the brothers had truly changed. They had confessed their guilt. But would their actions match their words? James 2 says, faith without works is dead. Real change shows in what we do when it costs us. Today, consider how you would respond if tested in the area where you once failed. Ask God to make your change real, not just in words but in actions that cost you something.
S: Joseph had his silver cup placed in Benjamin's sack as a final test. Would the brothers abandon Rachel's son as they had abandoned Joseph? Ask God to make your change real, not only in words but in costly actions.

== 44:4
T: Evil for good
R: 'And when they were gone out of the city, and not yet far off, Joseph said unto his steward, Up, follow after the men; and when thou dost overtake them, say unto them, Wherefore have ye rewarded evil for good?' Joseph sent his steward after the brothers with an accusation: Why have you repaid good with evil? The words were part of the test, but they carried deep truth. Twenty years earlier, the brothers had indeed repaid good with evil. Joseph had come seeking their welfare, and they sold him. Proverbs 17 says, Whoso rewardeth evil for good, evil shall not depart from his house. God calls us to the opposite: to return good for evil. Romans 12 says, Recompense to no man evil for evil… overcome evil with good. Jesus taught, Love your enemies, bless them that curse you, do good to them that hate you. Today, examine whether you have repaid anyone's kindness with harm. Make it right. And when others wrong you, return good instead.

The brothers now experienced what Joseph had experienced with Potiphar's wife. They were innocent of this charge, yet accused. God sometimes allows us to experience what we caused others to suffer. This is not cruel revenge but a path to empathy and understanding. The brothers would learn what it felt like to be powerless and wrongly accused. First Peter 2 speaks of Christ, who when he was reviled, reviled not again. Today, if you are falsely accused, remember Jesus. He was accused unjustly and responded with grace. Let His example guide your response.
