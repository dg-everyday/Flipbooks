# Genesis 46: Jacob goes down to Egypt

== 46:1
T: Worship before the journey
R: 'And Israel took his journey with all that he had, and came to Beer-sheba, and offered sacrifices unto the God of his father Isaac.' Jacob set out for Egypt, but first he stopped at Beer-sheba to worship. This was the place where Abraham and Isaac had met with God. Before leaving the promised land, Jacob sought God. Worship before major decisions is wise. Jacob was about to leave Canaan, the land God had promised. Was it right to go? Jacob did not assume. He paused to seek God. Proverbs 3 says, In all thy ways acknowledge him, and he shall direct thy paths. Notice that Jacob offered sacrifices to the God of his father Isaac. He connected his journey to his family's history with God. Today, before your next big step, stop and worship. Seek God's guidance and presence. Let your decisions flow from time spent with Him.
S: Israel set out with all he had and stopped at Beer-sheba to offer sacrifices to the God of his father Isaac. Before leaving the promised land, he paused to worship and seek God. Before your next big step, stop and seek God's guidance in worship.
P: Worship before you go. Genesis 46:1: 'came to Beer-sheba, and offered sacrifices unto the God of his father Isaac.' Seek God before your next big step today.

== 46:2
T: Here am I, once more
R: 'And God spake unto Israel in the visions of the night, and said, Jacob, Jacob. And he said, Here am I.' God answered Jacob's worship with a vision. He called him by name twice: Jacob, Jacob. And Jacob responded, Here am I. After all these years, Jacob still answered God with readiness. God calls His people by name. A double calling shows urgency and affection. God called Abraham, Abraham at Moriah, and Samuel, Samuel in the temple. Each time, the response was the same: Here am I. This is the response of a willing heart. Jacob had come a long way since Bethel. Once he bargained with God; now he simply made himself available. Isaiah 43 says, I have called thee by thy name; thou art mine. Today, listen for God calling your name. He knows you personally. When He calls, answer as Jacob did: Here am I. Make yourself available for whatever He asks.
S: God spoke to Israel in a night vision, calling him Jacob, Jacob, and he answered, Here am I. After all his years, Jacob responded to God with a ready heart. Listen for God calling your name and answer with availability.
P: God calls you by name. Genesis 46:2: 'Jacob, Jacob. And he said, Here am I.' Answer God with a ready and willing heart today.

== 46:3
T: Fear not to go down
R: 'And he said, I am God, the God of thy father: fear not to go down into Egypt; for I will there make of thee a great nation.' God spoke directly to Jacob's fear. Fear not to go down into Egypt. Jacob may have been afraid. His grandfather Abraham had gotten into trouble in Egypt. And God had told Isaac not to go there. Was this right? God reassured him: I will make of thee a great nation there. God speaks to our specific fears. Jacob's concern was valid, but God answered it directly. He revealed that Egypt was part of His plan. The family would grow into a nation there. What seemed like leaving the promise was actually advancing it. God's plans sometimes lead us to unexpected places. Isaiah 41 says, Fear thou not; for I am with thee. Today, if you are afraid of a step God is calling you to take, listen for His reassurance. He knows your fear. And He may be leading you somewhere unexpected to fulfill His promise.

== 46:4
T: I will go down with thee
R: 'I will go down with thee into Egypt; and I will also surely bring thee up again: and Joseph shall put his hand upon thine eyes.' God made three promises. I will go down with thee. I will surely bring thee up again. Joseph will close your eyes when you die. Jacob would not go alone. God would be with him in Egypt and would bring his descendants back. And Jacob would die peacefully with Joseph beside him. The promise of God's presence is the greatest comfort. Wherever we go, God goes with us. Psalm 139 says, If I ascend up into heaven, thou art there: if I make my bed in hell, behold, thou art there. There is no place beyond God's presence. The promise to bring them up again was fulfilled in the Exodus. God keeps His promises across centuries. Today, rest in the promise that God goes with you. Whatever unknown place you are heading into, He is there. And He will bring you through.

== 46:5
T: Carried in the wagons
R: 'And Jacob rose up from Beer-sheba: and the sons of Israel carried Jacob their father, and their little ones, and their wives, in the wagons which Pharaoh had sent to carry him.' With God's assurance, Jacob rose up and set out. His sons carried him, the children, and the wives in the wagons Pharaoh had sent. The aged patriarch was carried by his sons. There is something beautiful about this picture. The sons who once caused their father so much grief now carried him with care. Their relationship had been restored. They honored him in his old age. Caring for aging parents is a sacred responsibility. First Timothy 5 says, if any provide not for his own, and specially for those of his own house, he hath denied the faith. The brothers now cared for their father as they should have all along. Today, think about the elderly people in your life. How can you carry them, support them, and honor them? Care for them as Jacob's sons did.

Psalm 103 says, He hath not dealt with us after our sins; nor rewarded us according to our iniquities. Reuben's sons received a place in God's plan. Today, if you have failed and lost something because of it, take heart. God has not cast you aside. His grace keeps you in His family.

R: 'And the sons of Levi; Gershon, Kohath, and Merari.' Levi had three sons: Gershon, Kohath, and Merari. These names would become very important. Their descendants became the Levites, the tribe set apart for service at the tabernacle and temple. Moses and Aaron descended from Kohath. Levi had been violent at Shechem, and Jacob would later rebuke him. Yet God redeemed Levi's line. His descendants became priests and servants of God. God can redeem even our worst failures. Levi's anger led to massacre, but his descendants led Israel in worship. Numbers 3 describes how each family of Levites had specific duties in caring for the tabernacle. God turned a line marked by violence into a line devoted to worship. Second Corinthians 5 says, if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature. Today, take hope that God can redeem your past. What began in failure can become a story of service to Him.

From Pharez and Hezron would come Boaz, Jesse, David, and eventually Jesus. Ruth 4 traces this line exactly: Now these are the generations of Pharez: Pharez begat Hezron… and Jesse begat David. Matthew 1 includes these names in Jesus' genealogy. The messy story of Judah and Tamar produced the line of kings. God works through complicated stories to accomplish His purposes. Today, rejoice that God can bring royal purposes out of messy beginnings. Your story, however complicated, can be part of His plan.

== 46:24
T: The sons of Naphtali
R: 'And the sons of Naphtali; Jahzeel, and Guni, and Jezer, and Shillem.' Naphtali had four sons. His name meant wrestling, from Rachel's struggle with her sister. Yet his descendants would settle in Galilee, where Jesus would minister. Isaiah 9 mentions the land of Naphtali along with Zebulun as the place where people in darkness would see a great light. A name born of rivalry became connected to the light of the world. God transforms the meaning of our stories. Naphtali's name came from family conflict, but his territory became the place where Jesus brought light and healing. Matthew 4 says, The people which sat in darkness saw great light. Our origins do not determine our destiny. God can take what began in struggle and make it a place of His glory. Today, if your story began in conflict or difficulty, take heart. God can transform it into a story of His light and grace.
S: Naphtali's four sons are listed, though his name came from Rachel's rivalry with her sister. His tribe's land in Galilee later saw the great light of Jesus' ministry. Take heart that God can transform stories that began in struggle into stories of His light.
P: From struggle to light. Genesis 46:24: 'the sons of Naphtali.' His name meant wrestling, but his land saw the light of Jesus! God transforms our stories.

== 46:25
T: Bilhah's seven
R: 'These are the sons of Bilhah, which Laban gave unto Rachel his daughter, and she bare these unto Jacob: all the souls were seven.' Bilhah's descendants numbered seven. Like Zilpah, she had been given as a servant. Like Zilpah, she became a mother of tribes. Seven souls came from her line into Egypt. God includes everyone in His purposes. The four mothers of Israel's tribes included two wives and two servants. In God's accounting, all were counted. Their children were equally sons of Israel. This foreshadows the gospel, where social status does not determine our place in God's family. James 2 warns against favoring the rich over the poor. In God's kingdom, the last shall be first. Today, look at the people around you without regard to status. Every person has a place in God's purposes. Treat everyone as someone God values, because He does.
S: Bilhah, whom Laban had given to Rachel as a servant, had seven descendants who came into Egypt. Two wives and two servants all became mothers of Israel's tribes, equally counted. Treat everyone around you as someone God values, regardless of status.
P: Everyone counts in God's family. Genesis 46:25: 'These are the sons of Bilhah… all the souls were seven.' Wives and servants were equally counted. Value everyone today.

== 46:26
T: Sixty-six souls
R: 'All the souls that came with Jacob into Egypt, which came out of his loins, besides Jacob's sons' wives, all the souls were threescore and six.' Sixty-six people came with Jacob into Egypt, all his direct descendants. This number did not include the wives of his sons. A modest family entered Egypt. Yet God had promised Abraham descendants as numerous as the stars. From these sixty-six would come millions. God's promises often start small. Abraham had one son of promise. Isaac had two sons. Jacob had twelve. Now sixty-six went to Egypt. Exodus 1 says the children of Israel were fruitful, and increased abundantly, and multiplied, and waxed exceeding mighty. In Egypt, the family would become a nation. God's timeline is long, but His promises are sure. Today, if God's promise in your life seems small right now, trust Him. He is able to multiply beyond what you can imagine. Small numbers do not limit God.
S: Sixty-six of Jacob's direct descendants came with him into Egypt, not counting his sons' wives. From this modest family God would build a nation of millions. If God's promise in your life seems small now, trust Him to multiply it.
P: God multiplies. Genesis 46:26: 'all the souls were threescore and six.' Sixty-six became a nation of millions! Small numbers don't limit God. Trust His promise today.

== 46:27
T: Seventy souls
R: 'And the sons of Joseph, which were born him in Egypt, were two souls: all the souls of the house of Jacob, which came into Egypt, were threescore and ten.' Including Joseph's two sons and Jacob himself, the total was seventy. Seventy souls of Jacob's house in Egypt. The number seventy echoes the seventy nations of Genesis 10. Israel was a small family meant to bless all nations. Deuteronomy 10 later says, Thy fathers went down into Egypt with threescore and ten persons; and now the LORD thy God hath made thee as the stars of heaven for multitude. Seventy became a multitude. God's faithfulness is shown in the growth from a few to many. Jesus later sent out seventy disciples, perhaps echoing this number. A small group can change the world when God is in it. Today, rejoice that God uses small groups to accomplish great things. Your family, your church, your circle of friends can be part of His world-changing purposes.
S: Including Joseph's two sons, seventy souls of Jacob's house came into Egypt. Deuteronomy says God later made them as numerous as the stars, and the number recalls the nations God meant to bless. Rejoice that God uses small groups to accomplish world-changing purposes.
P: Seventy became a multitude. Genesis 46:27: 'all the souls of the house of Jacob… were threescore and ten.' God made them as the stars! He uses small groups for great things.

== 46:28
T: Judah sent ahead
R: 'And he sent Judah before him unto Joseph, to direct his face unto Goshen; and they came into the land of Goshen.' Jacob sent Judah ahead to Joseph to get directions to Goshen. Judah, who had offered himself for Benjamin, was now trusted to lead the way. Judah had become a leader the family could rely on. Trust is earned through transformed character. Once, Judah had led the brothers in selling Joseph. Now Jacob trusted him to lead the family to Joseph. Judah's sacrificial offer had restored his father's confidence. God restores trust when we demonstrate genuine change. Luke 16 says, He that is faithful in that which is least is faithful also in much. Judah's tribe would later lead Israel. In the wilderness, Judah's tribe marched first. From Judah came David and Jesus. Today, if you have broken trust in the past, take heart. Consistent faithfulness can restore it. And look for opportunities to lead others toward Christ, as Judah led his family to Joseph.
S: Jacob sent Judah ahead to Joseph to get directions to Goshen. Judah, once the leader in selling Joseph, was now trusted to lead the family. Take heart that consistent faithfulness can restore trust, and lead others toward Christ.

== 46:29
T: A father and son reunited
R: 'And Joseph made ready his chariot, and went up to meet Israel his father, to Goshen, and presented himself unto him; and he fell on his neck, and wept on his neck a good while.' Joseph prepared his chariot and rushed to meet his father. When he saw Jacob, he threw his arms around his neck and wept for a long time. After twenty-two years, father and son were reunited. Joseph, the ruler of Egypt, wept on his father's neck. All the years of separation, grief, and longing poured out. Jacob had believed his son was dead. Now he held him in his arms. This scene echoes the parable of the prodigal son. The father saw his son, ran to him, fell on his neck, and kissed him. Our heavenly Father longs to embrace us the same way. Today, rest in the knowledge that God's arms are open to you. And if you have been separated from someone you love, pray for reunion. God specializes in restoration.
S: Joseph rode out to meet his father in Goshen, fell on his neck and wept a good while. After twenty-two years, father and son were reunited. Rest in your heavenly Father's open arms and pray for reunion where you have been separated from loved ones.

== 46:30
T: Now let me die
R: 'And Israel said unto Joseph, Now let me die, since I have seen thy face, because thou art yet alive.' Jacob said, Now I can die, because I have seen your face and you are alive. His deepest longing had been fulfilled. He could die in peace. This echoes the words of Simeon when he held the infant Jesus in the temple. Lord, now lettest thou thy servant depart in peace… For mine eyes have seen thy salvation. Both men had waited for something precious. Both said they could now die in peace. Seeing the face of one we love brings deep satisfaction. For believers, the greatest longing is to see the face of Jesus. First John 3 says, we shall see him as he is. Revelation 22 says, they shall see his face. Today, let the hope of seeing Jesus' face fill you with peace. Like Jacob, you can face anything knowing that the One you love most is alive.
S: Israel told Joseph that he could now die, since he had seen his face and knew he was alive. His deepest longing was fulfilled, echoing Simeon's peace on seeing the infant Jesus. Let the hope of one day seeing Jesus' face fill you with peace.

== 46:31
T: Joseph's plan
R: 'And Joseph said unto his brethren, and unto his father's house, I will go up, and shew Pharaoh, and say unto him, My brethren, and my father's house… are come unto me.' Joseph immediately made plans to present his family to Pharaoh. He wanted to secure their place in Egypt. He would speak on their behalf. Joseph acted as an advocate. He did not leave his family to fend for themselves. He went before Pharaoh to represent them. This is a picture of Christ, who represents us before the Father. First John 2 says, we have an advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the righteous. Hebrews 7 says, he ever liveth to make intercession for them. Joseph was not ashamed to call them my brethren before Pharaoh. Hebrews 2 says Jesus is not ashamed to call us brethren. Today, rejoice that Jesus is your advocate. He represents you before God, and He is not ashamed to call you family.
S: Joseph told his family he would go to Pharaoh and tell him that his brothers and his father's house had come. He acted as their advocate before the king. Rejoice that Jesus is your advocate before the Father and is not ashamed to call you family.

== 46:32
T: Honest about their work
R: 'And the men are shepherds, for their trade hath been to feed cattle; and they have brought their flocks, and their herds, and all that they have.' Joseph planned to tell Pharaoh honestly that his brothers were shepherds. Their trade was raising livestock. He would not hide or disguise their occupation. Joseph was not ashamed of his family's work. Honesty about who we are is important. Joseph could have tried to make his family seem more impressive. Instead, he told the truth. Their work as shepherds was honorable, even if Egyptians looked down on it. Every honest occupation has dignity. Shepherds would later receive the first announcement of Jesus' birth. David was a shepherd. Jesus called Himself the good shepherd. God honors humble work. Colossians 3 says, whatsoever ye do, do it heartily, as to the Lord. Today, do not be ashamed of honest work. Whatever your occupation, it has dignity when done for God's glory.
S: Joseph planned to tell Pharaoh honestly that his brothers were shepherds who raised livestock. He was not ashamed of their humble occupation. Do not be ashamed of honest work, for every occupation done for God's glory has dignity.
P: Honest work has dignity. Genesis 46:32: 'the men are shepherds, for their trade hath been to feed cattle.' Joseph wasn't ashamed. Do your work heartily for the Lord today.

== 46:33
T: When asked
R: 'And it shall come to pass, when Pharaoh shall call you, and shall say, What is your occupation?' Joseph prepared his brothers for Pharaoh's question. He knew Pharaoh would ask what they did. He wanted them ready to answer. Preparation for important conversations is wise. Joseph coached his brothers so they would answer well. He knew the culture and what Pharaoh would ask. This kind of thoughtful preparation helps us communicate effectively. First Peter 3 says, be ready always to give an answer to every man that asketh you a reason of the hope that is in you with meekness and fear. When people ask about our faith, we should be prepared to answer clearly and graciously. Joseph's guidance helped his family navigate a new culture. Today, think about how you would answer if someone asked about your faith. Prepare a simple, honest answer. Be ready to share the hope you have in Christ.

== 46:34
T: Set apart in Goshen
R: 'That ye shall say, Thy servants' trade hath been about cattle from our youth even until now… that ye may dwell in the land of Goshen; for every shepherd is an abomination unto the Egyptians.' Joseph told his brothers to answer honestly that they were herdsmen. This would lead Pharaoh to settle them in Goshen, separate from the Egyptians, who despised shepherds. Their occupation would keep them apart. This separation was protective. In Goshen, Israel could grow as a distinct people without being absorbed into Egyptian culture. Their identity would be preserved. Sometimes God uses separation to protect His people. Being set apart is not always comfortable, but it guards our identity. Second Corinthians 6 says, come out from among them, and be ye separate, saith the Lord. Believers are called to be in the world but not of it. Today, embrace the ways God sets you apart. Your distinct identity as His child is worth protecting, even if it means being different from those around you.
S: Joseph told his brothers to say they were herdsmen so Pharaoh would settle them in Goshen, apart from Egyptians who despised shepherds. Their separation would protect their identity as God's people. Embrace the ways God sets you apart, for your identity in Him is worth protecting.
P: Set apart for protection. Genesis 46:34: 'that ye may dwell in the land of Goshen; for every shepherd is an abomination unto the Egyptians.' God sets His people apart. Embrace it today.
