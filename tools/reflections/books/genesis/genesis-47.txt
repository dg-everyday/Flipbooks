# Genesis 47: Jacob before Pharaoh; the famine in Egypt

== 47:1
T: Speaking for his family
R: 'Then Joseph came and told Pharaoh, and said, My father and my brethren, and their flocks, and their herds, and all that they have, are come out of the land of Canaan; and, behold, they are in the land of Goshen.' Joseph went to Pharaoh and announced his family's arrival. He spoke openly about them: my father and my brethren. Joseph was not ashamed of his shepherd family before the most powerful king on earth. He had risen to great power, but he did not distance himself from his humble roots. He claimed his family publicly. Jesus does the same for us. Hebrews 2 says, he is not ashamed to call them brethren. Before the Father, Christ claims us as His own. Matthew 10 says, Whosoever therefore shall confess me before men, him will I confess also before my Father which is in heaven. Today, do not be ashamed of your faith or your spiritual family before others. Claim them openly, as Joseph did, and as Christ claims you.
S: Joseph told Pharaoh that his father and brothers had come with all they had and were in Goshen. He was not ashamed to claim his humble family before the king. Do not be ashamed of your faith or spiritual family, as Christ is not ashamed of you.
P: Not ashamed of family. Genesis 47:1: 'My father and my brethren… are come out of the land of Canaan.' Joseph claimed them openly. Jesus isn't ashamed to call you His!

== 47:2
T: Five brothers presented
R: 'And he took some of his brethren, even five men, and presented them unto Pharaoh.' Joseph chose five of his brothers to present to Pharaoh. He introduced them personally. These men who had once sold him into slavery were now introduced to the king by the brother they had wronged. This is grace in action. Joseph did not hold their past against them. He sponsored them before Pharaoh, opening the door to blessing. Their future in Egypt depended on Joseph's introduction. This is what Christ does for us. We come before God not on our own merit but through Jesus, who presents us. Jude says Christ is able to present you faultless before the presence of his glory with exceeding joy. Colossians 1 says He will present you holy and unblameable and unreproveable in his sight. Today, rejoice that Jesus presents you to the Father. Your past does not disqualify you. He introduces you as His own.
S: Joseph took five of his brothers and presented them to Pharaoh. The brother they had wronged now opened the door to blessing by introducing them to the king. Rejoice that Jesus presents you faultless before the Father.

== 47:3
T: Thy servants are shepherds
R: 'And Pharaoh said unto his brethren, What is your occupation? And they said unto Pharaoh, Thy servants are shepherds, both we, and also our fathers.' Pharaoh asked about their occupation, just as Joseph predicted. They answered honestly: we are shepherds, like our fathers. They did not try to impress Pharaoh with grander titles. They told the truth about their humble work. Honesty about who we are builds trust. The brothers could have exaggerated, but they told the simple truth. Shepherding was not glamorous in Egypt, but it was honest work. Throughout Scripture, shepherds hold a special place. Abel, Abraham, Moses, and David were shepherds. And Jesus is the Good Shepherd. Psalm 23 says, The LORD is my shepherd. Shepherds care for the vulnerable, guide the lost, and protect the flock. Today, be honest about who you are. Do not inflate your status. Humble honesty is respected by God and by wise people.
S: When Pharaoh asked their occupation, the brothers answered honestly that they were shepherds like their fathers. They did not exaggerate or try to impress. Be honest about who you are today, for humble honesty is respected by God.

== 47:4
T: Asking respectfully
R: 'They said moreover unto Pharaoh, For to sojourn in the land are we come; for thy servants have no pasture for their flocks… now therefore, we pray thee, let thy servants dwell in the land of Goshen.' The brothers explained their need. They had come to sojourn because the famine had left no pasture. They humbly asked to live in Goshen. Their request was respectful and clear. Asking for help with humility is wise. The brothers did not demand or presume. They explained their situation honestly and asked politely. Notice they said they came to sojourn, to stay temporarily. They saw themselves as guests. This attitude of humility opened Pharaoh's heart. James 4 says, ye have not, because ye ask not. Sometimes we do not receive because we do not ask. And when we ask, humility and honesty matter. Philippians 4 says, let your requests be made known unto God. Today, bring your needs to God with honesty and humility. Ask Him clearly for what you need.
S: The brothers explained to Pharaoh that famine had left no pasture and humbly asked to dwell in Goshen. Their respectful, honest request opened Pharaoh's heart. Bring your needs to God today with honesty and humility.

== 47:9
T: A pilgrimage
R: 'And Jacob said unto Pharaoh, The days of the years of my pilgrimage are an hundred and thirty years: few and evil have the days of the years of my life been.' Jacob called his life a pilgrimage. He was one hundred thirty years old. And he described his years as few and evil, meaning difficult and troubled. Jacob was honest about his hard life. Jacob's description of life as a pilgrimage is significant. He saw himself as a traveler, not a permanent resident. His true home was elsewhere. Hebrews 11 says the patriarchs confessed that they were strangers and pilgrims on the earth. Jacob had known much hardship: conflict with Esau, years with Laban, the loss of Rachel, the grief over Joseph. He did not pretend his life had been easy. Yet God had been faithful through it all. Today, see your life as a pilgrimage. Hardships are real, but they are temporary. You are traveling toward a better home that God has prepared.
S: Jacob told Pharaoh that the years of his pilgrimage were one hundred thirty, few and evil. He saw his life as a pilgrimage and was honest about its hardships. See your life as a pilgrimage toward the better home God has prepared.

== 47:13
T: No bread anywhere
R: 'And there was no bread in all the land; for the famine was very sore, so that the land of Egypt and all the land of Canaan fainted by reason of the famine.' The famine became so severe that there was no bread anywhere. Egypt and Canaan fainted from hunger. The crisis was overwhelming. In contrast to Jacob's family, who were nourished by Joseph, the rest of the land was starving. Those connected to Joseph were provided for; others were fainting. This contrast illustrates a spiritual truth. Those connected to Christ are spiritually nourished, while the world without Him is spiritually starving. Amos 8 speaks of a famine not of bread, nor a thirst for water, but of hearing the words of the LORD. Many people today are spiritually hungry. They are fainting for meaning, hope, and love. Jesus said, he that cometh to me shall never hunger. Today, pray for those who are spiritually starving. And share the bread of life with someone who is hungry for hope.

== 47:25
