# Genesis 48: Jacob blesses Ephraim and Manasseh

== 48:1
T: Thy father is sick
R: 'And it came to pass after these things, that one told Joseph, Behold, thy father is sick: and he took with him his two sons, Manasseh and Ephraim.' When Joseph heard that his father was sick, he came at once. And he brought his two sons with him. Joseph was a busy ruler over all Egypt, yet he made time for his dying father. He also made sure his sons would meet their grandfather and receive his blessing. Honoring aging parents is a sacred duty. Exodus 20 says, Honour thy father and thy mother. Joseph did not send a servant or wait for a convenient moment. He went himself. And by bringing his sons, he connected the generations. Children need to know where they come from and receive the faith of those before them. Psalm 78 speaks of telling the generation to come the praises of the LORD. Today, honor the older people in your life. Visit them, care for them, and help your children receive the blessing of their faith.
S: When Joseph heard his father was sick, he came at once and brought his sons Manasseh and Ephraim. Though busy ruling Egypt, he made time for his father and connected the generations. Honor the older people in your life and help your children receive their blessing.
P: Make time for those who raised you. Genesis 48:1: 'Behold, thy father is sick: and he took with him his two sons.' Joseph came and brought the next generation. Visit someone today.

== 48:2
T: Strengthened to bless
R: 'And one told Jacob, and said, Behold, thy son Joseph cometh unto thee: and Israel strengthened himself, and sat upon the bed.' When Jacob heard that Joseph was coming, he gathered his strength and sat up in bed. Though weak and near death, he summoned his energy for this important moment. He had a blessing to give. Notice the change of name: Jacob is told, but Israel strengthens himself. The man of faith rose to the occasion. Even in weakness, God gives strength for what matters. Isaiah 40 says, He giveth power to the faint; and to them that have no might he increaseth strength. Jacob had one more task to complete, and God enabled him to do it. Second Corinthians 12 says God's strength is made perfect in weakness. Today, if you feel weak or weary, ask God for strength to do what matters most. He can lift you up for the tasks He has given you, even when your own strength is gone.
S: When Jacob heard Joseph was coming, Israel strengthened himself and sat upon the bed. Though weak and near death, he rose to give an important blessing. Ask God for strength to do what matters most, even when your own strength is gone.

== 48:3
T: Remembering God's appearing
R: 'And Jacob said unto Joseph, God Almighty appeared unto me at Luz in the land of Canaan, and blessed me.' Jacob began by recalling his encounter with God. At Luz, which he renamed Bethel, God Almighty had appeared to him and blessed him. Before blessing his grandsons, Jacob told the story of God's faithfulness. Testimony is the foundation of blessing. Jacob did not just pass on words; he passed on his experience of God. He wanted Joseph and his sons to know that the God who appeared to him was real. Bethel had been a turning point in Jacob's life, when he was fleeing from Esau with nothing. There God met him. Psalm 66 says, Come and hear, all ye that fear God, and I will declare what he hath done for my soul. Our testimonies strengthen the faith of those who come after us. Today, tell someone about a time God met you. Your story of His faithfulness can become a foundation for their faith.
S: Jacob told Joseph that God Almighty had appeared to him at Luz and blessed him. Before blessing his grandsons, he recalled his own encounter with God. Tell someone today about a time God met you, for your testimony can strengthen their faith.

== 48:4
T: The promise passed on
R: 'And said unto me, Behold, I will make thee fruitful, and multiply thee, and I will make of thee a multitude of people; and will give this land to thy seed after thee for an everlasting possession.' Jacob repeated God's promise: fruitfulness, multiplication, a multitude of people, and the land as an everlasting possession. Jacob wanted Joseph to know that these promises were still in force, even though the family now lived in Egypt. God's promises do not expire. The family was far from Canaan, but the promise remained. Circumstances had changed, but God's word had not. The land would still belong to Jacob's descendants. Jacob passed the promise to the next generation so they would not forget. Numbers 23 says, God is not a man, that he should lie. Hebrews 6 speaks of the immutability of his counsel. Today, remember God's promises, even if your circumstances seem far from them. And pass them on to those who come after you, so they too will trust God's unchanging word.
S: Jacob repeated God's promise to make him fruitful and give the land to his seed as an everlasting possession. Though the family was in Egypt, the promise remained in force. Remember God's promises in every circumstance and pass them on to the next generation.

== 48:5
T: Adopted as my own
R: 'And now thy two sons, Ephraim and Manasseh, which were born unto thee in the land of Egypt before I came unto thee into Egypt, are mine; as Reuben and Simeon, they shall be mine.' Jacob adopted Joseph's two sons as his own. They would be counted as Jacob's sons, equal to Reuben and Simeon, his firstborn. Through this adoption, Joseph received a double portion, since each of his sons would become a tribe of Israel. These boys were born in Egypt to an Egyptian mother. Yet Jacob claimed them fully as his own. Adoption brings outsiders into the family with full rights. This beautifully pictures our adoption by God. Ephesians 1 says God predestinated us unto the adoption of children by Jesus Christ. Romans 8 says we have received the Spirit of adoption, whereby we cry, Abba, Father. We were outsiders, yet God claims us as His own children with a full inheritance. Today, rejoice that you are adopted into God's family. You belong to Him completely.

Jacob's attention to detail shows wisdom in planning for the future. He wanted the inheritance to be clear and fair. Ambiguity about inheritance can cause conflict. Jacob, who had seen plenty of family strife, made sure the arrangement was settled. God is also a God of order. First Corinthians 14 says, Let all things be done decently and in order. And God makes sure every one of His children has a place in His inheritance. First Peter 1 speaks of an inheritance incorruptible, and undefiled, and that fadeth not away, reserved in heaven for you. Today, rest in the certainty of your inheritance in Christ. And bring wise order to your own family's future.

== 48:7
T: Remembering Rachel
R: 'And as for me, when I came from Padan, Rachel died by me in the land of Canaan in the way… and I buried her there in the way of Ephrath; the same is Bethlehem.' In the middle of this solemn moment, Jacob remembered Rachel. She had died on the road near Bethlehem, and he buried her there. Many years had passed, but Jacob's grief for her was still close to the surface. Rachel was Joseph's mother. Perhaps adopting her grandsons felt like honoring her memory. Love does not fade with time. Jacob was old and dying, yet he still spoke tenderly of the wife he had lost. Grief can remain with us for a lifetime, and that is not a lack of faith. It is a sign of deep love. Jesus Himself wept at the grave of Lazarus. Notice that Rachel was buried near Bethlehem, where Christ would one day be born. Today, if you carry long grief, bring it to God. He understands and holds your memories with tenderness.

== 48:8
T: Who are these?
R: 'And Israel beheld Joseph's sons, and said, Who are these?' Jacob saw the two young men but could not recognize them. His eyesight was failing. So he asked, Who are these? It was a simple question, but it opened the way for blessing. Before blessing, Jacob wanted to know who stood before him. This mirrors God's personal knowledge of us. Unlike Jacob, God never needs to ask who we are. He knows each of us by name. Isaiah 43 says, I have called thee by thy name; thou art mine. Jesus said in John 10, I am the good shepherd, and know my sheep. God's blessings are personal, not generic. He knows your history, your struggles, and your heart. Psalm 139 says, O LORD, thou hast searched me, and known me. Today, rejoice that you are fully known by God. He sees you clearly, calls you by name, and blesses you personally.

== 48:10
T: Kissed and embraced
R: 'Now the eyes of Israel were dim for age, so that he could not see. And he brought them near unto him; and he kissed them, and embraced them.' Jacob was nearly blind with age. So Joseph brought his sons close. Jacob kissed them and embraced them. Before words of blessing came touch and affection. This scene is tender and moving. Blessing is not only spoken; it is shown through love. Jacob drew his grandsons close and held them. Physical affection communicates acceptance and love in ways words cannot. Interestingly, this recalls another scene. Decades earlier, Jacob's own father Isaac was blind when Jacob deceived him to steal a blessing. Now Jacob, also blind, gives blessing freely and lovingly. God had transformed the deceiver into a loving patriarch. Jesus also took children in His arms and blessed them in Mark 10. Today, show love to those in your care, especially children. Let your affection communicate God's love. Blessing grows best in the warmth of a loving embrace.

Jacob marveled: I never thought I would see your face again, and now God has let me see your children too. For years Jacob believed Joseph was dead. Now he was holding Joseph's sons. God had given him more than he had dared to hope. God's goodness often exceeds our expectations. Jacob had given up hope of seeing Joseph. He would have been overjoyed just to see Joseph alive. But God gave him even more: grandchildren. Ephesians 3 says God is able to do exceeding abundantly above all that we ask or think. Sometimes we limit our hopes because of past disappointments. But God is not limited by our expectations. Psalm 31 says, Oh how great is thy goodness, which thou hast laid up for them that fear thee. Today, do not limit what God can do. He may restore what you thought was lost and add blessings you never imagined.

Then Joseph bowed with his face to the ground before his father. The ruler of Egypt humbled himself before his aged father. Joseph's position never made him proud. He was second only to Pharaoh, yet he bowed before his father in honor and reverence. True greatness includes humility. Joseph recognized that his father held spiritual authority, and that the blessing he was about to give was from God. Honor flows from humility. First Peter 5 says, Humble yourselves therefore under the mighty hand of God, that he may exalt you in due time. Philippians 2 tells us to esteem others better than ourselves. Today, honor those to whom honor is due. Whatever position you hold, remain humble before God and before those He has placed over you. Humility prepares the heart to receive blessing.

== 48:13
T: Careful positioning
R: 'And Joseph took them both, Ephraim in his right hand toward Israel's left hand, and Manasseh in his left hand toward Israel's right hand, and brought them near unto him.' Joseph carefully positioned his sons. He placed Manasseh, the firstborn, near Jacob's right hand, the hand of greater blessing. He placed Ephraim, the younger, near Jacob's left hand. Joseph arranged everything according to custom. Joseph did what seemed right according to tradition. The firstborn traditionally received the greater blessing. Joseph had a plan for how things should go. But as we will see, God had a different plan. This is a reminder that our careful arrangements do not always match God's purposes. Proverbs 16 says, A man's heart deviseth his way: but the LORD directeth his steps. We can plan wisely, and we should. But we must hold our plans open to God's direction. Today, make your plans prayerfully, but stay open to God's surprises. His ways may differ from yours, and His ways are always better.

== 48:14
T: Crossed hands
R: 'And Israel stretched out his right hand, and laid it upon Ephraim's head, who was the younger, and his left hand upon Manasseh's head, guiding his hands wittingly; for Manasseh was the firstborn.' Jacob crossed his hands. He deliberately placed his right hand on Ephraim, the younger, and his left hand on Manasseh, the firstborn. The word wittingly means knowingly. This was not a mistake. Jacob was guided by God. This pattern runs throughout Genesis. God chose Abel over Cain, Isaac over Ishmael, Jacob over Esau, and now Ephraim over Manasseh. God's choices are not bound by human customs. His grace is not based on birth order or natural advantage. Romans 9 says, it is not of him that willeth, nor of him that runneth, but of God that sheweth mercy. Many have also seen in Jacob's crossed hands a picture of the cross, through which blessing comes to us. Today, rejoice that God's grace does not depend on your natural position. He chooses freely, and He has chosen to bless you.

Psalm 23 says, The LORD is my shepherd; I shall not want. Today, look back over your life and see how God has shepherded you. He has fed you all your life long. They would carry the family identity and the covenant promise. Redemption means being rescued and bought back. Christ is our ultimate Redeemer. Galatians 3 says, Christ hath redeemed us from the curse of the law. Titus 2 says He gave himself for us, that he might redeem us from all iniquity. Today, thank God for redeeming you. == 48:19

Jacob refused gently: I know, my son, I know. Jacob was not confused. He knew exactly what he was doing. Manasseh would also become great, but Ephraim would be greater. Jacob's tenderness is striking. He did not rebuke Joseph harshly. He reassured him: I know it, my son. Jacob had once been the younger son who received the greater blessing, though through deception. Now he understood God's purposes and gave the blessing with clear conviction. Notice that Manasseh was not rejected. He also would be great. God's choice of one does not mean rejection of the other. History confirmed Jacob's words: Ephraim became so prominent that the northern kingdom was often called by his name. Today, when God says I know it, trust Him. He sees what you cannot see, and His purposes are good for everyone involved.

== 48:22
T: One portion above
R: 'Moreover I have given to thee one portion above thy brethren, which I took out of the hand of the Amorite with my sword and with my bow.' Jacob gave Joseph an extra portion of land, above his brothers. The Hebrew word for portion is Shechem, the place where Jacob had bought land and where Joseph's bones would one day be buried, as Joshua 24 records. Joseph received a double honor. Joseph's faithfulness was rewarded. He had suffered the most, been betrayed the most, and forgiven the most. Now he received a special portion. God sees faithfulness in suffering and rewards it. Hebrews 6 says, God is not unrighteous to forget your work and labour of love. The extra portion also pointed to the future. Generations later, Joseph's bones were carried out of Egypt and buried in this very place. Jacob's gift became Joseph's resting place in the promised land. Today, remain faithful in hardship.
