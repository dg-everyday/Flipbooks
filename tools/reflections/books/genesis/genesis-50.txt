# Genesis 50: Jacob's burial; Joseph's forgiveness; Joseph's death

== 50:1
T: Joseph wept
R: 'And Joseph fell upon his father's face, and wept upon him, and kissed him.' When Jacob died, Joseph fell on his father's face, wept, and kissed him. The powerful ruler of Egypt grieved openly as a son. He did not hide his sorrow behind his position. Joseph is often described as weeping. He wept when he saw his brothers, when he met Benjamin, when he revealed himself, and when he embraced his father. Now he wept at his father's death. Joseph was a man of deep feeling. Grief is a natural expression of love. Scripture never asks us to hide our sorrow. Ecclesiastes 3 says there is a time to weep. Jesus wept at Lazarus's tomb, even knowing He would raise him. First Thessalonians 4 says we sorrow not, even as others which have no hope. Christians grieve, but with hope. Today, if you are grieving, let yourself weep. God honors your tears. And hold on to the hope that death is not the end for those who belong to Him.
S: When Jacob died, Joseph fell upon his father's face, wept upon him and kissed him. The ruler of Egypt grieved openly as a loving son. Let yourself grieve when loss comes, for God honors your tears and gives hope beyond death.
P: Grief is love. Genesis 50:1: 'Joseph fell upon his father's face, and wept upon him, and kissed him.' God honors your tears. We grieve, but with hope.

== 50:2
T: Caring for the body
R: 'And Joseph commanded his servants the physicians to embalm his father: and the physicians embalmed Israel.' Joseph had his father's body embalmed by Egyptian physicians. This preserved Jacob's body for the long journey to Canaan. Joseph used the resources of Egypt to honor his father's final wish. Caring for the dead with dignity is an act of love and respect. Joseph did not neglect the practical details. He ensured his father's body could be carried to the promised land as Jacob had requested. Faith and practical action work together. Joseph trusted God's promise about Canaan, and he took the necessary steps to fulfill his father's wish. Notice that Joseph used Egyptian expertise for a godly purpose. God's people can use the skills and resources of the world to serve His purposes. Today, honor those you love with practical care. Faith is not just belief; it is shown in action. Do what is needed to honor the commitments you have made.
S: Joseph commanded the physicians to embalm his father so his body could be carried to Canaan. He used Egypt's resources to honor Jacob's final wish with dignity. Honor those you love with practical care, for faith shows itself in action.

== 50:3
T: Egypt mourned
R: 'And forty days were fulfilled for him; for so are fulfilled the days of those which are embalmed: and the Egyptians mourned for him threescore and ten days.' The embalming took forty days, and the Egyptians mourned for Jacob seventy days. This was nearly the mourning period given to a Pharaoh. A foreign shepherd was honored by the whole nation. Why did Egypt honor Jacob so greatly? Partly because of Joseph's position. But also because of Jacob's blessing. He had blessed Pharaoh. Through Joseph, Jacob's family had saved Egypt from famine. God's people had been a blessing to the nation, and the nation responded with honor. When God's people live faithfully, the world often notices. Matthew 5 says, Let your light so shine before men, that they may see your good works, and glorify your Father which is in heaven. Jacob's family had brought life to Egypt. Today, live in such a way that your presence is a blessing to your community. Let your faithfulness bring honor to God.
S: Jacob's embalming took forty days, and the Egyptians mourned him seventy days, almost as for a Pharaoh. His family had been a blessing to Egypt, and the nation honored him. Live today so that your presence blesses your community and brings honor to God.
P: A blessing to the nation. Genesis 50:3: 'the Egyptians mourned for him threescore and ten days.' Jacob's family had blessed Egypt. Let your light shine in your community today.

== 50:4
T: Asking through proper channels
R: 'And when the days of his mourning were past, Joseph spake unto the house of Pharaoh, saying, If now I have found grace in your eyes, speak, I pray you, in the ears of Pharaoh.' After the mourning period, Joseph made a request. He went through Pharaoh's household rather than going directly to the king. Perhaps mourning customs kept him from appearing before Pharaoh. He spoke humbly: if I have found grace in your eyes. Joseph's approach shows respect for authority and process. Though he was powerful, he did not presume. He asked courteously and followed proper channels. Humility in requests reflects wisdom. Joseph knew that his position depended on Pharaoh's favor. He did not take that for granted. Romans 13 says, Render therefore to all their dues… honour to whom honour. We honor God by respecting the authorities He has placed over us. Today, approach those in authority with humility and respect. Even when you have influence, do not presume. Ask graciously, and trust God with the outcome.
S: After the mourning, Joseph humbly asked Pharaoh's household to speak to the king for him. Though powerful, he respected authority and did not presume. Approach those in authority with humility and respect, trusting God with the outcome.

He asked permission to go to Canaan to bury Jacob, and he promised to return. Joseph honored two commitments at once. He kept his oath to his father, and he assured Pharaoh of his continued loyalty. A person of integrity keeps every promise, even when they seem to pull in different directions. Psalm 15 describes the one who sweareth to his own hurt, and changeth not. Joseph's promise, I will come again, also echoes the words of Jesus. John 14 records Him saying, I will come again, and receive you unto myself. Like Joseph, Jesus keeps His word. Today, keep your promises faithfully. And rejoice that Jesus will keep His promise to return for you.

== 50:7
T: A great procession
R: 'And Joseph went up to bury his father: and with him went up all the servants of Pharaoh, the elders of his house, and all the elders of the land of Egypt.' Joseph went to Canaan with an enormous procession. Pharaoh's servants, the elders of his household, and all the elders of Egypt went with him. The leaders of the greatest nation on earth accompanied the burial of a shepherd. This is remarkable. Jacob had come to Egypt as a refugee fleeing famine. He left in death with royal honor. God had exalted His servant. First Samuel 2 says, them that honour me I will honour. Jacob had honored God throughout his life, and in death God honored him before the nations. This procession also anticipated the exodus. One day, all Israel would leave Egypt for Canaan. This funeral journey was a preview of that great departure. Today, trust that God honors those who honor Him. You may not see it now, but He will bring honor to His faithful servants in His time.

== 50:8
T: Little ones left behind
R: 'And all the house of Joseph, and his brethren, and his father's house: only their little ones, and their flocks, and their herds, they left in the land of Goshen.' Joseph's whole family went to the burial, along with his brothers and their households. But they left their little ones and their livestock in Goshen. They would return to Egypt after the burial. This detail echoes later in Exodus. Pharaoh would try to make Israel leave their children and flocks behind so they would return. Moses refused. When the true exodus came, they would take everything. This burial journey was temporary. The real departure was still to come. God's timing is precise. The family visited Canaan but could not yet stay. The promise was not yet fulfilled. Habakkuk 2 says, the vision is yet for an appointed time… though it tarry, wait for it. Today, if God's promise seems delayed, wait patiently. A glimpse of the promise is not yet the fulfillment, but the fulfillment will surely come.

R: 'And there went up with him both chariots and horsemen: and it was a very great company.' Chariots and horsemen escorted the funeral procession. It was a very great company. The military escort provided protection and showed the importance Egypt placed on this journey. Jacob's funeral was a state occasion. The great company is striking when we remember Jacob's beginnings. He left home with nothing but a staff. Genesis 32 records Jacob saying, with my staff I passed over this Jordan; and now I am become two bands. God had multiplied him beyond imagination. Now a great company carried him back to the land he had left. God's faithfulness accumulates over a lifetime. What begins small can grow into something great when God is involved. Zechariah 4 asks, who hath despised the day of small things? Today, trust God with your small beginnings. He who multiplied Jacob can multiply your life and influence for His glory.

There is no shortcut through sorrow. Psalm 30 says, weeping may endure for a night, but joy cometh in the morning. Today, give yourself or others permission to grieve fully. Do not rush the process. God walks with you through every stage of sorrow.

== 50:11

The Canaanites saw the great mourning and assumed it was Egyptian grief. They named the place the mourning of Egypt. The family's sorrow was so visible that it was remembered in the name of a place. Our grief can be a testimony. The Canaanites noticed the depth of love in this family. They saw a people who mourned deeply for their father. Genuine love and grief speak to observers. Interestingly, the Canaanites saw Egyptians mourning, but it was really Israelites grieving their patriarch. People sometimes misunderstand what they see. Yet even a misunderstood testimony can leave a mark. The place was named and remembered. John 13 says, By this shall all men know that ye are my disciples, if ye have love one to another. But now they united to honor their father. Death had brought them together in obedience. Obedience to the wishes of the dying is a mark of honor. Their united action showed a family that had been healed. The brothers who once acted against their father's heart now acted according to his word. This reflects the change God had worked in them through Joseph's forgiveness. Proverbs 6 says, My son, keep thy father's commandment. Today, honor the wishes of those who have shaped your life. And let shared obedience to God's word unite you with others, even those you once struggled with.

== 50:24
T: God will surely visit you

Near death, Joseph comforted his brothers with a promise. God would surely visit them and bring them out of Egypt to the land He promised the patriarchs. Joseph would not see it, but he was certain it would happen. Joseph's final words were about God's faithfulness. He did not speak of his own achievements, his power, or his wealth. He pointed to God's promise. His confidence was in God, not in Egypt. God will surely visit you. This promise sustained Israel through four hundred years of waiting and slavery. His word cannot fail, though the waiting may be long.

== 50:25
T: Carry up my bones
R: 'And Joseph took an oath of the children of Israel, saying, God will surely visit you, and ye shall carry up my bones from hence.' Joseph made the Israelites swear to carry his bones to Canaan when God delivered them. He did not ask for immediate burial in Canaan, as Jacob had. He would wait with his people and go up with them when God brought them out. Hebrews 11 says, By faith Joseph, when he died, made mention of the departing of the children of Israel; and gave commandment concerning his bones. Of all Joseph's acts of faith, this is the one Hebrews highlights. His bones became a constant reminder. For centuries, Israel kept Joseph's coffin. Every time they saw it, they remembered God's promise. Exodus 13 records that Moses took the bones of Joseph with him, and Joshua 24 records their burial at Shechem. Today, let your faith leave reminders for others. Live so that your life points people to God's promises long after you are gone.

== 50:26
T: In a coffin in Egypt
R: 'So Joseph died, being an hundred and ten years old: and they embalmed him, and he was put in a coffin in Egypt.' Genesis ends with Joseph in a coffin in Egypt. The book that began with God creating life in a garden ends with death in a foreign land. Yet this ending is full of hope, not despair. Joseph's coffin in Egypt was a promise waiting to be fulfilled. God had said He would bring Israel out, and Joseph's bones would go with them. Genesis ends with expectation. The story would continue in Exodus with deliverance. Our lives can feel like this too. Sometimes we are in a season that looks like an ending. But with God, every ending is a waiting room for His next act. Death itself is not the end for those who trust Him. Jesus said in John 11, I am the resurrection, and the life. Today, trust that God's story for you continues. What looks like an ending may be the doorway to deliverance.
