# Isaiah 20: Whither we flee for help

== 20:1
T: In the year that Tartan came unto Ashdod
R: 'In the year that Tartan came unto Ashdod, (when Sargon the king of Assyria sent him,) and fought against Ashdod, and took it' Notice in the year that Tartan came unto Ashdod. When the Assyrian commander came to Ashdod. Notice when Sargon the king of Assyria sent him. Sent by King Sargon. Notice and fought against Ashdod. Attacked Ashdod. Notice and took it. Captured it. Notice that this is real history. Notice that Ashdod was a Philistine city. Notice that it rebelled against Assyria. Notice that it trusted Egypt to help. Notice that Egypt did not come. Notice that the city fell. Notice that trusting the wrong helper fails. Notice that God speaks into real events. Notice that His word fits history. Notice that we can learn from it. Notice that Scripture is grounded in real places. Luke 1 says, 'that thou mightest know the certainty of those things' Notice certainty. Today, trust a faith grounded in real history.
S: In the year the Assyrian commander, sent by King Sargon, came to Ashdod, attacked it and captured it. Ashdod had rebelled trusting Egypt to help, but no help came, and God's word speaks into such real events. Trust a faith grounded in history, that thou mightest know the certainty of those things.
P: Real history. Isaiah 20:1: 'In the year that Tartan came unto Ashdod.' Certainty. That thou mightest know the certainty of those things. Trust God's word today. Walk in His ways today.

== 20:2
T: Walking naked and barefoot
R: 'Go and loose the sackcloth from off thy loins, and put off thy shoe from thy foot. And he did so' Notice at the same time spake the LORD by Isaiah. God spoke through Isaiah. Notice the son of Amoz. Amoz's son. Notice saying, Go and loose the sackcloth. Take off the sackcloth. Notice from off thy loins. From your body. Notice and put off thy shoe from thy foot. Take off your sandals. Notice and he did so. He obeyed. Notice walking naked and barefoot. Going stripped and barefoot. Notice that God asked Isaiah to act out a message. Notice that he dressed like a captive. Notice that this was humbling. Notice that he obeyed. Notice that God sometimes asks hard things. Notice that obedience can look strange. Notice that Isaiah's life became the message. Notice that faithfulness matters more than appearance. Notice that God honours obedience. 1 Samuel 15 says, 'Behold, to obey is better than sacrifice' Notice obey. Today, obey God even when it is humbling.
S: God told Isaiah to take off his sackcloth and sandals, and he obeyed, going about stripped like a captive and barefoot. God asked Isaiah to act out a humbling message with his own life, and he obeyed even though it looked strange. Value obedience, for behold, to obey is better than sacrifice.
P: Humbling obedience. Isaiah 20:2: 'And he did so, walking naked and barefoot.' Obey. Behold, to obey is better than sacrifice. Obey God today. Let this shape your day.

== 20:3
T: For a sign and wonder upon Egypt and upon Ethiopia
R: 'Like as my servant Isaiah hath walked naked and barefoot three years for a sign and wonder upon Egypt and upon Ethiopia' Notice and the LORD said. God said. Notice like as my servant Isaiah. Just as My servant Isaiah. Notice hath walked naked and barefoot three years. Has gone stripped and barefoot for three years. Notice for a sign and wonder. As a sign and warning. Notice upon Egypt and upon Ethiopia. Against Egypt and Cush. Notice that God calls Isaiah my servant. Notice that his humbling was honoured. Notice that it lasted three years. Notice that faithfulness required endurance. Notice that his life was a living sermon. Notice that it warned against trusting Egypt. Notice that God uses our obedience to speak to others. Notice that long faithfulness matters. Notice that God calls His faithful ones servants with honour. Notice that this is high praise. Matthew 25 says, 'Well done, thou good and faithful servant' Notice faithful servant. Today, be God's faithful servant, even when it is long and hard.
S: The LORD said that just as His servant Isaiah went stripped and barefoot for three years as a sign and warning against Egypt and Cush, so the message would come true. God honoured Isaiah's long, humbling obedience by calling him My servant, for his life became a living sermon. Be faithful, hoping to hear, well done, thou good and faithful servant.
P: My servant. Isaiah 20:3: 'Like as my servant Isaiah hath walked naked and barefoot three years.' Well done. Well done, thou good and faithful servant. Be faithful today. Take it to God in prayer.

== 20:4

R: 'So shall the king of Assyria lead away the Egyptians prisoners, and the Ethiopians captives, young and old' Notice so shall the king of Assyria. So the king of Assyria. Notice that the helpers would become captives. Notice that those Judah trusted would be shamed. Notice that trusting the wrong source leads to shame. Notice that God never shames those who trust Him. Notice that He covers them. Notice that He gives dignity. Notice that our hope should be in Him. Notice that He does not disappoint. Romans 10 says, 'Whosoever believeth on him shall not be ashamed.' Those Judah trusted for help would themselves be shamed as captives, but God never shames those who trust Him. Hope in Him, for whosoever believeth on him shall not be ashamed.

== 20:6

Notice shall say in that day. Will say on that day. Notice behold, such is our expectation. See what happened to those we trusted. Notice whither we flee for help. Those we ran to for help. Notice to be delivered from the king of Assyria. To be saved from Assyria. Notice and how shall we escape. How then can we escape. Notice that the coastal people realise their mistake. Notice that this is the end of trusting people over God. Notice that the question how shall we escape has an answer. Notice that the answer is God. Notice that He is the true deliverer. Notice that we should run to Him first. Hebrews 2 says, 'How shall we escape, if we neglect so great salvation' Notice so great salvation. Today, run to God first. He is the true deliverer.
S: The people of the coast will say, see what happened to those we relied on and ran to for help against Assyria, how then can we escape? When their helpers fell they had nowhere left to run, for the answer was always God, the deliverer who is never conquered. Run to Him first, for how shall we escape, if we neglect so great salvation?
P: How escape? Isaiah 20:6: 'whither we flee for help to be delivered from the king of Assyria.' Great salvation. How shall we escape, if we neglect so great salvation? Run to God first today. Let this shape your day.
