# Jeremiah 47: O thou sword of the LORD

== 47:1
T: Against the Philistines
R: 'The word of the LORD that came to Jeremiah the prophet against the Philistines, before that Pharaoh smote Gaza.' Notice the word of the LORD that came to Jeremiah the prophet against the Philistines. This is the word of the LORD that came to Jeremiah the prophet concerning the Philistines. Notice before that Pharaoh smote Gaza. Before Pharaoh attacked Gaza. Notice that the Philistines were Israel's old enemies. Notice that they fought Saul. Notice that they fought David. Notice that Goliath was a Philistine. Notice that now they hear God's word. Notice that God speaks to every nation. Notice that judgment reveals Him. Notice that mercy reveals Him. Notice that the psalmist prayed this. Notice that nations would learn His name. Notice that they would know He rules. Psalm 83 says, 'That men may know that thou, whose name alone is JEHOVAH, art the most high over all the earth.' Notice most high over all. Today, live so that others may know that God is most high.
S: This is the word of the LORD that came to Jeremiah the prophet concerning the Philistines before Pharaoh attacked Gaza. Israel's ancient enemies, the people of Goliath, now heard God's word, for He holds every nation accountable and wants all to know Him. Live so others may know Him, that men may know that thou, whose name alone is JEHOVAH, art the most high over all the earth.
P: To the Philistines. Jeremiah 47:1: 'the word of the LORD … against the Philistines.' Most high. That men may know that thou art the most high over all the earth. Make God known today. Take it to God in prayer.

== 47:2
T: Waters rise up out of the north
R: 'Behold, waters rise up out of the north, and shall be an overflowing flood, and shall overflow the land' Notice thus saith the LORD. This is what the LORD says. Notice behold, waters rise up out of the north, and shall be an overflowing flood. See how the waters are rising in the north; they will become an overflowing torrent. Notice and shall overflow the land, and all that is therein; the city, and them that dwell therein. They will overflow the land and everything in it, the towns and those who live in them. Notice that the verse adds that people will cry out and all who live in the land will wail. Notice that invasion is pictured as a flood. Notice that God remains a refuge. Notice that He is present in trouble. Notice that we need not fear. Psalm 46 says, 'Though the waters thereof roar and be troubled' Notice the waters roar. Today, when trouble rises like a flood, take refuge in God.
S: This is what the LORD says, see how the waters are rising in the north, becoming an overflowing torrent that will overflow the land, its towns and people, and the verse adds that people will cry out and wail. The Babylonian invasion is pictured as a terrifying flood sweeping everything away, yet God remains a refuge in such floods. Take refuge in Him, though the waters thereof roar and be troubled.
P: Rising waters. Jeremiah 47:2: 'waters rise up out of the north, and shall be an overflowing flood.' Waters roar. Though the waters thereof roar and be troubled. Take refuge in God today. Trust Him with it today.

== 47:3
T: The fathers shall not look back to their children
R: 'the fathers shall not look back to their children for feebleness of hands' Notice at the noise of the stamping of the hoofs of his strong horses. At the sound of the hooves of galloping steeds. Notice at the rushing of his chariots, and at the rumbling of his wheels. At the noise of enemy chariots and the rumble of their wheels. Notice the fathers shall not look back to their children for feebleness of hands. Parents will not turn to help their children; their hands will hang limp. Notice that the terror is overwhelming. Notice that parents are paralysed. Notice that they cannot help their children. Notice that even when parents fail, God does not. Notice that His love is greater than a parent's. Notice that Isaiah declared this. Notice that a mother might forget. Isaiah 49 says, 'yea, they may forget, yet will I not forget thee.' Notice yet will I not forget. Today, rest in God's love, which never fails even when human love does.
S: At the sound of galloping hooves and the rumble of enemy chariots, parents will not turn to help their children, for their hands will hang limp. Scripture honestly records terror so overwhelming that natural love is paralysed, and if you grieve or fear for a child, God sees you and His love does not fail. Rest in Him, for even if a mother should forget, yea, they may forget, yet will I not forget thee.
P: Limp hands. Jeremiah 47:3: 'the fathers shall not look back to their children.' Not forget. Yet will I not forget thee. Rest in God's love today. Let this shape your day.

== 47:4
T: Every helper that remaineth
R: 'Because of the day that cometh to spoil all the Philistines, and to cut off from Tyrus and Zidon every helper that remaineth' Notice because of the day that cometh to spoil all the Philistines. For the day has come to destroy all the Philistines. Notice and to cut off from Tyrus and Zidon every helper that remaineth. And to remove all survivors who could help Tyre and Sidon. Notice for the LORD will spoil the Philistines, the remnant of the country of Caphtor. The LORD is about to destroy the Philistines, the remnant from the coasts of Caphtor. Notice that allies are cut off. Notice that Tyre and Sidon lose their helpers. Notice that the Philistines fall. Notice that our help comes from Him. Notice that the psalmist lifted his eyes. Notice that he found his help. Psalm 121 says, 'My help cometh from the LORD, which made heaven and earth.' Notice my help cometh. Today, when other help fails, look to the LORD who made heaven and earth.
S: The day has come to destroy all the Philistines and remove every survivor who could help Tyre and Sidon, for the LORD is about to destroy the Philistines, the remnant from Caphtor. Every human ally was cut off, leaving no one to help, yet when human help fails God remains the true Helper. Look to Him, for my help cometh from the LORD, which made heaven and earth.
P: No helper left. Jeremiah 47:4: 'to cut off … every helper that remaineth.' My help. My help cometh from the LORD. Look to God for help today. Take it to God in prayer.

== 47:5
T: How long wilt thou cut thyself?
R: 'Baldness is come upon Gaza; Ashkelon is cut off with the remnant of their valley: how long wilt thou cut thyself' Notice baldness is come upon Gaza. Gaza will shave her head in mourning. Notice Ashkelon is cut off with the remnant of their valley. Ashkelon will be silenced; you remnant on the plain. Notice how long wilt thou cut thyself. How long will you cut yourselves. Notice that cities mourn. Notice that heads are shaved. Notice that people cut themselves in grief. Notice that if you are hurting yourself, please reach out to someone you trust today. Notice that God does not want your harm. Notice that He wants to heal. Notice that He gives beauty for ashes. Notice that He gives joy for mourning. Notice that He gives praise for heaviness. Notice that Isaiah promised this exchange. Isaiah 61 says, 'to give unto them beauty for ashes, the oil of joy for mourning' Notice beauty for ashes. Today, bring your grief to God, who exchanges ashes for beauty.
S: Gaza will shave her head in mourning and Ashkelon will be silenced, and God asks the remnant on the plain how long they will cut themselves. Grief was turning to self-harm and God saw the pain, and if you are hurting yourself, please reach out to someone you trust today, for God does not want your harm. Bring your grief to Him, for He came to give unto them beauty for ashes, the oil of joy for mourning.
P: How long? Jeremiah 47:5: 'how long wilt thou cut thyself?' Beauty for ashes. To give unto them beauty for ashes. Bring grief to God today. Trust Him with it today.

== 47:6
T: O thou sword of the LORD, how long?
R: 'O thou sword of the LORD, how long will it be ere thou be quiet? put up thyself into thy scabbard, rest, and be still.' Notice O thou sword of the LORD, how long will it be ere thou be quiet. Alas, sword of the LORD, how long till you rest. Notice put up thyself into thy scabbard, rest, and be still. Return to your sheath; cease and be still. Notice that someone cries to the sword. Notice that they want it to stop. Notice that God shares this longing. Notice that He hates violence. Notice that Jesus told Peter to sheathe his sword. Notice that He chose not to fight. Notice that He chose the cross. Notice that He brings true peace. Notice that His kingdom does not advance by the sword. Notice that it advances by love. Matthew 26 says, 'Put up again thy sword into his place' Notice put up again thy sword. Today, pray for an end to violence and follow Jesus' way of peace.
S: Someone cries, alas, sword of the LORD, how long till you rest, return to your sheath, cease and be still. This honest lament longs for endless violence to stop, a longing God shares, for Jesus chose the cross rather than the sword. Pray for peace and follow His way, for He told Peter, put up again thy sword into his place.
P: How long, O sword? Jeremiah 47:6: 'O thou sword of the LORD, how long will it be ere thou be quiet?' Put up thy sword. Put up again thy sword into his place. Pray for peace today. Let this shape your day.

== 47:7
T: How can it be quiet?
R: 'How can it be quiet, seeing the LORD hath given it a charge against Ashkelon, and against the sea shore? there hath he appointed it.' Notice how can it be quiet. But how can it rest. Notice seeing the LORD hath given it a charge against Ashkelon, and against the sea shore. When the LORD has commanded it, when He has ordered it to attack Ashkelon and the coast. Notice there hath he appointed it. There He has appointed it. Notice that the answer is sobering. Notice that the sword has a commission. Notice that it cannot rest until finished. Notice that judgment has a purpose. Notice that it is not random. Notice that spears will become pruning hooks. Notice that nations will not learn war anymore. Notice that this is God's final goal. Notice that Isaiah saw it. Isaiah 2 says, 'they shall beat their swords into plowshares, and their spears into pruninghooks' Notice swords into plowshares. Today, hope in God's promise of a coming day when war will end.
S: But how can the sword rest when the LORD has commanded it and ordered it to attack Ashkelon and the coast, where He has appointed it. The sword had a commission and could not rest until its purpose was done, for judgment is appointed and limited by God, who has promised a final peace. Hope in that day, for they shall beat their swords into plowshares, and their spears into pruninghooks.
P: Appointed. Jeremiah 47:7: 'How can it be quiet, seeing the LORD hath given it a charge?' Plowshares. They shall beat their swords into plowshares. Hope for peace today. Take it to God in prayer.
