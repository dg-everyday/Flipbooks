# Job 25: How then can man be justified with God

== 25:1
T: Then answered Bildad
R: 'Then answered Bildad the Shuhite, and said,' Notice then answered Bildad. Third speech. Notice the Shuhite. Second friend. Notice and said. Brief reply. Notice that Bildad's third speech is very short. Notice that it has only six verses. Notice that the friends were running out of words. Notice that their arguments were exhausted. Notice that they could not answer Job's evidence. Notice that Zophar would not speak at all. Notice that the debate was collapsing. Notice that repetition had replaced reasoning. Notice that Bildad repeated old themes. Notice that he added nothing new. Notice that Jesus warned against vain repetition. Notice that many words do not persuade God. Notice that they do not persuade sufferers either. Notice that when arguments fail, silence is wise. Notice that Bildad should have stopped. Notice that his brevity hints at defeat. Matthew 6 says, 'use not vain repetitions.' Notice repetition. Today, recognise when your arguments are exhausted. Silence can be wiser.
S: Bildad the Shuhite answered a third time with a speech of only six verses. The friends' arguments were exhausted, and repetition had replaced reasoning. Recognise when words have run out, and use not vain repetitions.
P: Brief reply. Job 25:1: 'Then answered Bildad the Shuhite.' Few words. Use not vain repetitions. Trust Him with it today. Walk in His ways today.

Notice in his high places. Heaven. Notice that Bildad praised God's sovereignty. Notice that he spoke of God's dominion. Notice that he said God establishes peace in heaven. Notice that this is true. Notice that God rules over all. Notice that He brings order. Notice that He is the source of peace. Notice that Isaiah named the coming King. Notice that He is the Prince of Peace. Notice that His government brings peace. Notice that heaven's peace will come to earth. Notice that Christ brings it. Notice that Job needed peace. Notice that he would receive it from God. Notice that God makes peace. Notice that we can trust Him for it. Isaiah 9 says, 'The Prince of Peace.' Notice the Prince. Today, seek peace from the God who makes it. Christ is the Prince of Peace.
S: Bildad said that dominion and awe belong to God, who establishes order in the heights of heaven. God is the source of peace, and in Christ heaven's peace comes to earth. Seek peace from Him who is called the Prince of Peace.
P: Peace. Job 25:2: 'he maketh peace in his high places.' The Prince. The Prince of Peace. Let this shape your day. Take it to God in prayer.

Notice that Bildad praised God's countless hosts. Notice that God's light reaches everyone. Notice that this is beautiful. Notice that God's light is universal. Notice that John spoke of the true Light. Notice that it lights everyone. Notice that Christ is that light. Notice that He came into the world. Notice that no one is beyond His light. Notice that Job was not beyond it. Notice that light would rise on him. Notice that God's armies are innumerable. Notice that His resources are infinite. Notice that His light is for all. Notice that darkness cannot resist it. John 1 says, 'That was the true Light, which lighteth every man that cometh into the world.' Notice the true Light. Today, receive Christ, the true Light. No one is beyond His light.
S: Bildad asked whether God's forces can be numbered and on whom His light does not rise. God's light reaches everyone, as Christ the true Light came into the world for all. Receive the true Light, which lighteth every man that cometh into the world.
P: Countless. Job 25:3: 'Is there any number of his armies.' True Light. That was the true Light. Live it out today. Hold on to His word today.

== 25:4
T: How then can man be justified with God
R: 'How then can man be justified with God' Notice how then can man. The question. Notice be justified with God. Righteous before Him. Notice that he asked how one born of woman can be pure. Impossible. Notice that Bildad asked the most important question. Notice that it is the question of the whole Bible. Notice that Job had asked it too. Notice that Bildad meant it hopelessly. Notice that he saw no answer. Notice that the gospel answers it. Notice that we are justified freely. Notice that it is through Christ's redemption. Notice that Paul answered Bildad's question. Notice that human effort cannot justify. Notice that God justifies the ungodly. Notice that faith receives it. Notice that Christ makes it possible. Notice that this is the best news. Notice that Bildad's despair has an answer. Romans 3 says, 'Being justified freely by his grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus.' Notice free justification. Today, rejoice that God justifies through Christ. Bildad's question has an answer.
S: Bildad asked how a mortal can be righteous before God, or one born of woman be pure. He asked hopelessly, but the gospel answers his question with free grace through Christ. Rejoice in God's answer, being justified freely by His grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus.
P: The question. Job 25:4: 'How then can man be justified with God.' The answer. Justified freely by his grace. Trust Him with it today.

== 25:5
T: Even to the moon
R: 'Behold even to the moon, and it shineth not; yea, the stars are not pure in his sight.' Notice behold even to the moon. Brightness. Notice and it shineth not. Dim by comparison. Notice yea, the stars. Heavenly lights. Notice are not pure in his sight. Imperfect. Notice that Bildad said even the moon and stars are dim before God. Notice that God's glory outshines them. Notice that this is true. Notice that creation pales beside its Creator. Notice that the psalmist marvelled at moon and stars. Notice that they are works of God's fingers. Notice that the psalmist saw them as glorious. Notice that both are true. Notice that they are glorious creations. Notice that God is more glorious. Notice that wonder should lead to worship. Notice that God is greater still. Psalm 8 says, 'When I consider thy heavens, the work of thy fingers, the moon and the stars.' Notice considered heavens. Today, let the moon and stars lead you to worship. God is greater still.
S: Bildad said that even the moon is not bright and the stars are not pure in God's eyes. Creation is glorious yet pales beside its Creator, and both truths should lead us to worship. Look up and worship, as the psalmist said, when I consider Thy heavens, the work of Thy fingers, the moon and the stars.
P: Moon and stars. Job 25:5: 'Behold even to the moon, and it shineth not.' Worship. The work of thy fingers. Walk in His ways today.

== 25:6
T: Man, that is a worm
R: 'How much less man, that is a worm' Notice how much less man. Comparison. Notice that is a worm. Lowly. Notice that he said the son of man is a maggot. Contempt. Notice that Bildad ended with contempt for humanity. Notice that he called people worms. Notice that this was his final word. Notice that the friends' speeches ended here. Notice that they ended in contempt. Notice that Bildad saw only human lowliness. Notice that he missed human dignity. Notice that Isaiah heard God speak to worm Jacob. Notice that God said fear not. Notice that He promised help. Notice that He called Himself Redeemer. Notice that God does not despise the lowly. Notice that He redeems them. Notice that the Son of Man came. Notice that He lifted humanity up. Isaiah 41 says, 'Fear not, thou worm Jacob, and ye men of Israel; I will help thee, saith the LORD.' Notice help for the lowly. Today, remember that God redeems the lowly. You are not despised.
S: Bildad ended by asking how much less is a mortal, who is but a maggot, a son of man, who is only a worm. The friends' speeches ended in contempt, but God does not despise the lowly and comes to redeem them. Remember God's tender word, fear not, thou worm Jacob; I will help thee, saith the LORD.
P: A worm. Job 25:6: 'How much less man, that is a worm.' Redeemed. Fear not, thou worm Jacob. Let this shape your day.
