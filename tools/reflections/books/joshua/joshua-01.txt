# Joshua 1: Be strong and of a good courage

== 1:1
T: After the death of Moses
R: 'Now after the death of Moses the servant of the LORD it came to pass, that the LORD spake unto Joshua the son of Nun…' After Moses died, the LORD spoke to Joshua, his assistant. Notice after the death of Moses. A great era had ended. Notice the servant of the LORD. Moses is remembered for his service. Notice the LORD spake unto Joshua. God's voice did not die with Moses. Notice that Joshua had been Moses' minister, his helper, for forty years. Faithful service in second place prepared him for first place. Notice that God speaks into seasons of loss and transition. Isaiah 6 begins, 'In the year that king Uzziah died I saw also the Lord.' Today, if a season has ended or a leader has gone, listen for God's voice. He still speaks. And if you serve quietly in someone's shadow, know that God is preparing you.
S: After the death of Moses the servant of the LORD, God spoke to Joshua, who had served as Moses' minister. God's voice did not end with Moses, and Joshua's faithful years in second place prepared him to lead. If a season or leader has passed, listen for God's voice, and if you serve quietly, trust that He is preparing you.
P: God still speaks. Joshua 1:1: 'after the death of Moses the servant of the LORD it came to pass, that the LORD spake unto Joshua.' Quiet service prepares you for what's next.

== 1:2
T: Now therefore arise
R: 'Moses my servant is dead; now therefore arise, go over this Jordan, thou, and all this people, unto the land which I do give to them, even to the children of Israel.' Moses is dead; now get ready to cross the Jordan into the land I am giving Israel. Notice Moses my servant is dead. God names the loss plainly. Notice now therefore arise. Grief gives way to action. Notice go over this Jordan. The river stood between promise and possession. Notice thou, and all this people. Joshua would not go alone. Notice which I do give. The gift was present tense. Notice that God does not ask us to pretend loss has not happened, but He also does not let us stay in it forever. Ecclesiastes 3 says there is 'a time to mourn, and a time to dance.' Today, if you have been grieving an ending, hear God's gentle call: arise. There is a Jordan to cross and a promise ahead.
S: God told Joshua that Moses His servant was dead and called him to arise and lead the people over the Jordan into the land He was giving. God named the loss plainly but did not let His people stay in grief forever. If you have been grieving an ending, hear God's gentle call to arise, for there is a promise ahead.
P: Arise. Joshua 1:2: 'Moses my servant is dead; now therefore arise, go over this Jordan.' God names the loss and then says arise. A promise waits across the river.

== 1:3
T: Every place your foot shall tread
R: 'Every place that the sole of your foot shall tread upon, that have I given unto you, as I said unto Moses.' I will give you every place where you set your foot, as I promised Moses. Notice every place. The scope was generous. Notice the sole of your foot shall tread. Possession required walking. Notice that have I given. Already given in God's mind. Notice as I said unto Moses. The same promise, carried forward. Notice the partnership: God gives, and His people step. The land was theirs, but they had to walk into it. Notice that faith is not passive. It moves its feet. Hebrews 11 says, 'By faith Abraham… went out, not knowing whither he went.' Today, ask where God is asking you to set your foot. What He has promised, you receive by stepping forward in obedience. Take the next step onto the ground He has given.
S: God promised Joshua that every place the sole of their foot would tread He had given them, as He told Moses. The land was already given, but Israel had to walk into it, for faith moves its feet. Ask where God is asking you to set your foot, and receive His promise by stepping forward in obedience.
P: Where your foot treads. Joshua 1:3: 'Every place that the sole of your foot shall tread upon, that have I given unto you.' God gives; we step. Faith moves its feet.

== 1:4
T: Shall be your coast
R: 'From the wilderness and this Lebanon even unto the great river, the river Euphrates, all the land of the Hittites, and unto the great sea toward the going down of the sun, shall be your coast.' Your territory will stretch from the desert to Lebanon, from the Euphrates to the Mediterranean. Notice from the wilderness. The southern edge. Notice and this Lebanon. The northern mountains. Notice unto the great river. The Euphrates in the east. Notice unto the great sea. The Mediterranean in the west. Notice that God described the promise in large, specific terms. Notice that Israel would not fully occupy all of it for centuries; the promise stood larger than their faith. Notice that God's plans are often bigger than our present grasp. Ephesians 3 speaks of Him who 'is able to do exceeding abundantly above all that we ask or think.' Today, do not shrink God's promises to the size of your current experience. His plans for you are wider than you can see.
S: God described the land He was giving from the wilderness and Lebanon to the Euphrates and the great sea. The promise was larger and more specific than Israel would grasp for centuries. Do not shrink God's promises to the size of your current experience, for His plans are wider than you can see.
P: Bigger than you think. Joshua 1:4: 'From the wilderness and this Lebanon even unto the great river… shall be your coast.' God's plans exceed our grasp. Don't shrink His promises.

== 1:5
T: As I was with Moses
R: 'There shall not any man be able to stand before thee all the days of thy life: as I was with Moses, so I will be with thee: I will not fail thee, nor forsake thee.' No one will stand against you; I will be with you as with Moses. Notice not any man… stand before thee. Opposition would not prevail. Notice all the days of thy life. A lifelong promise. Notice as I was with Moses. Joshua had watched God with Moses for forty years. Notice so I will be with thee. The same God, the same presence. Notice I will not fail thee, nor forsake thee. The promise Moses had spoken, now from God's own mouth. Notice that Joshua did not need to become Moses; he needed God's presence. Hebrews 13 says, 'I will never leave thee, nor forsake thee.' Today, stop comparing yourself with those who went before. You need not be them. You need the God who was with them.
S: God promised Joshua that no one would stand before him all his life, and that as He was with Moses, so He would be with him, never failing or forsaking him. Joshua did not need to become Moses but needed the same God's presence. Stop comparing yourself with those who went before, for what you need is the God who was with them.
P: As with Moses, so with you. Joshua 1:5: 'as I was with Moses, so I will be with thee: I will not fail thee, nor forsake thee.' You don't need to be them; you need their God.

Be strong and courageous, because you will lead these people to inherit the land I swore to their ancestors. Notice be strong and of a good courage. The first of three charges in this chapter. Notice for unto this people shalt thou divide. Joshua's task was to lead others into their inheritance. Notice which I sware unto their fathers. The promise was old and sure. Notice that courage was needed not for Joshua's own sake but for the people's. Leadership calls for courage because others depend on you. Notice that God's oath grounded Joshua's courage. Psalm 31 says, 'Be of good courage, and he shall strengthen your heart, all ye that hope in the LORD.' Today, find courage in God's promises. Someone may depend on your faithfulness. Be strong for their sake.
S: God told Joshua to be strong and of good courage, for he would divide the promised land to the people as an inheritance. Courage was needed for the people's sake, grounded in God's ancient oath. Find courage in God's promises, and be strong for the sake of those who depend on your faithfulness.
P: Courage for others. Joshua 1:6: 'Be strong and of a good courage: for unto this people shalt thou divide for an inheritance the land.' Others depend on your faithfulness.

== 1:8

R: 'This book of the law shall not depart out of thy mouth; but thou shalt meditate therein day and night, that thou mayest observe to do according to all that is written therein: for then thou shalt make thy way prosperous, and then thou shalt have good success.' Meditate on the law day and night. Notice shall not depart out of thy mouth. Spoken and repeated. Notice meditate therein day and night. Constant reflection. Notice that thou mayest observe to do. Meditation leads to obedience. Notice then thou shalt… have good success. God's definition of success begins in His word. Notice that a military commander was told to start with Scripture, not strategy. Psalm 1 says the blessed man's 'delight is in the law of the LORD; and in his law doth he meditate day and night.' Today, put God's word in your mouth and mind. Read it, speak it, think on it. That is where good success begins.
S: God told Joshua to keep the book of the law in his mouth and meditate on it day and night so he would obey it, prosper and have good success. A military commander was told to begin with Scripture rather than strategy. Put God's word in your mouth and mind, reading, speaking and thinking on it, for good success begins there.
P: Day and night. Joshua 1:8: 'thou shalt meditate therein day and night… then thou shalt have good success.' Success begins in God's word, not strategy.

== 1:9
T: The LORD thy God is with thee
R: 'Have not I commanded thee? Be strong and of a good courage; be not afraid, neither be thou dismayed: for the LORD thy God is with thee whithersoever thou goest.' Have I not commanded you? Be strong and courageous. Do not be afraid or discouraged, for the LORD your God is with you wherever you go. Notice have not I commanded thee. Courage was a command, not a suggestion. Notice be not afraid. Fear was natural; God addressed it. Notice neither be thou dismayed. Discouragement too. Notice for the LORD thy God is with thee. The reason for courage. Notice whithersoever thou goest. No place outside His presence. Notice that this is one of the most loved verses in Scripture because every believer needs it. Psalm 139 asks, 'Whither shall I go from thy spirit?' Today, take this verse with you wherever you go. Into the meeting, the hospital, the hard conversation. God is with you there. Be strong.
S: God reminded Joshua that He had commanded him to be strong and of good courage, not afraid or dismayed, for the LORD was with him wherever he went. Courage was a command grounded in God's presence everywhere. Take this promise into every place you go today, for God is with you there.
P: Wherever you go. Joshua 1:9: 'Be strong and of a good courage; be not afraid… for the LORD thy God is with thee whithersoever thou goest.' Take this verse with you today.
