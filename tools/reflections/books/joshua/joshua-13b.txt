# Joshua 13:15-33: Reuben, Gad and half Manasseh

== 13:15
T: According to their families
R: 'And Moses gave unto the tribe of the children of Reuben inheritance according to their families.' Moses had given an inheritance to the tribe of Reuben, clan by clan. Notice Moses gave. The gift came through God's servant. Notice unto the tribe of the children of Reuben. The firstborn's tribe. Notice inheritance. A lasting possession. Notice according to their families. Every household included. Notice that Reuben, Jacob's firstborn, had lost the birthright through sin, yet his tribe still received an inheritance. Notice that grace did not erase consequences, but it did not withhold blessing either. Notice that God cares for families, not just nations. Notice that inheritance came household by household. Psalm 68 says, 'God setteth the solitary in families.' Today, thank God for His care for your family. Even where past failures have left marks, His grace still provides a place and a portion.
S: Moses gave the tribe of Reuben an inheritance according to their families. Though Reuben, the firstborn, had lost the birthright through sin, his tribe still received a portion, as grace provided despite past failure. Thank God for His care for your family, for even where failures have left marks, His grace still provides a place.
P: Grace for families. Joshua 13:15: 'Moses gave unto the tribe of the children of Reuben inheritance according to their families.' Despite past failure, grace still provides.

== 13:16
T: Their coast was from Aroer
R: 'And their coast was from Aroer, that is on the bank of the river Arnon, and the city that is in the midst of the river, and all the plain by Medeba.' Notice their coast was from Aroer. The boundary began. Notice on the bank of the river Arnon. The southern limit. Notice the city that is in the midst of the river. A town in the valley. Notice all the plain by Medeba. Open, fertile ground. Notice that Reuben received good grazing land. Notice that God gave each tribe land suited to their life. Notice that the boundaries were clear. Notice that clear boundaries prevent conflict between brothers. Notice that God is a God of order, not confusion. Acts 17 says God 'hath determined the times before appointed, and the bounds of their habitation.' Today, trust that God has placed you where you are for a reason. Your boundaries are not accidental. He has determined the bounds of your habitation in love.
S: Reuben's border ran from Aroer on the Arnon, including the city in the midst of the river and all the plain by Medeba. God gave each tribe land suited to their life with clear boundaries that prevented conflict. Trust that God has placed you where you are for a reason, for He has determined the bounds of your habitation in love.
P: Placed with purpose. Joshua 13:16: 'their coast was from Aroer… and all the plain by Medeba.' God determines the bounds of your habitation. You're where you are for a reason.

== 13:17
T: Heshbon and all her cities
R: 'Heshbon, and all her cities that are in the plain; Dibon.' Heshbon and all its towns on the plateau, including Dibon and the towns named with it. Notice Heshbon. Once Sihon's capital. Notice and all her cities that are in the plain. A network of towns. Notice Dibon. An important Moabite-border city. Notice that some of these towns carried the name of Baal, a reminder of the false worship that had filled the land. Notice that Israel received towns marked by idolatry. Notice that God's people must cleanse what they inherit. Notice that names can be changed and places reclaimed. Notice that Heshbon later appears in the Song of Solomon as a picture of beauty: 'thine eyes like the fishpools in Heshbon.' Today, ask God to cleanse what you have inherited, whether habits, attitudes or family patterns. He can reclaim and make beautiful what was once marked by idols.
S: Reuben received Heshbon and its cities in the plain, including Dibon and towns named after Baal. Israel inherited places marked by idolatry that needed cleansing, yet Heshbon later became a picture of beauty. Ask God to cleanse what you have inherited, for He can reclaim and make beautiful what was once marked by idols.
P: Reclaimed places. Joshua 13:17: 'Heshbon, and all her cities that are in the plain; Dibon.' Some towns bore Baal's name. God can cleanse and reclaim what you inherit.
H: #DailyGrace #Joshua1317 #Cleansing #Restoration #Holiness #BibleVerse

== 13:18
T: Jahaza, Kedemoth and Mephaath
R: 'And Jahaza, and Kedemoth, and Mephaath.' Jahaz, Kedemoth and Mephaath. Notice Jahaza. Where Sihon had come out to fight Israel and was defeated. Notice Kedemoth. From where Moses had sent messengers of peace to Sihon. Notice Mephaath. Later a city given to the Levites. Notice that each name holds a memory. Notice that from Kedemoth, Moses first offered peace before war came. Notice that God's people offered peace before fighting. Notice that the place of a peace offering became an inheritance. Notice that Sihon's refusal, not Israel's aggression, led to battle. Romans 12 says, 'If it be possible, as much as lieth in you, live peaceably with all men.' Today, offer peace first. Seek reconciliation before conflict. Let your first word be peace, as Moses did from Kedemoth, and leave the outcome to God.
S: Reuben received Jahaza, Kedemoth and Mephaath. From Kedemoth Moses had first sent messengers of peace to Sihon, and only Sihon's refusal led to battle at Jahaza. Offer peace first and seek reconciliation before conflict, leaving the outcome to God.
P: Peace first. Joshua 13:18: 'And Jahaza, and Kedemoth, and Mephaath.' From Kedemoth Moses offered peace before war. Live peaceably with all, as much as lies in you.

== 13:19

R: 'And Kirjathaim, and Sibmah, and Zareth-shahar in the mount of the valley.' Kiriathaim, Sibmah and Zereth Shahar on the hill in the valley. Notice Kirjathaim. A twin city. Notice Sibmah. Later famous for its vineyards. Notice Zareth-shahar. Its name may mean splendour of the dawn. Notice in the mount of the valley. A hill rising from low ground. Notice that the land contained both heights and depths. Notice that Sibmah's vines were later mourned by the prophets when Moab took them. Notice that what is not guarded can be lost. Notice that the dawn imagery suggests hope rising in low places. Isaiah 60 says, 'Arise, shine; for thy light is come.' Today, look for God's dawn in your valleys. Guard the fruitful vines He has given you. And trust that light can rise from the lowest places of your life.
S: Reuben received Kirjathaim, Sibmah and Zareth-shahar in the mount of the valley. Sibmah's vineyards were later lost and mourned, while the name Zareth-shahar suggests the splendour of dawn rising in low places. Guard the fruitful vines God has given you, and trust that light can rise from the lowest places of your life.
P: Dawn in the valley. Joshua 13:19: 'Zareth-shahar in the mount of the valley.' Guard the fruitful vines God gives you, and look for His dawn in your valleys.

== 13:20

Notice that one area held both deep failure and a faithful servant's grave. Notice that God's people lived among memories of sin and of faithfulness. Notice that Beth-jeshimoth was the last camp before crossing the Jordan. Today, remember both the failures and the faithful in your story. Learn from the one, honour the other, and keep moving forward into what God has promised.
S: Reuben received Beth-peor, Ashdoth-pisgah and Beth-jeshimoth, places near Israel's sin at Baal-peor and the grave of Moses. One area held both deep failure and a faithful servant's grave, and Beth-jeshimoth was the last camp before the crossing. Remember both the failures and the faithful in your story, learning from one and honouring the other.
P: Memories of failure and faithfulness. Joshua 13:20: 'And Ashdoth-pisgah, and Beth-jeshimoth.' Learn from failures, honour the faithful, and keep moving forward.

Your influence matters to God. Use it to draw people toward Him.

These Midianites had led Israel into sin at Peor, and judgment came on those who caused God's people to stumble. == 13:22
T: Balaam the son of Beor

Notice the son of Beor. Named fully, so he is remembered. Notice the soothsayer. He used divination, not true prophecy. Notice among them that were slain. He died with Israel's enemies. Notice that Balaam spoke true blessings from God but loved money more than God. Notice that he taught Midian how to lead Israel into sin. Notice that knowing God's words is not the same as loving God. Second Peter 2 speaks of 'the way of Balaam the son of Bosor, who loved the wages of unrighteousness.' Today, examine your heart. Do you love God or only His gifts? Gifting without faithfulness ends badly. Love God, not gain.
S: Balaam son of Beor, the soothsayer, was killed by Israel among their enemies. He spoke true blessings from God but loved money more and taught Midian how to lead Israel into sin. Examine your heart to see whether you love God or only His gifts, for gifting without faithfulness ends badly.
P: The way of Balaam. Joshua 13:22: 'Balaam also the son of Beor, the soothsayer, did the children of Israel slay.' Knowing God's words isn't loving God. Love Him, not gain.
