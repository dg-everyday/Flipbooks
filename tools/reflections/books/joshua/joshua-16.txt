# Joshua 16: The lot of Joseph and Ephraim

== 16:1
T: The lot of the children of Joseph
R: 'And the lot of the children of Joseph fell from Jordan by Jericho, unto the water of Jericho on the east, to the wilderness that goeth up from Jericho throughout mount Bethel.' Notice the lot of the children of Joseph. The second great allotment. Notice from Jordan by Jericho. Beginning where the conquest began. Notice unto the water of Jericho. The springs of the city. Notice throughout mount Bethel. Up into the central hills. Notice that Joseph's descendants received the heart of the land. Notice that Joseph, once rejected by his brothers, now had a central place among them. Notice that God honoured the one who had suffered. Genesis 49 says, 'Joseph is a fruitful bough, even a fruitful bough by a well.' Today, trust that God sees your suffering and will honour faithfulness in His time. Rejection is not the end of your story.
S: The lot of Joseph's children ran from the Jordan by Jericho up through the wilderness to mount Bethel. Joseph, once rejected by his brothers, now had descendants at the heart of the land, as God honoured the one who had suffered. Trust that God sees your suffering and will honour faithfulness in His time.
P: A fruitful bough. Joshua 16:1: 'the lot of the children of Joseph fell from Jordan by Jericho.' Joseph was once rejected; now his sons held the heart of the land. Rejection isn't the end.

== 16:2
T: From Bethel to Luz
R: 'And goeth out from Bethel to Luz, and passeth along unto the borders of Archi to Ataroth.' It went on from Bethel to Luz, crossed over to the territory of the Arkites in Ataroth. Notice from Bethel to Luz. Two names closely linked. Notice that Luz was the old name of Bethel. Notice that Jacob renamed it after his dream. Notice unto the borders of Archi. The Archites, from whom David's loyal friend Hushai came. Notice to Ataroth. A border town. Notice that the place where Jacob met God was now Joseph's border. Notice that Joseph's sons inherited the place of their grandfather's encounter with God. Notice that spiritual heritage passes down through families. Genesis 28 says, 'he called the name of that place Bethel: but the name of that city was called Luz at the first.' Today, cherish the places where your family met God. Let your home become a Bethel for the next generation.
S: Joseph's border went out from Bethel to Luz and along the borders of the Archites to Ataroth. Luz was the old name of Bethel, where Jacob met God, and Joseph's sons inherited their grandfather's place of encounter. Cherish the places where your family met God, and let your home become a Bethel for the next generation.
P: A family Bethel. Joshua 16:2: 'And goeth out from Bethel to Luz.' Jacob met God here; his grandsons inherited it. Let your home be a Bethel for the next generation.

== 16:3
T: Down westward to the sea
R: 'And goeth down westward to the coast of Japhleti, unto the coast of Beth-horon the nether, and to Gezer: and the goings out thereof are at the sea.' Notice goeth down westward. Descending toward the coast. Notice unto the coast of Beth-horon the nether. Lower Beth-horon, near the great battle where the sun stood still. Notice and to Gezer. The unconquered city. Notice at the sea. To the Mediterranean. Notice that Joseph's border passed the place of Joshua's great miracle. Notice that the inheritance was marked by memories of God's power. Notice that Gezer, still unconquered, sat on the same line. Notice that God's power and remaining challenges lie side by side. Psalm 77 says, 'I will remember the works of the LORD: surely I will remember thy wonders of old.' Today, let the memory of God's past wonders give you courage to face what remains unconquered.
S: Joseph's border went down westward past lower Beth-horon to Gezer and the sea. It passed the place where the sun stood still, yet Gezer remained unconquered on the same line, showing God's power and remaining challenges side by side. Let the memory of God's past wonders give you courage to face what remains unconquered.
P: Wonders and challenges. Joshua 16:3: 'unto the coast of Beth-horon the nether, and to Gezer.' Past wonders sit beside present challenges. Remember His works and take courage.

== 16:4
T: Manasseh and Ephraim
R: 'So the children of Joseph, Manasseh and Ephraim, took their inheritance.' So Manasseh and Ephraim, the descendants of Joseph, received their inheritance. Notice so the children of Joseph. Summary. Notice Manasseh and Ephraim. Two sons, two tribes. Notice took their inheritance. They received what was promised. Notice that Jacob had crossed his hands to bless the younger Ephraim above the elder Manasseh. Notice that God's choices do not always follow human order. Notice that both brothers received a portion. Notice that grace is not limited to the firstborn. Genesis 48 records Jacob saying, 'his younger brother shall be greater than he.' Notice that both were blessed, even if differently. Today, trust that God's blessings do not depend on your place in line. He chooses according to grace. And rejoice when He blesses others, too.
S: The children of Joseph, Manasseh and Ephraim, took their inheritance. Jacob had crossed his hands to bless the younger Ephraim above Manasseh, showing that God's grace does not follow human order, yet both brothers received a portion. Trust that God's blessings do not depend on your place in line, and rejoice when He blesses others too.
P: Crossed hands. Joshua 16:4: 'So the children of Joseph, Manasseh and Ephraim, took their inheritance.' God's grace doesn't follow human order. Both brothers were blessed.

== 16:5
T: Ataroth-addar unto Beth-horon the upper
R: 'And the border of the children of Ephraim according to their families was thus: even the border of their inheritance on the east side was Ataroth-addar, unto Beth-horon the upper.' Notice the border of the children of Ephraim. Ephraim's own boundary. Notice according to their families. Every household. Notice Ataroth-addar. Crowns of glory, by name. Notice unto Beth-horon the upper. Upper Beth-horon, a strategic pass. Notice that Ephraim's land was in the central hill country, rich and well-watered. Notice that Ephraim would become the leading northern tribe. Notice that Joshua himself was from Ephraim. Notice that Ephraim's influence would grow, but so would its pride. Proverbs 16 says, 'Pride goeth before destruction, and an haughty spirit before a fall.' Today, if God has given you influence, hold it humbly. Prominence is a gift to steward, not a crown to boast in.
S: Ephraim's border by their families ran on the east from Ataroth-addar to upper Beth-horon. Ephraim, Joshua's own tribe, received rich central land and would grow in influence and later in pride. If God has given you influence, hold it humbly as a gift to steward rather than a crown to boast in.
P: Influence held humbly. Joshua 16:5: 'the border of the children of Ephraim… was Ataroth-addar, unto Beth-horon the upper.' Prominence is a gift to steward, not a crown to boast in.

== 16:6
T: Taanath-shiloh
R: 'And the border went out toward the sea to Michmethah on the north side; and the border went about eastward unto Taanath-shiloh, and passed by it on the east to Janohah.' Notice toward the sea to Michmethah. The northern boundary point. Notice the border went about eastward. A turning line. Notice unto Taanath-shiloh. A town whose name echoes Shiloh. Notice to Janohah. A place of rest, perhaps. Notice that Shiloh would soon become the place of the tabernacle in Ephraim's land. Notice that God's dwelling would be among this tribe. Notice that a border town bore a name linked with Shiloh, which may mean peace or rest. Notice that God placed His presence in the heart of Ephraim's territory. Genesis 49 speaks of the coming of Shiloh and says, 'unto him shall the gathering of the people be.' Today, remember that God's presence brings rest. Gather to Christ, the true Shiloh.
S: Ephraim's border went to Michmethah, turned east to Taanath-shiloh and passed on to Janohah. Shiloh, in Ephraim's land, would soon hold the tabernacle, and its name points to the Shiloh to whom the gathering of the people would be. Remember that God's presence brings rest, and gather to Christ, the true Shiloh.
P: Toward Shiloh. Joshua 16:6: 'the border went about eastward unto Taanath-shiloh.' God's dwelling would rest in Ephraim's land. Gather to Christ, the true place of rest.

Notice that Ephraim's border circled back to where Israel's journey in the land began. Notice that Jericho and the Jordan framed Ephraim's inheritance. Today, let your life keep returning to the beginning: the grace that first brought you through the waters. Your story still begins and ends with God's mighty acts.

Notice that the inheritance was complete and settled. Notice that God brings every allotment to a clear conclusion. Isaiah 42 says, 'A bruised reed shall he not break.' Notice that even reeds remind us of God's gentle care. Today, rest in God's settled gifts. What He gives is clear and secure. And remember His gentleness with what is fragile.

It also included all the towns and their villages that were set aside for Ephraim within the inheritance of Manasseh. Notice the separate cities for… Ephraim. Special towns. Notice among the inheritance of… Manasseh. Inside a brother's land. Notice all the cities with their villages. Carefully allotted. Notice that Ephraim's towns lay within Manasseh's territory. Notice that brothers' inheritances overlapped. Notice that this required cooperation and trust. Notice that God's family is meant to live interconnected, not isolated. Notice that shared space calls for patience and generosity. Psalm 133 says, 'Behold, how good and how pleasant it is for brethren to dwell together in unity!' Today, practise unity with believers whose lives overlap yours. Share space generously. God designed His family to live interconnected.

== 16:10
T: They drave not out the Canaanites
R: 'And they drave not out the Canaanites that dwelt in Gezer: but the Canaanites dwell among the Ephraimites unto this day, and serve under tribute.' Notice they drave not out. Incomplete obedience again. Notice the Canaanites that dwelt in Gezer. A stronghold left standing. Notice dwell among the Ephraimites. Living side by side. Notice and serve under tribute. Israel profited from them instead of removing them. Notice that Ephraim chose profit over obedience. Notice that forced labour seemed useful but kept idolatry close. Notice that compromise is often attractive because it pays. Notice that what we profit from, we are slow to remove. First Timothy 6 says, 'the love of money is the root of all evil.' Today, examine whether you are tolerating sin because it benefits you. Profitable compromise is still compromise. Choose obedience over gain.
S: Ephraim did not drive out the Canaanites in Gezer but let them live among them under tribute. They chose profit over obedience, keeping idolatry close because compromise paid. Examine whether you tolerate sin because it benefits you, and choose obedience over gain.
P: Profitable compromise. Joshua 16:10: 'they drave not out the Canaanites that dwelt in Gezer… and serve under tribute.' Compromise that pays is still compromise. Choose obedience over gain.
