# Joshua 19:1-16: Simeon and Zebulun

== 19:1
T: Within the inheritance of Judah
R: 'And the second lot came forth to Simeon, even for the tribe of the children of Simeon according to their families: and their inheritance was within the inheritance of the children of Judah.' Notice the second lot came forth to Simeon. Next in turn. Notice according to their families. Every household included. Notice their inheritance was within… Judah. Simeon had no separate territory. Notice that this fulfilled Jacob's words about Simeon's violence at Shechem. Genesis 49 says, 'I will divide them in Jacob, and scatter them in Israel.' Notice that the consequence of past sin shaped the tribe's future. Notice that God still gave Simeon a home, sheltered within Judah. Notice that judgment and mercy met. Notice that Simeon's survival depended on Judah. Today, remember that sin has consequences, but God's mercy still provides. And thank God that we are sheltered within the tribe of Judah, in Christ, the Lion of Judah.
S: The second lot fell to Simeon, whose inheritance lay within Judah's territory. This fulfilled Jacob's word that Simeon would be scattered for violence, yet God still gave the tribe a home sheltered within Judah. Remember that sin has consequences but God's mercy still provides, and thank Him that we are sheltered in Christ, the Lion of Judah.
P: Sheltered within Judah. Joshua 19:1: 'their inheritance was within the inheritance of the children of Judah.' Judgment and mercy met. We too are sheltered in the Lion of Judah.

Notice that Simeon, a tribe under judgment, received a town marked by God's comfort. Notice that God's grace meets us even in our consequences. Today, hear God's word at your Beersheba: fear not, for I am with thee. Even when your circumstances are shaped by past mistakes, His presence comforts and sustains.

Even when your circumstances are shaped by past mistakes, hear God say, Fear not, for I am with thee.

Notice that these towns appear in Judah's list too. Notice that Simeon's cities were carved out of Judah's. Notice that two tribes shared the same places. R: 'And Eltolad, and Bethul, and Hormah.' Notice Eltolad. Another southern town. Notice and Bethul. A small town. Notice and Hormah. A name meaning devoted or destruction. Notice that Hormah's name recalled the vow Israel made to devote enemy cities to God. Notice that Israel once fled there in defeat but later took it after a vow. Notice that devotion to God turned defeat into victory. Notice that what is fully given to God is no longer ours to keep. Notice that devotion means wholeheartedness. Romans 12 says, 'present your bodies a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable unto God.' Notice that in Christ, devotion is not destruction but dedication. Notice that our whole selves are given to Him. Today, devote yourself fully to God. Hold nothing back. What is wholly His becomes a place of victory and blessing.
S: Simeon's towns included Eltolad, Bethul and Hormah, whose name recalls Israel's vow to devote enemy cities to God. Devotion turned earlier defeat into victory, and in Christ devotion means presenting ourselves as living sacrifices. Devote yourself fully to God, holding nothing back, for what is wholly His becomes a place of victory.
P: Wholly devoted. Joshua 19:4: 'And Eltolad, and Bethul, and Hormah.' Hormah recalls a vow of devotion. Present yourself a living sacrifice. Hold nothing back from God.

== 19:5
T: Beth-marcaboth and Hazar-susah
R: 'And Ziklag, and Beth-marcaboth, and Hazar-susah.' Notice Ziklag. David's future refuge. Notice and Beth-marcaboth. A name meaning house of chariots. Notice and Hazar-susah. Village of horses. Notice that these names suggest military stores. Notice that later kings would multiply horses and chariots, against God's law. Notice that Solomon built cities for his chariots. Notice that trust in military strength crept into Israel's life. Isaiah 31 says, 'Woe to them that go down to Egypt for help; and stay on horses, and trust in chariots.' Notice that horses and chariots are not evil, but trusting in them is. Notice that Ziklag sits beside these towns, the place where David learned to trust God alone. Today, examine what you rely on for security. Use your resources, but do not trust them. Let God alone be your confidence.
S: Simeon's towns included Ziklag, Beth-marcaboth and Hazar-susah, names meaning house of chariots and village of horses. Later kings multiplied horses and chariots, and Isaiah warned against trusting them, while at Ziklag David learned to trust God alone. Use your resources without trusting in them, and let God alone be your confidence.
P: Chariots or God? Joshua 19:5: 'and Beth-marcaboth, and Hazar-susah.' House of chariots, village of horses. Use your resources, but trust God alone.

Count your blessings with gratitude rather than comparison, numbering your days and gifts so your heart grows wise.

== 19:9

R: 'Out of the portion of the children of Judah was the inheritance of the children of Simeon: for the part of the children of Judah was too much for them: therefore the children of Simeon had their inheritance within the inheritance of them.' Notice out of the portion of… Judah. Simeon's land came from Judah's share. Notice for the part… was too much for them. Judah had more than it needed. Notice therefore the children of Simeon had their inheritance. Abundance was shared. Notice that Judah's surplus became Simeon's provision. Notice that God often supplies one through the abundance of another. Notice that Judah did not cling to excess. Notice that abundance is meant to flow. Second Corinthians 8 says, 'that now at this time your abundance may be a supply for their want.' Today, look at where you have too much. Your surplus may be God's provision for someone else. Share it freely.
S: Simeon's inheritance came out of Judah's portion because Judah's share was too much for them. Judah's surplus became Simeon's provision, as God often supplies one person through another's abundance. Look at where you have too much, for your surplus may be God's provision for someone else.
P: Abundance shared. Joshua 19:9: 'the part of the children of Judah was too much for them.' Judah's surplus became Simeon's home. Your abundance may be someone's supply.

== 19:16

R: 'This is the inheritance of the children of Zebulun according to their families, these cities with their villages.' This is the inheritance of Zebulun, clan by clan: these towns and their villages. Notice this is the inheritance. Zebulun's portion completed. Notice according to their families. Every clan. Notice these cities with their villages. All accounted for. Notice that Zebulun's land lay in lower Galilee, near where Nazareth would later stand. Notice that Jesus grew up in this region. Notice that people later scoffed at His hometown. John 1 records Nathanael asking, 'Can there any good thing come out of Nazareth?' Notice that the best thing ever came from a despised place. Notice that God's inheritance for Zebulun included the region of the Saviour's childhood. Today, do not judge by reputation. Good things, even the best, can come from unlikely places. Come and see Jesus for yourself.
S: This was the inheritance of Zebulun by their families, its cities and villages. Do not judge by reputation, for the best things can come from unlikely places, so come and see Jesus for yourself.
