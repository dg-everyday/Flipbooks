# Joshua 20: The cities of refuge

== 20:1
T: The LORD also spake
R: 'The LORD also spake unto Joshua, saying.' Then the LORD said to Joshua. Notice the LORD also spake. After the land was divided, God had more to say. Notice unto Joshua. The faithful leader still listened. Notice saying. A new instruction was coming. Notice that the next word was about mercy. Notice that after giving land, God provided refuge. Notice that God's concern was not only for possession but for protection. Notice that a nation needs justice and mercy as well as territory. Notice that God anticipated human failures and made provision for them. Notice that He thought of the person who would one day make a terrible mistake. Psalm 103 says, 'The LORD is merciful and gracious, slow to anger, and plenteous in mercy.' Today, listen for God's word of mercy. He has already planned refuge for your failures. His heart toward you is gracious.
S: After the land was divided, the LORD spoke again to Joshua with a new instruction. His next word was about refuge, showing that God anticipated human failures and planned mercy for them. Listen for God's word of mercy, for He has already planned refuge for your failures and His heart toward you is gracious.
P: Mercy planned ahead. Joshua 20:1: 'The LORD also spake unto Joshua.' After land came refuge. The LORD is merciful and gracious, and He has planned mercy for your failures.

== 20:2
T: Appoint out for you cities of refuge
R: 'Speak to the children of Israel, saying, Appoint out for you cities of refuge, whereof I spake unto you by the hand of Moses.' Notice speak to the children of Israel. A national instruction. Notice appoint out for you. Deliberate selection. Notice cities of refuge. Places of safety. Notice whereof I spake… by the hand of Moses. This was promised long before. Notice that God had planned refuge before Israel even entered the land. Notice that mercy was built into the structure of the nation. Notice that refuge was not an afterthought. Notice that God's provision for sinners precedes their sin. Ephesians 1 says God 'hath chosen us in him before the foundation of the world.' Notice that our refuge in Christ was planned before we needed it. Today, rest in the mercy God planned for you long ago. Your salvation was not an afterthought. Christ, your refuge, was appointed before you ever fell.
S: God told Joshua to have Israel appoint cities of refuge, as He had spoken through Moses. Refuge was planned before Israel entered the land and built into the nation's structure, just as our refuge in Christ was chosen before the foundation of the world. Rest in the mercy God planned for you long ago, for Christ your refuge was appointed before you ever fell.
P: Appointed refuge. Joshua 20:2: 'Appoint out for you cities of refuge.' Planned long before they were needed. Your refuge in Christ was chosen before the foundation of the world.
H: #DailyGrace #Joshua202 #Refuge #Christ #Grace #BibleVerse

== 20:3
T: Unawares and unwittingly
R: 'That the slayer that killeth any person unawares and unwittingly may flee thither: and they shall be your refuge from the avenger of blood.' Notice the slayer that killeth any person unawares. An accident. Notice and unwittingly. Without intent. Notice may flee thither. Running was required. Notice your refuge from the avenger of blood. Protection from vengeance. Notice that God distinguished between intent and accident. Notice that justice weighs the heart. Notice that the guilty-by-accident still needed refuge. Notice that he had to flee; safety was not automatic. Psalm 91 says, 'I will say of the LORD, He is my refuge and my fortress: my God; in him will I trust.' Notice that the refuge was available, but the one in danger had to run to it. Today, run to God as your refuge. Do not wait. Whatever you have done, flee to Him. In Christ there is safety from condemnation.
S: The cities of refuge were for one who killed a person unawares and unwittingly, to flee there from the avenger of blood. God distinguished between intent and accident, yet the one in danger still had to run to safety. Run to God as your refuge without waiting, for in Christ there is safety from condemnation.
P: Flee to refuge. Joshua 20:3: 'may flee thither: and they shall be your refuge from the avenger of blood.' The refuge was there, but he had to run. Run to Christ.
H: #DailyGrace #Joshua203 #Refuge #Salvation #Christ #BibleVerse

== 20:4
T: Declare his cause
R: 'And when he that doth flee unto one of those cities shall stand at the entering of the gate of the city, and shall declare his cause in the ears of the elders of that city, they shall take him into the city unto them, and give him a place, that he may dwell among them.' Notice stand at the entering of the gate. He came to the door. Notice declare his cause. Honest confession. Notice in the ears of the elders. Before the right authority. Notice they shall take him into the city. Received. Notice give him a place. Belonging. Notice that he may dwell among them. A new home. Notice that confession led to welcome. Notice that the refugee was not only protected but given a place. First John 1 says, 'If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins.' Today, come to God's gate and tell Him the truth. He will take you in, give you a place and let you dwell with Him.
S: The one who fled was to stand at the city gate and declare his cause to the elders, who would take him in and give him a place to dwell. Honest confession led to welcome, and the refugee was given belonging as well as protection. Come to God's gate and tell Him the truth, for He will take you in and give you a place with Him.
P: A place to dwell. Joshua 20:4: 'they shall take him into the city unto them, and give him a place.' Confess honestly. God takes you in and gives you a home.
H: #DailyGrace #Joshua204 #Confession #Welcome #Belonging #BibleVerse

== 20:5
T: They shall not deliver him up
R: 'And if the avenger of blood pursue after him, then they shall not deliver the slayer up into his hand; because he smote his neighbour unwittingly, and hated him not beforetime.' Notice if the avenger of blood pursue after him. Danger followed. Notice they shall not deliver the slayer up. The city protected him. Notice into his hand. The avenger could not reach him. Notice because he smote his neighbour unwittingly. The reason for mercy. Notice and hated him not beforetime. His heart was clear of malice. Notice that the city stood between the refugee and the avenger. Notice that refuge meant real protection. Notice that the pursuer could not enter. John 10 records Jesus saying, 'neither shall any man pluck them out of my hand.' Today, rest in the security of your refuge. Accusations may pursue you, but in Christ no one can snatch you away. He stands between you and condemnation.
S: If the avenger of blood pursued, the city would not hand over the one who killed his neighbour unwittingly and without prior hatred. The city stood between the refugee and the avenger, giving real protection. Rest in the security of your refuge, for in Christ no one can pluck you out of His hand.
P: Not handed over. Joshua 20:5: 'they shall not deliver the slayer up into his hand.' The city protected him. In Christ, no one can pluck you out of His hand.
H: #DailyGrace #Joshua205 #Security #Protection #Assurance #BibleVerse

== 20:6
T: Until the death of the high priest
R: 'And he shall dwell in that city, until he stand before the congregation for judgment, and until the death of the high priest that shall be in those days: then shall the slayer return, and come unto his own city, and unto his own house.' Notice he shall dwell in that city. Safety required staying. Notice until he stand before the congregation for judgment. A fair hearing. Notice until the death of the high priest. A surprising condition. Notice then shall the slayer return. Freedom. Notice unto his own house. Restored home. Notice that the high priest's death set the refugee free. Notice that this points directly to Christ, our great High Priest, whose death sets us free. Hebrews 9 says, 'by his own blood he entered in once into the holy place, having obtained eternal redemption for us.' Today, rejoice that our High Priest has died and risen. His death has set you free. You may go home.
S: The refugee stayed in the city until judged before the congregation and until the death of the high priest, after which he could return home. The high priest's death set him free, pointing to Christ, whose death obtained eternal redemption. Rejoice that our great High Priest has died and risen, for His death has set you free.
P: Freed by a high priest's death. Joshua 20:6: 'until the death of the high priest… then shall the slayer return.' Christ, our High Priest, died to set us free. Go home.
H: #DailyGrace #Joshua206 #Freedom #Christ #Redemption #BibleVerse

== 20:7
T: Kedesh, Shechem and Hebron
R: 'And they appointed Kedesh in Galilee in mount Naphtali, and Shechem in mount Ephraim, and Kirjath-arba, which is Hebron, in the mountain of Judah.' Notice they appointed. Obedience. Notice Kedesh in Galilee. North. Notice Shechem in mount Ephraim. Centre. Notice Hebron in the mountain of Judah. South. Notice that the cities were spread out so that everyone could reach one. Notice that they were on mountains, visible from afar. Notice the meanings: Kedesh means holy; Shechem means shoulder; Hebron means fellowship. Notice that refuge offers holiness, a shoulder to carry us and fellowship with God. Matthew 5 says, 'A city that is set on an hill cannot be hid.' Notice that God made refuge easy to find. Today, see Christ as your refuge, high and visible. In Him you find holiness, a shoulder to lean on and fellowship with God.
S: Israel appointed Kedesh in Galilee, Shechem in mount Ephraim and Hebron in Judah as cities of refuge west of the Jordan. They were spread out and set on mountains, easy to see and reach, with names suggesting holiness, a shoulder and fellowship. See Christ as your refuge, high and visible, where you find holiness, strength and fellowship with God.
P: Set on a hill. Joshua 20:7: 'Kedesh… Shechem… and Kirjath-arba, which is Hebron.' Holy, shoulder, fellowship. Refuge on the mountains, visible to all. That's Christ.

== 20:8
T: Bezer, Ramoth and Golan
R: 'And on the other side Jordan by Jericho eastward, they assigned Bezer in the wilderness upon the plain out of the tribe of Reuben, and Ramoth in Gilead out of the tribe of Gad, and Golan in Bashan out of the tribe of Manasseh.' Notice on the other side Jordan. East as well as west. Notice Bezer in the wilderness. A fortress in the wild. Notice Ramoth in Gilead. Heights. Notice Golan in Bashan. The far north-east. Notice that the eastern tribes also had refuge. Notice that no region was left without a city of safety. Notice that God made refuge available to everyone, wherever they lived. Notice that distance was considered so no one would be caught before reaching safety. Isaiah 55 says, 'Seek ye the LORD while he may be found, call ye upon him while he is near.' Today, remember that refuge is near you. Wherever you are, God is within reach. Call on Him now.
S: East of the Jordan, Israel assigned Bezer in the wilderness for Reuben, Ramoth in Gilead for Gad and Golan in Bashan for Manasseh. No region was left without a refuge, and distance was planned so no one would be caught before reaching safety. Remember that refuge is near you wherever you are, so call on God now while He is near.
P: Refuge within reach. Joshua 20:8: 'Bezer… Ramoth in Gilead… and Golan in Bashan.' No region without refuge. Seek the LORD while He may be found; He is near.

== 20:9
T: And for the stranger
R: 'These were the cities appointed for all the children of Israel, and for the stranger that sojourneth among them, that whosoever killeth any person at unawares might flee thither, and not die by the hand of the avenger of blood, until he stood before the congregation.' Notice for all the children of Israel. Every Israelite. Notice and for the stranger that sojourneth among them. Foreigners too. Notice that whosoever. An open invitation. Notice might flee thither, and not die. Life preserved. Notice until he stood before the congregation. A fair judgment. Notice that refuge was not limited to Israelites. Notice that God's mercy included outsiders. Notice the word whosoever. Notice that it anticipates the gospel. Romans 8 says, 'There is therefore now no condemnation to them which are in Christ Jesus.' Today, rejoice that God's refuge is for whosoever. Insider or outsider, all who flee to Christ find safety.
S: The cities of refuge were appointed for all Israel and for the stranger among them, so that whoever killed someone unawares could flee there and not die. God's mercy included outsiders, and the word whosoever anticipates the gospel invitation. Rejoice that God's refuge is for whosoever, for all who flee to Christ find no condemnation.
P: For whosoever. Joshua 20:9: 'for all the children of Israel, and for the stranger.' Refuge for insiders and outsiders. There is no condemnation for those in Christ.
