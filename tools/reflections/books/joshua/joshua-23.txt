# Joshua 23: Joshua's farewell to the leaders

== 23:1
T: Joshua waxed old
R: 'And it came to pass a long time after that the LORD had given rest unto Israel from all their enemies round about, that Joshua waxed old and stricken in age.' Notice a long time after. Years of peace had passed. Notice the LORD had given rest. God's gift. Notice from all their enemies round about. Complete security. Notice that Joshua waxed old and stricken in age. The faithful leader neared the end. Notice that rest did not mean Joshua's work was over. Notice that he used his final season to strengthen the next generation. Notice that old age is a time to pass on wisdom. Psalm 71 says, 'Now also when I am old and greyheaded, O God, forsake me not; until I have shewed thy strength unto this generation.' Notice that the psalmist wanted to finish by teaching. Today, whatever your age, think about what you will pass on. Use every season to show God's strength to the next generation.
S: A long time after the LORD had given Israel rest, Joshua grew old and advanced in years. He used his final season not to rest idly but to strengthen the next generation. Whatever your age, think about what you will pass on, using every season to show God's strength to those who come after you.
P: Passing it on. Joshua 23:1: 'Joshua waxed old and stricken in age.' When I am old, O God, forsake me not, until I have shewed thy strength unto this generation.

R: 'And Joshua called for all Israel, and for their elders, and for their heads, and for their judges, and for their officers, and said unto them, I am old and stricken in age.' Notice Joshua called for all Israel. A national gathering. Notice their elders… heads… judges… officers. Every level of leadership. Notice and said unto them. Final words. Notice I am old and stricken in age. Honest about his condition. Notice that Joshua did not pretend to be younger than he was. Notice that his honesty gave weight to his words. Notice that he addressed leaders, who would carry the message to everyone. Notice that last words carry special weight. Second Peter 1 says, 'I think it meet, as long as I am in this tabernacle, to stir you up by putting you in remembrance.' Notice that faithful leaders use their remaining time to remind. Today, if you lead others, use your influence to remind them of what matters most. Speak while you can.
S: Joshua called all Israel with its elders, heads, judges and officers and told them honestly that he was old. He used his final words to address leaders who would carry the message to everyone, for last words carry special weight. If you lead others, use your influence to remind them of what matters most while you can.
P: Last words matter. Joshua 23:2: 'Joshua called for all Israel… and said unto them, I am old.' Faithful leaders stir others up by putting them in remembrance.

Notice all that the LORD your God hath done. God's works. Notice because of you. For their sake. Notice for the LORD your God is he that hath fought for you. The secret of every victory. Notice that Joshua did not mention his own leadership. Notice that he pointed entirely to God. Notice that the people had seen it with their own eyes. Notice that experience of God's faithfulness should build trust. Exodus 14 says, 'The LORD shall fight for you, and ye shall hold your peace.' Notice that this was true from the Red Sea to the conquest. Today, remember what you have seen God do. Give Him the credit, not yourself or others. The LORD is the One who has fought for you.
S: Joshua reminded Israel that they had seen all the LORD did to the nations for their sake, for the LORD Himself fought for them. He took no credit for his own leadership but pointed entirely to God. Remember what you have seen God do and give Him the credit, for He is the One who has fought for you.
P: He fought for you. Joshua 23:3: 'for the LORD your God is he that hath fought for you.' Joshua took no credit. Remember what God has done and give Him the glory.

Notice that faith and patience go together. Today, take up the unfinished work God has given you. What previous generations began, you are called to continue with faith and patience.
S: Joshua said he had divided by lot the remaining nations as an inheritance for the tribes, from the Jordan to the great sea. He assigned land not yet conquered, leaving the next generation to complete the task with faith and effort. Take up the unfinished work God has given you, continuing with faith and patience what others began.
P: Unfinished work. Joshua 23:4: 'I have divided unto you by lot these nations that remain.' The next generation had to finish it. Through faith and patience, inherit the promises.

== 23:5
T: He shall expel them

Notice the LORD your God, he shall expel them. God would act. Notice and drive them from out of your sight. Complete removal. Notice and ye shall possess their land. Israel's part. Notice as the LORD your God hath promised. The guarantee. Notice that the work ahead still depended on God. Notice that Joshua was leaving, but God was staying. Notice that the next generation did not need Joshua; they needed God. Deuteronomy 31 says, 'he will be with thee, he will not fail thee, neither forsake thee.' Notice that leaders change, but God remains. Today, do not put your trust in leaders, however good. Put it in God, who outlives every leader. He will finish what He promised.
S: Joshua promised that the LORD would expel the remaining nations so Israel could possess their land as He promised. Joshua was leaving but God was staying, and the next generation needed God more than Joshua. Put your trust not in leaders, however good, but in God, who will not fail or forsake you.
P: Leaders go; God stays. Joshua 23:5: 'the LORD your God, he shall expel them from before you.' Joshua was leaving, but God remained. He will not fail thee, neither forsake thee.

Scripture as the standard. Notice turn not aside… to the right hand or to the left. Staying on course. Notice that Joshua passed on the charge he had received. Notice that courage was needed not for battle but for obedience. Notice that keeping God's word takes more courage than fighting giants. Do not drift to the left or right. Let God's word keep you on the straight path.
S: Joshua urged Israel to be very courageous to keep and do all that is written in the book of the law, turning neither to the right nor the left. He passed on the charge God had given him, for keeping God's word takes more courage than fighting giants. Be courageous in obedience, hiding God's word in your heart to keep you on the straight path.
P: Courage to obey. Joshua 23:6: 'Be ye therefore very courageous to keep and to do all that is written.' Obedience takes courage. Hide God's word in your heart.

== 23:7
T: Neither make mention of their gods
R: 'That ye come not among these nations, these that remain among you; neither make mention of the names of their gods, nor cause to swear by them, neither serve them, nor bow yourselves unto them.' Notice that ye come not among these nations. Keep distinct. Notice neither make mention of the names of their gods. Not even casual talk. Notice nor cause to swear by them. No oaths in their names. Notice neither serve them, nor bow yourselves. No worship. Notice the progression: mixing, mentioning, swearing, serving, bowing. Notice that idolatry begins with small steps. Notice that familiarity with idols leads to worship of them. Notice that Joshua warned against the first step, not just the last. First John 5 says, 'Little children, keep yourselves from idols.' Today, watch the small steps toward idolatry. What you casually mention, you may soon admire, then serve. Keep yourself from idols at the first step.
S: Joshua warned Israel not to mix with the remaining nations, mention their gods' names, swear by them, serve them or bow to them. The progression shows that idolatry begins with small steps, and Joshua warned against the first step, not just the last. Watch the small steps toward idolatry, keeping yourself from idols at the very first step.
P: The first step. Joshua 23:7: 'neither make mention of the names of their gods… nor bow yourselves unto them.' Idolatry begins small. Little children, keep yourselves from idols.

Notice that Joshua commended their faithfulness so far. Notice that the call was to persevere, not to start over. Notice that cleaving is the opposite of drifting. Notice that the same word describes a husband cleaving to his wife. Acts 11 records Barnabas exhorting believers 'that with purpose of heart they would cleave unto the Lord.' Notice that cleaving requires purpose of heart. Today, hold fast to God with purpose. Keep doing what you have been doing. Do not loosen your grip on Him. Cleave to the Lord.
S: Joshua told Israel to cleave to the LORD their God as they had done until that day. He commended their faithfulness and called them to persevere, for cleaving is the opposite of drifting. Hold fast to God with purpose of heart, and do not loosen your grip on Him.
P: Cleave. Joshua 23:8: 'But cleave unto the LORD your God, as ye have done unto this day.' Hold fast with purpose of heart. Don't loosen your grip on God.

Notice that numbers do not determine outcomes when God is involved. Notice that one faithful person with God is a majority.
