# Joshua 24:1-15: God's story and the choice at Shechem

== 24:1
T: They presented themselves before God
R: 'And Joshua gathered all the tribes of Israel to Shechem, and called for the elders of Israel, and for their heads, and for their judges, and for their officers; and they presented themselves before God.' Notice Joshua gathered all the tribes. One last assembly. Notice to Shechem. Where Abraham first received the promise and built an altar. Notice called for the elders… heads… judges… officers. Every leader. Notice and they presented themselves before God. A solemn act of worship. Notice that Shechem was where Israel had renewed the covenant after Ai. Notice that Joshua brought the people back to a place of beginnings. Notice that standing before God is the right posture for big decisions. James 4 says, 'Draw nigh to God, and he will draw nigh to you.' Notice that God meets those who present themselves to Him. Today, present yourself before God. Come to Him deliberately, especially when important choices lie ahead. He draws near to those who draw near.
S: Joshua gathered all the tribes and their leaders to Shechem, and they presented themselves before God. Shechem was where Abraham first built an altar and where Israel had renewed the covenant, a place of beginnings for a solemn decision. Present yourself before God deliberately, especially when important choices lie ahead, for He draws near to those who draw near.
P: Before God. Joshua 24:1: 'Joshua gathered all the tribes of Israel to Shechem… and they presented themselves before God.' Draw nigh to God, and He will draw nigh to you.

== 24:2
T: They served other gods
R: 'And Joshua said unto all the people, Thus saith the LORD God of Israel, Your fathers dwelt on the other side of the flood in old time, even Terah, the father of Abraham, and the father of Nachor: and they served other gods.' Notice thus saith the LORD God of Israel. God speaks through Joshua. Notice your fathers dwelt on the other side of the flood. Beyond the Euphrates. Notice even Terah, the father of Abraham. A family history. Notice and they served other gods. An honest admission. Notice that Israel's story began with idolaters. Notice that God did not hide their pagan origins. Notice that grace began where there was no merit. Notice that God chose a family that was not seeking Him. Ephesians 2 describes our former state as 'having no hope, and without God in the world.' Notice that every believer's story begins in need. Today, remember where God found you. Your story, too, began without Him. Grace reached you before you reached for it.
S: God reminded Israel through Joshua that their ancestors, including Terah the father of Abraham, lived beyond the Euphrates and served other gods. Israel's story began with idolaters, for grace began where there was no merit. Remember where God found you, for grace reached you before you reached for it.
P: Where God found us. Joshua 24:2: 'Your fathers dwelt on the other side of the flood… and they served other gods.' Israel's story began with idolaters. Grace reached us first.

== 24:3
T: I took your father Abraham
R: 'And I took your father Abraham from the other side of the flood, and led him throughout all the land of Canaan, and multiplied his seed, and gave him Isaac.' Notice I took. God took the initiative. Notice your father Abraham from the other side. Out of idolatry. Notice and led him throughout all the land. God guided his journey. Notice and multiplied his seed. A promise fulfilled. Notice and gave him Isaac. The child of promise. Notice the repeated I in this speech. God was the actor. Notice that Abraham's faith was a response to God's call. Hebrews 11 says Abraham 'went out, not knowing whither he went.' Notice that God led him step by step. Notice that Isaac was a gift, not an achievement. Today, recognise that God took the first step toward you. Follow where He leads, even without knowing the whole route. What He gives, receive as gift.
S: God said He took Abraham from beyond the river, led him through Canaan, multiplied his descendants and gave him Isaac. God was the actor at every step, and Abraham's faith was a response to His call, going out not knowing where. Recognise that God took the first step toward you, and follow where He leads even without knowing the whole route.
P: God took the first step. Joshua 24:3: 'I took your father Abraham… and led him… and gave him Isaac.' He went out, not knowing whither he went. Follow where God leads.

== 24:4
T: Jacob went down into Egypt
R: 'And I gave unto Isaac Jacob and Esau: and I gave unto Esau mount Seir, to possess it; but Jacob and his children went down into Egypt.' Notice I gave unto Isaac Jacob and Esau. Twin sons, both gifts. Notice I gave unto Esau mount Seir. Esau received land quickly. Notice but Jacob and his children went down into Egypt. The chosen line went into hardship. Notice the contrast. Notice that the son of promise went down, while the other settled. Notice that God's chosen path sometimes leads through difficulty first. Notice that Egypt became both refuge and bondage. Genesis 46 records God telling Jacob, 'fear not to go down into Egypt; for I will there make of thee a great nation.' Notice that God was with them in the going down. Today, do not envy those who seem to settle quickly. God's path for you may go down before it goes up. He is with you in Egypt, making you into something great.
S: God gave Isaac Jacob and Esau, giving Esau mount Seir while Jacob and his children went down into Egypt. The son of promise went into hardship while the other settled, yet God told Jacob not to fear, for He would make him a great nation there. Do not envy those who seem to settle quickly, for God's path for you may go down before it goes up.
P: Down before up. Joshua 24:4: 'Esau mount Seir… but Jacob and his children went down into Egypt.' Fear not to go down; God will make you great there.

== 24:5
T: Afterward I brought you out
R: 'I sent Moses also and Aaron, and I plagued Egypt, according to that which I did among them: and afterward I brought you out.' Notice I sent Moses also and Aaron. God provided leaders. Notice and I plagued Egypt. God's judgment on the oppressor. Notice according to that which I did among them. Mighty acts. Notice and afterward I brought you out. Deliverance. Notice the word afterward. Suffering came first, then rescue. Notice that God sent help before the people knew to ask. Notice that every part of the Exodus was God's doing. Exodus 6 says, 'I will redeem you with a stretched out arm, and with great judgments.' Notice that redemption was costly and powerful. Today, take heart if you are still in Egypt. God has an afterward. He sends help, He acts and He brings His people out. Your afterward is coming.
S: God said He sent Moses and Aaron, plagued Egypt and afterward brought Israel out. Suffering came first and then rescue, as every part of the Exodus was God's powerful redemption. Take heart if you are still in your Egypt, for God has an afterward and He brings His people out.
P: Afterward. Joshua 24:5: 'I plagued Egypt… and afterward I brought you out.' God has an afterward. He redeems with a stretched out arm. Your afterward is coming.

== 24:6
T: Unto the Red sea
R: 'And I brought your fathers out of Egypt: and ye came unto the sea; and the Egyptians pursued after your fathers with chariots and horsemen unto the Red sea.' Notice I brought your fathers out. God again. Notice and ye came unto the sea. A dead end. Notice and the Egyptians pursued. The enemy followed. Notice with chariots and horsemen. Overwhelming force. Notice unto the Red sea. Trapped between sea and army. Notice that freedom was followed immediately by crisis. Notice that God led them to an impossible place. Notice that God sometimes leads us to dead ends to show His power. Exodus 14 records Moses saying, 'Fear ye not, stand still, and see the salvation of the LORD.' Notice that the impossible place became the place of the greatest miracle. Today, if you feel trapped between a sea and an army, stand still and watch for God's salvation. Dead ends are His specialty.
S: God brought Israel out of Egypt to the sea, where the Egyptians pursued them with chariots and horsemen. Freedom was followed at once by crisis, as God led them to an impossible place to show His power. If you feel trapped, stand still and see the salvation of the LORD, for dead ends are where He works.
P: Trapped at the sea. Joshua 24:6: 'the Egyptians pursued after your fathers with chariots and horsemen unto the Red sea.' Fear not, stand still, and see the salvation of the LORD.

== 24:7
T: When they cried unto the LORD
R: 'And when they cried unto the LORD, he put darkness between you and the Egyptians, and brought the sea upon them, and covered them; and your eyes have seen what I have done in Egypt: and ye dwelt in the wilderness a long season.' Notice when they cried unto the LORD. Prayer in crisis. Notice he put darkness between you and the Egyptians. God's protective barrier. Notice and brought the sea upon them. Judgment on the pursuers. Notice your eyes have seen. They were witnesses. Notice ye dwelt in the wilderness a long season. A long chapter followed. Notice that the cry came before the answer. Notice that God placed Himself between His people and danger. Psalm 34 says, 'This poor man cried, and the LORD heard him, and saved him out of all his troubles.' Notice that even after the miracle came a long wilderness. Today, cry to the LORD in your trouble. He can stand between you and what pursues you. And trust Him through the long seasons that follow.
S: When Israel cried to the LORD, He put darkness between them and the Egyptians and brought the sea over the pursuers, and then Israel lived long in the wilderness. God placed Himself between His people and danger, yet a long season followed the miracle. Cry to the LORD in your trouble, and trust Him through the long seasons that follow.
P: They cried; He answered. Joshua 24:7: 'when they cried unto the LORD, he put darkness between you and the Egyptians.' This poor man cried, and the LORD heard him.

== 24:8
T: The land of the Amorites
R: 'And I brought you into the land of the Amorites, which dwelt on the other side Jordan; and they fought with you: and I gave them into your hand, that ye might possess their land; and I destroyed them from before you.' Notice I brought you into the land of the Amorites. After the wilderness. Notice on the other side Jordan. East of the river. Notice and they fought with you. Real battles. Notice and I gave them into your hand. God gave victory. Notice that ye might possess their land. Purpose. Notice I destroyed them from before you. God's work. Notice that Sihon and Og fell before the Jordan crossing. Notice that God gave the new generation early victories to build faith. Psalm 136 says, 'Sihon king of the Amorites: for his mercy endureth for ever.' Notice that even judgment on Israel's enemies is counted among God's mercies to His people. Today, thank God for early victories that strengthened your faith. They were preparation for greater things.
S: God said He brought Israel into the land of the Amorites east of the Jordan, gave them victory and destroyed their enemies before them. The defeat of Sihon and Og gave the new generation early victories to build their faith. Thank God for early victories that strengthened your faith, for they prepared you for greater things.
P: Early victories. Joshua 24:8: 'I brought you into the land of the Amorites… and I gave them into your hand.' God's mercy endureth for ever. Early wins build faith.

Rest in God's blessing, for no curse can stand against those He has blessed in Christ.
