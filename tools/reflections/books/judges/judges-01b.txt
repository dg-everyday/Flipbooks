# Judges 1:19-36: Tribe after tribe did not drive them out

== 1:19
T: Chariots of iron
R: 'And the LORD was with Judah; and he drave out the inhabitants of the mountain; but could not drive out the inhabitants of the valley, because they had chariots of iron.' Notice the LORD was with Judah. God's presence was real. Notice he drave out the inhabitants of the mountain. Success in the hills. Notice but could not drive out the inhabitants of the valley. Failure in the lowlands. Notice because they had chariots of iron. The stated reason. Notice the tension: the LORD was with them, yet they could not. Notice that Joshua had promised victory over iron chariots. Notice that the problem was not God's power but Judah's faith. Notice that fear of the enemy's strength limited what they received. Mark 9 records Jesus saying, 'all things are possible to him that believeth.' Today, do not let the enemy's strength define your limits when God is with you. Iron chariots are no match for Him.
S: The LORD was with Judah, who drove out the hill dwellers but could not drive out the valley dwellers because they had chariots of iron. Joshua had promised victory over iron chariots, so the limit lay not in God's power but in Judah's faith. Do not let the enemy's strength define your limits when God is with you, for all things are possible to him that believes.
P: With God, yet could not? Judges 1:19: 'the LORD was with Judah… but could not drive out… because they had chariots of iron.' All things are possible to him that believeth.
H: #DailyGrace #Judges119 #Faith #Fear #Power #BibleVerse

== 1:20
T: Hebron unto Caleb
R: 'And they gave Hebron unto Caleb, as Moses said: and he expelled thence the three sons of Anak.' Notice they gave Hebron unto Caleb. The promise honoured. Notice as Moses said. Decades earlier. Notice and he expelled thence the three sons of Anak. Caleb finished his task. Notice the contrast with the previous verse. Judah could not; Caleb did. Notice that Caleb faced the giants, while others feared chariots. Notice that one man's faith succeeded where a tribe's fear failed. Notice that Caleb's wholehearted faith is the standard in this chapter of failures. Numbers 14 records God saying, 'But my servant Caleb, because he had another spirit with him, and hath followed me fully.' Notice that a different spirit produced different results. Today, ask God for Caleb's spirit. Follow Him fully. Where others see chariots, see God's promise, and act on it.
S: Hebron was given to Caleb as Moses had said, and he drove out the three sons of Anak. Where Judah feared chariots, Caleb faced giants, because he had another spirit and followed the LORD fully. Ask God for Caleb's spirit to follow Him fully and act on His promises where others see only obstacles.
P: Another spirit. Judges 1:20: 'they gave Hebron unto Caleb… and he expelled thence the three sons of Anak.' Others feared chariots; Caleb defeated giants. Follow God fully.

== 1:21
T: Benjamin did not drive them out
R: 'And the children of Benjamin did not drive out the Jebusites that inhabited Jerusalem; but the Jebusites dwell with the children of Benjamin in Jerusalem unto this day.' Notice the children of Benjamin did not drive out. Not could not, but did not. Notice the Jebusites that inhabited Jerusalem. The future holy city. Notice but the Jebusites dwell with the children of Benjamin. Coexistence. Notice unto this day. A lasting compromise. Notice the shift in language from could not to did not. Notice that this chapter moves from inability toward unwillingness. Notice that neglect can look like peace. Notice that the most important city remained in enemy hands. James 4 says, 'to him that knoweth to do good, and doeth it not, to him it is sin.' Notice that knowing God's will and not doing it is disobedience. Today, examine where you have chosen not to obey rather than been unable to. Be honest. Did not is not the same as could not.
S: The children of Benjamin did not drive out the Jebusites from Jerusalem, who lived with them to that day. The chapter shifts from could not to did not, moving from inability toward unwillingness. Examine where you have chosen not to obey rather than been unable to, for knowing good and not doing it is sin.
P: Did not, not could not. Judges 1:21: 'the children of Benjamin did not drive out the Jebusites.' Be honest about the difference. To him that knoweth to do good, and doeth it not, it is sin.

Ephraim and Manasseh. Notice they also went up. They joined the effort. Notice against Bethel. The place of Jacob's dream. Notice and the LORD was with them. The same assurance as Judah. Notice that God's presence was offered to every tribe. Notice that God was ready to help each one. Notice that the key question was whether they would trust and obey fully. Notice that Bethel, the house of God, needed reclaiming. Matthew 28 records Jesus promising, 'lo, I am with you alway, even unto the end of the world.' Notice that Christ's presence goes with His people. Today, remember that the Lord is with you in your tasks. His presence is the same for you as for anyone. The question is your response to it.
S: The house of Joseph went up against Bethel, and the LORD was with them. God's presence was offered to every tribe, and the key question was whether they would trust and obey fully. Remember that the Lord is with you always, and respond to His presence with faith and obedience.
P: The LORD was with them. Judges 1:22: 'the house of Joseph… went up against Bethel: and the LORD was with them.' Lo, I am with you alway. Respond to His presence.

R: 'And the house of Joseph sent to descry Bethel. (Now the name of the city before was Luz.)' The house of Joseph sent men to spy out Bethel, formerly called Luz. Notice the house of Joseph sent. Careful planning. Notice to descry Bethel. To scout it out. Notice now the name of the city before was Luz. The old name. Notice that they did reconnaissance before attacking. Notice that wisdom and faith worked together. Notice that God's presence did not replace sensible preparation. Notice the echo of Joshua sending spies to Jericho. Notice that Luz had become Bethel through Jacob's encounter with God, but the city itself was still Canaanite. Proverbs 24 says, 'For by wise counsel thou shalt make thy war.' Notice that careful planning honours God. Today, combine faith with wise preparation. Trust God fully, but also scout out the ground before you. Faith is not careless.
S: The house of Joseph sent men to spy out Bethel, formerly called Luz. They combined God's presence with sensible reconnaissance, as Joshua had done at Jericho. Combine faith with wise preparation, for by wise counsel you make your war.
P: Faith and planning. Judges 1:23: 'the house of Joseph sent to descry Bethel.' God was with them, and they still scouted carefully. By wise counsel thou shalt make thy war.

== 1:24

An ordinary citizen. Notice come forth out of the city. Leaving Luz. Notice shew us… the entrance into the city. A request for help. Notice and we will shew thee mercy. A promise of protection. Notice the echo of Rahab's story. Notice that mercy was offered in exchange for help. Notice that God's people offered grace even in war. Notice that one person's choice could save a whole family. Notice that the man had the opportunity to side with God's people. Matthew 5 records Jesus saying, 'Blessed are the merciful: for they shall obtain mercy.' Today, be ready to offer mercy, even in conflict. And when mercy is offered to you, receive it gratefully. Grace often comes through unexpected meetings.
S: The spies saw a man leaving the city and asked him to show them the entrance, promising to show him mercy. Like Rahab's story, mercy was offered even in war, and one person's choice could save a whole family. Be ready to offer mercy even in conflict, for the merciful shall obtain mercy.
P: Mercy offered. Judges 1:24: 'Shew us, we pray thee, the entrance into the city, and we will shew thee mercy.' Blessed are the merciful. Offer grace even in conflict.

== 1:25

He helped. Notice they smote the city. Judgment came. Notice but they let go the man and all his family. Mercy kept. Notice that Israel kept its promise, as with Rahab. Notice that the man's family was spared too. Notice the difference from Rahab, however: he was let go, not welcomed in. Notice that he was spared but not joined to Israel. Notice that being rescued is not the same as belonging. Matthew 19 records the rich young ruler, who met Jesus but 'went away sorrowful.' Notice that some encounter grace but do not stay. S: When the man showed the entrance, Israel struck the city but let him and his family go. Israel kept its promise as with Rahab, yet unlike Rahab he was released rather than joined to God's people. Do not be content merely to be spared, but come all the way into God's family.
P: Spared but not joined. Judges 1:25: 'they let go the man and all his family.' Rahab joined Israel; this man went away. Don't just escape; belong to God's family.

Do not rebuild your old life after God has rescued you, but embrace the house of God.
