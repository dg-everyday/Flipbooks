# Judges 5:1-15: The song of Deborah and Barak

== 5:1
T: Then sang Deborah and Barak
R: 'Then sang Deborah and Barak the son of Abinoam on that day, saying.' On that day Deborah and Barak son of Abinoam sang this song. Notice then. After the victory. Notice sang. Worship was the first response. Notice Deborah and Barak. Leader and commander together. Notice on that day. Without delay. Notice that the battle ended in a song. Notice that victory turned into praise. Notice that this song is one of the oldest poems in Scripture. Notice that God's people have always sung their deliverances. Exodus 15 records an earlier song: 'Then sang Moses and the children of Israel this song unto the LORD.' Notice that Deborah's song echoes the song of the Red Sea. Notice that praise preserves the memory of God's acts. Today, sing about what God has done for you. Do not let victories pass without praise. A song keeps the memory of His goodness alive.
S: On the day of victory Deborah and Barak son of Abinoam sang a song. The battle ended in praise, echoing Moses' song at the Red Sea, for God's people have always sung their deliverances. Sing about what God has done for you, for praise keeps the memory of His goodness alive.
P: Victory becomes a song. Judges 5:1: 'Then sang Deborah and Barak the son of Abinoam on that day.' Don't let victories pass without praise. Sing what God has done.

God's justice. Notice when the people willingly offered themselves. Volunteers. Notice that God's victory came through willing people. Notice that no one was forced to fight. Notice that willingness is worth celebrating. Notice that God values those who offer themselves freely. Notice that leaders and people both stepped forward. Romans 12 says, 'present your bodies a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable unto God.' Notice that offering ourselves is the right response to God's mercy. Today, offer yourself willingly to God's work. Do not wait to be compelled. Volunteer your time, gifts and life. God celebrates willing hearts.
S: The song begins by praising the LORD for avenging Israel when the people willingly offered themselves. God's victory came through volunteers, and their willingness was worth celebrating. Offer yourself willingly to God's work as a living sacrifice, for He celebrates willing hearts.
P: Willing hearts. Judges 5:2: 'Praise ye the LORD for the avenging of Israel, when the people willingly offered themselves.' Present yourself a living sacrifice. Volunteer for God.

Notice that praise is meant to be heard beyond the congregation. Today, let your praise be public. Tell others what God has done. S: Deborah called on kings and princes to listen as she sang praise to the LORD God of Israel. Her song announced God's greatness to the world's rulers, making worship a witness. Let your praise be public, declaring God's glory and wonders to those who do not yet know Him.
P: Hear, O ye kings. Judges 5:3: 'I, even I, will sing unto the LORD.' Praise is a witness. Declare His glory among all people.

== 5:4
T: The earth trembled
R: 'LORD, when thou wentest out of Seir, when thou marchedst out of the field of Edom, the earth trembled, and the heavens dropped, the clouds also dropped water.' Notice LORD, when thou wentest out of Seir. God on the march. Notice when thou marchedst out of the field of Edom. From the south. Notice the earth trembled. Creation shook. Notice and the heavens dropped. Rain fell. Notice the clouds also dropped water. A storm. Notice that Deborah described God as a warrior marching from the desert. Notice that the rain that flooded the Kishon came from His hand. Notice that the victory was God's doing in nature. Psalm 68 echoes this: 'The earth shook, the heavens also dropped at the presence of God.' Notice that God's presence shakes creation. Today, remember that the God you worship commands earth and sky. Storms obey Him. Trust Him when you need Him to move on your behalf.
S: Deborah sang that when the LORD marched out of Seir and Edom, the earth trembled and the heavens and clouds poured down water. The rain that flooded the Kishon came from God's hand as He marched as a warrior. Remember that the God you worship commands earth and sky, and trust Him to move on your behalf.
P: Earth trembled. Judges 5:4: 'the earth trembled, and the heavens dropped, the clouds also dropped water.' The earth shook at the presence of God. He commands the storm.

Notice the mountains melted. Overwhelming power. Notice from before the LORD. In His presence. Notice even that Sinai. The mountain of the covenant. Notice from before the LORD God of Israel. Israel's covenant God. Notice that Deborah linked this victory to Sinai. Notice that the God who shook Sinai was the same God who fought at Kishon. Notice that God's power had not diminished since Moses. Notice that the covenant God still acted for His people. Psalm 97 says, 'The hills melted like wax at the presence of the LORD.' Notice that nothing stands firm before Him. Today, remember that the God of Sinai is your God. His power is the same today. Mountains of difficulty melt before His presence.
S: Deborah sang that the mountains melted before the LORD, even Sinai before the LORD God of Israel. She linked the victory to Sinai, showing that the covenant God's power had not diminished since Moses. Remember that the God of Sinai is your God, and mountains of difficulty melt before His presence.
P: Mountains melt. Judges 5:5: 'The mountains melted from before the LORD, even that Sinai.' The hills melted like wax at His presence. Your mountains can melt too.

Notice in the days of Jael. Her time too. Notice the highways were unoccupied. Main roads were abandoned. Notice and the travelers walked through byways. Hidden paths. Notice that fear had emptied the roads. Notice that ordinary life had become dangerous. Notice that oppression forced people into hiding. Notice that normal travel, trade and fellowship had ceased. Isaiah 35 says, 'And an highway shall be there, and a way, and it shall be called The way of holiness.' Notice that God promises a safe road for His people. Notice that His deliverance reopens the highways. Today, if fear has driven you onto hidden byways, ask God to restore your confidence. In Christ, the highway of holiness is open. Walk it boldly.
S: In the days of Shamgar and Jael the highways were empty, and travellers used hidden byways. Fear had emptied the roads and forced ordinary life into hiding under oppression. If fear has driven you onto hidden paths, ask God to restore your confidence, for in Christ the way of holiness is open.
P: Empty highways. Judges 5:6: 'the highways were unoccupied, and the travelers walked through byways.' Fear hid the people. God opens the highway of holiness. Walk boldly.

== 5:8
T: They chose new gods
R: 'They chose new gods; then was war in the gates: was there a shield or spear seen among forty thousand in Israel?' Notice they chose new gods. The root problem. Notice then was war in the gates. Consequence followed. Notice was there a shield or spear seen. Israel was disarmed. Notice among forty thousand in Israel. Helpless numbers. Notice the direct link: new gods, then war. Notice that spiritual unfaithfulness led to national danger. Notice that Israel had numbers but no weapons. Notice that idolatry left them defenceless. Jeremiah 2 asks, 'Hath a nation changed their gods, which are yet no gods?' Notice the foolishness of trading the true God for nothing. Notice that new gods are never better than the old true God. Today, be wary of chasing new gods, whatever form they take. Trends and idols promise much but leave you defenceless. Stay with the God who has proven Himself.
S: Deborah sang that Israel chose new gods, then war came to the gates, and no shield or spear was seen among forty thousand. Spiritual unfaithfulness led directly to national danger, leaving Israel numerous but defenceless. Be wary of chasing new gods in any form, and stay with the God who has proven Himself.
P: New gods, then war. Judges 5:8: 'They chose new gods; then was war in the gates.' Idolatry left Israel defenceless. Hath a nation changed their gods, which are yet no gods?
