# Judges 8:18-35: The LORD shall rule over you, a golden snare, and forgetfulness

== 8:18
T: The men slain at Tabor
R: 'Then said he unto Zebah and Zalmunna, What manner of men were they whom ye slew at Tabor? And they answered, As thou art, so were they; each one resembled the children of a king.' Notice then said he unto Zebah and Zalmunna. Gideon questioned the captives. Notice what manner of men were they whom ye slew at Tabor? A painful question. Notice as thou art, so were they. They looked like Gideon. Notice each one resembled the children of a king. Noble in bearing. Notice that the kings had killed men who resembled Gideon. Notice that a personal grief lay behind Gideon's pursuit. Notice that he was looking for the truth about his family. Notice that injustice to loved ones cries out. Genesis 4 records God saying to Cain, 'the voice of thy brother's blood crieth unto me from the ground.' Notice that God hears the cry of the wronged. Today, know that God sees every injustice done to those you love. Bring your grief to Him. He hears.
S: Gideon asked the captured kings about the men they had killed at Tabor, and they said each looked like Gideon, like a king's son. A personal grief lay behind his pursuit, for the slain were his own family. Know that God sees every injustice done to those you love, and bring your grief to Him, for He hears the cry of the wronged.
P: A personal grief. Judges 8:18: 'What manner of men were they whom ye slew at Tabor?… As thou art, so were they.' God hears the cry of the wronged. Bring Him your grief.

== 8:19
T: They were my brethren
R: 'And he said, They were my brethren, even the sons of my mother: as the LORD liveth, if ye had saved them alive, I would not slay you.' Notice they were my brethren. Gideon's own brothers. Notice even the sons of my mother. Close family. Notice as the LORD liveth. A solemn oath. Notice if ye had saved them alive. If they had spared them. Notice I would not slay you. Mercy would have been possible. Notice that Gideon's motive was now personal revenge. Notice that his mission had shifted from God's deliverance to family vengeance. Notice that grief can turn into vendetta. Notice that the man called by God now acted as an avenger. Romans 12 says, 'Recompense to no man evil for evil.' Notice that even justified anger needs God's limits. Today, do not let personal grief turn God's work into your vendetta. Serve God's purposes, not your own revenge. Leave recompense to Him.
S: Gideon said the slain were his own brothers, the sons of his mother, and swore he would have spared the kings had they spared them. His mission had shifted from God's deliverance toward personal revenge, as grief turned into vendetta. Do not let personal grief turn God's work into your vendetta, but leave recompense to God.
P: Grief or vendetta? Judges 8:19: 'They were my brethren, even the sons of my mother.' Gideon's mission turned personal. Recompense to no man evil for evil.

R: 'And he said unto Jether his firstborn, Up, and slay them. But the youth drew not his sword: for he feared, because he was yet a youth.' Notice he said unto Jether his firstborn. His eldest son. Notice up, and slay them. A command to kill. Notice but the youth drew not his sword. He hesitated. Notice for he feared. Fear held him back. Notice because he was yet a youth. Too young. Notice that Gideon tried to pull his son into his vengeance. Notice that the boy's fear echoes Gideon's own early fear. Notice that what we model, our children absorb. Notice that the youth was not ready for such a task. Proverbs 22 says, 'Train up a child in the way he should go: and when he is old, he will not depart from it.' Notice that training shapes children. Today, think about what you are training your children to do. Do not pull them into your battles of anger. Train them in the way of the LORD.
S: Gideon told his firstborn son Jether to kill the kings, but the youth was afraid and did not draw his sword. Gideon tried to draw his son into his own vengeance, though the boy was not ready. Think about what you train your children to do, and train them in the LORD's way rather than in your battles of anger.
P: What we teach children. Judges 8:20: 'Up, and slay them. But the youth drew not his sword: for he feared.' Train up a child in the way he should go.

When others praise you for what God has done, redirect the glory to Him, for success is a dangerous moment for the heart.

== 8:23
T: The LORD shall rule over you
R: 'And Gideon said unto them, I will not rule over you, neither shall my son rule over you: the LORD shall rule over you.' Notice and Gideon said unto them. His answer. Notice I will not rule over you. A refusal. Notice neither shall my son rule over you. No dynasty. Notice the LORD shall rule over you. God alone is King. Notice that Gideon gave the right answer. Notice that he affirmed God's kingship over Israel. Notice that he refused the title, though his actions afterward did not fully match his words. Notice that saying the right thing is not the same as living it. First Samuel 8 records God's later grief, 'they have rejected me, that I should not reign over them.' Notice that God desires to be King of His people. Today, let the LORD rule over you. Not leaders, not celebrities, not yourself. Say it and live it: The LORD shall rule over me.
S: Gideon refused to rule over Israel or let his son rule, declaring that the LORD would rule over them. He affirmed God's kingship rightly, though his later actions did not fully match his words. Let the LORD rule over you, not only in what you say but in how you live.
P: The LORD shall rule. Judges 8:23: 'I will not rule over you… the LORD shall rule over you.' The right words. Now live them. Let God be King of your life.

Notice we will willingly give them. Eager generosity. Notice and they spread a garment. A collection cloth. Notice and did cast therein every man the earrings. Everyone gave. Notice that the people gave gladly. Notice that generosity is good, but the purpose matters. Notice that this scene echoes Israel giving gold for the golden calf. Notice that a generous heart can be misdirected. First Chronicles 29 records David's prayer, 'all things come of thee, and of thine own have we given thee.' Notice that true giving is directed to God. Today, give generously, but give wisely. Make sure your gifts go toward God's purposes, not toward something that will become a snare.
S: The people willingly spread a garment and each threw in the golden earrings from his plunder. Their generosity was eager, but the scene echoes Israel giving gold for the golden calf, for a generous heart can be misdirected. Give generously but wisely, making sure your gifts serve God's purposes.
P: Generous, but directed where? Judges 8:25: 'We will willingly give them.' Generosity is good; direction matters. Of thine own have we given thee.

== 8:27
T: A snare unto Gideon
R: 'And Gideon made an ephod thereof, and put it in his city, even in Ophrah: and all Israel went thither a whoring after it: which thing became a snare unto Gideon, and to his house.' Notice Gideon made an ephod thereof. A priestly garment of gold. Notice and put it in his city, even in Ophrah. Where he once built an altar to the LORD. Notice and all Israel went thither a whoring after it. They worshipped it. Notice which thing became a snare. A trap. Notice unto Gideon, and to his house. His family suffered. Notice that the man who tore down Baal's altar made a new object of false worship. Notice that his intentions may have been good, but the result was idolatry. Second Kings 18 records that Israel burned incense to Moses' bronze serpent. Notice that even good things can become idols. Today, examine the good things in your life. Have any become objects of trust in place of God? Remove them before they become snares.
S: Gideon made the gold into an ephod and placed it in Ophrah, and all Israel worshipped it, so it became a snare to Gideon and his house. The man who tore down Baal's altar created a new object of false worship, perhaps with good intentions. Examine the good things in your life, and remove any that have become objects of trust in place of God.
P: A golden snare. Judges 8:27: 'Gideon made an ephod… and all Israel went thither a whoring after it.' Even good intentions can produce idols. Remove snares before they trap you.
