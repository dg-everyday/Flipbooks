# Judges 17: Micah's idols and a priest for hire

== 17:1
T: A man named Micah
R: 'And there was a man of mount Ephraim, whose name was Micah.' Now a man named Micah from the hill country of Ephraim. Notice and there was a man. A new story begins. Notice of mount Ephraim. Near Shiloh, where the tabernacle stood. Notice whose name was Micah. A name meaning who is like the LORD? Notice that this section of Judges no longer follows a judge. Notice that it shows the spiritual condition of ordinary people. Notice the irony of his name, for he would make idols. Notice that a name can confess what a life denies. Micah 7 asks, 'Who is a God like unto thee, that pardoneth iniquity?' Notice that the true answer to Micah's name is: no one. Notice that nothing compares to the LORD. Today, let your life match the confession of your name as a Christian. No idol is like the LORD. Live as one who knows there is none like Him.
S: A man named Micah lived in the hill country of Ephraim, near the tabernacle at Shiloh. His name means who is like the LORD, yet he would make idols, a confession his life denied. Let your life match the confession of your name, for no idol is like the LORD who pardons iniquity.
P: Who is like the LORD? Judges 17:1: 'there was a man of mount Ephraim, whose name was Micah.' His name asked who is like the LORD; his life made idols. Let your life match your confession.

== 17:2
T: The silver is with me
R: 'And he said unto his mother, The eleven hundred shekels of silver that were taken from thee, about which thou cursedst… behold, the silver is with me; I took it. And his mother said, Blessed be thou of the LORD, my son.' Notice the eleven hundred shekels of silver. A large sum. Notice that were taken from thee. Stolen. Notice about which thou cursedst. The mother had pronounced a curse. Notice behold, the silver is with me; I took it. Micah confessed. Notice and his mother said, Blessed be thou of the LORD. She reversed the curse. Notice that Micah stole from his own mother. Notice that he confessed, perhaps out of fear of the curse. Notice that confession was good, but fear rather than love may have prompted it. Exodus 20 says, 'Thou shalt not steal.' Notice that the commandments were being broken within a family. Today, confess wrongs quickly and honestly. Make restitution. But let love for God, not just fear of consequences, drive your repentance.
S: Micah confessed to his mother that he had taken the eleven hundred shekels of silver over which she had pronounced a curse, and she blessed him in the LORD's name. He stole from his own mother and confessed perhaps more from fear of the curse than love. Confess wrongs quickly and make restitution, letting love for God rather than fear drive your repentance.
P: Stolen from his mother. Judges 17:2: 'behold, the silver is with me; I took it.' Confession is good; let love, not fear, drive it. Thou shalt not steal.

== 17:3
T: To make a graven image
R: 'And when he had restored the eleven hundred shekels of silver to his mother, his mother said, I had wholly dedicated the silver unto the LORD from my hand for my son, to make a graven image and a molten image: now therefore I will restore it unto thee.' Notice when he had restored the silver. Restitution. Notice I had wholly dedicated the silver unto the LORD. Religious language. Notice to make a graven image and a molten image. Direct disobedience. Notice now therefore I will restore it unto thee. A twisted blessing. Notice that she dedicated silver to the LORD to break His command. Notice the confusion of mixing God's name with idolatry. Notice that sincere intentions do not make wrong worship right. Exodus 20 says, 'Thou shalt not make unto thee any graven image.' Notice that God had clearly forbidden this. Today, do not use God's name to justify what His word forbids. Sincere feelings are not enough. Worship Him as He commands.
S: When Micah returned the silver, his mother said she had dedicated it to the LORD to make a carved image and a cast idol for her son. She used God's name to break His command, mixing sincere devotion with forbidden worship. Do not use God's name to justify what His word forbids, but worship Him as He commands.
P: Dedicated to the LORD for an idol. Judges 17:3: 'I had wholly dedicated the silver unto the LORD… to make a graven image.' Sincerity isn't enough. Worship as God commands.

== 17:4
T: Two hundred shekels
R: 'Yet he restored the money unto his mother; and his mother took two hundred shekels of silver, and gave them to the founder, who made thereof a graven image and a molten image: and they were in the house of Micah.' Notice and his mother took two hundred shekels. Only part of it. Notice and gave them to the founder. A silversmith. Notice who made thereof a graven image and a molten image. The idols made. Notice and they were in the house of Micah. Idols at home. Notice that she had wholly dedicated eleven hundred, yet used only two hundred. Notice that even her false devotion was partial. Notice that the rest was apparently kept. Acts 5 records Ananias, who 'kept back part of the price.' Notice that partial dedication while claiming full is deceit. Today, be honest about what you give to God. Do not claim to give all while keeping back. And give to Him as He desires, not to idols.
S: Micah returned the money, and his mother gave two hundred shekels to a silversmith, who made a carved image and a cast idol that were placed in Micah's house. She had wholly dedicated eleven hundred shekels yet used only two hundred, making even her false devotion partial. Be honest about what you give to God, not claiming to give all while keeping back.
P: Partial devotion. Judges 17:4: 'his mother took two hundred shekels of silver, and gave them to the founder.' She dedicated all, gave part. Like Ananias, kept back part. Be honest with God.

== 17:5
T: An house of gods
R: 'And the man Micah had an house of gods, and made an ephod, and teraphim, and consecrated one of his sons, who became his priest.' Notice and the man Micah had an house of gods. A private shrine. Notice and made an ephod. Copying the priest's garment. Notice and teraphim. Household idols. Notice and consecrated one of his sons. Self-appointed priesthood. Notice who became his priest. Family religion. Notice that Micah built his own religion at home. Notice that he copied parts of true worship while ignoring God's commands. Notice that he did not go to the tabernacle nearby. Notice that do-it-yourself religion replaces God's way with ours. Colossians 2 warns of practices that 'have indeed a shew of wisdom in will worship.' Notice that self-made worship only looks wise. Today, do not build your own religion from bits you like. Worship God His way. A private shrine of our own design cannot replace His appointed way.
S: Micah had a shrine with an ephod and household idols and made one of his sons his priest. He built his own religion at home, copying parts of true worship while ignoring God's commands and the nearby tabernacle. Do not build your own religion from pieces you like, but worship God in His appointed way.
P: Do-it-yourself religion. Judges 17:5: 'Micah had an house of gods, and made an ephod, and teraphim.' Self-made worship only looks wise. Worship God His way.

== 17:6
T: Right in his own eyes
R: 'In those days there was no king in Israel, but every man did that which was right in his own eyes.' Notice in those days. A summary of the age. Notice there was no king in Israel. No central leadership. Notice but every man did that which was right in his own eyes. Moral chaos. Notice that this verse explains everything that follows. Notice that without authority, everyone became their own standard. Notice that the problem was not only no human king but rejection of God as King. Notice that personal opinion replaced God's law. Isaiah 53 says, 'All we like sheep have gone astray; we have turned every one to his own way.' Notice that going our own way is the essence of sin. Notice that this verse describes every heart apart from God. Today, do not let your own eyes be your final authority. Submit to God's word. Let Christ be your King, not your preferences.
S: In those days there was no king in Israel, and everyone did what was right in his own eyes. Without authority everyone became his own standard, for the deeper problem was rejecting God as King. Do not let your own eyes be your final authority, but let Christ be your King, for all we like sheep have gone astray.
P: Right in his own eyes. Judges 17:6: 'every man did that which was right in his own eyes.' All we like sheep have gone astray. Let Christ be King, not your preferences.

== 17:7
T: A young Levite
R: 'And there was a young man out of Bethlehemjudah of the family of Judah, who was a Levite, and he sojourned there.' Notice and there was a young man. A new character. Notice out of Bethlehemjudah. From Bethlehem in Judah. Notice of the family of Judah. Living among Judah. Notice who was a Levite. A minister by birth. Notice and he sojourned there. Temporarily living there. Notice that Bethlehem was not a Levitical city. Notice that this Levite was outside his appointed place. Notice that the Levites were meant to teach God's law, but he was wandering. Notice that spiritual leaders had lost their way too. Jeremiah 23 records God saying, 'I have not sent these prophets, yet they ran.' Notice that not every minister is following God's call. Today, pray for spiritual leaders to stay faithful to their calling. And examine whether you are where God has placed you or merely wandering.
S: A young Levite from Bethlehem in Judah was living there temporarily. Bethlehem was not a Levitical city, so this minister was outside his appointed place, showing that spiritual leaders too had lost their way. Pray for spiritual leaders to stay faithful, and examine whether you are where God placed you or merely wandering.
P: A wandering Levite. Judges 17:7: 'a young man out of Bethlehemjudah… who was a Levite, and he sojourned there.' Even ministers had lost their way. Stay where God places you.

== 17:8
T: To sojourn where he could find a place
R: 'And the man departed out of the city from Bethlehemjudah to sojourn where he could find a place: and he came to mount Ephraim to the house of Micah, as he journeyed.' Notice the man departed out of the city. Leaving. Notice to sojourn where he could find a place. Looking for opportunity. Notice and he came to mount Ephraim. Wandering north. Notice to the house of Micah. By chance, it seemed. Notice as he journeyed. Without clear direction. Notice that the Levite was looking for a position, not following a call. Notice that his search was about where he could find a place, not where God wanted him. Notice that restlessness led him to an idol shrine. Philippians 4 says, 'I have learned, in whatsoever state I am, therewith to be content.' Notice that contentment guards against restless wandering. Today, seek God's direction before seeking opportunity. Restless searching can lead you into the wrong house. Be content where He leads.
S: The Levite left Bethlehem to live wherever he could find a place and came to Micah's house in the hill country of Ephraim. He was looking for a position rather than following a call, and restlessness led him to an idol shrine. Seek God's direction before seeking opportunity, learning contentment where He leads.
P: Looking for a place. Judges 17:8: 'to sojourn where he could find a place.' Opportunity, not calling. Restlessness led him astray. Learn contentment in God's leading.

== 17:9
T: Whence comest thou?
R: 'And Micah said unto him, Whence comest thou? And he said unto him, I am a Levite of Bethlehemjudah, and I go to sojourn where I may find a place.' Notice whence comest thou? Micah asked. Notice I am a Levite of Bethlehemjudah. He identified himself by role. Notice and I go to sojourn. Still wandering. Notice where I may find a place. Seeking a position. Notice that the Levite openly admitted he had no fixed purpose. Notice that his identity as a Levite became a marketable asset. Notice that Micah saw an opportunity. Notice that both men were seeking advantage. Hebrews 13 says, 'For here have we no continuing city, but we seek one to come.' Notice that believers seek a heavenly home, not merely a comfortable position. Today, let your identity in God shape your direction, not just your search for security. Seek first the city to come, and let God place you.
S: Micah asked where he came from, and the Levite said he was from Bethlehem in Judah, looking for a place to live. He openly admitted he had no fixed purpose, and his identity as a Levite became a marketable asset. Let your identity in God shape your direction, seeking first the city to come rather than merely a comfortable position.
P: A Levite for hire. Judges 17:9: 'I am a Levite of Bethlehemjudah, and I go to sojourn where I may find a place.' Here we have no continuing city. Seek the one to come.

== 17:10
T: Ten shekels by the year
R: 'And Micah said unto him, Dwell with me, and be unto me a father and a priest, and I will give thee ten shekels of silver by the year, and a suit of apparel, and thy victuals. So the Levite went in.' Notice dwell with me. An invitation. Notice and be unto me a father and a priest. An honoured role. Notice and I will give thee ten shekels of silver by the year. A salary. Notice so the Levite went in. He accepted. Notice that the Levite was hired to serve at an idol shrine. Notice that ministry became a job for pay, not a calling. Notice that he did not question the idols. Notice that money purchased his conscience. First Peter 5 urges elders to serve 'not for filthy lucre, but of a ready mind.' Notice that ministry for money corrupts. Today, serve God for love, not for gain. Let your service flow from calling, not from salary. Do not let money buy your convictions.
S: Micah offered the Levite ten shekels a year, clothing and food to live with him as father and priest, and the Levite accepted. He was hired to serve at an idol shrine without questioning it, as ministry became a job for pay rather than a calling. Serve God for love rather than gain, and do not let money buy your convictions.
P: A priest for pay. Judges 17:10: 'I will give thee ten shekels of silver by the year… So the Levite went in.' Serve not for filthy lucre, but of a ready mind.

== 17:11

R: 'And the Levite was content to dwell with the man; and the young man was unto him as one of his sons.' Notice the Levite was content. Comfortable. Notice to dwell with the man. Settled in. Notice and the young man was unto him as one of his sons. Treated as family. Notice that the Levite found comfort and belonging. Notice that he was content in a place of idolatry. Notice that contentment can be misplaced. Notice that comfort silenced his conscience. Notice the contrast with godly contentment, which rests in God's will. First Timothy 6 says, 'But godliness with contentment is great gain.' Notice that contentment is gain only when joined with godliness. Notice that comfort without godliness is a trap. Today, examine where you are content. Are you comfortable in God's will, or merely comfortable? Seek godliness with contentment, not contentment without godliness.
S: The Levite was content to live with Micah and was treated like one of his sons. He found comfort and belonging in a place of idolatry, where comfort silenced his conscience. Examine where you are content, for godliness with contentment is great gain, but comfort without godliness is a trap.
P: Content in the wrong place. Judges 17:11: 'the Levite was content to dwell with the man.' Comfortable among idols. Godliness with contentment is gain; contentment alone is a trap.

== 17:12
T: Micah consecrated the Levite
R: 'And Micah consecrated the Levite; and the young man became his priest, and was in the house of Micah.' Micah installed the Levite, and the young man became his priest and lived in his house. Notice and Micah consecrated the Levite. Self-appointed authority. Notice and the young man became his priest. A private priest. Notice and was in the house of Micah. Serving one family's shrine. Notice that Micah had no authority to ordain anyone. Notice that only Aaron's descendants were priests. Notice that the Levite accepted an office God had not given. Notice that ministry was now privately owned. Hebrews 5 says, 'And no man taketh this honour unto himself, but he that is called of God, as was Aaron.' Notice that spiritual authority comes from God. Today, recognise that true ministry is God's calling, not human appointment for private benefit. Do not try to own or control God's servants. Respect His order.
S: Micah installed the Levite, who became his priest and lived in his house. Micah had no authority to ordain anyone, and the Levite accepted an office God had not given, so ministry became privately owned. Recognise that true ministry is God's calling, for no one takes this honour unless called by God.
P: A self-appointed priest. Judges 17:12: 'Micah consecrated the Levite; and the young man became his priest.' No man taketh this honour unto himself. Ministry is God's calling.
