# Judges 18:17-31: Stolen gods and the city of Dan

== 18:17
T: They took the graven image
R: 'And the five men that went to spy out the land went up, and came in thither, and took the graven image, and the ephod, and the teraphim, and the molten image: and the priest stood in the entering of the gate with the six hundred men.' Notice the five men… went up. The spies led the theft. Notice and took the graven image. The idol. Notice and the ephod, and the teraphim. The whole shrine. Notice and the molten image. Everything. Notice and the priest stood in the entering of the gate. Watching passively. Notice that the Danites stole gods that could not protect themselves. Notice that idols can be carried off by thieves. Notice the absurdity of trusting what can be stolen. Isaiah 44 says of the idol-maker, 'He feedeth on ashes: a deceived heart hath turned him aside.' Notice that idolatry deceives. Today, trust only the God who cannot be stolen, carried off or destroyed. Anything that can be taken from you is not worthy of your worship.
S: The five spies went in and took the carved image, ephod, household idols and cast image while the priest stood at the gate with the six hundred armed men. They stole gods that could not protect themselves, showing the absurdity of trusting what can be stolen. Trust only the God who cannot be taken from you, for a deceived heart feeds on ashes.
P: Stolen gods. Judges 18:17: 'took the graven image, and the ephod, and the teraphim.' Gods that couldn't protect themselves. Trust only the God who cannot be stolen.

== 18:18
T: What do ye?
R: 'And fetched the carved image, the ephod, and the teraphim, and the molten image. Then said the priest unto them, What do ye?' When the five men went into Micah's house and took the idols, the priest said to them, What are you doing? Notice and fetched the carved image. Carried out. Notice the ephod, and the teraphim, and the molten image. Everything taken. Notice then said the priest unto them. A weak protest. Notice what do ye? Only a question. Notice that the priest objected only mildly. Notice that he did not defend the shrine he served. Notice that a hired servant has little loyalty. John 10 says of the hireling that he 'seeth the wolf coming, and leaveth the sheep, and fleeth.' Notice that hirelings abandon their charge when threatened. Today, be faithful in your responsibilities even when it is costly. Do not serve only for pay. True servants stand their ground.
S: The spies took the idols from Micah's house, and the priest merely asked what they were doing. He objected only mildly and did not defend the shrine he served, for a hired servant has little loyalty. Be faithful in your responsibilities even when costly, unlike the hireling who flees when the wolf comes.
P: A hireling's protest. Judges 18:18: 'Then said the priest unto them, What do ye?' A weak question, no defence. The hireling fleeth. Be faithful when it costs.

== 18:19

Notice hold thy peace. Be quiet. Notice lay thine hand upon thy mouth. Silence. Notice and be to us a father and a priest. A promotion. Notice is it better… one man, or… a tribe? A tempting offer. Notice that the Danites appealed to his ambition. Notice that they offered him a bigger stage. Notice that he was asked to betray his employer for advancement. Matthew 16 asks, 'what is a man profited, if he shall gain the whole world, and lose his own soul?' Notice that gain without integrity is loss. Today, beware of offers that require betraying others for advancement. A bigger platform is not worth a smaller conscience.
S: The Danites told the priest to be silent and come with them to be priest for a whole tribe rather than one man's house. They appealed to his ambition, offering a promotion that required betraying his employer. Beware of offers that require betrayal for advancement, for what profits a man to gain the world and lose his soul?
P: A tempting promotion. Judges 18:19: 'is it better for thee to be a priest unto the house of one man, or… unto a tribe?' What profits a man to gain the world and lose his soul?

== 18:20
T: The priest's heart was glad
R: 'And he took the ephod, and the teraphim, and the graven image, and went in the midst of the people.' The priest was very pleased. He took the ephod, the household gods and the idol and went along with the people. Notice the priest's heart was glad. He was delighted. Notice and he took the ephod. He joined the theft. Notice and the teraphim, and the graven image. Carrying the idols. Notice and went in the midst of the people. Taking his new place. Notice that the priest happily betrayed Micah. Notice that he cared about position more than faithfulness. Notice that he served whoever offered more. Galatians 1 asks, 'do I seek to please men? for if I yet pleased men, I should not be the servant of Christ.' Notice that pleasing people competes with serving Christ. Today, examine what makes your heart glad. Is it promotion and approval, or faithfulness to God? Serve Christ, not the highest bidder.
S: The priest was glad, took the ephod, household idols and carved image and went along among the people. He happily betrayed Micah, caring more about position than faithfulness and serving whoever offered more. Examine what makes your heart glad, and serve Christ rather than seeking to please men or the highest bidder.
P: Glad to betray. Judges 18:20: 'the priest… took the ephod… and went in the midst of the people.' Do I seek to please men? Serve Christ, not the highest bidder.

== 18:21
T: The little ones before them
R: 'So they turned and departed, and put the little ones and the cattle and the carriage before them.' Putting their little children, their livestock and their possessions in front of them, they turned away and left. Notice so they turned and departed. Leaving quickly. Notice and put the little ones. Children in front. Notice and the cattle and the carriage. Possessions too. Notice before them. Protected from pursuit. Notice that the Danites expected trouble from behind. Notice that they protected their families even while stealing. Notice that people can care for their own while wronging others. Notice that love for family does not excuse injustice to neighbours. First Timothy 5 says, 'if any provide not for his own… he hath denied the faith.' Notice that caring for family is right. Notice that it must be joined to justice for others. Today, care well for your family, but never at the expense of doing right to others. Love for your own must include justice toward your neighbours.
S: The Danites turned and left, putting their children, livestock and goods in front of them to protect them from pursuit. They cared for their own families even while wronging another, but love for family does not excuse injustice. Care well for your family, but never at the expense of doing right by your neighbours.
P: Caring for their own. Judges 18:21: 'put the little ones and the cattle and the carriage before them.' Love for family must include justice toward others.

== 18:22
T: They overtook the children of Dan
R: 'And when they were a good way from the house of Micah, the men that were in the houses near to Micah's house were gathered together, and overtook the children of Dan.' Notice when they were a good way from the house of Micah. Distance gained. Notice the men that were in the houses near. Micah's neighbours. Notice were gathered together. A small band. Notice and overtook the children of Dan. Catching up. Notice that Micah's neighbours tried to help him. Notice that they were vastly outnumbered. Notice that they pursued what had been stolen. Notice that wrongdoing provokes pursuit. Proverbs 11 says, 'he that seeketh mischief, it shall come unto him.' Notice that mischief invites consequences. Today, remember that wrongs done tend to catch up. Stolen things bring pursuit. Choose honesty, and you will have nothing to flee from.
S: When the Danites were some distance from Micah's house, his neighbours gathered and overtook them. Micah's neighbours tried to help him though vastly outnumbered, for wrongdoing provokes pursuit. Choose honesty so you have nothing to flee from, for he who seeks mischief, it shall come to him.
P: Wrongs catch up. Judges 18:22: 'the men… near to Micah's house were gathered together, and overtook the children of Dan.' He that seeketh mischief, it shall come unto him.

== 18:23

Notice that the thieves pretended not to know what was wrong. Notice the arrogance of the powerful toward the wronged. Notice that callousness adds insult to injury. Notice that they treated the victim as the problem. Proverbs 21 says, 'Whoso stoppeth his ears at the cry of the poor, he also shall cry himself, but shall not be heard.' Notice that ignoring the cry of the wronged has consequences. Today, do not dismiss the cries of those you have wronged. Listen, acknowledge and make it right. Callousness hardens the heart.
S: When Micah's neighbours shouted, the Danites turned and asked Micah what was wrong that he came with such a company. The thieves feigned innocence, treating the victim as the problem with callous arrogance. Do not dismiss the cries of those you have wronged, for whoever stops his ears at the cry of the poor shall cry and not be heard.
P: Feigned innocence. Judges 18:23: 'What aileth thee, that thou comest with such a company?' The thieves mocked the victim. Don't stop your ears to the wronged.

== 18:24
T: Ye have taken away my gods
R: 'And he said, Ye have taken away my gods which I made, and the priest, and ye are gone away: and what have I more? and what is this that ye say unto me, What aileth thee?' Notice ye have taken away my gods. His loss. Notice which I made. The irony. Notice and the priest. His religious professional. Notice and what have I more? Nothing left. Notice that Micah's gods could be stolen. Notice that he had made them himself. Notice that when they were gone, he had nothing. Notice that idols leave us empty when they are taken away. Jeremiah 10 says of idols, 'they must needs be borne, because they cannot go.' Notice that idols must be carried; the true God carries us. Notice the tragedy of a man whose religion was entirely portable and stealable. Today, ask what would remain if your possessions and supports were taken. Build your life on the God who cannot be stolen. He carries you.
S: Micah said they had taken the gods he made and his priest, and asked what he had left. His self-made gods could be stolen, and when they were gone he had nothing, for idols must be carried while the true God carries us. Build your life on the God who cannot be stolen, for He carries you when everything else is gone.
P: What have I more? Judges 18:24: 'Ye have taken away my gods which I made… and what have I more?' Idols must be carried; God carries us. Build on Him.

== 18:25

Notice let not thy voice be heard among us. Silencing him. Notice lest angry fellows run upon thee. A threat. Notice and thou lose thy life. Death threat. Notice with the lives of thy household. Endangering his family. Notice that the Danites answered complaint with intimidation. Notice that might silenced right. Notice that the powerful threatened the powerless. Notice that injustice often silences its victims. Psalm 10 says, 'The wicked in his pride doth persecute the poor.' Notice that pride fuels oppression. Notice that God hears what the oppressed cannot say. Today, do not silence those with legitimate complaints. Use power to protect, not to threaten. And if you are silenced, know that God hears.
S: The Danites warned Micah not to let his voice be heard or angry men might attack and kill him and his household. They answered complaint with intimidation, as might silenced right and the powerful threatened the powerless. Do not silence those with legitimate complaints, and if you are silenced, know that God hears the oppressed.
P: Silenced by threats. Judges 18:25: 'Let not thy voice be heard among us, lest angry fellows run upon thee.' The wicked in pride persecute the poor. God hears the silenced.

Notice that the wandering Levite was a grandson of Moses himself. Notice that the great lawgiver's family fell into idolatry within two generations. Notice how quickly faith can be lost. Lamentations 4 laments, 'How is the gold become dim! how is the most fine gold changed!' Notice that godly heritage does not guarantee faithfulness. Today, do not rely on your spiritual heritage. Each generation must choose God for itself. Pass on faith deliberately.
S: The Danites set up the carved image, and Jonathan son of Gershom, likely Moses' grandson, and his sons served as priests to Dan until the captivity. The great lawgiver's family fell into idolatry within two generations, showing how quickly heritage can be lost. Do not rely on your spiritual heritage, but choose God yourself and pass on faith deliberately.
