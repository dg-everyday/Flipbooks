R: 'And the children of Benjamin did so, and took them wives, according to their number, of them that danced, whom they caught: and they went and returned unto their inheritance, and repaired the cities, and dwelt in them.' Notice the children of Benjamin did so. They followed the plan. Notice and took them wives… whom they caught. By force. Notice and they went and returned unto their inheritance. Home again. Notice and repaired the cities. Rebuilding. Notice and dwelt in them. Life resumed. Notice that Benjamin survived and rebuilt. Notice that God preserved the tribe despite the sinful means. Notice that God's purposes continued even through human failure. Notice that this is grace, not approval. Isaiah 61 says, 'they shall repair the waste cities, the desolations of many generations.' Notice that God promises restoration. Today, take hope that God can rebuild what sin has ruined. He does not approve our wrongs, but He can bring restoration from the ruins.
S: The Benjamites did as told, took wives from the dancers they caught, returned to their inheritance, rebuilt the cities and lived in them. Benjamin survived and rebuilt as God preserved the tribe despite sinful means, an act of grace rather than approval. Take hope that God can rebuild what sin has ruined, for He promises to repair waste cities.
P: Rebuilding from ruins. Judges 21:23: 'returned unto their inheritance, and repaired the cities, and dwelt in them.' Grace, not approval. They shall repair the waste cities.

== 21:24
T: Every man to his inheritance
R: 'And the children of Israel departed thence at that time, every man to his tribe and to his family, and they went out from thence every man to his inheritance.' Notice and the children of Israel departed thence. The gathering ended. Notice at that time. After everything. Notice every man to his tribe and to his family. Back home. Notice every man to his inheritance. To their land. Notice the echo of Joshua's day, when Israel also went home to their inheritance. Notice the contrast: then they went home after covenant renewal; now after civil war. Notice that the same land held very different stories. Notice that Israel needed restoration of soul. Psalm 23 says, 'He restoreth my soul: he leadeth me in the paths of righteousness for his name's sake.' Notice that God restores weary souls. Today, after conflict and sorrow, ask God to restore your soul. Let Him lead you back into paths of righteousness.
S: The children of Israel left, each to his tribe, family and inheritance. The scene echoes Joshua's day, but then they went home after covenant renewal and now after civil war, needing restoration of soul. After conflict and sorrow, ask God to restore your soul and lead you in paths of righteousness.
P: Going home after sorrow. Judges 21:24: 'they went out from thence every man to his inheritance.' After civil war, not covenant. He restoreth my soul. Let Him lead you home.

== 21:25
T: No king in Israel
R: 'In those days there was no king in Israel: every man did that which was right in his own eyes.' In those days Israel had no king; everyone did as they saw fit. Notice in those days. The whole era. Notice there was no king in Israel. The book's refrain. Notice every man did that which was right in his own eyes. Moral chaos. Notice that the book of Judges ends with this summary. Notice that its final chapters show what happens without God as King. Notice that Israel needed a righteous king. Notice that this longing points ahead to David and ultimately to Jesus. Zechariah 9 says, 'behold, thy King cometh unto thee: he is just, and having salvation.' Notice that the true King brings justice and salvation. Today, let Jesus be your King. The answer to a world where everyone does what is right in their own eyes is the rule of the righteous King. Bow to Him.
S: The book of Judges ends by saying there was no king in Israel and everyone did what was right in his own eyes. Its final chapters show the chaos that follows without God as King, pointing ahead to David and ultimately to Jesus, the King who is just and brings salvation. Let Jesus be your King, for the answer to a world of self-rule is the rule of the righteous King.
P: The King we need. Judges 21:25: 'there was no king in Israel: every man did that which was right in his own eyes.' Behold, thy King cometh, just and having salvation. Bow to Jesus.
