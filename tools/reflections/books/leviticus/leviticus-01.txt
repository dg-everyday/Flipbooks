# Leviticus 1: The burnt offering

== 1:1
T: Called from the tabernacle
R: 'And the LORD called unto Moses, and spake unto him out of the tabernacle of the congregation, saying.' Leviticus begins where Exodus ended. The tabernacle was finished, and God's glory filled it. Now the LORD spoke from inside His dwelling. The God who thundered from Sinai now spoke from the tent among His people. Notice the word called. The Hebrew title of this book comes from it. God initiates. He calls His people into relationship and teaches them how to draw near. This whole book answers one great question: how can sinful people live with a holy God? God Himself provides the answer. Hebrews 1 says God, who at sundry times… spake in time past… Hath in these last days spoken unto us by his Son. The God who spoke from the tabernacle now speaks through Christ. Today, listen for God's call. He still speaks to His people and teaches them how to come near.
S: The LORD called to Moses and spoke from the tabernacle of the congregation. The God who thundered on Sinai now spoke from His dwelling among His people, teaching them how to draw near. Listen for God's call, for He still speaks and teaches His people to come near.
P: God calls. Leviticus 1:1: 'And the LORD called unto Moses, and spake unto him out of the tabernacle.' He teaches sinners how to draw near. Listen for His voice today.

== 1:2
T: If any man bring an offering
R: 'Speak unto the children of Israel, and say unto them, If any man of you bring an offering unto the LORD, ye shall bring your offering of the cattle, even of the herd, and of the flock.' God's first instruction concerned offerings. If anyone brings an offering to the LORD, it should come from the herd or the flock. The way to approach God was through an offering. Notice any man of you. The invitation was open to every Israelite. Not only priests or leaders could approach God. Anyone could bring an offering. Notice also bring. The Hebrew word for offering comes from a root meaning to draw near. An offering was a way of coming close to God. Worship is drawing near. The offering came from their own livestock. It was costly and personal. Romans 12 calls us to present our bodies a living sacrifice. Hebrews 10 invites us to draw near with a true heart. Today, draw near to God. The invitation is open to you through Christ, the perfect offering.
S: God told Israel that if anyone brought an offering to the LORD, it should come from the herd or the flock. The invitation was open to every Israelite, and an offering meant drawing near. Draw near to God today, for the invitation is open to you through Christ.
P: An open invitation. Leviticus 1:2: 'If any man of you bring an offering unto the LORD.' Anyone could draw near. Through Christ, the invitation is yours today.
H: #DailyGrace #Leviticus12 #Offering #DrawNear #Worship #BibleVerse

== 1:3
T: Of his own voluntary will
R: 'If his offering be a burnt sacrifice of the herd, let him offer a male without blemish: he shall offer it of his own voluntary will at the door of the tabernacle of the congregation before the LORD.' The burnt offering from the herd had to be a male without defect. And it was to be offered of the worshiper's own free will at the tabernacle entrance. Notice without blemish. God required the best. A flawed animal would not do. This points to Christ, the Lamb without blemish and without spot, as First Peter 1 says. Notice also of his own voluntary will. The burnt offering was freely given. No one could force it. God desires willing worship. Psalm 54 says, I will freely sacrifice unto thee. Notice before the LORD. The offering was not a ritual for show. It was presented to God personally. Today, give God your best, freely and willingly. And thank Him for Christ, the perfect sacrifice offered willingly for you.
S: The burnt offering from the herd had to be a male without blemish, offered of the worshiper's own voluntary will before the LORD. God desired the best, given freely, pointing to Christ the spotless Lamb. Give God your best freely and thank Him for Christ's willing sacrifice.
P: Freely given. Leviticus 1:3: 'a male without blemish: he shall offer it of his own voluntary will.' Give God your best willingly. Christ gave Himself freely for you.
H: #DailyGrace #Leviticus13 #Worship #Willing #Christ #BibleVerse

== 1:4
T: Hand on the head
R: 'And he shall put his hand upon the head of the burnt offering; and it shall be accepted for him to make atonement for him.' The worshiper laid his hand on the animal's head. Then the offering would be accepted on his behalf to make atonement for him. The animal took his place. Laying on the hand was an act of identification. It said, this animal stands for me. My guilt is transferred to it. Its death will count for me. This is substitution. Notice accepted for him. The offering was accepted in the worshiper's place. He was accepted because the sacrifice was accepted. Ephesians 1 says we are accepted in the beloved. We are received by God because Christ was received. Isaiah 53 says, the LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all. Today, by faith, lay your hand on Christ. Let Him stand in your place. Through Him, you are accepted.
S: The worshiper put his hand on the head of the burnt offering, and it was accepted for him to make atonement. The animal stood in his place as a substitute. By faith, lay your hand on Christ, for through Him you are accepted.
P: Accepted through a substitute. Leviticus 1:4: 'it shall be accepted for him to make atonement for him.' Christ took your place. In Him, you're accepted.

== 1:5
T: Blood round about the altar
R: 'And he shall kill the bullock before the LORD: and the priests, Aaron's sons, shall bring the blood, and sprinkle the blood round about upon the altar.' The worshiper killed the bull before the LORD. The priests then brought the blood and sprinkled it on all sides of the altar. The worshiper himself killed the animal. He could not stand at a distance. He faced the cost of his sin directly. Sin brings death, and he saw it with his own eyes. This made sin's seriousness personal. Notice the priests' role. They handled the blood. The worshiper brought the offering, but the priest applied the blood. Both were involved. Christ is both our sacrifice and our priest. Hebrews 9 says He entered the holy place by his own blood. Notice round about. The blood covered the altar on every side. Atonement was complete. Today, remember the cost of your sin. And thank Christ, who offered His own blood to cover you completely.
S: The worshiper killed the bullock before the LORD, and the priests sprinkled its blood round about upon the altar. The worshiper faced sin's cost personally, while the priest applied the blood. Remember the cost of sin and thank Christ, who is both your sacrifice and your priest.
P: Facing the cost. Leviticus 1:5: 'he shall kill the bullock before the LORD.' Sin brings death. Christ offered His own blood for you. Thank Him today.

== 1:6
T: Into his pieces
R: 'And he shall flay the burnt offering, and cut it into his pieces.' The worshiper skinned the burnt offering and cut it into pieces. The offering was prepared carefully for the altar. Nothing was hidden; every part was exposed. This was hard, messy work. Worship was not a spectator activity. The worshiper was fully involved. He handled the offering with his own hands. Notice that the animal was laid open. Every part was exposed before God. Hebrews 4 says, all things are naked and opened unto the eyes of him with whom we have to do. Nothing is hidden from God. The burnt offering pictures complete surrender. Every piece would go to God. Psalm 139 prays, Search me, O God, and know my heart. Today, open every part of your life to God. Hold nothing back. Let Him see you fully, and offer yourself completely to Him.
S: The worshiper flayed the burnt offering and cut it into pieces for the altar. Every part was exposed and prepared, picturing complete surrender. Open every part of your life to God and offer yourself completely to Him.
P: Every part laid open. Leviticus 1:6: 'he shall flay the burnt offering, and cut it into his pieces.' Hold nothing back. Offer every part of your life to God today.

== 1:7
T: Fire and wood in order
R: 'And the sons of Aaron the priest shall put fire upon the altar, and lay the wood in order upon the fire.' Aaron's sons put fire on the altar and arranged the wood carefully. The altar was prepared for the offering. Order marked every step. Notice in order. The wood was not thrown on carelessly. It was arranged. Worship involves care and preparation. First Corinthians 14 says, Let all things be done decently and in order. The fire on the altar came originally from God. Leviticus 9 records fire coming out from before the LORD to consume the first offering. The priests kept that fire burning. Leviticus 6 says, The fire shall ever be burning upon the altar; it shall never go out. Our devotion is also meant to burn continually. Romans 12 says, fervent in spirit; serving the Lord. Today, prepare your heart for worship. Arrange your life in order, and keep the fire of devotion burning.
S: Aaron's sons put fire on the altar and laid the wood in order upon it. The altar was carefully prepared, and the fire from God was to be kept burning. Prepare your heart for worship and keep the fire of devotion burning.
P: Keep the fire burning. Leviticus 1:7: 'put fire upon the altar, and lay the wood in order.' Prepare your heart and keep devotion burning bright today.

== 1:8
T: Head and fat on the fire
R: 'And the priests, Aaron's sons, shall lay the parts, the head, and the fat, in order upon the wood that is on the fire which is upon the altar.' The priests arranged the pieces, including the head and the fat, on the burning wood. Every part was placed carefully in the fire. The offering was given to God completely. Notice the head and the fat. The head represents thoughts and will. The fat was considered the best part. Both went to God. The burnt offering gave God the mind and the best of the worshiper. God wants our thinking and our best, not just our actions. Romans 12 says, be ye transformed by the renewing of your mind. Second Corinthians 10 speaks of bringing every thought into captivity to the obedience of Christ. Notice again in order. Even in the fire, things were arranged with care. Today, give God your thoughts and your best. Let your mind and your finest gifts be placed on the altar of devotion.
S: The priests laid the parts, including the head and the fat, in order on the wood upon the fire. The head and the best portions went to God, picturing the surrender of mind and best gifts. Give God your thoughts and your best today.
P: Mind and best to God. Leviticus 1:8: 'the head, and the fat, in order upon the wood.' Give God your thinking and your finest. Let Him renew your mind today.

== 1:9
T: A sweet savour
R: 'But his inwards and his legs shall he wash in water: and the priest shall burn all on the altar, to be a burnt sacrifice, an offering made by fire, of a sweet savour unto the LORD.' The inner parts and legs were washed with water. Then the priest burned everything on the altar. It was a burnt offering, a pleasing aroma to the LORD. Notice washed. The inner parts and legs were cleansed before burning. The heart and the walk were made clean. Notice burn all. Nothing was kept back. The whole animal went up to God. This is what made the burnt offering unique. It was total. And it was a sweet savour unto the LORD. Complete surrender delights God. Ephesians 5 says Christ hath given himself for us an offering and a sacrifice to God for a sweetsmelling savour. Christ's total self-giving pleased the Father perfectly. Today, give yourself fully to God. A life completely offered rises to Him as a sweet aroma.
S: The inwards and legs were washed, and the priest burned all on the altar as a burnt sacrifice, a sweet savour to the LORD. Complete surrender delighted God, as Christ's total self-giving pleased the Father. Give yourself fully to God, and let your life rise to Him as a sweet aroma.
P: All of it. Leviticus 1:9: 'the priest shall burn all on the altar… of a sweet savour unto the LORD.' Total surrender delights God. Give Him everything today.

== 1:10
T: Of the flocks
R: 'And if his offering be of the flocks, namely, of the sheep, or of the goats, for a burnt sacrifice; he shall bring it a male without blemish.' If the burnt offering came from the flock, sheep or goats, it also had to be a male without defect. A smaller animal was allowed, but the standard of perfection remained. God provided options. Not everyone owned cattle. Sheep and goats were more common. God made a way for people with fewer resources to worship Him. Yet the requirement did not change: without blemish. The offering could be smaller, but it still had to be the best. God does not ask for what we do not have. He asks for the best of what we do have. Second Corinthians 8 says, it is accepted according to that a man hath, and not according to that he hath not. Today, give God the best of what you have. He welcomes your offering, whatever its size, when it comes from a sincere heart.
S: A burnt offering from the flock, of sheep or goats, also had to be a male without blemish. God allowed smaller offerings for those with fewer resources, but still asked for the best. Give God the best of what you have, whatever its size.
P: The best of what you have. Leviticus 1:10: 'of the sheep, or of the goats… a male without blemish.' God accepts according to what you have. Give Him your best today.

== 1:11
T: Northward before the LORD
R: 'And he shall kill it on the side of the altar northward before the LORD: and the priests, Aaron's sons, shall sprinkle his blood round about upon the altar.' The animal from the flock was killed on the north side of the altar before the LORD. The priests sprinkled its blood on all sides of the altar. Notice northward before the LORD. Even the place of sacrifice was specified. Nothing was random in God's worship. Some have noted that Golgotha, where Jesus was crucified, lay to the north of the temple area. Whether or not that was intended, Christ was indeed offered before the LORD outside the city. Notice again the blood round about. Every side of the altar was covered. The blood of atonement reached in every direction. First John 2 says Christ is the propitiation for our sins: and not for ours only, but also for the sins of the whole world. Today, rejoice that Christ's blood covers every side. His atonement reaches you wherever you stand.
S: The flock animal was killed on the north side of the altar before the LORD, and its blood was sprinkled round about. Every detail of the sacrifice was specified, and the blood covered every side. Rejoice that Christ's atonement reaches every side and covers you completely.
P: Blood on every side. Leviticus 1:11: 'sprinkle his blood round about upon the altar.' Christ's atonement reaches in every direction. It covers you today.

The animal was cut into pieces, including the head and fat. The priest arranged them in order on the burning wood. The same careful process applied to every offering. Notice that the same care was given to the smaller offering. A sheep received the same treatment as a bull. God did not value the poorer worshiper's gift less. Every sincere offering was honored. This reflects God's heart. Mark 12 records Jesus praising the widow who gave two mites. She gave more than the rich because she gave all she had. God measures the heart, not the size. Notice in order once more. Order honored God in every offering, large or small. Today, know that God honors your sincere offering, however small. He sees the heart behind it and receives it with the same care.

== 1:14
T: Turtledoves or young pigeons
R: 'And if the burnt sacrifice for his offering to the LORD be of fowls, then he shall bring his offering of turtledoves, or of young pigeons.' If someone could not afford a bull or a sheep, they could bring turtledoves or young pigeons. God made a way for the poorest to offer burnt sacrifices. No one was excluded from worship because of poverty. This is God's grace. He did not set a price that only the wealthy could pay. The poor could approach Him too. Their offering was smaller, but it was fully acceptable. Luke 2 records that Mary and Joseph offered a pair of turtledoves, or two young pigeons, when they presented Jesus at the temple. They were poor. The Son of God was born into a family that brought the poor person's offering. Second Corinthians 8 says, though he was rich, yet for your sakes he became poor. Today, rejoice that God welcomes everyone. No one is too poor to approach Him. Christ became poor so that you could be made rich.
S: Those who could not afford a bull or sheep could bring turtledoves or young pigeons as a burnt offering. God made a way for the poorest to worship, and Mary and Joseph later brought this offering for Jesus. Rejoice that God welcomes everyone and that Christ became poor to make you rich.
P: The poor person's offering. Leviticus 1:14: 'turtledoves, or of young pigeons.' Mary and Joseph brought this for Jesus. No one is too poor to come to God.

== 1:15
T: Brought to the altar
R: 'And the priest shall bring it unto the altar, and wring off his head, and burn it on the altar; and the blood thereof shall be wrung out at the side of the altar.' For a bird offering, the priest brought it to the altar, wrung off its head, burned it, and drained its blood at the side of the altar. The priest handled the small offering himself. Notice that the priest did more of the work for the bird offering. With larger animals, the worshiper did much of the preparation. With birds, the priest took the offering and handled it. Christ, our High Priest, also cares for the weak and poor. Hebrews 4 says He can be touched with the feeling of our infirmities. He helps us where we are weak. Notice that even a bird's blood was treated with reverence. Every life offered mattered. Today, take comfort that Christ helps you in your weakness. He handles what you cannot. Bring Him even your small offering.
S: The priest brought the bird offering to the altar, wrung off its head, burned it and drained its blood at the side. The priest did more of the work for the poor worshiper's offering. Take comfort that Christ helps you in your weakness and receives your small offering.
P: The priest helps. Leviticus 1:15: 'the priest shall bring it unto the altar.' He did more for the poor. Christ helps you in your weakness. Bring Him your offering.

== 1:16
T: By the place of the ashes
R: 'And he shall pluck away his crop with his feathers, and cast it beside the altar on the east part, by the place of the ashes.' The priest removed the bird's crop and feathers and threw them beside the altar on the east side, where the ashes were placed. What was unclean was set aside. Notice that not everything was burned. The crop, which held undigested food, and the feathers were removed. Only what was clean went on the altar. Impurities were separated. Our offerings to God should also be cleansed. We bring ourselves, but God removes what does not belong. First John 1 says He will cleanse us from all unrighteousness. Notice by the place of the ashes. The removed parts were placed with the ashes, the remains of past offerings. This was the east side, toward the entrance. Today, let God remove what does not belong in your offering. Bring yourself, and let Him cleanse what needs cleansing.
S: The priest plucked away the bird's crop and feathers and cast them beside the altar on the east, by the place of the ashes. Only what was clean went on the altar, while impurities were set aside. Bring yourself to God and let Him remove what does not belong.
P: Cleansed for the altar. Leviticus 1:16: 'he shall pluck away his crop with his feathers, and cast it beside the altar.' Let God remove what doesn't belong as you offer yourself.

== 1:17
T: Not divided asunder
R: 'And he shall cleave it with the wings thereof, but shall not divide it asunder: and the priest shall burn it upon the altar… it is a burnt sacrifice, an offering made by fire, of a sweet savour unto the LORD.' The priest split the bird open by its wings but did not divide it into two parts. Then he burned it on the altar. It too was a burnt offering, a sweet aroma to the LORD. Notice not divide it asunder. The bird remained whole even when opened. Genesis 15 records Abraham not dividing the birds in his covenant sacrifice either. The smallest offering ended with the same words as the largest: a sweet savour unto the LORD. The poor man's pigeon pleased God as fully as the rich man's bull. God's acceptance does not depend on wealth. Acts 10 says God is no respecter of persons. Today, rejoice that your offering pleases God as much as anyone's. Whole devotion, however humble, is a sweet savour to Him.
S: The priest split the bird by its wings without dividing it and burned it as a burnt sacrifice, a sweet savour to the LORD. The poor man's pigeon pleased God as fully as the rich man's bull. Rejoice that your whole devotion, however humble, pleases God as much as anyone's.
P: Equally pleasing. Leviticus 1:17: 'it is a burnt sacrifice… of a sweet savour unto the LORD.' The pigeon pleased God like the bull. Your humble devotion delights Him.
