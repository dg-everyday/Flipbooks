# Leviticus 2: The meat (grain) offering

== 2:1
T: Fine flour, oil, and frankincense
R: 'And when any will offer a meat offering unto the LORD, his offering shall be of fine flour; and he shall pour oil upon it, and put frankincense thereon.' The meat offering was a grain offering, not an animal. It was made of fine flour, with oil poured on it and frankincense added. It represented the fruit of daily labor given to God. The burnt offering pictured dedication of life. The grain offering pictured dedication of work. Farmers grew the grain, ground it, and brought their best to God. Their daily toil became worship. Notice fine flour. The flour was finely ground, without lumps. Many see this as a picture of Christ's even, perfect life. Notice oil and frankincense. Oil suggests the Spirit; frankincense suggests prayer and fragrance. Colossians 3 says, whatsoever ye do, do it heartily, as to the Lord. Today, offer your daily work to God. Let your labor, empowered by the Spirit and lifted in prayer, become an act of worship.
S: The meat offering was fine flour with oil poured on it and frankincense added. It pictured the dedication of daily work, joined with the Spirit and prayer. Offer your daily work to God as an act of worship.
P: Work as worship. Leviticus 2:1: 'his offering shall be of fine flour; and he shall pour oil upon it.' Offer your daily labor to God, empowered by His Spirit, today.

== 2:2
T: A memorial
R: 'And he shall bring it to Aaron's sons the priests: and he shall take thereout his handful of the flour… with all the frankincense thereof; and the priest shall burn the memorial of it upon the altar.' The worshiper brought the grain offering to the priests. A priest took a handful with all the frankincense and burned it on the altar as a memorial portion. It was a pleasing aroma to the LORD. Notice the memorial. A portion was burned to bring the worshiper to God's remembrance. Not that God forgets, but the offering represented the worshiper before Him. Acts 10 records an angel telling Cornelius, Thy prayers and thine alms are come up for a memorial before God. Our prayers and giving rise before God and are remembered. Notice all the frankincense went to God. The fragrance belonged entirely to Him. Our praise belongs to Him alone. Today, let your prayers and offerings rise as a memorial before God. He remembers the faithfulness of His people.
S: The worshiper brought the grain offering to the priests, who burned a handful with all the frankincense as a memorial on the altar. The portion represented the worshiper before God, as prayers and alms rise as a memorial. Let your prayers and offerings rise before God, who remembers His people.
P: A memorial before God. Leviticus 2:2: 'the priest shall burn the memorial of it upon the altar.' Your prayers and giving rise before Him and are remembered.

== 2:3
T: Most holy
R: 'And the remnant of the meat offerings shall be Aaron's and his sons': it is a thing most holy of the offerings of the LORD made by fire.' What remained of the grain offering belonged to Aaron and his sons. It was most holy, part of the offerings made by fire to the LORD. The priests were fed from what was offered to God. God provided for His servants through the people's worship. The priests had no land. They lived from the offerings. What was given to God sustained those who served Him. First Corinthians 9 says, they which minister about holy things live of the things of the temple. Supporting those who serve God is part of worship. Notice most holy. The portion the priests ate was not ordinary food. It was sacred. They ate in God's presence, as His servants. Today, consider how your giving supports God's servants. What you offer to God can sustain those who serve Him.
S: The remainder of the grain offering belonged to Aaron and his sons as a most holy portion. God provided for His servants through the people's worship. Consider how your giving to God sustains those who serve Him.

== 2:4
T: Baken in the oven
R: 'And if thou bring an oblation of a meat offering baken in the oven, it shall be unleavened cakes of fine flour mingled with oil, or unleavened wafers anointed with oil.' A grain offering could be baked in an oven, either as unleavened cakes mixed with oil or as unleavened wafers brushed with oil. Different forms were acceptable. Notice the variety. God allowed different preparations. Not every worshiper had to bring the same thing. Each could offer according to their resources and skill. Notice unleavened. No leaven was allowed. Leaven often pictures sin and corruption. The offering was pure. Notice mingled with oil and anointed with oil. The oil was mixed in and spread on. Many see in this a picture of Christ, whose life was filled with the Spirit within and anointed by the Spirit without. Luke 4 says Jesus was full of the Holy Ghost and that the Spirit anointed Him. Today, offer God what you have, prepared in purity. Let His Spirit fill and anoint your offering.
S: A grain offering could be baked in the oven as unleavened cakes mingled with oil or wafers anointed with oil. God allowed varied forms, all pure and touched with oil. Offer God what you have in purity and let His Spirit fill and anoint it.
P: Filled and anointed. Leviticus 2:4: 'unleavened cakes of fine flour mingled with oil, or unleavened wafers anointed with oil.' Let the Spirit fill and anoint your offering today.

== 2:5
T: Baken in a pan
R: 'And if thy oblation be a meat offering baken in a pan, it shall be of fine flour unleavened, mingled with oil.' A grain offering could also be cooked on a griddle, made of fine flour without leaven, mixed with oil. Different cooking methods were all acceptable. Notice that God accepted offerings prepared in different ways. Oven, pan, or griddle, each was fine. What mattered was that the offering was pure and mixed with oil. God accepts worship in many forms. Some worship through song, others through service, others through quiet prayer. Different temperaments and gifts produce different offerings. First Corinthians 12 says, there are diversities of operations, but it is the same God which worketh all in all. The requirements remained: fine flour, no leaven, mingled with oil. Purity and the Spirit were constant. Today, offer God worship in the way He has gifted you. The form may differ from others, but let it be pure and filled with His Spirit.
S: A grain offering could also be baked in a pan, of fine unleavened flour mingled with oil. God accepted different preparations as long as the offering was pure and joined with oil. Worship God in the way He has gifted you, with purity and His Spirit.
P: Different forms, same heart. Leviticus 2:5: 'a meat offering baken in a pan… unleavened, mingled with oil.' Worship God your way, in purity and by His Spirit.

== 2:6
T: Part it in pieces
R: 'Thou shalt part it in pieces, and pour oil thereon: it is a meat offering.' The griddle offering was broken into pieces, and oil was poured on it. It was a grain offering. Breaking it allowed the oil to soak in. Notice part it in pieces. The offering was broken. Then oil was poured over it. Brokenness prepared it to receive more oil. Many see in this a picture of Christ's body broken for us. At the Last Supper, Luke 22 records Jesus taking bread and breaking it, saying, This is my body which is given for you. The broken bread recalls His sacrifice. Brokenness in our own lives can also make room for the Spirit. Psalm 51 says, a broken and a contrite heart, O God, thou wilt not despise. When we are broken before God, He pours in His Spirit. Today, do not fear brokenness before God. Let it make room for His Spirit. And remember Christ's body, broken for you.
S: The pan offering was parted in pieces and oil poured on it. Breaking the offering let the oil soak in, recalling Christ's body broken for us. Do not fear brokenness before God, for it makes room for His Spirit.
P: Broken and anointed. Leviticus 2:6: 'Thou shalt part it in pieces, and pour oil thereon.' Christ's body was broken for you. Brokenness makes room for the Spirit.

== 2:7
T: The frying pan
R: 'And if thy oblation be a meat offering baken in the frying pan, it shall be made of fine flour with oil.' A grain offering could be cooked in a covered pan, made of fine flour with oil. Yet another method was allowed. God accommodated many kinds of worshipers. The list of options shows God's accessibility. He did not limit worship to one method that only some could follow. Every household could bring an offering in a way that suited them. Some had ovens, others only pans. All could worship. God meets us where we are. He does not demand what we cannot give. Second Corinthians 8 says, if there be first a willing mind, it is accepted according to that a man hath. Willingness matters more than method. Notice that fine flour and oil remained constant. The method varied, but the quality and the oil did not. Today, come to God with what you have and how you can. He accepts sincere worship from every kind of heart and home.
S: A grain offering could also be cooked in a frying pan, made of fine flour with oil. God provided many methods so every household could worship. Come to God with what you have and how you can, for He accepts sincere worship.

== 2:8
T: Presented to the priest
R: 'And thou shalt bring the meat offering that is made of these things unto the LORD: and when it is presented unto the priest, he shall bring it unto the altar.' The worshiper brought the grain offering to the LORD and presented it to the priest. The priest then took it to the altar. The worshiper offered; the priest carried it to God. Notice the partnership. The worshiper prepared and brought. The priest presented and offered. Neither could do the other's part. Worship needed both. Our worship also passes through a priest. Christ presents our offerings to the Father. First Peter 2 says we offer up spiritual sacrifices, acceptable to God by Jesus Christ. Our prayers and praise are made acceptable through Him. Notice unto the LORD. The offering was ultimately for God. The priest was the mediator, not the recipient. Today, bring your offerings to God through Christ. He presents them to the Father and makes them acceptable.
S: The worshiper brought the grain offering to the LORD and presented it to the priest, who brought it to the altar. The priest carried the offering to God, as Christ now presents our worship. Bring your offerings to God through Christ, who makes them acceptable.

== 2:9
T: A sweet savour
R: 'And the priest shall take from the meat offering a memorial thereof, and shall burn it upon the altar: it is an offering made by fire, of a sweet savour unto the LORD.' The priest took a memorial portion from the grain offering and burned it on the altar. It was a pleasing aroma to the LORD. Notice sweet savour unto the LORD. The same words used for the burnt offering were used here. A simple offering of flour pleased God as much as a sacrifice. Our daily work offered to God is a pleasing aroma. Second Corinthians 2 says, we are unto God a sweet savour of Christ. Our lives can be fragrant before God. The grain offering also had no blood. It was not for atonement but for dedication and thanksgiving. Not every act of worship is about sin. Some is simply gratitude. Psalm 50 says, Offer unto God thanksgiving. Today, offer God your thanksgiving and your work. Let your gratitude rise as a sweet savour before Him.

== 2:10
T: For Aaron and his sons
R: 'And that which is left of the meat offering shall be Aaron's and his sons': it is a thing most holy of the offerings of the LORD made by fire.' The rest of the grain offering belonged to Aaron and his sons. It was most holy. The priests were sustained by what the people offered. This principle is repeated for emphasis. God cares for those who serve Him. The priests depended entirely on God's provision through the offerings. They had no land of their own. God was their portion. Numbers 18 records God saying to Aaron, I am thy part and thine inheritance. Those who serve God trust Him for provision. And God's people are part of that provision. Notice most holy again. The priests' food was sacred. Even their meals were acts of worship. First Corinthians 10 says, whether therefore ye eat, or drink, or whatsoever ye do, do all to the glory of God. Today, trust God as your provider. And let even your meals become acts of thanksgiving to Him.

== 2:11
T: No leaven, no honey
R: 'No meat offering, which ye shall bring unto the LORD, shall be made with leaven: for ye shall burn no leaven, nor any honey, in any offering of the LORD made by fire.' No grain offering burned on the altar could contain leaven or honey. Both were excluded from offerings made by fire. God gave clear limits. Leaven causes fermentation and spreads through dough. In Scripture it often represents sin and corruption. First Corinthians 5 says, a little leaven leaveneth the whole lump. Honey was sweet but could also ferment. Some see it as representing natural sweetness or human pleasure that cannot sustain the fire of God's presence. What is offered to God must be free of corruption. Our worship should not be mixed with sin. Nor should it depend on mere sweetness or emotion. First Corinthians 5 also says, let us keep the feast… with the unleavened bread of sincerity and truth. Today, offer God worship free of corruption. Let it be sincere and true, not mixed with sin or shallow sentiment.
S: No grain offering burned to the LORD could contain leaven or honey. Leaven pictured corruption, and honey a sweetness that could not sustain the fire. Offer God worship that is sincere and true, free of sin and shallow sentiment.
P: Sincere and true. Leviticus 2:11: 'ye shall burn no leaven, nor any honey, in any offering.' Worship God with sincerity and truth, not mixed with sin.

== 2:12
T: Firstfruits offered
R: 'As for the oblation of the firstfruits, ye shall offer them unto the LORD: but they shall not be burnt on the altar for a sweet savour.' Firstfruits could be offered to the LORD, even those with leaven or honey. But they would not be burned on the altar. They were presented to God and given to the priests. Notice the distinction. Firstfruits were offered, but not burned. They acknowledged that the harvest came from God. Leviticus 23 describes loaves with leaven offered at Pentecost. Even ordinary bread could be presented as firstfruits. God receives our firstfruits even when they are imperfect. We bring the beginning of our harvest to Him in gratitude. Proverbs 3 says, Honour the LORD with thy substance, and with the firstfruits of all thine increase. The firstfruits acknowledged that everything belonged to God. Today, give God the first of what you receive. Even if it is imperfect, offer it in gratitude. He receives your firstfruits with joy.
S: Firstfruits were offered to the LORD but not burned on the altar for a sweet savour. Even ordinary bread could be presented as firstfruits, acknowledging God as the source. Give God the first of what you receive, even if imperfect, in gratitude.
P: Firstfruits to God. Leviticus 2:12: 'As for the oblation of the firstfruits, ye shall offer them unto the LORD.' Give God the first of your increase with gratitude today.

== 2:13
T: The salt of the covenant
R: 'And every oblation of thy meat offering shalt thou season with salt; neither shalt thou suffer the salt of the covenant of thy God to be lacking from thy meat offering: with all thine offerings thou shalt offer salt.' Every grain offering was to be seasoned with salt. The salt of the covenant must never be missing. Salt was to accompany all offerings. Salt preserves and prevents decay. It represented the permanence of God's covenant. Numbers 18 speaks of a covenant of salt for ever. God's covenant does not spoil. Notice the salt of the covenant of thy God. Every offering reminded the worshiper of God's lasting commitment. Jesus said in Matthew 5, Ye are the salt of the earth. Believers are to preserve and flavor the world. Colossians 4 says, Let your speech be alway with grace, seasoned with salt. Today, remember that God's covenant with you will not spoil. And let your life be salt in the world.

A firstfruits grain offering was made of fresh heads of grain, roasted in the fire and crushed. The earliest harvest was brought to God. Notice green ears. These were the first ripe heads, before the full harvest. Giving firstfruits expresses faith. It says, God has given this, and He will give more. We do not wait until we have plenty to give. We give from the first. Notice dried by the fire and beaten out. The grain was roasted and crushed. Many see Christ here, the firstfruits who passed through fire and suffering. First Corinthians 15 says, now is Christ risen from the dead, and become the firstfruits. Today, give God your first, trusting Him for the rest. And rejoice in Christ, the firstfruits of resurrection.

== 2:16
T: All the frankincense to God
R: 'And the priest shall burn the memorial of it, part of the beaten corn thereof, and part of the oil thereof, with all the frankincense thereof: it is an offering made by fire unto the LORD.' The priest burned the memorial portion: some of the grain, some of the oil, and all the frankincense. It was an offering made by fire to the LORD. Notice part of the grain and oil, but all the frankincense. The priests could eat some of the grain. But the frankincense went entirely to God. The fragrance belonged to Him alone. Frankincense represents praise and worship. Praise belongs entirely to God. We may receive many benefits from our faith, but the glory goes to Him. Psalm 115 says, Not unto us, O LORD, not unto us, but unto thy name give glory. Isaiah 42 says, my glory will I not give to another. Today, give God all the glory. Receive His gifts gratefully, but let the fragrance of praise rise entirely to Him.
