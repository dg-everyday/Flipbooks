# Leviticus 3: The peace offering

== 3:1
T: A sacrifice of peace
R: 'And if his oblation be a sacrifice of peace offering, if he offer it of the herd; whether it be a male or female, he shall offer it without blemish before the LORD.' The peace offering could be male or female from the herd, but it had to be without defect. This offering celebrated peace and fellowship with God. The peace offering was different from the burnt offering. Part was burned for God, part went to the priest, and part was eaten by the worshiper. It was a shared meal, a picture of communion. Notice peace. The Hebrew word is shalom, meaning wholeness, well-being, and right relationship. The peace offering celebrated being at peace with God. Romans 5 says, being justified by faith, we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ. Ephesians 2 says, he is our peace. Christ makes peace possible. Today, rejoice that you have peace with God through Christ. Enjoy fellowship with Him as a reconciled friend.
S: The peace offering could be male or female from the herd, but it had to be without blemish before the LORD. It celebrated shalom, a shared meal of fellowship and right relationship with God. Rejoice that you have peace with God through Christ and enjoy fellowship with Him.
P: Peace with God. Leviticus 3:1: 'if his oblation be a sacrifice of peace offering.' Through Christ, you have peace with God. Enjoy His fellowship today.

== 3:2
T: At the door
R: 'And he shall lay his hand upon the head of his offering, and kill it at the door of the tabernacle of the congregation: and Aaron's sons the priests shall sprinkle the blood upon the altar round about.' The worshiper laid his hand on the animal's head and killed it at the tabernacle entrance. The priests sprinkled its blood on all sides of the altar. Even the peace offering required blood. Notice that peace came through sacrifice. Fellowship with God was not cheap. Blood had to be shed. Colossians 1 says Christ made peace through the blood of his cross. Our peace with God was purchased by Christ's death. Notice again the hand on the head. The worshiper identified with the offering. The peace was personal. Each worshiper laid his own hand. Notice at the door. The offering was made at the entrance to God's dwelling. Peace opens the way to fellowship. Today, remember that your peace with God came through Christ's blood. Rejoice in a peace that cost Him everything.
S: The worshiper laid his hand on the peace offering and killed it at the door, and the priests sprinkled its blood around the altar. Even peace with God came through sacrifice, as Christ made peace through the blood of His cross. Remember that your peace cost Christ everything and rejoice in it.

== 3:3
T: The fat for the LORD
R: 'And he shall offer of the sacrifice of the peace offering an offering made by fire unto the LORD; the fat that covereth the inwards, and all the fat that is upon the inwards.' From the peace offering, the worshiper presented the fat covering and surrounding the inner organs as a fire offering to the LORD. The richest part went to God. In ancient thought, the fat was the best and richest part. In the peace offering, God received it first. Before the worshiper ate, God was honored. This order matters. God's portion comes first. Then we enjoy what He gives. Proverbs 3 says, Honour the LORD with thy substance, and with the firstfruits. Matthew 6 says, seek ye first the kingdom of God. Notice the inwards. The fat around the inner organs represents the deepest, richest part. God wants our inner devotion, not just outward acts. Today, give God the best first. Honor Him before you enjoy His gifts.
S: From the peace offering, the fat covering the inwards was offered by fire to the LORD. The richest part went to God first, before the worshiper ate. Give God the best first and honor Him before enjoying His gifts.
P: God first. Leviticus 3:3: 'the fat that covereth the inwards… an offering made by fire unto the LORD.' Give God the best before you enjoy His gifts today.

== 3:4
T: The innermost parts
R: 'And the two kidneys, and the fat that is on them, which is by the flanks, and the caul above the liver, with the kidneys, it shall he take away.' The two kidneys with their fat and the covering of the liver were removed and offered to God. These hidden, inner parts belonged to the LORD. In Hebrew thought, the kidneys represented the seat of emotions and secret thoughts. Psalm 7 says, the righteous God trieth the hearts and reins. Reins means kidneys. God examines our innermost being. Offering the kidneys symbolized giving God our deepest feelings and motives. Nothing hidden was held back. God wants our whole heart, including the parts no one else sees. Jeremiah 17 says, I the LORD search the heart, I try the reins. He knows us completely. Psalm 51 says, thou desirest truth in the inward parts. Today, offer God your innermost self. Let Him have your deepest feelings, thoughts, and motives.
S: The two kidneys with their fat and the caul above the liver were taken away and offered to God. The kidneys represented the innermost feelings and motives that God searches. Offer God your innermost self, including thoughts and motives no one else sees.
P: The inward parts. Leviticus 3:4: 'the two kidneys, and the fat that is on them.' God searches the heart and reins. Offer Him your deepest self today.

== 3:5
T: Upon the burnt sacrifice
R: 'And Aaron's sons shall burn it on the altar upon the burnt sacrifice, which is upon the wood that is on the fire: it is an offering made by fire, of a sweet savour unto the LORD.' The priests burned the fat of the peace offering on top of the burnt offering already on the altar. It was a pleasing aroma to the LORD. Notice upon the burnt sacrifice. The peace offering was placed on top of the burnt offering. Peace rested on dedication. Fellowship was built on total surrender. This order teaches something important. Peace with God follows surrender to God. We cannot enjoy fellowship while holding back our lives. Romans 12 calls us to be a living sacrifice. Then comes the peace of God. Philippians 4 says, the peace of God, which passeth all understanding, shall keep your hearts. Notice sweet savour. God was pleased with both offerings together. Today, let your peace rest on surrender. Give yourself fully to God, and enjoy the peace that follows.

== 3:6
T: Male or female

A peace offering from the flock could be male or female, but it had to be without defect. The same standard of quality applied to smaller animals. Notice male or female. Unlike the burnt offering, which required a male, the peace offering allowed either. There was more flexibility in this offering of celebration. Fellowship with God welcomes all. Notice again without blemish. Flexibility did not mean lower standards. The offering still had to be perfect. God's grace is generous, but His holiness remains. This balance appears throughout Scripture. Grace and holiness go together. Titus 2 says, the grace of God that bringeth salvation… Teaching us that, denying ungodliness… we should live soberly, righteously, and godly. Today, rejoice in God's generous welcome. And honor His holiness in how you approach Him.

From the lamb, the fat and the entire fat tail, cut off near the backbone, were offered to the LORD along with the fat around the inner organs. This was the choicest portion. The fat tail of certain sheep was considered a delicacy. It was the richest part. God received it. The best went to Him. Notice the whole rump. Not part, but the whole. God's portion was complete. We are tempted to give God part of our best while keeping the rest. But God asks for the whole of His portion. Malachi 1 rebukes those who offered God what was lame and sick. God deserves our finest. Notice hard by the backbone. It was cut carefully, close to the core. Today, give God the whole of what belongs to Him. Do not hold back the best for yourself.

== 3:11

R: 'And the priest shall burn it upon the altar: it is the food of the offering made by fire unto the LORD.' The priest burned the portions on the altar. It was called food, an offering made by fire to the LORD. The language of a meal described the peace offering. Notice the food of the offering. God does not need food. But the language pictures a shared meal. God received His portion, the priest received his, and the worshiper ate his. All shared one meal. This is fellowship. A meal shared is a sign of friendship and peace. God invited His people to eat in His presence. Revelation 3 records Jesus saying, I will come in to him, and will sup with him, and he with me. Christ desires table fellowship with us. The Lord's Supper continues this picture. Today, enjoy fellowship with God. He invites you to share His table. Come and commune with Him.

== 3:17
T: Neither fat nor blood
R: 'It shall be a perpetual statute for your generations throughout all your dwellings, that ye eat neither fat nor blood.' God made it a lasting rule for all generations, wherever they lived: do not eat fat or blood. These belonged to God. The fat was God's portion. The blood represented life and was used for atonement. Leviticus 17 says, the life of the flesh is in the blood… it is the blood that maketh an atonement for the soul. It belonged to God alone. This rule taught reverence for life. Life belongs to God. Blood was not to be treated as common. It was set apart for His purposes. Notice throughout all your dwellings. The rule applied everywhere, not just at the tabernacle. Holiness reached into ordinary homes. Today, believers are not under these food laws, as Acts 15 and Mark 7 show. But the principle remains: life belongs to God, and Christ's blood is holy. Today, honor the sacredness of life. And treasure the blood of Christ, shed for your atonement.
S: God made it a perpetual statute for every generation and dwelling that Israel eat neither fat nor blood. The fat was God's portion and the blood, representing life, was set apart for atonement. Honor the sacredness of life and treasure the blood of Christ shed for you.
P: Life belongs to God. Leviticus 3:17: 'that ye eat neither fat nor blood.' Blood was sacred, set apart for atonement. Treasure the blood of Christ today.
