# Leviticus 5: The trespass offering

== 5:1
T: The silent witness
R: 'And if a soul sin, and hear the voice of swearing, and is a witness… if he do not utter it, then he shall bear his iniquity.' A person who heard a public call to testify, and who had seen or known something, sinned if he stayed silent. Silence could be sin. God's people are called to speak the truth when justice needs it. Keeping quiet to avoid trouble can leave the innocent unprotected. Proverbs 31 says, Open thy mouth for the dumb in the cause of all such as are appointed to destruction. Notice he shall bear his iniquity. The silent witness shared the guilt of the wrong he refused to expose. Courage is part of honesty. Ephesians 4 says, speaking the truth in love. Truth must be spoken with love, but it must be spoken. Today, ask God for courage to speak up when truth and justice require it. Do not hide behind silence.
S: A person who heard a call to testify and knew the facts sinned if he stayed silent, and he bore his iniquity. Silence could leave the innocent unprotected and share in the wrong. Ask God for courage to speak the truth in love when justice requires it.
P: Silence can be sin. Leviticus 5:1: 'if he do not utter it, then he shall bear his iniquity.' Speak the truth in love when justice needs your voice today.

== 5:2
T: Touching the unclean
R: 'Or if a soul touch any unclean thing… and if it be hidden from him; he also shall be unclean, and guilty.' Touching the carcass of an unclean animal made a person unclean, even if he did not realize it. Uncleanness was not the same as moral sin. It was ritual impurity that barred a person from the sanctuary. These laws taught Israel that death and holiness do not mix. God is the living God. Contact with death had to be dealt with before coming near Him. Notice hidden from him. A person could be defiled without knowing it. We too pick up the defilement of the world without noticing. James 1 calls us to keep ourselves unspotted from the world. Christ has fulfilled these ritual laws, as Mark 7 shows. But the lesson remains: God is holy, and we need cleansing to approach Him. Today, ask God to cleanse you from defilement you have not noticed. Come near to Him washed and renewed.
S: Touching the carcass of an unclean animal made a person unclean and guilty, even when it was hidden from him. These laws taught that death and the living God's holiness do not mix. Ask God to cleanse you from defilement you have not noticed and come near to Him renewed.
P: Unspotted from the world. Leviticus 5:2: 'if a soul touch any unclean thing… he also shall be unclean.' We pick up defilement unaware. Ask God to cleanse you today.
H: #DailyGrace #Leviticus52 #Holiness #Cleansing #Purity #BibleVerse

== 5:3
T: When he knoweth of it
R: 'Or if he touch the uncleanness of man… and it be hid from him; when he knoweth of it, then he shall be guilty.' A person who touched human uncleanness without realizing it became guilty once he learned of it. Knowledge brought responsibility. God does not hold us accountable in the same way for what we truly cannot know. But when the truth comes to light, we must respond. Notice when he knoweth of it. The moment of awareness was the moment of decision. He could deal with it, or ignore it. Delay turns an accident into a choice. Acts 17 says the times of this ignorance God winked at; but now commandeth all men every where to repent. When God reveals something, He calls for a response. Psalm 119 says, I made haste, and delayed not to keep thy commandments. Today, when God shows you something that needs cleansing, do not delay. Respond while the conviction is fresh.
S: A person who touched human uncleanness unknowingly became guilty once he knew of it. The moment of awareness became the moment of decision. When God shows you something that needs cleansing, respond without delay.
P: Haste, not delay. Leviticus 5:3: 'when he knoweth of it, then he shall be guilty.' Awareness calls for response. Don't delay what God has shown you today.

== 5:4
T: Rash words
R: 'Or if a soul swear, pronouncing with his lips to do evil, or to do good… and it be hid from him; when he knoweth of it, then he shall be guilty in one of these.' A person who made a careless oath, whether to do harm or good, and forgot it, was guilty once he realized it. Words mattered to God. Thoughtless promises are easy to make. We say things in anger or excitement without thinking. Then we forget them. But God remembers our words. Ecclesiastes 5 says, Be not rash with thy mouth… when thou vowest a vow unto God, defer not to pay it. Jesus said in Matthew 12 that every idle word will be brought into account. Matthew 5 says, let your communication be, Yea, yea; Nay, nay. Simple, honest speech is better than rash oaths. Today, guard your words. Think before you promise. And where you have spoken rashly, confess it to God and make it right.
S: A person who swore rashly to do evil or good and forgot it was guilty when he realized it. God takes our words seriously, even careless ones. Guard your words, think before you promise, and confess rash speech to God.
P: Guard your words. Leviticus 5:4: 'if a soul swear, pronouncing with his lips.' God remembers careless promises. Let your yes be yes. Think before you speak today.

== 5:5
T: He shall confess
R: 'And it shall be, when he shall be guilty in one of these things, that he shall confess that he hath sinned in that thing.' When a person realized his guilt, he had to confess the specific sin. Confession came before the offering. Notice in that thing. Confession was specific. Not a vague admission of being imperfect, but naming the actual sin. General confession is easy. Specific confession is humbling and freeing. Notice also that confession came first. The sacrifice did not replace honest admission. Ritual without repentance was empty. Psalm 32 says, I acknowledged my sin unto thee… and thou forgavest the iniquity of my sin. Proverbs 28 says, He that covereth his sins shall not prosper. First John 1 promises, If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us. Today, name your sin before God specifically. Do not hide behind general words. Confess it plainly, and receive His faithful forgiveness.
S: When a person realized his guilt, he had to confess that he had sinned in that specific thing. Confession came before the offering, and it was plain and specific. Name your sin before God specifically and receive His faithful forgiveness.
P: Name it. Leviticus 5:5: 'he shall confess that he hath sinned in that thing.' Specific confession frees. If we confess, He is faithful to forgive.

== 5:6
T: A trespass offering
R: 'And he shall bring his trespass offering unto the LORD for his sin which he hath sinned… and the priest shall make an atonement for him concerning his sin.' After confession, the person brought a female lamb or goat as an offering, and the priest made atonement. Confession led to atonement. Notice the order. First confession, then sacrifice, then atonement. Words alone did not remove guilt. A sacrifice was needed. But the sacrifice was not mechanical; it followed honest repentance. We confess our sins, and Christ's sacrifice atones for them. Our confession does not pay for sin. Christ's blood does. Hebrews 10 says, by one offering he hath perfected for ever them that are sanctified. Notice unto the LORD. The offering was brought to God. Sin is first an offense against Him, and He is the one who forgives. Today, confess your sin, then rest on Christ's sacrifice. Your confession opens the door; His blood cleanses.
S: After confessing, the person brought his trespass offering to the LORD, and the priest made atonement for his sin. Confession opened the way, but the sacrifice atoned. Confess your sin, then rest on Christ's sacrifice, whose blood cleanses.
P: Confession and atonement. Leviticus 5:6: 'the priest shall make an atonement for him concerning his sin.' Confession opens the door; Christ's blood cleanses. Rest in Him today.

== 5:7
T: If he be not able
R: 'And if he be not able to bring a lamb, then he shall bring for his trespass… two turtledoves, or two young pigeons, unto the LORD.' If a person could not afford a lamb, he could bring two doves or two pigeons. One was for a sin offering and one for a burnt offering. Poverty did not bar anyone from forgiveness. This is God's tenderness. He made provision for the poor. No one had to stay guilty because they lacked money. Luke 2 shows Mary and Joseph offering a pair of turtledoves at Jesus' birth. They were poor. The Son of God was born into a family that used this very provision. Second Corinthians 8 says, though he was rich, yet for your sakes he became poor. Christ identified with the poor. Today, rejoice that God's grace is within reach of everyone. No one is too poor to be forgiven. Christ made Himself poor to make you rich.
S: If a person could not afford a lamb, he could bring two turtledoves or two young pigeons. Poverty never barred anyone from forgiveness, and Mary and Joseph used this very provision. Rejoice that grace is within everyone's reach through Christ, who became poor for you.
P: Grace for the poor. Leviticus 5:7: 'if he be not able to bring a lamb… two turtledoves.' No one is too poor to be forgiven. Christ became poor for you.

== 5:8
T: The sin offering first
R: 'And he shall bring them unto the priest, who shall offer that which is for the sin offering first, and wring off his head from his neck, but shall not divide it asunder.' The priest offered the bird for the sin offering first. He wrung its neck without separating the head completely. Notice first. The sin offering came before the burnt offering. Sin had to be dealt with before dedication could be accepted. We cannot offer ourselves to God while sin stands between us. Cleansing comes first, then consecration. Isaiah 6 shows this order. Isaiah's lips were cleansed first. Then he said, Here am I; send me. Forgiveness prepares us for service. Notice the careful handling. Even a small bird was offered with precision. The poor person's offering was treated with the same seriousness as any other. God does not treat humble gifts carelessly. Today, let God cleanse you first. Then offer yourself freely for His service.
S: The priest offered the bird for the sin offering first, wringing its neck without dividing it. Sin had to be dealt with before dedication, as Isaiah was cleansed before he said, Here am I. Let God cleanse you first, then offer yourself freely for His service.
P: Cleansing before service. Leviticus 5:8: 'offer that which is for the sin offering first.' Forgiveness prepares us to serve. Let God cleanse you, then say, Here am I.

== 5:9
T: The side of the altar
R: 'And he shall sprinkle of the blood of the sin offering upon the side of the altar; and the rest of the blood shall be wrung out at the bottom of the altar: it is a sin offering.' The priest sprinkled some of the bird's blood on the side of the altar and drained the rest at its base. Even the smallest sin offering involved blood. A small bird gave little blood, yet it was enough for atonement. God accepted what the poor could bring. The value was not in the quantity but in God's provision. Notice it is a sin offering. The bird's offering was fully valid. It was not a second-class atonement. The poor person was as forgiven as the rich. Christ's blood is not measured out more to some and less to others. Romans 3 says all are justified freely by his grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus. Today, rejoice that God's atonement is fully valid for you. However small you feel, His provision is complete.
S: The priest sprinkled the bird's blood on the side of the altar and wrung out the rest at the bottom. The poor person's small offering was a fully valid sin offering. Rejoice that God's atonement is complete for you, however small you feel.
P: Fully valid. Leviticus 5:9: 'it is a sin offering.' The poor person's small bird brought full atonement. However small you feel, Christ's provision for you is complete.

== 5:10
T: According to the manner
R: 'And he shall offer the second for a burnt offering, according to the manner: and the priest shall make an atonement for him… and it shall be forgiven him.' The second bird was offered as a burnt offering in the usual way. The priest made atonement, and the person was forgiven. Notice the second bird. After the sin offering came the burnt offering. After forgiveness came dedication. The poor worshiper did not just get cleansed; he also gave himself to God. Forgiveness is not the end of the story. It opens the way for a life given to God. Romans 6 says, yield yourselves unto God, as those that are alive from the dead. Notice according to the manner. God's order was followed even for the poor. Their worship mattered just as much. Notice again it shall be forgiven him. The poor received the same promise as the rich. Today, receive God's forgiveness, then give yourself to Him. Let cleansing lead to dedication.

== 5:11
T: A handful of flour
R: 'But if he be not able to bring two turtledoves, or two young pigeons, then he that sinned shall bring for his offering the tenth part of an ephah of fine flour for a sin offering.' If a person could not afford even two birds, he could bring a small amount of fine flour. No oil or frankincense was added, because it was a sin offering. This is the lowest level of provision. The very poorest could still come. God went down to the bottom of society to make sure no one was left out. Notice no oil or frankincense. The grain offering of chapter 2 was joyful and fragrant. This one was plain. Sin is not a time for celebration. Yet it was still accepted. His mercy bends to reach the lowest. Psalm 113 says He raiseth up the poor out of the dust. Today, know that God's mercy reaches you wherever you are. No one is beneath His grace.
S: If a person could not afford two birds, he could bring a tenth of an ephah of fine flour, without oil or frankincense, for a sin offering. God's provision reached down to the very poorest. Know that God's mercy reaches you wherever you are, for no one is beneath His grace.

The priest took a handful of the flour as a memorial and burned it on the altar. It counted as a sin offering. Notice even a memorial thereof. The handful was a reminder before God. The poor sinner was remembered. He was not forgotten because his offering was small. God sees the humble. Mark 12 tells of the widow who gave two mites. Jesus said she gave more than all the rich. God measures by the heart, not the amount. Notice it is a sin offering. The flour was not a lesser category. God declared it a true sin offering. His word made it valid. Our acceptance rests on God's declaration, not on the size of our offering. Today, bring God whatever you have. He remembers you and accepts what is offered in faith.

R: 'And the priest shall make an atonement for him as touching his sin that he hath sinned in one of these, and it shall be forgiven him.' The priest made atonement for the sin, whichever offering was brought, and the person was forgiven. The remainder of the flour belonged to the priest. Notice in one of these. Whether lamb, birds, or flour, the result was the same. Atonement was made, and forgiveness was granted. God did not give different levels of forgiveness for different offerings. Forgiveness is not partial. We are either forgiven or not. And in Christ, we are fully forgiven. Colossians 2 says, having forgiven you all trespasses. Not some, but all. Notice the certainty again: it shall be forgiven him. This promise runs through these chapters like a refrain. God wants His people to be sure. Today, be assured of full forgiveness. Whatever you brought to God in faith, His forgiveness is complete, not partial.

God's guidance is detailed because He cares about how we live. Some wrongs need confession. Others also need restitution. God shows us which. Faithful leaders keep listening for God's next word. Today, listen for God's guidance in His word. He knows exactly what each situation needs. Let Him teach you His way.

== 5:15
T: The holy things
R: 'If a soul commit a trespass, and sin through ignorance, in the holy things of the LORD; then he shall bring for his trespass unto the LORD a ram without blemish.' If someone unintentionally misused what belonged to God, such as tithes or offerings, he had to bring a ram without defect. Its value was set in silver shekels. Notice the holy things of the LORD. Some things belong to God in a special way. Treating them carelessly is a trespass against Him. Withholding what is God's is a serious wrong. Malachi 3 asks, Will a man rob God? Yet ye have robbed me… In tithes and offerings. Notice a ram valued in silver. This offering was costly. Wronging God was not a small matter. Its cost was calculated precisely. Christ paid the full value of our debt to God. Today, examine how you handle what belongs to God. Honor Him with your time, gifts, and worship. Thank Christ for paying what you owed.
S: Someone who unintentionally trespassed in the holy things of the LORD had to bring a costly ram without blemish, valued in silver. Misusing what belongs to God was a serious wrong that required payment. Honor God with what belongs to Him and thank Christ for paying what you owed.

The person had to repay the harm he had done and add twenty percent. Then the priest made atonement with the ram, and he was forgiven. Notice make amends. Forgiveness did not cancel responsibility to repair. The wrong had to be made right. Repentance includes restitution where possible. Notice the fifth part. He gave back more than he took. True repentance does not seek the minimum. It goes beyond. Zacchaeus in Luke 19 said, if I have taken any thing from any man by false accusation, I restore him fourfold. Grace produced generosity. Notice that restitution came with the sacrifice. Both were needed. Restitution did not earn forgiveness; the ram's atonement did. Today, ask if there is harm you need to repair. Make amends generously, as a fruit of God's grace.

== 5:17
T: Though he wist it not
R: 'And if a soul sin, and commit any of these things which are forbidden to be done by the commandments of the LORD; though he wist it not, yet is he guilty, and shall bear his iniquity.' Even if a person broke God's commands without knowing it, he was still guilty and responsible. Ignorance did not erase guilt. This is a humbling truth. We sin in more ways than we realize. Our awareness is not the measure of our guilt. God's holiness is. First Corinthians 4 records Paul saying, I know nothing by myself; yet am I not hereby justified: but he that judgeth me is the Lord. A clear conscience does not prove innocence. We need more than self-assessment. We need a Savior. This verse drives us to Christ. If even unknown sins make us guilty, then no one can stand on their own record. Today, humble yourself before God. Do not trust your own sense of innocence. Trust Christ, who covers sins you do not even know.

== 5:19
T: Against the LORD
R: 'It is a trespass offering: he hath certainly trespassed against the LORD.' The chapter closes with a firm statement. This was a trespass offering, because the person had truly wronged the LORD. Notice certainly. There was no doubt about the guilt. Even an unknowing wrong was a real wrong against God. We tend to minimize our sin. We call it a mistake, a slip, a weakness. God calls it what it is. Notice against the LORD. Every sin is ultimately against God, even sins that hurt people. David, after wronging Bathsheba and Uriah, prayed in Psalm 51, Against thee, thee only, have I sinned. Seeing sin as against God changes how we repent. We do not just regret consequences. We grieve over offending the One we love. Yet the very naming of sin is the doorway to forgiveness. Today, call your sin what God calls it. Then receive the forgiveness He provides through Christ.
S: The trespass offering was required because the person had certainly trespassed against the LORD. Every sin, even an unknowing one, is a real wrong against God. Call your sin what God calls it, then receive the forgiveness He provides through Christ.
