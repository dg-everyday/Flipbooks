# Leviticus 6: Restitution and the laws for the priests

== 6:1
T: God speaks about neighbours
R: 'And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying.' God spoke again, now turning to wrongs done against other people. The next verses deal with lies, theft, and fraud between neighbours. Notice that God cares about how we treat one another. Worship is not only about altars and sacrifices. It reaches into business, property, and honesty. The God of the tabernacle is also the God of the marketplace. Micah 6 says, what doth the LORD require of thee, but to do justly, and to love mercy, and to walk humbly with thy God. Justice toward people and humility before God belong together. Jesus summed up the law in two commands: love God and love your neighbour. Neither can be separated from the other. First John 4 says, he that loveth not his brother whom he hath seen, how can he love God whom he hath not seen. Today, remember that God cares about your dealings with people. Let your love for Him show in honesty toward others.
S: The LORD spoke to Moses again, now addressing wrongs done to neighbours. The God of the tabernacle is also the God of the marketplace. Let your love for God show in honest and just dealings with the people around you.
P: Love God, love neighbour. Leviticus 6:1: 'And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying.' Worship reaches into how you treat people. Do justly and love mercy today.

== 6:2
T: A trespass against the LORD
R: 'If a soul sin, and commit a trespass against the LORD, and lie unto his neighbour in that which was delivered him to keep… or hath deceived his neighbour.' Lying to a neighbour about something entrusted, a partnership, or stolen goods was called a trespass against the LORD. Wronging people meant wronging God. Notice the connection. The sin was against the neighbour, yet God called it a trespass against Himself. Every person is made in God's image. To cheat them is to offend their Maker. Proverbs 14 says, He that oppresseth the poor reproacheth his Maker. Notice the kinds of wrong: broken trust, dishonest partnership, theft, and deception. They happen in families, workplaces, and friendships. God sees them all. Jesus said in Matthew 25, Inasmuch as ye have done it unto one of the least of these… ye have done it unto me. How we treat people is how we treat God. Today, deal honestly with everyone. Remember that every wrong against a person is a wrong against the God who made them.
S: Lying to a neighbour about what was entrusted, shared or taken was called a trespass against the LORD. To wrong someone made in God's image is to offend their Maker. Deal honestly with everyone, remembering that God takes every wrong against people personally.
P: Wronging people wrongs God. Leviticus 6:2: 'commit a trespass against the LORD, and lie unto his neighbour.' Every person bears God's image. Treat them honestly today.
H: #DailyGrace #Leviticus62 #Honesty #Neighbour #ImageOfGod #BibleVerse

== 6:3
T: That which was lost
R: 'Or have found that which was lost, and lieth concerning it, and sweareth falsely; in any of all these that a man doeth, sinning therein.' Finding something lost and lying about it, even under oath, was sin. Keeping what belonged to someone else was not innocent luck. Notice that which was lost. Finders keepers was not God's rule. A found item still belonged to its owner. Deuteronomy 22 commanded Israel to return a neighbour's lost ox or sheep, and anything lost. Honesty applies even when no one is watching. Notice sweareth falsely. One sin led to another. Keeping the item led to lying, and lying led to false oaths. Sin rarely stays alone. It pulls more sin behind it. Integrity means doing right when it costs us and when no one would know. Proverbs 11 says, The integrity of the upright shall guide them. Today, practice integrity in small things. Return what is not yours. Tell the truth even when a lie would be easier.
S: Finding something lost and lying about it, even swearing falsely, was sin. A found item still belonged to its owner, and one sin led to another. Practice integrity in small things, returning what is not yours and telling the truth when no one is watching.
P: Integrity unseen. Leviticus 6:3: 'have found that which was lost, and lieth concerning it.' Do right when no one would know. Integrity guides the upright.

== 6:4
T: He shall restore
R: 'Then it shall be, because he hath sinned, and is guilty, that he shall restore that which he took violently away, or the thing which he hath deceitfully gotten.' Once guilty, the wrongdoer had to give back what he had stolen, gained by fraud, been entrusted with, or found. Restoration was required. Notice he shall restore. Saying sorry was not enough. The stolen thing had to be returned. Repentance that keeps the profits of sin is not real repentance. Ezekiel 33 says, If the wicked restore the pledge, give again that he had robbed… he shall surely live. Restitution shows that the heart has truly changed. Notice the list: violence, deceit, trust, and lost things. Each kind of wrong had the same remedy: give it back. Restoring what we took heals relationships as well as consciences. Matthew 5 says, first be reconciled to thy brother, and then come and offer thy gift. Today, if you are holding what belongs to another, return it. Let your repentance be real.
S: The guilty person had to restore what he took violently, gained deceitfully, was entrusted with or found. Repentance that keeps the profits of sin is not real repentance. If you hold what belongs to another, return it and let your repentance be real.
P: Give it back. Leviticus 6:4: 'he shall restore that which he took violently away.' Sorry isn't enough when something must be returned. Be reconciled, then worship.

== 6:5
T: The principal and a fifth
R: 'He shall even restore it in the principal, and shall add the fifth part more thereto, and give it unto him to whom it appertaineth, in the day of his trespass offering.' The guilty person repaid the full amount plus twenty percent. He gave it directly to the owner on the same day he brought his offering. Notice the fifth part more. The victim was not just restored but compensated. Restitution went beyond the loss. Notice in the day of his trespass offering. Making things right with the neighbour came at the same time as making things right with God. They could not be separated. We cannot be right with God while refusing to make things right with people we have wronged. Notice to him to whom it appertaineth. Restitution went to the person harmed, not to a general fund. It was personal. Luke 19 shows Zacchaeus giving back fourfold. Grace made him generous. Today, make things right with those you have harmed. Go beyond the minimum, and do it promptly.
S: The guilty person restored the principal plus a fifth more, giving it to the owner on the day of his trespass offering. Making things right with the neighbour went together with making things right with God. Make things right with those you have harmed, going beyond the minimum.
P: Beyond the minimum. Leviticus 6:5: 'restore it in the principal, and shall add the fifth part more thereto.' Right with people, right with God. Make amends generously today.

== 6:6
T: A ram for the trespass
R: 'And he shall bring his trespass offering unto the LORD, a ram without blemish out of the flock, with thy estimation, for a trespass offering, unto the priest.' After repaying his neighbour, the person brought a ram without blemish to the priest as a trespass offering to the LORD. Notice the order. First the neighbour was repaid. Then the offering was brought. Restitution came before the sacrifice. God would not accept worship from someone who refused to make things right. Yet restitution alone was not enough. Repaying the neighbour addressed the human side. The ram addressed the offense against God. Both relationships needed healing. Christ is our trespass offering. Isaiah 53 says, when thou shalt make his soul an offering for sin. The Hebrew word there is the same as for this trespass offering. Jesus paid what we owed God. Today, make things right with people, and rest in Christ, who made things right with God for you.
S: After repaying his neighbour, the person brought a ram without blemish to the LORD as a trespass offering. Restitution healed the human relationship, while the sacrifice dealt with the offense against God. Make things right with people and rest in Christ, our trespass offering.
P: Both relationships healed. Leviticus 6:6: 'he shall bring his trespass offering unto the LORD, a ram without blemish.' Christ is our trespass offering. Rest in Him today.

== 6:7
T: Any thing of all

The priest made atonement before the LORD, and the person was forgiven for anything he had done wrong in the matter. Forgiveness was complete. Notice any thing of all. Whatever the trespass, theft, fraud, lies, or false oaths, all could be forgiven. No sin in the list was beyond atonement. Even deliberate deception could be forgiven when the person repented and made restitution. This is remarkable grace. These were not accidents. They were lies and theft. Yet God forgave. First Corinthians 6 lists thieves and extortioners, then says, such were some of you: but ye are washed. Notice before the LORD. Forgiveness came from God Himself. Today, believe that God can forgive any thing of all you have done. Repent, make it right, and receive His full pardon.
