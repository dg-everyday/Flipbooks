# Leviticus 7:1-21: The laws of the trespass and peace offerings

== 7:1
T: Most holy
R: 'Likewise this is the law of the trespass offering: it is most holy.' God continued His instructions for the priests, now turning to the trespass offering. Like the sin offering, it was most holy. Notice most holy. The trespass offering dealt with wrongs against God and neighbour. Yet the offering itself was holy. What God provides to remove guilt is sacred. We must never treat forgiveness lightly. Grace is free to us, but it is precious. Romans 6 asks, Shall we continue in sin, that grace may abound? God forbid. Forgiveness is not permission to keep sinning. It is a holy gift that changes us. Notice likewise. The trespass offering followed the same holy pattern as the others. God's ways are consistent. Today, treat God's forgiveness as most holy. Let the grace that cleanses you also lead you to live differently.
S: The law of the trespass offering declared that it was most holy. What God provides to remove guilt is sacred, never to be treated lightly. Treat God's forgiveness as most holy and let grace lead you to live differently.
P: Holy forgiveness. Leviticus 7:1: 'this is the law of the trespass offering: it is most holy.' Grace is free but precious. Let it change how you live today.

== 7:2
T: Blood round about
R: 'In the place where they kill the burnt offering shall they kill the trespass offering: and the blood thereof shall he sprinkle round about upon the altar.' The trespass offering was killed where the burnt offering was killed. Its blood was sprinkled on all sides of the altar. Notice the same place. Every offering met at the one altar. There was not one place for small sins and another for big ones. All came to the same place of sacrifice. For us, every sin comes to one place: the cross. There is no other altar. Acts 4 says, Neither is there salvation in any other. Notice round about. The blood covered every side. No part of the altar was left untouched. Christ's atonement covers every side of our guilt. Colossians 1 says He made peace through the blood of his cross. Today, bring every sin to the one place God has provided. At the cross, the blood covers it all.
S: The trespass offering was killed where the burnt offering was killed, and its blood was sprinkled round about the altar. Every offering met at one altar, just as every sin comes to one cross. Bring every sin to the cross, where Christ's blood covers it all.
P: One altar. Leviticus 7:2: 'In the place where they kill the burnt offering shall they kill the trespass offering.' Every sin comes to one cross. The blood covers it all.

== 7:3
T: All the fat thereof
R: 'And he shall offer of it all the fat thereof; the rump, and the fat that covereth the inwards.' From the trespass offering, the priest offered all the fat, including the fat tail and the fat covering the inner organs. God's portion was the same as in the peace offering. Notice all the fat. Even an offering that dealt with wrongdoing gave God the best. Restoring a relationship with God was an occasion to honor Him. Repentance is not only about relief from guilt. It is about returning to God with our whole selves. Hosea 14 says, take with you words, and turn to the LORD. Repentance offers God our hearts again. Notice that the inner parts were given. The guilty person surrendered his inner self. Real repentance changes the heart, not just behavior. Today, when you repent, give God your best. Let returning to Him be an act of wholehearted worship.
S: From the trespass offering, all the fat, including the rump and the fat on the inwards, was offered to God. Even an offering for wrongdoing gave God the best. When you repent, return to God with your whole heart in worship.
P: Return with your best. Leviticus 7:3: 'he shall offer of it all the fat thereof.' Repentance gives God your heart again. Take words, and turn to the LORD.

== 7:4
T: Taken away
R: 'And the two kidneys, and the fat that is on them, which is by the flanks, and the caul that is above the liver, with the kidneys, it shall he take away.' The kidneys, their fat, and the covering of the liver were removed from the trespass offering for God. The same inward parts belonged to the LORD in every offering. Notice the steady repetition across these chapters. Burnt, peace, sin, and trespass offerings all gave God the innermost parts. Whatever the reason for coming to God, He claims the heart. This repetition is a quiet sermon. God is not satisfied with outward religion. He wants the inner person. First Samuel 16 says, the LORD looketh on the heart. Hebrews 4 says the word of God is a discerner of the thoughts and intents of the heart. God sees within. Today, whatever brings you to God, give Him your innermost self. Let your worship and repentance reach the heart.
S: The kidneys, their fat and the caul above the liver were taken away from the trespass offering for God. Every offering gave God the innermost parts, for He looks on the heart. Whatever brings you to God, give Him your innermost self.
P: God looks on the heart. Leviticus 7:4: 'the two kidneys… it shall he take away.' Every offering gave God the inner parts. Give Him your inner self today.

== 7:5
T: It is a trespass offering
R: 'And the priest shall burn them upon the altar for an offering made by fire unto the LORD: it is a trespass offering.' The priest burned the fat portions on the altar as an offering by fire to the LORD. It was a trespass offering. Notice it is a trespass offering. God named it clearly. Each offering had its purpose. The trespass offering dealt with debt, with wrong that needed repayment. Sin creates a debt we cannot pay. Jesus taught in Matthew 6 to pray, forgive us our debts. Sin is a debt to God. Christ became our trespass offering and paid that debt. Colossians 2 says He blotted out the handwriting of ordinances that was against us… nailing it to his cross. The record of our debt was cancelled. Notice unto the LORD. The debt was owed to God, and it was paid to Him. Today, rejoice that your debt to God is paid. Christ nailed it to His cross.
S: The priest burned the fat on the altar as an offering made by fire to the LORD, for it was a trespass offering. The trespass offering dealt with the debt sin creates, which Christ paid in full. Rejoice that your debt to God is paid and nailed to the cross.
P: Debt paid. Leviticus 7:5: 'it is a trespass offering.' Sin is a debt we cannot pay. Christ nailed it to His cross. Rejoice today.

== 7:6
T: Eaten in the holy place
R: 'Every male among the priests shall eat thereof: it shall be eaten in the holy place: it is most holy.' The male priests ate the meat of the trespass offering in the holy place. It was most holy and had to be treated with reverence. Notice in the holy place. The meal was eaten in God's presence, not taken home. The priests' provision was tied to their service. What God gives for ministry must be received with reverence. Notice most holy again. The phrase keeps returning. God wanted His servants to remember the sacredness of what they handled. Familiarity can dull reverence. Those who serve in holy things every day must be careful not to grow casual. Hebrews 12 says, let us have grace, whereby we may serve God acceptably with reverence and godly fear. Today, serve God with reverence. Do not let familiarity dull your awe of His holiness.
S: The male priests ate the trespass offering in the holy place, for it was most holy. Their provision was tied to their service and received with reverence. Serve God with reverence and do not let familiarity dull your awe of His holiness.

== 7:7
T: One law for them
R: 'As the sin offering is, so is the trespass offering: there is one law for them: the priest that maketh atonement therewith shall have it.' The sin offering and trespass offering followed the same rule. The priest who made atonement received the meat. Notice one law for them. God's rules were consistent. The priest who did the work of atonement received the provision. Faithful service was rewarded. Galatians 6 says, Let him that is taught in the word communicate unto him that teacheth in all good things. Those who minister should be supported. Notice the priest that maketh atonement. The priest stood between God and the sinner. His work was costly and holy. Christ is the Priest who made atonement. Unlike the priests of Israel, He did not receive meat for His work. He gave Himself. Philippians 2 says He humbled himself, and became obedient unto death. Today, thank Christ for making atonement at His own cost. And support those who faithfully serve you in His name.
S: The sin and trespass offerings shared one law, and the priest who made atonement received the meat. Faithful service was provided for, while Christ made atonement by giving Himself. Thank Christ for His costly atonement and support those who serve you in His name.

== 7:8
T: The skin of the burnt offering
R: 'And the priest that offereth any man's burnt offering, even the priest shall have to himself the skin of the burnt offering which he hath offered.' The priest who offered a burnt offering kept its hide. Everything else was burned, but the skin was his. Notice the skin. In Genesis 3, God clothed Adam and Eve with coats of skins after their sin. An animal died, and its skin covered their shame. The priest received the skin of the sacrifice. This quiet detail echoes Eden. A sacrifice provides a covering. Isaiah 61 says, he hath clothed me with the garments of salvation, he hath covered me with the robe of righteousness. Christ's death provides our covering. We are clothed in His righteousness. Galatians 3 says, as many of you as have been baptized into Christ have put on Christ. Today, rejoice that you are clothed by a sacrifice. Christ covers your shame with His righteousness.
S: The priest who offered a burnt offering kept its skin for himself. This detail echoes Eden, where God clothed Adam and Eve with skins after their sin. Rejoice that Christ's sacrifice clothes you with the robe of righteousness.
P: Clothed by a sacrifice. Leviticus 7:8: 'the priest shall have to himself the skin of the burnt offering.' Christ covers your shame with His righteousness. Put on Christ today.

== 7:9
T: The oven and the pan
R: 'And all the meat offering that is baken in the oven, and all that is dressed in the fryingpan, and in the pan, shall be the priest's that offereth it.' Cooked grain offerings, baked in an oven or prepared in a pan, belonged to the priest who offered them. Notice the variety. Oven, frying pan, and griddle. Worshipers prepared their offerings in different ways. Each was acceptable. God received offerings of different shapes and styles. Christian worship also takes many forms. Different cultures, gifts, and personalities offer praise in different ways. First Corinthians 12 says, there are diversities of gifts, but the same Spirit. God is not limited to one style. Notice shall be the priest's that offereth it. The priest who served received the provision. Service and provision went together. Today, bring God your worship in the form He has given you. Your offering, prepared in your way, is welcome to Him.
S: Grain offerings baked in the oven or dressed in the frying pan or pan belonged to the priest who offered them. God received offerings prepared in different ways. Bring God your worship in the form He has given you, for it is welcome.

== 7:10
T: One as much as another
R: 'And every meat offering, mingled with oil, and dry, shall all the sons of Aaron have, one as much as another.' Uncooked grain offerings, whether mixed with oil or dry, were shared equally among all of Aaron's sons. No priest received more than another. Notice one as much as another. Equal sharing prevented rivalry among the priests. No one could claim a bigger portion. Fairness kept peace in God's household. Envy and competition can poison ministry. First Corinthians 3 says, ye are yet carnal: for whereas there is among you envying, and strife… are ye not carnal. Comparison breeds division. Notice that this applied to the raw offerings, while cooked ones went to the officiating priest. God's arrangements were wise. He balanced reward for service with fairness for all. Second Corinthians 8 speaks of equality, that there be no lack. Today, guard against envy in God's work. Rejoice when others are blessed. In God's family, there is enough for all.
S: Uncooked grain offerings were shared equally among all the sons of Aaron, one as much as another. Fair sharing prevented rivalry and envy among those who served. Guard against envy in God's work and rejoice when others are blessed.
P: No rivalry. Leviticus 7:10: 'shall all the sons of Aaron have, one as much as another.' Envy poisons ministry. Rejoice when others are blessed today.

== 7:11
T: The law of peace offerings
R: 'And this is the law of the sacrifice of peace offerings, which he shall offer unto the LORD.' God now gave the priests detailed instructions for the peace offerings. This was the offering of fellowship and celebration. Notice the peace offering came last in this list. After burnt offerings, grain offerings, sin offerings, and trespass offerings came peace. The order is meaningful. Dedication and atonement lead to fellowship. Peace is the goal. God did not want His people stuck in guilt. He wanted them to enjoy fellowship with Him. Atonement clears the way; peace is the destination. Romans 5 says, we also joy in God through our Lord Jesus Christ, by whom we have now received the atonement. Joy in God is the outcome of atonement. Today, move beyond guilt into fellowship. Christ has made atonement. Now enjoy peace and joy in God.
S: God gave the law of the sacrifice of peace offerings, the offering of fellowship. Peace came last because atonement leads to fellowship and joy. Move beyond guilt into fellowship and enjoy peace with God through Christ.
P: Peace is the goal. Leviticus 7:11: 'this is the law of the sacrifice of peace offerings.' Atonement clears the way; fellowship is the destination. Joy in God today.

== 7:12
T: For a thanksgiving
R: 'If he offer it for a thanksgiving, then he shall offer with the sacrifice of thanksgiving unleavened cakes mingled with oil, and unleavened wafers anointed with oil.' A peace offering could be brought to give thanks. With it came cakes and wafers made with oil. Thanksgiving was expressed with a meal. Notice for a thanksgiving. Some peace offerings were simply to say thank you. The worshiper remembered God's goodness and responded with a gift. Gratitude was a form of worship. Psalm 107 says, Oh that men would praise the LORD for his goodness. Psalm 116 says, I will offer to thee the sacrifice of thanksgiving. Hebrews 13 calls us to offer the sacrifice of praise to God continually, that is, the fruit of our lips giving thanks. Notice the generous bread. Thanksgiving was not stingy. It came with abundance. Today, offer God a sacrifice of thanksgiving. Name His goodness to you, and thank Him generously.

R: 'Besides the cakes, he shall offer for his offering leavened bread with the sacrifice of thanksgiving of his peace offerings.' Along with the unleavened cakes, the worshiper brought leavened bread with his thanksgiving offering. This was unusual, since leaven was normally excluded. Notice leavened bread. Leaven was not burned on the altar. But it could be part of the shared meal. Ordinary, everyday bread joined the celebration. Thanksgiving is not only for holy moments. It reaches into our ordinary lives. Leavened bread was common household food. God welcomed it at the thanksgiving meal. We can thank God for daily bread. Matthew 6 says, Give us this day our daily bread. Ordinary provision is cause for gratitude. First Thessalonians 5 says, In every thing give thanks. Notice besides the cakes. == 7:14
T: An heave offering
R: 'And of it he shall offer one out of the whole oblation for an heave offering unto the LORD, and it shall be the priest's that sprinkleth the blood of the peace offerings.' From the bread offering, one piece of each kind was lifted up as a heave offering to the LORD. It then belonged to the priest who sprinkled the blood. Notice heave offering. The portion was lifted up toward God, then given to the priest. It was dedicated upward, then shared outward. Our gifts go up to God and then out to serve others. Notice one out of the whole. A portion represented the whole. Giving part acknowledged that all came from God. First Chronicles 29 says, all things come of thee, and of thine own have we given thee. When we give, we return what was always His. Today, lift your gifts to God. Then let Him use them to bless those who serve.

R: 'And the flesh of the sacrifice of his peace offerings for thanksgiving shall be eaten the same day that it is offered; he shall not leave any of it until the morning.' The meat of a thanksgiving offering had to be eaten on the day it was offered. None could be kept until morning. Notice the same day. The thanksgiving meal had to be shared at once. The worshiper could not store it for himself. A large animal meant inviting many people to eat. Thanksgiving became hospitality. This was gratitude that overflowed to others. Deuteronomy 16 says, thou shalt rejoice… thou, and thy son, and thy daughter… and the stranger, and the fatherless, and the widow. Notice that it could not be hoarded. Thanksgiving is not stored but shared. Gratitude moves outward. Luke 14 tells us to invite the poor, the maimed, the lame, the blind. Today, let your gratitude become generosity. Share your blessings with others while the day is fresh.

A peace offering for a vow or a freewill offering could be eaten on the day it was offered and the next day. Notice a vow. Some peace offerings fulfilled promises made to God. A person in trouble might vow to offer thanks when God delivered him. When the answer came, he kept his word. Psalm 66 says, I will pay thee my vows, Which my lips have uttered… when I was in trouble. Notice a voluntary offering. Others were given freely, without any vow. Second Corinthians 9 says, Every man according as he purposeth in his heart, so let him give. God welcomes both kept promises and spontaneous gifts. == 7:17
T: The third day
R: 'But the remainder of the flesh of the sacrifice on the third day shall be burnt with fire.' Any meat left over until the third day had to be burned. It could not be eaten. Notice the time limit. Fellowship meals were meant to be fresh. Meat in a hot climate would spoil. God's law protected health while teaching a lesson. Worship should be fresh, not stale. We cannot live on old experiences of God. Yesterday's blessing is not enough for today. Exodus 16 shows that manna kept overnight bred worms. God gives fresh provision daily. Lamentations 3 says His mercies are new every morning. Our fellowship with Him should also be renewed daily. Notice burnt with fire. What was left was not wasted carelessly but given to God. Today, seek fresh fellowship with God. Do not live on old experiences. Come to Him new each day.

== 7:18

If any meat was eaten on the third day, the whole offering was rejected. It was not credited to the worshiper, and the one who ate it bore his guilt. Notice it shall not be accepted. Disobedience in the details could spoil the whole offering. God's instructions were not suggestions. Worship on our own terms is not acceptable worship. First Samuel 15 records Samuel saying, to obey is better than sacrifice. Saul offered sacrifices, but he had disobeyed. His worship was rejected. Notice neither shall it be imputed. The offering did not count. Outward religion without obedience gains nothing. John 4 says the Father seeks those who worship Him in spirit and in truth. Today, worship God His way. Let obedience shape your offering, so that it is accepted.
S: If any peace offering was eaten on the third day, it was not accepted or imputed to the one who offered it. Worship on our own terms is not acceptable worship. Worship God His way, letting obedience shape your offering.

The fellowship meal was open to everyone who was clean. It was not limited to priests. Ordinary families could share in it. Cleanness, not status, determined who could eat. Through Christ, all who are cleansed are welcome at God's table. First Corinthians 11 tells us to examine ourselves before the Lord's Supper. We come cleansed by Christ and with honest hearts. == 7:20

R: 'But the soul that eateth of the flesh of the sacrifice of peace offerings, that pertain unto the LORD, having his uncleanness upon him, even that soul shall be cut off from his people.' Anyone who ate the peace offering while unclean was to be cut off from Israel. Coming to God's meal in an unclean state was serious. Notice that pertain unto the LORD. The peace offering was God's meal. Eating it was sharing in something holy. Treating God's table casually was an insult to Him. First Corinthians 11 warns that whoever eats the Lord's Supper unworthily shall be guilty of the body and blood of the Lord. Paul says some were weak and sickly for this cause. Notice cut off. The penalty was severe because the offense was against God's holiness. Yet the remedy was simple: be cleansed first. God always provided a way to become clean. Today, approach God's table with reverence. Do not come carelessly. Let Christ cleanse you, then come with joy.

== 7:21
T: Any unclean thing
R: 'Moreover the soul that shall touch any unclean thing… and eat of the flesh of the sacrifice of peace offerings, which pertain unto the LORD, even that soul shall be cut off from his people.' Anyone who touched something unclean, human or animal, and then ate the peace offering would be cut off. Defilement had to be dealt with before fellowship. Notice touch any unclean thing. Defilement could come from many sources. Some were unavoidable in daily life. That was not the problem. The problem was coming to God's table without first being cleansed. God never condemned people for becoming unclean in ordinary life. He condemned ignoring His provision for cleansing. Second Corinthians 7 says, let us cleanse ourselves from all filthiness of the flesh and spirit, perfecting holiness in the fear of God. Cleansing is our responsibility in response to His grace. Today, do not ignore the defilement you pick up in daily life. Bring it to God for cleansing, then enjoy fellowship with Him freely.
