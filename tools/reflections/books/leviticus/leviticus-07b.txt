# Leviticus 7:22-38: Fat, blood and the priests' portion

== 7:22
T: The LORD spake again
R: 'And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying.' God spoke again with instructions for all the people, not just the priests. These laws concerned what Israel could eat. Notice that God cared about ordinary meals. His law reached into kitchens and dinner tables. Holiness was not confined to the tabernacle. It shaped daily life. First Corinthians 10 says, Whether therefore ye eat, or drink, or whatsoever ye do, do all to the glory of God. Even eating can honor God. Notice that the word went to all Israel. Every family needed to know God's ways. Faith is not just for leaders and specialists. Deuteronomy 6 tells parents to teach God's words when thou sittest in thine house. Faith belongs at home. Today, let God's word shape your ordinary life. Bring Him into your meals, your home, and your daily routines.
S: The LORD spoke to Moses again with laws for all the people about their meals. Holiness reached into ordinary kitchens and dinner tables. Let God's word shape your ordinary life and do all, even eating, to His glory.
P: Holy everyday life. Leviticus 7:22: 'And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying.' Whether you eat or drink, do all to God's glory. Bring Him into your home today.

== 7:23
T: No manner of fat
R: 'Speak unto the children of Israel, saying, Ye shall eat no manner of fat, of ox, or of sheep, or of goat.' Israel was not to eat the fat of cattle, sheep, or goats. These were the animals used for sacrifice, and their fat belonged to God. Notice the animals named. These were sacrificial animals. Their fat was God's portion at the altar. Even at home, Israel was not to eat it. God's portion was respected everywhere. This taught Israel that some things belong to God wherever we are. Reverence was not just for the sanctuary. It followed them home. Notice the principle behind it. What God claims, we do not take for ourselves. Christians are not under these food laws, as Mark 7 and Acts 10 show. But the lesson remains. Romans 14 says, he that eateth, eateth to the Lord, for he giveth God thanks. Today, honor what belongs to God, at home as well as in church. Let reverence follow you everywhere.
S: Israel was to eat no fat of ox, sheep or goat, for the fat of sacrificial animals belonged to God. God's portion was respected at home as well as at the altar. Honor what belongs to God everywhere and let reverence follow you home.
P: Reverence at home. Leviticus 7:23: 'Ye shall eat no manner of fat, of ox, or of sheep, or of goat.' What God claims is His everywhere. Honor Him in daily life.

== 7:24
T: Any other use
R: 'And the fat of the beast that dieth of itself, and the fat of that which is torn with beasts, may be used in any other use: but ye shall in no wise eat of it.' Fat from an animal that died naturally or was killed by wild beasts could be used for other purposes. But it could never be eaten. Notice any other use. God's law was practical. Fat had uses in daily life, perhaps for lamps or leather. God did not waste it. He allowed its use while keeping the boundary on eating. God's commands are not meant to burden us needlessly. They are wise and balanced. First John 5 says, his commandments are not grievous. God draws lines where they matter and gives freedom elsewhere. Notice in no wise. On the main point, there was no compromise. Clear boundaries protect us. Today, trust that God's commands are wise. Enjoy the freedom He gives, and keep firmly the lines He draws.
S: Fat from animals that died naturally or were torn could be used for other purposes, but never eaten. God's law was practical, giving freedom while keeping a firm boundary. Trust that God's commands are wise, enjoying His freedom and keeping His lines firmly.
P: Wise boundaries. Leviticus 7:24: 'may be used in any other use: but ye shall in no wise eat of it.' His commandments are not grievous. Keep His lines, enjoy His freedom.
H: #DailyGrace #Leviticus724 #Wisdom #Freedom #Obedience #BibleVerse

== 7:25
T: Cut off
R: 'For whosoever eateth the fat of the beast, of which men offer an offering made by fire unto the LORD, even the soul that eateth it shall be cut off from his people.' Anyone who ate the fat of an animal used for sacrifice was to be cut off from Israel. The penalty was severe. Notice the reason: of which men offer an offering made by fire unto the LORD. Eating the fat meant taking God's portion for oneself. It was stealing from God. The seriousness shows how much God cares about what is His. Acts 5 tells of Ananias and Sapphira, who kept back part of what they claimed to give God. Their sin was not that they kept money, but that they lied to God about it. God takes His portion seriously. Notice cut off from his people. Sin against God affected one's place in the community. Today, do not take for yourself what belongs to God. Be honest with Him about your giving and devotion.
S: Whoever ate the fat of an animal offered by fire to the LORD was to be cut off from his people. Taking God's portion for oneself was stealing from Him. Do not take what belongs to God, and be honest with Him in your giving and devotion.
P: God's portion. Leviticus 7:25: 'whosoever eateth the fat… shall be cut off.' Don't take what is God's. Be honest with Him in your giving today.
H: #DailyGrace #Leviticus725 #Honesty #Giving #Reverence #BibleVerse

== 7:26
T: No manner of blood
R: 'Moreover ye shall eat no manner of blood, whether it be of fowl or of beast, in any of your dwellings.' Israel was not to eat any blood, whether from birds or animals, anywhere they lived. Blood was sacred. Notice no manner of blood. The ban was total. Blood represented life, and life belonged to God. From Genesis 9, after the flood, God told Noah not to eat flesh with its blood. The command was older than Moses. Notice in any of your dwellings. Not just at the tabernacle, but in every home. The sacredness of life was honored everywhere. This taught a deep respect for life. Life is not ours to treat casually. It is God's gift. Acts 15 shows the early church asking Gentile believers to abstain from blood, out of sensitivity to Jewish brothers. The principle of respect for life remains. Today, honor life as God's gift. Treat every life with the respect due to its Creator.
S: Israel was to eat no manner of blood, of fowl or beast, in any of their dwellings. Blood represented life, and life belonged to God everywhere. Honor life as God's gift and treat every life with the respect due to its Creator.
P: Life is sacred. Leviticus 7:26: 'ye shall eat no manner of blood.' Life belongs to God. Honor every life as His gift today.

== 7:27
T: Whatsoever soul
R: 'Whatsoever soul it be that eateth any manner of blood, even that soul shall be cut off from his people.' Anyone who ate blood of any kind was to be cut off from Israel. The penalty applied to everyone. Notice whatsoever soul. No one was exempt. Rich or poor, leader or common person, the rule applied to all. God's standards are not adjusted for status. Notice the reason for such seriousness. Leviticus 17 explains that the blood was given to make atonement. Eating blood treated the means of atonement as common food. It disregarded God's provision for forgiveness. We can do the same today. Hebrews 10 warns against those who have counted the blood of the covenant… an unholy thing. Treating Christ's sacrifice lightly is a grave matter. Today, never treat the blood of Christ as common. Honor the sacrifice that made your forgiveness possible.
S: Whatsoever soul ate any manner of blood was to be cut off from his people. The blood was given for atonement, so eating it treated God's provision as common. Never treat Christ's blood as common, but honor the sacrifice that made your forgiveness possible.
P: Never common. Leviticus 7:27: 'Whatsoever soul it be that eateth any manner of blood.' The blood was for atonement. Honor Christ's sacrifice today.

== 7:28
T: More instructions
R: 'And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying.' God spoke again, this time about the priests' share of the peace offerings. He had more to say about how worship should be ordered. Notice how often this phrase appears in Leviticus. It reminds us that these laws were not Moses' inventions. They came from God. The authority behind them was divine. Second Timothy 3 says, All scripture is given by inspiration of God. Every part of Scripture, even detailed laws, comes from Him. Notice that God was patient in His teaching. He gave instruction piece by piece. He did not grow tired of guiding His people. Isaiah 28 describes His way: precept upon precept; line upon line. God teaches us in layers. Today, approach Scripture as God's own word. Even the detailed parts carry His voice. Read patiently, and let Him teach you line upon line.

== 7:29
T: Bring his oblation
R: 'Speak unto the children of Israel, saying, He that offereth the sacrifice of his peace offerings unto the LORD shall bring his oblation unto the LORD of the sacrifice of his peace offerings.' The person offering a peace offering had to bring God's portion himself. He could not send it with someone else. Notice shall bring his oblation. The worshiper personally carried God's portion to the altar. Fellowship with God was not done by proxy. Each person had to come. There is no substitute for personal relationship with God. Others can pray for us and teach us, but we must come ourselves. James 4 says, Draw nigh to God, and he will draw nigh to you. The invitation is personal. Notice unto the LORD, twice. The gift was for God. Today, come to God personally. Do not rely on someone else's faith. Bring your own offering of love and gratitude to Him.
S: The one offering a peace offering had to bring God's portion to the LORD himself. Fellowship with God was personal and could not be done by proxy. Come to God personally today and bring your own offering of love and gratitude.
P: Come yourself. Leviticus 7:29: 'he… shall bring his oblation unto the LORD.' No one can draw near for you. Draw nigh to God, and He will draw nigh to you.

== 7:30
T: His own hands
R: 'His own hands shall bring the offerings of the LORD made by fire, the fat with the breast, it shall he bring, that the breast may be waved for a wave offering before the LORD.' The worshiper carried the fat and the breast with his own hands. The breast was waved before the LORD as a wave offering. Notice his own hands. The emphasis is personal. The worshiper did not just watch. He was involved. Worship is participation, not spectatorship. Romans 12 says, present your bodies a living sacrifice. We bring ourselves, with our own hands. Notice waved before the LORD. The breast was moved toward the altar and back, presenting it to God and then receiving it back as His gift to the priests. What we give to God, He often returns to bless others. Today, worship with your whole self. Do not just watch. Bring your offering with your own hands.
S: The worshiper brought the fat and breast with his own hands, and the breast was waved before the LORD. Worship was active participation, not watching. Worship God with your whole self, bringing your offering with your own hands.
P: Not a spectator. Leviticus 7:30: 'His own hands shall bring the offerings of the LORD.' Worship is participation. Bring yourself fully to God today.

== 7:31
T: The breast for Aaron
R: 'And the priest shall burn the fat upon the altar: but the breast shall be Aaron's and his sons'.' The fat was burned for God, but the breast was given to Aaron and his sons. God's servants shared in the offering. Notice the division. God received the fat. The priests received the breast. The worshiper ate the rest. Everyone had a share. The peace offering created a shared table. Notice the breast. Some see in this a picture of love and affection, since the breast is near the heart. The priests were sustained by what was offered in love. Those who serve God's people are cared for through the people's worship. Philippians 4 shows Paul thanking the Philippians for their gifts: an odour of a sweet smell, a sacrifice acceptable, wellpleasing to God. Gifts to God's servants are offerings to God. Today, care for those who serve you in God's name. Your support is worship that pleases Him.
S: The priest burned the fat on the altar, but the breast belonged to Aaron and his sons. God, the priests and the worshiper each had a share at one table. Care for those who serve you in God's name, for your support is worship that pleases Him.
P: A shared table. Leviticus 7:31: 'the breast shall be Aaron's and his sons'.' Gifts to God's servants are a sweet-smelling sacrifice. Support them today.

== 7:32
T: The right shoulder
R: 'And the right shoulder shall ye give unto the priest for an heave offering of the sacrifices of your peace offerings.' The right thigh or shoulder of the peace offering was given to the priest as a heave offering. It was lifted to God, then given to His servant. Notice the right shoulder. The right side often represented strength and honor in Scripture. The choicest cut went to the priest. God made sure His servants received good portions. Notice shall ye give. This was not optional. Supporting the priests was part of Israel's worship. They were commanded to give. Malachi 3 rebuked Israel for neglecting tithes, which left the Levites unsupported. First Timothy 5 says, Let the elders that rule well be counted worthy of double honour… The labourer is worthy of his reward. Today, honor those who teach you God's word. Give them not leftovers but your best, as unto the Lord.
S: The right shoulder of the peace offering was given to the priest as an heave offering. Israel was commanded to give God's servants a choice portion. Honor those who teach you God's word by giving them your best, as unto the Lord.
P: Worthy of honour. Leviticus 7:32: 'the right shoulder shall ye give unto the priest.' The labourer is worthy of his reward. Honor those who teach you God's word.

== 7:33
T: For his part
R: 'He among the sons of Aaron, that offereth the blood of the peace offerings, and the fat, shall have the right shoulder for his part.' The priest who actually offered the blood and fat of a peace offering received the right shoulder as his share. Notice that offereth. The priest who did the work received the portion. Service and provision went together. Those who labor in ministry are rewarded for their faithfulness. First Corinthians 3 says, every man shall receive his own reward according to his own labour. God sees and rewards faithful service. Notice for his part. Each priest had his part. God assigned portions fairly. No one was forgotten. Hebrews 6 says, God is not unrighteous to forget your work and labour of love. Your service for God is noticed, even when people overlook it. Today, serve faithfully, knowing that God sees your labor. He has a part for you, and He will not forget.
S: The priest who offered the blood and fat of the peace offerings received the right shoulder for his part. Service and reward went together, and each priest had his portion. Serve faithfully, knowing God sees your labor and will not forget it.

== 7:34
T: Taken and given
R: 'For the wave breast and the heave shoulder have I taken of the children of Israel from off the sacrifices of their peace offerings, and have given them unto Aaron the priest and unto his sons by a statute for ever.' God said He took the wave breast and heave shoulder from Israel's offerings and gave them to the priests as a lasting statute. Notice have I taken… and have given. God took from the people and gave to the priests. Our gifts pass through God's hands. When we give to support His work, we give to Him, and He distributes. This lifts giving above human obligation. We are not just paying a minister. We are giving to God. Second Corinthians 9 says, he that ministereth seed to the sower both minister bread for your food, and multiply your seed. God supplies the giver and the receiver. Today, give to God, trusting Him to use it well. He takes your gift and gives it where it is needed.
S: God said He had taken the wave breast and heave shoulder from Israel's peace offerings and given them to the priests forever. Gifts passed through God's hands as He provided for His servants. Give to God, trusting Him to use your gift where it is needed.
P: Through God's hands. Leviticus 7:34: 'have I taken of the children of Israel… and have given them unto Aaron.' Your gift goes to God. Trust Him to use it well.

== 7:35
T: The portion of the anointing
R: 'This is the portion of the anointing of Aaron, and of the anointing of his sons, out of the offerings of the LORD made by fire, in the day when he presented them to minister unto the LORD.' These portions belonged to Aaron and his sons because they were anointed to serve. Their share began the day they were set apart for ministry. Notice the portion of the anointing. Their provision came with their calling. God did not call them and leave them without support. Whom God calls, He provides for. Philippians 4 says, my God shall supply all your need according to his riches in glory. Notice to minister unto the LORD. The priests ministered first to God. Their primary service was to Him, not to people. Ministry flows from relationship with God. Acts 13 shows the church at Antioch ministering to the Lord before being sent out. Today, trust that God provides for your calling. Serve Him first, and He will supply what you need.
S: These portions belonged to Aaron and his sons from the day they were anointed to minister unto the LORD. Their provision came with their calling. Trust that God provides for your calling, and serve Him first.
P: Provision with calling. Leviticus 7:35: 'This is the portion of the anointing of Aaron.' Whom God calls, He supplies. Minister to the Lord first today.

== 7:36
T: In the day that he anointed them
R: 'Which the LORD commanded to be given them of the children of Israel, in the day that he anointed them, by a statute for ever throughout their generations.' God commanded Israel to give these portions to the priests from the day of their anointing. It was a permanent statute for all generations. Notice the LORD commanded. The priests' support was not a favor from the people. It was God's command. Israel's generosity toward the priests was obedience to God. Notice throughout their generations. Each new generation was responsible to continue supporting God's servants. Faithfulness in giving must be passed down. Second Timothy 2 says, the things that thou hast heard of me… the same commit thou to faithful men, who shall be able to teach others also. Ministry continues across generations, and so must its support. Today, teach the next generation to support God's work. Generosity is an inheritance worth passing on.

== 7:37
T: This is the law
R: 'This is the law of the burnt offering, of the meat offering, and of the sin offering, and of the trespass offering, and of the consecrations, and of the sacrifice of the peace offerings.' The section closes with a summary of the offerings: burnt, grain, sin, trespass, consecration, and peace. Each had its law. Notice the list. Together these offerings covered the whole of Israel's relationship with God: dedication, gratitude, forgiveness, restitution, ordination, and fellowship. Every need was addressed. Christ fulfills them all. He is our burnt offering, wholly given to God. He is the grain offering, the perfect life. He is the sin and trespass offering, bearing our guilt. He is our peace. Hebrews 10 says, by one offering he hath perfected for ever them that are sanctified. One sacrifice fulfills every offering. Colossians 2 says, ye are complete in him. Today, rejoice that Christ is everything these offerings pictured. In Him, you are complete.
S: The section closes with the law of the burnt, meat, sin, trespass, consecration and peace offerings. Together they covered every part of Israel's relationship with God, and Christ fulfills them all. Rejoice that Christ is everything these offerings pictured, and in Him you are complete.
P: Complete in Him. Leviticus 7:37: 'This is the law of the burnt offering, of the meat offering, and of the sin offering.' Christ fulfills every offering. You are complete in Him.

== 7:38
T: In mount Sinai
R: 'Which the LORD commanded Moses in mount Sinai, in the day that he commanded the children of Israel to offer their oblations unto the LORD, in the wilderness of Sinai.' These laws were given by the LORD to Moses at Mount Sinai, in the wilderness. God commanded Israel to bring their offerings to Him there. Notice in the wilderness of Sinai. God gave His law in a barren place. Israel had not yet reached the Promised Land. Yet worship began in the wilderness. We do not have to wait for perfect circumstances to worship God. He meets us in wilderness seasons. Hosea 2 says, I will allure her, and bring her into the wilderness, and speak comfortably unto her. God speaks tenderly in the desert. Notice the LORD commanded. These offerings came from God's own mouth. Israel's worship was His design. Today, worship God where you are, even in the wilderness. He speaks and meets His people in hard places.
S: The LORD commanded these laws to Moses in mount Sinai, in the wilderness, before Israel reached the land. Worship began in a barren place, where God met His people. Worship God where you are, even in a wilderness season, for He meets His people there.
P: Worship in the wilderness. Leviticus 7:38: 'Which the LORD commanded Moses in mount Sinai… in the wilderness of Sinai.' God speaks tenderly in hard places. Worship Him where you are.
