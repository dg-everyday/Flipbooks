# Leviticus 8:1-18: The consecration of Aaron and his sons

== 8:1
T: Time to ordain
R: 'And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying.' After giving the laws of the offerings, God spoke to Moses about ordaining Aaron and his sons. The time had come to set the priests apart. Notice the order. First God gave the laws of worship. Then He appointed people to carry them out. God's work needs both truth and servants. Exodus 29 had already given these instructions. Now they would be carried out. What God commands, He also brings to pass. There was a waiting period between the command and its fulfillment. God's timing is perfect. Habakkuk 2 says, though it tarry, wait for it; because it will surely come. Notice that God spoke the word that started it. Ordination began with God, not with Aaron's ambition. Hebrews 5 says, no man taketh this honour unto himself, but he that is called of God, as was Aaron. Today, trust God's timing for your calling. What He has spoken, He will bring to pass.
S: After the laws of the offerings, the LORD spoke to Moses about ordaining Aaron and his sons. What God had commanded in Exodus was now carried out in His time. Trust God's timing for your calling, for what He has spoken He will bring to pass.
P: Called of God. Leviticus 8:1: 'And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying.' No one takes this honour himself. Trust God's timing for your calling today.

== 8:2
T: Everything prepared
R: 'Take Aaron and his sons with him, and the garments, and the anointing oil, and a bullock for the sin offering, and two rams, and a basket of unleavened bread.' God told Moses to gather Aaron and his sons, the priestly garments, the anointing oil, a bull for the sin offering, two rams, and a basket of unleavened bread. Notice the list. Everything needed for ordination was gathered beforehand. God prepares thoroughly. Nothing was left to chance. Notice what was included. Garments for covering, oil for anointing, a sin offering for cleansing, rams for dedication, and bread for fellowship. Each element had meaning. Ordination involved cleansing, consecration, and communion. Notice the sin offering was first in the list of animals. Before Aaron could serve others, his own sin had to be dealt with. Hebrews 7 contrasts this with Christ, who needed not daily… to offer up sacrifice, first for his own sins. Today, let God prepare you for His service. He provides everything needed, starting with cleansing.
S: God told Moses to take Aaron and his sons with the garments, anointing oil, a sin offering bullock, two rams and a basket of unleavened bread. Everything needed for cleansing, consecration and fellowship was prepared. Let God prepare you for His service, beginning with His cleansing.
P: Fully prepared. Leviticus 8:2: 'Take Aaron and his sons with him, and the garments, and the anointing oil.' God provides everything His servants need. Let Him prepare you today.

== 8:3
T: Gather all the congregation
R: 'And gather thou all the congregation together unto the door of the tabernacle of the congregation.' God told Moses to assemble the whole community at the entrance to the tabernacle. The ordination would be public. Notice all the congregation. The priests were set apart in front of everyone. The people needed to see who God had chosen. Public ordination gave the priests recognized authority. It also made them accountable. Everyone knew who was supposed to serve. Ministry is never a private arrangement. It belongs to the whole community of God's people. Acts 6 shows the church choosing deacons before all the people, then praying and laying hands on them. Notice the door of the tabernacle. The ceremony happened at the entrance, where the people could gather. God brought His people near to witness His work. Today, honor those God has called to serve you. Recognize their calling, and pray for them as they serve.
S: God told Moses to gather all the congregation to the door of the tabernacle for the ordination. The priests were set apart publicly, giving them recognized authority and accountability. Honor those God has called to serve you and pray for them as they serve.
P: Publicly set apart. Leviticus 8:3: 'gather thou all the congregation together unto the door.' Ministry belongs to the whole church. Honor and pray for those who serve you.

== 8:4
T: Moses did as commanded
R: 'And Moses did as the LORD commanded him; and the assembly was gathered together unto the door of the tabernacle of the congregation.' Moses obeyed God's instructions exactly. The people gathered at the entrance of the tabernacle. Notice Moses did as the LORD commanded him. This phrase echoes throughout chapter 8. Moses' obedience is emphasized again and again. The ordination followed God's word precisely. Moses did not improvise or improve on God's plan. He simply obeyed. Faithful servants do what God says. John 14 records Jesus saying, If ye love me, keep my commandments. Notice also that the people came. They responded to the call. A leader's obedience invites the people's response. When leaders follow God, others gather to follow too. First Corinthians 11 says, Be ye followers of me, even as I also am of Christ. Today, obey God simply and fully. Do as He commands, and others may follow your example.
S: Moses did as the LORD commanded him, and the assembly gathered at the door of the tabernacle. His exact obedience echoes through the chapter and invited the people's response. Obey God simply and fully, and others may follow your example.
P: Simple obedience. Leviticus 8:4: 'And Moses did as the LORD commanded him.' No improvising, just obeying. If ye love me, keep my commandments.

== 8:5
T: This is the thing
R: 'And Moses said unto the congregation, This is the thing which the LORD commanded to be done.' Before beginning, Moses explained to the people that what they were about to see was commanded by the LORD. He made the source of authority clear. Notice this is the thing which the LORD commanded. Moses did not present the ceremony as his own idea. He pointed to God's command. This protected Aaron's priesthood from charges of favoritism. Aaron was Moses' brother. People might have thought Moses was promoting his family. But Moses made it clear: this is God's choice, not mine. Numbers 16 shows that Korah later challenged Aaron's priesthood. God Himself defended it. Leaders must ground their actions in God's word, not personal preference. Today, base your decisions on God's word. When you lead, point people to what God has said, not just to what you think.
S: Moses told the congregation that this was the thing the LORD had commanded to be done. He grounded the ordination in God's command, not his own preference or family ties. Base your decisions on God's word and point people to what He has said.
P: God's command, not mine. Leviticus 8:5: 'This is the thing which the LORD commanded to be done.' Ground your leadership in God's word, not personal preference.

== 8:6
T: Washed with water
R: 'And Moses brought Aaron and his sons, and washed them with water.' The first act of ordination was washing. Moses brought Aaron and his sons forward and washed them with water. Notice that washing came first. Before garments, before oil, before sacrifice, there was cleansing. The priests had to be clean before they could serve. God's servants must first be washed. Titus 3 says, he saved us, by the washing of regeneration, and renewing of the Holy Ghost. Salvation begins with cleansing. Notice that Moses washed them. They did not wash themselves. Cleansing was done to them. We cannot cleanse ourselves from sin. God washes us. John 13 shows Jesus washing His disciples' feet. He told Peter, If I wash thee not, thou hast no part with me. We must let Christ wash us. Today, let Christ wash you. Before you serve Him, receive His cleansing. You cannot wash yourself, but He can make you clean.
S: Moses brought Aaron and his sons and washed them with water, the first act of ordination. Cleansing came before garments, oil or sacrifice, and it was done to them. Let Christ wash you, for before you serve Him you need His cleansing.
P: Washed first. Leviticus 8:6: 'Moses brought Aaron and his sons, and washed them with water.' If I wash thee not, thou hast no part with me. Let Christ cleanse you today.

== 8:7
T: Clothed for service
R: 'And he put upon him the coat, and girded him with the girdle, and clothed him with the robe, and put the ephod upon him.' Moses dressed Aaron in the priestly garments: the tunic, the sash, the robe, and the ephod with its woven waistband. Aaron was clothed for service. Notice that Moses clothed Aaron. Aaron did not dress himself. The garments were put on him. This pictures how God clothes us. We do not make our own righteousness. God gives it. Isaiah 61 says, he hath clothed me with the garments of salvation. Notice the layers. Coat, girdle, robe, ephod. Each piece added to the glory of the priest. Exodus 28 said the garments were for glory and for beauty. God's servants are dressed in beauty that comes from Him. Colossians 3 tells us to put on… bowels of mercies, kindness, humbleness of mind. Today, receive the clothing God gives you. Put on Christ's righteousness and the virtues He provides.
S: Moses clothed Aaron with the coat, girdle, robe and ephod for his service. Aaron did not dress himself; the garments of glory and beauty were put on him. Receive the clothing God gives and put on Christ's righteousness and virtues today.
P: Clothed by God. Leviticus 8:7: 'he put upon him the coat… and put the ephod upon him.' God clothes you with garments of salvation. Put on Christ today.

== 8:8
T: The breastplate
R: 'And he put the breastplate upon him: also he put in the breastplate the Urim and the Thummim.' Moses placed the breastplate on Aaron and put the Urim and Thummim inside it. These were used to seek God's will. Notice the breastplate. It bore twelve stones engraved with the names of Israel's tribes. Aaron carried the people over his heart. Exodus 28 says Aaron shall bear the names of the children of Israel… upon his heart. A priest carries his people with love. Notice the Urim and Thummim. Through them, the priest sought God's guidance for the nation. The priest carried both the people and God's counsel. Christ, our High Priest, carries us on His heart. Hebrews 7 says, he ever liveth to make intercession for them. He also guides us by His Spirit. Today, take comfort that Christ carries your name on His heart. And seek His guidance in every decision.
S: Moses put the breastplate on Aaron and placed the Urim and Thummim in it. Aaron bore the names of Israel on his heart and carried the means of seeking God's counsel. Take comfort that Christ carries your name on His heart, and seek His guidance.
P: On His heart. Leviticus 8:8: 'he put the breastplate upon him.' Aaron bore Israel's names over his heart. Christ carries your name and intercedes for you.

== 8:9
T: The holy crown
R: 'And he put the mitre upon his head; also upon the mitre, even upon his forefront, did he put the golden plate, the holy crown; as the LORD commanded Moses.' Moses placed the turban on Aaron's head and attached the golden plate, the holy crown, to the front. It was done as the LORD commanded. Notice the holy crown. Exodus 28 says the plate was engraved, HOLINESS TO THE LORD. Aaron wore holiness on his forehead. His thoughts and mind were consecrated to God. Notice that it was a crown. The priest bore royal dignity. Revelation 1 says Christ hath made us kings and priests unto God. Believers share this dignity. Romans 12 says, be ye transformed by the renewing of your mind. Our minds are to be holy to the LORD. What we think about matters. Philippians 4 says, whatsoever things are pure… think on these things. Today, let holiness to the LORD be written on your mind. Set your thoughts on what pleases Him.
S: Moses put the mitre on Aaron's head with the golden plate, the holy crown, on its front. Aaron wore holiness to the LORD on his forehead, his mind consecrated to God. Let holiness to the LORD mark your mind and set your thoughts on what pleases Him.
P: A holy mind. Leviticus 8:9: 'upon his forefront, did he put the golden plate, the holy crown.' Let holiness mark your thoughts. Think on what is pure today.

T: Anointing the tabernacle
R: 'And Moses took the anointing oil, and anointed the tabernacle and all that was therein, and sanctified them.' Moses took the anointing oil and anointed the tabernacle and everything in it. Everything was set apart for God. Notice all that was therein. Nothing in the tabernacle was left unanointed. Every piece of furniture and every vessel was made holy. The whole dwelling belonged to God. Oil represents the Holy Spirit. The Spirit sets apart for God's use. First Corinthians 6 says, your body is the temple of the Holy Ghost which is in you. We are God's dwelling, anointed by His Spirit. Every part of us is meant for Him. First John 2 says, ye have an unction from the Holy One. Believers are anointed. Notice sanctified them. To sanctify is to set apart. Today, remember that you are God's temple. Let the Spirit set apart every part of your life for Him.

== 8:11
T: Seven times on the altar
R: 'And he sprinkled thereof upon the altar seven times, and anointed the altar and all his vessels, both the laver and his foot, to sanctify them.' Moses sprinkled oil on the altar seven times. He anointed the altar, its utensils, and the bronze basin with its stand, setting them apart. Notice seven times. The altar received special attention. Seven signified completeness. The place of sacrifice was fully consecrated. Notice the laver and his foot. Even the basin and its base were anointed. The place of washing was holy too. Notice all his vessels. Every tool used in worship was set apart. The ordinary utensils of service became holy. Second Timothy 2 speaks of a vessel unto honour, sanctified, and meet for the master's use. We are vessels in God's house. Romans 6 says, yield… your members as instruments of righteousness unto God. Today, offer yourself as a vessel for God's use. Let Him sanctify you completely, so that you are ready for every good work.
S: Moses sprinkled oil on the altar seven times and anointed the altar, its vessels and the laver with its foot. Every tool of worship, down to the basin's base, was fully set apart. Offer yourself as a vessel sanctified and ready for the Master's use.
P: A vessel for His use. Leviticus 8:11: 'anointed the altar and all his vessels… to sanctify them.' Be a vessel unto honour, meet for the Master's use.

== 8:12
T: Oil on Aaron's head
R: 'And he poured of the anointing oil upon Aaron's head, and anointed him, to sanctify him.' Moses poured the anointing oil on Aaron's head, setting him apart for God's service. Aaron was anointed as high priest. Notice poured. The oil was not dabbed but poured. It was abundant. Psalm 133 describes this moment: like the precious ointment upon the head, that ran down upon the beard, even Aaron's beard: that went down to the skirts of his garments. The anointing flowed down over him. The psalm compares it to the unity of brothers dwelling together. Notice that Aaron was anointed alone first. The high priest received this special pouring. Christ means Anointed One. Acts 10 says God anointed Jesus of Nazareth with the Holy Ghost and with power. Jesus is the true Anointed High Priest. From Him, the Spirit flows down to His people. Today, worship Christ, the Anointed One. Receive the Spirit He pours out on His people.
S: Moses poured the anointing oil on Aaron's head to sanctify him as high priest. The abundant oil ran down over him, picturing Christ, the Anointed One, from whom the Spirit flows. Worship Christ, the Anointed One, and receive the Spirit He pours out on His people.
P: The Anointed One. Leviticus 8:12: 'he poured of the anointing oil upon Aaron's head.' Christ means Anointed. From Him the Spirit flows down to you.

Moses brought Aaron's sons forward and dressed them in tunics, sashes, and caps. They too were clothed for priestly service. Notice that the sons received simpler garments than Aaron. Coats, girdles, and caps. They had a different role from the high priest. Yet they were still clothed by God's command for holy service. Every member of God's household has a place. Some lead; others assist. All are clothed for service. First Corinthians 12 says, the members should have the same care one for another. Every role matters. Notice as the LORD commanded Moses. Even the details of their dress followed God's word. Today, accept the role God has given you. Whether prominent or quiet, it is a holy calling. Serve faithfully in the garments He provides.

== 8:14
T: Hands on the sin offering
R: 'And he brought the bullock for the sin offering: and Aaron and his sons laid their hands upon the head of the bullock for the sin offering.' Moses brought the bull for the sin offering. Aaron and his sons laid their hands on its head. Even the newly anointed priests needed atonement. Notice that the priests confessed their own sin first. They had been washed, clothed, and anointed. Yet they still needed a sin offering. Ceremony could not make them sinless. Every human leader is a sinner in need of grace. No matter how gifted or anointed, they need forgiveness too. First John 1 says, If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves. Notice Aaron and his sons together. Father and sons stood side by side in confession. Families can repent together. Hebrews 5 says the high priest must offer for himself as well as for the people. Today, if you serve God, never forget your own need of grace. Begin your service with humble confession.
S: Moses brought the sin offering bullock, and Aaron and his sons laid their hands on its head. Even the newly anointed priests needed atonement for their own sin. If you serve God, never forget your need of grace and begin with humble confession.

== 8:15
T: Reconciliation on the altar
R: 'And he slew it; and Moses took the blood, and put it upon the horns of the altar round about with his finger, and purified the altar… and sanctified it, to make reconciliation upon it.' The bull was killed. Moses put blood on the altar's horns with his finger, purified the altar, poured the rest at its base, and consecrated it for making atonement. Notice purified the altar. Even the altar needed cleansing. It had been made by human hands. Before it could be used to atone for others, it had to be made holy. Notice to make reconciliation upon it. This altar would be the place where God and His people were reconciled. Its purpose was peace. Second Corinthians 5 says, God… hath reconciled us to himself by Jesus Christ, and hath given to us the ministry of reconciliation. The cross is the place of reconciliation. And we are sent to tell others about it. Today, rejoice that you are reconciled to God. Then share that message with someone who needs it.
S: Moses put the bullock's blood on the horns of the altar, purified it and sanctified it to make reconciliation upon it. The altar was cleansed to become the place where God and His people were reconciled. Rejoice that you are reconciled to God and share that message with someone today.
P: The ministry of reconciliation. Leviticus 8:15: 'and sanctified it, to make reconciliation upon it.' You are reconciled to God. Share that message with someone today.

== 8:16
T: Moses burned it
R: 'And he took all the fat that was upon the inwards, and caul above the liver, and the two kidneys, and their fat, and Moses burned it upon the altar.' Moses took the fat from the inner organs, the covering of the liver, and the kidneys with their fat, and burned them on the altar. Notice Moses burned it. At this point, Moses acted as priest. Aaron was not yet fully ordained. Moses, the mediator of the covenant, performed the service. This shows that the priesthood was given, not seized. Aaron did not ordain himself. Another ordained him. Hebrews 5 says, Christ glorified not himself to be made an high priest; but he that said unto him, Thou art my Son. Even Christ was appointed by the Father. Notice all the fat. As always, the best went to God, even in the ordination sin offering. Today, receive your calling as a gift. Do not grasp for position. Let God appoint you in His time.
S: Moses took the fat of the inwards, the caul and the kidneys and burned them on the altar. Moses acted as priest because Aaron's priesthood was given to him, not seized. Receive your calling as a gift and let God appoint you in His time.
P: Given, not grasped. Leviticus 8:16: 'and Moses burned it upon the altar.' Aaron didn't ordain himself; even Christ was appointed. Let God appoint you in His time.

Notice as the LORD commanded Moses. Even the disposal of the remains followed God's word. Today, go to Christ outside the camp. Be willing to bear His reproach, for He bore your sin outside the gate.
S: The rest of the sin offering bullock was burnt with fire without the camp, as the LORD commanded. The priests' sin was carried away, pointing to Christ who suffered outside the gate. Go to Christ outside the camp, willing to bear His reproach.

== 8:18
T: The ram for the burnt offering
R: 'And he brought the ram for the burnt offering: and Aaron and his sons laid their hands upon the head of the ram.' Moses brought the ram for the burnt offering. Aaron and his sons laid their hands on its head. After cleansing came dedication. Notice the order. First the sin offering, then the burnt offering. Cleansing before consecration. The priests could not dedicate themselves to God while their sin stood in the way. Once forgiven, they could offer themselves wholly. The burnt offering represented total surrender. The whole ram would go up to God. The priests were saying, we belong entirely to you. Romans 12 says, present your bodies a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable unto God. This follows Romans 1 to 11, where God's mercies are explained. Forgiveness comes first; then surrender. Notice their hands on the ram again. Personal identification. Today, having been forgiven, give yourself wholly to God. Let your life go up to Him as a burnt offering.
S: Moses brought the ram for the burnt offering, and Aaron and his sons laid their hands on its head. After cleansing came total dedication. Having been forgiven, give yourself wholly to God as a living sacrifice.
P: Forgiven, then surrendered. Leviticus 8:18: 'he brought the ram for the burnt offering.' Cleansing comes first, then total dedication. Present yourself to God today.
