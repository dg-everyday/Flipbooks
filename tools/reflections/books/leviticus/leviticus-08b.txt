# Leviticus 8:19-36: The ram of consecration and the seven days

== 8:19
T: Blood round about
R: 'And he killed it; and Moses sprinkled the blood upon the altar round about.' The ram was killed, and Moses sprinkled its blood on all sides of the altar. The burnt offering began with blood. Notice that dedication also required blood. Even total surrender to God rested on sacrifice. We cannot give ourselves to God on our own merit. Our offering is accepted because of the blood. First Peter 2 says we offer up spiritual sacrifices, acceptable to God by Jesus Christ. Our worship is acceptable only through Christ. Without Him, our best efforts fall short. Notice round about. The blood surrounded the altar. Every side of the priests' dedication was covered. Ephesians 1 says, he hath made us accepted in the beloved. We are accepted not because we are worthy, but because we are in Christ. Today, rest in your acceptance through Christ. Your surrender is welcomed because His blood surrounds it.
S: The ram was killed, and Moses sprinkled its blood on the altar round about. Even dedication to God rested on sacrifice, accepted through blood. Rest in your acceptance through Christ, whose blood makes your surrender welcome.
P: Accepted in the Beloved. Leviticus 8:19: 'Moses sprinkled the blood upon the altar round about.' Your offering is accepted through Christ alone. Rest in that today.

== 8:20
T: The head and the pieces
R: 'And he cut the ram into pieces; and Moses burnt the head, and the pieces, and the fat.' The ram was cut into pieces. Moses burned the head, the pieces, and the fat on the altar. Notice the head. The head represents thought, will, and direction. In the burnt offering, the head was offered to God. The priests' minds were surrendered. Notice the pieces. The whole animal was offered, piece by piece. Every part of life is to be given to God. Not just one area, but all. Second Corinthians 10 speaks of bringing into captivity every thought to the obedience of Christ. Our thinking belongs to Him. Notice the fat. The best still went to God. Total surrender includes our best. Mark 12 says, love the Lord thy God with all thy heart, and with all thy soul, and with all thy mind, and with all thy strength. Today, offer God your mind and every part of your life. Hold nothing back from Him.
S: The ram was cut into pieces, and Moses burnt the head, the pieces and the fat. The priests surrendered their minds and every part of life to God. Offer God your thoughts and every area of your life, holding nothing back.
P: Every piece. Leviticus 8:20: 'Moses burnt the head, and the pieces, and the fat.' Bring every thought captive to Christ. Love God with all your mind today.
H: #DailyGrace #Leviticus820 #Surrender #Mind #WholeHeart #BibleVerse

== 8:21
T: The whole ram
R: 'And he washed the inwards and the legs in water; and Moses burnt the whole ram upon the altar: it was a burnt sacrifice for a sweet savour.' The inner parts and legs were washed, and Moses burned the whole ram on the altar. It was a pleasing aroma, an offering by fire to the LORD. Notice washed. The inward parts and the legs were cleaned before burning. Our inner life and our walk need cleansing. What we desire and where we go must be made clean. Notice the whole ram. Nothing was kept back. The priests' dedication was complete. They gave God everything. Notice sweet savour. God was pleased. Total dedication delights God. It is not a burden to Him but a joy. Ephesians 5 says Christ hath given himself for us an offering and a sacrifice to God for a sweetsmelling savour. Christ's whole self-giving pleased the Father. Today, let your whole life be a sweet savour to God. Give yourself completely, and know that it delights Him.
S: The inwards and legs were washed, and Moses burnt the whole ram on the altar as a sweet savour to the LORD. The priests' dedication was complete, and God was pleased. Give your whole life to God as a sweet savour, knowing it delights Him.
P: A sweet savour. Leviticus 8:21: 'Moses burnt the whole ram upon the altar.' Complete dedication delights God. Give yourself wholly to Him today.

== 8:22
T: The ram of consecration
R: 'And he brought the other ram, the ram of consecration: and Aaron and his sons laid their hands upon the head of the ram.' Moses brought the second ram, the ram of ordination. Aaron and his sons laid their hands on its head. This sacrifice would set them apart for priestly service. Notice the ram of consecration. The Hebrew literally means the ram of filling. The priests' hands would be filled for service. Ordination meant being filled for a task. Notice the three offerings. First the sin offering for cleansing. Then the burnt offering for dedication. Now the consecration offering for service. Cleansed, dedicated, and then commissioned. This is the pattern for all who serve God. Ephesians 2 says, we are his workmanship, created in Christ Jesus unto good works. We are saved, then set apart, then sent. Today, know that God has a work for you. Having been cleansed and dedicated, let Him fill your hands for service.
S: Moses brought the ram of consecration, and Aaron and his sons laid their hands on its head. After cleansing and dedication, the priests were filled for service. Know that God has a work for you and let Him fill your hands for His service.
P: Hands filled for service. Leviticus 8:22: 'the other ram, the ram of consecration.' Cleansed, dedicated, then sent. God has good works prepared for you.
H: #DailyGrace #Leviticus822 #Consecration #Service #Calling #BibleVerse

== 8:23
T: Ear, thumb and toe
R: 'And he slew it; and Moses took of the blood of it, and put it upon the tip of Aaron's right ear, and upon the thumb of his right hand, and upon the great toe of his right foot.' Moses put blood from the ram on Aaron's right earlobe, right thumb, and right big toe. Aaron was marked from head to foot. Notice the ear. Aaron's hearing was consecrated. He must listen to God's voice above all others. Notice the thumb. His work was consecrated. What he did with his hands belonged to God. Notice the toe. His walk was consecrated. Where he went was to be God's way. Ear, hand, and foot covered the whole person: what he heard, what he did, and where he went. Romans 6 says, yield… your members as instruments of righteousness unto God. Every part of us is to be set apart. Today, consecrate your ears, your hands, and your feet. Listen to God, work for Him, and walk in His ways.
S: Moses put the ram's blood on the tip of Aaron's right ear, his right thumb and his right great toe. His hearing, his work and his walk were all consecrated to God. Consecrate your ears, hands and feet to listen to God, work for Him and walk in His ways.
P: Ear, hand, foot. Leviticus 8:23: 'upon the tip of Aaron's right ear, and upon the thumb… and upon the great toe.' Consecrate what you hear, do, and where you go.

== 8:24
T: The sons marked too
R: 'And he brought Aaron's sons, and Moses put of the blood upon the tip of their right ear, and upon the thumbs of their right hands, and upon the great toes of their right feet.' Moses did the same for Aaron's sons. Blood went on their right ears, thumbs, and big toes. Then he sprinkled the rest of the blood around the altar. Notice that the sons received the same marking as their father. The high priest and the ordinary priests were consecrated the same way. Every priest belonged wholly to God. This pictures every believer. First Peter 2 calls all Christians a royal priesthood. Every believer is called to consecrated ears, hands, and feet. Not just pastors or leaders. Everyone. Notice the blood. Consecration came through sacrifice. Hebrews 10 says we are sanctified through the offering of the body of Jesus Christ once for all. Christ's blood sets us apart. Today, remember that you are a priest in God's household. Your listening, working, and walking belong to Him.
S: Moses put the blood on the right ears, thumbs and great toes of Aaron's sons as well. Every priest was consecrated in the same way, picturing the royal priesthood of all believers. Remember that you are a priest in God's household and that your whole life belongs to Him.
P: A royal priesthood. Leviticus 8:24: 'Moses put of the blood upon the tip of their right ear.' Every believer is consecrated. Your whole life belongs to God.

== 8:25
T: The right shoulder
R: 'And he took the fat, and the rump, and all the fat that was upon the inwards, and the caul above the liver, and the two kidneys, and their fat, and the right shoulder.' Moses took the fat portions, the fat tail, the inner organs' fat, the liver covering, the kidneys, and the right thigh from the ram of consecration. Notice the right shoulder. Normally this went to the priest. But at the ordination, it was offered to God. The priests' own portion was given up. At the start of their ministry, they gave back what would normally be theirs. True ministry begins with sacrifice. Jesus said in Mark 10, the Son of man came not to be ministered unto, but to minister, and to give his life a ransom for many. Servants of God begin by giving, not taking. Notice again the inner parts. Their hearts were offered too. Today, begin your service with sacrifice. Give God what you might have kept, and serve rather than seeking to be served.
S: From the ram of consecration Moses took the fat, the inner portions and the right shoulder, which normally went to the priest. At the start of their ministry, the priests gave up their own portion. Begin your service with sacrifice, giving rather than seeking to be served.
P: Ministry starts with giving. Leviticus 8:25: 'and the right shoulder.' The priests gave up their own portion. Serve, don't seek to be served.

== 8:26
T: Bread on the fat
R: 'And out of the basket of unleavened bread, that was before the LORD, he took one unleavened cake, and a cake of oiled bread, and one wafer, and put them on the fat, and upon the right shoulder.' From the basket before the LORD, Moses took an unleavened cake, a cake with oil, and a wafer. He placed them on the fat portions and the right thigh. Notice the bread joined the meat. The ordination combined sacrifice and bread. Life and labor were offered together. Bread represents daily sustenance, the fruit of work. The priests' daily lives were included in their consecration. Notice the variety: plain cake, oiled cake, and wafer. Ordinary and anointed bread together. Our common work and our Spirit-filled service both belong to God. John 6 records Jesus saying, I am the bread of life. Christ sustains our service. Notice before the LORD. The basket stood in God's presence. Today, bring your daily work to God along with your worship. Let Him sanctify the ordinary and the spiritual alike.
S: Moses took an unleavened cake, an oiled cake and a wafer from the basket before the LORD and placed them on the fat and right shoulder. Daily bread joined the sacrifice, bringing ordinary life into the consecration. Bring your daily work to God with your worship and let Him sanctify both.
P: Daily bread consecrated. Leviticus 8:26: 'out of the basket of unleavened bread, that was before the LORD.' Bring your ordinary work to God. Christ is the bread of life.

== 8:27
T: Filled hands
R: 'And he put all upon Aaron's hands, and upon his sons' hands, and waved them for a wave offering before the LORD.' Moses placed everything in the hands of Aaron and his sons. They waved it before the LORD as a wave offering. Notice their hands were filled. This is the meaning of consecration: filling the hands. The priests' hands now held what belonged to God. Their hands were no longer empty or their own. Notice they waved it before the LORD. They presented it to God. What fills our hands should be offered to Him. Our skills, resources, and opportunities are placed in our hands to be lifted to God. First Peter 4 says, As every man hath received the gift, even so minister the same one to another, as good stewards of the manifold grace of God. Today, look at what God has placed in your hands. Lift it up to Him, and use it for His service.
S: Moses put everything on the hands of Aaron and his sons, and they waved it before the LORD. Their hands were filled with what belonged to God and lifted to Him. Look at what God has placed in your hands and lift it to Him for His service.
P: Filled hands. Leviticus 8:27: 'he put all upon Aaron's hands… and waved them for a wave offering before the LORD.' What's in your hands? Lift it to God today.

== 8:28
T: Consecrations for a sweet savour
R: 'And Moses took them from off their hands, and burnt them on the altar upon the burnt offering: they were consecrations for a sweet savour: it is an offering made by fire unto the LORD.' Moses took the offerings from their hands and burned them on the altar on top of the burnt offering. They were ordination offerings, a pleasing aroma to the LORD. Notice that Moses took them from their hands. What they held was given to God. The priests did not keep what filled their hands. They let it go to God. Consecration means letting go. We hold God's gifts loosely. Notice upon the burnt offering. The ordination offering rested on the burnt offering of total dedication. Service rests on surrender. Notice sweet savour. God was pleased with their consecration. Second Corinthians 2 says, we are unto God a sweet savour of Christ. A consecrated life pleases God and spreads the fragrance of Christ. Today, release what God has given you back to Him. Let your consecrated life be a sweet savour.
S: Moses took the offerings from their hands and burnt them on the altar upon the burnt offering as consecrations for a sweet savour. The priests let go of what filled their hands, and their service rested on surrender. Release God's gifts back to Him and let your consecrated life be a sweet savour.
P: Letting go. Leviticus 8:28: 'Moses took them from off their hands, and burnt them on the altar.' Hold God's gifts loosely. Be a sweet savour of Christ today.

== 8:29
T: Moses' part
R: 'And Moses took the breast, and waved it for a wave offering before the LORD: for of the ram of consecration it was Moses' part; as the LORD commanded Moses.' Moses took the breast and waved it before the LORD. From the ram of ordination, this was Moses' portion, as God had commanded. Notice Moses' part. Moses served as priest for this ceremony, so he received the priest's share. God provided for the one who did the work. Even Moses, the great leader, received a portion from God's hand. Notice that Moses did not take more than his share. He received exactly what God assigned. Faithful servants do not grab. They receive what God gives. First Corinthians 4 says, it is required in stewards, that a man be found faithful. Notice as the LORD commanded Moses, once again. Moses' obedience continued even in receiving. Today, receive your portion from God with gratitude. Do not grasp for more. Be faithful with what He gives.
S: Moses took the breast and waved it before the LORD, for it was his part of the ram of consecration. He received exactly what God assigned, no more. Receive your portion from God with gratitude and be faithful with what He gives.
P: Content with God's portion. Leviticus 8:29: 'of the ram of consecration it was Moses' part.' Don't grasp for more. Be a faithful steward of what God gives.

== 8:30
T: Oil and blood together
R: 'And Moses took of the anointing oil, and of the blood which was upon the altar, and sprinkled it upon Aaron, and upon his garments, and upon his sons, and upon his sons' garments with him.' Moses took anointing oil and blood from the altar and sprinkled them on Aaron and his garments, and on his sons and their garments. This set them all apart as holy. Notice oil and blood together. The blood speaks of atonement. The oil speaks of the Spirit. Both were needed for holy service. Christ's blood cleanses us; the Spirit empowers us. We need both. Without the blood, we have no standing. Without the Spirit, we have no power. Acts 1 says, ye shall receive power, after that the Holy Ghost is come upon you. Notice the garments too. Not only the men but their clothing was sprinkled. Their whole appearance was marked as holy. Today, rejoice in both the blood and the Spirit. Christ's blood cleanses you, and His Spirit empowers you to serve.
S: Moses sprinkled anointing oil and blood from the altar on Aaron, his sons and their garments, sanctifying them. Blood for atonement and oil for the Spirit were both needed for holy service. Rejoice that Christ's blood cleanses you and His Spirit empowers you to serve.
P: Blood and oil. Leviticus 8:30: 'Moses took of the anointing oil, and of the blood… and sprinkled it upon Aaron.' Christ's blood cleanses; His Spirit empowers. You need both.

Moses told Aaron and his sons to boil the meat at the entrance of the tabernacle and eat it there with the bread from the ordination basket. Notice eat it. The priests shared a meal from the consecration offering. Ordination ended in fellowship. They ate in God's presence. Notice that the consecration became nourishment. What was offered to God now fed them. When we give ourselves to God, He sustains us. John 4 records Jesus saying, My meat is to do the will of him that sent me. Doing God's will feeds the soul. Notice at the door of the tabernacle. They ate at the threshold of God's house. Psalm 84 says, I had rather be a doorkeeper in the house of my God. Today, let your service to God nourish you. Find your strength in doing His will.

R: 'And that which remaineth of the flesh and of the bread shall ye burn with fire.' Whatever meat or bread was left over had to be burned. Nothing from the consecration meal could be kept. Notice nothing was kept. The ordination meal was holy. Leftovers could not be taken home as ordinary food. What was set apart for God stayed set apart. This guarded the sacredness of the moment. Holy experiences are not to be cheapened or treated as common. When God meets us in a special way, we treasure it. Luke 2 says Mary kept all these things, and pondered them in her heart. Some moments with God are meant to be cherished, not paraded. Notice burn with fire. What remained was given back to God rather than wasted. Nothing holy was treated carelessly. Today, guard the holy moments God gives you. Treasure them in your heart, and keep them sacred.

== 8:33
T: Seven days
R: 'And ye shall not go out of the door of the tabernacle of the congregation in seven days, until the days of your consecration be at an end: for seven days shall he consecrate you.' The priests had to stay at the tabernacle entrance for seven days until their ordination was complete. The process took a full week. Notice seven days. Consecration was not instant. It took time. The priests had to wait at God's door. They could not rush into ministry. Preparation for service takes patience. Moses spent forty years in Midian before leading Israel. Paul spent years in Arabia and Tarsus before his missionary journeys. Galatians 1 records his time away. God prepares His servants over time. Notice ye shall not go out. The priests stayed put. Waiting in God's presence was part of their preparation. Isaiah 40 says, they that wait upon the LORD shall renew their strength. Today, be patient in God's preparation. Wait at His door. He is making you ready.
S: The priests were not to go out of the tabernacle door for seven days until their consecration was complete. Preparation for service took time and waiting in God's presence. Be patient in God's preparation and wait at His door, for He is making you ready.

== 8:34
T: As he hath done this day
R: 'As he hath done this day, so the LORD hath commanded to do, to make an atonement for you.' Moses explained that what had been done that day would be repeated each day, as God commanded, to make atonement for the priests. Notice to make an atonement for you. The seven days of ordination involved daily sacrifices for the priests. Their own atonement was repeated over and over. This shows how much cleansing the priests needed before serving. Day after day, sacrifice was offered for them. The repetition highlights the limits of animal sacrifice. Hebrews 10 says those yearly sacrifices could never make the comers thereunto perfect. They had to be repeated because they could not finish the work. Christ's sacrifice is different. Hebrews 10 says, by one offering he hath perfected for ever them that are sanctified. Today, rejoice that your atonement does not need repeating. Christ's single sacrifice has perfected you forever.
S: Moses explained that what was done that day would be repeated, as the LORD commanded, to make atonement for the priests. The daily repetition showed the limits of animal sacrifice. Rejoice that Christ's one offering has perfected you forever and needs no repeating.
P: Once for all. Leviticus 8:34: 'As he hath done this day, so the LORD hath commanded to do, to make an atonement for you.' Christ's one sacrifice perfected you forever.

== 8:35
T: Keep the charge
R: 'Therefore shall ye abide at the door of the tabernacle of the congregation day and night seven days, and keep the charge of the LORD, that ye die not: for so I am commanded.' The priests had to stay at the tabernacle entrance day and night for seven days, keeping the LORD's charge, so that they would not die. Notice keep the charge of the LORD. The priests had a duty to guard. They watched over God's dwelling and obeyed His instructions. Holy service came with serious responsibility. Notice that ye die not. Disobedience in holy matters was dangerous. Chapter 10 will show this tragically with Nadab and Abihu. God's holiness is not to be taken lightly. Notice day and night. Their watch was constant. First Timothy 6 says, keep that which is committed to thy trust. Faithful service requires constant vigilance. Today, keep the charge God has given you. Be faithful day and night in what He has entrusted to you.
S: The priests abode at the door of the tabernacle day and night for seven days, keeping the charge of the LORD so that they would not die. Holy service carried serious responsibility and constant vigilance. Keep faithfully the charge God has given you, day and night.
P: Keep the charge. Leviticus 8:35: 'keep the charge of the LORD, that ye die not.' Holy service demands faithfulness. Guard what God has entrusted to you.

== 8:36
T: All things commanded
R: 'So Aaron and his sons did all things which the LORD commanded by the hand of Moses.' The chapter ends with a simple statement. Aaron and his sons did everything the LORD commanded through Moses. Their ordination was complete. Notice all things. Not some, not most, but all. Their obedience was complete. They did not pick and choose. This is the goal of every servant of God: full obedience. Jesus said in John 15, Ye are my friends, if ye do whatsoever I command you. Notice by the hand of Moses. God's commands came through His appointed messenger. The priests listened to God's word as delivered. We receive God's commands through Scripture, the word given by His messengers. Second Peter 1 says holy men of God spake as they were moved by the Holy Ghost. Sadly, chapter 10 will show that this full obedience did not last for all of them. Faithfulness must continue. Today, aim for full obedience. Do all that God commands, and keep doing it.
S: Aaron and his sons did all things which the LORD commanded by the hand of Moses. Their obedience was complete, though chapter 10 shows it must continue. Aim for full obedience to all God commands, and keep going in it.
P: All things. Leviticus 8:36: 'Aaron and his sons did all things which the LORD commanded.' Not some, but all. Aim for full obedience, and keep at it today.
