# Leviticus 9: Aaron's first offerings and the glory of the LORD

== 9:1
T: The eighth day
R: 'And it came to pass on the eighth day, that Moses called Aaron and his sons, and the elders of Israel.' After the seven days of ordination, on the eighth day, Moses called Aaron, his sons, and the elders. The priests' ministry was about to begin. Notice the eighth day. Seven days completed the preparation. The eighth day began something new. In Scripture, the eighth day often marks new beginnings. Circumcision happened on the eighth day. Jesus rose on the first day of the week, the day after the seventh, a new beginning. Notice that the waiting ended. The priests had waited a full week at God's door. Now the time to serve had come. God's preparation leads to His purpose. Ecclesiastes 3 says, To every thing there is a season. There is a time to wait and a time to act. Today, trust that your season of preparation will give way to a season of service. God's eighth day will come.
S: On the eighth day, after seven days of ordination, Moses called Aaron, his sons and the elders. The waiting ended and a new beginning of ministry came. Trust that your season of preparation will give way to a season of service.
P: A new beginning. Leviticus 9:1: 'And it came to pass on the eighth day.' Preparation leads to purpose. God's eighth day will come for you.

== 9:2
T: First for himself
R: 'And he said unto Aaron, Take thee a young calf for a sin offering, and a ram for a burnt offering, without blemish, and offer them before the LORD.' Moses told Aaron to take a young calf for a sin offering and a ram for a burnt offering, both without defect, and offer them before the LORD. Notice a young calf. Some Jewish teachers have seen a reminder here of the golden calf Aaron had made in Exodus 32. Whatever the reason for the choice, Aaron's first act as high priest was to deal with his own sin. Aaron had failed badly at Sinai. Yet God did not reject him. He gave him a sin offering and a ministry. God restores those who fail. Peter denied Christ three times. Yet Jesus restored him in John 21: Feed my sheep. Failure is not final when grace is received. Today, if you have failed, bring your sin to God. He can restore you and still use you.
S: Moses told Aaron to take a young calf for a sin offering and a ram for a burnt offering, both without blemish. Aaron, who had failed at Sinai, began his ministry by dealing with his own sin, and God still used him. If you have failed, bring your sin to God, who restores and still uses His servants.
P: Restored to serve. Leviticus 9:2: 'Take thee a young calf for a sin offering.' Aaron failed, yet God gave him ministry. Failure isn't final when grace is received.

Now they brought a calf as an offering to the true God. What had been an idol became a sacrifice. Repentance turns away from idols toward God. First Thessalonians 1 says, ye turned to God from idols to serve the living and true God. Notice of the first year. Young, healthy animals were brought. The best was given. Today, turn from any idol in your heart. Bring your best to the living and true God.

Second Corinthians 4 says God's glory shines in the face of Jesus Christ. Today, obey what God has said. Walk in His ways, and watch for His glory to be revealed.

== 9:24
T: Fire from before the LORD
R: 'And there came a fire out from before the LORD, and consumed upon the altar the burnt offering and the fat: which when all the people saw, they shouted, and fell on their faces.' Fire came out from the LORD's presence and consumed the burnt offering and the fat on the altar. When the people saw it, they shouted and fell face down. Notice a fire out from before the LORD. God Himself lit the altar fire. He accepted the sacrifice visibly. This fire was to be kept burning continually, as chapter 6 commanded. Notice they shouted, and fell on their faces. Joy and reverence came together. They shouted with joy and bowed in awe. True worship holds both. First Kings 18 shows fire falling at Elijah's prayer, and the people fell on their faces: The LORD, he is the God. Acts 2 shows tongues of fire at Pentecost. God's fire marks His acceptance and presence. Today, worship God with joy and awe. Shout His praise, and bow before His holiness.
