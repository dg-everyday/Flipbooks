# Leviticus 10: Nadab and Abihu, and the priests' duties

== 10:1
T: Strange fire
R: 'And Nadab and Abihu, the sons of Aaron, took either of them his censer, and put fire therein, and put incense thereon, and offered strange fire before the LORD, which he commanded them not.' On the very day of glory, Aaron's sons Nadab and Abihu took their censers, put fire and incense in them, and offered unauthorized fire before the LORD. God had not commanded it. Notice which he commanded them not. Their sin was not ignorance. It was presumption. They did their own thing in God's holy presence. They treated worship as theirs to design. Notice the timing. Right after God's fire fell in glory. Spiritual highs can be dangerous moments. Pride and carelessness can follow great blessing. Notice they were the sons of Aaron. They had seen God on Sinai in Exodus 24. Privilege did not protect them. Proverbs 16 says, Pride goeth before destruction. Today, approach God humbly, on His terms. Do not treat holy things as your own to rearrange.
S: Nadab and Abihu offered strange fire before the LORD, which He had not commanded. On the day of glory, they presumed to worship on their own terms. Approach God humbly and on His terms, never treating holy things as yours to rearrange.
P: Strange fire. Leviticus 10:1: 'offered strange fire before the LORD, which he commanded them not.' Worship is not ours to redesign. Approach God humbly, on His terms.

== 10:2
T: They died before the LORD
R: 'And there went out fire from the LORD, and devoured them, and they died before the LORD.' Fire came from the LORD and consumed Nadab and Abihu. They died in God's presence. This is one of the most sobering moments in Scripture. Notice fire from the LORD. The same fire that had accepted the sacrifice now judged the priests. God's holiness both blesses and consumes. Hebrews 12 says, our God is a consuming fire. The fire of His presence is life to the humble and danger to the presumptuous. This was a new beginning, like Ananias and Sapphira in Acts 5 at the church's start. God marked from the outset that His holiness is real. We should not rush past this passage, nor become harsh. It grieves us. Yet it teaches us that grace never makes God casual. Today, approach God with reverence. Thank Christ, through whom we can draw near to a holy God without being consumed.
S: Fire went out from the LORD and devoured Nadab and Abihu, and they died before the LORD. The same fire that accepted the sacrifice judged their presumption, showing that God's holiness is real. Approach God with reverence and thank Christ, through whom we can draw near safely.
P: A consuming fire. Leviticus 10:2: 'there went out fire from the LORD, and devoured them.' God's holiness is real. Thank Christ that you can draw near through Him.

== 10:3
T: Aaron held his peace
R: 'Then Moses said unto Aaron, This is it that the LORD spake, saying, I will be sanctified in them that come nigh me, and before all the people I will be glorified. And Aaron held his peace.' Moses explained God's word: those who come near Him must treat Him as holy, and He will be glorified before all. Aaron was silent. Notice I will be sanctified in them that come nigh me. Closeness to God brings greater responsibility. Those who serve nearest must honor Him most carefully. Notice and Aaron held his peace. Aaron had just lost two sons. Yet he did not argue with God. His silence was not indifference. It was grief bowing before God's holiness. Job 1 records Job saying, the LORD gave, and the LORD hath taken away. Psalm 46 says, Be still, and know that I am God. Aaron's silence shows deep faith under unbearable sorrow. Today, if you face grief you cannot explain, it is all right to be silent before God. He sees your sorrow.
S: Moses told Aaron that God would be sanctified in those who come near Him and glorified before all, and Aaron held his peace. Aaron's silence was grief bowing before God's holiness. If you face grief you cannot explain, be still before God, who sees your sorrow.
P: Silent before God. Leviticus 10:3: 'And Aaron held his peace.' In unbearable grief, Aaron bowed in silence. Be still, and know that He is God.

== 10:4
T: Carry your brethren
R: 'And Moses called Mishael and Elzaphan, the sons of Uzziel the uncle of Aaron, and said unto them, Come near, carry your brethren from before the sanctuary out of the camp.' Moses called Mishael and Elzaphan, Aaron's cousins, and told them to carry the bodies of Nadab and Abihu away from the sanctuary and outside the camp. Notice that family members carried the dead. The cousins did the hard and painful work. Grief requires practical help. Someone must carry the burden. Galatians 6 says, Bear ye one another's burdens. In times of loss, practical care is a gift. Notice out of the camp. The bodies were removed from the holy area. Death and the sanctuary could not mix. Notice that Aaron and his remaining sons could not carry their own family members. Others had to do it for them. Sometimes we cannot help ourselves, and others must step in. Today, be ready to carry burdens for those who are grieving. Offer practical help when others cannot help themselves.
S: Moses called Aaron's cousins Mishael and Elzaphan to carry the bodies of Nadab and Abihu out of the camp. Others did the painful work that the grieving priests could not do. Be ready to carry burdens for those who are grieving and offer practical help.
P: Carry one another. Leviticus 10:4: 'Come near, carry your brethren from before the sanctuary.' In grief, practical help is a gift. Bear one another's burdens today.

== 10:5
T: In their coats
R: 'So they went near, and carried them in their coats out of the camp; as Moses had said.' Mishael and Elzaphan came and carried the bodies, still in their priestly tunics, outside the camp, as Moses had instructed. Notice in their coats. The two men died wearing their priestly garments. They had been clothed for holy service only days before. Now they were carried out in those same clothes. Outward signs of calling do not guarantee faithfulness. Matthew 7 warns that many will say, Lord, Lord, have we not prophesied in thy name… And then will I profess unto them, I never knew you. Garments are not the same as a heart that obeys. Yet notice the dignity. They were carried, not dragged. Their bodies were handled with care. Even in judgment, there was respect. Today, examine whether your faith is more than outward garments. Let your inner obedience match your outward calling.
S: Mishael and Elzaphan carried Nadab and Abihu out of the camp still in their priestly coats. Outward garments of calling did not guarantee a faithful heart, yet the bodies were handled with dignity. Examine whether your faith is more than outward signs and let your obedience match your calling.
P: More than garments. Leviticus 10:5: 'carried them in their coats out of the camp.' Outward calling is not enough. Let your heart obey as your garments profess.

== 10:6
T: Let the people bewail
R: 'And Moses said unto Aaron… Uncover not your heads, neither rend your clothes; lest ye die… but let your brethren, the whole house of Israel, bewail the burning which the LORD hath kindled.' Moses told Aaron and his remaining sons not to show the usual signs of mourning. But the whole nation could mourn the loss. Notice that the priests could not mourn publicly. They were anointed and on duty. Their service had to continue. This was painfully hard. Yet the people could grieve for them. Notice let your brethren… bewail. The community carried the grief that the priests could not express. When leaders must keep serving through loss, the community can mourn on their behalf. Romans 12 says, weep with them that weep. Notice that grief was not forbidden. It was redirected. God did not despise sorrow. Today, weep with those who weep. When others must carry on through loss, share their grief and pray for them.
S: Moses told Aaron and his sons not to show mourning while on duty, but let the whole house of Israel bewail the loss. The community carried the grief that the serving priests could not express. Weep with those who weep and share the grief of those who must keep serving through loss.
P: Weep with them. Leviticus 10:6: 'let your brethren, the whole house of Israel, bewail the burning.' Grieve with those who must carry on through loss. Pray for them today.

== 10:7
T: The anointing oil is upon you
R: 'And ye shall not go out from the door of the tabernacle of the congregation, lest ye die: for the anointing oil of the LORD is upon you. And they did according to the word of Moses.' Aaron and his sons were not to leave the tabernacle entrance, because the LORD's anointing oil was on them. They obeyed Moses' word. Notice for the anointing oil of the LORD is upon you. Their calling came before their personal grief. The anointing set them apart. They belonged to God's service in a special way. This was costly. Jesus spoke of costly discipleship in Luke 9: Let the dead bury their dead: but go thou and preach the kingdom of God. Following God can require painful sacrifices. Notice they did according to the word of Moses. In their sorrow, they obeyed. This is remarkable faith. Grief did not turn into rebellion. Today, if obedience is costly right now, trust God with your pain. Keep faithful, even through tears.
S: Aaron and his sons were not to leave the tabernacle, for the anointing oil of the LORD was upon them, and they obeyed. Their calling came before personal grief, and they stayed faithful through sorrow. If obedience is costly now, trust God with your pain and keep faithful through tears.
P: Faithful through tears. Leviticus 10:7: 'for the anointing oil of the LORD is upon you. And they did according to the word of Moses.' Keep faithful in your sorrow.

== 10:8
T: God spoke to Aaron
R: 'And the LORD spake unto Aaron, saying.' After the tragedy, God spoke directly to Aaron. Usually God spoke through Moses. This time He addressed Aaron himself. Notice that God spoke to Aaron personally. In his grief, Aaron was not left alone. God came to him with His word. This is tender. After judgment came personal instruction. God did not abandon the grieving father. Psalm 34 says, The LORD is nigh unto them that are of a broken heart. Notice the rarity. Leviticus mostly records God speaking to Moses. Here, God honored Aaron with a direct word. He affirmed his priesthood after the loss of his sons. Aaron's calling was not cancelled. Isaiah 43 says, When thou passest through the waters, I will be with thee. Today, know that God speaks to you in your grief. He has not abandoned you. His word comes close to the brokenhearted.
S: After the tragedy, the LORD spoke directly to Aaron, which was rare in Leviticus. God came near to the grieving father and affirmed his calling. Know that God draws near to you in grief and has not abandoned you.
P: Near to the brokenhearted. Leviticus 10:8: 'And the LORD spake unto Aaron, saying.' God spoke personally to the grieving father. He draws near to you too.

== 10:9
T: No wine or strong drink
R: 'Do not drink wine nor strong drink, thou, nor thy sons with thee, when ye go into the tabernacle of the congregation, lest ye die: it shall be a statute for ever throughout your generations.' God told Aaron that he and his sons must not drink wine or strong drink when they went into the tabernacle, so that they would not die. Notice the placement. This command came right after Nadab and Abihu's death. Many have wondered whether drink clouded their judgment. Scripture does not say so directly, but the connection is suggestive. Clear minds are needed in holy service. Proverbs 31 says, it is not for kings to drink wine… Lest they drink, and forget the law. Anything that dulls our judgment is dangerous in God's service. Ephesians 5 says, be not drunk with wine… but be filled with the Spirit. Notice a statute for ever. This was a lasting rule. Today, keep your mind clear for God. Avoid what dulls your judgment, and be filled with the Spirit instead.
S: God told Aaron that he and his sons must not drink wine or strong drink when entering the tabernacle, lest they die. Holy service required clear minds and sound judgment. Keep your mind clear for God, avoiding what dulls your judgment, and be filled with the Spirit.
P: A clear mind. Leviticus 10:9: 'Do not drink wine nor strong drink… when ye go into the tabernacle.' Be not drunk with wine, but filled with the Spirit.

== 10:10

R: 'And that ye may put difference between holy and unholy, and between unclean and clean.' God explained the purpose. The priests needed clear minds to distinguish between holy and common, and between unclean and clean. Notice put difference. The priests' job was to discern. They had to know what was holy and what was common. Confusing the two had just proven deadly. This is still needed. Hebrews 5 speaks of those who by reason of use have their senses exercised to discern both good and evil. Spiritual discernment is a skill developed over time. Notice that holiness requires distinction. Not everything is the same. Some things are set apart for God. Our culture blurs these lines. Ezekiel 22 rebuked priests who put no difference between the holy and profane. Today, ask God for discernment. Learn to distinguish holy from common, and help others see the difference.
S: The priests needed clear minds to put difference between holy and unholy, unclean and clean. Discernment was essential, for confusing the two had just proven deadly. Ask God for discernment to distinguish holy from common and help others see the difference.

They explained God's word to the people. Malachi 2 says, the priest's lips should keep knowledge, and they should seek the law at his mouth. A clear mind was needed for teaching too. Notice all the statutes. They were to teach the whole counsel of God, not just favorite parts. Acts 20 records Paul saying, I have not shunned to declare unto you all the counsel of God. Notice that God's word came by the hand of Moses. The priests did not make up their teaching. They passed on God's word. Today, learn God's word well enough to share it. Teach what He has said, faithfully and fully.
