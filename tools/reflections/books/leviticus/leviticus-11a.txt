# Leviticus 11:1-23: Clean and unclean animals

== 11:1
T: To Moses and to Aaron
R: 'And the LORD spake unto Moses and to Aaron, saying unto them.' A new section of Leviticus begins. God spoke to both Moses and Aaron about what was clean and unclean. Notice and to Aaron. After chapter 10, Aaron was included. God had just told him that priests must put difference between clean and unclean. Now God gave him the details. Teaching required knowledge. Notice the shift. The sanctuary was set up and the priests were serving. Now God turned to daily life: food, health, and home. Holiness would reach every corner of Israel's life. These laws are often puzzling to modern readers. Jesus later declared all foods clean, as Mark 7 explains. Yet these chapters still teach us about God's holiness and His care for His people's whole life. Romans 15 says, whatsoever things were written aforetime were written for our learning. Today, read even the puzzling parts of Scripture patiently. God has something to teach you there.
S: The LORD spoke to Moses and Aaron, beginning the laws of clean and unclean. After the sanctuary was established, holiness reached into daily life, food and home. Read even the puzzling parts of Scripture patiently, for they were written for our learning.
P: Written for our learning. Leviticus 11:1: 'And the LORD spake unto Moses and to Aaron.' Even puzzling passages teach us. Read Scripture patiently today.

== 11:2
T: These ye shall eat
R: 'Speak unto the children of Israel, saying, These are the beasts which ye shall eat among all the beasts that are on the earth.' God told Israel which land animals they could eat. Their diet would mark them as His people. Notice these… ye shall eat. God began with permission, not prohibition. He showed them what they could enjoy. God's commands come within a world of His generous provision. Genesis 1 shows God giving food to humanity. Genesis 9 gives Noah every moving thing for food. God is a generous provider. Notice that food became a sign of identity. Every meal reminded Israel that they belonged to God. In Christ, believers are not bound by these food laws. Acts 10 shows Peter's vision: What God hath cleansed, that call not thou common. Yet we still honor God at the table. First Timothy 4 says food is received with thanksgiving. Today, receive your food as a gift from God. Let every meal remind you whose you are.
S: God told Israel which beasts they could eat among all the beasts of the earth. Their diet became a daily sign that they belonged to Him. Receive your food with thanksgiving and let every meal remind you whose you are.
P: Received with thanksgiving. Leviticus 11:2: 'These are the beasts which ye shall eat.' God is a generous provider. Let every meal remind you that you belong to Him.

== 11:3
T: Parteth the hoof
R: 'Whatsoever parteth the hoof, and is clovenfooted, and cheweth the cud, among the beasts, that shall ye eat.' Land animals with fully split hooves that also chewed the cud could be eaten. Cattle, sheep, and goats met these standards. Notice the two marks. Both had to be present. One without the other was not enough. Some teachers have drawn a lesson from this. Chewing the cud pictures meditation, going over God's word again and again. The divided hoof pictures a careful walk. Inner reflection and outward conduct belong together. Whatever the original purpose, the lesson fits Scripture. Psalm 1 says the blessed man meditates in God's law day and night. Ephesians 5 says, walk circumspectly. A healthy faith both ponders and walks. Hearing without doing, or doing without understanding, falls short. Today, chew on God's word, and let it shape how you walk. Meditation and obedience belong together.
S: Land animals that parted the hoof, were clovenfooted and chewed the cud could be eaten. Both marks had to be present, a picture that teachers have linked to meditation and careful walking. Meditate on God's word and let it shape how you walk today.
P: Meditate and walk. Leviticus 11:3: 'Whatsoever parteth the hoof… and cheweth the cud.' Ponder God's word day and night, and walk circumspectly in every step today.
H: #DailyGrace #Leviticus113 #Meditation #Walk #Obedience #BibleVerse

== 11:4
T: The camel
R: 'Nevertheless these shall ye not eat of them that chew the cud, or of them that divide the hoof: as the camel, because he cheweth the cud, but divideth not the hoof; he is unclean unto you.' The camel chewed the cud but did not have a fully divided hoof, so it was unclean for Israel. Notice nevertheless. Partial conformity was not enough. The camel had one sign but not the other. It looked close, but it did not qualify. This reminds us that half-hearted faith falls short. Some people have outward religion but no inward change. Others claim inner faith but live carelessly. Matthew 15 records Jesus quoting Isaiah: This people… honoureth me with their lips; but their heart is far from me. God wants the whole person. Notice unclean unto you. It was unclean for Israel specifically. These laws were part of Israel's covenant identity, not a statement that camels were evil. Today, ask God to make your faith whole. Let your heart and your life both belong to Him.
S: The camel chewed the cud but did not divide the hoof, so it was unclean for Israel. Having one sign without the other was not enough, picturing half-hearted faith. Ask God to make your faith whole, with heart and life both belonging to Him.
P: Not halfway. Leviticus 11:4: 'the camel, because he cheweth the cud, but divideth not the hoof.' Partial conformity falls short. Give God your whole life today.

== 11:5
T: The coney
R: 'And the coney, because he cheweth the cud, but divideth not the hoof; he is unclean unto you.' The coney, often identified as the rock badger or hyrax, appeared to chew the cud but did not divide the hoof. It was unclean for Israel. Notice the coney. Proverbs 30 praises this small creature: The conies are but a feeble folk, yet make they their houses in the rocks. It was unclean for food, yet Scripture admires its wisdom. Unclean did not mean worthless. These laws set boundaries for Israel's table, not for God's love of His creatures. Psalm 104 says, the rocks for the conies. God made a home for them. Psalm 145 says, his tender mercies are over all his works. Notice the lesson of Proverbs. A feeble creature finds safety in the rock. We too are weak, and we find refuge in God, our Rock. Today, take shelter in the Rock. Like the coney, find your safety in God.
S: The coney chewed the cud but did not divide the hoof, so it was unclean for Israel. Yet Proverbs praises the feeble conies for making their homes in the rocks. Like the coney, take shelter in God, your Rock.

== 11:7
T: The swine
R: 'And the swine, though he divide the hoof, and be clovenfooted, yet he cheweth not the cud; he is unclean to you.' The pig had a divided hoof but did not chew the cud, so it was unclean for Israel. Notice the reverse of the camel. The pig had the outward sign but lacked the inner one. Its feet looked right, but it did not chew the cud. Outward appearance without inward reality falls short. Matthew 23 records Jesus telling the Pharisees they were like whited sepulchres, beautiful outward, but full of uncleanness within. God sees the inside. The pig became a symbol of uncleanness among Jews. In Luke 15, the prodigal son fed pigs, showing how far he had fallen. Yet the father ran to meet him. No one falls too low for the Father's embrace. Today, let your inner life match your outward conduct. And if you have fallen far, come home. The Father is running to meet you.
S: The swine divided the hoof but did not chew the cud, so it was unclean for Israel. It had the outward sign without the inner one, and the prodigal son feeding swine shows how far he fell. Let your inner life match your outward conduct, and if you have fallen, come home to the Father.
P: Inside and out. Leviticus 11:7: 'the swine, though he divide the hoof… yet he cheweth not the cud.' Let your heart match your walk. The Father welcomes the prodigal home.

== 11:8
T: Not touch
R: 'Of their flesh shall ye not eat, and their carcase shall ye not touch; they are unclean to you.' Israel was not to eat the meat of these animals or touch their dead bodies. They were unclean. Notice not touch. Contact with the carcass made a person ritually unclean. Death was connected to uncleanness throughout these laws. God is the God of life. Contact with death needed cleansing before coming near Him. Second Corinthians 6 quotes these ideas: Come out from among them, and be ye separate… and touch not the unclean thing; and I will receive you. Paul applied it to moral separation from sin and idolatry. Believers are not under the food laws, but the call to separation remains. We avoid what defiles the heart. Notice that the separation leads to welcome. God says, I will receive you. Holiness is not rejection but invitation into God's family. Today, step away from what defiles you. God receives those who separate themselves for Him.
S: Israel was not to eat the flesh of unclean animals or touch their carcases. Paul applies this call to separation from sin, followed by God's promise to receive His people. Step away from what defiles you, for God receives those who separate themselves for Him.
P: Touch not the unclean thing. Leviticus 11:8: 'their carcase shall ye not touch.' Come out and be separate, and God will receive you. Step away from what defiles.

== 11:9
T: Fins and scales
R: 'These shall ye eat of all that are in the waters: whatsoever hath fins and scales in the waters, in the seas, and in the rivers, them shall ye eat.' Israel could eat any water creature with both fins and scales, whether from seas or rivers. Notice again permission comes first. God gave Israel the fish of seas and rivers. A wide range was allowed. God's boundaries sit inside a large space of freedom. Notice fins and scales. Fins give direction and movement. Scales give protection. Some have seen a picture of a believer who moves against the current and is protected from the world. Whether or not that was the original intent, the image fits. Romans 12 says, be not conformed to this world. Fish with fins can swim upstream. Believers can resist the flow of culture. Ephesians 6 tells us to put on the whole armour of God. God gives us protection. Today, swim against the current when the world pulls you away from God. Rely on His armour to protect you.
S: Israel could eat whatever had fins and scales in the seas and rivers. God gave wide freedom, and fins and scales picture moving against the current with protection. Resist the world's pull and rely on God's armour to protect you.
P: Against the current. Leviticus 11:9: 'whatsoever hath fins and scales… them shall ye eat.' Be not conformed to this world. Swim upstream in God's strength today.

== 11:10
T: An abomination unto you
R: 'And all that have not fins and scales in the seas, and in the rivers… they shall be an abomination unto you.' Water creatures without fins and scales, such as shellfish and eels, were to be detestable to Israel. Notice unto you. The word abomination here refers to what was ritually detestable for Israel. It did not mean the creatures were evil. God made them and called them good in Genesis 1. The issue was Israel's covenant boundaries. This helps us read carefully. The same word can mean different things in different contexts. Not every abomination in Leviticus is a moral evil. Some were ritual boundaries for Israel's time. Second Timothy 2 says, rightly dividing the word of truth. Careful reading honors God. Notice that Israel was taught to feel strongly about God's boundaries. Holiness shaped their instincts. Today, read Scripture carefully and rightly divide it. Let God's word shape not only your thinking but also your instincts.
S: Water creatures without fins and scales were an abomination unto Israel. The word here marked ritual boundaries for Israel, not moral evil in God's creatures. Read Scripture carefully, rightly dividing it, and let God's word shape your instincts.
P: Rightly dividing. Leviticus 11:10: 'they shall be an abomination unto you.' Read every passage carefully, in its context. Let God's word shape your instincts today.

Not just avoid sin, but despise it. Hebrews 1 says of Christ, Thou hast loved righteousness, and hated iniquity. Today, ask God to train your affections. Micah 6 sums up God's will: do justly, love mercy, walk humbly. Jesus summed up the law in two commands. Simplicity helps obedience. Notice that this was the third statement in a row about water creatures. God was patient in teaching. Isaiah 28 describes His way: line upon line; here a little, and there a little. God repeats Himself because He wants us to learn. Today, hold on to the simple truths God has given. When life is complicated, His clear word still guides you.

== 11:13
T: The eagle
R: 'And these are they which ye shall have in abomination among the fowls; they shall not be eaten, they are an abomination: the eagle, and the ossifrage, and the ospray.' Among birds, the eagle, the vulture-like ossifrage, and the osprey were not to be eaten. They were detestable for Israel. Notice that most of the forbidden birds were birds of prey or scavengers. They ate flesh, often dead flesh, and blood. Israel was forbidden to eat blood. Birds that fed on carcasses carried the uncleanness of death. Yet notice the eagle. The same bird is used beautifully elsewhere. Isaiah 40 says, they that wait upon the LORD… shall mount up with wings as eagles. Exodus 19 says God bore Israel on eagles' wings. Unclean for food, but glorious as a picture of God's care. God's creatures serve many purposes in His word. Today, wait upon the LORD. He will renew your strength and lift you up as on eagles' wings.

== 11:23
T: All other flying creeping things
R: 'But all other flying creeping things, which have four feet, shall be an abomination unto you.' All other winged insects that walked on four legs were unclean for Israel. Notice but all other. After the exception came the boundary again. God was clear about what was allowed and what was not. Exceptions did not erase the rule. Grace does not cancel holiness. Notice the balance of this section. Most flying insects were forbidden, a few allowed. God's ways include both yes and no. A believer's life includes both. Today, receive God's yes with joy, and practice His no with faith. Both come from His love.
