# Leviticus 11:24-47: Carcases, cleansing and the call to holiness

== 11:24
T: Unclean until the even
R: 'And for these ye shall be unclean: whosoever toucheth the carcase of them shall be unclean until the even.' Anyone who touched the carcass of an unclean animal was unclean until evening. Notice until the even. The uncleanness had a limit. It lasted until sunset, then it ended. Ritual uncleanness was temporary. It was not a permanent condemnation. A new day brought a fresh start. This is gracious. God did not leave His people stuck in defilement. He set an end to it. Lamentations 3 says, his compassions fail not. They are new every morning. Psalm 30 says, weeping may endure for a night, but joy cometh in the morning. Notice that touching was enough. Contact with death defiled. Israel learned daily that death and God's holiness do not mix. Today, rest in God's fresh starts. Whatever has defiled you, His mercy sets a limit. A new day of grace is coming.
S: Whoever touched the carcase of an unclean animal was unclean until the even. The uncleanness had a set limit, and a new day brought a fresh start. Rest in God's fresh starts, for His mercy sets a limit and a new day of grace is coming.
P: Until the even. Leviticus 11:24: 'whosoever toucheth the carcase of them shall be unclean until the even.' Defilement had an end. God's mercies are new every morning.

== 11:25
T: Wash his clothes
R: 'And whosoever beareth ought of the carcase of them shall wash his clothes, and be unclean until the even.' Anyone who carried part of an unclean carcass had to wash his clothes and was unclean until evening. Notice beareth. Carrying was more involved than touching. So the person had to wash his clothes as well. Deeper contact required more cleansing. The more closely we handle what defiles, the more cleansing we need. Notice wash his clothes. Cleansing involved action. The person had to do something. Washing was the response to defilement. Revelation 22 says, Blessed are they that do his commandments… and wash their robes. James 4 says, Cleanse your hands, ye sinners; and purify your hearts. We respond to God's grace by seeking cleansing. Notice that the washing was available. God provided the means of becoming clean. Today, do not stay defiled. Bring what you have carried to God, and let Him wash you.
S: Whoever carried any part of an unclean carcase had to wash his clothes and be unclean until the even. Closer contact required more cleansing, and God provided the way to be clean. Do not stay defiled, but bring what you have carried to God and let Him wash you.
P: Wash and be clean. Leviticus 11:25: 'whosoever beareth ought of the carcase of them shall wash his clothes.' God provides the way to be clean. Come to Him today.

== 11:26
T: Every one that toucheth
R: 'The carcases of every beast which divideth the hoof, and is not clovenfooted, nor cheweth the cud, are unclean unto you: every one that toucheth them shall be unclean.' The carcasses of animals that did not meet the clean standards were unclean. Anyone who touched them became unclean. Notice every one. No one was exempt. Priests, rulers, and common people alike became unclean through contact. The law applied to all equally. Notice that uncleanness spread by touch. Defilement was contagious. But in the Gospels, Jesus reversed this. When He touched lepers, they became clean. When the dead girl was touched by Jesus in Mark 5, she rose. His holiness spread outward and overcame uncleanness. Matthew 8 says Jesus put forth his hand, and touched him, saying, I will; be thou clean. Today, rejoice that Christ's touch makes the unclean clean. His holiness is stronger than any defilement.
S: The carcases of beasts that were not clean were unclean, and every one who touched them became unclean. Defilement spread by touch, but Jesus reversed this, making the unclean clean by His touch. Rejoice that Christ's touch is stronger than any defilement.
P: His touch cleanses. Leviticus 11:26: 'every one that toucheth them shall be unclean.' Jesus touched the unclean and made them clean. His holiness overcomes defilement.
H: #DailyGrace #Leviticus1126 #Jesus #Touch #Cleansing #BibleVerse

== 11:27
T: Upon his paws
R: 'And whatsoever goeth upon his paws, among all manner of beasts that go on all four, those are unclean unto you: whoso toucheth their carcase shall be unclean until the even.' Four-footed animals that walked on paws, such as dogs, cats, lions, and bears, were unclean. Touching their carcasses made a person unclean until evening. Notice paws. Most animals with paws are predators. They kill and eat flesh. Like the birds of prey, they were linked with blood and death. Israel was to stay away from what lived by killing. God's people were called to life. Isaiah 11 pictures a day when the wolf also shall dwell with the lamb, and the lion shall eat straw like the ox. God's final kingdom will end predation. Peace will fill creation. Romans 8 says creation itself shall be delivered from the bondage of corruption. Today, long for the peace of God's coming kingdom. Live now as a person of peace, not of harm.
S: Beasts that went upon their paws, mostly predators, were unclean, and touching their carcases defiled until the even. God's people were called away from what lived by killing toward life. Long for the peace of God's coming kingdom and live now as a person of peace.
P: The peaceable kingdom. Leviticus 11:27: 'whatsoever goeth upon his paws… those are unclean.' One day the lion will eat straw like the ox. Live as a person of peace today.
H: #DailyGrace #Leviticus1127 #Peace #Kingdom #Hope #BibleVerse

== 11:28
T: He that beareth
R: 'And he that beareth the carcase of them shall wash his clothes, and be unclean until the even: they are unclean unto you.' Anyone who carried the carcass of these animals had to wash his clothes and was unclean until evening. Notice the consistent pattern. Touching required waiting until evening. Carrying required washing as well. God's laws were orderly and fair. Israel could know exactly what to do. Notice that someone had to carry dead animals. Life in an agricultural society made contact with death unavoidable. God did not forbid necessary work. He provided cleansing for it. Some defilement comes from simply living in a fallen world. God understands this. Psalm 103 says, he knoweth our frame; he remembereth that we are dust. He does not condemn us for living in a broken world. He provides cleansing. Today, bring the defilement of ordinary life to God. He knows your frame and gladly cleanses you.
S: Whoever carried the carcase of these beasts had to wash his clothes and was unclean until the even. Contact with death was often unavoidable in daily work, and God provided cleansing for it. Bring the defilement of ordinary life to God, who knows your frame and cleanses you.

== 11:29
T: Creeping things
R: 'These also shall be unclean unto you among the creeping things that creep upon the earth; the weasel, and the mouse, and the tortoise after his kind.' Among small creatures that moved along the ground, the weasel, the mouse, and the tortoise or great lizard were unclean. Notice the mouse. Mice and weasels live close to people. They get into homes, grain, and food. These laws reached right into Israel's kitchens. Holiness was practical. Notice creeping things. These creatures moved low on the ground. In Genesis 3, the serpent was cursed to go on its belly. Creeping became associated with the curse. Yet God named even these small creatures. Nothing was too small for His attention. Luke 12 says, not one of them is forgotten before God. He sees the smallest things in our lives too. The small habits and hidden corners of our homes matter to Him. Today, invite God into the small corners of your life. Holiness reaches even into the kitchen.
S: The weasel, mouse and tortoise were unclean among the creeping things. These creatures lived close to people, bringing God's holiness into the homes and kitchens of Israel. Invite God into the small corners of your life, for His holiness reaches even there.
P: Small corners. Leviticus 11:29: 'the weasel, and the mouse, and the tortoise.' God sees the smallest details. Invite Him into every corner of your home today.

== 11:30
T: The chameleon
R: 'And the ferret, and the chameleon, and the lizard, and the snail, and the mole.' The ferret or gecko, the chameleon, the lizard, the snail or sand lizard, and the mole were also unclean for Israel. Notice the chameleon. It changes color to blend into its surroundings. Some have drawn a lesson here. Believers are not to blend in with the world. We are not called to change our colors depending on who is around. Daniel refused to blend into Babylon. Daniel 1 says Daniel purposed in his heart that he would not defile himself. He remained distinct in a foreign land. Notice the mole. It lives in darkness underground. Isaiah 2 speaks of casting idols to the moles and to the bats. Idols belong in darkness. Today, refuse to be a spiritual chameleon. Like Daniel, purpose in your heart to stay faithful, whoever is watching.
S: The ferret, chameleon, lizard, snail and mole were unclean for Israel. The chameleon, which blends into its surroundings, pictures the believer who changes colors with the crowd. Purpose in your heart, like Daniel, to stay faithful wherever you are.

== 11:31
T: When they be dead
R: 'These are unclean to you among all that creep: whosoever doth touch them, when they be dead, shall be unclean until the even.' Touching these small creatures when they were dead made a person unclean until evening. Notice when they be dead. It was their dead bodies that defiled. Death was the issue. Throughout these laws, death and uncleanness are linked. God is the God of life, and death is the enemy. First Corinthians 15 says, The last enemy that shall be destroyed is death. These laws pointed forward to God's victory over death. Every evening of waiting reminded Israel that death was abnormal. It was not how things were supposed to be. Jesus came to defeat it. John 11 records Him saying, I am the resurrection, and the life. Revelation 21 promises, there shall be no more death. Today, rejoice that Christ has conquered death. One day, uncleanness and death will be gone forever.
S: Touching these creeping things when they were dead made a person unclean until the even. Death defiled because it is the enemy that God will destroy. Rejoice that Christ, the resurrection and the life, has conquered death.
P: Death defeated. Leviticus 11:31: 'whosoever doth touch them, when they be dead, shall be unclean.' Death is the last enemy. Christ is the resurrection and the life.

== 11:32
T: Put into water
R: 'And upon whatsoever any of them, when they are dead, doth fall, it shall be unclean; whether it be any vessel of wood, or raiment, or skin, or sack… it must be put into water, and it shall be unclean until the even; so it shall be cleansed.' If a dead creeping creature fell on any wooden item, clothing, leather, or sack, the item became unclean. It had to be put in water and was unclean until evening. Then it was clean. Notice so it shall be cleansed. Even household objects could be restored. They were not thrown away. Water and time made them clean again. God's plan was restoration, not destruction. Notice wherein any work is done. The tools of daily work could be defiled and cleansed. God cared about the ordinary objects of life. Ephesians 5 speaks of Christ cleansing the church with the washing of water by the word. God's word cleanses us. Today, let God's word wash you. Whatever has fallen on you, He restores rather than discards.
S: Any vessel, garment, skin or sack touched by a dead creeping thing had to be put into water and was unclean until the even, then cleansed. God's plan for defiled things was restoration, not disposal. Let God's word wash you, for He restores rather than discards.
P: Restored, not discarded. Leviticus 11:32: 'it must be put into water… so it shall be cleansed.' God restores what is defiled. Let His word wash you today.

== 11:33
T: Ye shall break it
R: 'And every earthen vessel, whereinto any of them falleth, whatsoever is in it shall be unclean; and ye shall break it.' If an unclean dead creature fell into a clay pot, everything in the pot became unclean, and the pot had to be broken. Notice earthen vessel. Clay absorbs. It could not be washed clean like wood or cloth. So it had to be broken. Some defilement goes too deep for surface cleaning. Jeremiah 18 shows the potter remaking a marred vessel: so he made it again another vessel, as seemed good to the potter. God sometimes breaks and remakes. Second Corinthians 4 says we have this treasure in earthen vessels. We are clay pots. Sin soaks into us deeply. Outward cleaning is not enough. We need to be made new. Second Corinthians 5 says, if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature. Today, let God remake you. Where sin has soaked deep, He can make a new vessel.
S: An earthen vessel into which a dead creeping thing fell had to be broken, along with all that was in it made unclean. Clay absorbed defilement too deeply for washing, picturing our need to be made new. Let God remake you where sin has soaked deep, for in Christ you are a new creature.
P: Made new. Leviticus 11:33: 'every earthen vessel, whereinto any of them falleth… ye shall break it.' The Potter makes a new vessel. In Christ, you are a new creature.

== 11:34
T: Meat and drink
R: 'Of all meat which may be eaten, that on which such water cometh shall be unclean: and all drink that may be drunk in every such vessel shall be unclean.' Food that came into contact with water from such a vessel was unclean. Any drink in such a vessel was unclean too. Notice how defilement spread. From the creature to the vessel, from the vessel to the water, from the water to the food. Uncleanness traveled. Sin spreads in the same way. First Corinthians 5 says, a little leaven leaveneth the whole lump. Hebrews 12 warns, lest any root of bitterness springing up trouble you, and thereby many be defiled. One unaddressed sin can affect many. Notice meat and drink. Daily nourishment was involved. What defiles can get into what feeds us. We must guard what we take in. Philippians 4 tells us to think on whatsoever things are pure. Today, guard what you take in. Deal with small defilements before they spread.
S: Food touched by water from a defiled vessel became unclean, and any drink in it was unclean too. Defilement spread step by step, as sin and bitterness spread when left unaddressed. Guard what you take in and deal with small defilements before they spread.
P: A little leaven. Leviticus 11:34: 'all drink that may be drunk in every such vessel shall be unclean.' Defilement spreads. Deal with it early and guard what you take in.

Jesus spoke in Matthew 5 of cutting off the hand that causes sin. He meant radical action against sin. Sometimes we must break down habits, relationships, or patterns that defile us. Notice that this was a real cost to a family. Holiness was not cheap. Today, consider what in your life needs to be broken down. Take radical action against what defiles, even when it costs you.

== 11:36
T: A fountain shall be clean
R: 'Nevertheless a fountain or pit, wherein there is plenty of water, shall be clean: but that which toucheth their carcase shall be unclean.' A spring or cistern with plenty of water stayed clean, even if a dead creature fell in. Only the person who touched the carcass became unclean. Notice nevertheless. Here was an important exception. A large, flowing source of water could not be defiled. Its abundance overcame the contamination. Living water cleanses rather than being made unclean. This points beautifully to Christ. John 4 records Jesus saying, the water that I shall give him shall be in him a well of water springing up into everlasting life. Jeremiah 2 calls God the fountain of living waters. Zechariah 13 promises a fountain opened… for sin and for uncleanness. Christ's fountain is never defiled. It cleanses all who come. Today, drink from the fountain of living water. In Christ, there is a spring that cleanses and never runs dry.
S: A fountain or pit with plenty of water remained clean even when a carcase fell in. Abundant water overcame defilement, picturing Christ, the fountain opened for sin and uncleanness. Drink from Christ's fountain of living water, which cleanses and never runs dry.
P: A fountain that stays clean. Leviticus 11:36: 'a fountain or pit, wherein there is plenty of water, shall be clean.' Christ is the fountain for sin and uncleanness. Come and drink.

== 11:38

But if the seed had been soaked in water and then a carcass fell on it, the seed became unclean. Notice the difference water made. Dry seed resisted defilement. Wet seed absorbed it. Moisture made the seed vulnerable. Some seasons of life leave us more open. When we are softened by grief, stress, or change, we can more easily absorb harmful influences. Wisdom recognizes vulnerable times. First Peter 5 says, Be sober, be vigilant; because your adversary the devil… walketh about, seeking whom he may devour. He often strikes when we are vulnerable. Matthew 4 shows Jesus tempted after forty days of fasting, when He was hungry. Today, be especially watchful in vulnerable seasons. When you are tired, grieving, or stressed, guard your heart with extra care.

So His people must be holy too. Notice for I am holy. This is the heart of Leviticus. The food laws were not random. They flowed from God's character. Because He is holy, His people must be distinct. Holiness means being set apart, different, belonging to God. First Peter 1 quotes this verse directly: Be ye holy; for I am holy. Peter applied it to believers. The food laws have passed, but the call to holiness remains. Notice your God. God's holiness was not distant. He was their God, in relationship with them. Holiness flows from belonging. Today, hear God's call to be holy. Not to earn His love, but because you belong to Him and He is holy.

== 11:46
T: This is the law
R: 'This is the law of the beasts, and of the fowl, and of every living creature that moveth in the waters, and of every creature that creepeth upon the earth.' The chapter closes with a summary. This was God's law about land animals, birds, water creatures, and creeping things. Notice the scope. Land, air, water, and ground. Every realm of creation was covered. This mirrors Genesis 1, where God created each realm and filled it. God is Lord of all creation. Nothing lies outside His care or His instruction. Psalm 24 says, The earth is the LORD'S, and the fulness thereof. Every creature belongs to Him. Notice this is the law. These instructions were God's teaching, not mere rules. The Hebrew word torah means instruction. God was teaching His people how to live in His world. Psalm 119 says, Open thou mine eyes, that I may behold wondrous things out of thy law. Today, see God as Lord of all creation. Ask Him to open your eyes to the wonders in His word.
S: The chapter summarizes the law of beasts, fowl, water creatures and creeping things. Every realm of creation was covered, for the earth is the LORD's. See God as Lord of all creation and ask Him to open your eyes to the wonders of His word.

== 11:47
T: To make a difference
R: 'To make a difference between the unclean and the clean, and between the beast that may be eaten and the beast that may not be eaten.' The purpose of the law was to distinguish between unclean and clean, and between what could be eaten and what could not. Notice to make a difference. Distinction was the goal. Israel was to learn to discern. Every meal trained their minds to separate clean from unclean. This prepared them to discern in moral matters too. In Christ, the food distinctions are fulfilled. Mark 7 says Jesus made all meats clean. Acts 10 shows the barrier between Jew and Gentile removed. Peter learned that God is no respecter of persons. Yet the call to discernment remains. Philippians 1 prays that love may abound in knowledge and in all judgment; That ye may approve things that are excellent. Today, ask God to sharpen your discernment. Learn to tell the difference between what pleases Him and what does not.
S: The law was given to make a difference between unclean and clean, and between what could and could not be eaten. In Christ the food distinctions are fulfilled, but the call to discernment remains. Ask God to sharpen your discernment so you can approve things that are excellent.
