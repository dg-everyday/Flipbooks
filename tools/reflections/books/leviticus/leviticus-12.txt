# Leviticus 12: Purification after childbirth

== 12:1
T: God cares about mothers
R: 'And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying.' God spoke to Moses about women who had given birth. A whole chapter, though short, was given to mothers. Notice that God addressed childbirth. It is one of life's most important and vulnerable moments. God did not overlook it. He gave instructions that guided mothers through recovery and back into worship. God cares about the experiences of women. Throughout Scripture, He sees mothers. Genesis 21 shows God hearing Hagar and her child. First Samuel 1 shows God remembering Hannah. Luke 1 shows God visiting Elizabeth and Mary. Isaiah 66 says, As one whom his mother comforteth, so will I comfort you. God even compares His own care to a mother's. Notice that childbirth was connected to worship. God's law reached into the most personal moments of family life. Today, thank God for mothers. And know that God sees you in life's most tender and vulnerable seasons.
S: The LORD spoke to Moses about women who had given birth. God gave a whole chapter to mothers in one of life's most vulnerable moments. Thank God for mothers and know that He sees you in life's tender seasons.
P: God sees mothers. Leviticus 12:1: 'And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying.' As one whom his mother comforteth, so will I comfort you. Thank God for mothers today.

== 12:2
T: A time of separation
R: 'Speak unto the children of Israel, saying, If a woman have conceived seed, and born a man child: then she shall be unclean seven days.' After giving birth to a son, a mother was ceremonially unclean for seven days, as during her monthly period. Notice that uncleanness was not sin. Childbirth was a blessing. Psalm 127 says, children are an heritage of the LORD. The uncleanness was ritual, connected to the loss of blood. It did not mean the mother had done wrong. Notice the separation. This period kept her from duties and visitors. It gave her time to rest and recover. In a demanding world, God built in a time of quiet for new mothers. His laws included care for the body. Isaiah 40 says, he… shall gently lead those that are with young. God is gentle with mothers. Today, honor new mothers by giving them rest and support. God gently leads those with young.
S: After bearing a son, a mother was ceremonially unclean for seven days. This was not sin but ritual separation, which also gave her rest and recovery. Honor new mothers with rest and support, for God gently leads those that are with young.
P: Gently led. Leviticus 12:2: 'If a woman have conceived seed, and born a man child.' Children are a heritage of the LORD. Support new mothers with rest and care.

== 12:3
T: The eighth day
R: 'And in the eighth day the flesh of his foreskin shall be circumcised.' On the eighth day, the baby boy was to be circumcised. This was the sign of God's covenant with Abraham. Notice the eighth day. Genesis 17 first gave this command to Abraham. Every Israelite boy received the covenant sign on his eighth day. He belonged to God's people from infancy. Luke 2 records that Jesus was circumcised on the eighth day and named Jesus. Christ submitted to the law. Galatians 4 says, God sent forth his Son, made of a woman, made under the law, To redeem them that were under the law. Notice the covenant sign. Circumcision marked the body. But Scripture always pointed deeper. Deuteronomy 30 says, the LORD thy God will circumcise thine heart. Romans 2 says, circumcision is that of the heart, in the spirit. Colossians 2 speaks of the circumcision of Christ. Today, ask God for a circumcised heart. Let Him mark you as His own on the inside.
S: On the eighth day the baby boy was circumcised, receiving the covenant sign given to Abraham. Jesus Himself was circumcised on the eighth day, yet Scripture always pointed to the circumcision of the heart. Ask God to mark your heart as His own from the inside.
P: A circumcised heart. Leviticus 12:3: 'in the eighth day the flesh of his foreskin shall be circumcised.' Jesus kept this law. Ask God to mark your heart as His.

== 12:4
T: The days of her purifying
R: 'And she shall then continue in the blood of her purifying three and thirty days; she shall touch no hallowed thing, nor come into the sanctuary, until the days of her purifying be fulfilled.' After the first week, the mother continued in purification for thirty-three more days. She could not touch holy things or enter the sanctuary until this time was complete. Notice the forty days in total. In Scripture, forty often marks a time of transition. Rain fell forty days in Noah's time. Moses spent forty days on the mountain. Jesus fasted forty days. This was a season of transition for the mother. Notice that she was not cast out of her home. She stayed with her family. Only temple duties were paused. She was freed from obligation while her body healed. Ecclesiastes 3 says, To every thing there is a season. There is a season for rest and recovery. Today, accept seasons of rest when God gives them. Recovery is not failure. It is part of His good design.
S: The mother continued in the blood of her purifying thirty-three more days, not touching holy things or entering the sanctuary. The forty days formed a season of transition, freeing her from obligation while she healed. Accept seasons of rest and recovery as part of God's good design.
P: A season to recover. Leviticus 12:4: 'until the days of her purifying be fulfilled.' Rest is not failure. Accept the recovery seasons God gives you today.

== 12:5
T: A maid child
R: 'But if she bear a maid child, then she shall be unclean two weeks, as in her separation: and she shall continue in the blood of her purifying threescore and six days.' If the baby was a girl, the mother was unclean for two weeks, then continued purification for sixty-six days, double the time for a boy. Notice the difference in time. Scripture does not explain why. Some suggest it reflects the girl's future ability to bear life. Others see different reasons. We should be careful not to read inferiority into it. The offering at the end was exactly the same for a son or a daughter, as verse 6 shows. Genesis 1 says God created male and female in His image. Galatians 3 says, there is neither male nor female: for ye are all one in Christ Jesus. Daughters are equally precious to God. Notice maid child. God gave daughters their own place in His law. Today, celebrate daughters and sons alike. Both bear God's image and are equally loved.
S: For a daughter, the mother was unclean two weeks and continued her purifying sixty-six days. Scripture does not explain the difference, and the same offering was brought for son or daughter. Celebrate daughters and sons alike, for both bear God's image and are equally loved.
P: Equally precious. Leviticus 12:5: 'if she bear a maid child.' Male and female bear God's image. All are one in Christ. Celebrate your daughters and sons today.

== 12:6
T: For a son, or for a daughter
R: 'And when the days of her purifying are fulfilled, for a son, or for a daughter, she shall bring a lamb of the first year for a burnt offering, and a young pigeon, or a turtledove, for a sin offering.' When the purification was complete, whether for a son or a daughter, the mother brought a year-old lamb for a burnt offering and a pigeon or dove for a sin offering. Notice for a son, or for a daughter. The same offering for both. No difference in value. Notice the burnt offering. It expressed dedication and thanksgiving. The mother gave herself and her child to God. Hannah did this with Samuel in First Samuel 1: I have lent him to the LORD. Parents dedicate their children to God. Notice the sin offering. This was for ritual cleansing, not for sin in giving birth. The mother was restored to full worship. Today, dedicate your family to God. Thank Him for every child, and give them back to Him.
S: When her purifying was fulfilled, for a son or a daughter, the mother brought a lamb for a burnt offering and a pigeon or turtledove for a sin offering. The same offering for both expressed dedication and restored her to worship. Dedicate your family to God and give every child back to Him with thanks.
P: Dedicated to God. Leviticus 12:6: 'for a son, or for a daughter, she shall bring a lamb.' Like Hannah, lend your children to the LORD. Dedicate your family to Him today.

== 12:7
T: She shall be cleansed
R: 'Who shall offer it before the LORD, and make an atonement for her; and she shall be cleansed from the issue of her blood. This is the law for her that hath born a male or a female.' The priest offered the sacrifices before the LORD and made atonement for the mother. She was cleansed and restored. Notice she shall be cleansed. The goal was restoration. The mother returned to full participation in worship. Her season of separation ended with welcome. God does not keep people at a distance forever. He brings them back. Notice the issue of her blood. Mark 5 tells of a woman with an issue of blood for twelve years. Under the law, she was continually unclean. She touched Jesus' garment, and she was healed. Jesus said, Daughter, thy faith hath made thee whole. What the law could only manage, Christ healed. Today, come to Jesus with whatever has kept you at a distance. He makes whole and welcomes you back.
S: The priest made atonement for the mother, and she was cleansed from the issue of her blood. Her separation ended with full restoration, as Christ later healed the woman with an issue of blood. Come to Jesus with whatever has kept you at a distance, for He makes whole.
P: Made whole. Leviticus 12:7: 'she shall be cleansed from the issue of her blood.' Jesus healed the woman who touched His hem. Come to Him today.

== 12:8
T: Two turtles
R: 'And if she be not able to bring a lamb, then she shall bring two turtles, or two young pigeons; the one for the burnt offering, and the other for a sin offering.' If a mother could not afford a lamb, she could bring two turtledoves or two young pigeons instead. The priest made atonement, and she was clean. Notice that this is exactly what Mary brought. Luke 2 says Joseph and Mary came to offer a sacrifice… A pair of turtledoves, or two young pigeons. They could not afford a lamb. The family of Jesus was poor. Yet the very Lamb of God was in Mary's arms. She brought birds because she could not afford a lamb, while holding the Lamb who would take away the sin of the world. What a beautiful irony. Second Corinthians 8 says Jesus became poor so that we might be rich. Today, marvel at the humility of Christ. He came to a poor family to make you rich in grace.
S: A mother who could not afford a lamb could bring two turtles or two young pigeons. Mary brought this very offering, unable to afford a lamb, while holding the Lamb of God. Marvel at Christ's humility, who became poor to make you rich in grace.
P: The Lamb in her arms. Leviticus 12:8: 'if she be not able to bring a lamb, then she shall bring two turtles.' Mary brought birds while holding the Lamb of God.
