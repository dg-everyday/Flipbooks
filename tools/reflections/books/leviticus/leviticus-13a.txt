# Leviticus 13:1-28: Examining skin disease

== 13:1
T: To Moses and Aaron
R: 'And the LORD spake unto Moses and Aaron, saying.' God spoke to Moses and Aaron about skin diseases. The priests would be responsible for examining people and declaring them clean or unclean. Notice that the priests were given this task. They were not doctors in the modern sense. Their role was to decide whether someone could take part in worship and community life. The disease called leprosy in the KJV covered a range of skin conditions, not only what we now call leprosy. Notice that God cared about the sick. These chapters are long and detailed because suffering people mattered to Him. He gave careful rules so that no one would be wrongly excluded or wrongly included. Matthew 4 says Jesus went about healing all manner of sickness. God's heart for the sick is seen fully in Christ. Today, remember that God cares about your body and your health. Bring your illnesses to Him, and care for those who are sick.
S: The LORD spoke to Moses and Aaron about skin diseases, giving the priests the task of examination. These detailed laws show that suffering people mattered to God. Bring your illnesses to God and care for those who are sick.
P: God cares for the sick. Leviticus 13:1: 'And the LORD spake unto Moses and Aaron.' Jesus healed all manner of sickness. Bring your health concerns to Him today.

== 13:2
T: Brought unto the priest
R: 'When a man shall have in the skin of his flesh a rising, a scab, or a bright spot, and it be in the skin of his flesh like the plague of leprosy; then he shall be brought unto Aaron the priest.' If someone had a swelling, rash, or bright spot on the skin that looked like a serious disease, he was brought to a priest. Notice he shall be brought. The person did not diagnose himself. Someone brought him to the priest. Others took action on his behalf. Mark 2 tells of four men who carried a paralyzed friend to Jesus. They brought him. We can bring others to Christ. Notice that the condition was examined, not ignored. Hiding illness or problems makes them worse. Bringing them into the light is the first step to healing. James 5 says, Is any sick among you? let him call for the elders of the church. Today, bring your concerns into the light. And bring others who are hurting to Jesus.
S: Someone with a rising, scab or bright spot like a plague of leprosy was brought to Aaron the priest. Others noticed and helped, and the problem was brought into the light. Bring your concerns into the light and bring others who are hurting to Jesus.
P: Brought to the priest. Leviticus 13:2: 'then he shall be brought unto Aaron the priest.' Like the four friends, bring others to Jesus. Don't hide what needs healing.

== 13:3
T: The priest shall look
R: 'And the priest shall look on the plague in the skin of the flesh: and when the hair in the plague is turned white, and the plague in sight be deeper than the skin… the priest shall look on him, and pronounce him unclean.' The priest examined the affected skin. If the hair had turned white and the sore went deeper than the skin, it was a serious disease, and he declared the person unclean. Notice the priest shall look. He examined carefully. Notice deeper than the skin. A surface problem was different from one that went deep. Sin too can be deeper than it appears. Jeremiah 17 says, The heart is deceitful above all things. What seems small on the surface can be rooted deep. Yet Christ sees all the way down. Hebrews 4 says all things are naked… unto the eyes of him with whom we have to do. Today, let God look deeply at your heart. He sees what lies beneath the surface, and He heals.
S: The priest looked on the plague, and if the hair was white and it was deeper than the skin, he pronounced the person unclean. == 13:4
T: Shut up seven days
R: 'If the bright spot be white in the skin of his flesh, and in sight be not deeper than the skin, and the hair thereof be not turned white; then the priest shall shut up him that hath the plague seven days.' If the spot was white but not deep and the hair had not turned white, the priest isolated the person for seven days. Notice that the priest did not rush to a verdict. When the signs were unclear, he waited. Isolation allowed time to see what would happen. This was wise and fair. Rushing to judgment harms people. Proverbs 18 says, He that answereth a matter before he heareth it, it is folly and shame unto him. First Corinthians 4 says, judge nothing before the time. Waiting can protect someone from being wrongly condemned. Notice seven days. A full, complete period. The waiting had purpose. Today, be slow to judge others. When things are unclear, wait and look again before drawing conclusions.
S: If the bright spot was not deeper than the skin and the hair was not white, the priest shut the person up seven days. When the signs were unclear, he waited rather than rushing to a verdict. Be slow to judge others, and wait and look again when things are unclear.
P: Don't rush to judge. Leviticus 13:4: 'the priest shall shut up him that hath the plague seven days.' When unclear, wait and look again. Judge nothing before the time.

== 13:5
T: Seven days more
R: 'And the priest shall look on him the seventh day: and, behold, if the plague in his sight be at a stay, and the plague spread not in the skin; then the priest shall shut him up seven days more.' After seven days, the priest examined again. If the condition had not changed or spread, he isolated the person for another seven days. Notice seven days more. The priest was patient. One week was not enough to be sure. He gave it more time. Notice at a stay. The condition had paused. It was not getting worse, but it was not clearly gone. Sometimes life is like that. Waiting is hard, but God strengthens us in it. Today, if you are in a season of waiting with no clear answer, take courage. God strengthens those who wait on Him.
