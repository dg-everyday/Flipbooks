# Leviticus 14:33-57: The plague in a house

== 14:33
T: Looking ahead
R: 'And the LORD spake unto Moses and unto Aaron, saying.' God spoke to Moses and Aaron again, this time about something that would happen in the future, in houses in the Promised Land. Notice that Israel was still living in tents. They had no houses yet. Yet God gave them laws for houses. He was preparing them for a future He had promised. God's instructions assumed His promises would come true. This is faith-building. When God gives instructions for the future, He is guaranteeing that future. Hebrews 11 says, faith is the substance of things hoped for. Israel received house laws while living in tents, because God's promise of the land was certain. John 14 records Jesus saying, I go to prepare a place for you. Our future home is just as certain. Today, live as someone whose future is guaranteed by God. Prepare for what He has promised, even while you live in tents.
S: The LORD spoke to Moses and Aaron about houses while Israel still lived in tents. God gave laws for a future He guaranteed. Live as one whose future is secured by God, preparing for what He has promised.
P: Laws for a promised future. Leviticus 14:33: 'And the LORD spake unto Moses and unto Aaron.' Living in tents, they got laws for houses. God's promises are certain.

== 14:34
T: Which I give to you
R: 'When ye be come into the land of Canaan, which I give to you for a possession, and I put the plague of leprosy in a house of the land of your possession.' When Israel entered Canaan, the land God was giving them, God might put a plague of mildew in one of their houses. Notice which I give to you. The land was a gift. Israel did not earn it. God gave it. Every house they would live in was ultimately His gift. Deuteronomy 6 speaks of houses full of all good things, which thou filledst not. Notice I put the plague. God took responsibility for the mildew. Even hardship came under His sovereignty. Nothing happened outside His control. This does not mean every trouble is a punishment. But nothing escapes His hand. Romans 8 says, all things work together for good to them that love God. Today, thank God for your home as His gift. And trust His sovereign hand, even in the troubles that come to it.
S: When Israel came into the land God gave them, He might put a plague of leprosy in a house. The land was His gift, and even hardship lay under His sovereign hand. Thank God for your home as His gift and trust Him even in its troubles.
P: Gift and sovereignty. Leviticus 14:34: 'the land of Canaan, which I give to you for a possession.' Your home is God's gift. Trust His hand in every trouble.

== 14:35
T: It seemeth to me
R: 'And he that owneth the house shall come and tell the priest, saying, It seemeth to me there is as it were a plague in the house.' The homeowner was to come to the priest and report: It seems to me there is something like a plague in my house. Notice he… shall come and tell. The owner took initiative. He did not ignore the problem or hide it. He reported it. Openness about problems at home invites help. Notice it seemeth to me… as it were. The owner spoke humbly. He did not claim certainty. He said what it seemed like and let the priest decide. This is wise. We should report concerns honestly without overstating them. James 3 says, the wisdom that is from above is first pure, then peaceable, gentle, and easy to be intreated. Humble speech leaves room for others' judgment. Today, if something seems wrong in your home, speak up humbly. Seek help without hiding or exaggerating.
S: The homeowner was to tell the priest, It seemeth to me there is as it were a plague in the house. He took initiative and spoke humbly, without hiding or overstating the problem. If something seems wrong in your home, speak up humbly and seek help.
P: It seemeth to me. Leviticus 14:35: 'he that owneth the house shall come and tell the priest.' Don't hide problems at home. Speak humbly and seek help today.

== 14:36
T: Empty the house
R: 'Then the priest shall command that they empty the house, before the priest go into it to see the plague, that all that is in the house be not made unclean.' Before examining the house, the priest ordered it emptied so that its contents would not be declared unclean. Notice that all… be not made unclean. The priest protected the family's belongings. If the house was declared unclean, everything inside would be too. By emptying it first, the possessions were saved. God's law was considerate. It sought to minimize loss. God cares about the practical impact of His laws on families. He did not want them to lose more than necessary. Matthew 12 says Jesus would not break a bruised reed. His care extends to the details of our lives. Notice the order. First empty, then examine. Wisdom prepares before acting. Today, notice God's care for the practical details of your life. And when you must deal with problems, protect others from unnecessary loss.
S: Before going in to see the plague, the priest commanded that the house be emptied so its contents would not become unclean. God's law sought to minimize the family's loss. Notice God's care for practical details and protect others from unnecessary loss when dealing with problems.
P: Considerate care. Leviticus 14:36: 'empty the house… that all that is in the house be not made unclean.' God cares about your practical losses. Be considerate of others too.

== 14:57
T: To teach
R: 'To teach when it is unclean, and when it is clean: this is the law of leprosy.' The purpose of these two chapters was to teach when something was unclean and when it was clean. Notice to teach. The law was instruction. God was teaching His people to discern. These chapters were a school of holiness. Notice unclean… clean. Both verdicts were possible. The law did not aim only to condemn. It also declared people clean and restored them. God's word is both a mirror that shows our condition and a door that leads to restoration. Romans 3 says, by the law is the knowledge of sin. But Romans 3 also says we are justified freely by his grace. The law reveals the problem; Christ provides the cure. Notice this is the law of leprosy. The section closes. Two long chapters, one message: God knows how to make the unclean clean. Today, let God's word teach you. Let it show you your need, and lead you to Christ, who makes you clean.
