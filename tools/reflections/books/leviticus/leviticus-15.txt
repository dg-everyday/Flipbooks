# Leviticus 15: Bodily discharges and cleansing

== 15:1
T: God speaks about the body
R: 'And the LORD spake unto Moses and to Aaron, saying.' God spoke to Moses and Aaron about bodily discharges, private matters of the body. Notice that God addressed private things. Many people feel these subjects are too personal for faith. But God spoke to them openly. Nothing about our bodies is outside His concern. God made our bodies, and He cares about them. Psalm 139 says, I am fearfully and wonderfully made. First Corinthians 6 says, your body is the temple of the Holy Ghost. Our bodies matter to God. Notice that these laws were ritual, not moral. Most discharges described here were natural and not sinful. They affected worship, not standing before God. Ritual uncleanness was a teaching tool about God's holiness, not a statement of shame. Today, thank God for your body. Bring every part of your life to Him, including the private and personal. He cares about it all.
S: The LORD spoke to Moses and Aaron about private matters of the body. These ritual laws were not about shame but showed that nothing in our bodies is outside God's concern. Thank God for your body and bring every part of your life to Him.
P: Fearfully and wonderfully made. Leviticus 15:1: 'And the LORD spake unto Moses and to Aaron.' God cares about your body. Bring even private matters to Him.

== 15:2
T: A running issue
R: 'Speak unto the children of Israel, and say unto them, When any man hath a running issue out of his flesh, because of his issue he is unclean.' A man with an abnormal bodily discharge, likely from illness, was ceremonially unclean. Notice a running issue. This described a chronic condition, not normal bodily function. It was a sickness. The man suffered, and it affected his ability to worship. Notice that God provided guidance for a difficult, embarrassing condition. He did not leave the man without direction. Chronic illness can be isolating and discouraging. Yet God had a path for this man, through waiting and cleansing, back to worship. Psalm 41 says, The LORD will strengthen him upon the bed of languishing: thou wilt make all his bed in his sickness. God cares for the chronically ill. Today, if you live with a long illness, know that God sees you. He strengthens you on your bed of languishing.
S: A man with a running issue out of his flesh was unclean because of his issue. This chronic, embarrassing illness affected his worship, yet God provided a path back. If you live with a long illness, know that God sees you and strengthens you.
P: On the bed of languishing. Leviticus 15:2: 'When any man hath a running issue out of his flesh.' God sees the chronically ill. He will strengthen you today.

== 15:3
T: Run or stopped
R: 'And this shall be his uncleanness in his issue: whether his flesh run with his issue, or his flesh be stopped from his issue, it is his uncleanness.' Whether the discharge was flowing or blocked, the man was unclean as long as the condition lasted. Notice whether… run… or… stopped. The condition was present in both cases. A blocked discharge was still an illness. The visible symptom did not determine everything. The underlying problem did. Sin works the same way. Sometimes it is visible. Sometimes it is hidden or stopped up. But hidden sin is still sin. Covering it does not cure it. Psalm 32 says, When I kept silence, my bones waxed old through my roaring all the day long. David's hidden sin damaged him inside. Only confession brought relief. Numbers 32 says, be sure your sin will find you out. Today, deal with the underlying condition, not just the symptoms. Bring hidden things to God for real healing.
S: Whether his flesh ran with the issue or was stopped from it, it was still his uncleanness. The underlying condition mattered, not just the visible symptom, as hidden sin is still sin. Deal with the underlying condition and bring hidden things to God for real healing.
P: Hidden is still there. Leviticus 15:3: 'whether his flesh run with his issue, or his flesh be stopped.' Covering isn't curing. Bring hidden things to God today.

== 15:4
T: Bed and seat
R: 'Every bed, whereon he lieth that hath the issue, is unclean: and every thing, whereon he sitteth, shall be unclean.' The bed the man lay on and anything he sat on became unclean. Notice bed and seat. The man's uncleanness affected the places of his daily life: where he rested and where he sat. His condition touched his whole household. Our struggles affect those around us. Illness, grief, and sin ripple outward into our homes. No one lives in isolation. Romans 14 says, none of us liveth to himself. Our lives are connected. Notice also that the law did not forbid the man to rest. He could lie down and sit. He was not driven from his home. The law simply guided how others should respond. Grace balanced protection. Today, remember that your life affects your household. Seek healing not just for yourself but for the sake of those you love.

== 15:5
T: Bathe and wait
R: 'And whosoever toucheth his bed shall wash his clothes, and bathe himself in water, and be unclean until the even.' Anyone who touched the man's bed had to wash his clothes, bathe, and remain unclean until evening. Notice the simple remedy. Wash, bathe, wait. Contact with the unclean was not a disaster. It had a clear, simple solution. By evening, the person was clean again. This made it possible to care for the sick. Family members could tend to the man, knowing they could be cleansed afterward. God's law allowed compassion. It did not force people to abandon the sick. Matthew 25 says, I was sick, and ye visited me. Care for the sick is care for Christ. Notice until the even. Again, a limit. Uncleanness did not last forever. Today, do not be afraid to care for those who are struggling. God provides cleansing for those who serve. Love the sick without fear.
