# Leviticus 16: The Day of Atonement

== 16:1
T: After the death
R: 'And the LORD spake unto Moses after the death of the two sons of Aaron, when they offered before the LORD, and died.' God spoke to Moses after the death of Nadab and Abihu. The Day of Atonement was given in the shadow of that tragedy. Notice after the death. God remembered what had happened. The deaths of Aaron's sons showed how dangerous it was to approach God carelessly. Now God gave the right way to come near. Tragedy was followed by instruction. God does not leave us in confusion after painful lessons. He shows the way forward. Notice that God did not abandon Aaron. Despite his sons' failure, Aaron would still be the one to enter God's presence. Grace continued after judgment. Hebrews 10 says, Having therefore… boldness to enter into the holiest by the blood of Jesus, By a new and living way. God has given a way to come near. Today, learn from painful lessons. And rejoice that God has opened a new and living way into His presence.
S: The LORD spoke to Moses after the death of Aaron's two sons, who had offered before Him and died. After the tragedy, God gave the right way to come near His presence. Learn from painful lessons and rejoice in the new and living way God has opened through Christ.
P: A new and living way. Leviticus 16:1: 'after the death of the two sons of Aaron.' After tragedy, God showed the way to come near. Christ is that way.

== 16:2
T: Not at all times
R: 'Speak unto Aaron thy brother, that he come not at all times into the holy place within the vail before the mercy seat… that he die not: for I will appear in the cloud upon the mercy seat.' Aaron could not enter the Most Holy Place, behind the veil, whenever he wished, or he would die. God appeared there in the cloud above the mercy seat. Notice not at all times. Even the high priest could enter only once a year. The veil was a barrier. Notice the mercy seat. The lid of the ark was where God met His people. It was the seat of mercy. Matthew 27 says that when Jesus died, the veil of the temple was rent in twain from the top to the bottom. God tore the barrier from above. Now access is open at all times. Hebrews 4 says, Let us therefore come boldly unto the throne of grace. Today, rejoice that the veil is torn. You can come to God at any time through Christ.
S: Aaron could not come at all times within the vail before the mercy seat, lest he die, for God appeared there in the cloud. Access was restricted, but when Jesus died, the veil was torn from top to bottom. Rejoice that the veil is torn and you can come to God at any time through Christ.
P: The veil is torn. Leviticus 16:2: 'that he come not at all times into the holy place within the vail.' Christ tore the veil. Come boldly to the throne of grace now.

== 16:3
T: Thus shall Aaron come
R: 'Thus shall Aaron come into the holy place: with a young bullock for a sin offering, and a ram for a burnt offering.' God told Aaron how to enter the holy place: with a young bull for a sin offering and a ram for a burnt offering. Notice thus shall Aaron come. There was a right way to approach God. Not any way, but God's way. Nadab and Abihu had come their own way and died. Aaron must come God's way. This is still true. John 14 records Jesus saying, I am the way, the truth, and the life: no man cometh unto the Father, but by me. There is one way to God. Notice with a sin offering. Aaron could not come empty-handed. He needed a sacrifice for his own sin. No one enters God's presence on his own merit. Notice the burnt offering too. Cleansing and dedication. Today, come to God His way, through Christ. Do not invent your own path. He is the way.
S: Aaron was to come into the holy place with a young bullock for a sin offering and a ram for a burnt offering. There was one right way to approach God, with a sacrifice. Come to God His way, through Christ, who is the way, the truth and the life.
P: Thus shall Aaron come. Leviticus 16:3: 'with a young bullock for a sin offering.' There is one way to the Father. Come through Christ today.

== 16:4
T: The holy linen coat
R: 'He shall put on the holy linen coat, and he shall have the linen breeches upon his flesh, and shall be girded with a linen girdle, and with the linen mitre shall he be attired: these are holy garments.' On this day, Aaron did not wear his glorious robes. He wore simple white linen garments, after bathing. Notice the simple linen. The gold, the jewels, the colors were set aside. On the Day of Atonement, the high priest dressed humbly. He came before God not in glory but in plain white. This pictures Christ. Philippians 2 says He made himself of no reputation, and took upon him the form of a servant. Christ laid aside His glory to make atonement. He came in humility. Notice wash his flesh. Aaron bathed before dressing. Purity was required. Christ was pure without needing washing. Today, marvel at Christ's humility. He laid aside His glory to make atonement for you.
S: Aaron put on the holy linen coat, breeches, girdle and mitre after washing, setting aside his glorious garments. On the Day of Atonement the high priest came humbly, picturing Christ laying aside His glory. Marvel at Christ's humility, who laid aside His glory to make atonement for you.
P: Plain linen. Leviticus 16:4: 'He shall put on the holy linen coat.' The high priest set aside his glory. Christ made Himself of no reputation for you.

== 16:5
T: Two kids of the goats
R: 'And he shall take of the congregation of the children of Israel two kids of the goats for a sin offering, and one ram for a burnt offering.' From the people, Aaron took two male goats for a sin offering and one ram for a burnt offering. Notice two goats. Both together made one sin offering. One would die; one would be sent away. Two goats were needed to show the full meaning of atonement. One showed sin paid for. The other showed sin removed. Both are true in Christ. Romans 3 says He is a propitiation through faith in his blood. That is the first goat. Psalm 103 says, As far as the east is from the west, so far hath he removed our transgressions from us. That is the second. Notice of the congregation. The people provided the goats. The atonement was for them. Today, rejoice in both truths. Your sin is paid for, and your sin is removed. Christ has done both.
S: Aaron took from the congregation two kids of the goats for a sin offering and one ram for a burnt offering. The two goats together showed sin both paid for and removed. Rejoice that in Christ your sin is both paid for and carried away.
P: Two goats, one atonement. Leviticus 16:5: 'two kids of the goats for a sin offering.' Sin paid for and sin removed. Christ has done both for you.

== 16:6
T: For himself and his house
R: 'And Aaron shall offer his bullock of the sin offering, which is for himself, and make an atonement for himself, and for his house.' Aaron first offered the bull as a sin offering for himself and his household. Notice for himself. Even on the holiest day, the high priest needed atonement first. He was a sinner like everyone else. His own sin had to be covered before he could help others. Notice for his house. Aaron's family, still grieving the loss of two sons, needed atonement too. Hebrews 7 says Christ is different: Who needeth not daily, as those high priests, to offer up sacrifice, first for his own sins. Jesus had no sin to atone for. He is the perfect High Priest. Hebrews 7 says He is holy, harmless, undefiled, separate from sinners. Today, thank God that your High Priest needed no sacrifice for Himself. His whole sacrifice was for you.
S: Aaron offered his bullock for a sin offering for himself and his house. Even the high priest needed atonement first, but Christ needed no sacrifice for Himself. Thank God that your sinless High Priest offered His whole sacrifice for you.
P: For himself first. Leviticus 16:6: 'make an atonement for himself, and for his house.' Aaron needed atonement; Christ did not. His whole sacrifice was for you.

== 16:7
T: Before the LORD
R: 'And he shall take the two goats, and present them before the LORD at the door of the tabernacle of the congregation.' Aaron took the two goats and presented them before the LORD at the tabernacle entrance. Notice present them before the LORD. Both goats stood before God. They were set apart for His purpose. The people could see them. The drama of atonement was public. Everyone knew what was happening. Notice that the two goats looked the same. Nothing distinguished them yet. Their roles would be determined by God. This reminds us that God's purposes are His to decide. Proverbs 16 says, The lot is cast into the lap; but the whole disposing thereof is of the LORD. Notice at the door. The goats stood where all could see. Atonement was not secret. Christ's sacrifice was public too, on a hill outside Jerusalem. Today, look to Christ's public sacrifice. He was presented before God and the world for you.
S: Aaron presented the two goats before the LORD at the door of the tabernacle. Atonement was public, and God would determine each goat's role. Look to Christ's public sacrifice, presented before God and the world for you.

== 16:8
T: Lots for the two goats
R: 'And Aaron shall cast lots upon the two goats; one lot for the LORD, and the other lot for the scapegoat.' Aaron cast lots over the two goats. One was chosen for the LORD; the other was chosen as the scapegoat. Notice cast lots. Aaron did not choose. God chose through the lots. The roles were God's decision. Atonement was not designed by human preference. It was God's plan from beginning to end. Acts 2 says Jesus was delivered by the determinate counsel and foreknowledge of God. The cross was God's plan. Notice the scapegoat. The Hebrew word is Azazel, often understood as the goat that goes away. This is where our word scapegoat comes from. One goat would carry the sins away. Today, rest in God's plan of salvation. It was His design, not an accident. Christ's death was planned in love before the world began.
S: Aaron cast lots upon the two goats, one for the LORD and one for the scapegoat. God, not Aaron, chose their roles, for atonement was His plan from beginning to end. Rest in God's plan of salvation, designed in love before the world began.
P: God's plan. Leviticus 16:8: 'one lot for the LORD, and the other lot for the scapegoat.' Atonement was God's design. Christ was delivered by His determinate counsel.

T: The LORD's lot
R: 'And Aaron shall bring the goat upon which the LORD'S lot fell, and offer him for a sin offering.' The goat chosen by lot for the LORD was offered as a sin offering. Notice the LORD'S lot. This goat belonged to God. Its blood would be brought into the Most Holy Place. It would die to satisfy God's justice. This goat pictures Christ's death as payment to God. Sin is an offense against God. It must be dealt with before Him. Romans 3 says God set forth Christ to be a propitiation… to declare his righteousness. God is both just and the justifier. His justice is satisfied, and His mercy is shown. Notice for a sin offering. The goat died for the people's sin. It took their place. Isaiah 53 says, the LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all. Today, thank God that His justice has been satisfied through Christ. Your sin has been dealt with before God.
S: The goat on which the LORD's lot fell was offered for a sin offering. It died to satisfy God's justice, picturing Christ as the propitiation for our sins. Thank God that His justice is satisfied through Christ and your sin has been dealt with before Him.

The other goat was presented alive before the LORD. It would be used for atonement and then sent into the wilderness as the scapegoat. Notice presented alive. This goat did not die at the altar. It lived and carried something away. Notice into the wilderness. The goat went far away, into a barren place. It did not come back. The sins it carried went with it. This is the picture of removal.
