# Leviticus 17: The life is in the blood

== 17:1
T: To all Israel
R: 'And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying.' God spoke to Moses again. A new section of Leviticus begins here, often called the Holiness Code. It runs through the chapters that follow. Notice the shift. Chapters 1 to 16 focused largely on the sanctuary and the priests. Now the focus widens to the daily life of every Israelite. Holiness moves from the tabernacle into the home, the field, and the community. Notice that God kept speaking. After the Day of Atonement, He did not fall silent. Forgiveness was not the end of the story. It was the beginning of a holy life. Titus 2 says the grace of God teaches us that, denying ungodliness and worldly lusts, we should live soberly, righteously, and godly. Grace leads to holy living. Today, let God's forgiveness lead you into a holy life. Atonement is the beginning, not the end, of your walk with Him.
S: The LORD spoke to Moses again, beginning the section often called the Holiness Code. After the Day of Atonement, holiness moved from the sanctuary into everyday life. Let God's forgiveness lead you into a holy life, for atonement is the beginning of your walk with Him.
P: Grace teaches holiness. Leviticus 17:1: 'And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying.' Forgiveness is the beginning, not the end. Let grace shape your daily life.

== 17:2
T: To Aaron and all Israel
R: 'Speak unto Aaron, and unto his sons, and unto all the children of Israel, and say unto them; This is the thing which the LORD hath commanded.' This message was for everyone: Aaron, his sons, and all the people. It was what the LORD commanded. Notice unto all. Priests and people heard the same word. God's commands were not secret knowledge for a few. They were public truth for everyone. Every Israelite was responsible to know and obey. This is still true. God's word is for all His people. Colossians 3 says, Let the word of Christ dwell in you richly. Not just in pastors, but in every believer. Notice this is the thing which the LORD hath commanded. The authority was clear. It came from God. Moses was only the messenger. When we read Scripture, we hear what the LORD has commanded. Today, take God's word as addressed to you personally. It is not only for leaders. Let it dwell in you richly.
S: God's message was spoken to Aaron, his sons and all the children of Israel as what the LORD commanded. God's word was public truth for every person, not secret knowledge for a few. Take God's word as addressed to you and let it dwell in you richly.
P: For all of us. Leviticus 17:2: 'unto Aaron… and unto all the children of Israel.' God's word is for every believer. Let it dwell in you richly today.

== 17:3
T: In the camp or out
R: 'What man soever there be of the house of Israel, that killeth an ox, or lamb, or goat, in the camp, or that killeth it out of the camp.' This concerned any Israelite who slaughtered an ox, lamb, or goat, whether inside or outside the camp. Notice in the camp, or… out of the camp. Location did not matter. Wherever an animal was killed, the same rule applied. There was no private space outside God's concern. Psalm 139 says, If I ascend up into heaven, thou art there: if I make my bed in hell, behold, thou art there. God is present everywhere. Notice the domestic animals. Ox, lamb, goat. These were the animals of sacrifice. In the wilderness, killing them was connected with worship. Every life taken was a matter before God. Today, remember that God is present in every place you go. There is no corner of life outside His concern.
S: Any Israelite who killed an ox, lamb or goat, in the camp or out of it, was addressed by this law. Location did not matter, for no space was outside God's concern. Remember that God is present everywhere you go and every part of your life matters to Him.
P: Everywhere. Leviticus 17:3: 'in the camp, or that killeth it out of the camp.' There's no corner outside God's concern. He is present wherever you go.

== 17:4
T: Blood shall be imputed
R: 'And bringeth it not unto the door of the tabernacle of the congregation, to offer an offering unto the LORD… blood shall be imputed unto that man; he hath shed blood.' Anyone who killed such an animal without bringing it to the tabernacle as an offering would be guilty of bloodshed and cut off. Notice blood shall be imputed. Killing an animal outside God's appointed place was treated as shedding blood wrongly. Life was so sacred that even an animal's death had to be brought before God. This taught deep respect for life. Life is not cheap. It belongs to God. Genesis 9 says, at the hand of every beast will I require it. God takes account of life. Notice the door of the tabernacle. God's people were to bring their sacrifices to one place. Worship was centralized. This protected them from scattering into idolatry, as the following verses show. Today, treat life as sacred. Honor God as the giver of every life.
S: Anyone who killed such an animal and did not bring it to the door of the tabernacle had blood imputed to him. Even an animal's life was sacred and had to be brought before God. Treat every life as sacred and honor God as the giver of life.
P: Life is sacred. Leviticus 17:4: 'blood shall be imputed unto that man; he hath shed blood.' Life belongs to God. Honor Him as the giver of every life today.

== 17:5
T: Unto the LORD
R: 'To the end that the children of Israel may bring their sacrifices, which they offer in the open field, even that they may bring them unto the LORD, unto the door of the tabernacle… and offer them for peace offerings unto the LORD.' The purpose was to make Israel bring sacrifices they had been offering in the open fields to the LORD at the tabernacle, as peace offerings. Notice in the open field. Israel had been offering sacrifices wherever they pleased. That was dangerous. Unregulated worship easily drifted into false worship. God called them to bring it to Him, at His place, through His priest. Notice peace offerings. Their meals would become fellowship with God. Ordinary eating became communion. What was done carelessly in the fields became worship at God's door. John 4 says true worshippers shall worship the Father in spirit and in truth. Worship must be directed to the true God, in His way. Today, bring your worship to God His way. Turn ordinary moments into fellowship with Him.
S: Israel was to bring the sacrifices they had offered in the open field to the LORD at the tabernacle as peace offerings. Unregulated worship drifted easily, so God gathered it to Himself and turned meals into fellowship. Bring your worship to God His way and turn ordinary moments into fellowship with Him.
P: Bring it to Him. Leviticus 17:5: 'that they may bring them unto the LORD.' Don't let worship drift. Direct it to the true God, in spirit and in truth.

== 17:6
T: A sweet savour
R: 'And the priest shall sprinkle the blood upon the altar of the LORD at the door of the tabernacle of the congregation, and burn the fat for a sweet savour unto the LORD.' At the tabernacle, the priest would sprinkle the blood on the LORD's altar and burn the fat as a pleasing aroma to Him. Notice the altar of the LORD. When the people brought their animals to the tabernacle, the blood went to God's altar. It was used rightly. In the open fields, it may have gone to idols. Now it went to the LORD. Notice sweet savour. God was pleased. What could have been idolatry became acceptable worship. The same animal, brought to the right place, pleased God. Our lives are the same. Brought to God, they please Him. Romans 12 says, present your bodies a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable unto God. Today, bring your life to God's altar. What you give Him becomes a sweet savour.
S: The priest sprinkled the blood on the LORD's altar and burned the fat for a sweet savour. What might have gone to idols in the fields became acceptable worship. Bring your life to God's altar, for what you give Him becomes a sweet savour.
P: A sweet savour. Leviticus 17:6: 'burn the fat for a sweet savour unto the LORD.' Brought to God, your life pleases Him. Present yourself to Him today.

== 17:7
T: No more unto devils
R: 'And they shall no more offer their sacrifices unto devils, after whom they have gone a whoring. This shall be a statute for ever unto them throughout their generations.' Israel was to stop offering sacrifices to goat-demons, which they had been chasing after like prostitutes. This was a lasting command. Notice unto devils. Some Israelites had been offering sacrifices in the fields to demonic spirits, possibly goat-idols. Idolatry was a real danger. Spiritual unfaithfulness was happening. Notice gone a whoring. God described idolatry as unfaithfulness in marriage. Israel was His bride. Worshiping other gods was betrayal. First Corinthians 10 says, the things which the Gentiles sacrifice, they sacrifice to devils… I would not that ye should have fellowship with devils. Ye cannot drink the cup of the Lord, and the cup of devils. Today, examine your heart for anything you serve other than God. Be faithful to Him alone, as a loving spouse.
S: Israel was to no more offer sacrifices unto devils, after whom they had gone a whoring. God described idolatry as betrayal of a marriage covenant. Examine your heart for anything you serve besides God and be faithful to Him alone.
P: Faithful to Him alone. Leviticus 17:7: 'they shall no more offer their sacrifices unto devils.' Idolatry is betrayal. Ye cannot drink the cup of the Lord and of devils.

== 17:8
T: Or of the strangers
R: 'And thou shalt say unto them, Whatsoever man there be of the house of Israel, or of the strangers which sojourn among you, that offereth a burnt offering of sacrifice.' This law applied to Israelites and to foreigners living among them who offered burnt offerings or sacrifices. Notice or of the strangers. Foreigners were included. They too could worship the LORD. They too were held to the same standard. God welcomed outsiders into His worship. Exodus 12 had already allowed strangers to keep Passover if they were circumcised. Israel's God was not only a tribal deity. He was the God of all who would come to Him. Isaiah 56 says, mine house shall be called an house of prayer for all people. Jesus quoted this in Mark 11. God's house is for all nations. Notice the same standard. Welcome did not mean a lower standard. Strangers worshiped God's way too. Today, welcome outsiders into God's worship. And remember that His standards apply equally to all who come.
S: The law applied to Israelites and to the strangers who sojourned among them who offered burnt offerings. Foreigners were welcomed into worship and held to the same standard. Welcome outsiders into God's worship, remembering that His house is a house of prayer for all people.
P: A house of prayer for all. Leviticus 17:8: 'or of the strangers which sojourn among you.' God welcomes outsiders. Welcome them into worship today.

== 17:9
T: Cut off
R: 'And bringeth it not unto the door of the tabernacle of the congregation, to offer it unto the LORD; even that man shall be cut off from among his people.' Anyone who offered a sacrifice without bringing it to the tabernacle would be cut off from his people. Notice cut off. The penalty was serious. Offering sacrifices elsewhere opened the door to idolatry. It also rejected God's appointed way. God guarded the purity of worship strongly. Notice unto the LORD. The issue was not where, in the end, but to whom. Bringing it to the tabernacle ensured it went to the LORD. Worship apart from God's way could easily be worship of something else. Acts 4 says, Neither is there salvation in any other: for there is none other name under heaven given among men, whereby we must be saved. There is one way to God. Today, come to God through the one way He has provided. Do not create your own paths to Him.
S: Whoever did not bring his sacrifice to the door of the tabernacle to offer it unto the LORD was cut off. God guarded worship strongly, for any other way risked idolatry. Come to God through the one way He has provided in Christ.
P: One way. Leviticus 17:9: 'bringeth it not unto the door of the tabernacle… shall be cut off.' There is none other name whereby we must be saved. Come through Christ.

== 17:10
T: I will set my face
R: 'And whatsoever man there be of the house of Israel, or of the strangers that sojourn among you, that eateth any manner of blood; I will even set my face against that soul that eateth blood.' God said He would set His face against anyone, Israelite or foreigner, who ate blood, and would cut him off. Notice I will set my face against. This is strong language. God personally opposed those who ate blood. It was not just a rule. It touched something close to God's heart. Why? Because the blood was given for atonement, as the next verse explains. Eating it treated atonement as common. Notice the contrast with the priestly blessing in Numbers 6: The LORD make his face shine upon thee. God's face can shine in favor or be set in opposition. It depends on how we treat what He holds holy. Hebrews 10 warns against counting the blood of the covenant an unholy thing. Today, treat Christ's blood as holy. Live under the shining of God's face, not His opposition.
S: God said He would set His face against any soul, Israelite or stranger, who ate any manner of blood. The blood was given for atonement, and treating it as common provoked His opposition. Treat Christ's blood as holy and live under the shining of God's face.
P: His face. Leviticus 17:10: 'I will even set my face against that soul that eateth blood.' God holds atonement holy. Live under the shining of His face today.

== 17:11
T: The life is in the blood
R: 'For the life of the flesh is in the blood: and I have given it to you upon the altar to make an atonement for your souls: for it is the blood that maketh an atonement for the soul.' God explained why. Life is in the blood. He had given blood on the altar to make atonement for their souls. The blood atones for the soul. Notice the life of the flesh is in the blood. A substitute's life was given for the sinner's. Notice I have given it to you. Atonement was God's gift. God provided it out of mercy. Notice it is the blood that maketh an atonement. This is one of the most important verses in the Bible. Hebrews 9 says, without shedding of blood is no remission. First Peter 1 says we were redeemed with the precious blood of Christ. Christ gave His life, His blood, for our souls. Today, thank God for the gift of atonement. The life of Christ was given for yours.
S: God explained that the life of the flesh is in the blood, given on the altar to make atonement for souls. Atonement was God's gift, a life given for a life, fulfilled in Christ's precious blood. Thank God for the gift of atonement, for Christ gave His life for yours.
P: The life is in the blood. Leviticus 17:11: 'it is the blood that maketh an atonement for the soul.' Christ gave His life for yours. Thank Him today.

== 17:12
T: Therefore I said
R: 'Therefore I said unto the children of Israel, No soul of you shall eat blood, neither shall any stranger that sojourneth among you eat blood.' Because blood makes atonement, God commanded that no Israelite or foreigner should eat blood. Notice therefore. The command was based on a reason. God did not give arbitrary rules. He explained. Because blood atones, it must not be eaten. Understanding the reason helps us obey from the heart. God invites understanding. Isaiah 1 says, Come now, and let us reason together. God reasons with His people. Notice no soul… neither… any stranger. Everyone, without exception. The holiness of blood applied to all. Acts 15 shows the early church asking Gentile believers to abstain from blood, honoring this ancient principle. Today, seek to understand the reasons behind God's commands. Obey not only because He says so but because you understand His heart.
S: Because the blood makes atonement, God said no Israelite or stranger should eat blood. God gave His reason so that obedience could come from understanding. Seek to understand the reasons behind God's commands and obey from the heart.
P: Therefore. Leviticus 17:12: 'Therefore I said… No soul of you shall eat blood.' God gives reasons. Come, let us reason together. Obey from understanding today.

== 17:13
T: Cover it with dust
R: 'And whatsoever man there be of the children of Israel, or of the strangers… which hunteth and catcheth any beast or fowl that may be eaten; he shall even pour out the blood thereof, and cover it with dust.' A hunter who caught a clean animal or bird had to drain its blood and cover it with dirt. Notice pour out… and cover it with dust. Even in the wild, far from the altar, blood was treated with respect. It was not left exposed. It was poured out and buried. This was like a burial. Life was honored even when the animal was killed for food. Notice hunteth. Hunting was not forbidden. God allowed it. But reverence went with it. Gratitude and respect accompanied the taking of life. First Timothy 4 says, every creature of God is good, and nothing to be refused, if it be received with thanksgiving. Today, receive your food with thanksgiving. Show respect for the life that sustains you.
S: A hunter who caught a clean beast or fowl had to pour out its blood and cover it with dust. Even in the wild, life was honored with reverence, as in a burial. Receive your food with thanksgiving and respect for the life that sustains you.
P: Reverence in the field. Leviticus 17:13: 'he shall even pour out the blood thereof, and cover it with dust.' Receive every good gift with thanksgiving today.

== 17:14
T: The life of all flesh
R: 'For it is the life of all flesh; the blood of it is for the life thereof: therefore I said unto the children of Israel, Ye shall eat the blood of no manner of flesh: for the life of all flesh is the blood thereof.' God repeated: the life of every creature is its blood. Therefore no one should eat blood of any creature. Notice the repetition. God restated the principle again. Life is in the blood. He wanted it deeply understood. Notice the life of all flesh. Every creature's life is sacred to God. God cares for His creation. Psalm 36 says, O LORD, thou preservest man and beast. Jonah 4 shows God's concern for Nineveh's people and also much cattle. God values life. Notice the connection to Christ. When Christ's blood was shed, His life was poured out. John 19 says blood and water came from His side. His life was given for ours. Today, value life as God does. And thank Christ, whose life-blood was poured out for you.
S: God repeated that the life of all flesh is in the blood, so no blood of any flesh was to be eaten. Every creature's life is sacred to God, and Christ's life-blood was poured out for us. Value life as God does and thank Christ for pouring out His life for you.
P: All life is sacred. Leviticus 17:14: 'the life of all flesh is the blood thereof.' God preserves man and beast. Thank Christ, whose life was poured out for you.

== 17:15
T: Then shall he be clean
R: 'And every soul that eateth that which died of itself, or that which was torn with beasts… he shall both wash his clothes, and bathe himself in water, and be unclean until the even: then shall he be clean.' Anyone who ate meat from an animal that died naturally or was killed by wild animals had to wash his clothes, bathe, and be unclean until evening. Then he was clean. Notice then shall he be clean. A clear path back. The person who ate improperly drained meat was not cut off. He washed, waited, and was restored. Perhaps poverty or hunger drove someone to eat such meat. God provided a simple remedy. He did not punish need harshly. Notice his own country, or a stranger. Same rule, same mercy for all. Psalm 103 says, He hath not dealt with us after our sins. God's mercy is generous. Today, if you have stumbled through weakness or need, follow God's path back. He makes the way to cleansing clear and simple.
S: Whoever ate what died of itself or was torn washed his clothes, bathed and was unclean until the even, then clean. God provided a simple path back, perhaps for those driven by hunger or need. If you have stumbled through weakness, follow God's clear and simple path back to cleansing.
P: Then shall he be clean. Leviticus 17:15: 'he shall both wash his clothes… then shall he be clean.' God makes the way back simple. Follow it today.

== 17:16
T: If he wash not
R: 'But if he wash them not, nor bathe his flesh; then he shall bear his iniquity.' But if he did not wash his clothes and bathe, he would bear his guilt. Notice if he wash them not. The remedy was available, but he had to use it. Refusing cleansing left him guilty. God's provision does not help us if we ignore it. Grace must be received. This is sobering. Hebrews 2 asks, How shall we escape, if we neglect so great salvation? The gift is free, but it must be received. Notice bear his iniquity. The guilt stayed with him. Not because God was harsh, but because the person refused the way out. John 3 says, he that believeth not is condemned already. Rejection, not lack of provision, is the problem. Today, do not neglect God's provision. Receive the cleansing He offers. It is freely available, but you must come.
S: If he did not wash his clothes or bathe, he would bear his iniquity. The remedy was available but had to be used, as God's free salvation must be received. Do not neglect God's provision but receive the cleansing He freely offers.
