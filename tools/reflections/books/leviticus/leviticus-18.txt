# Leviticus 18: Holiness in sexual life and the family

== 18:1
T: A word about the family
R: 'And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying.' God spoke to Moses about how His people must live in their closest relationships. This chapter deals with sexual conduct and family boundaries. Notice that God addressed intimate matters. Sexuality is not outside His concern. He created it. He also set its boundaries. Genesis 2 shows God bringing the woman to the man and establishing marriage. God designed intimacy for a covenant between husband and wife. Notice that these laws protected families. Many of the commands in this chapter guard against abuse within households. Vulnerable people, especially women and children, were protected. Psalm 68 says God is a father of the fatherless, and a judge of the widows… God setteth the solitary in families. God cares about safe, healthy families. Today, thank God for His design of family and sexuality. Ask Him to help you honor His good boundaries in your relationships.
S: The LORD spoke to Moses about holiness in sexual conduct and family relationships. God created sexuality and set its boundaries, protecting families and the vulnerable. Thank God for His design of family and sexuality and ask Him to help you honor His boundaries.
P: God's design. Leviticus 18:1: 'And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying.' God created intimacy and set its boundaries to protect families. Honor His design today.

== 18:2
T: I am the LORD your God
R: 'Speak unto the children of Israel, and say unto them, I am the LORD your God.' God began with a simple statement of who He is: I am the LORD your God. Notice that the commands begin with relationship. Before telling Israel what to do, God reminded them whose they were. Obedience flows from belonging. They were His people, and He was their God. This phrase appears again and again in this chapter. It anchors every command. Rules mean little without relationship. But relationship gives rules meaning. We obey because we belong to Him. First Corinthians 6 says, ye are not your own? For ye are bought with a price: therefore glorify God in your body. Our bodies belong to God because He bought us. Today, remember whose you are. Let every choice about your body and relationships flow from belonging to the LORD your God.
S: God began by declaring, I am the LORD your God. The commands flowed from relationship, for Israel belonged to Him. Remember whose you are and let every choice about your body flow from belonging to God.
P: Whose you are. Leviticus 18:2: 'I am the LORD your God.' Ye are not your own; ye are bought with a price. Glorify God in your body today.

== 18:3
T: Not after Egypt or Canaan
R: 'After the doings of the land of Egypt, wherein ye dwelt, shall ye not do: and after the doings of the land of Canaan, whither I bring you, shall ye not do: neither shall ye walk in their ordinances.' Israel was not to imitate Egypt, where they had lived, or Canaan, where they were going. They were not to follow those nations' customs. Notice Egypt behind, Canaan ahead. Israel was caught between two cultures. Israel had to be different from both their past and their future surroundings. We face the same challenge. The culture we come from and the culture around us both pull at us. Romans 12 says, be not conformed to this world: but be ye transformed by the renewing of your mind. God calls us to be distinct. Notice neither shall ye walk in their ordinances. Not just avoiding specific acts, but rejecting the whole way of life. Today, ask where you are being shaped by your culture rather than by God. Choose to be transformed, not conformed.
S: Israel was not to follow the doings of Egypt behind them or Canaan ahead of them. They were to be distinct from both their past and their surroundings. Ask where your culture is shaping you more than God and choose to be transformed, not conformed.
P: Not conformed. Leviticus 18:3: 'After the doings of the land of Egypt… shall ye not do.' Be not conformed to this world, but transformed. Choose God's way today.

He is the LORD their God. Notice the positive command. Not only avoid the nations' ways but follow God's ways. Holiness is not only saying no. It is saying yes to something better. God gave them a path to walk. Notice to walk therein. God's laws were a way of life, a path for daily walking. Psalm 119 says, Thy word is a lamp unto my feet, and a light unto my path. God's word lights the way step by step. Notice I am the LORD your God again. Every command rested on this foundation. Today, do not just avoid what is wrong. Walk actively in God's ways. Let His word be a lamp to your feet.
S: Israel was to do God's judgments and keep His ordinances, walking in them, for He is the LORD their God. Holiness was not only avoiding wrong but walking in a better way. Walk actively in God's ways and let His word light your path.

== 18:5
T: He shall live in them
R: 'Ye shall therefore keep my statutes, and my judgments: which if a man do, he shall live in them: I am the LORD.' Israel was to keep God's decrees and laws, for whoever obeys them will live by them. He is the LORD. Notice he shall live in them. God's laws were life-giving. Following them led to flourishing. They were not designed to crush but to bless. God's commands are for our good. Deuteronomy 10 says, keep the commandments of the LORD… for thy good. Notice also how Paul uses this verse. Romans 10 and Galatians 3 quote it to show that no one perfectly keeps the law. If life depends on perfect obedience, all fall short. That is why we need Christ. He kept the law perfectly and gives His righteousness to us. Romans 8 says, what the law could not do… God sending his own Son… condemned sin in the flesh. Today, see God's commands as life-giving. And rest in Christ, who kept them perfectly for you.

== 18:6
T: Near of kin
R: 'None of you shall approach to any that is near of kin to him, to uncover their nakedness: I am the LORD.' No one was to have sexual relations with a close relative. God is the LORD. Notice near of kin. This is the general principle for the verses that follow. Family relationships were to be safe. Those closest to us must never be treated as objects of desire. The home must be a place of safety, not exploitation. Notice that this protected the vulnerable. In ancient households, women and children often lived under the power of older men. These laws drew firm lines. God defended the weak within families. Sadly, abuse within families still happens today. God's word stands against it. Psalm 10 says God is the helper of the fatherless. Today, pray for families to be places of safety. If you have been hurt by someone close, know that God stands with you and against what was done.
S: No one was to approach any near of kin to uncover their nakedness, for He is the LORD. The home was to be a place of safety, with firm lines protecting the vulnerable. Pray for families to be safe places, and if you have been hurt by someone close, know God stands with you.
P: Safe families. Leviticus 18:6: 'None of you shall approach to any that is near of kin to him.' God guards the vulnerable at home. He stands with those who were hurt.

== 18:7
T: She is thy mother
R: 'The nakedness of thy father, or the nakedness of thy mother, shalt thou not uncover: she is thy mother; thou shalt not uncover her nakedness.' A son was never to dishonor his father or mother in this way. Notice she is thy mother. God named the relationship to make the wrongness plain. A mother is to be honored, not violated. The relationship itself carries sacred boundaries. Notice the link with the fifth commandment. Exodus 20 says, Honour thy father and thy mother. Honor includes protecting their dignity. Genesis 9 tells of Ham, who looked on his father's nakedness and told his brothers. Shem and Japheth covered their father without looking. They protected his dignity. Honor covers; dishonor exposes. Ephesians 6 says this is the first commandment with promise. Today, honor your parents. Protect their dignity, cover their weaknesses, and treat them with respect.
S: A son was never to uncover the nakedness of his father or mother, for she is his mother. Relationships carry sacred boundaries, and honoring parents includes protecting their dignity. Honor your parents, protecting their dignity and treating them with respect.

== 18:8
T: Thy father's wife
R: 'The nakedness of thy father's wife shalt thou not uncover: it is thy father's nakedness.' A man was not to have relations with his father's wife, even if she was not his mother. Notice it is thy father's nakedness. Violating her was violating the father. Family relationships are connected. An offense against one is an offense against the other. Reuben did this in Genesis 35 with Bilhah, his father's concubine. Genesis 49 records that he lost his birthright because of it. Unstable as water, thou shalt not excel. First Corinthians 5 addresses this very sin in the church: that one should have his father's wife. Paul called the church to deal with it seriously. Yet Second Corinthians 2 shows that when the man repented, Paul urged them to forgive him, and comfort him. Today, honor family boundaries. And remember that even serious sin can be forgiven when there is true repentance.
S: A man was not to uncover the nakedness of his father's wife, for it was his father's nakedness. Reuben lost his birthright for this sin, yet Paul urged forgiveness for one who repented of it. Honor family boundaries and remember that serious sin can be forgiven through true repentance.

Tamar pleaded, do not thou this folly. He refused to listen. She remained desolate in her brother's house. Her life was devastated. This story shows the real harm these laws prevented. God's boundaries protect from devastating pain. Notice that Scripture tells Tamar's story honestly. God sees the victims. Psalm 34 says, The LORD is nigh unto them that are of a broken heart. Today, pray for those harmed within families. And honor brothers and sisters with protective love.

== 18:23
T: It is confusion
R: 'Neither shalt thou lie with any beast to defile thyself therewith: neither shall any woman stand before a beast to lie down thereto: it is confusion.' No man or woman was to have relations with an animal. It was a perversion. Notice it is confusion. The Hebrew suggests mixing what should be separate. It violated the order of creation. Genesis 1 shows God creating humans in His image, distinct from animals. Humans were given dominion over creatures, not union with them. This sin erased that distinction. Notice that both men and women were addressed. The command applied to all. Notice how far human sin can go.
