# Leviticus 19: Be holy, love your neighbour

== 19:1
T: Another word
R: 'And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying.' God spoke to Moses again. What follows is one of the richest chapters in Leviticus, full of practical commands for daily life. Notice the variety ahead. Parents, sabbath, idols, harvests, honesty, the deaf, the blind, justice, gossip, love, strangers, and fair weights. Holiness touches everything. There is no separation between religious life and ordinary life. Notice that God spoke these words. They are not merely good advice. They carry divine authority. Jesus quoted from this chapter when He named the second greatest commandment. James quoted it when speaking of the royal law. Peter quoted its opening call to holiness. This chapter shaped the New Testament. Second Timothy 3 says, All scripture is… profitable for doctrine, for reproof, for correction, for instruction in righteousness. Today, open your heart to God's practical word. Let it shape every part of your ordinary life.
S: The LORD spoke to Moses again, introducing a chapter rich in practical commands for daily life. Its words shaped the teaching of Jesus, James and Peter. Open your heart to God's practical word and let it shape every part of your ordinary life.
P: Holiness in everything. Leviticus 19:1: 'And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying.' This chapter shaped the New Testament. Let it shape your daily life today.

== 19:2
T: Ye shall be holy
R: 'Speak unto all the congregation of the children of Israel, and say unto them, Ye shall be holy: for I the LORD your God am holy.' God told Moses to speak to the whole community: be holy, because I, the LORD your God, am holy. Notice all the congregation. Holiness was not only for priests. Every Israelite was called to be holy. Holiness is for everyone. Notice for I… am holy. The reason is God's own character. We are to be like Him. Children resemble their father. God's people should reflect His nature. First Peter 1 quotes this verse: But as he which hath called you is holy, so be ye holy in all manner of conversation. Peter applies it to every believer and every area of life. Notice what follows in this chapter. Holiness is not mysterious. It looks like honoring parents, caring for the poor, telling the truth, and loving neighbors. Holiness is practical. Today, hear God's call to be holy. Reflect His character in your ordinary choices.
S: God told all the congregation, Ye shall be holy, for I the LORD your God am holy. Holiness was for every Israelite and looks like practical love and truth in daily life. Hear God's call to be holy and reflect His character in your ordinary choices.
P: Be holy. Leviticus 19:2: 'Ye shall be holy: for I the LORD your God am holy.' Holiness is practical. Reflect your Father's character in ordinary choices today.

== 19:3
T: Fear thy mother and father
R: 'Ye shall fear every man his mother, and his father, and keep my sabbaths: I am the LORD your God.' Each person was to respect his mother and father and keep God's sabbaths. Notice that holiness begins at home. The first practical command of holiness concerns parents. How we treat our families reveals our holiness. Notice mother first. Here the mother is named before the father, unlike the fifth commandment. Both parents deserve equal honor. Notice fear. This means reverent respect, not terror. Honoring parents is a lifelong duty. Ephesians 6 says, Honour thy father and mother; which is the first commandment with promise. Notice keep my sabbaths alongside it. Family and worship were linked. Rest and honor belong together. Today, show respect to your parents. Honor them in your words and actions. And keep a rhythm of rest and worship before God.
S: Every man was to fear his mother and father and keep God's sabbaths. Holiness began at home with honoring both parents, joined with rest and worship. Show respect to your parents and keep a rhythm of rest and worship before God.
P: Holiness starts at home. Leviticus 19:3: 'Ye shall fear every man his mother, and his father.' Honor your parents and keep God's rest today.

== 19:4
T: Turn ye not unto idols
R: 'Turn ye not unto idols, nor make to yourselves molten gods: I am the LORD your God.' Israel was not to turn to idols or make metal gods. The LORD was their God. Notice turn ye not. Idolatry begins with a turn. We look away from God toward something else. It is a direction of the heart before it is an act. Notice make to yourselves. Idols are human creations. We make them. Psalm 115 says, They have mouths, but they speak not: eyes have they, but they see not… They that make them are like unto them. We become like what we worship. Idols today may not be metal statues. Money, success, pleasure, and even relationships can become idols. Anything we trust more than God is an idol. First John 5 says, Little children, keep yourselves from idols. Today, examine where your heart is turning. Turn away from idols and toward the living God.
S: Israel was not to turn to idols or make molten gods, for the LORD was their God. Idolatry begins with a turn of the heart, and we become like what we worship. Examine where your heart is turning and turn away from idols to the living God.
P: Keep yourselves from idols. Leviticus 19:4: 'Turn ye not unto idols.' Anything trusted more than God is an idol. Turn your heart to Him today.

== 19:5
T: At your own will
R: 'And if ye offer a sacrifice of peace offerings unto the LORD, ye shall offer it at your own will.' When offering a peace offering, Israel was to offer it in a way that would be accepted, freely and willingly. Notice at your own will. The phrase means so that it may be accepted for you. Worship had to be offered rightly to be accepted. But it also implies willingness. God wants free, glad worship. Forced worship is not true worship. Second Corinthians 9 says, not grudgingly, or of necessity: for God loveth a cheerful giver. Psalm 54 says, I will freely sacrifice unto thee. God delights in willing hearts. Notice peace offerings. These were fellowship offerings. Fellowship cannot be forced. It must be wanted. God desires a relationship freely chosen. Today, bring God willing worship. Come to Him gladly, not out of duty alone.
S: A peace offering was to be offered at one's own will, so that it would be accepted. God desires free, glad worship and willing fellowship. Bring God willing worship, coming to Him gladly rather than out of duty alone.
P: Willing worship. Leviticus 19:5: 'ye shall offer it at your own will.' God loves a cheerful giver. Come to Him gladly and freely today.

== 19:6
T: The same day and the morrow
R: 'It shall be eaten the same day ye offer it, and on the morrow: and if ought remain until the third day, it shall be burnt in the fire.' The peace offering had to be eaten on the day it was offered or the next day. Anything left until the third day had to be burned. Notice the time limit. Fellowship meals were to be fresh. They were not to be stretched out. Freshness mattered in God's presence. Notice that the meat could not be hoarded. It had to be shared quickly. Large peace offerings meant inviting neighbors and the poor. Generosity was built into the timing. Deuteronomy 12 says, thou shalt rejoice before the LORD thy God… thou, and thy son, and thy daughter… and the Levite. Shared joy was the point. Today, share your blessings quickly. Do not hoard what God gives. Let fellowship with God overflow into fellowship with others.
S: The peace offering was eaten the same day and the morrow, and anything left until the third day was burned. Freshness mattered, and the time limit encouraged sharing with others. Share your blessings quickly and let fellowship with God overflow to others.
P: Share it fresh. Leviticus 19:6: 'It shall be eaten the same day ye offer it, and on the morrow.' Don't hoard blessings. Let fellowship overflow today.

== 19:7
T: It shall not be accepted
R: 'And if it be eaten at all on the third day, it is abominable; it shall not be accepted.' If any of the offering was eaten on the third day, it was detestable and would not be accepted. Notice it shall not be accepted. Disobeying the time limit spoiled the whole offering. Worship on our own terms is not acceptable. God sets the terms. Notice abominable. Strong language for eating stale meat. The seriousness was not about food but about disregarding God's word. Small disobedience revealed a heart that did not take God seriously. Luke 16 says, he that is unjust in the least is unjust also in much. Faithfulness in small things matters. Notice the link with chapter 7. This rule was repeated. God wanted it remembered. Today, take God's instructions seriously, even the small ones. Faithfulness in little things shows a heart that honors Him.
S: If any of the offering was eaten on the third day, it was abominable and not accepted. Disregarding God's word in small things revealed a careless heart. Take God's instructions seriously, even small ones, for faithfulness in little shows a heart that honors Him.

== 19:8
T: The hallowed thing
R: 'Therefore every one that eateth it shall bear his iniquity, because he hath profaned the hallowed thing of the LORD: and that soul shall be cut off from among his people.' Anyone who ate it on the third day would bear his guilt, because he had profaned what was holy to the LORD, and he would be cut off. Notice profaned the hallowed thing. The offering belonged to God. Treating it carelessly was profaning something holy. Holy things must be treated with reverence. Notice that ordinary actions can profane. Eating at the wrong time seemed small. But it showed disregard for what was sacred. Hebrews 12 warns against being like Esau, a profane person, who for one morsel of meat sold his birthright. Esau treated something holy as common. Notice bear his iniquity. Disregarding holiness had consequences. Today, treat holy things with reverence. God's word, His worship, and His people are hallowed. Do not treat them as common.
S: Whoever ate it on the third day bore his iniquity, for he had profaned the hallowed thing of the LORD. Treating something holy as common, like Esau, had consequences. Treat God's word, worship and people with reverence, never as common.
P: Hallowed things. Leviticus 19:8: 'he hath profaned the hallowed thing of the LORD.' Don't be like Esau. Treat holy things with reverence today.

== 19:9
T: The corners of thy field
R: 'And when ye reap the harvest of your land, thou shalt not wholly reap the corners of thy field, neither shalt thou gather the gleanings of thy harvest.' When harvesting, Israel was not to reap the edges of the fields or pick up what was dropped. Notice the corners. Farmers were to leave part of their harvest unreaped. Generosity was built into their work. They did not squeeze every last bit of profit. Notice the gleanings. What fell to the ground was to be left. The poor could come and gather it. This gave them dignity. They worked for their food rather than begging. Ruth 2 shows Ruth gleaning in Boaz's field. Boaz even told his workers to drop extra handfuls on purpose. Through this law, Ruth met Boaz and became an ancestor of David and of Jesus. Generosity opens doors to God's purposes. Today, leave the corners of your field. Build generosity into your work and income. Let the needy find dignity in your provision.
S: When reaping, Israel was not to wholly reap the corners of the field or gather the gleanings. Generosity was built into their work, giving the poor dignity, as with Ruth in Boaz's field. Build generosity into your work and income so that the needy find dignity in your provision.
P: Leave the corners. Leviticus 19:9: 'thou shalt not wholly reap the corners of thy field.' Through this law, Ruth met Boaz. Build generosity into your work today.

== 19:10
T: For the poor and stranger
R: 'And thou shalt not glean thy vineyard, neither shalt thou gather every grape of thy vineyard; thou shalt leave them for the poor and stranger: I am the LORD your God.' Israel was not to go over their vineyards a second time or pick up fallen grapes. These were for the poor and the foreigner. Notice the poor and stranger. Two vulnerable groups. Those without land and those without a homeland. God made provision for both. The stranger was not excluded. Immigrants and refugees were cared for in God's law. Notice I am the LORD your God. Generosity was grounded in who God is. He is generous; His people should be too. Proverbs 19 says, He that hath pity upon the poor lendeth unto the LORD; and that which he hath given will he pay him again. Giving to the poor is lending to God. Today, leave something for the poor and the stranger. Let God's generosity flow through you to the vulnerable.
S: Israel was not to glean the vineyard or gather every grape, but leave them for the poor and stranger. God provided for the landless and the foreigner, grounding generosity in His own character. Leave something for the poor and the stranger and let God's generosity flow through you.
P: For the poor and stranger. Leviticus 19:10: 'thou shalt leave them for the poor and stranger.' Pity on the poor is lending to the LORD. Give generously today.

== 19:11
T: Lie not one to another
R: 'Ye shall not steal, neither deal falsely, neither lie one to another.' Israel was not to steal, deceive, or lie to one another. Notice the three together. Stealing, false dealing, lying. They are connected. Theft often involves deception. Deception requires lies. One sin leads to another. Honesty must be complete. Notice one to another. The focus is on community. Lies damage trust between people. A community cannot thrive without truth. Ephesians 4 says, putting away lying, speak every man truth with his neighbour: for we are members one of another. We belong to each other, so we must be truthful. Notice deal falsely. This covers business, contracts, and promises. Integrity in daily dealings matters to God. Proverbs 12 says, Lying lips are abomination to the LORD: but they that deal truly are his delight. Today, be honest in everything. Speak truth, deal fairly, and respect what belongs to others.
S: Israel was not to steal, deal falsely or lie to one another. These connected sins destroy trust, while honesty builds community. Be honest in everything, speaking truth, dealing fairly and respecting what belongs to others.
P: Speak truth. Leviticus 19:11: 'Ye shall not steal, neither deal falsely, neither lie one to another.' We are members one of another. Be honest in everything today.

== 19:12
T: Swear not falsely
R: 'And ye shall not swear by my name falsely, neither shalt thou profane the name of thy God: I am the LORD.' Israel was not to swear falsely by God's name, for that would profane it. Notice by my name falsely. Using God's name to back up a lie was especially serious. It dragged God into deception. It made Him a witness to falsehood. Notice profane the name of thy God. God's name represents His character. Misusing it dishonors Him. Exodus 20 says, Thou shalt not take the name of the LORD thy God in vain. Notice that Jesus went further. Matthew 5 says, Swear not at all… But let your communication be, Yea, yea; Nay, nay. Believers should be so honest that oaths are unnecessary. Our simple word should be trustworthy. Today, honor God's name in your speech. Be so truthful that your yes means yes and your no means no.
S: Israel was not to swear falsely by God's name, which would profane it. Using His name to back a lie dishonored Him, and Jesus taught that our simple word should be trustworthy. Honor God's name in your speech and let your yes mean yes and your no mean no.

== 19:13
T: Pay wages promptly
R: 'Thou shalt not defraud thy neighbour, neither rob him: the wages of him that is hired shall not abide with thee all night until the morning.' Israel was not to cheat or rob their neighbor. They were to pay hired workers the same day. Notice the wages… not… all night. Day laborers lived hand to mouth. Withholding pay overnight could mean a family went hungry. God required prompt payment. Deuteronomy 24 says, for he is poor, and setteth his heart upon it. God cared about the worker's need. Notice defraud. Cheating workers is fraud. James 5 warns rich employers: the hire of the labourers… which is of you kept back by fraud, crieth: and the cries of them… are entered into the ears of the Lord. God hears unpaid workers. Notice thy neighbour. Workers are neighbors, not just labor. Today, treat those who work for you fairly. Pay promptly and justly. And if you are a worker, know that God sees your labor.
S: Israel was not to defraud or rob their neighbour, and hired workers were to be paid before morning. Day laborers depended on prompt pay, and God hears the cry of the unpaid. Treat those who work for you fairly and pay promptly, for God sees every worker.
P: Pay promptly. Leviticus 19:13: 'the wages of him that is hired shall not abide with thee all night.' God hears the cry of unpaid workers. Be fair and prompt today.

== 19:14
T: The deaf and the blind
R: 'Thou shalt not curse the deaf, nor put a stumblingblock before the blind, but shalt fear thy God: I am the LORD.' Israel was not to curse the deaf or put an obstacle in front of the blind. They were to fear God. Notice the deaf. They cannot hear the curse. Notice the blind. They cannot see the stumbling block. These sins target those who cannot defend themselves or even know they are being wronged. Only God sees. Notice but shalt fear thy God. Even if the victim cannot hear or see, God does. He is the defender of the vulnerable. Taking advantage of weakness is a sin against Him. Romans 14 says, that no man put a stumblingblock or an occasion to fall in his brother's way. Believers must not trip others spiritually either. Today, protect those who are weak or disabled. Treat them with dignity. Remember that God sees what others cannot.

== 19:15
T: In righteousness shalt thou judge
R: 'Ye shall do no unrighteousness in judgment: thou shalt not respect the person of the poor, nor honour the person of the mighty: but in righteousness shalt thou judge thy neighbour.' Israel was to judge fairly, not favoring the poor or showing preference to the powerful. Notice not… the poor, nor… the mighty. Justice must not be biased in either direction. Favoring the rich is unjust. But so is favoring the poor just because they are poor. Justice looks at the facts. Notice in righteousness. True justice is based on what is right, not on who is involved. James 2 warns against showing favoritism to the rich in church. Deuteronomy 1 says, ye shall hear the small as well as the great; ye shall not be afraid of the face of man; for the judgment is God's. Justice belongs to God. Today, judge fairly in every situation. Do not let wealth, poverty, or power sway you. Seek what is right.
S: Israel was to do no unrighteousness in judgment, favoring neither the poor nor the mighty, but judging in righteousness. True justice looks at the facts, not at who is involved. Judge fairly in every situation, letting neither wealth, poverty nor power sway you.
P: Impartial justice. Leviticus 19:15: 'thou shalt not respect the person of the poor, nor honour the person of the mighty.' The judgment is God's. Judge fairly today.

T: Not a talebearer
R: 'Thou shalt not go up and down as a talebearer among thy people: neither shalt thou stand against the blood of thy neighbour: I am the LORD.' Israel was not to spread slander among the people, nor do anything that endangered a neighbor's life. Notice talebearer. Gossip is spread person to person, up and down. It damages reputations and relationships. Proverbs 18 says, The words of a talebearer are as wounds. Proverbs 26 says, where there is no talebearer, the strife ceaseth. Gossip fuels conflict. Notice stand against the blood of thy neighbour. This could mean standing idle while a neighbor's life is in danger, or testifying falsely in a way that endangers him. Words can kill. Silence can also kill. James 3 says the tongue is a fire. Today, refuse to spread gossip. Guard your neighbor's reputation and life. Speak up when someone is in danger.
S: Israel was not to go about as a talebearer or stand against the blood of their neighbour. Gossip wounds, and words or silence can endanger a neighbour's life. Refuse to spread gossip and guard your neighbour's reputation and life.
P: No gossip. Leviticus 19:16: 'Thou shalt not go up and down as a talebearer.' Gossip wounds and fuels strife. Guard your neighbour's name today.

== 19:17
T: Rebuke, don't hate
R: 'Thou shalt not hate thy brother in thine heart: thou shalt in any wise rebuke thy neighbour, and not suffer sin upon him.' Israel was not to hate their brothers in their hearts. They were to rebuke their neighbor frankly, so they would not share in his guilt. Notice in thine heart. Hidden hatred is still sin. We can smile outwardly while resenting inwardly. God sees the heart. First John 3 says, Whosoever hateth his brother is a murderer. Notice rebuke thy neighbour. Instead of secret resentment, speak honestly. Address the problem directly. This is love, not hostility. Hiding resentment harms both people. Matthew 18 says, if thy brother shall trespass against thee, go and tell him his fault between thee and him alone. Proverbs 27 says, Open rebuke is better than secret love. Today, if you hold resentment, deal with it honestly. Speak to the person gently and directly rather than hating in silence.
S: Israel was not to hate their brother in their heart but to rebuke their neighbour frankly and not suffer sin upon him. Honest, loving correction is better than hidden resentment. If you hold resentment, speak to the person gently and directly rather than hating in silence.

== 19:18
T: Love thy neighbour as thyself

S: Israel was not to avenge or bear any grudge, but to love their neighbour as themselves. Jesus named this the second great commandment, and love begins by letting go of revenge. Let go of grudges and love the people around you as you love yourself.

== 19:19
T: Not mingled
R: 'Ye shall keep my statutes. Thou shalt not let thy cattle gender with a diverse kind: thou shalt not sow thy field with mingled seed: neither shall a garment mingled of linen and woollen come upon thee.' Israel was not to crossbreed animals, plant mixed seed, or wear clothing of mixed wool and linen. Just as God separated light from darkness and land from sea in Genesis 1, Israel was to honor distinctions. Notice the garment. Wool and linen together were used in the tabernacle and priestly garments. Ordinary people may have been barred from wearing what was reserved for holy use. These laws were part of Israel's covenant symbols. They are fulfilled in Christ, as Hebrews 8 and Colossians 2 show. But they still teach us that God values order and distinctness. Second Corinthians 6 says, Be ye not unequally yoked together with unbelievers. Today, honor God's call to be distinct. Do not let your life become a mixture of His ways and the world's.
S: Israel was not to crossbreed cattle, sow mingled seed or wear linen mixed with wool. These laws taught distinction, echoing creation, and are fulfilled in Christ while still teaching God's love of order. Honor God's call to be distinct and do not mix His ways with the world's.
P: Not mingled. Leviticus 19:19: 'thou shalt not sow thy field with mingled seed.' God values distinction. Don't let your life become a mix of His ways and the world's.

== 19:20
T: Because she was not free
R: 'And whosoever lieth carnally with a woman, that is a bondmaid, betrothed to an husband, and not at all redeemed, nor freedom given her… they shall not be put to death, because she was not free.' If a man slept with a slave woman engaged to another man but not yet freed, there was to be inquiry and punishment, but not death, because she was not free. Notice because she was not free. The law recognized the woman's vulnerable position. A slave woman had little power to refuse. She was not treated as fully guilty. The law took her lack of freedom into account. Notice that the man still bore guilt. He would bring a trespass offering. This was not excused. Ancient slavery was very different from later forms, and Scripture moves steadily toward freedom. Galatians 3 says, there is neither bond nor free… ye are all one in Christ. Today, consider those whose choices are limited by their circumstances. Show them compassion, and thank God that in Christ there is freedom for all.
S: A man who lay with a betrothed bondmaid not yet freed faced punishment, but not death, because she was not free. The law took her vulnerable position into account, while the man still bore guilt. Show compassion to those whose choices are limited and thank God that in Christ there is freedom for all.
P: Because she was not free. Leviticus 19:20: 'they shall not be put to death, because she was not free.' God sees the vulnerable. In Christ, there is neither bond nor free.

== 19:23
T: Three years uncircumcised
R: 'And when ye shall come into the land, and shall have planted all manner of trees for food, then ye shall count the fruit thereof as uncircumcised: three years shall it be as uncircumcised unto you: it shall not be eaten of.' When Israel planted fruit trees in the land, they were not to eat the fruit for the first three years. Notice three years. Young trees needed time. Eating fruit too early could weaken them.
