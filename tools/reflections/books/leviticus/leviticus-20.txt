# Leviticus 20: The seriousness of sin

== 20:1
T: The weight of sin
R: 'And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying.' God spoke to Moses again. Chapter 18 listed forbidden practices. Chapter 20 now gives the penalties for them. Notice that this chapter is sobering. Many penalties are severe. They show how seriously God takes sin. These laws governed Israel as a nation under God's direct rule. They are not instructions for the church to carry out today. The New Testament never calls believers to enforce these civil penalties. But they still reveal God's view of sin. Romans 6 says, the wages of sin is death. Every sin deserves death before a holy God. These penalties make that truth visible. Notice that the same verse in Romans continues: but the gift of God is eternal life through Jesus Christ our Lord. The gospel answers the penalty. Today, take sin seriously, as God does. And rejoice that Christ bore its penalty for you.
S: The LORD spoke to Moses again, introducing the penalties for the sins listed in chapter 18. These laws governed Israel under God's direct rule and show how seriously He takes sin. Take sin seriously and rejoice that Christ bore its penalty for you.
P: The wages of sin. Leviticus 20:1: 'And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying.' Sin's penalty is death, but the gift of God is eternal life through Christ.

== 20:2
T: Seed unto Molech
R: 'Again, thou shalt say to the children of Israel, Whosoever he be… that giveth any of his seed unto Molech; he shall surely be put to death: the people of the land shall stone him with stones.' Anyone, Israelite or foreigner, who sacrificed a child to Molech was to be put to death by stoning. Notice that the first penalty concerns child sacrifice. Of all the evils of Canaan, this was among the worst. Killing children for a false god was utterly abhorrent to the LORD. Notice the people of the land. The whole community was responsible. They could not tolerate this evil in their midst. Jeremiah 7 says of child sacrifice, which I commanded them not, neither came it into my mind. It was foreign to God's very heart. God is the protector of children. Psalm 127 says, children are an heritage of the LORD. Today, value every child as a gift from God. Stand against anything that harms children for the sake of other gods, whether pleasure, profit, or convenience.
S: Anyone who gave his child to Molech was to be put to death by the people of the land. Child sacrifice headed the list, for it was utterly foreign to God's heart. Value every child as a gift from God and stand against anything that sacrifices children to other gods.
P: Children are His heritage. Leviticus 20:2: 'that giveth any of his seed unto Molech.' Child sacrifice never entered God's mind. Protect and value every child today.

== 20:3
T: My holy name
R: 'And I will set my face against that man, and will cut him off from among his people; because he hath given of his seed unto Molech, to defile my sanctuary, and to profane my holy name.' God said He would personally oppose such a person and cut him off, because sacrificing children to Molech defiled God's sanctuary and profaned His holy name. Notice I will set my face. God Himself would act. Even if the community failed, God would not. He would personally oppose this evil. Notice defile my sanctuary. How did child sacrifice defile the sanctuary? Perhaps people offered to Molech and then came to worship the LORD, mixing the two. Divided worship defiles. Notice profane my holy name. Those who claimed to be God's people while harming children disgraced His name. Romans 2 says, the name of God is blasphemed among the Gentiles through you. Our conduct reflects on God's name. Today, live in a way that honors God's holy name. Let your life never bring disgrace on Him.
S: God said He would set His face against anyone who gave a child to Molech, for it defiled His sanctuary and profaned His holy name. God Himself would act, and divided worship disgraced His name. Live in a way that honors God's holy name and never brings disgrace on Him.
P: Honor His name. Leviticus 20:3: 'to defile my sanctuary, and to profane my holy name.' Our conduct reflects on God. Let your life honor His name today.

== 20:4
T: Hide their eyes
R: 'And if the people of the land do any ways hide their eyes from the man, when he giveth of his seed unto Molech, and kill him not.' If the community ignored such a person and refused to act when he sacrificed his child, God would take note. Notice hide their eyes. Looking away from evil is also sin. The community that ignored child sacrifice shared responsibility. Silence in the face of evil is complicity. Proverbs 24 says, If thou forbear to deliver them that are drawn unto death… doth not he that pondereth the heart consider it? God sees when we look away. Notice any ways. Even partial blindness was not excused. We cannot pretend not to know. James 4 says, to him that knoweth to do good, and doeth it not, to him it is sin. Today, do not hide your eyes from evil. When the vulnerable are harmed, speak up and act. Silence is not neutral.
S: If the people of the land hid their eyes from the man who gave his child to Molech, they shared his guilt. Looking away from evil is complicity, and God sees when we do. Do not hide your eyes from evil, but speak up and act when the vulnerable are harmed.
P: Don't look away. Leviticus 20:4: 'if the people of the land do any ways hide their eyes.' Silence in the face of evil is not neutral. Speak up for the vulnerable.

== 20:5
T: Against his family
R: 'Then I will set my face against that man, and against his family, and will cut him off, and all that go a whoring after him, to commit whoredom with Molech, from among their people.' God would oppose the man, his family, and all who followed him into Molech worship. Notice and against his family. Sin affects families. The man's idolatry drew others in. His household and followers were affected. No one sins in isolation. Exodus 20 speaks of iniquity visited unto the third and fourth generation of them that hate me. Sinful patterns can pass through families. But the same verse continues: shewing mercy unto thousands of them that love me. God's mercy outlasts judgment. Notice all that go a whoring after him. Leaders who sin draw followers with them. Influence carries responsibility. Today, consider who follows your example. Lead your family and others toward God, not away from Him.
S: God would set His face against the man, his family and all who followed him into whoredom with Molech. Sin spread through families and followers, yet God's mercy outlasts judgment for those who love Him. Consider who follows your example and lead them toward God.
P: Your influence. Leviticus 20:5: 'against his family… and all that go a whoring after him.' Sin spreads through influence. Lead others toward God today.

== 20:6
T: Turneth after familiar spirits
R: 'And the soul that turneth after such as have familiar spirits, and after wizards, to go a whoring after them, I will even set my face against that soul, and will cut him off from among his people.' God would oppose anyone who turned to mediums and spiritists, and would cut him off. Notice go a whoring after them. Seeking mediums was spiritual adultery. It meant turning from God to other spiritual powers. God took it as betrayal. Notice turneth after. It describes a direction of the heart. People turn to the occult when they want answers God has not given, or want control God has not offered. It reflects distrust. Isaiah 8 says, should not a people seek unto their God? for the living to the dead? God invites us to seek Him. Notice the phrase I will set my face appears again. God takes spiritual unfaithfulness personally. Today, turn to God for answers, not to other spiritual sources. Trust Him with what you do not know.
S: God would set His face against the soul who turned after familiar spirits and wizards. Seeking mediums was spiritual adultery and distrust of God. Turn to God for answers rather than other spiritual sources, and trust Him with what you do not know.
P: Seek your God. Leviticus 20:6: 'the soul that turneth after such as have familiar spirits.' Should not a people seek unto their God? Turn to Him today.

Notice therefore. After warnings came invitation. God did not only threaten. He called His people to something better. The penalties pointed to the path of holiness. Notice sanctify yourselves. The people had a part to play. They were to set themselves apart. Holiness involves our choices. Second Timothy 2 says, If a man therefore purge himself… he shall be a vessel unto honour, sanctified, and meet for the master's use. We cooperate with God in becoming holy. Notice for I am the LORD your God. The reason was relationship. Holiness flows from belonging to Him. Today, respond to God's call. Set yourself apart for Him. Choose holiness because He is your God.
S: In the middle of the penalties came the call, Sanctify yourselves and be holy, for I am the LORD your God. Warnings gave way to invitation, and the people had a part in their holiness. Respond to God's call and set yourself apart for Him today.

Notice I am the LORD which sanctify you. Verse 7 said, sanctify yourselves. Verse 8 says, I sanctify you. Both are true. We pursue holiness, and God makes us holy. == 20:9

R: 'For every one that curseth his father or his mother shall be surely put to death: he hath cursed his father or his mother; his blood shall be upon him.' Anyone who cursed his father or mother was to be put to death. His blood would be on his own head. Notice curseth. This was not mere disagreement or a moment of frustration. It was a deliberate calling down of evil on one's parents, rejecting their place entirely. Notice how seriously God took the family. Parents were God's representatives in the home. Cursing them was like cursing God's order. Jesus quoted it in Matthew 15, rebuking those who used religious excuses to avoid caring for their parents. Notice his blood shall be upon him. The person was responsible for his own fate. Proverbs 20 says, Whoso curseth his father or his mother, his lamp shall be put out in obscure darkness. Today, guard your words toward your parents. Even when relationships are hard, speak with respect and seek peace.
S: Whoever cursed his father or mother was to be put to death, his blood upon him. Deliberately calling down evil on parents rejected God's order in the home. Guard your words toward your parents, speaking with respect even when relationships are hard.

== 20:10
T: The adulterer and the adulteress
R: 'And the man that committeth adultery with another man's wife… the adulterer and the adulteress shall surely be put to death.' A man who committed adultery with his neighbor's wife was to be put to death, along with the woman. Notice the adulterer and the adulteress. Both were held responsible. The law did not punish only the woman. In many ancient cultures, women bore more blame. God's law held both equally accountable. Notice John 8. The religious leaders brought a woman taken in adultery to Jesus, citing this law. They did not bring the man. Jesus said, He that is without sin among you, let him first cast a stone. No one did. Then Jesus said, Neither do I condemn thee: go, and sin no more. He did not deny her sin. But He offered mercy and called her to a new life. Today, take adultery seriously, as God does. And rejoice in Christ, who forgives and says, go, and sin no more.
S: The adulterer and the adulteress were both to be put to death. Both were held equally accountable, and Jesus later showed mercy to a woman taken in adultery while calling her to sin no more. Take faithfulness seriously and rejoice in Christ, who forgives and calls us to new life.

First Corinthians 6 names many sins, including this one, and then says, And such were some of you: but ye are washed. There is hope and new life for all who come to Christ. == 20:23

R: 'And ye shall not walk in the manners of the nation, which I cast out before you: for they committed all these things, and therefore I abhorred them.' Because God had set them apart, Israel was to distinguish between clean and unclean animals and birds. They were not to defile themselves. Notice therefore. The food laws flowed from Israel's separation. God had distinguished them from the nations. So they were to make distinctions in their food. Their daily diet reflected their identity. == 20:27
T: A familiar spirit
R: 'A man also or woman that hath a familiar spirit, or that is a wizard, shall surely be put to death: they shall stone them with stones: their blood shall be upon them.' A man or woman who acted as a medium or spiritist was to be put to death by stoning. Notice that the chapter ends with the occult. It began with Molech and ends with mediums. Both involved turning to other spiritual powers. Both led Israel away from God. Notice a man also or woman. Both sexes were addressed equally. Notice the contrast with verse 26. God wanted Israel to be His. Mediums drew people to other spirits. That was a direct challenge to God's claim. In Acts 16, Paul cast a spirit of divination out of a slave girl. Christ's power freed her. The answer to occult bondage is not death but deliverance through Christ. Colossians 2 says Christ spoiled principalities and powers, triumphing over them. Today, rejoice that Christ has triumphed over every dark power. Turn to Him alone.
