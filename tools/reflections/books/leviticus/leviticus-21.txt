# Leviticus 21: The holiness of the priests

== 21:1
T: Not defiled for the dead
R: 'And the LORD said unto Moses, Speak unto the priests the sons of Aaron, and say unto them, There shall none be defiled for the dead among his people.' God told Moses to instruct the priests not to make themselves ceremonially unclean by touching dead bodies of ordinary people. Notice the priests. Higher responsibility brought higher standards. Those who served at God's altar had to guard their purity more carefully than others. James 3 says teachers shall receive the greater condemnation. Leadership increases accountability. Notice for the dead. Contact with death made a person unclean. Priests served the living God. They had to remain ready to serve at any time. Death and the sanctuary did not mix. Luke 9 records Jesus saying, Let the dead bury their dead: but go thou and preach the kingdom of God. Service to God sometimes requires costly priorities. Today, if you serve God in leadership, guard your life carefully. Higher calling brings higher responsibility.
S: God told the priests not to be defiled for the dead among their people. Those who served at the altar had to guard their purity more carefully, ready to serve the living God. If you serve God in leadership, guard your life carefully, for higher calling brings higher responsibility.
P: Higher calling. Leviticus 21:1: 'There shall none be defiled for the dead among his people.' Leaders guard their lives more carefully. Serve the living God faithfully today.

== 21:2
T: For his near kin
R: 'But for his kin, that is near unto him, that is, for his mother, and for his father, and for his son, and for his daughter, and for his brother.' An ordinary priest could become unclean for close relatives: his mother, father, son, daughter, or brother. Notice but for his kin. God made an exception for family. Priests were allowed to grieve and to care for the bodies of those closest to them. God did not demand that they ignore their families. Grief is human. God allowed it. Notice the list. Mother, father, son, daughter, brother. These were the people a priest could not be expected to abandon. God understood family love. John 11 says Jesus wept at the tomb of Lazarus. He grieved for those He loved. God does not despise our tears. Psalm 56 says, put thou my tears into thy bottle. Today, know that God honors your grief for loved ones. He made room for it even in His holiest servants.
S: An ordinary priest could be defiled for his near kin, his mother, father, son, daughter or brother. God made room for family grief even among His holiest servants. Know that God honors your grief for loved ones and keeps your tears.
P: Room for grief. Leviticus 21:2: 'for his mother, and for his father, and for his son.' Jesus wept. God honors your grief and keeps your tears in His bottle.
H: #DailyGrace #Leviticus212 #Grief #Family #Comfort #BibleVerse

== 21:3
T: His sister a virgin
R: 'And for his sister a virgin, that is nigh unto him, which hath had no husband; for her may he be defiled.' A priest could also become unclean for an unmarried sister who depended on him. Notice which hath had no husband. An unmarried sister lived under her family's care. If she died, her brother was responsible for her. A married sister belonged to her husband's household. God's law recognized who depended on whom. Notice for her may he be defiled. The priest's duty to his vulnerable sister was honored. Caring for dependents was a holy duty. First Timothy 5 says, if any provide not for his own, and specially for those of his own house, he hath denied the faith. Caring for family is part of faith. Notice nigh unto him. Closeness mattered. Today, care for those who depend on you. Honor your responsibility to vulnerable family members. God sees it as holy work.
S: A priest could be defiled for his virgin sister who had no husband and depended on him. God honored his duty to care for a vulnerable family member. Care faithfully for those who depend on you, for God sees it as holy work.
P: Care for your own. Leviticus 21:3: 'for his sister a virgin, that is nigh unto him… for her may he be defiled.' Caring for dependents is holy work. Honor it today.

== 21:4
T: A chief man
R: 'But he shall not defile himself, being a chief man among his people, to profane himself.' The priest was not to make himself unclean for others, because he was a leader among his people. Notice a chief man. The priest was a leader. His position carried weight. People looked to him. What he did set an example. Leaders must remember that others watch them. Notice to profane himself. Carelessness would lower him and his office. Leadership requires guarding one's conduct. First Timothy 4 says, be thou an example of the believers, in word, in conversation, in charity, in spirit, in faith, in purity. Leaders model faith for others. Titus 2 says, In all things shewing thyself a pattern of good works. Today, if you lead in any way, remember that others are watching. Guard your conduct so that you set a godly example.
S: The priest was not to defile himself, being a chief man among his people. His position set an example, and carelessness would profane his office. If you lead in any way, guard your conduct so that you set a godly example.
P: An example. Leviticus 21:4: 'being a chief man among his people.' Others watch leaders. Be an example in word, conduct, love, faith and purity.

== 21:5
T: No pagan mourning
R: 'They shall not make baldness upon their head, neither shall they shave off the corner of their beard, nor make any cuttings in their flesh.' Priests were not to shave their heads, trim the edges of their beards, or cut their bodies in mourning. Notice these were pagan mourning customs. Other nations expressed grief by shaving and cutting. Priests of the LORD were to grieve differently. Their hope was in the living God. Chapter 19 gave this rule to all Israel. Here it is repeated for the priests. Leaders must model hope in grief. Notice cuttings in their flesh. Self-harm in grief was common among the nations. God did not want His servants to harm themselves. First Thessalonians 4 says, sorrow not, even as others which have no hope. Christian grief is real, but it is full of hope. Today, if you grieve, grieve with hope. Do not harm yourself in sorrow. Let others see your hope in the living God.
S: Priests were not to make baldness, shave their beards' corners or cut their flesh in mourning. These pagan customs expressed hopeless grief, while God's servants were to model hope. Grieve with hope, never harming yourself, and let others see your hope in the living God.
P: Grief with hope. Leviticus 21:5: 'nor make any cuttings in their flesh.' Sorrow not as those without hope. Let others see your hope in God today.

== 21:6
T: The bread of their God
R: 'They shall be holy unto their God, and not profane the name of their God: for the offerings of the LORD made by fire, and the bread of their God, they do offer: therefore they shall be holy.' The priests were to be holy to their God and not profane His name, because they offered the food offerings to the LORD. Notice the bread of their God. The offerings were called God's food. Not because God needed food, but because the altar was like His table. Priests served at God's table. Notice therefore they shall be holy. Their work determined their standard. Because they handled holy things, they had to be holy. Romans 15 says Paul ministered the gospel of God, that the offering up of the Gentiles might be acceptable, being sanctified by the Holy Ghost. Believers also handle holy things: God's word and God's people. Today, remember that you handle holy things. Let your life match the holiness of what you serve.
S: The priests were to be holy and not profane God's name, for they offered the bread of their God. Because they served at God's table, their lives had to match their work. Remember that you handle holy things in God's word and people, and let your life match them.
P: Serving at God's table. Leviticus 21:6: 'the bread of their God, they do offer: therefore they shall be holy.' You handle holy things. Let your life match them.

== 21:7
T: Holy unto his God
R: 'They shall not take a wife that is a whore, or profane; neither shall they take a woman put away from her husband: for he is holy unto his God.' Priests were not to marry a prostitute, a defiled woman, or a divorced woman, because they were holy to their God. Notice that marriage reflected the priest's holiness. His home was connected to his ministry. Whom he married mattered. First Timothy 3 requires that church leaders rule well their own house. Family life and ministry are linked. Notice that this was not a judgment on the worth of these women. God's grace reaches everyone. Rahab, a former harlot, became an ancestor of Jesus in Matthew 1. The issue was the priest's symbolic role as a picture of purity before the people. Notice for he is holy unto his God. Holiness shaped every part of his life. Today, recognize that your relationships reflect your faith. Seek God's wisdom in your closest relationships.
S: Priests were not to marry a harlot, a profane woman or a divorced woman, for they were holy to God. The priest's home reflected his ministry, though God's grace reached women like Rahab. Recognize that your relationships reflect your faith and seek God's wisdom in them.

== 21:8
T: Thou shalt sanctify him
R: 'Thou shalt sanctify him therefore; for he offereth the bread of thy God: he shall be holy unto thee: for I the LORD, which sanctify you, am holy.' Israel was to regard the priest as holy because he offered God's food. He was to be holy to them, because God, who makes them holy, is holy. Notice thou shalt sanctify him. The people had a role too. They were to honor the priest's holiness. Respect for God's servants was the people's duty. First Thessalonians 5 says, esteem them very highly in love for their work's sake. Notice for I the LORD, which sanctify you, am holy. God makes holy. Both priests and people were sanctified by Him. The priest's holiness came from God, not from himself. Notice for their work's sake. Honor was due because of the work, not the person's perfection. Today, honor those who serve you spiritually. Esteem them in love for their work's sake.
S: Israel was to sanctify the priest, for he offered the bread of their God, and the LORD who sanctifies them is holy. The people were to honor God's servants because of their holy work. Honor those who serve you spiritually and esteem them in love for their work's sake.

== 21:9
T: She profaneth her father
R: 'And the daughter of any priest, if she profane herself by playing the whore, she profaneth her father: she shall be burnt with fire.' If a priest's daughter became a prostitute, she disgraced her father and was to be burned. Notice she profaneth her father. Her actions reflected on her father's priestly office. A priest's household was meant to model holiness for Israel. Her sin brought disgrace on the sanctuary itself. Notice the severity. This penalty belonged to Israel's theocracy and is not for the church to enforce. But it shows how seriously God viewed sin within a leader's household. Proverbs 17 says, A foolish son is a grief to his father. Children's choices affect parents deeply. Yet notice the gospel. Luke 15 shows the prodigal son, who wasted his living with harlots. When he returned, his father ran to embrace him. Today, pray for the children of leaders. And remember that no one who returns to the Father is beyond His embrace.
S: A priest's daughter who profaned herself by harlotry profaned her father and was to be burned. A leader's household was to model holiness, and her sin disgraced the sanctuary. Pray for the children of leaders and remember that the Father embraces every prodigal who returns.
P: The Father runs. Leviticus 21:9: 'she profaneth her father.' Children's choices affect parents deeply. Yet the Father runs to embrace every returning prodigal.

== 21:10
T: The high priest
R: 'And he that is the high priest among his brethren, upon whose head the anointing oil was poured… shall not uncover his head, nor rend his clothes.' The high priest, anointed and consecrated to wear the holy garments, was not to let his hair hang loose or tear his clothes in mourning. Notice the high priest among his brethren. He was one of the priests, yet set above them. His standard was the highest. Notice the anointing oil was poured. He bore a special anointing. His whole life was marked by it. Notice shall not… rend his clothes. Tearing garments was a sign of grief. The high priest could not do this. His holy garments must stay whole. In Matthew 26, Caiaphas the high priest rent his clothes at Jesus' trial, breaking this very law. The true High Priest stood before him. Hebrews 4 says Jesus is a great high priest, touched with the feeling of our infirmities. Today, worship Jesus, the anointed High Priest. He bears your sorrows perfectly.

== 21:16
T: The LORD spake again
R: 'And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying.' God spoke to Moses again, now about priests with physical defects. Notice that God addressed a sensitive matter. The following verses describe priests with disabilities who could not offer sacrifices. This can be hard for modern readers. But it must be read carefully and in light of the whole Bible. The rule concerned the ritual picture at the altar, which had to display perfection. It was not a statement about a person's worth to God. As the next verses show, these priests were still fed from the holy food and remained part of the priestly family. And in Christ, the picture is fulfilled. Matthew 21 says, the blind and the lame came to him in the temple; and he healed them. Jesus welcomed them into God's house. Today, read difficult passages patiently. Look for how they point to Christ, who welcomes everyone.

The sacrifices had to be without blemish. So did the priest who offered them. The whole scene pictured perfection. This was a visual lesson about God's holiness. Notice that it pointed forward. No human priest was truly without blemish inwardly. Every priest had sin. Only Christ was perfect. Hebrews 7 says, such an high priest became us, who is holy, harmless, undefiled, separate from sinners. Christ is the unblemished priest. First Peter 1 calls Him a lamb without blemish and without spot. He is both priest and sacrifice, perfect in every way. Today, thank God for Christ, the perfect High Priest. Your access to God depends on His perfection, not yours.

T: I the LORD do sanctify them
R: 'Only he shall not go in unto the vail, nor come nigh unto the altar, because he hath a blemish; that he profane not my sanctuaries: for I the LORD do sanctify them.' He could not go near the veil or approach the altar, so as not to profane the sanctuary. God made it holy. Notice only. The restriction was limited. Only the veil and altar were off-limits. Everything else of priestly life remained his. Notice I the LORD do sanctify them. God made the sanctuary holy, and He kept it as a picture of perfection. In Christ, the picture is fulfilled. The veil has been torn. Matthew 27 says the veil of the temple was rent in twain from the top to the bottom. Now every believer, with any weakness, may enter. Hebrews 10 says we have boldness to enter into the holiest by the blood of Jesus. No blemish keeps you out when you come through Christ. Today, enter God's presence boldly. In Christ, the veil is open to you.
S: A priest with a blemish could not go in unto the vail or come nigh the altar, for God sanctified the sanctuary as a picture of perfection. In Christ the veil is torn, and every believer may enter boldly. Enter God's presence boldly, for in Christ the veil is open to you.

== 21:24
T: Moses told it
R: 'And Moses told it unto Aaron, and to his sons, and unto all the children of Israel.' Moses gave these instructions to Aaron, his sons, and all the Israelites. Notice unto all the children of Israel. The priestly laws were not secret. Everyone knew the standards for priests. Transparency protected the people and the priests. Leaders were accountable before the whole community. Notice Moses told it. He faithfully passed on what God said. He did not edit or soften it. Faithful messengers deliver God's whole word. Acts 20 records Paul saying, I have not shunned to declare unto you all the counsel of God. Notice that everyone heard. The people knew what to expect from their priests. They could hold them accountable. Healthy communities know God's standards for leaders. Today, know what God expects of leaders, and pray for yours. And pass on God's word faithfully to others, without editing it.
S: Moses told these instructions to Aaron, his sons and all the children of Israel. The standards for priests were public, keeping leaders accountable before the community. Know what God expects of leaders, pray for yours, and pass on God's word faithfully.
