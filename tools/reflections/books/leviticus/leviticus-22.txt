# Leviticus 22: Holy things and acceptable offerings

== 22:1
T: Guarding holy things
R: 'And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying.' God spoke to Moses again, now about how priests must handle the holy offerings. Notice that God returned to the priests. Chapter 21 dealt with who could serve. Chapter 22 deals with how they must treat holy things. Both character and conduct mattered. Notice the theme of this chapter. Holy things must be treated as holy. Offerings were not ordinary food. Gifts to God were not ordinary property. Familiarity can make us careless with sacred things. Priests handled holy things every day. They could grow used to them. Malachi 1 rebukes priests who said, The table of the LORD is contemptible. They had grown careless. Today, guard against treating sacred things casually. God's word, worship, and people deserve reverence every day, not just on special occasions.
S: The LORD spoke to Moses about how priests must handle holy offerings. Those who handled sacred things daily could grow careless, as Malachi warned. Guard against treating God's word, worship and people casually, and keep reverence every day.
P: Guard against casualness. Leviticus 22:1: 'And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying.' Familiarity can breed carelessness. Keep reverence for holy things every day.

== 22:2
T: Separate themselves
R: 'Speak unto Aaron and to his sons, that they separate themselves from the holy things of the children of Israel, and that they profane not my holy name in those things which they hallow unto me: I am the LORD.' God told Aaron and his sons to treat the sacred offerings with respect, so they would not profane His holy name. Notice separate themselves from the holy things. At times, priests had to stay away from offerings, such as when they were unclean. Knowing when not to handle holy things was part of reverence. Notice which they hallow unto me. The people dedicated these gifts to God. Priests were stewards, not owners. They had to honor the people's devotion. Notice profane not my holy name. Careless handling of offerings dishonored God's name. First Corinthians 4 says, it is required in stewards, that a man be found faithful. Today, treat what others give to God with reverence. Be a faithful steward of every gift entrusted to you.
S: Aaron and his sons were to separate themselves from the holy things when needed and not profane God's name. They were stewards of what the people dedicated to God. Treat what others give to God with reverence and be a faithful steward.
P: Faithful stewards. Leviticus 22:2: 'that they profane not my holy name in those things which they hallow unto me.' Handle what others give to God with reverence.
H: #DailyGrace #Leviticus222 #Stewardship #Reverence #Faithfulness #BibleVerse

== 22:3
T: Cut off from my presence
R: 'Say unto them, Whosoever he be of all your seed… that goeth unto the holy things… having his uncleanness upon him, that soul shall be cut off from my presence: I am the LORD.' Any descendant of Aaron who approached the sacred offerings while unclean would be cut off from God's presence. Notice from my presence. This was the gravest loss for a priest. His whole life was lived near God. To be cut off from His presence was the deepest separation. Notice having his uncleanness upon him. The problem was approaching holy things in an unclean state. Uncleanness was common and could be cleansed. But ignoring it was dangerous. Psalm 24 asks, Who shall ascend into the hill of the LORD? He that hath clean hands, and a pure heart. Today, come to God with clean hands and a pure heart. Bring your uncleanness to Christ for cleansing before you serve.
S: Any priest who went to the holy things with uncleanness upon him would be cut off from God's presence. Approaching holy things carelessly risked the deepest separation. Come to God with clean hands and a pure heart, bringing your uncleanness to Christ first.
P: Clean hands, pure heart. Leviticus 22:3: 'having his uncleanness upon him, that soul shall be cut off from my presence.' Bring uncleanness to Christ before you serve.

== 22:4
T: Until he be clean
R: 'What man soever of the seed of Aaron is a leper, or hath a running issue; he shall not eat of the holy things, until he be clean.' A priest with a skin disease or a bodily discharge could not eat the holy offerings until he was cleansed. Notice until he be clean. The restriction was temporary. It lasted only until cleansing. Even priests could become unclean. They followed the same laws as everyone else. No one was above God's standards. Notice that priests were not immune to illness. They suffered like others. Their position did not protect them from life's troubles. Hebrews 5 says the high priest is himself compassed with infirmity. Their weakness gave them compassion for others. Notice the hope in until. Cleansing would come. The priest would return to his portion. Today, if you are in a season of limitation, remember the word until. It will not last forever. God restores.
S: A priest with leprosy or a running issue could not eat the holy things until he was clean. The restriction was temporary, and priests shared the same weaknesses as everyone else. If you are in a season of limitation, remember the word until, for God restores.
P: Until. Leviticus 22:4: 'he shall not eat of the holy things, until he be clean.' Seasons of limitation end. God restores. Hold on to hope today.

== 22:5
T: Whatsoever uncleanness
R: 'Or whosoever toucheth any creeping thing, whereby he may be made unclean, or a man of whom he may take uncleanness, whatsoever uncleanness he hath.' A priest who touched an unclean creeping thing or an unclean person would also become unclean. Notice whatsoever uncleanness. Uncleanness could come from many sources. A priest might touch something without realizing it. Notice that priests lived in the same world as everyone else. They faced the same defilements. Their calling did not shield them from contact with a broken world. John 17 records Jesus praying, I pray not that thou shouldest take them out of the world, but that thou shouldest keep them from the evil. We live in the world without being of it. We will encounter defilement. The answer is regular cleansing. First John 1 says, if we walk in the light… the blood of Jesus Christ his Son cleanseth us from all sin. Today, walk in the light. Let Christ's blood cleanse you continually as you live in this world.
S: A priest who touched any creeping thing or unclean person became unclean. Priests lived in the same broken world as everyone else and needed regular cleansing. Walk in the light and let Christ's blood cleanse you continually as you live in the world.
P: In, not of. Leviticus 22:5: 'whosoever toucheth any creeping thing… whatsoever uncleanness he hath.' We live in the world but need constant cleansing. Walk in the light.

== 22:6
T: Unless he wash
R: 'The soul which hath touched any such shall be unclean until even, and shall not eat of the holy things, unless he wash his flesh with water.' A priest who touched such things was unclean until evening. He could not eat holy food unless he had bathed. Notice unless he wash. The condition was simple. Wash, then eat. The priest could not skip the washing and go straight to the holy food. Cleansing came before communion. This pattern runs through Scripture. First Corinthians 11 says, let a man examine himself, and so let him eat of that bread. Before the Lord's Supper, believers examine and confess. Cleansing prepares us for fellowship. Notice until even. A set time. Evening brought the end of uncleanness. Today, before you come to God's table or His worship, examine your heart. Let Him wash you, then come with joy.
S: A priest who touched such things was unclean until even and could not eat holy food unless he washed. Cleansing came before communion. Before coming to God's table or worship, examine your heart, let Him wash you, then come with joy.
P: Wash, then eat. Leviticus 22:6: 'shall not eat of the holy things, unless he wash.' Let a man examine himself, and so eat. Let God cleanse you before you come.

== 22:7
T: Because it is his food
R: 'And when the sun is down, he shall be clean, and shall afterward eat of the holy things; because it is his food.' When the sun went down, the priest was clean and could eat the sacred offerings, because they were his food. Notice when the sun is down. A new day began at sunset. Uncleanness ended, and the priest returned to his portion. Each evening brought fresh standing. Notice because it is his food. God did not want His priests to go hungry. The holy offerings were their livelihood. God cared for their needs. Deuteronomy 18 says, the LORD is their inheritance. God provided for those who served Him. Matthew 6 says, your heavenly Father knoweth that ye have need of all these things. God knows what we need. Notice afterward eat. Restoration led back to provision. Today, trust that God provides for you. After any season of difficulty, He restores you to His provision.
S: When the sun was down, the priest was clean and could eat the holy things, for they were his food. Each evening brought fresh standing, and God provided for His servants' needs. Trust that God provides for you and restores you to His provision after hard seasons.

== 22:8
T: Not defile himself
R: 'That which dieth of itself, or is torn with beasts, he shall not eat to defile himself therewith: I am the LORD.' Priests were not to eat anything that died naturally or was torn by wild animals, because it would defile them. Notice that ordinary Israelites who ate such meat could be cleansed, as chapter 17 showed. But priests were held to a higher standard. They were not to eat it at all. Leaders must avoid what others are sometimes excused for. First Corinthians 8 says, take heed lest by any means this liberty of yours become a stumblingblock to them that are weak. Leaders sometimes give up freedoms for the sake of others. Their example matters more. Notice I am the LORD. The standard came from God. Priests obeyed because of who He is. Today, if you lead, consider giving up even permitted things for the sake of your example. Let love guide your liberty.
S: Priests were not to eat what died of itself or was torn by beasts, though ordinary Israelites could be cleansed after eating it. Leaders were held to a higher standard for the sake of their example. If you lead, consider giving up even permitted things, letting love guide your liberty.

The priests were to keep God's requirements so they would not become guilty and die for treating them with contempt. God made them holy. Notice lest they bear sin… and die. The stakes were high. Nadab and Abihu had shown what could happen. Carelessness with holy things was dangerous. Notice I the LORD do sanctify them. Their holiness came from God. They were not to throw it away through carelessness. What God has made holy must be guarded. Second Timothy 1 says, That good thing which was committed unto thee keep by the Holy Ghost which dwelleth in us. We guard what God has given, with the Spirit's help. Today, guard the holiness God has given you. Do not treat it carelessly. Rely on the Spirit to help you keep it.

== 22:10
T: No stranger
R: 'There shall no stranger eat of the holy thing: a sojourner of the priest, or an hired servant, shall not eat of the holy thing.' No one outside a priest's family could eat the sacred offering. Not a guest, nor a hired worker. Notice no stranger. Here stranger means someone outside the priestly family. The holy food was for the priest's household. Guests and hired workers, though welcome in his home, could not share it. Notice the distinction between belonging and visiting. A hired servant worked for the priest but did not belong to the family. Belonging gave access. Visiting did not. Ephesians 2 says, ye are no more strangers and foreigners, but fellowcitizens with the saints, and of the household of God. In Christ, we are not visitors but family. We have full access to God's table. Today, rejoice that in Christ you are not a stranger or a hired servant. You are family, with full access to God's blessings.
S: No stranger, sojourner or hired servant could eat the holy thing in a priest's house. Belonging, not visiting, gave access to the holy food. Rejoice that in Christ you are not a stranger but a member of God's household with full access.
P: Family, not visitors. Leviticus 22:10: 'There shall no stranger eat of the holy thing.' In Christ you're no more a stranger, but of the household of God.

They gained access to the holy food. Their status changed through purchase. This beautifully pictures redemption. First Corinthians 6 says, ye are bought with a price. Christ purchased us. We now belong to His household. Notice he that is born in his house. Birth also brought belonging. John 3 says, Except a man be born again, he cannot see the kingdom of God. New birth brings us into God's family. Both pictures, purchase and birth, describe our salvation. Today, rejoice that you have been bought by Christ and born into God's family. You eat at His table by right of redemption.

R: 'If the priest's daughter also be married unto a stranger, she may not eat of an offering of the holy things.' If a priest's daughter married someone outside the priestly line, she could no longer eat the sacred offerings. Notice married unto a stranger. Her marriage moved her into another household. Ruth 1 shows Orpah choosing to return to her people and gods, while Ruth chose Naomi's God. Their choices determined their futures. Joshua 24 says, choose you this day whom ye will serve. Notice that this was not a punishment. It was simply a change of household. Today, recognize that your commitments shape your identity. Choose to belong to God's household above all.

== 22:14

R: 'And if a man eat of the holy thing unwittingly, then he shall put the fifth part thereof unto it, and shall give it unto the priest with the holy thing.' If someone ate a sacred offering by mistake, he had to repay it with an extra fifth. Notice unwittingly. The mistake was not deliberate. Yet it still had to be made right. Unintentional wrongs still need correction. Notice the fifth part. Restitution went beyond the loss. The person paid back more than he had taken. This followed the pattern of chapter 5. Making things right was generous, not minimal. Notice that God provided a clear remedy. The mistake did not bring death or exclusion. It brought restitution and restoration. God's response to honest mistakes is gracious. Today, when you make mistakes, make them right generously. Do not ignore unintentional wrongs. Repair them, and trust God's grace.

This was for everyone, Israelite or foreigner, who brought a burnt offering to fulfill a vow or as a freewill offering. Notice vows. These were promises made to God, often in times of trouble. When God answered, the person fulfilled the vow. Notice freewill offerings. These were spontaneous gifts from a grateful heart. No obligation. Pure love. Psalm 54 says, I will freely sacrifice unto thee. Notice the strangers in Israel. Foreigners too could bring offerings. God welcomed worship from all nations. Isaiah 56 promises that strangers who join themselves to the LORD will be accepted on His altar. Today, keep your promises to God. And give Him something freely, simply out of love.

== 22:23
T: For a freewill offering
R: 'Either a bullock or a lamb that hath any thing superfluous or lacking in his parts, that mayest thou offer for a freewill offering; but for a vow it shall not be accepted.' A bull or lamb with a deformed or stunted limb could be given as a freewill offering, but not to fulfill a vow. Notice the exception. For a freewill offering, slight imperfection was allowed. For a vow, it was not. The difference was important. A vow was a promise. Promises had to be kept exactly. A freewill offering was a spontaneous gift. God welcomed it with grace. Notice God's flexibility. He is not rigid where rigidity serves no purpose. He welcomed sincere, spontaneous worship even if imperfect. Mark 14 shows a woman pouring ointment on Jesus. Some criticized her. Jesus said, She hath done what she could. God values sincere hearts. Today, give God what you can, freely and sincerely. He receives spontaneous love with grace. But keep your promises to Him exactly.

== 22:25
T: Their corruption is in them

== 22:27
