# Leviticus 23:1-22: Sabbath, Passover, Firstfruits and Pentecost

== 23:1
T: The calendar of God
R: 'And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying.' God spoke to Moses about the appointed festivals. This chapter lays out Israel's sacred calendar. Notice that God organized time. He did not leave His people to drift through the year. He gave them a rhythm of rest, remembrance, and celebration. Time itself became holy. Notice that each feast pointed to something. Passover recalled the exodus. Firstfruits celebrated the harvest. Pentecost marked the giving of the law. Tabernacles remembered the wilderness. And many Christians see that they also point forward to Christ. Colossians 2 calls them a shadow of things to come; but the body is of Christ. Psalm 90 says, So teach us to number our days, that we may apply our hearts unto wisdom. Today, let God shape your calendar. Build rhythms of rest, remembrance, and celebration into your year.
S: The LORD spoke to Moses about the appointed feasts, Israel's sacred calendar. God gave His people a rhythm of rest, remembrance and celebration, a shadow of things fulfilled in Christ. Let God shape your calendar with rhythms of rest, remembrance and celebration.
P: Holy time. Leviticus 23:1: 'And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying.' God gave His people a rhythm for the year. Teach us to number our days. Let Him shape your time.

== 23:2
T: My feasts
R: 'Speak unto the children of Israel, and say unto them, Concerning the feasts of the LORD, which ye shall proclaim to be holy convocations, even these are my feasts.' God told Israel that these were His appointed festivals, to be proclaimed as sacred assemblies. Notice the feasts of the LORD. They were not Israel's feasts but God's. He hosted them. He set the dates. His people were invited guests. Notice holy convocations. Sacred gatherings. God called His people together. Worship was communal. Hebrews 10 says, Not forsaking the assembling of ourselves together. Notice my feasts. God loves celebration. His calendar was full of feasts, not just fasts. Joy was part of worship. Nehemiah 8 says, the joy of the LORD is your strength. Today, accept God's invitation to gather with His people. Celebrate His goodness together. He is the host, and you are His guest.
S: God told Israel that the feasts of the LORD, proclaimed as holy convocations, were His feasts. He hosted them, gathered His people together and filled the year with celebration. Accept God's invitation to gather with His people and celebrate His goodness.
P: His feasts. Leviticus 23:2: 'even these are my feasts.' God is the host; you are His guest. Gather with His people and celebrate His goodness.

== 23:3
T: The sabbath of the LORD
R: 'Six days shall work be done: but the seventh day is the sabbath of rest, an holy convocation; ye shall do no work therein: it is the sabbath of the LORD in all your dwellings.' Israel was to work six days, but the seventh was a sabbath of rest and a sacred assembly. No work was to be done. Notice the sabbath came first. Before any yearly feast, the weekly sabbath was named. Rest was the foundation of the whole calendar. Notice six days… but the seventh. Work was good. But it was not everything. Rest reminded Israel that their lives depended on God, not their labor. Notice in all your dwellings. The sabbath was not only kept at the tabernacle. It reached every home. Mark 2 records Jesus saying, The sabbath was made for man. Matthew 11 records Him saying, Come unto me… and I will give you rest. Christ is our true rest. Today, practice rest. Step away from work and trust God. Find your deepest rest in Christ.
S: Israel was to work six days, but the seventh was the sabbath of rest and a holy convocation in all their dwellings. Rest was the foundation of God's calendar, reminding them their lives depended on Him. Practice rest, trust God, and find your deepest rest in Christ.
P: Rest first. Leviticus 23:3: 'the seventh day is the sabbath of rest.' Rest reminds us we depend on God. Come to Christ and find rest today.

== 23:4
T: In their seasons
R: 'These are the feasts of the LORD, even holy convocations, which ye shall proclaim in their seasons.' These were the LORD's appointed festivals, sacred assemblies to be proclaimed at their set times. Notice in their seasons. Each feast had its time. They could not be moved for convenience. God's timing structured the year. Ecclesiastes 3 says, To every thing there is a season. God's appointed times bring order and meaning. Notice proclaim. The feasts were announced publicly. Israel did not forget them. They were proclaimed so everyone could prepare. Notice that the feasts followed the agricultural year. Spring harvest, summer harvest, autumn harvest. God connected worship with ordinary life. The rhythms of work and worship were woven together. Galatians 4 says, when the fulness of the time was come, God sent forth his Son. God's timing in history is also perfect. Today, trust God's timing in your life. Each season has its purpose.
S: These were the feasts of the LORD, holy convocations proclaimed in their seasons. Each had its set time, weaving worship into the rhythm of the farming year. Trust God's timing in your life, for each season has its purpose.
P: In their seasons. Leviticus 23:4: 'which ye shall proclaim in their seasons.' To every thing there is a season. Trust God's timing in your life today.

== 23:5
T: The LORD's passover
R: 'In the fourteenth day of the first month at even is the LORD'S passover.' The LORD's Passover began at twilight on the fourteenth day of the first month. Notice the first month. Passover marked the start of Israel's religious year. Redemption was the beginning. Exodus 12 says, This month shall be unto you the beginning of months. Their life as a nation began with deliverance. Notice the LORD'S passover. It belonged to God. He passed over the houses marked with blood. The lamb died, and the firstborn lived. First Corinthians 5 says, Christ our passover is sacrificed for us. Jesus died at Passover. He is the true Passover Lamb. His blood marks us, and judgment passes over. Notice at even. Twilight, as the day ended. Jesus also died in the afternoon, near evening. Today, rejoice that Christ is your Passover. His blood covers you, and judgment passes over.
S: The LORD's passover began at even on the fourteenth day of the first month. Redemption began Israel's year, and Christ our passover was sacrificed for us. Rejoice that Christ is your Passover, whose blood covers you so judgment passes over.
P: Christ our passover. Leviticus 23:5: 'In the fourteenth day of the first month at even is the LORD'S passover.' His blood covers you. Judgment passes over.

== 23:6
T: Unleavened bread
R: 'And on the fifteenth day of the same month is the feast of unleavened bread unto the LORD: seven days ye must eat unleavened bread.' The day after Passover began the Feast of Unleavened Bread, lasting seven days. Notice unleavened bread. In Exodus 12, Israel left Egypt in haste. There was no time for bread to rise. Unleavened bread recalled their hurried escape. Notice that leaven often pictures sin in Scripture. First Corinthians 5 says, Purge out therefore the old leaven… For even Christ our passover is sacrificed for us: Therefore let us keep the feast, not with old leaven… but with the unleavened bread of sincerity and truth. After Passover comes a life free from the leaven of sin. Notice seven days. A complete period. Redemption leads to a whole life of purity. Today, having been redeemed by Christ, put away the old leaven. Live with sincerity and truth.
S: The feast of unleavened bread followed Passover for seven days. It recalled the hasty exodus and pictured a redeemed life purged of the leaven of sin. Having been redeemed by Christ, put away the old leaven and live with sincerity and truth.
P: Purge out the leaven. Leviticus 23:6: 'seven days ye must eat unleavened bread.' Redeemed by Christ, live each day with sincerity and truth before Him.

== 23:7
T: No servile work
R: 'In the first day ye shall have an holy convocation: ye shall do no servile work therein.' On the first day of the feast, Israel held a sacred assembly and did no regular work. Notice no servile work. Ordinary labor stopped. Israel had been slaves in Egypt, forced to work without rest. Now, as free people, they rested and worshiped. Their rest was a sign of their freedom. Deuteronomy 5 connects the sabbath with remembering slavery: remember that thou wast a servant in the land of Egypt. Rest proclaimed redemption. Notice an holy convocation. They gathered together. Freedom was celebrated in community. Galatians 5 says, Stand fast therefore in the liberty wherewith Christ hath made us free, and be not entangled again with the yoke of bondage. Today, rest as a free person. You are no longer a slave to sin. Celebrate your freedom in Christ with His people.
S: On the first day of the feast there was a holy convocation with no servile work. Former slaves rested and worshiped as free people, proclaiming their redemption. Rest as a free person in Christ and celebrate your freedom with His people.
P: Rest as free people. Leviticus 23:7: 'ye shall do no servile work therein.' Former slaves now rest. Stand fast in the liberty Christ has given you.

== 23:8
T: Seven days of offering
R: 'But ye shall offer an offering made by fire unto the LORD seven days: in the seventh day is an holy convocation: ye shall do no servile work therein.' For seven days, Israel presented food offerings to the LORD. The seventh day was another sacred assembly with no regular work. Notice seven days of offerings. The feast was not just one day of worship. It lasted all week. Each day had its offering. Worship continued. Notice the first and seventh days. The feast began and ended with holy assemblies. It was framed by gathering. Romans 12 says, present your bodies a living sacrifice. Not one day, but every day. Our whole lives are offerings. Psalm 145 says, Every day will I bless thee. Daily worship is the pattern for believers. Today, offer yourself to God again. Make every day of your week an offering to Him.
S: For seven days Israel offered an offering made by fire, with a holy convocation on the seventh day. Worship continued every day of the feast, framed by gathering. Offer yourself to God again and make every day of your week an offering.
P: Every day. Leviticus 23:8: 'ye shall offer an offering made by fire unto the LORD seven days.' Every day will I bless Thee. Make each day an offering to God.

== 23:9
T: When you come into the land
R: 'And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying.' God spoke to Moses again, giving instructions for a feast that could only be kept after Israel entered the land. Notice that God was looking forward. Israel was in the wilderness. They had no fields to harvest. Yet God gave them harvest laws. He was promising that they would have a harvest. His instructions contained His promises. Notice the confidence. God did not say if you come into the land, but when. His promise was certain. Numbers 23 says, God is not a man, that he should lie. What He promises, He fulfills. Philippians 1 says, he which hath begun a good work in you will perform it. Today, trust God's promises for your future. He speaks of what is coming with certainty. Prepare for His blessings even before you see them.
S: The LORD spoke to Moses again, giving laws for a harvest Israel could only reap in the land. God's instructions contained His certain promises for their future. Trust God's promises for your future and prepare for His blessings before you see them.
P: When, not if. Leviticus 23:9: 'And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying.' Harvest laws in the wilderness. God's promises are certain. Trust Him today.

== 23:10
T: The firstfruits
R: 'Speak unto the children of Israel… When ye be come into the land which I give unto you, and shall reap the harvest thereof, then ye shall bring a sheaf of the firstfruits of your harvest unto the priest.' When Israel entered the land and reaped their first harvest, they were to bring the first sheaf to the priest. Notice the firstfruits. The first portion of the harvest went to God. Before Israel enjoyed the crop, they honored the Giver. Notice the land which I give unto you. The land was a gift. The harvest was a gift. The firstfruits acknowledged this. First Corinthians 15 says, now is Christ risen from the dead, and become the firstfruits of them that slept. Christ rose on the day of Firstfruits. His resurrection was the first sheaf of a great harvest. All who belong to Him will rise too. Today, rejoice that Christ is the firstfruits of the resurrection. And give God the first of what you receive.
S: When Israel reaped their first harvest in the land, they were to bring a sheaf of the firstfruits to the priest. The first portion honored the Giver, and Christ rose as the firstfruits of the resurrection. Rejoice in Christ's resurrection and give God the first of what you receive.
P: Christ the firstfruits. Leviticus 23:10: 'ye shall bring a sheaf of the firstfruits of your harvest.' Christ is risen, the firstfruits of them that slept. Rejoice today.

== 23:11
T: The morrow after the sabbath
R: 'And he shall wave the sheaf before the LORD, to be accepted for you: on the morrow after the sabbath the priest shall wave it.' The priest waved the sheaf before the LORD, so it would be accepted on their behalf. He did this on the day after the sabbath. Notice on the morrow after the sabbath. The first day of the week. Jesus rose on the first day of the week, the day after the sabbath. Matthew 28 says, In the end of the sabbath, as it began to dawn toward the first day of the week… He is not here: for he is risen. The firstfruits sheaf was waved on resurrection morning. Notice to be accepted for you. The firstfruits represented the whole harvest. If the first sheaf was accepted, the whole harvest was blessed. Christ's resurrection guarantees ours. Romans 4 says He was raised again for our justification. Today, celebrate the resurrection. Christ rose on the first day, and in Him you are accepted.
S: The priest waved the sheaf before the LORD on the morrow after the sabbath, to be accepted for the people. Jesus rose on that very first day of the week, the firstfruits guaranteeing the whole harvest. Celebrate the resurrection, for in the risen Christ you are accepted.
P: Resurrection morning. Leviticus 23:11: 'on the morrow after the sabbath the priest shall wave it.' Christ rose on the first day. In Him, you are accepted.

The blessing of harvest was joined with dedication to God. Notice without blemish. Perfection again. The burnt offering expressed total dedication. Notice that Christ is both the Lamb and the Firstfruits. He died as the Lamb and rose as the Firstfruits. Both pictures meet in Him. Revelation 5 shows a Lamb as it had been slain, now standing alive. The slain Lamb is the risen Lord. Today, worship Christ, the Lamb who was slain and is alive forever. == 23:13
T: Bread and wine
R: 'And the meat offering thereof shall be two tenth deals of fine flour mingled with oil… and the drink offering thereof shall be of wine, the fourth part of an hin.' With the lamb, Israel offered fine flour mixed with oil and a drink offering of wine. Notice the grain and wine. Bread and wine accompanied the sacrifice. These are the elements of the Lord's Supper. Luke 22 shows Jesus taking bread and wine at Passover. This is my body… This cup is the new testament in my blood. The ancient offerings anticipated the table of the Lord. Notice the drink offering. Wine was poured out. Philippians 2 records Paul saying, if I be offered upon the sacrifice and service of your faith, I joy. Paul saw his own life as a drink offering, poured out for others. Notice sweet savour. God delighted in these offerings. Today, remember Christ's body and blood. And let your life be poured out in service, a sweet savour to God.
S: With the lamb came fine flour mingled with oil and a drink offering of wine. Bread and wine anticipated the Lord's Supper, and Paul saw his life as a drink offering. Remember Christ's body and blood and let your life be poured out in service.

== 23:14
T: God first
R: 'And ye shall eat neither bread, nor parched corn, nor green ears, until the selfsame day that ye have brought an offering unto your God.' Israel was not to eat any bread, roasted grain, or new grain until they had brought their offering to God. Notice until. They had to wait. The new harvest was in front of them. They were hungry. But God came first. They could not eat until they honored Him. Notice how hard this would be. After a long winter, fresh grain was ready. But they waited until God received His portion. Matthew 6 says, seek ye first the kingdom of God, and his righteousness; and all these things shall be added unto you. God first, then everything else. Proverbs 3 says, Honour the LORD… with the firstfruits of all thine increase: So shall thy barns be filled with plenty. Today, put God first before you enjoy His gifts. Honor Him first with your time, money, and attention.
S: Israel was to eat no bread, parched corn or green ears until they had brought their offering to God. Even when hungry for the new harvest, they honored God first. Put God first before you enjoy His gifts, honoring Him with your time, money and attention.

R: 'And ye shall count unto you from the morrow after the sabbath, from the day that ye brought the sheaf of the wave offering; seven sabbaths shall be complete.' From the day of the firstfruits sheaf, Israel was to count seven full weeks. Notice count. Each day was numbered. Israel counted toward the next feast with anticipation. Waiting had purpose. Every day moved them closer. Notice seven sabbaths. Seven weeks, forty-nine days. A complete period. Notice what this pictures. After Christ's resurrection, the disciples waited. Acts 1 says Jesus told them to wait for the promise of the Father. They counted the days from resurrection to Pentecost. Their waiting was full of expectation. Acts 1 says they continued with one accord in prayer. Today, wait on God with expectation. Count the days with faith. He keeps His appointments.

== 23:16
T: Fifty days
R: 'Even unto the morrow after the seventh sabbath shall ye number fifty days; and ye shall offer a new meat offering unto the LORD.' On the day after the seventh sabbath, the fiftieth day, Israel presented an offering of new grain to the LORD. Notice fifty days. The Greek word for fiftieth is Pentecost. This feast became known by that name. Notice what happened on this day in Acts 2. When the day of Pentecost was fully come… they were all filled with the Holy Ghost. God poured out His Spirit on the very feast day. Notice a new meat offering. Something new was offered. At Pentecost, something new began: the church. The Spirit came, and three thousand were saved. The firstfruits of the church's harvest. Today, rejoice in the gift of the Holy Spirit. God's appointed time came, and the Spirit was poured out. Be filled with Him.
S: On the fiftieth day Israel offered a new meat offering to the LORD, the feast later called Pentecost. On that very day God poured out His Spirit and the church began. Rejoice in the gift of the Holy Spirit and be filled with Him.

== 23:17
T: Baken with leaven
R: 'Ye shall bring out of your habitations two wave loaves of two tenth deals: they shall be of fine flour; they shall be baken with leaven; they are the firstfruits unto the LORD.' From their homes, Israel brought two loaves made with fine flour and baked with yeast, as a wave offering of firstfruits. Notice baken with leaven. Unusual. Most grain offerings had no leaven. But these loaves did. Many see here a picture of the church. The church is made of real people, still imperfect, still with sin. Yet God accepts them as firstfruits. Notice two loaves. Some see Jew and Gentile, two peoples made one in Christ. Ephesians 2 says He hath made both one. Notice out of your habitations. The bread came from ordinary homes. James 1 says believers are a kind of firstfruits of his creatures. Today, rejoice that God accepts imperfect people in Christ. You are part of His firstfruits.
S: Israel brought from their homes two wave loaves baked with leaven as firstfruits to the LORD. The leavened loaves picture the church of imperfect people accepted by God, Jew and Gentile made one. Rejoice that God accepts imperfect people in Christ and makes you part of His firstfruits.
P: Firstfruits with leaven. Leviticus 23:17: 'two wave loaves… baken with leaven; they are the firstfruits.' God accepts imperfect people in Christ. You are His firstfruits.

Seven lambs, a bull, two rams. A generous offering. Pentecost was a great celebration. Today, rest in Christ's perfect sacrifice. Your imperfection is covered by His perfection.

== 23:22
T: Even at the feast
R: 'And when ye reap the harvest of your land, thou shalt not make clean riddance of the corners of thy field… thou shalt leave them unto the poor, and to the stranger: I am the LORD your God.' In the middle of the festival laws, God repeated: when harvesting, leave the edges of your fields and the gleanings for the poor and foreigner. Notice the placement. Right after Pentecost, God reminded them of the poor. Celebration must include compassion. Worship and justice belong together. Isaiah 58 says the true fast is to deal thy bread to the hungry. God is not pleased with festivals that ignore the needy. Notice that this law appeared in chapter 19 too. God repeated it here, in the context of feasts. Acts 2 says the early church, after Pentecost, sold their possessions and parted them to all men, as every man had need. The Spirit's coming produced generosity. Today, let your worship lead to generosity. Remember the poor even in your celebrations.
S: Right after Pentecost, God repeated that Israel must leave the corners and gleanings for the poor and stranger. Celebration was to include compassion, as the Spirit-filled church shared with all in need. Let your worship lead to generosity and remember the poor even in your celebrations.
P: Remember the poor. Leviticus 23:22: 'thou shalt leave them unto the poor, and to the stranger.' Worship and generosity belong together. Share with those in need today.
