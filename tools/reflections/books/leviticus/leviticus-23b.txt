# Leviticus 23:23-44: Trumpets, Atonement and Tabernacles

== 23:23
T: The seventh month
R: 'And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying.' God spoke to Moses again. Now the calendar turned to the seventh month and its feasts. Notice the gap. The spring feasts were close together. Then came the summer, then the fall feasts. Many have seen in this pattern a picture of God's plan. The spring feasts were fulfilled in Christ's first coming: Passover at His death, Firstfruits at His resurrection, Pentecost at the Spirit's coming. The fall feasts still look ahead to what God will yet do. Notice the seventh month. Seven signifies completion. The fall feasts complete the year. They speak of completion and fulfillment. Philippians 1 says, he which hath begun a good work in you will perform it until the day of Jesus Christ. What God begins, He completes. Today, trust that God will complete His plan. What He has started in history and in you, He will finish.
S: The LORD spoke to Moses about the feasts of the seventh month. The spring feasts were fulfilled in Christ's first coming, while the autumn feasts speak of completion still ahead. Trust that God will complete what He has begun in history and in you.
P: He will complete it. Leviticus 23:23: 'And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying.' What God begins, He completes. Trust His plan for history and for you.

== 23:24
T: Blowing of trumpets
R: 'Speak unto the children of Israel, saying, In the seventh month, in the first day of the month, shall ye have a sabbath, a memorial of blowing of trumpets, an holy convocation.' On the first day of the seventh month, Israel was to have a day of rest, a sacred assembly announced with trumpet blasts. Notice the trumpets. Trumpets called the people together. They announced important events. They warned of danger and called to battle. Notice a memorial. The trumpets reminded God and the people. Numbers 10 says the trumpets would be a memorial before your God. First Thessalonians 4 says, the Lord himself shall descend from heaven with a shout… and with the trump of God. First Corinthians 15 says, at the last trump… the dead shall be raised. A trumpet will announce Christ's return. Today, listen for the trumpet. Live ready for Christ's return. The trumpet will sound, and He will come.
S: On the first day of the seventh month, Israel kept a sabbath, a memorial of blowing of trumpets. Trumpets gathered and alerted God's people, and a trumpet will announce Christ's return. Live ready for Christ's return, for the trumpet will sound.
P: The trump of God. Leviticus 23:24: 'a memorial of blowing of trumpets.' The Lord shall descend with the trump of God. Live ready for His return today.

== 23:25
T: An offering made by fire
R: 'Ye shall do no servile work therein: but ye shall offer an offering made by fire unto the LORD.' On the Feast of Trumpets, Israel did no regular work and presented a food offering to the LORD. Notice no servile work. Rest again. The trumpet called the people from work to worship. When God calls, ordinary labor stops. Notice but ye shall offer. Rest was not idleness. It was redirected toward God. The day was filled with worship. True rest is active toward God. Notice how the trumpet pattern works. The trumpet sounded; the people stopped and turned to God. When God calls us, we should respond. Hebrews 3 says, To day if ye will hear his voice, harden not your hearts. The call of God requires a response. Isaiah 55 says, Seek ye the LORD while he may be found. Today, respond when God calls. Set aside your work, and turn toward Him in worship.
S: On the Feast of Trumpets, Israel did no servile work but offered an offering made by fire to the LORD. When the trumpet called, ordinary labor stopped and turned to worship. Respond when God calls, setting aside your work to turn to Him.
P: Respond to His call. Leviticus 23:25: 'ye shall do no servile work therein: but ye shall offer.' To day if ye will hear His voice, harden not your hearts.

== 23:26
T: Ten days later
R: 'And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying.' God spoke again about the next feast, which came only ten days after Trumpets: the Day of Atonement. Notice the timing. Trumpets announced; Atonement followed. The trumpet call was followed by a day of deep repentance. Jewish tradition calls these ten days the Days of Awe, a time of reflection and repentance before the great day. They were days to search the heart and make things right with others. Notice the order. A call, then a cleansing. God summons His people, then makes them clean. Joel 2 says, Blow the trumpet in Zion, sanctify a fast, call a solemn assembly. The trumpet calls to repentance. Second Corinthians 7 says, godly sorrow worketh repentance to salvation. Today, let God's call lead you to repentance. Use quiet days to examine your heart before Him.
S: The LORD spoke again about the Day of Atonement, coming ten days after the Feast of Trumpets. The trumpet call was followed by a season of reflection and repentance. Let God's call lead you to examine your heart and repent before Him.
P: A call to repent. Leviticus 23:26: 'And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying.' Blow the trumpet; sanctify a fast. Let God's call lead you to examine your heart today.

== 23:27
T: A day of atonement
R: 'Also on the tenth day of this seventh month there shall be a day of atonement: it shall be an holy convocation unto you; and ye shall afflict your souls.' The tenth day of the seventh month was the Day of Atonement, a sacred assembly when Israel humbled themselves and presented offerings. Notice a day of atonement. The holiest day of Israel's year. Chapter 16 described it in detail. Here it takes its place in the calendar. Notice afflict your souls. The people humbled themselves, likely through fasting. It was a solemn day, not a festive one. Some days call for joy; others for sorrow over sin. Ecclesiastes 3 says, A time to weep, and a time to laugh. Notice the place of atonement in the year. Between the trumpet call and the harvest celebration. Cleansing came before rejoicing. Today, take time to humble yourself before God. Let honest sorrow over sin lead you to the joy of forgiveness.
S: The tenth day of the seventh month was the day of atonement, a holy convocation when the people afflicted their souls. It came between the trumpet call and the harvest joy, as cleansing comes before rejoicing. Take time to humble yourself before God and let sorrow over sin lead to the joy of forgiveness.
P: A time to weep. Leviticus 23:27: 'there shall be a day of atonement… and ye shall afflict your souls.' Cleansing comes before rejoicing. Humble yourself before God today.

== 23:28
T: Before the LORD your God
R: 'And ye shall do no work in that same day: for it is a day of atonement, to make an atonement for you before the LORD your God.' Israel did no work on that day, because it was the Day of Atonement, when atonement was made for them before the LORD. Notice no work. On the Day of Atonement, the people could not work. Atonement was not their doing. The high priest did the work. The people rested and received. Salvation is the same. Titus 3 says, Not by works of righteousness which we have done, but according to his mercy he saved us. We cannot work for our atonement. It is done for us. Notice to make an atonement for you. For you. Personal. The atonement was on their behalf. Galatians 2 says Christ loved me, and gave himself for me. Today, stop trying to earn God's forgiveness. Rest in the atonement Christ has made for you.
S: Israel did no work on the day of atonement, for atonement was made for them before the LORD. The people rested and received while the high priest worked, as salvation is not by our works. Stop trying to earn forgiveness and rest in the atonement Christ made for you.
P: Not by works. Leviticus 23:28: 'ye shall do no work… for it is a day of atonement.' Atonement is done for you. Rest in what Christ has done.

== 23:29
T: Not afflicted
R: 'For whatsoever soul it be that shall not be afflicted in that same day, he shall be cut off from among his people.' Anyone who did not humble himself on that day would be cut off from his people. Notice shall not be afflicted. Refusing to humble oneself was serious. Pride on the day of atonement showed contempt for God's provision. The proud person was saying, I do not need this. Notice cut off. Those who refused humility excluded themselves. Atonement is received with a humble heart. James 4 says, God resisteth the proud, but giveth grace unto the humble. Luke 18 shows the proud Pharisee and the humble tax collector. Only the humble one went home justified. Today, approach God humbly. Pride shuts the door to grace. Humility opens it.
S: Whoever was not afflicted on the day of atonement was to be cut off from among his people. Refusing humility showed contempt for God's provision and shut the door to grace. Approach God humbly, for He resists the proud and gives grace to the humble.
P: Grace to the humble. Leviticus 23:29: 'whatsoever soul it be that shall not be afflicted.' Pride shuts the door to grace; humility opens it. Humble yourself today.

== 23:30
T: Doeth any work
R: 'And whatsoever soul it be that doeth any work in that same day, the same soul will I destroy from among his people.' God would destroy from among the people anyone who did work on that day. Notice doeth any work. Working on the Day of Atonement was as serious as refusing to humble oneself. Why? Because working implied self-reliance. It suggested that one could add to the atonement or did not need it. Notice that both pride and self-effort were rejected. Verse 29 condemned the proud. Verse 30 condemned the self-reliant. Both refuse God's grace. Galatians 2 says, if righteousness come by the law, then Christ is dead in vain. If we could earn salvation, Christ's death would be pointless. Romans 4 says, to him that worketh not, but believeth… his faith is counted for righteousness. Today, stop trusting your own efforts for salvation. Believe in Christ, and rest in His finished work.
S: Whoever did any work on the day of atonement would be destroyed from among his people. Working implied self-reliance, as if atonement needed human effort. Stop trusting your own efforts for salvation and rest in Christ's finished work.
P: Rest, don't work. Leviticus 23:30: 'whatsoever soul it be that doeth any work in that same day.' To him that worketh not, but believeth, faith is counted for righteousness.

== 23:31
T: No manner of work
R: 'Ye shall do no manner of work: it shall be a statute for ever throughout your generations in all your dwellings.' Israel was to do no work at all on that day. It was a lasting command for every generation and every home. Notice no manner of work. Not even small tasks. Complete rest. The emphasis was total. The day belonged entirely to God. Notice in all your dwellings. Not just at the tabernacle, but in every home. Every family stopped. Every household rested. The whole nation paused before God. Notice the repetition. Verses 28, 30, and 31 all forbid work. God emphasized the point three times. Atonement is entirely God's work. We contribute nothing. Ephesians 2 says, For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man should boast. Today, rest fully in God's grace. You cannot add anything to what Christ has done.
S: Israel was to do no manner of work on that day, a statute for ever in all their dwellings. God repeated the point three times, stressing that atonement is entirely His work. Rest fully in God's grace, for you cannot add anything to what Christ has done.
P: Not of works. Leviticus 23:31: 'Ye shall do no manner of work.' By grace are ye saved, not of works. Rest fully in what Christ has done.

== 23:32
T: From even unto even
R: 'It shall be unto you a sabbath of rest, and ye shall afflict your souls: in the ninth day of the month at even, from even unto even, shall ye celebrate your sabbath.' The Day of Atonement was a sabbath of rest. Israel humbled themselves from the evening of the ninth day to the evening of the tenth. Notice from even unto even. The day began at sunset. In Israel, days started in the evening. Genesis 1 says, the evening and the morning were the first day. God's days begin in darkness and move toward light. Notice a sabbath of rest and afflict your souls together. Rest and repentance. Stillness and sorrow. These are not opposites. Humble repentance leads to deep rest. Isaiah 30 says, In returning and rest shall ye be saved; in quietness and in confidence shall be your strength. Returning to God brings rest. Today, return to God in humble repentance. Find the deep rest that comes from being forgiven.
S: The day of atonement was a sabbath of rest and humility, kept from even unto even. Days began in darkness and moved to light, and repentance led to deep rest. Return to God in humble repentance and find the rest of being forgiven.

Psalm 30 says, weeping may endure for a night, but joy cometh in the morning. Isaiah 61 promises the oil of joy for mourning. Notice that God planned joy. He did not merely permit celebration. He commanded it. Joy was part of His design for His people. Nehemiah 8 says, neither be ye sorry; for the joy of the LORD is your strength. Today, remember that God's story moves toward joy. == 23:34
T: The feast of tabernacles
R: 'Speak unto the children of Israel, saying, The fifteenth day of this seventh month shall be the feast of tabernacles for seven days unto the LORD.' The Feast of Tabernacles began on the fifteenth day of the seventh month and lasted seven days. Notice tabernacles. Booths, temporary shelters. Israel lived in them during the feast. It recalled their wilderness journey. Notice that tabernacle also describes God's dwelling. John 1 says, the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us. The Greek word for dwelt means tabernacled. Christ came and lived among us in a temporary body, like a tent. Revelation 21 says, the tabernacle of God is with men, and he will dwell with them. The feast looks forward to God dwelling with His people forever. Today, rejoice that God came to dwell among us in Christ. And look forward to the day He will dwell with us forever.
S: The feast of tabernacles began on the fifteenth day of the seventh month for seven days. It recalled the wilderness booths and points to Christ, who tabernacled among us, and to God dwelling with His people forever. Rejoice that God came to dwell among us and look forward to dwelling with Him forever.
P: God with us. Leviticus 23:34: 'the feast of tabernacles for seven days unto the LORD.' The Word tabernacled among us. One day God will dwell with us forever.

Second Corinthians 5 calls our bodies an earthly house of this tabernacle. Our present life is a tent. Our permanent home is with God. Today, hold earthly things loosely. You are a pilgrim. Look for the city God is building.
