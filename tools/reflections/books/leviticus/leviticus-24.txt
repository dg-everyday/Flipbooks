# Leviticus 24: The lamp, the bread and the holy name

== 24:1
T: Daily things
R: 'And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying.' After the yearly calendar of feasts, God spoke to Moses about daily and weekly tasks in the tabernacle: the lamps and the bread. Notice the shift. Chapter 23 covered the great feasts. Chapter 24 turns to the ordinary rhythm of daily worship. Big festivals matter, but so do daily faithfulness. The lamp had to be tended every night. The bread had to be replaced every week. Notice that God cared about both. The spectacular and the routine both belong to Him. Much of the Christian life is daily faithfulness. Prayer, Scripture, and small acts of love. Lamentations 3 says His mercies are new every morning. Luke 9 says, take up his cross daily. Today, honor God in daily routines. Faithfulness in ordinary days matters as much as great celebrations.
S: After the feasts, the LORD spoke to Moses about the daily lamps and weekly bread. God cared about the routine as well as the spectacular. Honor God in your daily routines, for faithfulness in ordinary days matters.
P: Daily faithfulness. Leviticus 24:1: 'And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying.' After the feasts came daily tasks. Honor God in your ordinary routines today.

== 24:2
T: Pure oil olive beaten
R: 'Command the children of Israel, that they bring unto thee pure oil olive beaten for the light, to cause the lamps to burn continually.' The Israelites were to bring pure oil of pressed olives for the light, so the lamps would keep burning. Notice the children of Israel. The people supplied the oil. Their gifts kept the light burning. Everyone shared in maintaining worship. Notice pure oil olive beaten. Pressed olives gave the purest oil. The process involved crushing. Some see a picture here. Light comes through pressure. Christ was crushed in Gethsemane, whose name means oil press. From His suffering came light for the world. Notice continually. The light never went out. Matthew 5 records Jesus saying, Ye are the light of the world… Let your light so shine before men. Today, let your light keep shining. Even when pressed by trials, God can bring pure light through you.
S: The people were to bring pure oil olive beaten for the light, to keep the lamps burning continually. Their gifts kept the light burning, and the crushed olive pictures light coming through pressure. Let your light keep shining, for even in trials God can bring pure light through you.
P: Beaten for the light. Leviticus 24:2: 'pure oil olive beaten for the light.' Light comes through pressure. Let your light shine before men today.

Notice twelve cakes. One for each tribe of Israel. All twelve were represented before God. No tribe was forgotten. Every part of God's people had a place at His table. Notice fine flour. The best ingredients. Each loaf was large and generous. God's people were represented by what was excellent. Notice that this bread was called the showbread, or bread of the presence. It was always before God's face. Israel was always in His sight. Jesus chose twelve apostles, echoing the twelve tribes. Revelation 21 shows the twelve tribes' names on the gates of the New Jerusalem. God's people are always before Him. Today, know that you are never forgotten. You have a place before God, like the twelve loaves.

The old bread belonged to Aaron and his sons. They ate it in the holy place, because it was a most holy part of the offerings to the LORD. Notice they shall eat it. == 24:18

R: 'And he that killeth a beast shall make it good; beast for beast.' Anyone who killed someone's animal had to make restitution, life for life. Notice make it good. Killing an animal was a property loss. The owner had to be compensated. The response was restitution, not death. Notice the distinction. Human life required life. Animal life required replacement. God's law distinguished between humans and animals. Humans bear God's image; animals do not. Yet animals still mattered. Their loss had to be repaid. Notice that God cared about the owner's livelihood. Notice beast for beast. Fair compensation. Not more, not less. Justice was proportional. Exodus 22 gives more detail on restitution laws. Zacchaeus in Luke 19 restored fourfold what he had taken. Grace made him generous in repairing wrongs. Today, take responsibility for any loss you cause others. Make things right fairly and fully.
S: Whoever killed someone's beast had to make it good, beast for beast. Animal loss required restitution, distinguishing it from the taking of human life. Take responsibility for any loss you cause others and make things right fairly.

== 24:19
T: As he hath done
R: 'And if a man cause a blemish in his neighbour; as he hath done, so shall it be done to him.' Anyone who injured his neighbor would receive the same injury in return. Notice as he hath done. The principle of proportional justice. The punishment matched the crime. This was not cruel. It was a limit. In the ancient world, revenge often went far beyond the original harm. A person might be killed for a minor injury. This law prevented excessive revenge. Notice his neighbour. The injury was against a neighbor. The law protected community relationships. Genesis 4 shows Lamech boasting, I have slain a man to my wounding… If Cain shall be avenged sevenfold, truly Lamech seventy and sevenfold. Unlimited revenge was the world's way. God set limits. Today, never seek more revenge than justice requires. Better still, leave vengeance to God.
S: Whoever caused a blemish in his neighbour would have the same done to him. The principle limited revenge to proportional justice, unlike the unlimited vengeance of Lamech. Never seek more than justice requires, and better still, leave vengeance to God.
P: Limits on revenge. Leviticus 24:19: 'as he hath done, so shall it be done to him.' God limited revenge. Better still, leave vengeance to Him.

The injury given would be the injury received. Notice eye for eye. This famous principle was meant for courts, not personal revenge. It set the maximum penalty. Judges applied it, often through compensation rather than literal injury. It ensured fairness. Notice what Jesus said. Matthew 5 records Jesus saying, Ye have heard that it hath been said, An eye for an eye, and a tooth for a tooth: But I say unto you, That ye resist not evil: but whosoever shall smite thee on thy right cheek, turn to him the other also. Jesus did not abolish justice. But He called His followers to go beyond it, to grace. Romans 12 says, overcome evil with good. Today, let go of personal revenge. Respond to wrongs with grace, as Jesus taught.
