# Leviticus 25:29-55: Redemption of houses, help for the poor and release of servants

== 25:29
T: Within a full year
R: 'And if a man sell a dwelling house in a walled city, then he may redeem it within a whole year after it is sold; within a full year may he redeem it.' Someone who sold a house in a walled city could buy it back within one year of the sale. Notice a walled city. City houses were different from farmland. They were not part of the family's agricultural inheritance. Different rules applied. Notice within a full year. The window for redemption was limited. After a year, the opportunity passed. Some opportunities do not last forever. Second Corinthians 6 says, behold, now is the accepted time; behold, now is the day of salvation. God's offers have timing. Notice that there was still a window. A year to reconsider, to gather funds, to reclaim. God gave time. Second Peter 3 says God is longsuffering… not willing that any should perish. He gives time to respond. Today, do not delay responding to God. Now is the accepted time.
S: A man who sold a house in a walled city could redeem it within a full year. The window was limited, as God gives time to respond but some opportunities pass. Do not delay responding to God, for now is the accepted time.
P: Now is the time. Leviticus 25:29: 'within a full year may he redeem it.' Some windows close. Now is the accepted time; respond to God today.

== 25:30
T: Established for ever
R: 'And if it be not redeemed within the space of a full year, then the house that is in the walled city shall be established for ever to him that bought it… it shall not go out in the jubile.' If the city house was not redeemed within a year, it belonged permanently to the buyer and his descendants. It would not be returned in the jubilee. Notice it shall not go out in the jubile. City houses were an exception. They were not tied to the family's ancestral land. Notice the difference between land and houses. Farmland was God's gift to each family, part of their inheritance. City houses were more like ordinary property. The jubilee protected inheritance, not every possession. Notice that missed opportunities can become final. Hebrews 12 tells of Esau, who found no place of repentance, though he sought it carefully with tears. Some choices cannot be undone. Today, value what God has given as your true inheritance. And act on opportunities while they remain.
S: A city house not redeemed within a full year was established for ever to the buyer and did not go out in the jubile. The jubile protected family inheritance, not every possession, and missed windows could become final. Value your true inheritance from God and act on opportunities while they remain.
P: Don't miss the window. Leviticus 25:30: 'it shall not go out in the jubile.' Some choices become final. Act on God's opportunities while they remain.

== 25:31
T: The villages
R: 'But the houses of the villages which have no wall round about them shall be counted as the fields of the country: they may be redeemed, and they shall go out in the jubile.' Houses in unwalled villages were treated like farmland. They could be redeemed and were returned in the jubilee. Notice the villages. Rural homes were part of the agricultural life. They were connected to the land. So they were protected like the fields. Notice they shall go out in the jubile. Village families were guaranteed their homes back. The jubilee protected rural communities. Small farmers were not permanently displaced. Notice God's care for the vulnerable. City dwellers were often wealthier. Village farmers were more vulnerable to losing everything. God's law gave extra protection to those most at risk. Psalm 72 says, He shall deliver the needy when he crieth; the poor also, and him that hath no helper. Today, notice how God protects the vulnerable. Seek ways to protect those most at risk around you.
S: Houses in unwalled villages were counted as fields, able to be redeemed and returned in the jubile. God gave extra protection to vulnerable rural families who could lose everything. Notice how God protects the vulnerable and seek ways to protect those most at risk around you.

== 25:32
T: At any time
R: 'Notwithstanding the cities of the Levites, and the houses of the cities of their possession, may the Levites redeem at any time.' The Levites always had the right to redeem their houses in their own cities. Notice at any time. Unlike other city dwellers, Levites had no time limit. They could always redeem. Their homes were specially protected. Notice why. Levites had no tribal territory. They lived in forty-eight scattered cities. Their homes were their only inheritance. God protected it with special care. Numbers 18 says, I am thy part and thine inheritance. The LORD was the Levites' inheritance. Their houses were their only earthly possession. Notice that God cares for those who serve Him. He made sure His servants were never permanently homeless. Matthew 6 says, seek ye first the kingdom of God… and all these things shall be added unto you. Today, trust that God cares for those who serve Him. If you give your life to His work, He will provide for you.
S: The Levites could redeem the houses in their cities at any time. With no tribal land, their homes were specially protected because the LORD was their inheritance. Trust that God cares for those who serve Him and will provide for you as you seek His kingdom.
P: God cares for His servants. Leviticus 25:32: 'may the Levites redeem at any time.' The LORD was their inheritance. Seek first His kingdom; He provides.

== 25:35
T: Thou shalt relieve him
R: 'And if thy brother be waxen poor, and fallen in decay with thee; then thou shalt relieve him: yea, though he be a stranger, or a sojourner; that he may live with thee.' If a fellow Israelite became poor and could not support himself, Israel was to help him as they would a foreigner or temporary resident, so he could continue living among them. Notice thou shalt relieve him. Helping the poor was not optional. It was commanded. God's people must support those in need. Notice fallen in decay. The person's situation had collapsed. He needed someone to hold him up. Notice that he may live with thee. The goal was keeping him in the community. Not pushing him out. Restoring him. == 25:36

They were to fear God, so their brother could continue living among them. Notice no usury. No interest on loans to the poor. Lending to the needy was an act of help, not a business opportunity. Profiting from someone's poverty was forbidden. Notice but fear thy God. Again, the fear of God was the safeguard. When we remember that God watches, we treat the poor with mercy. Notice that thy brother may live. The goal was the brother's survival and restoration, not the lender's gain. Luke 6 records Jesus saying, lend, hoping for nothing again; and your reward shall be great. Psalm 15 describes the righteous man as one who putteth not out his money to usury. Today, help those in need without seeking profit. Let your generosity be free and kind.

Notice money and victuals. Both money and food were covered. Whatever the poor man needed, it was to be given without profit. Notice victuals. Food. Charging extra for food to a hungry person was exploitation. Basic needs must not become sources of profit. Nehemiah 5 tells of nobles charging interest during famine. Nehemiah rebuked them. They returned what they had taken. Notice the repetition with verse 36. God emphasized this point. Today, never profit from someone's basic needs. Share food and money freely with those who are struggling.

== 25:44
T: Of the heathen round about
R: 'Both thy bondmen, and thy bondmaids, which thou shalt have, shall be of the heathen that are round about you; of them shall ye buy bondmen and bondmaids.' Israel's bondservants were to come from the surrounding nations. Notice that Scripture regulated a practice common in the ancient world. Bondservice existed in every nation. God did not abolish it immediately in Israel's law. But He limited it and protected those within it. Exodus 21 says a servant injured by his master was to go free. Deuteronomy 23 forbade returning an escaped servant to his master. Kidnapping and selling people was punishable by death in Exodus 21. These protections were remarkable for their time. Notice the direction of Scripture. The gospel moves steadily toward freedom and equality. Galatians 3 says, there is neither bond nor free… for ye are all one in Christ Jesus. First Timothy 1 condemns menstealers. Today, read this hard passage in the light of the whole Bible. Rejoice that in Christ, all are one and all are free.
