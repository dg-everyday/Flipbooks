# Leviticus 26:24-46: Exile, confession and the remembered covenant

== 26:24
T: I also will walk contrary
R: 'Then will I also walk contrary unto you, and will punish you yet seven times for your sins.' God would be hostile toward them and afflict them seven times over for their sins. Notice I also will walk contrary. God would respond in kind. If they opposed Him, He would oppose them. The relationship had turned. This is sobering. Psalm 18 says, with the froward thou wilt shew thyself froward. God meets stubbornness with resistance. Notice seven times again. The discipline kept increasing. Each refusal brought heavier consequences. Notice that even this was not God's final word. The chapter will end with promises of restoration. His opposition was meant to break their resistance and bring them back. James 4 says, Draw nigh to God, and he will draw nigh to you. The reverse is also true. Today, do not set yourself against God. Draw near to Him, and He will draw near to you.
S: God warned He would walk contrary to them and punish them seven times more for their sins. He met stubbornness with resistance, yet His aim was still to bring them back. Do not set yourself against God, but draw near to Him and He will draw near to you.
P: Draw nigh. Leviticus 26:24: 'Then will I also walk contrary unto you.' Don't set yourself against God. Draw nigh to Him, and He will draw nigh to you.
H: #DailyGrace #Leviticus2624 #Repentance #Nearness #Warning #BibleVerse

== 26:25
T: The quarrel of my covenant
R: 'And I will bring a sword upon you, that shall avenge the quarrel of my covenant: and when ye are gathered together within your cities, I will send the pestilence among you; and ye shall be delivered into the hand of the enemy.' God would bring the sword to avenge the broken covenant. When they fled to their cities, He would send plague, and they would be handed to their enemies. Notice the quarrel of my covenant. The covenant had been broken. God was defending it. Notice gathered together within your cities. They would retreat to walled cities for safety. Plague would follow them inside. Notice that no human refuge is safe without God. Psalm 127 says, except the LORD keep the city, the watchman waketh but in vain. Psalm 91 says, He that dwelleth in the secret place of the most High shall abide under the shadow of the Almighty. True refuge is in God alone. Today, make God your refuge. Walls and plans cannot protect you without Him.
S: God warned He would bring a sword to avenge the quarrel of His covenant and send pestilence into their cities. Walled cities would fail without God, for true refuge is in Him alone. Make God your refuge, for walls and plans cannot protect you without Him.
P: Except the LORD keep the city. Leviticus 26:25: 'when ye are gathered together within your cities, I will send the pestilence.' Make God your refuge today.
H: #DailyGrace #Leviticus2625 #Refuge #Covenant #Protection #BibleVerse

== 26:26
T: Eat, and not be satisfied
R: 'And when I have broken the staff of your bread, ten women shall bake your bread in one oven… and ye shall eat, and not be satisfied.' God would cut off their food supply. Ten women would bake bread in one oven and ration it out by weight. They would eat but not be satisfied. Notice the staff of your bread. Bread was the staff of life. God would break it. Notice ten women… one oven. So little flour that ten households shared one oven. Notice ye shall eat, and not be satisfied. Even eating would not satisfy. Haggai 1 says, ye eat, but ye have not enough. Without God, nothing truly satisfies. Isaiah 55 asks, wherefore do ye spend money for that which is not bread? and your labour for that which satisfieth not? John 6 records Jesus saying, I am the bread of life: he that cometh to me shall never hunger. Today, seek satisfaction in Christ, not in things. Only He can fill the hunger of your soul.
S: God warned He would break the staff of their bread so that ten women baked in one oven, and they would eat and not be satisfied. Without God, nothing truly satisfies. Seek satisfaction in Christ, the bread of life, for only He fills the soul's hunger.
P: Eat and not be satisfied. Leviticus 26:26: 'ye shall eat, and not be satisfied.' Only Christ, the bread of life, satisfies the soul. Come to Him today.
H: #DailyGrace #Leviticus2626 #Satisfaction #BreadOfLife #Hunger #BibleVerse

== 26:27
T: For all this
R: 'And if ye will not for all this hearken unto me, but walk contrary unto me.' If despite all this they still would not listen but continued to oppose Him, even greater consequences would come. Notice for all this. After plagues, defeat, famine, and loss, still no repentance. Human stubbornness can be astonishing. Pharaoh hardened his heart through ten plagues. Israel could do the same. Notice the repetition. Not hearken, walk contrary. The same phrases appear again and again. The pattern of rebellion was persistent. Jeremiah 5 says, thou hast stricken them, but they have not grieved… they have refused to return. Hardship alone does not change hearts. Notice that only God can soften a hard heart. Ezekiel 36 says, A new heart also will I give you… and I will take away the stony heart out of your flesh. Today, ask God to keep your heart soft. Pray that hardship would lead you toward Him, not away.
S: If after all this Israel still would not hearken but walked contrary, greater consequences would come. Hardship alone does not change hearts, but God can give a new heart. Ask God to keep your heart soft so that hardship leads you toward Him.
P: A soft heart. Leviticus 26:27: 'if ye will not for all this hearken unto me.' Hardship alone doesn't change hearts. Ask God for a heart of flesh today.
H: #DailyGrace #Leviticus2627 #Heart #Repentance #Stubbornness #BibleVerse

== 26:28
T: In fury
R: 'Then I will walk contrary unto you also in fury; and I, even I, will chastise you seven times for your sins.' God would be hostile toward them in His anger, and He Himself would punish them seven times over. Notice in fury. God's anger is real. It is not like human rage, uncontrolled and selfish. It is holy anger against sin and rebellion. Romans 1 says, the wrath of God is revealed from heaven against all ungodliness. Notice I, even I. God emphasized that He Himself would act. This was personal. The covenant God was responding to covenant betrayal. Notice chastise. The word still implies correction. Even in fury, God's discipline aimed at His people. Notice the gospel. First Thessalonians 1 says Jesus delivered us from the wrath to come. Romans 5 says, being now justified by his blood, we shall be saved from wrath through him. Christ bore God's wrath for us. Today, take God's holiness seriously. And rejoice that Christ has delivered you from the wrath you deserved.
S: God warned He would walk contrary to them in fury and chastise them seven times for their sins. His holy anger against sin is real, yet Christ has delivered believers from the wrath to come. Take God's holiness seriously and rejoice that Christ bore the wrath you deserved.
P: Saved from wrath. Leviticus 26:28: 'I will walk contrary unto you also in fury.' God's anger at sin is real. Christ delivered you from the wrath to come.
H: #DailyGrace #Leviticus2628 #Wrath #Salvation #Holiness #BibleVerse

== 26:29
T: The depths of judgment
R: 'And ye shall eat the flesh of your sons, and the flesh of your daughters shall ye eat.' In the worst of the siege and famine, people would be driven to eat their own children. Notice the horror. This is among the most terrible verses in Scripture. It describes the depths of human desperation under siege. Notice that it was fulfilled. Second Kings 6 records it during the siege of Samaria. Lamentations 4 records it in the fall of Jerusalem: The hands of the pitiful women have sodden their own children. God's warnings were not empty. Notice why God spoke so plainly. He wanted to shock His people away from the path that led there. The severity of the warning showed the depth of His desire to spare them. Ezekiel 33 says, I have no pleasure in the death of the wicked; but that the wicked turn from his way and live. Today, hear the seriousness of sin's end. And turn to God, who longs to spare you from it.
S: God warned that in the worst siege and famine His people would eat the flesh of their own children. The horror was fulfilled in Samaria and Jerusalem, and God spoke plainly to turn them from that path. Hear the seriousness of sin's end and turn to God, who longs to spare you.
P: Turn and live. Leviticus 26:29: 'ye shall eat the flesh of your sons.' God's warnings are severe because He longs to spare. He has no pleasure in death.

== 26:30
T: Your high places destroyed
R: 'And I will destroy your high places, and cut down your images, and cast your carcases upon the carcases of your idols, and my soul shall abhor you.' God would destroy their places of idol worship, cut down their incense altars, and pile their dead bodies on their lifeless idols. Notice your high places. Places of false worship. God would destroy them. Notice carcases upon the carcases of your idols. A grim irony. Dead people on dead gods. The idols they trusted could not save them. Psalm 115 says idols have mouths, but they speak not… They that make them are like unto them. Worshipers become like their lifeless idols. Notice my soul shall abhor you. The reversal of verse 11. God had promised not to abhor them. Now idolatry would bring His rejection. Notice how idolatry ends. It leads to death. Jonah 2 says, They that observe lying vanities forsake their own mercy. Today, turn away from anything you trust instead of God. Lifeless idols cannot save.
S: God warned He would destroy their high places and cast their carcases upon the carcases of their idols. Dead idols could not save their worshipers, and idolatry led to death and rejection. Turn away from anything you trust instead of God, for lifeless idols cannot save.
P: Idols cannot save. Leviticus 26:30: 'cast your carcases upon the carcases of your idols.' Those who trust lying vanities forsake their own mercy. Trust God alone.

== 26:31
T: I will not smell
R: 'And I will make your cities waste, and bring your sanctuaries unto desolation, and I will not smell the savour of your sweet odours.' God would turn their cities into ruins and their sanctuaries into desolation. He would take no delight in their offerings. Notice I will not smell the savour. Throughout Leviticus, offerings were a sweet savour to the LORD. Now God would refuse them. Worship without obedience would be rejected. Isaiah 1 says, Bring no more vain oblations; incense is an abomination unto me. Amos 5 says, I hate, I despise your feast days. God rejects empty religion. Notice your sanctuaries. Even holy places would be destroyed. Sacred buildings cannot protect a disobedient people. Jeremiah 7 warns against trusting in lying words, saying, The temple of the LORD. Religious places do not replace a faithful heart. First Samuel 15 says, to obey is better than sacrifice. Today, offer God worship that comes from an obedient heart. Rituals alone do not please Him.
S: God warned He would make their cities waste, desolate their sanctuaries and not smell the savour of their offerings. Worship without obedience would be rejected, for sacred places cannot replace a faithful heart. Offer God worship that flows from an obedient heart, not ritual alone.
P: To obey is better. Leviticus 26:31: 'I will not smell the savour of your sweet odours.' God rejects empty religion. Worship Him with an obedient heart.
H: #DailyGrace #Leviticus2631 #Worship #Obedience #Sincerity #BibleVerse

== 26:32
T: Your enemies shall be astonished
R: 'And I will bring the land into desolation: and your enemies which dwell therein shall be astonished at it.' God would lay waste the land so completely that even the enemies who settled there would be appalled. Notice astonished. The destruction would be so complete that even enemies would be shocked. Israel's fall would become a warning to the nations. Deuteronomy 29 says nations would ask, Wherefore hath the LORD done thus unto this land? The answer would be: Because they have forsaken the covenant. Notice the land. The good land flowing with milk and honey would become desolate. God's gift would be lost through unfaithfulness. Notice that God's people are a witness, for good or ill. Their faithfulness shows God's goodness. Their unfaithfulness also teaches the nations. Matthew 5 says, Let your light so shine before men. Romans 2 says, the name of God is blasphemed among the Gentiles through you. Today, remember that your life is a witness. Let it show God's goodness to those watching.
S: God warned He would bring the land into such desolation that even the enemies dwelling there would be astonished. Israel's fall would teach the nations, for God's people are a witness for good or ill. Remember that your life is a witness and let it show God's goodness to those watching.
P: Your life is a witness. Leviticus 26:32: 'your enemies which dwell therein shall be astonished.' Others are watching. Let your light shine for God today.

== 26:33
T: Scattered among the heathen
R: 'And I will scatter you among the heathen, and will draw out a sword after you: and your land shall be desolate, and your cities waste.' God would scatter them among the nations and draw His sword to pursue them. Their land would be laid waste, and their cities left in ruins. Notice scatter you among the heathen. Exile. This was the ultimate covenant curse. Second Kings 17 records Israel's exile to Assyria. Second Kings 25 records Judah's exile to Babylon. Notice that even scattering had a purpose. Among the nations, Israel would learn. Daniel, Esther, and Ezekiel were faithful in exile. Notice that God did not abandon them in exile. Jeremiah 29 says, seek the peace of the city whither I have caused you to be carried away… For I know the thoughts that I think toward you… thoughts of peace, and not of evil, to give you an expected end. Today, if you feel scattered or far from home, know that God's thoughts toward you are still thoughts of peace.
S: God warned He would scatter them among the heathen and leave their land desolate. Exile came, yet God did not abandon His people, whose faithful ones witnessed among the nations. If you feel scattered or far from home, know that God's thoughts toward you are still of peace.
P: Thoughts of peace. Leviticus 26:33: 'I will scatter you among the heathen.' Even in exile, God's thoughts were of peace and hope. He has not abandoned you.

== 26:34
T: The land shall enjoy her sabbaths
R: 'Then shall the land enjoy her sabbaths, as long as it lieth desolate, and ye be in your enemies' land; even then shall the land rest, and enjoy her sabbaths.' While the land lay empty and they were in exile, the land would finally enjoy the sabbath rests they had denied it. Notice the land shall enjoy her sabbaths. Israel had ignored the sabbath years of chapter 25. So God would give the land its rest through exile. What they refused to give, God would take. Second Chronicles 36 says this happened: to fulfil the word of the LORD… until the land had enjoyed her sabbaths: for as long as she lay desolate she kept sabbath, to fulfil threescore and ten years. Notice that God's word is always fulfilled. If not through obedience, then through discipline. Notice that rest was God's design. He would ensure it happened. Isaiah 46 says, My counsel shall stand. Today, honor God's rhythms of rest willingly. What we refuse to give, He may take another way.
S: While Israel was in exile, the land would enjoy the sabbaths they had denied it. Second Chronicles records seventy years of exile fulfilling this word, for God's purposes always stand. Honor God's rhythms of rest willingly rather than having Him take them another way.
P: The land rested. Leviticus 26:34: 'Then shall the land enjoy her sabbaths.' Israel refused rest; exile enforced it. Honor God's rhythms willingly today.

== 26:35
T: Because it did not rest
R: 'As long as it lieth desolate it shall rest; because it did not rest in your sabbaths, when ye dwelt upon it.' All the time the land lay desolate, it would have the rest it never had while they lived there. Notice because it did not rest. The reason was clear. Israel had worked the land without stopping. Notice the deeper sin. Failing to keep the sabbath years showed a lack of trust. They did not believe God would provide. Unbelief drove them to overwork. Psalm 127 says, It is vain for you to rise up early, to sit up late, to eat the bread of sorrows: for so he giveth his beloved sleep. God gives rest to those who trust Him. Notice that neglecting rest has consequences. People, families, and lands suffer when rest is ignored. Mark 6 records Jesus telling His disciples, Come ye yourselves apart… and rest a while. Today, examine whether you trust God enough to rest. Overwork may reveal unbelief. Rest in Him.
S: The land would rest while desolate because it did not rest in Israel's sabbaths. Their failure to rest revealed distrust that God would provide. Examine whether you trust God enough to rest, for He gives His beloved sleep.
P: Trust enough to rest. Leviticus 26:35: 'because it did not rest in your sabbaths.' Overwork can reveal unbelief. Come apart and rest awhile with Jesus.

Without God, even small things become threats. Notice faintness into their hearts. Courage would drain away. Notice the contrast with faith. Faith produces courage. Second Timothy 1 says, God hath not given us the spirit of fear; but of power, and of love, and of a sound mind. Today, let your heart be fixed on God. S: God warned that survivors in enemy lands would be so faint of heart that the sound of a shaken leaf would chase them. Without God, even small things become terrors, while faith produces courage. Fix your heart on God, for He has not given you a spirit of fear.

== 26:39
T: Pine away
R: 'And they that are left of you shall pine away in their iniquity in your enemies' lands; and also in the iniquities of their fathers shall they pine away with them.' Those who survived would waste away in enemy lands because of their own sins and their ancestors' sins. Notice pine away. A slow decline. Wasting away from within. Sin's consequences are often gradual. It eats away at people over time. Notice the iniquities of their fathers. Sinful patterns passed from generation to generation. Children inherited their parents' unfaithfulness and continued it. Yet Ezekiel 18 says each person is responsible for their own sin. The cycle can be broken. Notice Psalm 32. When I kept silence, my bones waxed old. Unconfessed sin causes us to pine away. But David continued: I acknowledged my sin unto thee… and thou forgavest. Confession ends the pining. Today, do not let unconfessed sin waste you away. Confess it to God, and break any sinful patterns passed down to you.
S: Survivors would pine away in their iniquity and in the iniquities of their fathers in enemy lands. Sin consumes slowly and passes through generations, yet confession ends the wasting and breaks the cycle. Do not let unconfessed sin waste you away, but confess it and break inherited patterns.
P: Stop pining. Leviticus 26:39: 'they… shall pine away in their iniquity.' Silence wastes us; confession heals. Break the cycle of inherited sin with honest confession today.

== 26:45
T: For their sakes
R: 'But I will for their sakes remember the covenant of their ancestors, whom I brought forth out of the land of Egypt in the sight of the heathen, that I might be their God: I am the LORD.' For their sake, God would remember His covenant with their ancestors, whom He brought out of Egypt before the nations to be their God. He is the LORD. Notice for their sakes. God acted for His people's good. Notice in the sight of the heathen. The exodus was public. His faithfulness to Israel displays His character to the world. == 26:46
T: Statutes, judgments and laws
R: 'These are the statutes and judgments and laws, which the LORD made between him and the children of Israel in mount Sinai by the hand of Moses.' This verse sums up the laws God established between Himself and Israel at Mount Sinai through Moses. Notice between him and the children of Israel. The laws were relational. A covenant between God and His people. Not cold rules, but terms of a relationship. Notice in mount Sinai. The place where God revealed Himself. Where He came down in fire. Where He made Israel His people. Notice by the hand of Moses. God used a human mediator. Galatians 3 says the law was ordained by angels in the hand of a mediator. Hebrews 8 says Christ is the mediator of a better covenant, which was established upon better promises. Moses brought the old covenant; Christ brings the new. Today, thank God for His word, given through Moses and fulfilled in Christ. Live within His covenant of grace.
