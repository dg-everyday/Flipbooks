# Numbers 1:28-54: The census completed and the Levites set apart

== 1:28
T: The children of Issachar
R: 'Of the children of Issachar, by their generations, after their families, by the house of their fathers, according to the number of the names, from twenty years old and upward, all that were able to go forth to war.' The descendants of Issachar were counted by name. Notice Issachar's blessing in Genesis 49: Issachar is a strong ass couching down between two burdens: And he saw that rest was good… and bowed his shoulder to bear. A hardworking tribe. They carried burdens and valued rest. Notice the balance. Work and rest together. Issachar knew how to bear loads and also saw that rest was good. Healthy lives need both. Matthew 11 records Jesus saying, Take my yoke upon you… and ye shall find rest unto your souls. With Christ, even burdens become restful. Galatians 6 says, Bear ye one another's burdens. Today, be willing to carry burdens for others, as Issachar did. And find your rest in Christ, whose yoke is easy.
S: The children of Issachar were counted by name. Issachar was blessed as a strong tribe that bore burdens and saw that rest was good. Be willing to carry burdens for others and find your rest in Christ, whose yoke is easy.
P: Burden and rest. Numbers 1:28: 'Of the children of Issachar.' Issachar bore burdens and saw that rest was good. Take Christ's yoke and find rest today.

== 1:29
T: Issachar's number
R: 'Those that were numbered of them, even of the tribe of Issachar, were fifty and four thousand and four hundred.' Issachar numbered 54,400 men. Notice that Issachar grew by the second census to 64,300. A hardworking, discerning tribe increased. Notice what First Chronicles 12 says later: the children of Issachar, which were men that had understanding of the times, to know what Israel ought to do. They were not the largest tribe, but they were wise. Wisdom matters more than numbers. Ecclesiastes 9 says, wisdom is better than strength. Proverbs 24 says, A wise man is strong; yea, a man of knowledge increaseth strength. Notice that understanding the times is still needed. Christians must discern what is happening and how to respond faithfully. Romans 13 says, knowing the time, that now it is high time to awake out of sleep. Today, ask God for wisdom to understand your times. Seek to know what you ought to do.
S: Issachar numbered fifty-four thousand four hundred men, and the tribe later grew. Issachar became known for understanding the times and knowing what Israel ought to do. Ask God for wisdom to understand your times and know what you ought to do.
P: Understanding the times. Numbers 1:29: 'the tribe of Issachar, were fifty and four thousand.' Wisdom is better than strength. Ask God to help you discern your times.

== 1:31

Notice that Zebulun also grew by the second census, to 60,500. A faithful tribe increased. Notice where Zebulun settled. Their land was in lower Galilee. Nazareth lay in this region. Jesus grew up near Zebulun's territory. Matthew 4 quotes Isaiah 9: The land of Zabulon… The people which sat in darkness saw great light. Isaiah 9 continues: For unto us a child is born, unto us a son is given. The census list points forward to Christmas. Today, rejoice that the light came into the darkness. And trust that God's plans for you span far beyond what you can see.

== 1:32
T: The children of Ephraim
R: 'Of the children of Joseph, namely, of the children of Ephraim, by their generations, after their families… from twenty years old and upward, all that were able to go forth to war.' From Joseph's line, the descendants of Ephraim were counted. Notice Ephraim. Joseph's younger son. Yet in Genesis 48, Jacob crossed his hands and gave Ephraim the greater blessing. Joseph protested, but Jacob said, his younger brother shall be greater than he. God often chooses the younger. Abel over Cain. Isaac over Ishmael. Jacob over Esau. David over his brothers. Notice that God's choices are not based on human custom. First Samuel 16 says, the LORD seeth not as man seeth. He chooses by grace. Notice Ephraim's future. Joshua came from Ephraim. The tribe became so prominent that the northern kingdom was often called Ephraim. Today, take heart if you feel overlooked. God does not choose as people do. His grace often lifts the younger and the least expected.
S: From Joseph's line, the children of Ephraim were counted. Jacob gave Ephraim, the younger son, the greater blessing, for God often chooses by grace rather than custom. Take heart if you feel overlooked, for God's grace often lifts the least expected.

== 1:33
T: Ephraim's number
R: 'Those that were numbered of them, even of the tribe of Ephraim, were forty thousand and five hundred.' Ephraim numbered 40,500 men. Notice the surprise. Ephraim had the greater blessing, yet here was smaller than Manasseh would later become. By the second census, Ephraim dropped to 32,500 while Manasseh grew to 52,700. Blessing did not mean constant growth. Notice that God's purposes are not measured by numbers at every moment. Ephraim would still become prominent. Joshua, the leader who brought Israel into the land, was from Ephraim. Notice Zechariah 4: who hath despised the day of small things? Seasons of decline are not the end of the story. First Corinthians 3 says, God gave the increase. Growth comes from Him in His time. Today, do not judge God's blessing by your present size or success. He is working through seasons of growth and of decline.
S: Ephraim numbered forty thousand five hundred men and later declined while Manasseh grew. Ephraim's blessing was not measured by constant growth, yet Joshua came from this tribe. Do not judge God's blessing by present size, for He works through seasons of growth and decline.
P: Day of small things. Numbers 1:33: 'the tribe of Ephraim, were forty thousand and five hundred.' Don't measure blessing by numbers. God gives the increase in His time.

Notice what came from that remnant. King Saul. Queen Esther's cousin Mordecai, and Esther herself, were Benjamites. And the apostle Paul. A tribe that nearly vanished produced remarkable people. Isaiah 1 says, Except the LORD of hosts had left unto us a very small remnant, we should have been as Sodom. Today, take hope in God's preserving grace. Notice that small beginnings are not limits for God. Job 8 says, Though thy beginning was small, yet thy latter end should greatly increase. Matthew 13 says the kingdom of heaven is like a grain of mustard seed… the least of all seeds: but when it is grown, it is the greatest among herbs. Today, do not despise small beginnings. God can grow a great harvest from a single seed.

== 1:50
T: Over the tabernacle
R: 'But thou shalt appoint the Levites over the tabernacle of testimony, and over all the vessels thereof… they shall bear the tabernacle… and they shall minister unto it, and shall encamp round about the tabernacle.' Instead, the Levites were put in charge of the tabernacle and everything in it. They would carry it, care for it, and camp around it. Notice appoint the Levites over the tabernacle. Their calling was God's house. They served His presence. Notice they shall bear the tabernacle. They literally carried God's dwelling through the wilderness. What an honor and responsibility. Notice encamp round about. They lived closest to God's presence. Their tents surrounded the tabernacle. == 1:51
T: Take it down, set it up
R: 'And when the tabernacle setteth forward, the Levites shall take it down: and when the tabernacle is to be pitched, the Levites shall set it up: and the stranger that cometh nigh shall be put to death.' Whenever the tabernacle moved, the Levites took it down. When it stopped, they set it up. Anyone else who came near would be put to death. Notice take it down… set it up. The Levites' work followed God's movement. When God moved, they moved. When God stopped, they stopped. Their lives were shaped by His leading. Notice the repetitive work. Taking down and setting up, again and again for forty years. Faithfulness in repeated tasks. Galatians 6 says, let us not be weary in well doing. Notice the stranger that cometh nigh. Holiness was dangerous to approach carelessly. Only those appointed could handle holy things. Today, follow God's movement in your life. And be faithful in the repeated, ordinary tasks He gives you.

== 1:52

== 1:53
T: No wrath upon the congregation
R: 'But the Levites shall pitch round about the tabernacle of testimony, that there be no wrath upon the congregation of the children of Israel: and the Levites shall keep the charge of the tabernacle of testimony.' The Levites camped around the tabernacle so that God's anger would not fall on Israel. They were responsible for its care. Notice that there be no wrath. The Levites formed a protective barrier. Their presence guarded the people from approaching God's holiness carelessly. They stood between the holy God and sinful people. Notice that this pictures mediation. The Levites stood in the gap. Christ is the ultimate mediator. First Timothy 2 says, there is one God, and one mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus. First Thessalonians 1 says Jesus delivered us from the wrath to come. Notice keep the charge. A sacred duty, carefully guarded. Today, thank Christ for standing between you and God's wrath. He keeps you safe in God's presence.
S: The Levites camped round the tabernacle so that there would be no wrath upon the congregation. They stood between the holy God and the people, picturing Christ, the one mediator. Thank Christ for standing between you and wrath and keeping you safe in God's presence.

== 1:54
T: According to all
R: 'And the children of Israel did according to all that the LORD commanded Moses, so did they.' The Israelites did everything just as the LORD commanded Moses. Notice according to all. Full obedience. Not partial, not selective. They did everything God commanded. Notice so did they. The chapter ends with a clear statement of faithfulness. At this moment, Israel was obedient. The camp was ordered. The census was done. Everything was ready. Notice the sad contrast with what came later. Chapters 11 to 14 tell of complaint and rebellion. The same people who obeyed here would later refuse to enter the land. Beginnings can be bright. Endurance is the test. Galatians 5 asks, Ye did run well; who did hinder you that ye should not obey the truth? Today, aim for full obedience, as Israel did here. And pray for grace to keep obeying, not only at the beginning but to the end.
S: The children of Israel did according to all the LORD commanded Moses. The chapter ends with full obedience, though this same people would later rebel. Aim for full obedience and pray for grace to keep obeying to the end.
P: Ye did run well. Numbers 1:54: 'the children of Israel did according to all that the LORD commanded.' A bright start. Pray for grace to obey to the end.
