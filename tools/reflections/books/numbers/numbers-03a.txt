# Numbers 3:1-25: Aaron's sons and the Levites given to serve

== 3:1
T: The generations of Aaron and Moses
R: 'These also are the generations of Aaron and Moses in the day that the LORD spake with Moses in mount Sinai.' This is the family record of Aaron and Moses at the time the LORD spoke with Moses on Mount Sinai. Notice Aaron before Moses. Usually Moses is named first. Here Aaron comes first because the chapter focuses on the priestly family. God gives each servant a place of honor in his own sphere. Notice in the day that the LORD spake. Their family history was tied to God's word. What made them significant was not their bloodline alone but God speaking to them. Notice that the chapter will name only Aaron's sons, not Moses'. Moses' sons remained ordinary Levites. Moses did not build a dynasty. First Chronicles 23 says, concerning Moses the man of God, his sons were named of the tribe of Levi. Today, seek to be shaped by God's word rather than by status. Let your legacy be faithfulness, not position.
S: This is the family record of Aaron and Moses from the day the LORD spoke with Moses on Sinai. Aaron is named first in this priestly chapter, and Moses did not build a dynasty for his own sons. Let God's word shape your life and seek a legacy of faithfulness rather than position.
P: Legacy, not dynasty. Numbers 3:1: 'These also are the generations of Aaron and Moses.' Moses didn't build a dynasty. Seek a legacy of faithfulness today.

== 3:2
T: The sons of Aaron
R: 'And these are the names of the sons of Aaron; Nadab the firstborn, and Abihu, Eleazar, and Ithamar.' Aaron's sons were Nadab the firstborn, Abihu, Eleazar, and Ithamar. Notice Nadab the firstborn. The eldest son, heir to the high priesthood. He had seen God on Mount Sinai in Exodus 24. He had every privilege. Yet he would die for offering strange fire. Notice the four names. Two would die young. Two would carry on. The same family, the same privileges, different outcomes. Notice that privilege does not guarantee faithfulness. Each son made his own choices. Ezekiel 18 says, The soul that sinneth, it shall die… the son shall not bear the iniquity of the father. Each person stands before God individually. Second Corinthians 5 says we must all appear before the judgment seat of Christ. Today, do not rely on your family's faith or privileges. Make your own choice to follow God faithfully.
S: Aaron's sons were Nadab the firstborn, Abihu, Eleazar and Ithamar. Two would die for strange fire and two would carry on, showing that privilege does not guarantee faithfulness. Do not rely on your family's faith but make your own choice to follow God faithfully.
P: Your own choice. Numbers 3:2: 'Nadab the firstborn, and Abihu, Eleazar, and Ithamar.' Same family, different outcomes. Choose to follow God faithfully yourself.

== 3:3
T: The priests which were anointed
R: 'These are the names of the sons of Aaron, the priests which were anointed, whom he consecrated to minister in the priest's office.' These were Aaron's sons, the anointed priests, ordained to serve as priests. Notice anointed. All four had been anointed with oil and consecrated in Leviticus 8. All had the same beginning. All had God's calling on their lives. Notice to minister. Their purpose was service. Anointing was not for status but for ministry. Notice that anointing must be followed by faithfulness. Two of these anointed men would fail. A good start is not enough. First Samuel 16 shows David anointed. Yet later he sinned grievously. Anointing did not make him immune. He needed repentance and grace. Second Timothy 1 says, stir up the gift of God, which is in thee. Gifts must be nurtured. Today, remember that God's calling on your life must be lived out daily. Stir up the gift He has given you and serve faithfully.
S: Aaron's sons were the anointed priests, consecrated to minister in the priest's office. All four shared the same anointing, yet two would fail, for anointing must be followed by faithfulness. Stir up the gift God has given you and live out His calling faithfully every day.
P: Anointed to serve. Numbers 3:3: 'the priests which were anointed… to minister.' Anointing must be followed by faithfulness. Stir up God's gift in you today.

== 3:4
T: They had no children
R: 'And Nadab and Abihu died before the LORD, when they offered strange fire before the LORD… and they had no children: and Eleazar and Ithamar ministered in the priest's office in the sight of Aaron their father.' Nadab and Abihu died before the LORD when they offered unauthorized fire. They had no sons. So Eleazar and Ithamar served as priests during Aaron's lifetime. Notice they had no children. Their line ended with them. Their sin cost them their future. No descendants would carry their names. Notice in the sight of Aaron their father. Aaron lived to see his younger sons serve. He grieved two, yet saw two faithful. Loss and hope together. Notice that the story is remembered. Scripture records their failure so we can learn. First Corinthians 10 says, these things… are written for our admonition. Notice that God's work continued. When two failed, two stepped up. God's purposes are not stopped by human failure. Today, learn from the failures recorded in Scripture. And trust that God's work continues even when some fall.
S: Nadab and Abihu died before the LORD for offering strange fire and had no children, so Eleazar and Ithamar served in Aaron's sight. Their sin ended their line, yet God's work continued through the faithful brothers. Learn from Scripture's recorded failures and trust that God's work continues.
P: God's work continues. Numbers 3:4: 'Eleazar and Ithamar ministered in the priest's office.' Two fell; two served. Human failure can't stop God's purposes.

== 3:5

R: 'And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying.' God spoke to Moses about the Levites and their role. Notice that God gave specific instructions for the Levites. Their service was not left to human invention. God designed their roles. After the tragedy of Nadab and Abihu, careful obedience mattered more than ever. Notice the rhythm of Numbers. Again and again, the LORD spake unto Moses. God's word guided every step. Israel was not making it up as they went. Notice that service in God's house is defined by God. We do not decide on our own how to serve Him. Ephesians 2 says, we are his workmanship, created in Christ Jesus unto good works, which God hath before ordained that we should walk in them. God prepares our work. Romans 12 says, prove what is that good, and acceptable, and perfect, will of God. Today, seek God's guidance for how you should serve Him. Let His word define your ministry.

== 3:6

R: 'Bring the tribe of Levi near, and present them before Aaron the priest, that they may minister unto him.' God told Moses to bring the tribe of Levi and present them to Aaron to assist him. Notice bring… near. The Levites were brought close. They had the privilege of nearness to God's house. Notice that this was an honor earned at the golden calf. Exodus 32 says the Levites stood with Moses when others worshiped the idol. Their loyalty brought them near. Notice minister unto him. The Levites served Aaron, who served God. Hebrews 10 says, Let us draw near. Today, rejoice that God has brought you near. Serve Him faithfully, as the Levites did, by supporting His work and His servants.

== 3:10

R: 'And thou shalt appoint Aaron and his sons, and they shall wait on their priest's office: and the stranger that cometh nigh shall be put to death.' Aaron and his sons were appointed to serve as priests. Anyone else who approached the sanctuary would be put to death. Notice wait on their priest's office. Priests attended to their duty. They were faithful to their specific calling. Not everyone could do everything. Notice the stranger that cometh nigh. Even Levites could not perform priestly duties. Boundaries protected holiness. Second Chronicles 26 tells of King Uzziah, who entered the temple to burn incense. He was struck with leprosy. Even a king could not take the priest's role. Notice that respecting God's boundaries is a form of reverence. Romans 12 says, Having then gifts differing… let us wait on our ministering. Focus on your own calling. Today, faithfully attend to the calling God has given you. Do not reach for roles He has not assigned.

== 3:24
T: Eliasaph son of Lael
R: 'And the chief of the house of the father of the Gershonites shall be Eliasaph the son of Lael.' The leader of the Gershonites was Eliasaph son of Lael. Notice Lael. His name means belonging to God. A beautiful name for a Levite. Notice that each branch had a leader. Organization ensured that work was done. Responsibility was clear. Everyone knew who to follow. Notice the name Eliasaph. The same name as Gad's leader in chapter 1, though a different man. Names were shared across tribes. Notice belonging to God. Every Levite belonged to God in a special way. Lael's name expressed this. Song of Solomon 2 says, My beloved is mine, and I am his. Isaiah 44 says, One shall say, I am the LORD'S. Today, say with confidence that you belong to God. Let your life, like Lael's name, declare whose you are.
S: Eliasaph son of Lael led the Gershonites. Lael's name means belonging to God, fitting for a Levite set apart for Him. Say with confidence that you belong to God and let your life declare whose you are.
P: Belonging to God. Numbers 3:24: 'Eliasaph the son of Lael.' Lael means belonging to God. Say today, I am the LORD's, and live like it.

== 3:25
T: The tent and its coverings
R: 'And the charge of the sons of Gershon in the tabernacle of the congregation shall be the tabernacle, and the tent, the covering thereof, and the hanging for the door of the tabernacle of the congregation.' The Gershonites were responsible for the tabernacle fabrics: the tent, its coverings, and the curtain at the entrance. Notice the coverings. The layers of curtains and skins that protected the holy place. Gershon cared for what sheltered God's presence. Notice the hanging for the door. The entrance curtain. Gershon guarded the way in. Notice that coverings protect what is inside. Psalm 91 says, He that dwelleth in the secret place of the most High shall abide under the shadow of the Almighty. God covers His people. Notice John 10. Jesus said, I am the door: by me if any man enter in, he shall be saved. Christ is the true entrance to God's presence. Today, rest under God's covering. And enter His presence through Christ, the true door.
S: The Gershonites had charge of the tabernacle's tent, coverings and the hanging for the door. They cared for what sheltered God's presence and the way in. Rest under God's covering and enter His presence through Christ, the true door.
P: The covering and the door. Numbers 3:25: 'the covering thereof, and the hanging for the door.' Rest under God's shadow. Christ is the door into His presence.
