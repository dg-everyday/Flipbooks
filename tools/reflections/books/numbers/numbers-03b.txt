# Numbers 3:26-51: Kohath, Merari and the redemption of the firstborn

== 3:26
T: The cords of it
R: 'And the hangings of the court, and the curtain for the door of the court, which is by the tabernacle, and by the altar round about, and the cords of it for all the service thereof.' The Gershonites also cared for the courtyard curtains, the entrance curtain to the courtyard, and the ropes, everything related to their use. Notice the cords. Even the ropes were assigned. Small items were not overlooked. Every piece mattered. Without cords, curtains would fall. Notice that small things hold large things together. A rope is humble, but essential. Zechariah 4 says, who hath despised the day of small things? Notice the hangings of the court. The outer boundary of the sanctuary. The court marked off holy space. Gershon maintained the boundary. Notice that faithfulness in small tasks keeps everything standing. Luke 16 says, He that is faithful in that which is least is faithful also in much. Today, do not despise small tasks. The cords you tend may be holding something important together.

== 3:27
T: The families of the Kohathites
R: 'And of Kohath was the family of the Amramites, and the family of the Izeharites, and the family of the Hebronites, and the family of the Uzzielites: these are the families of the Kohathites.' The Kohathites included the Amramite, Izharite, Hebronite, and Uzzielite clans. Notice the Amramites. This was the family of Moses and Aaron. The Kohathites were the most privileged branch, carrying the holiest objects. Notice that Moses' own family served as ordinary Kohathites, while Aaron's line served as priests. Within one family, different callings. Moses did not claim special status for his descendants. Notice four families. Kohath was the largest branch, with the heaviest spiritual responsibility. Notice that privilege brought greater care. Those who carried the ark had to be most careful. James 3 says, be not many masters, knowing that we shall receive the greater condemnation. Today, if you are given greater privilege, handle it with greater care. Closeness to holy things calls for reverence.
S: The Kohathites included the Amramites, Izeharites, Hebronites and Uzzielites, the family of Moses and Aaron. They had the privilege of carrying the holiest objects, which required the greatest care. If you are given greater privilege, handle it with greater care and reverence.

== 3:29
T: Southward
R: 'The families of the sons of Kohath shall pitch on the side of the tabernacle southward.' The Kohathite clans were to camp on the south side of the tabernacle. Notice southward. Between Reuben's camp and the tabernacle. Each Levite branch had its assigned side. The tabernacle was surrounded by those who served it. Notice that Korah, a Kohathite, camped here. So did Reuben's men Dathan and Abiram. In Numbers 16, they joined in rebellion together. Proximity can spread influence, for good or ill. Notice First Corinthians 15. Be not deceived: evil communications corrupt good manners. The people near us shape us. Proverbs 13 says, He that walketh with wise men shall be wise: but a companion of fools shall be destroyed. Today, consider who camps near you. Choose companions who draw you toward God, and be a good influence on those beside you.

== 3:30
T: Elizaphan son of Uzziel
R: 'And the chief of the house of the father of the families of the Kohathites shall be Elizaphan the son of Uzziel.' The leader of the Kohathites was Elizaphan son of Uzziel. Notice Elizaphan. Notice that faithful service in hard moments prepares for leadership. He did difficult work without complaint. Matthew 25 says, Well done, thou good and faithful servant: thou hast been faithful over a few things, I will make thee ruler over many things. Today, be faithful in hard tasks. God may be preparing you for greater responsibility.

Today, carry God's presence personally and carefully. Some things cannot be delegated to machinery. They must be borne on our own shoulders.

Today, if you feel weak, remember Merari. God gives strength to carry heavy loads, even to those whose names speak of weakness.
