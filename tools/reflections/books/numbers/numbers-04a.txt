# Numbers 4:1-24: The Kohathites and the most holy things

== 4:1
T: Instructions for the journey
R: 'And the LORD spake unto Moses and unto Aaron, saying.' God spoke to Moses and Aaron about how the Levites would carry the tabernacle when Israel moved. Notice that God prepared them for movement. The tabernacle was not a permanent building. It traveled with the people. Worship went where they went. Notice Moses and Aaron together. Both leaders received these instructions. Aaron's priestly role was essential to the moving process. Notice that God gave details for transitions. Moving is often a time when things go wrong. Holy things could be mishandled. God gave careful instructions for the in-between times. Notice that God's presence travels with His people. Exodus 33 records Moses saying, If thy presence go not with me, carry us not up hence. Matthew 28 records Jesus saying, lo, I am with you alway. Today, trust that God goes with you through every move and transition. His presence is not left behind.
S: The LORD spoke to Moses and Aaron about how the tabernacle would be carried when Israel moved. God prepared His people for transitions, and His presence traveled with them. Trust that God goes with you through every move and transition.
P: His presence goes with you. Numbers 4:1: 'And the LORD spake unto Moses and unto Aaron.' God prepared them for moving. He is with you through every transition.

== 4:2
T: Take the sum of Kohath
R: 'Take the sum of the sons of Kohath from among the sons of Levi, after their families, by the house of their fathers.' God told them to count the Kohathite branch of the Levites by clans and families. Notice Kohath first. Though Gershon was the eldest son of Levi, Kohath was counted first. Their work with the most holy things gave them priority. Notice that importance of task, not birth order, determined the sequence. God values the work done for His presence. Notice from among the sons of Levi. Kohath was set apart within a set-apart tribe. Layers of holiness. The closer to God's presence, the greater the care. Notice that God gives some people especially sacred responsibilities. Second Timothy 2 speaks of vessels unto honour, sanctified, and meet for the master's use. Today, consider how God may be calling you to serve near His presence. Prepare yourself to be a vessel fit for His use.
S: God told Moses and Aaron to take the sum of the sons of Kohath from among the Levites. Kohath was counted first because of their work with the most holy things. Prepare yourself to be a vessel fit for the Master's use, ready to serve near His presence.
P: Set apart within set apart. Numbers 4:2: 'Take the sum of the sons of Kohath.' The task, not birth order, came first. Be a vessel meet for the Master's use.

== 4:3
T: Thirty to fifty
R: 'From thirty years old and upward even until fifty years old, all that enter into the host, to do the work in the tabernacle of the congregation.' They were to count all men from thirty to fifty years old who could serve at the tabernacle. Notice thirty years old. Maturity was required. Young men could learn, but the heavy service began at thirty. Luke 3 says Jesus began his ministry at about thirty years of age. Joseph stood before Pharaoh at thirty. David became king at thirty. Notice until fifty. Service had an end. After fifty, the heavy carrying stopped. Numbers 8 says older Levites could still assist and keep the charge. Their role changed, but they still served. Notice the seasons of service. Preparation, active service, then mentoring. Each stage matters. Ecclesiastes 3 says, To every thing there is a season. Today, embrace your current season of service. Prepare well, serve fully, and when the time comes, pass on what you know.
S: The Kohathites were numbered from thirty to fifty years old, all who could do the work of the tabernacle. Service began at maturity, as Jesus began His ministry at about thirty, and later shifted to assisting. Embrace your current season of service, preparing well, serving fully and passing on what you know.
P: Seasons of service. Numbers 4:3: 'From thirty years old and upward even until fifty years old.' Prepare, serve, then mentor. Embrace your current season today.

== 4:4

R: 'This shall be the service of the sons of Kohath in the tabernacle of the congregation, about the most holy things.' The work of the Kohathites at the tabernacle was the care of the most holy things. Notice about the most holy things. The ark, the table, the lampstand, the altars. The objects closest to God's presence. Kohath was entrusted with them. Notice the weight of this calling. Most holy things required most careful handling. One mistake could be fatal. First Peter 4 says, If any man minister, let him do it as of the ability which God giveth. Hebrews 12 says, let us have grace, whereby we may serve God acceptably with reverence and godly fear. Today, approach your service to God with reverence. Whatever holy things you handle, His word, His people, His worship, treat them with care.

When the camp moved, Aaron and his sons went in, took down the veil, and covered the ark with it. Notice Aaron shall come, and his sons. Only the priests could handle the ark directly. The Kohathites would carry it, but only after the priests covered it. Notice the covering vail. The veil that hid the Most Holy Place now covered the ark itself. God's glory was veiled in Christ's humanity. John 1 says, the Word was made flesh… and we beheld his glory. Today, marvel that God veiled His glory in human flesh to come near to us. In Christ, the hidden God is revealed.

The Hebrew word for service here is often used for military service. The Levites were enlisted for sacred duty. Their work was like a soldier's. Notice the same age range as Kohath. All Levite branches served the same years. Equal expectations. Notice that serving God is like serving in an army. Discipline, duty, and teamwork. Second Timothy 2 says, Thou therefore endure hardness, as a good soldier of Jesus Christ. No man that warreth entangleth himself with the affairs of this life; that he may please him who hath chosen him to be a soldier. Today, see your service to God as a sacred enlistment. Serve with discipline and devotion.
S: Moses was to number the Gershonites from thirty to fifty years old, all who entered in to perform the service. The word for service echoes military enlistment, showing the discipline of sacred duty. See your service to God as a sacred enlistment and serve with discipline and devotion.

== 4:24
T: To serve, and for burdens
R: 'This is the service of the families of the Gershonites, to serve, and for burdens.' This was the work of the Gershonite clans, their service and their carrying. Notice to serve, and for burdens. Two parts of their calling. Service and burden-bearing. They worked and they carried. Notice that service includes carrying loads. Ministry is not only words and worship. It includes heavy lifting, both literal and figurative. Galatians 6 says, Bear ye one another's burdens, and so fulfil the law of Christ. Notice that burdens were assigned. No one carried everything. Each had a share. When burdens are shared, they become lighter. Matthew 11 records Jesus saying, my yoke is easy, and my burden is light. Christ shares our load. Today, embrace both service and burden-bearing. Carry your share, help others carry theirs, and rest in Christ, whose burden is light.
S: The Gershonites' work was to serve and to bear burdens. Their calling included carrying loads, with each sharing a portion. Embrace both service and burden-bearing, help others carry theirs, and rest in Christ, whose burden is light.
P: To serve and to carry. Numbers 4:24: 'to serve, and for burdens.' Bear one another's burdens. Christ's yoke is easy and His burden light.
