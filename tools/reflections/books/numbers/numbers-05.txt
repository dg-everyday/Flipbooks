# Numbers 5: A clean camp, confession and the trial of jealousy

== 5:1
T: A holy camp
R: 'And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying.' God spoke to Moses about keeping the camp pure. Notice the timing. The camp had just been organized around the tabernacle. Now God addressed its purity. Order was not enough. Holiness was required. Notice that chapters 5 and 6 turn from arrangement to character. How the camp was set up mattered, but how the people lived mattered more. Notice that God cares about the inner life of His community. Structure without holiness is empty. A well-organized church can still be spiritually sick. Revelation 3 says to Sardis, thou hast a name that thou livest, and art dead. Second Timothy 3 warns of those having a form of godliness, but denying the power thereof. Today, ask God to make your life holy, not just orderly. Let His presence shape your character, not only your schedule.
S: The LORD spoke to Moses about keeping the newly ordered camp pure. Order was not enough without holiness, as structure without spiritual life is empty. Ask God to make your life holy, not merely orderly, and let His presence shape your character.
P: Holy, not just orderly. Numbers 5:1: 'And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying.' Structure without holiness is empty. Let God shape your character today.

== 5:2
T: Put out of the camp
R: 'Command the children of Israel, that they put out of the camp every leper, and every one that hath an issue, and whosoever is defiled by the dead.' The Israelites were to send out of the camp anyone with a serious skin disease, a discharge, or uncleanness from contact with a dead body. Notice put out of the camp. These conditions were ritual uncleanness, not necessarily sin. Now they were applied to the traveling camp. Notice that the separation was temporary. Lepers could be healed and return. Those defiled by the dead could be cleansed after seven days. It was not permanent banishment. Notice that this shows how seriously God took His dwelling among them. The camp was holy because He was there. Yet notice Jesus. Mark 1 says Jesus touched a leper and healed him. Luke 17 shows Him meeting lepers outside a village. Jesus went to those outside the camp. Today, remember that Christ comes to those on the outside. He reaches into exclusion to bring people home.
S: Israel was to put out of the camp every leper, everyone with an issue and whoever was defiled by the dead. The separation was temporary and ritual, showing God's holiness in their midst, while Jesus later went to those outside. Remember that Christ comes to those on the outside to bring them home.
P: Outside the camp. Numbers 5:2: 'put out of the camp every leper.' The camp was holy because God was there. Jesus went to those outside to bring them home.

== 5:3
T: In the midst whereof I dwell
R: 'Both male and female shall ye put out, without the camp shall ye put them; that they defile not their camps, in the midst whereof I dwell.' Both men and women were to be sent outside the camp so they would not defile it, because God lived among them. Notice in the midst whereof I dwell. This is the reason. God lived in the camp. His presence made it holy. Everything flowed from the fact that God was there. Notice both male and female. The same rule for all. No partiality. Notice that God's presence changes everything. Where God dwells, holiness matters. First Corinthians 3 says, Know ye not that ye are the temple of God, and that the Spirit of God dwelleth in you? Because God lives in His people, their lives must be holy. Second Corinthians 6 says, I will dwell in them, and walk in them. Today, remember that God dwells in you. Let that truth shape how you live and what you allow into your life.
S: Both male and female were to be put outside the camp so they would not defile the camps in whose midst God dwelt. God's presence among them made holiness matter. Remember that God dwells in you and let that shape how you live and what you allow in.
P: God dwells here. Numbers 5:3: 'in the midst whereof I dwell.' God's presence made the camp holy. You are His temple. Let that shape your life today.

Notice recompense. Making it right. Paying back what was taken. Confession without restitution is incomplete. Notice add the fifth part. Twenty percent extra. Restitution went beyond the loss. Generous repentance. Notice give it unto him. The money went to the victim, not to a general fund. The person harmed was restored.
