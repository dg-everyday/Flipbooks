# Numbers 6: The Nazarite vow and the priestly blessing

== 6:1
T: Special devotion
R: 'And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying.' God spoke to Moses about a special vow of dedication that any Israelite could make. Notice the placement. After laws for purity and faithfulness came a law for extraordinary devotion. God made room for people who wanted to give Him more. Notice that God welcomes wholehearted devotion. Not everyone was a priest or Levite. But anyone could set himself apart in a special way. The door to deeper devotion was open to all. Notice that devotion was voluntary. God did not require it. He invited it. Psalm 110 says, Thy people shall be willing in the day of thy power. Romans 12 says, present your bodies a living sacrifice. Today, consider how God may be inviting you to deeper devotion. The door is open to anyone who wants to draw closer.
S: The LORD spoke to Moses about a special vow of dedication open to any Israelite. God made room for voluntary, extraordinary devotion beyond what was required. Consider how God may be inviting you to deeper devotion, for the door is open to all.
P: An open door. Numbers 6:1: 'And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying.' God welcomes wholehearted devotion from anyone. Draw closer to Him today.

== 6:2
T: Either man or woman
R: 'Speak unto the children of Israel, and say unto them, When either man or woman shall separate themselves to vow a vow of a Nazarite, to separate themselves unto the LORD.' When any man or woman wanted to make a special vow of separation to the LORD as a Nazarite, these rules applied. Notice either man or woman. Women could take this vow too. Deep devotion was open to all. Gender did not limit dedication. Notice separate themselves unto the LORD. The Nazarite's purpose was not mainly to give things up but to be set apart for God. The focus was on God, not on rules. Notice the word Nazarite. It comes from a Hebrew word meaning separated or consecrated. Samson and Samuel were Nazarites from birth. John the Baptist lived a similar life. Galatians 1 records Paul saying God separated me from my mother's womb. Today, remember that devotion to God is open to everyone. Set yourself apart for Him, whether man or woman.
S: Either a man or a woman could vow the vow of a Nazarite to separate themselves to the LORD. Deep devotion was open to all, focused on being set apart for God. Remember that devotion is open to everyone and set yourself apart for Him.
P: Open to all. Numbers 6:2: 'When either man or woman shall separate themselves… unto the LORD.' Deep devotion is for everyone. Set yourself apart for God today.

== 6:3
T: Separate from wine
R: 'He shall separate himself from wine and strong drink, and shall drink no vinegar of wine… neither shall he drink any liquor of grapes, nor eat moist grapes, or dried.' The Nazarite had to abstain from wine, other fermented drink, vinegar made from them, grape juice, and grapes, fresh or dried. Notice wine. A symbol of joy and celebration. Psalm 104 speaks of wine that maketh glad the heart of man. The Nazarite gave up even good pleasures. Notice the thoroughness. Not just wine, but grapes, juice, raisins. Everything from the vine. This was a complete fast from one area of life. Notice that devotion sometimes means giving up good things, not just bad ones. Hebrews 12 says, lay aside every weight. A weight is not necessarily sin, but it can slow us down. First Corinthians 9 records Paul saying he kept his body under for the sake of the gospel. Today, ask whether God is calling you to set aside a good thing for a season of deeper devotion.
S: The Nazarite separated from wine, strong drink, vinegar, grape juice and grapes, fresh or dried. Devotion meant giving up even good pleasures completely for a season. Ask whether God is calling you to set aside a good thing for deeper devotion.
P: Laying aside good things. Numbers 6:3: 'He shall separate himself from wine and strong drink.' Not sin, but a weight. Lay aside what slows your devotion today.

== 6:4
T: From kernels to husk
R: 'All the days of his separation shall he eat nothing that is made of the vine tree, from the kernels even to the husk.' During the whole period of his vow, he was not to eat anything from the grapevine, not even the seeds or skins. Notice from the kernels even to the husk. Nothing from the vine. Not even the smallest part. The Nazarite drew a clear line and kept it completely. Notice all the days. The vow had a defined period. Throughout it, the commitment was total. Notice that half-hearted vows are not vows. If he ate seeds but not grapes, the vow would be compromised. Completeness mattered. Notice that drawing clear lines helps keep commitments. Vague promises are easy to break. Clear boundaries make faithfulness possible. Daniel 1 says Daniel purposed in his heart that he would not defile himself. He drew a clear line. Today, if you make a commitment to God, draw clear lines. Keep them completely, from kernel to husk.

== 6:27
