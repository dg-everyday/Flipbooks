# Numbers 7:48-71: The offerings of Ephraim, Manasseh, Benjamin and Dan

== 7:48
T: Ephraim on the seventh day
R: 'On the seventh day Elishama the son of Ammihud, prince of the children of Ephraim, offered.' On the seventh day, Elishama son of Ammihud, leader of Ephraim, brought his offering. Notice the seventh day. Many think the offerings continued even through a sabbath. Worship at God's altar did not stop. Bringing gifts to God was itself holy work. Notice Ephraim. The tribe that received Jacob's greater blessing. Now its prince came to worship. Blessing should lead to worship. Notice Elishama. His name means God has heard. Ephraim's prince came to the God who hears. Notice that the western camp began here. Rachel's descendants now had their turn. Psalm 116 says, I love the LORD, because he hath heard my voice. First John 5 says, if we ask any thing according to his will, he heareth us. Today, worship the God who hears you. Let every blessing you have received lead you back to Him in gratitude.
S: On the seventh day Elishama son of Ammihud, prince of Ephraim, offered, beginning the western camp's gifts. The tribe of Jacob's greater blessing came to worship, led by a man whose name means God has heard. Worship the God who hears you and let every blessing lead you back to Him.
P: The God who hears. Numbers 7:48: 'On the seventh day Elishama the son of Ammihud.' His name means God has heard. Let every blessing lead you to worship.

== 7:49
T: Ephraim's silver
R: 'His offering was one silver charger, the weight whereof was an hundred and thirty shekels, one silver bowl of seventy shekels, after the shekel of the sanctuary; both of them full of fine flour mingled with oil for a meat offering.' His offering was the same silver plate and bowl filled with fine flour and oil. Notice that Ephraim, the favored tribe, gave the same as the others. Greater blessing did not mean showier giving. Humility marked the offering. Notice that God's favor is not a reason for pride. Ephraim later struggled with pride. Judges 8 and 12 show Ephraim complaining when not included in battles. Hosea 13 says, When Ephraim spake trembling, he exalted himself in Israel. Pride followed blessing. Notice that here, at the altar, Ephraim gave humbly and equally. Proverbs 16 says, Pride goeth before destruction. First Peter 5 says, Humble yourselves therefore under the mighty hand of God. Today, if God has blessed you greatly, respond with humility, not pride. Give as one among equals.
S: Elishama offered the same silver charger and bowl as every tribe. Ephraim, the favored tribe, gave humbly and equally here, though pride later troubled them. If God has blessed you greatly, respond with humility, not pride, giving as one among equals.
P: Blessed and humble. Numbers 7:49: 'His offering was one silver charger.' Favored Ephraim gave the same as others. Respond to blessing with humility today.

== 7:51

He lingered in God's presence. His surrender was complete. Notice that the burnt offering pictures a life like Joshua's. Wholly given. Joshua 24 records him saying, as for me and my house, we will serve the LORD. Total commitment declared publicly. Notice Numbers 32. Caleb and Joshua wholly followed the LORD. Wholehearted devotion made them the only adults of that generation to enter the land. Today, follow the LORD wholly, like Joshua. Let your surrender be complete.
S: Elishama brought a young bullock, a ram and a lamb for a burnt offering. Ephraim was Joshua's tribe, and Joshua lingered in God's presence and wholly followed the LORD. Follow the LORD wholly, like Joshua, and let your surrender be complete.

Today, receive God as your reward. Begin again with a fresh start, knowing the Rock has redeemed you.

== 7:55
T: Manasseh's gift
R: 'His offering was one silver charger of the weight of an hundred and thirty shekels, one silver bowl of seventy shekels, after the shekel of the sanctuary; both of them full of fine flour mingled with oil for a meat offering.' His offering was the same silver plate and bowl filled with fine flour and oil. Notice that Manasseh was the smallest tribe in the census. Yet it gave the same as Judah, the largest. Smallness did not reduce its offering. Notice that small tribes gave proportionally more. For Manasseh, this gift represented a greater share of its resources. Mark 12 shows the widow who gave all she had. Jesus said she gave more than they all. Notice that God measures gifts by sacrifice, not size. A small tribe's equal gift was a great gift. Second Corinthians 8 praises the Macedonians whose deep poverty abounded unto the riches of their liberality. Today, do not think your gift is too small. God measures by the heart and the sacrifice behind it.
S: Gamaliel offered the same silver charger and bowl as every tribe. Manasseh, the smallest tribe, gave as much as the largest, a greater sacrifice for them. Do not think your gift is too small, for God measures by the heart and the sacrifice behind it.
P: Measured by sacrifice. Numbers 7:55: 'His offering was one silver charger.' Smallest tribe, same gift as the largest. God sees the sacrifice behind your gift.

== 7:71
T: Ahiezer's offering
R: 'And for a sacrifice of peace offerings, two oxen, five rams, five he goats, five lambs of the first year: this was the offering of Ahiezer the son of Ammishaddai.' For the peace offering he brought two oxen, five rams, five male goats, and five year-old lambs. This was Ahiezer's offering. Notice the tenth day of feasting. Ten days of fellowship meals. The camp had been celebrating for over a week. Notice Ammishaddai. The Almighty is with my people. Dan's prince was son of a man named for God's presence among His people. The feast celebrated exactly that. Notice that God's presence is worth celebrating. The tabernacle meant God lived among them. Every peace offering rejoiced in that truth. Matthew 1 says, they shall call his name Emmanuel, which being interpreted is, God with us. Today, rejoice that the Almighty is with His people. Celebrate God's presence with you.
