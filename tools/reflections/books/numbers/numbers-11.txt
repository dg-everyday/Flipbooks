# Numbers 11: Complaints, the seventy elders and the quails

== 11:1
T: When the people complained
R: 'And when the people complained, it displeased the LORD: and the LORD heard it; and his anger was kindled; and the fire of the LORD burnt among them, and consumed them that were in the uttermost parts of the camp.' The people complained about their hardships, and the LORD heard. His anger burned, and fire from the LORD consumed some of the outskirts of the camp. Notice the sudden change. Chapter 10 ended with Moses' beautiful prayer. Chapter 11 begins with complaining. Only three days into the journey. Notice the LORD heard it. Complaints are not private. God hears grumbling as clearly as prayer. Notice the uttermost parts of the camp. The edges. Perhaps those far from the tabernacle complained most. Distance from God's presence breeds discontent. Philippians 2 says, Do all things without murmurings and disputings. First Corinthians 10 warns, Neither murmur ye, as some of them also murmured, and were destroyed. Today, guard your heart against complaining. God hears your words. Choose gratitude instead.
S: The people complained, the LORD heard it, and fire burned among them at the uttermost parts of the camp. Only days into the journey, complaining replaced praise, and God heard their grumbling. Guard your heart against complaining and choose gratitude, for God hears your words.
P: God hears complaints. Numbers 11:1: 'when the people complained, it displeased the LORD.' Do all things without murmuring. Choose gratitude in every circumstance today.

== 11:2
T: Moses prayed
R: 'And the people cried unto Moses; and when Moses prayed unto the LORD, the fire was quenched.' When the people cried out to Moses, he prayed to the LORD, and the fire died down. Notice the people cried unto Moses. In trouble, they turned to their mediator. They did not pray themselves; they ran to Moses. Notice when Moses prayed… the fire was quenched. Intercession worked. One man's prayer saved many. Notice that Moses prayed for those who complained. He did not say they deserved it. He interceded. Notice James 5. The effectual fervent prayer of a righteous man availeth much. Hebrews 7 says Christ ever liveth to make intercession for them. Christ prays for us as Moses did, even when we complain. Today, pray for others, even those who frustrate you. And thank Christ, who intercedes for you.
S: The people cried to Moses, and when he prayed to the LORD, the fire was quenched. One man's intercession for the complainers saved many, as Christ intercedes for us. Pray for others, even those who frustrate you, and thank Christ for interceding for you.
P: Intercession quenches fire. Numbers 11:2: 'when Moses prayed unto the LORD, the fire was quenched.' Moses prayed for complainers. Christ prays for you. Pray for others.

== 11:3
T: Taberah
R: 'And he called the name of the place Taberah: because the fire of the LORD burnt among them.' So that place was called Taberah, because fire from the LORD had burned among them. Notice Taberah. The name means burning. The place was named after the judgment. A memorial of what happened. Notice that some places remind us of lessons learned. Israel would remember Taberah. Deuteronomy 9 lists it among the places where Israel provoked the LORD. Notice that memorials can warn. Not all memories are happy. Some remind us not to repeat mistakes. First Corinthians 10 says, these things were our examples, to the intent we should not lust after evil things, as they also lusted. Notice that God's mercy also happened there. The fire was quenched. Judgment and mercy met at Taberah. Today, remember your own Taberahs. Learn from past mistakes, and thank God for the mercy that quenched the fire.
S: Moses called the place Taberah, burning, because the fire of the LORD burned among them. The name preserved a warning and also recalled God's mercy in quenching the fire. Remember your own Taberahs, learning from past mistakes and thanking God for His mercy.
P: Remember Taberah. Numbers 11:3: 'he called the name of the place Taberah.' A place of burning and mercy. Learn from past mistakes and thank God for grace.

== 11:4
T: The mixt multitude
R: 'And the mixt multitude that was among them fell a lusting: and the children of Israel also wept again, and said, Who shall give us flesh to eat?' The mixed crowd with them began craving other food, and the Israelites started wailing again: If only we had meat to eat! Notice the mixt multitude. Non-Israelites who left Egypt with them. Exodus 12 mentions them. They started the craving. Discontent spread from the edges inward. Notice the children of Israel also wept. God's people caught the same attitude. Complaining is contagious. Notice fell a lusting. Strong craving. Not just hunger but desire for what they did not have. Notice that bad influences spread. First Corinthians 15 says, evil communications corrupt good manners. Notice also that the root issue was desire. James 1 says, every man is tempted, when he is drawn away of his own lust. Today, watch your influences and your desires. Do not let others' discontent become yours.
S: The mixt multitude among Israel fell a lusting, and the children of Israel also wept, asking for flesh to eat. Craving spread from the edges inward, for discontent is contagious. Watch your influences and desires and do not let others' discontent become yours.
P: Contagious discontent. Numbers 11:4: 'the mixt multitude… fell a lusting: and the children of Israel also wept.' Complaining spreads. Guard your heart from others' discontent.

== 11:5
T: We remember the fish
R: 'We remember the fish, which we did eat in Egypt freely; the cucumbers, and the melons, and the leeks, and the onions, and the garlick.' We remember the fish we ate in Egypt at no cost, also the cucumbers, melons, leeks, onions, and garlic. Notice we remember. Selective memory. They remembered food but forgot slavery. They remembered fish but forgot the whips. Notice freely. Nothing in Egypt was free. They paid with their freedom and their sons' lives. Exodus 1 says the Egyptians made their lives bitter with hard bondage. Notice that nostalgia distorts. The past looks better than it was. Ecclesiastes 7 says, Say not thou, What is the cause that the former days were better than these? for thou dost not enquire wisely concerning this. Notice that Satan uses false memories of sin's pleasures. Hebrews 11 says Moses chose rather to suffer affliction… than to enjoy the pleasures of sin for a season. Today, do not romanticize your old life. Remember the chains, not just the cucumbers.
S: The people remembered the fish they ate freely in Egypt and its cucumbers, melons, leeks, onions and garlic. Their nostalgia forgot the slavery and cruelty, for nothing in Egypt was truly free. Do not romanticize your old life, but remember the chains, not just the cucumbers.
P: Selective memory. Numbers 11:5: 'We remember the fish, which we did eat in Egypt freely.' Nothing in Egypt was free. Don't romanticize your old chains today.
