# Numbers 12: Miriam and Aaron speak against Moses

== 12:1
T: The Ethiopian woman
R: 'And Miriam and Aaron spake against Moses because of the Ethiopian woman whom he had married: for he had married an Ethiopian woman.' Miriam and Aaron began to criticize Moses because of the Cushite woman he had married. Notice that the attack came from family. Moses' own sister and brother turned against him. The closest wounds often hurt most. Notice the stated reason. His foreign wife. Perhaps prejudice played a part. Yet the next verse reveals the deeper issue. Notice that criticism often hides its true motive. They complained about his wife but really envied his position. Notice that God welcomed this Ethiopian woman. In Christ, there is neither Jew nor Greek. Acts 8 shows an Ethiopian official baptized and rejoicing. Today, examine your criticism of others. Is it truly about what you say, or is something deeper, like envy, at work?
S: Miriam and Aaron spoke against Moses because of the Ethiopian woman he had married. The criticism came from family and hid a deeper motive of envy. Examine your criticism of others and ask whether envy is the real cause.
P: Hidden motives. Numbers 12:1: 'Miriam and Aaron spake against Moses because of the Ethiopian woman.' Criticism often hides envy. Examine your heart before you speak.

== 12:2
T: Hath he not spoken also by us?
R: 'And they said, Hath the LORD indeed spoken only by Moses? hath he not spoken also by us? And the LORD heard it.' They asked, Has the LORD spoken only through Moses? Hasn't He also spoken through us? And the LORD heard this. Notice the real issue. Jealousy of Moses' authority. They wanted equal standing. Notice that Miriam was a prophetess and Aaron the high priest. God had used them. Yet they wanted more. Gifts can breed pride. Notice and the LORD heard it. The same words as in chapter 11. God hears what we say about His servants. Notice Proverbs 6. Among the things God hates is he that soweth discord among brethren. Today, guard against jealousy of others' callings. Be content with how God uses you, and remember that He hears your words.
S: Miriam and Aaron asked whether the LORD had spoken only by Moses and not also by them, and the LORD heard it. Their real issue was jealousy of Moses' authority, though God had used them too. Guard against jealousy of others' callings and be content with how God uses you.
P: The LORD heard it. Numbers 12:2: 'hath he not spoken also by us? And the LORD heard it.' Jealousy of others' callings grieves God. Be content with your own.
H: #DailyGrace #Numbers122 #Jealousy #Contentment #Calling #BibleVerse

== 12:3
T: Very meek
R: '(Now the man Moses was very meek, above all the men which were upon the face of the earth.)' Moses was a very humble man, more humble than anyone else on the face of the earth. Notice the parenthesis. Scripture pauses to describe Moses. He did not defend himself. He was meek. Notice meek. Not weak, but strong under control. Moses had confronted Pharaoh. Yet he did not fight for his own position. Notice that meekness does not retaliate. Moses let God defend him. Notice Matthew 5. Blessed are the meek: for they shall inherit the earth. Matthew 11 records Jesus saying, I am meek and lowly in heart. Christ is the perfect example. Today, practice meekness. When criticized, let God defend you rather than rushing to defend yourself.
S: Moses was very meek, above all men on the earth. He did not defend himself against criticism but let God answer for him, as Christ is meek and lowly. Practice meekness and let God defend you when you are criticized.
P: Very meek. Numbers 12:3: 'the man Moses was very meek.' Strength under control, letting God defend him. Blessed are the meek. Follow Christ's way.

== 12:4
T: Come out ye three
R: 'And the LORD spake suddenly unto Moses, and unto Aaron, and unto Miriam, Come out ye three unto the tabernacle of the congregation. And they three came out.' At once the LORD said to Moses, Aaron, and Miriam, Come out to the tabernacle, all three of you. So the three of them went out. Notice suddenly. God acted quickly. He did not let the gossip spread. Notice ye three. All three were summoned. Moses too. God would settle the matter openly. Notice that God deals with conflict directly. He brought everyone together. Matthew 18 teaches going to a brother directly. Notice that God defended Moses because Moses did not defend himself. Psalm 37 says, Commit thy way unto the LORD… And he shall bring forth thy righteousness as the light. Today, trust God to address conflicts in His time. Bring matters into the light rather than letting them fester in whispers.
S: The LORD suddenly called Moses, Aaron and Miriam to come out to the tabernacle, and they three came. God dealt with the conflict quickly and openly, defending Moses who had not defended himself. Trust God to address conflicts and bring matters into the light rather than whispers.
P: Into the light. Numbers 12:4: 'the LORD spake suddenly… Come out ye three.' God addressed the conflict openly. Commit your way to Him; He brings forth your righteousness.

== 12:5
T: The LORD came down
R: 'And the LORD came down in the pillar of the cloud, and stood in the door of the tabernacle, and called Aaron and Miriam: and they both came forth.' Then the LORD came down in a pillar of cloud. He stood at the entrance to the tabernacle and summoned Aaron and Miriam. When the two of them stepped forward, He spoke. Notice the LORD came down. God Himself came to settle the matter. This was serious. Notice called Aaron and Miriam. Moses stood aside. God addressed the two who had spoken against him. Notice that God sees and responds to how we treat His servants. Notice Psalm 105. Touch not mine anointed, and do my prophets no harm. God protects those He has called. Today, be careful how you speak of those God has appointed. He stands beside His faithful servants.
S: The LORD came down in the pillar of cloud, stood at the tabernacle door and called Aaron and Miriam forward. God Himself came to address those who had spoken against His servant. Be careful how you speak of those God has appointed, for He stands beside His faithful servants.
P: God came down. Numbers 12:5: 'the LORD came down in the pillar of the cloud… and called Aaron and Miriam.' Touch not Mine anointed. God defends His servants.

== 12:6
T: In a vision, in a dream
R: 'And he said, Hear now my words: If there be a prophet among you, I the LORD will make myself known unto him in a vision, and will speak unto him in a dream.' He said, Listen to my words: When there is a prophet among you, I, the LORD, reveal myself to them in visions, I speak to them in dreams. Notice hear now my words. God commanded attention. They had been speaking; now they must listen. Notice the usual way. Prophets received visions and dreams. Real revelation, but indirect. Notice that God acknowledged Aaron and Miriam's gifts. He did not deny that He spoke through others. But Moses was different. Notice James 1. be swift to hear, slow to speak. Aaron and Miriam had spoken quickly. Now they had to listen. Today, listen to God's word before speaking your own. Let Him correct your understanding.
S: God told Aaron and Miriam to hear His words, explaining that He spoke to prophets in visions and dreams. They had spoken quickly and now had to listen. Listen to God's word before speaking your own and let Him correct your understanding.
P: Hear now My words. Numbers 12:6: 'Hear now my words: If there be a prophet among you.' They spoke; now they listened. Be swift to hear and slow to speak.

== 12:7
T: Faithful in all mine house
R: 'My servant Moses is not so, who is faithful in all mine house.' But this is not true of my servant Moses; he is faithful in all my house. Notice my servant Moses. God called him servant. Not master, not king. His greatest title was servant. Notice faithful in all mine house. Moses' defining quality was faithfulness. In everything God entrusted, Moses was reliable. Notice Hebrews 3. It quotes this verse and says Christ is greater: Moses verily was faithful in all his house, as a servant… But Christ as a son over his own house; whose house are we. Notice that faithfulness is what God values most. Matthew 25 says, Well done, thou good and faithful servant. Today, aim for faithfulness. Let God call you His faithful servant.
S: God said Moses was not like other prophets, for he was His servant, faithful in all His house. Hebrews quotes this to show that Christ, the Son over God's house, is greater still. Aim for faithfulness, that God may call you His faithful servant.
P: Faithful in all His house. Numbers 12:7: 'My servant Moses… is faithful in all mine house.' Faithfulness is what God values. Well done, good and faithful servant.

== 12:8
T: Mouth to mouth
R: 'With him will I speak mouth to mouth, even apparently, and not in dark speeches; and the similitude of the LORD shall he behold: wherefore then were ye not afraid to speak against my servant Moses?' With him I speak face to face, clearly and not in riddles; he sees the form of the LORD. Why then were you not afraid to speak against my servant Moses? Notice mouth to mouth. Direct communication. No riddles. A unique intimacy. Notice the similitude of the LORD. Moses saw a form of God's glory. Exodus 33 shows Moses seeing God's back. Notice wherefore then were ye not afraid? God rebuked their boldness. Speaking against His servant was speaking against Him. Notice John 1. No man hath seen God at any time; the only begotten Son… he hath declared him. Christ reveals God even more fully than Moses. Today, value the clear revelation God has given in Christ and His word. And fear to speak lightly against those He has appointed.
S: God said He spoke with Moses mouth to mouth and asked why Aaron and Miriam were not afraid to speak against His servant. Moses had unique intimacy with God, and Christ reveals God more fully still. Value God's clear revelation in Christ and fear to speak lightly against His servants.
P: Mouth to mouth. Numbers 12:8: 'With him will I speak mouth to mouth.' Moses saw God's form; Christ declares Him fully. Value His revelation today.

== 12:9
T: He departed
R: 'And the anger of the LORD was kindled against them; and he departed.' The anger of the LORD burned against them, and He left them. Notice the anger of the LORD. Jealousy and criticism of God's servant provoked God. Notice he departed. God's presence withdrew. The cloud lifted. Nothing is more serious than God leaving. Gifts and positions mean little without His presence. Aaron and Miriam were still prophet and priest, yet the cloud lifted from them. Notice that sin disrupts fellowship with God. Isaiah 59 says, your iniquities have separated between you and your God. Notice that the departure was a warning. God's absence was meant to bring repentance. Psalm 51 says, Cast me not away from thy presence; and take not thy holy spirit from me. Today, value God's presence above all. Guard your heart against the sins that grieve Him.
S: The LORD's anger was kindled against Aaron and Miriam, and He departed. His withdrawn presence was the most serious consequence and a call to repentance. Value God's presence above all and guard your heart against sins that grieve Him.
P: He departed. Numbers 12:9: 'the anger of the LORD was kindled against them; and he departed.' Sin separates. Cast me not away from Thy presence. Guard your heart.
H: #DailyGrace #Numbers129 #Presence #Sin #Repentance #BibleVerse

== 12:10
T: White as snow
R: 'And the cloud departed from off the tabernacle; and, behold, Miriam became leprous, white as snow: and Aaron looked upon Miriam, and, behold, she was leprous.' When the cloud lifted from above the tabernacle, Miriam's skin was leprous, like snow. Aaron turned toward her and saw that she had a defiling skin disease. Notice Miriam became leprous. Judgment fell on her, likely because she led the criticism. Her name is first in verse 1. Notice white as snow. Leprosy made her unclean. Exclusion from the camp would follow. Notice that Aaron was spared, perhaps because his priestly role was needed, or because Miriam led. Yet Aaron saw and was shaken. Notice the sobering lesson. Speaking against God's servants is serious. Yet the story ends with healing, as the next verses show. Today, take seriously the harm of critical words. And trust that God's discipline aims at restoration.
S: When the cloud departed, Miriam became leprous, white as snow, and Aaron saw it. Judgment fell on the one who led the criticism, showing the seriousness of speaking against God's servant. Take seriously the harm of critical words and trust that God's discipline aims at restoration.
P: A sobering lesson. Numbers 12:10: 'Miriam became leprous, white as snow.' Critical words against God's servants are serious. His discipline aims to restore.

== 12:11
T: Wherein we have done foolishly
R: 'And Aaron said unto Moses, Alas, my lord, I beseech thee, lay not the sin upon us, wherein we have done foolishly, and wherein we have sinned.' Aaron said to Moses, Please, my lord, I ask you not to hold against us the sin we have so foolishly committed. Notice my lord. Aaron, who had challenged Moses, now called him lord. Humility replaced pride. Notice we have done foolishly… we have sinned. Honest confession. No excuses. Aaron owned the sin. Notice that Aaron turned to Moses for intercession. The one they criticized became the one they needed. Notice Proverbs 28. whoso confesseth and forsaketh them shall have mercy. Today, when you have wronged someone, confess it humbly. And do not be too proud to ask for the prayers of the very person you hurt.
S: Aaron pleaded with Moses, calling him lord, and confessed that they had done foolishly and sinned. Pride gave way to honest confession, and they needed the one they had criticized. When you have wronged someone, confess humbly and do not be too proud to ask for their prayers.
P: We have sinned. Numbers 12:11: 'wherein we have done foolishly, and wherein we have sinned.' Aaron confessed without excuse. Whoso confesseth shall have mercy.

== 12:12
T: Let her not be as one dead
R: 'Let her not be as one dead, of whom the flesh is half consumed when he cometh out of his mother's womb.' Do not let her be like a stillborn infant coming from its mother's womb with its flesh half eaten away. Notice Aaron's plea for his sister. Vivid and desperate. He begged for Miriam's healing. Notice the love between siblings. Despite their shared sin, Aaron cared deeply for Miriam. Notice that sin's effects are terrible. Leprosy was like living death. Sin also brings spiritual decay. Romans 6 says, the wages of sin is death. Notice that intercession for others flows from love. Aaron could not heal her, but he could plead. Love does not stop praying when someone suffers the result of their own choices. It brings them to God again and again. Today, plead for those suffering under sin's effects. Bring their need to God with compassion.
S: Aaron begged that Miriam not be as one dead with flesh half consumed. Despite their shared sin, he pleaded for his sister out of love, showing how terrible sin's effects are. Plead with compassion for those suffering under sin's effects and bring their need to God.
P: Plead for others. Numbers 12:12: 'Let her not be as one dead.' Aaron begged for his sister. Bring those suffering under sin to God with compassion.

== 12:13
T: Heal her now
R: 'And Moses cried unto the LORD, saying, Heal her now, O God, I beseech thee.' So Moses cried out to the LORD, Please, God, heal her! Notice Moses cried. He did not gloat. He did not say she deserved it. He prayed for the one who had attacked him. Notice heal her now. Urgent, short, heartfelt. One of the shortest prayers in Scripture. Notice that this is what meekness looks like. Moses forgave and interceded. He could have stayed silent and let justice take its course. Instead he spent his first words on her healing. Meekness turns injury into intercession. Notice Matthew 5. pray for them which despitefully use you. Luke 23 records Jesus praying, Father, forgive them. Christ prayed for His attackers. Today, pray for those who have hurt you. Like Moses, ask God to heal them rather than harm them.
S: Moses cried to the LORD, Heal her now, O God, I beseech thee. He prayed for the sister who had attacked him, showing true meekness and forgiveness. Pray for those who have hurt you, asking God to heal them rather than harm them.
P: Heal her now. Numbers 12:13: 'Heal her now, O God, I beseech thee.' Moses prayed for his attacker. Pray for those who despitefully use you today.

== 12:14
T: Seven days
R: 'And the LORD said unto Moses, If her father had but spit in her face, should she not be ashamed seven days? let her be shut out from the camp seven days, and after that let her be received in again.' The LORD replied to Moses, If her father had spit in her face, would she not have been in disgrace for seven days? Confine her outside the camp for seven days; after that she can be brought back. Notice the LORD answered. Healing would come, but after a time of discipline. Seven days outside. Notice that discipline had a limit. Seven days, then received in again. Not permanent exclusion. Notice that restoration was promised. After that let her be received in again. God's discipline ends in welcome. Notice Hebrews 12. whom the Lord loveth he chasteneth… afterward it yieldeth the peaceable fruit of righteousness. Today, if you face discipline, remember it has a limit. God's goal is to receive you in again.
S: God said Miriam should be shut out of the camp seven days, and after that be received in again. Discipline had a limit and ended with the promise of restoration. If you face God's discipline, remember it has an end and His goal is to receive you again.
P: Received in again. Numbers 12:14: 'let her be shut out… seven days, and after that let her be received in again.' Discipline has a limit. Restoration is God's goal.

== 12:15
T: The people journeyed not
R: 'And Miriam was shut out from the camp seven days: and the people journeyed not till Miriam was brought in again.' So Miriam was confined outside the camp for seven days, and the people did not move on till she was brought back. Notice the people journeyed not. The whole nation waited for Miriam. They did not leave her behind. Notice the honor in this. Though disciplined, Miriam was still valued. The camp waited for her restoration. Notice that community waits for restoration. When one member is disciplined, the body waits and welcomes them back. Galatians 6 says, restore such an one in the spirit of meekness. Second Corinthians 2 urges forgiving and comforting a repentant brother. Today, wait patiently for those being restored. Do not leave them behind. Welcome them back when the time comes.
S: Miriam was shut out seven days, and the people did not journey until she was brought in again. The whole nation waited for her, valuing her even in discipline. Wait patiently for those being restored and welcome them back when the time comes.
P: They waited for her. Numbers 12:15: 'the people journeyed not till Miriam was brought in again.' The camp waited. Don't leave the disciplined behind. Welcome them back.

== 12:16
T: To the wilderness of Paran
R: 'And afterward the people removed from Hazeroth, and pitched in the wilderness of Paran.' After that, the people left Hazeroth and encamped in the Desert of Paran. Notice afterward. After Miriam's restoration. The journey continued together. Notice the wilderness of Paran. On the edge of the promised land. Chapter 13 would send spies from here. A decisive moment approached. Notice that the camp moved on whole. Miriam was with them. The family was restored. Seven days of waiting had not been wasted. A restored sister walked with them toward the land, and the whole camp was stronger for it. Notice that unity matters before great challenges. The next chapter brought the greatest test yet. Ephesians 4 says, Endeavouring to keep the unity of the Spirit in the bond of peace. Today, seek unity and restoration in your relationships before facing great challenges. Move forward together.
S: After Miriam was restored, the people removed from Hazeroth and pitched in the wilderness of Paran. The camp moved on whole and united toward the edge of the promised land and its greatest test. Seek unity and restoration in your relationships before facing great challenges.
P: Moving on together. Numbers 12:16: 'afterward the people removed from Hazeroth.' Restored and united, they faced the next test. Keep the unity of the Spirit.
