# Numbers 14:1-23: Rebellion at Kadesh and Moses' intercession

== 14:1
T: They wept that night
R: 'And all the congregation lifted up their voice, and cried; and the people wept that night.' That night all the members of the community raised their voices and wept aloud. Notice all the congregation. The evil report spread through the whole nation. One night of fear swept everyone. Notice wept that night. A night of despair. Not over sin, but over fear. They grieved the giants, not their unbelief. Notice that fear spreads like fire. Ten men's words brought a nation to tears. Notice the contrast with what could have been. They could have rejoiced over the land. Instead they wept over imagined defeat. Psalm 30 says, weeping may endure for a night, but joy cometh in the morning. For Israel, the morning brought rebellion instead of joy. Today, do not weep over imagined defeats. Bring your fears to God before they turn into despair.
S: All the congregation lifted up their voice and wept that night after the evil report. Ten men's fear swept a whole nation into despair over imagined defeat. Do not weep over imagined defeats, but bring your fears to God before they turn to despair.
P: A night of fear. Numbers 14:1: 'the people wept that night.' Ten men's fear spread to millions. Bring your fears to God before they become despair.

== 14:2
T: Would God we had died
R: 'And all the children of Israel murmured against Moses and against Aaron: and the whole congregation said unto them, Would God that we had died in the land of Egypt! or would God we had died in this wilderness!' All the Israelites grumbled against Moses and Aaron, and the whole assembly said to them, If only we had died in Egypt! Or in this wilderness! Notice murmured against Moses and against Aaron. They blamed their leaders. Fear turned to accusation. Notice would God that we had died. They wished for death. A terrible wish. And God would grant it in verse 28. Notice that careless words can come true. They said they wanted to die in the wilderness. That is exactly what happened. Proverbs 18 says, Death and life are in the power of the tongue. Matthew 12 says, by thy words thou shalt be condemned. Today, guard your words, especially in fear. Do not speak despair over your own life.
S: All Israel murmured against Moses and Aaron, wishing they had died in Egypt or in the wilderness. Their careless words in fear would later be granted, for death and life are in the power of the tongue. Guard your words in fear and do not speak despair over your life.
P: Careless words. Numbers 14:2: 'would God we had died in this wilderness!' They got what they said. Death and life are in the power of the tongue. Speak life.

== 14:3

Notice wherefore hath the LORD brought us? They accused God of bad intentions. Notice our wives and our children should be a prey. They used their children as an excuse. Yet God would bring those very children into the land. Notice return into Egypt. Fear made bondage seem attractive. Notice Second Peter 2. The dog is turned to his own vomit again. Returning to bondage is tragic. Galatians 5 says, be not entangled again with the yoke of bondage. Today, never question God's good intentions. He did not save you to destroy you. Do not go back to your old chains.
S: The people asked why the LORD had brought them to die and whether returning to Egypt would be better. Fear made them question God's intentions and made slavery seem attractive. Never question God's good intentions toward you and do not go back to your old chains.
P: Don't go back. Numbers 14:3: 'were it not better for us to return into Egypt?' Fear makes bondage look good. God didn't save you to destroy you. Stay free.

Then Moses and Aaron fell face down in front of the whole Israelite assembly gathered there. Notice fell on their faces. Not in anger but in humility and grief. They did not argue. They bowed. Notice before all the assembly. Publicly. The people saw their leaders humbled. Perhaps it was intercession; perhaps despair. Either way, they turned to God. Notice that humility is the right response to rebellion. Moses did not fight for his position. He fell before God. Notice Second Chronicles 7.
