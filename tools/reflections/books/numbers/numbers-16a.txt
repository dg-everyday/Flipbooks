# Numbers 16:1-25: Korah's rebellion

== 16:1
T: Korah took men
R: 'Now Korah, the son of Izhar, the son of Kohath, the son of Levi, and Dathan and Abiram, the sons of Eliab, and On, the son of Peleth, sons of Reuben, took men.' Korah son of Izhar, a Levite from Kohath's clan, and certain Reubenites, Dathan and Abiram, sons of Eliab, and On son of Peleth, became insolent. Notice Korah. A Levite, a Kohathite. He carried the holy things. He was Moses' cousin. Privilege did not prevent rebellion. Notice Dathan and Abiram. Reubenites. Their camp was beside Kohath's on the south side. Proximity bred alliance. Notice took men. Korah gathered followers. Rebellion recruits. Notice that discontent seeks company. Proverbs 13 says, a companion of fools shall be destroyed. Notice Jude 11. Woe unto them!… and perished in the gainsaying of Core. The New Testament remembers Korah as a warning. Today, guard against discontent with your place. Do not let resentment gather companions who lead you into rebellion.
S: Korah the Levite joined with Dathan, Abiram and On of Reuben and took men to rebel. A privileged cousin of Moses gathered followers from the neighboring camp, as discontent seeks company. Guard against discontent with your place and do not let resentment lead you into rebellion.
P: Discontent recruits. Numbers 16:1: 'Now Korah… and Dathan and Abiram… took men.' Privilege didn't prevent rebellion. Guard your heart against resentment toward your place today.

== 16:2
T: Men of renown
R: 'And they rose up before Moses, with certain of the children of Israel, two hundred and fifty princes of the assembly, famous in the congregation, men of renown.' They rose up against Moses. With them were 250 Israelite men, well-known community leaders who had been appointed members of the council. Notice two hundred and fifty princes. Not common people but leaders. Famous and respected. Rebellion often comes from those with status. Notice men of renown. Reputation does not equal righteousness. These famous men were on the wrong side. Notice that numbers and fame can make rebellion seem legitimate. Two hundred fifty respected leaders surely could not be wrong? But they were. Notice Exodus 23. Thou shalt not follow a multitude to do evil. Notice First Corinthians 1. not many wise men after the flesh, not many mighty, not many noble, are called. Today, do not be swayed by the fame or numbers of those who oppose God's ways. Truth is not decided by popularity.
S: Two hundred fifty princes, famous and renowned, rose up with Korah against Moses. Fame and numbers made the rebellion look legitimate, but reputation did not equal righteousness. Do not be swayed by the fame or numbers of those who oppose God's ways.
P: Famous but wrong. Numbers 16:2: 'two hundred and fifty princes… famous in the congregation, men of renown.' Truth isn't decided by popularity. Don't follow a multitude to evil.

== 16:3
T: Ye take too much upon you
R: 'And they gathered themselves together against Moses and against Aaron, and said unto them, Ye take too much upon you, seeing all the congregation are holy… wherefore then lift ye up yourselves above the congregation of the LORD?' They came as a group to oppose Moses and Aaron and said to them, You have gone too far! The whole community is holy, every one of them, and the LORD is with them. Why then do you set yourselves above the LORD's assembly? Notice ye take too much upon you. The accusation was pride. Notice all the congregation are holy. A half-truth. Israel was called holy. But that did not erase God's appointed roles. Notice that half-truths are dangerous. Notice that Moses had not lifted himself up. God had chosen him. Moses was the meekest of men. Notice Second Corinthians 10. not he that commendeth himself is approved, but whom the Lord commendeth. Today, be wary of half-truths used to justify rebellion. Respect the roles God has assigned.
S: Korah's company accused Moses and Aaron of taking too much upon them, claiming all the congregation was holy. They used a half-truth to justify rebellion, though God Himself had appointed Moses and Aaron. Be wary of half-truths that justify rebellion and respect the roles God has assigned.
P: Half-truths. Numbers 16:3: 'Ye take too much upon you, seeing all the congregation are holy.' A half-truth justified rebellion. Respect the roles God assigns.

Moses prayed first, and his calm before God prepared him to speak wisely. Notice that falling on one's face is a posture of prayer and dependence. Moses knew he could not handle this alone. Notice Psalm 37. Fret not thyself because of evildoers… Commit thy way unto the LORD. Notice First Peter 2. Christ, when he was reviled, reviled not again… but committed himself to him that judgeth righteously. Today, when you are attacked, fall before God first. Let prayer, not anger, be your first response.
S: When Moses heard the accusation, he fell upon his face. His first response was humility and prayer rather than angry self-defense. When you are attacked, fall before God first and let prayer, not anger, be your first response.

== 16:24
T: Get you up

Notice get you up. The people were told to separate from the rebels. God gave them a way to escape judgment. Notice the tabernacle of Korah. Korah's tent. Perhaps he had set up a rival dwelling. == 16:25
T: Moses went to them
R: 'And Moses rose up and went unto Dathan and Abiram; and the elders of Israel followed him.' Moses got up and went to Dathan and Abiram, and the elders of Israel followed him. Notice Moses went unto Dathan and Abiram. They refused to come to him. So he went to them. One last chance. Notice that Moses kept reaching out. Even after their insults, he went personally. Grace pursues. Notice the elders followed him. The seventy from chapter 11 supported Moses. He was not alone. Notice Luke 15. The shepherd goes after the lost sheep. Love pursues even the rebellious. Notice Romans 10. All day long I have stretched forth my hands unto a disobedient and gainsaying people. God reaches out again and again. Today, keep reaching out to those who refuse you. Grace goes to people who will not come.
