# Numbers 17: Aaron's rod that budded

== 17:1
T: God settles the question
R: 'And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying.' After the rebellion and plague, God spoke to Moses about settling the question of the priesthood once and for all. Notice the timing. Korah's rebellion and the plague had shaken the nation. Questions remained. Who had God really chosen? God would answer clearly. Notice that God settles disputes. He does not leave His people in confusion forever. He provides clear signs. He answered Korah's challenge not with argument but with evidence. Notice that this time the sign would be life, not death. Korah's rebellion ended in judgment. Now God would show His choice through a miracle of life. First Corinthians 14 says, God is not the author of confusion, but of peace. Today, trust God to settle the questions that trouble you. He brings clarity in His time.
S: After the rebellion and plague, the LORD spoke to Moses about settling the question of the priesthood. This time God would show His choice through a sign of life rather than death. Trust God to settle the questions that trouble you, for He brings clarity and peace.
P: God brings clarity. Numbers 17:1: 'And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying.' After confusion, God settled the question with life. He is not the author of confusion.

== 17:2
T: Twelve rods
R: 'Speak unto the children of Israel, and take of every one of them a rod according to the house of their fathers, of all their princes according to the house of their fathers twelve rods: write thou every man's name upon his rod.' Speak to the Israelites and get twelve staffs from them, one from the leader of each ancestral tribe. Write the name of each man on his staff. Notice twelve rods. Every tribe represented. Fair and public. No tribe excluded from the test. Notice write thou every man's name. Each rod was personal. Each leader's identity was attached. Notice that a rod was a staff of authority. A shepherd's tool. A symbol of leadership. Each tribe's authority would be tested. Notice that God's tests are fair. Every tribe had an equal chance. The result would be undeniable. Psalm 75 says, God is the judge: he putteth down one, and setteth up another. Today, trust that God's choices are fair. He sees every name and chooses wisely.
S: God told Moses to take twelve rods, one from each tribe's prince, and write each man's name upon his rod. The fair, public test gave every tribe an equal chance before God. Trust that God's choices are fair, for He sees every name and chooses wisely.
P: Every name written. Numbers 17:2: 'write thou every man's name upon his rod.' A fair test for every tribe. God is the judge; He chooses wisely. Trust Him.

== 17:3
T: Aaron's name
R: 'And thou shalt write Aaron's name upon the rod of Levi: for one rod shall be for the head of the house of their fathers.' On the staff of Levi write Aaron's name, for there must be one staff for the head of each ancestral tribe. Notice Aaron's name upon the rod of Levi. Aaron represented Levi. His name was on the line. Notice one rod for the head. Each tribe had one leader. One rod, one name. Notice that the question was specifically about Aaron. Korah had challenged him. Now God would answer directly. Notice that God vindicates those He chooses. Aaron did not defend himself with words. God would defend him with a sign. Psalm 37 says, he shall bring forth thy righteousness as the light. Isaiah 54 says, every tongue that shall rise against thee in judgment thou shalt condemn. Today, if you are challenged, let God vindicate you. You do not need to win every argument.
S: Aaron's name was written on the rod of Levi, one rod for the head of each tribe. The test directly answered Korah's challenge, letting God vindicate Aaron instead of words. If you are challenged, let God vindicate you rather than trying to win every argument.
P: Let God vindicate. Numbers 17:3: 'thou shalt write Aaron's name upon the rod of Levi.' Aaron didn't argue; God answered. He brings forth your righteousness as light.

== 17:4
T: Where I will meet with you
R: 'And thou shalt lay them up in the tabernacle of the congregation before the testimony, where I will meet with you.' Place them in the tabernacle in front of the ark of the covenant law, where I meet with you. Notice before the testimony. In front of the ark, in God's presence. The rods would spend the night before God. Notice where I will meet with you. God's meeting place. The decision would be made there. Not by human vote. Notice that God's presence is where questions are answered. Moses did not decide. He placed the rods before God. Notice Psalm 73. Until I went into the sanctuary of God; then understood I. Clarity comes in God's presence. Today, bring your questions into God's presence. Lay them before Him and wait for His answer.
S: The rods were laid up in the tabernacle before the testimony, where God met with Moses. The decision would come from God's presence, not a human vote. Bring your questions into God's presence, lay them before Him and wait for His answer.
P: Laid before God. Numbers 17:4: 'lay them up… before the testimony, where I will meet with you.' Clarity comes in His presence. Bring your questions to Him today.

== 17:5
T: Shall blossom
R: 'And it shall come to pass, that the man's rod, whom I shall choose, shall blossom: and I will make to cease from me the murmurings of the children of Israel, whereby they murmur against you.' The staff belonging to the man I choose will sprout, and I will rid myself of this constant grumbling against you by the Israelites. Notice shall blossom. A dead stick would come to life. Only God could do that. Notice whom I shall choose. God's choice, revealed by life. Notice make to cease… the murmurings. God's goal was peace. He wanted to end the constant complaining. Notice that God's chosen leaders are marked by life. Not by power or fame, but by the life God gives. John 15 says, Ye have not chosen me, but I have chosen you… that ye should go and bring forth fruit. Today, look for the fruit of God's life in leaders. And let your own life blossom with the fruit He gives.
S: God said the rod of the man He chose would blossom, ending the murmurings against Moses and Aaron. A dead stick coming to life would reveal God's choice, marked by life rather than power. Look for the fruit of God's life in leaders and let your own life blossom with His fruit.

== 17:6
T: Among their rods
R: 'And Moses spake unto the children of Israel, and every one of their princes gave him a rod apiece… even twelve rods: and the rod of Aaron was among their rods.' So Moses spoke to the Israelites, and their leaders gave him twelve staffs, one for the leader of each ancestral tribe, and Aaron's staff was among them. Notice every one of their princes gave him a rod. The leaders cooperated. They accepted the test. Notice the rod of Aaron was among their rods. Aaron's rod was not set apart beforehand. It was mixed in with the others. No advantage. Notice that God's choice would be clear even among equals. All rods were dead wood. Only God could make one different. Notice First Corinthians 1. God hath chosen the foolish things of the world to confound the wise. God's choice often surprises. Today, trust that God can distinguish His chosen even among many. You do not need to stand out by your own effort.
S: Every prince gave Moses a rod, twelve in all, and Aaron's rod was among them. Aaron's rod had no advantage, for all were dead wood until God acted. Trust that God can distinguish His chosen among many without your own striving.
P: Among the rest. Numbers 17:6: 'the rod of Aaron was among their rods.' No advantage, all dead wood. Only God makes the difference. Trust Him.

Notice that faith places things before God and waits. Moses did not tamper with the rods. He did not help God. He waited. Notice Psalm 37. Rest in the LORD, and wait patiently for him. Psalm 62 says, My soul, wait thou only upon God; for my expectation is from him. Today, lay your concerns before the LORD and leave them there. Do not try to manipulate the outcome. == 17:8
T: Budded, bloomed, yielded almonds
R: 'And it came to pass, that on the morrow Moses went into the tabernacle of witness; and, behold, the rod of Aaron for the house of Levi was budded, and brought forth buds, and bloomed blossoms, and yielded almonds.' The next day Moses entered the tent and saw that Aaron's staff, which represented the tribe of Levi, had not only sprouted but had budded, blossomed and produced almonds. Notice budded… bloomed… yielded almonds. All stages at once. Buds, flowers, and ripe fruit overnight. Abundant life from dead wood. Notice almonds. The almond tree blooms first in spring. Jeremiah 1 uses it as a sign of God watching over His word. Notice that life from death is God's signature. A dead rod brought to life pictures resurrection. Notice Romans 1. Christ was declared to be the Son of God with power… by the resurrection from the dead. God's chosen High Priest was proven by resurrection. Today, rejoice in God's power to bring life from death. What is dead in your life, He can make bloom.
S: The next day Aaron's rod had budded, blossomed and yielded almonds, all at once from dead wood. Life from death proved God's choice, picturing Christ declared the Son of God by resurrection. Rejoice in God's power to bring life from death and trust Him with what seems dead in your life.
P: Life from dead wood. Numbers 17:8: 'the rod of Aaron… was budded… bloomed blossoms, and yielded almonds.' God brings life from death. He can make your dead places bloom.

== 17:9
T: They looked
R: 'And Moses brought out all the rods from before the LORD unto all the children of Israel: and they looked, and took every man his rod.' Then Moses brought out all the staffs from the LORD's presence to all the Israelites. They looked at them, and each of the leaders took his own staff. Notice they looked. Everyone saw. The evidence was public and undeniable. Notice took every man his rod. Eleven dead sticks. One living branch. The contrast was clear. Notice that God's answers are clear when He gives them. No one could argue with a budding rod. Notice that each leader took back his own dead rod. A humbling moment. They saw their own lifelessness beside Aaron's life. Notice John 15. I am the vine, ye are the branches… without me ye can do nothing. Life comes only from God. Today, recognize that life comes from God alone. Without Him, we are dead sticks. With Him, we bloom.
S: Moses brought out all the rods, the people looked, and each prince took his own. Eleven dead sticks beside one living branch made God's answer undeniable. Recognize that life comes from God alone, for without Him we are dead sticks and with Him we bloom.
P: Dead sticks and life. Numbers 17:9: 'they looked, and took every man his rod.' Eleven dead, one alive. Without Him we can do nothing. Abide in the Vine.

== 17:10
T: A token against the rebels
R: 'And the LORD said unto Moses, Bring Aaron's rod again before the testimony, to be kept for a token against the rebels; and thou shalt quite take away their murmurings from me, that they die not.' The LORD said to Moses, Put back Aaron's staff in front of the ark of the covenant law, to be kept as a sign to the rebellious. This will put an end to their grumbling against me, so that they will not die. Notice to be kept for a token. Aaron's rod became a lasting sign. Hebrews 9 says it was kept in the ark with the manna and the tables of the covenant. Notice that they die not. It aimed to stop rebellion before more died. Notice that God's warnings are acts of mercy. He shows signs so we can turn before judgment. Second Peter 3 says, The Lord… is longsuffering to us-ward, not willing that any should perish. Today, heed God's signs. His warnings come from love, so that you may live.
S: God told Moses to put Aaron's rod back before the testimony as a token against the rebels, so their murmurings would end and they would not die. The lasting sign was a merciful warning to prevent further judgment. Heed God's signs, for His warnings come from love so that you may live.
P: A merciful token. Numbers 17:10: 'to be kept for a token against the rebels… that they die not.' God's warnings are mercy. He is not willing that any should perish.

== 17:11

R: 'And Moses did so: as the LORD commanded him, so did he.' Moses did just as the LORD commanded him. Notice the simplicity. God commanded; Moses obeyed. No delay, no debate. Notice as the LORD commanded him, so did he. A phrase that defines Moses. His life was marked by obedience. Notice that after all the conflict, Moses simply obeyed. He did not gloat over his brother's vindication. He did what God said. Notice that obedience is quiet strength. Moses had been insulted, challenged and threatened, yet he answered with steady faithfulness. Victory did not make him proud; it made him obedient. Notice Hebrews 3. Moses verily was faithful in all his house, as a servant. John 14 records Jesus saying, If ye love me, keep my commandments. Today, let simple obedience mark your life. Whatever God commands, do it.
S: Moses did as the LORD commanded him. After all the conflict, he did not gloat but simply obeyed, as his whole life was marked by faithfulness. Let simple obedience mark your life and do whatever God commands.

== 17:12
T: We perish
R: 'And the children of Israel spake unto Moses, saying, Behold, we die, we perish, we all perish.' The Israelites said to Moses, We will die! We are lost, we are all lost! Notice we die, we perish, we all perish. Terror. After Korah, the plague, and the rod, the people finally grasped God's holiness. They swung from rebellion to despair. Notice the extremes. Earlier they challenged God's leaders. Now they felt hopeless. Neither was faith. Notice that awareness of God's holiness should lead to humility, not despair. God had just provided Aaron as a mediator. They did not need to perish. Notice Isaiah 6. Isaiah cried, Woe is me! for I am undone. Then God cleansed him. Awareness of sin led to grace. Today, when you see God's holiness, do not despair. He has provided a mediator. Come through Christ, and you will not perish.
S: The children of Israel cried to Moses that they would die and perish, all of them. They swung from rebellion to despair, though God had just provided a mediator. When you see God's holiness, do not despair, but come through Christ and you will not perish.
P: Not despair, but grace. Numbers 17:12: 'we die, we perish, we all perish.' They swung from rebellion to despair. God provided a mediator. In Christ, you won't perish.

== 17:13
T: Shall we be consumed?
R: 'Whosoever cometh any thing near unto the tabernacle of the LORD shall die: shall we be consumed with dying?' Anyone who even comes near the tabernacle of the LORD will die. Are we all going to die? Notice whosoever cometh… near… shall die. They feared approaching God at all. His holiness terrified them. Notice shall we be consumed? A real question. How could sinful people live near a holy God? Chapter 18 would answer: through the priests and Levites God appointed. Notice that this question finds its full answer in Christ. Hebrews 10 says we have boldness to enter into the holiest by the blood of Jesus. Through Him, sinners can draw near without being consumed. Notice John 3. God so loved the world… that whosoever believeth in him should not perish. Today, rejoice that you can draw near to God through Christ. You will not be consumed. You will be welcomed.
S: The people feared that anyone who came near the tabernacle would die and asked whether they would be consumed. Their question about living near a holy God was answered by appointed mediators and fully in Christ. Rejoice that through Christ you can draw near to God without being consumed.
P: Not consumed. Numbers 17:13: 'shall we be consumed with dying?' How can sinners draw near? Through Christ, with boldness. Whosoever believeth shall not perish.
