# Numbers 19: The red heifer and the water of separation

== 19:1
T: An answer to death
R: 'And the LORD spake unto Moses and unto Aaron, saying.' The LORD spoke to Moses and Aaron. Notice the setting. Thousands had just died in the plague after Korah's rebellion. Dead bodies, graves, and grief filled the camp. Death touched every family. Notice that God spoke into that grief. He gave a way to be cleansed from the defilement of death. Notice unto Moses and unto Aaron. The leader and the newly vindicated priest received it together. Cleansing would come through the priesthood God had just confirmed. Notice that God does not ignore the reality of death. He addresses it directly and provides a remedy. Hebrews 2 says Christ came to deliver them who through fear of death were all their lifetime subject to bondage. Today, bring your grief and your fear of death to God. He has provided cleansing and hope even in the valley of the shadow.
S: The LORD spoke to Moses and Aaron after the plague had filled the camp with death and grief. He provided a way to be cleansed from death's defilement through the priesthood He had just confirmed. Bring your grief and fear of death to God, for He has provided cleansing and hope.
P: God speaks into grief. Numbers 19:1: 'And the LORD spake unto Moses and unto Aaron, saying.' After the plague, God gave a remedy for death's defilement. Christ delivers us from the fear of death.

== 19:2
T: Without spot
R: 'Speak unto the children of Israel, that they bring thee a red heifer without spot, wherein is no blemish, and upon which never came yoke.' Israel was to bring a perfect red heifer that had never worked under a yoke. Notice a red heifer. A female, rare in sacrifice. Red, perhaps recalling the color of blood. Notice without spot… no blemish. Perfect. Nothing less would do for cleansing. Notice upon which never came yoke. Never used for common labor. Wholly set apart for this purpose. Notice how this points to Christ. First Peter 1 says we were redeemed with the precious blood of Christ, as of a lamb without blemish and without spot. Hebrews 9 says, if… the ashes of an heifer sprinkling the unclean, sanctifieth… How much more shall the blood of Christ… purge your conscience. Today, rejoice that Christ is the spotless sacrifice. His blood cleanses what nothing else can.
S: Israel was to bring a red heifer without spot or blemish that had never borne a yoke. The perfect, set apart heifer pointed to Christ, the Lamb without blemish and without spot. Rejoice that Christ is the spotless sacrifice whose blood cleanses what nothing else can.
P: Without spot. Numbers 19:2: 'a red heifer without spot, wherein is no blemish, and upon which never came yoke.' The ashes of an heifer pointed ahead. How much more shall the blood of Christ purge your conscience.

Christ died outside Jerusalem as one rejected. Notice that He went to the place of uncleanness to make us clean. Today, go to Christ outside the camp. Bear His reproach gladly, for He bore yours.

== 19:6
T: Cedar, hyssop and scarlet
R: 'And the priest shall take cedar wood, and hyssop, and scarlet, and cast it into the midst of the burning of the heifer.' The priest threw cedar wood, hyssop and scarlet wool into the fire. Notice the same three items used in Leviticus 14 for cleansing a leper. Cleansing from death and cleansing from leprosy shared these signs. Notice cedar wood. Durable, fragrant, resistant to decay. Notice hyssop. A humble plant used for sprinkling. Psalm 51 prays, Purge me with hyssop, and I shall be clean. Notice scarlet. The color of blood and of sin. Isaiah 1 says, though your sins be as scarlet, they shall be as white as snow. Notice that all three went into the fire with the heifer. Everything pointed to one cleansing sacrifice. Today, pray as David did. Ask God to purge you with hyssop. He makes scarlet sins white as snow.
S: The priest cast cedar wood, hyssop and scarlet into the burning heifer, the same signs used to cleanse a leper. Cedar, hyssop and scarlet together pictured lasting cleansing from the deepest sin. Ask God to purge you, for He makes scarlet sins white as snow.

== 19:7
T: The priest made unclean
R: 'Then the priest shall wash his clothes, and he shall bathe his flesh in water, and afterward he shall come into the camp, and the priest shall be unclean until the even.' The priest washed his clothes and bathed, came back into the camp and stayed unclean until evening. Notice the paradox. The rite that would cleanse others made the priest unclean. Handling the remedy for defilement defiled him. Notice wash… bathe… afterward… come into the camp. He returned only after washing. Cleansing was required even for the one who served. Notice that this pictures Christ, who took our uncleanness on Himself. Second Corinthians 5 says, he hath made him to be sin for us, who knew no sin; that we might be made the righteousness of God in him. Notice that serving others in their mess costs something. Today, thank Christ, who became unclean that you might be clean.

P: Unclean for others. Numbers 19:7: 'the priest shall be unclean until the even.' The one who prepared cleansing became unclean. Christ was made sin for us, that we might be made righteous.

== 19:11
T: Seven days unclean
R: 'He that toucheth the dead body of any man shall be unclean seven days.' Whoever touched a human corpse was unclean for seven days. Notice the dead body of any man. Death was the greatest defilement in the law. Touching a corpse made a person unclean longer than most other causes. Notice seven days. A full week of separation. Death is no small thing. Notice that this law taught Israel that death is unnatural. God made life. Death came through sin, and contact with it required cleansing. Notice that it also reminded them of their own mortality. Every funeral was a lesson. Psalm 90 prays, So teach us to number our days, that we may apply our hearts unto wisdom. Notice that Jesus touched the dead and was not defiled. He raised them. Luke 7 shows Him touching the bier and saying, Young man, I say unto thee, Arise. Today, remember that Christ conquers death. Number your days, and trust the One who gives life.
