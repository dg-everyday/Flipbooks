# Numbers 21: Hormah, the serpent of brass and the first victories

== 21:1
T: Taken prisoners
R: 'And when king Arad the Canaanite, which dwelt in the south, heard tell that Israel came by the way of the spies; then he fought against Israel, and took some of them prisoners.' When the Canaanite king of Arad heard that Israel was coming along the road to Atharim, he attacked them and captured some of them. Notice the way of the spies. The route the twelve spies had walked forty years before. Old memories along an old road. Notice took some of them prisoners. A painful setback. The new generation's first battle began with loss. Notice that opposition often comes just as we move forward. Israel had only just left Kadesh, and an enemy struck. Notice that losses do not mean God has abandoned us. The story would not end here. Psalm 34 says, Many are the afflictions of the righteous: but the LORD delivereth him out of them all. Today, if you have suffered a setback, do not lose heart. The first blow is not the final word.
S: The Canaanite king of Arad heard that Israel was coming by the way of the spies, fought against them and took some prisoners. The new generation's first battle began with loss, just as they moved forward. If you have suffered a setback, do not lose heart, for the first blow is not the final word.
P: Not the final word. Numbers 21:1: 'he fought against Israel, and took some of them prisoners.' A painful first blow. Many are the afflictions of the righteous, but the LORD delivereth him out of them all.

== 21:2
T: Israel vowed a vow
R: 'And Israel vowed a vow unto the LORD, and said, If thou wilt indeed deliver this people into my hand, then I will utterly destroy their cities.' Israel made a vow to the LORD: If you will deliver these people into our hands, we will totally destroy their cities. Notice Israel vowed a vow unto the LORD. Their first response was to turn to God. Notice the contrast with their parents. In chapter 14, the earlier generation attacked without God and lost at this same Hormah. Now Israel sought Him first. Notice utterly destroy. They pledged that nothing would be taken as personal plunder. Everything would be devoted to God. This was a unique act in Israel's history, not a pattern for believers today. Notice that the new generation was learning dependence. Psalm 20 says, Some trust in chariots, and some in horses: but we will remember the name of the LORD our God. Today, turn to God first when you face opposition. Seek His help before your own strength.
S: Israel vowed to the LORD that if He delivered the Canaanites into their hand, they would utterly destroy their cities and take no plunder. Unlike their parents, who fought without God at the same place, this generation sought Him first. Turn to God first when you face opposition and seek His help before your own strength.
P: Seek Him first. Numbers 21:2: 'Israel vowed a vow unto the LORD.' The new generation turned to God before battle. Some trust in chariots, but we will remember the name of the LORD.

== 21:3
T: Hormah
R: 'And the LORD hearkened to the voice of Israel, and delivered up the Canaanites; and they utterly destroyed them and their cities: and he called the name of the place Hormah.' The LORD listened to Israel's plea and gave the Canaanites over to them. They completely destroyed them and their towns, so the place was named Hormah. Notice the LORD hearkened. God heard their prayer. He answered the new generation. Notice Hormah. The name means devotion to destruction. It was the very place where their parents were chased in defeat in chapter 14. Notice that God reversed the story at the same spot. Defeat became victory where the earlier generation failed. Notice that this judgment fell on Canaanite cities whose sin God had long endured. Genesis 15 said the iniquity of the Amorites was not yet full. Now it was. Today, take heart that God can turn places of past failure into places of victory. Seek Him, and He hears.
S: The LORD heard Israel's voice and delivered the Canaanites, and the place was called Hormah. Victory came at the very place where the previous generation had been defeated. Take heart that God can turn places of past failure into places of victory when you seek Him.
P: Failure reversed. Numbers 21:3: 'the LORD hearkened to the voice of Israel… and he called the name of the place Hormah.' Victory where their parents fell. God can redeem your places of past failure.

== 21:4

== 21:5
