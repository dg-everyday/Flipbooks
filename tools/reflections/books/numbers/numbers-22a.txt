# Numbers 22:1-20: Balak sends for Balaam

== 22:1
T: The plains of Moab
R: 'And the children of Israel set forward, and pitched in the plains of Moab on this side Jordan by Jericho.' Israel traveled to the plains of Moab and camped along the Jordan across from Jericho. Notice the plains of Moab. This would be Israel's last camp before entering the land. The rest of Numbers and all of Deuteronomy take place here. Notice by Jericho. Across the river stood the first city they would conquer. The promise was in sight. Notice that the journey from Egypt was nearly complete. Forty years of wandering had brought them to the edge. Notice that the greatest spiritual battles often come at the threshold. Here Balak would hire a prophet to curse them, and Moab would tempt them to sin. Ephesians 6 says, Put on the whole armour of God, that ye may be able to stand. Today, if you stand at the edge of a promise, be watchful. Stand firm in the Lord until you cross.
S: Israel set forward and camped in the plains of Moab beside the Jordan across from Jericho. This was their last camp before the land, where great spiritual battles still awaited them. If you stand at the edge of a promise, be watchful and stand firm in the Lord until you cross.
P: At the threshold. Numbers 22:1: 'pitched in the plains of Moab on this side Jordan by Jericho.' The promise in sight, battles still ahead. Put on the whole armour of God and stand firm today.

== 22:2
T: Balak saw
R: 'And Balak the son of Zippor saw all that Israel had done to the Amorites.' Balak son of Zippor saw all that Israel had done to the Amorites. Notice Balak saw. He watched Israel defeat Sihon and Og. The news traveled fast. Notice all that Israel had done. Two kings had fallen. Balak drew his conclusions. Notice that Israel had no plan to attack Moab. God had forbidden it. Balak feared what was never coming. Notice that fear often reacts to imagined threats. Balak acted on assumptions, not facts. Notice that God's work among His people is visible to others. What God did for Israel was seen by the nations. Joshua 2 shows Rahab saying, we have heard how the LORD dried up the water of the Red sea. Today, let God's work in your life be seen. And do not let fear of imagined threats drive your choices.
S: Balak the son of Zippor saw all that Israel had done to the Amorites. He feared an attack that God had already forbidden, acting on assumptions rather than facts. Let God's work in your life be seen and do not let fear of imagined threats drive your choices.
P: Fear of what never came. Numbers 22:2: 'Balak the son of Zippor saw all that Israel had done to the Amorites.' Israel had no plan to attack Moab. Don't let imagined threats drive you.

== 22:3
T: Sore afraid
R: 'And Moab was sore afraid of the people, because they were many: and Moab was distressed because of the children of Israel.' Moab was terrified because there were so many people. Indeed, Moab was filled with dread because of the Israelites. Notice sore afraid… because they were many. The promise to Abraham had come true. His descendants were as many as the stars. Notice distressed. The word suggests loathing and dread. Moab felt sick with fear. Notice the irony. Forty years earlier, Israel was afraid of Canaan. Now the nations were afraid of Israel. Exodus 15 had said, the inhabitants of Canaan shall melt away… Fear and dread shall fall upon them. Notice that God fulfills His word in surprising ways. Today, remember that God can turn your fears around. Those who once seemed too strong may come to fear the God who is with you.
S: Moab was sore afraid of Israel because they were so many and was distressed because of them. The nation that once feared others was now feared, fulfilling God's word to Abraham and Moses. Remember that God can turn your fears around, for He is with you.
P: Fear turned around. Numbers 22:3: 'Moab was sore afraid of the people, because they were many.' Once Israel feared; now the nations feared Israel. God fulfills His word in surprising ways.

== 22:4
T: As the ox licketh up the grass
R: 'And Moab said unto the elders of Midian, Now shall this company lick up all that are round about us, as the ox licketh up the grass of the field…' Moab said to the elders of Midian, This horde is going to lick up everything around us, as an ox licks up the grass of the field. Notice the elders of Midian. Moab sought allies. Fear drives people to form alliances. Notice as the ox licketh up the grass. A vivid picture. Moab imagined Israel consuming everything. Notice Balak… was king. The leader responsible for Moab's response. He chose fear over inquiry. Notice that Moab and Midian were both related to Abraham. Moab through Lot, Midian through Keturah. Relatives joined against relatives. Notice that fear often exaggerates. Israel was not grazing on Moab. Second Timothy 1 says, God hath not given us the spirit of fear; but of power, and of love, and of a sound mind. Today, do not let fear exaggerate your troubles. Ask God for a sound mind.
S: Moab told the elders of Midian that Israel would lick up everything around them as an ox licks up grass. Fear exaggerated the threat and drove Abraham's relatives to unite against Israel. Do not let fear exaggerate your troubles, but ask God for a sound mind.
P: Fear exaggerates. Numbers 22:4: 'as the ox licketh up the grass of the field.' Moab imagined the worst. God hath not given us the spirit of fear, but of a sound mind.

== 22:5
T: Balaam the son of Beor
R: 'He sent messengers therefore unto Balaam the son of Beor to Pethor… saying, Behold, there is a people come out from Egypt: behold, they cover the face of the earth, and they abide over against me.' Balak sent messengers to summon Balaam son of Beor at Pethor, near the Euphrates, saying, A people has come out of Egypt; they cover the face of the land and have settled next to me. Notice Balaam. A famous diviner from far away. Pethor was near the Euphrates, hundreds of miles off. Notice that Balak sought spiritual help, not military help. He knew this was more than an ordinary war. Notice they cover the face of the earth. Again, exaggeration born of fear. Notice that Balaam is a warning throughout Scripture. Second Peter 2 says he loved the wages of unrighteousness. Today, recognize that spiritual battles are real. And beware the lure of gain that ruined Balaam.
S: Balak sent messengers to summon Balaam the son of Beor from distant Pethor, saying a people from Egypt covered the face of the earth. He sought a spiritual weapon, and Balaam became a lasting warning about loving the wages of unrighteousness. Recognize that spiritual battles are real and beware the lure of gain that ruined Balaam.
P: A spiritual battle. Numbers 22:5: 'He sent messengers therefore unto Balaam the son of Beor.' Balak sought a curse, not an army. Balaam loved the wages of unrighteousness. Beware that lure.

== 22:6
T: Curse me this people
R: 'Come now therefore, I pray thee, curse me this people; for they are too mighty for me… for I wot that he whom thou blessest is blessed, and he whom thou cursest is cursed.' Now come and put a curse on these people, because they are too powerful for me. For I know that whoever you bless is blessed, and whoever you curse is cursed. Notice curse me this people. Balak wanted spiritual power against Israel. Notice too mighty for me. He admitted his weakness. Notice he whom thou blessest is blessed. Balak believed Balaam controlled blessing and cursing. He was wrong. Only God does. Notice Genesis 12. God told Abraham, I will bless them that bless thee, and curse him that curseth thee. Balak was attempting what God had already ruled out. Notice that no human can curse whom God has blessed. Romans 8 says, Who shall lay any thing to the charge of God's elect? Today, rest in God's blessing. No curse can override what He has spoken over you.
S: Balak asked Balaam to curse Israel, believing whoever Balaam blessed was blessed and whoever he cursed was cursed. But only God controls blessing and cursing, and He had promised to bless Abraham's descendants. Rest in God's blessing, for no curse can override what He has spoken over you.
P: No curse can override. Numbers 22:6: 'curse me this people; for they are too mighty for me.' Balak trusted a man's power. Only God blesses and curses. Who shall lay any thing to the charge of God's elect?

S: The elders of Moab and Midian came to Balaam with the rewards of divination in their hands. Money stood at the center of Balaam's story from the start, a warning against greed in spiritual things. Guard your heart against the love of money and never let gain shape your service to God.
P: Payment in hand. Numbers 22:7: 'the rewards of divination in their hand.' Balak tried to buy a curse. Some ran greedily after the error of Balaam for reward. Guard your heart today.

== 22:8

Spend the night here, Balaam said to them, and I will report back to you with the answer the LORD gives me. So the Moabite officials stayed with him. Notice lodge here this night. Balaam did not refuse outright. He kept the door open. Notice as the LORD shall speak unto me. Balaam used God's name. He knew of the LORD. Notice that he should have known the answer already. Cursing God's people was plainly wrong. Yet he invited the princes to stay and asked again. Notice that lingering with temptation is dangerous. Balaam entertained the offer instead of rejecting it. James 1 says, every man is tempted, when he is drawn away of his own lust, and enticed. Today, do not keep temptation overnight. When the answer is clear, say no at once.
S: Balaam told the princes to lodge for the night while he waited for the LORD's word. Instead of rejecting an obviously wrong request, he kept the door open and entertained the offer. Do not keep temptation overnight, but when the answer is clear, say no at once.
P: Don't lodge temptation. Numbers 22:8: 'Lodge here this night, and I will bring you word again.' Balaam kept the door open. When the answer is clear, say no at once.
