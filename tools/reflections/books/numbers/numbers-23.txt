# Numbers 23: Balaam's first and second oracles

== 23:1
T: Seven altars
R: 'And Balaam said unto Balak, Build me here seven altars, and prepare me here seven oxen and seven rams.' Balaam told Balak to build seven altars and prepare seven bulls and seven rams. Notice seven altars. An impressive display. Seven suggested completeness. Balaam wanted a powerful setting. Notice that Balaam used religious ritual to try to influence God. More altars, more animals, more pressure. Notice that God cannot be manipulated by ritual. He does not trade blessings for sacrifices. First Samuel 15 says, to obey is better than sacrifice. Notice that the altars stood on the high places of Baal. True worship mixed with idolatry. Notice Psalm 51. thou desirest not sacrifice… The sacrifices of God are a broken spirit. God wants a humble heart, not an impressive show. Today, do not try to move God with religious effort. Come to Him with a broken and honest heart.
S: Balaam told Balak to build seven altars and prepare seven oxen and seven rams. The impressive ritual was an attempt to influence God, who cannot be moved by sacrifices without obedience. Do not try to move God with religious effort, but come to Him with a broken and honest heart.
P: Ritual can't buy God. Numbers 23:1: 'Build me here seven altars, and prepare me here seven oxen and seven rams.' To obey is better than sacrifice. Bring Him a broken and contrite heart.

== 23:2
T: On every altar
R: 'And Balak did as Balaam had spoken; and Balak and Balaam offered on every altar a bullock and a ram.' Balak did as Balaam said, and the two of them offered a bull and a ram on each altar. Notice Balak did as Balaam had spoken. The king obeyed the seer. Both wanted the same outcome. Notice Balak and Balaam offered. They sacrificed together. A partnership against God's people. Notice on every altar. Fourteen animals. A costly display. Notice that sacrifices offered to gain a curse could not please God. Proverbs 15 says, The sacrifice of the wicked is an abomination to the LORD. Notice that the motive behind worship matters. The same offering can honor God or offend Him, depending on the heart. Isaiah 1 says, To what purpose is the multitude of your sacrifices unto me? Today, examine why you worship. Offer God your gifts with a pure motive.
S: Balak did as Balaam said, and together they offered a bullock and a ram on every altar. Their costly sacrifices aimed to gain a curse, and the motive made them worthless before God. Examine why you worship and offer God your gifts with a pure motive.
P: Motive matters. Numbers 23:2: 'Balak and Balaam offered on every altar a bullock and a ram.' Costly worship with a wicked aim. The sacrifice of the wicked is an abomination. Worship with a pure heart.

Notice that God does meet people, but on His terms. Jeremiah 29 says, ye shall seek me, and find me, when ye shall search for me with all your heart. == 23:4
T: I have prepared seven altars
R: 'And God met Balaam: and he said unto him, I have prepared seven altars, and I have offered upon every altar a bullock and a ram.' God met with him, and Balaam said, I have prepared seven altars, and on each altar I have offered a bull and a ram. Notice God met Balaam. God came, not because of the altars, but for His own purpose. Notice I have prepared… I have offered. Balaam listed his efforts. He reminded God of his sacrifices, as if God owed him. Notice Luke 18. The Pharisee prayed, I fast twice in the week, I give tithes. The tax collector prayed, God be merciful to me a sinner. Today, do not come to God listing your works. Come asking for mercy.
S: God met Balaam, and Balaam told Him of the seven altars and offerings he had prepared. He listed his efforts as if God owed him, but God simply gave His word. Do not come to God listing your works, but come asking for mercy.
P: God is not in debt. Numbers 23:4: 'I have prepared seven altars, and I have offered upon every altar.' Balaam listed his works. God be merciful to me a sinner is the better prayer.

== 23:8
T: How shall I curse?
R: 'How shall I curse, whom God hath not cursed? or how shall I defy, whom the LORD hath not defied?' How can I curse those whom God has not cursed? How can I denounce those whom the LORD has not denounced? Notice how shall I curse. A rhetorical question. The answer is, I cannot. Notice whom God hath not cursed. The power of cursing belongs to God alone. If He has not cursed, no one can. Notice that Balaam's reputation meant nothing. His words had no power against God's will. Notice the security of God's people. No enemy's word can override God's. Romans 8 says, If God be for us, who can be against us? Isaiah 54 says, No weapon that is formed against thee shall prosper. Today, rest secure. If God has not condemned you, no one else can. In Christ, there is no condemnation.
S: Balaam declared that he could not curse those whom God had not cursed or defy those the LORD had not defied. The power of blessing and cursing belongs to God alone. Rest secure, for if God has not condemned you in Christ, no one else can.
P: Who can curse? Numbers 23:8: 'How shall I curse, whom God hath not cursed?' No one can override God. If God be for us, who can be against us? Rest secure today.
