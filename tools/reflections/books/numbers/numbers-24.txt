# Numbers 24: Balaam's third and final oracles

== 24:1
T: It pleased the LORD to bless
R: 'And when Balaam saw that it pleased the LORD to bless Israel, he went not, as at other times, to seek for enchantments, but he set his face toward the wilderness.' When Balaam saw that it pleased the LORD to bless Israel, he did not resort to divination as at other times, but turned his face toward the wilderness. Notice it pleased the LORD to bless Israel. God's delight was to bless. Not reluctantly, but gladly. Notice he went not… to seek for enchantments. Balaam's earlier trips were sorcery. Now he gave that up. Notice he set his face toward the wilderness. He looked directly at Israel's camp. No more searching for omens. Notice that God's pleasure is the deepest reason for blessing. Luke 12 says, it is your Father's good pleasure to give you the kingdom. Today, rejoice that God delights to bless His people. His blessing comes from His pleasure, not your performance.
S: When Balaam saw that it pleased the LORD to bless Israel, he stopped seeking enchantments and set his face toward the wilderness. God's delight was the deepest reason for His blessing. Rejoice that God delights to bless His people, for His blessing comes from His pleasure, not your performance.
P: God delights to bless. Numbers 24:1: 'when Balaam saw that it pleased the LORD to bless Israel.' It is your Father's good pleasure to give you the kingdom. Rest in His delight.

== 24:2
T: The spirit of God came upon him
R: 'And Balaam lifted up his eyes, and he saw Israel abiding in his tents according to their tribes; and the spirit of God came upon him.' When Balaam looked out and saw Israel encamped tribe by tribe, the Spirit of God came on him. Notice he saw Israel abiding in his tents according to their tribes. The ordered camp of chapter 2. Each tribe in its place around the tabernacle. Notice the beauty of God's order. What Balaam saw was a people arranged around God's presence. Notice the spirit of God came upon him. This time God's Spirit took hold of Balaam. The message would be even stronger. Notice that the Spirit came upon even an unfaithful man for God's purpose. This did not mean Balaam was godly. First Samuel 19 shows the Spirit coming on Saul as well. Gifts are not the same as faithfulness. Today, value a faithful heart above spiritual experiences. And rejoice that God orders His people around His presence.
S: Balaam lifted his eyes and saw Israel camped by tribes, and the spirit of God came upon him. God's Spirit used even an unfaithful man, showing that spiritual gifts are not the same as faithfulness. Value a faithful heart above spiritual experiences and rejoice that God orders His people around His presence.
P: Gifts aren't faithfulness. Numbers 24:2: 'he saw Israel abiding in his tents… and the spirit of God came upon him.' The Spirit used Balaam, yet his heart was unfaithful. Seek a faithful heart.

== 24:3
T: The man whose eyes are open
R: 'And he took up his parable, and said, Balaam the son of Beor hath said, and the man whose eyes are open hath said.' And he spoke his message: The prophecy of Balaam son of Beor, the prophecy of one whose eye sees clearly. Notice Balaam the son of Beor hath said. A formal opening. Balaam introduced himself as a seer. Notice the man whose eyes are open. A strong irony. Days earlier his donkey saw what he could not. Now God had opened his eyes. Notice that clear sight came only when God gave it. Balaam's famous vision was nothing without God. Notice that open eyes are a gift. We see spiritual truth only as God reveals it. Matthew 13 says, blessed are your eyes, for they see: and your ears, for they hear. Today, thank God for any spiritual sight you have. It is His gift, not your achievement.
S: Balaam began his third oracle calling himself the man whose eyes are open. Days earlier his donkey saw what he could not, so his clear sight was plainly God's gift. Thank God for any spiritual sight you have, for it is His gift, not your achievement.
P: Eyes opened by God. Numbers 24:3: 'the man whose eyes are open hath said.' The donkey once saw more than he did. Blessed are your eyes, for they see. Sight is God's gift.

== 24:4
T: The vision of the Almighty
R: 'He hath said, which heard the words of God, which saw the vision of the Almighty, falling into a trance, but having his eyes open.' The prophecy of one who hears the words of God, who sees a vision from the Almighty, who falls prostrate, and whose eyes are opened. Notice heard the words of God. Balaam received real revelation. Notice the vision of the Almighty. Shaddai. The all-powerful God. Notice falling into a trance. Literally falling down. Overwhelmed by God's presence. Notice but having his eyes open. Prostrate yet seeing. Humbled, then enlightened. Notice the tragedy. Balaam heard and saw so much, yet his heart remained greedy. Revelation did not transform him. Notice James 1. be ye doers of the word, and not hearers only. Today, do not be content with hearing and seeing. Let God's word change your heart and your life.
S: Balaam described himself as one who heard God's words and saw the vision of the Almighty, falling down with open eyes. He received real revelation, yet his greedy heart remained unchanged. Do not be content with hearing and seeing, but let God's word change your heart and your life.
P: Hearing isn't enough. Numbers 24:4: 'which heard the words of God, which saw the vision of the Almighty.' Yet Balaam's heart stayed greedy. Be doers of the word, not hearers only.

T: How goodly are thy tents
R: 'How goodly are thy tents, O Jacob, and thy tabernacles, O Israel!' How beautiful are your tents, Jacob, your dwelling places, Israel! Notice how goodly. Beautiful. Balaam marveled at what he saw. Notice thy tents… thy tabernacles. Simple wilderness dwellings. Yet beautiful because God dwelt among them. Notice that God sees beauty in His people. Even in the wilderness. Even after forty years of failure. Notice that these words are still used in Jewish worship today, recited when entering the synagogue. Notice Song of Solomon 4. Thou art all fair, my love; there is no spot in thee. Christ sees His church this way. Ephesians 5 says He will present it a glorious church, not having spot, or wrinkle. Today, know that God sees beauty in you. In Christ, your dwelling is goodly in His sight.
S: Balaam exclaimed how goodly were the tents of Jacob and the tabernacles of Israel. Simple wilderness dwellings were beautiful because God dwelt among His people, despite their failures. Know that God sees beauty in you, for in Christ your dwelling is goodly in His sight.

T: As gardens by the river's side

Like valleys they spread out, like gardens beside a river, like aloes planted by the LORD, like cedars beside the waters. Notice gardens by the river's side. Fruitful, green, flourishing. == 24:7
T: Water out of his buckets
R: 'He shall pour the water out of his buckets, and his seed shall be in many waters, and his king shall be higher than Agag, and his kingdom shall be exalted.' Water will flow from their buckets; their seed will have abundant water. Their king will be greater than Agag; their kingdom will be exalted. Notice he shall pour the water out of his buckets. Overflowing abundance. More than enough to share. Notice his seed shall be in many waters. Fruitful descendants. Growth and life. Notice his king shall be higher than Agag. Agag was a royal title of Amalek's kings. Israel would one day have a king. First Samuel 15 shows Saul facing an Agag. Notice his kingdom shall be exalted. A prophecy of David's kingdom and, ultimately, Christ's. Notice John 7. out of his belly shall flow rivers of living water. Today, let God's abundance overflow from you. Share the living water He gives.
S: Balaam prophesied that Israel would pour water from its buckets, its seed would flourish, and its king would be higher than Agag. The overflowing abundance and exalted kingdom pointed to David and ultimately to Christ. Let God's abundance overflow from you and share the living water He gives.

R: 'God brought him forth out of Egypt; he hath as it were the strength of an unicorn: he shall eat up the nations his enemies, and shall break their bones, and pierce them through with his arrows.' God brought them out of Egypt; they have the strength of a wild ox. They devour hostile nations and break their bones in pieces; with their arrows they pierce them. Notice God brought him forth out of Egypt. Repeated from chapter 23. Redemption is the foundation. Notice the strength of an unicorn. A wild ox. Untamable power from God. Notice he shall eat up the nations his enemies. Strong imagery of victory over those who opposed God's people. Notice that the source of strength remained God. Israel was strong only because God brought them out. Psalm 18 says, It is God that girdeth me with strength. Today, remember where your strength comes from. The God who redeemed you is the God who makes you strong.
S: Balaam repeated that God brought Israel out of Egypt and gave them the strength of a wild ox to overcome their enemies. Their power came entirely from the God who redeemed them. Remember where your strength comes from, for the God who redeemed you makes you strong.

== 24:9
T: Blessed is he that blesseth thee
R: 'He couched, he lay down as a lion, and as a great lion: who shall stir him up? Blessed is he that blesseth thee, and cursed is he that curseth thee.' Like a lion they crouch and lie down, like a lioness, who dares to rouse them? May those who bless you be blessed and those who curse you be cursed! Notice he lay down as a lion. Resting in strength. Secure. Genesis 49 used the same words of Judah. Notice who shall stir him up? No one dares disturb him. Notice blessed is he that blesseth thee, and cursed is he that curseth thee. The very promise of Genesis 12, now spoken by the man hired to curse. Notice the irony. Balak, who wanted to curse Israel, heard that cursers are cursed. He brought judgment on himself. Notice Galatians 3. the blessing of Abraham comes on the Gentiles through Jesus Christ. Today, rest like a lion in God's protection. Bless God's people, and you share their blessing.
S: Balaam pictured Israel resting like a great lion and repeated God's promise that whoever blesses Israel is blessed and whoever curses it is cursed. The man hired to curse ended by declaring the blessing of Abraham. Rest securely in God's protection and bless His people, sharing in their blessing.
P: The promise to Abraham. Numbers 24:9: 'Blessed is he that blesseth thee, and cursed is he that curseth thee.' The hired curser declared the blessing. Rest like a lion in God's care.

Then Balak's anger burned against Balaam. He struck his hands together and said, I summoned you to curse my enemies, but you have blessed them these three times. Notice Balak's anger was kindled. His patience was gone. Notice he smote his hands together. A gesture of scorn and fury. Notice these three times. Three attempts, three blessings. Each blessing greater than the last. Notice the parallel to the donkey. Balaam struck his donkey three times when God blocked him. Now Balak was furious three times because God blocked him. Notice that fighting God only brings frustration. Isaiah 45 says, Woe unto him that striveth with his Maker! Today, stop striving against God. Surrender is the way to peace.
S: Balak's anger burned, and he struck his hands together because Balaam had blessed Israel three times instead of cursing them. Each attempt to oppose God ended in a greater blessing and deeper frustration. Stop striving against God, for surrender is the way to peace.

== 24:24
T: Ships from Chittim
R: 'And ships shall come from the coast of Chittim, and shall afflict Asshur, and shall afflict Eber, and he also shall perish for ever.' Ships will come from the shores of Cyprus; they will subdue Ashur and Eber, but they too will come to ruin. Notice ships… from Chittim. Chittim was Cyprus, and the name came to mean western seafaring powers. Notice shall afflict Asshur. Even mighty Assyria would be humbled. Notice he also shall perish. The conquerors would also fall. Every empire ends. Notice the pattern. Moab, Edom, Amalek, the Kenites, Assyria, the western powers. All pass. Only God's kingdom remains. Daniel 2 says, the God of heaven set up a kingdom, which shall never be destroyed. Today, do not place your hope in any earthly power. Every empire perishes. God's kingdom alone endures.
S: Balaam foresaw ships from Chittim afflicting Asshur and Eber, and then perishing themselves. Every empire, however mighty, rises and falls, while only God's kingdom remains. Do not place your hope in any earthly power, for God's kingdom alone endures.

== 24:25
T: Returned to his place
R: 'And Balaam rose up, and went and returned to his place: and Balak also went his way.' Then Balaam got up and returned home, and Balak went his own way. Notice Balaam… returned to his place. The curse had failed. The blessing stood. Notice Balak also went his way. Two men, defeated by God's word. Notice the quiet ending. No curse, no reward, no victory for Balak. Notice that the story is not over. Chapter 25 shows Israel falling into sin at Peor, and chapter 31 reveals it came through the counsel of Balaam. What cursing could not do, temptation did. Notice First Corinthians 10. Wherefore let him that thinketh he standeth take heed lest he fall. Today, rejoice that no curse can touch God's people. But stay watchful, for the subtle snare is often more dangerous than the open attack.
S: Balaam rose and returned to his place, and Balak went his way, both defeated by God's word. Yet the story was not over, for Balaam's later counsel would lead Israel into sin at Peor. Rejoice that no curse can touch God's people, but stay watchful against subtle snares.
