# Numbers 25: Sin at Baal-peor and the zeal of Phinehas

== 25:1
T: In Shittim
R: 'And Israel abode in Shittim, and the people began to commit whoredom with the daughters of Moab.' While Israel was staying in Shittim, the men began to indulge in sexual immorality with Moabite women. Notice Israel abode in Shittim. Resting. Settled. The battles were won and the curse had failed. Notice the people began. A slow beginning. Sin often starts in moments of ease. Notice the daughters of Moab. What Balak could not achieve by curse, Moab achieved by seduction. Revelation 2 says Balaam taught Balak to cast a stumblingblock before the children of Israel. Notice the danger of rest after victory. David's great fall came when he stayed home from battle, as Second Samuel 11 records. Notice First Corinthians 10. Neither let us commit fornication, as some of them committed. Today, guard your heart most carefully in seasons of ease. Victory is not the time to lower your guard.
S: While Israel abode in Shittim, the people began to commit whoredom with the daughters of Moab. What Balak could not achieve by cursing, Balaam's counsel achieved through seduction in a season of rest. Guard your heart most carefully in seasons of ease, for victory is not the time to lower your guard.
P: Danger in ease. Numbers 25:1: 'Israel abode in Shittim, and the people began to commit whoredom.' Where cursing failed, seduction worked. Guard your heart most in seasons of rest and victory.

Immorality led to idolatry. Notice the people did eat, and bowed down. Step by step. Invitation, meal, worship. Notice how sin escalates. Physical temptation opened the door to spiritual betrayal. Notice that relationships shape worship. Those they joined led them to other gods. Second Corinthians 6 says, Be ye not unequally yoked together with unbelievers. First Kings 11 says Solomon's wives turned away his heart after other gods. Today, consider who is shaping your worship. Choose close companions who draw you toward God, not away.
S: The Moabite women called the people to their gods' sacrifices, and Israel ate and bowed down to those gods. Immorality led step by step into idolatry through close relationships. Consider who is shaping your worship and choose close companions who draw you toward God.
P: From feast to idol. Numbers 25:2: 'the people did eat, and bowed down to their gods.' Invitation, meal, worship. Relationships shape worship. Choose companions who draw you to God.

Do not yoke your heart to anything that rivals Him.
S: Israel joined itself to Baal-peor, and the LORD's anger was kindled against them. God's jealousy is covenant love that will not share His people with false gods. Be joined to the Lord alone and do not yoke your heart to anything that rivals Him.
P: Joined to whom? Numbers 25:3: 'Israel joined himself unto Baal-peor: and the anger of the LORD was kindled.' God's covenant love is jealous. He that is joined unto the Lord is one spirit.

== 25:4
T: That the fierce anger may be turned away
R: 'And the LORD said unto Moses, Take all the heads of the people, and hang them up before the LORD against the sun, that the fierce anger of the LORD may be turned away from Israel.' The LORD said to Moses, Take all the leaders of these people, kill them and expose them in broad daylight before the LORD, so that the LORD's fierce anger may turn away from Israel. Notice the heads of the people. Leaders who led or allowed the sin. Accountability began at the top. Notice against the sun. Publicly. The judgment was open. Notice that the fierce anger… may be turned away. The goal was to stop the plague and save the nation. Notice that this was God's direct judgment in Israel's covenant setting, not a pattern for today. Yet it shows sin's seriousness. James 3 says teachers shall receive the greater condemnation. Today, take seriously the responsibility of leadership. And thank God that Christ bore the wrath we deserved.
S: God told Moses to take the heads of the people and hang them up publicly so His fierce anger would turn from Israel. This unique judgment in Israel's covenant setting showed that leaders bear great responsibility. Take seriously the responsibility of leadership and thank God that Christ bore the wrath we deserved.
P: Leaders accountable. Numbers 25:4: 'that the fierce anger of the LORD may be turned away from Israel.' Judgment began with leaders. Christ bore the wrath we deserved. Lead with care.

== 25:5
T: Every one his men
R: 'And Moses said unto the judges of Israel, Slay ye every one his men that were joined unto Baal-peor.' So Moses said to Israel's judges, Each of you must put to death those of your people who have yoked themselves to the Baal of Peor. Notice the judges of Israel. The tribal leaders appointed in Exodus 18. They had responsibility over their people. Notice every one his men. Each judge dealt with those under his care. Order, not chaos. Notice that judgment targeted those who joined Baal-peor, not the whole nation. God distinguished between the guilty and the innocent. Notice that this was a civil judgment within Israel's theocracy. The church does not wield the sword. Instead, First Corinthians 5 calls for discipline in love and restoration. Notice the seriousness of idolatry. It threatened Israel's entire covenant with God. Today, deal honestly with sin in your own life. Take it seriously before it spreads.
S: Moses told Israel's judges to slay those under them who had joined Baal-peor. The judgment was ordered and targeted the guilty within Israel's theocracy, while the church today practices loving discipline instead. Deal honestly with sin in your own life and take it seriously before it spreads.
P: Sin taken seriously. Numbers 25:5: 'Slay ye every one his men that were joined unto Baal-peor.' Idolatry threatened the whole covenant. Deal honestly with sin before it spreads.

== 25:7

When Phinehas son of Eleazar, the son of Aaron, the priest, saw this, he left the assembly, took a spear in his hand. Notice Phinehas. Aaron's grandson. A young priest. Notice saw it. He did not look away. He saw the defiance and understood its danger. Notice he rose up from among the congregation. While others wept, Phinehas acted. Notice that his action was unique. P: Zeal for God. Numbers 25:7: 'when Phinehas… saw it, he rose up from among the congregation.' He cared when God was dishonored. The zeal of Thine house hath eaten me up.
