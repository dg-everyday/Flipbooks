# Numbers 31:1-24: Judgment on Midian

== 31:1
T: One last task
R: 'And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying.' The LORD said to Moses. Notice that God spoke near the end of Moses' life. His death had already been announced. Yet God still had work for him. Notice that the remaining task concerned Midian. Chapter 25 had ended with God's command to vex the Midianites. Now the time had come. Notice that God does not forget unfinished business. What He decrees, He carries out in His time. Notice that Moses' final acts were acts of obedience. He did not retire early. Notice Second Timothy 4. I have fought a good fight, I have finished my course. Paul served to the end, as Moses did. Notice John 9. I must work the works of him that sent me, while it is day. Today, serve God faithfully in every season, including the last. As long as He gives you breath, He has work for you.

== 31:2
T: Afterward gathered
R: 'Avenge the children of Israel of the Midianites: afterward shalt thou be gathered unto thy people.' Take vengeance on the Midianites for the Israelites. After that, you will be gathered to your people. Notice avenge the children of Israel. Midian had deliberately led Israel into idolatry and immorality at Peor. Twenty-four thousand died. Notice that this was God's judgment, not personal revenge. Vengeance belongs to God. Deuteronomy 32 says, To me belongeth vengeance, and recompence. Notice afterward shalt thou be gathered. Moses knew this would be his last mission. His death would follow. Notice that Moses did not delay. Knowing his death came after, he still obeyed promptly. Notice that this command was unique to Israel's theocracy. Believers today are told in Romans 12, Avenge not yourselves… Vengeance is mine; I will repay, saith the Lord. Today, leave justice in God's hands. And obey Him promptly, even when obedience brings your own season to a close.
S: God told Moses to avenge Israel on the Midianites, who had led them into deadly sin at Peor, after which Moses would be gathered to his people. This was God's judgment within Israel's theocracy, while believers today leave vengeance to Him. Leave justice in God's hands and obey Him promptly, even when obedience closes your own season.
P: Vengeance is God's. Numbers 31:2: 'Avenge the children of Israel of the Midianites: afterward shalt thou be gathered.' God's judgment, not personal revenge. Avenge not yourselves. Leave justice to Him.

== 31:23
T: Through the fire
R: 'Every thing that may abide the fire, ye shall make it go through the fire, and it shall be clean: nevertheless it shall be purified with the water of separation…' Everything that can withstand fire must be put through the fire, and then it will be clean. But it must also be purified with the water of cleansing. And whatever cannot withstand fire must be put through that water. Notice that may abide the fire… go through the fire. Metals were refined by fire. Notice that abideth not the fire… through the water. Fabrics and wood were washed instead. Notice that God fits the cleansing to the material. Fire for some, water for others. Notice First Peter 1. the trial of your faith, being much more precious than of gold that perisheth, though it be tried with fire. God refines us through trials. Notice Ephesians 5. the washing of water by the word. Today, trust God's way of cleansing you. Whether by fire or by water, trial or word, He makes you clean.
S: Things that could endure fire were passed through the fire and then purified with water, while other things were washed in water. God fitted the cleansing to each material, as He refines believers by trials and washes them by His word. Trust God's way of cleansing you, whether by fire or by water.
P: Fire or water. Numbers 31:23: 'Every thing that may abide the fire, ye shall make it go through the fire.' God fits the cleansing to us. Faith tried with fire; washed by water of the word.

== 31:24
T: Afterward ye shall come into the camp
R: 'And ye shall wash your clothes on the seventh day, and ye shall be clean, and afterward ye shall come into the camp.' On the seventh day wash your clothes and you will be clean. Then you may come into the camp. Notice wash your clothes on the seventh day. The final step. Notice ye shall be clean. A promise. The process would end in cleanness. Notice afterward ye shall come into the camp. Restored to the community. Welcomed home. Notice that cleansing led to restoration. The soldiers were not left outside forever. Notice that God's goal is always to bring us back. Separation was temporary; belonging was the aim. Notice Psalm 51. Wash me, and I shall be whiter than snow. Luke 15 shows the father running to welcome his son home. Today, receive God's cleansing and come home. He washes you so that you can belong.
S: On the seventh day the soldiers washed their clothes, were declared clean and came back into the camp. Cleansing led to restoration, for God's goal was always to bring His people back into belonging. Receive God's cleansing and come home, for He washes you so that you can belong.
P: Clean and home. Numbers 31:24: 'ye shall be clean, and afterward ye shall come into the camp.' Cleansing leads to belonging. Wash me, and I shall be whiter than snow. Come home today.
