# Numbers 33:29-56: From the wilderness to the Jordan, and instructions for the land

== 33:29
T: Hashmonah
R: 'And they went from Mithcah, and pitched in Hashmonah.' They left Mithkah and camped at Hashmonah. Notice Hashmonah. Another wilderness stop. Some suggest the name relates to fruitfulness or abundance. Notice that the long years were not all barren. God sustained His people with manna, water, and clothes that did not wear out. Deuteronomy 8 says, Thy raiment waxed not old upon thee, neither did thy foot swell, these forty years. Notice that God's provision was steady even in the wandering. They lacked nothing they truly needed. Notice Deuteronomy 2. these forty years the LORD thy God hath been with thee; thou hast lacked nothing. Notice that faithful provision often goes unnoticed because it is constant. Daily bread can feel ordinary. Notice that every stop on this list was a place where God fed them. Today, notice God's steady provision. Thank Him for the clothes that have not worn out and the bread that has not failed.
S: Israel moved from Mithcah and camped at Hashmonah, another stop in the wandering years. Throughout those years God sustained His people so that they lacked nothing they truly needed. Notice God's steady provision and thank Him for the daily care that never fails.
P: Lacked nothing. Numbers 33:29: 'pitched in Hashmonah.' Forty years, and their raiment waxed not old. Thou hast lacked nothing. Thank God for steady, faithful provision today.

== 33:30
T: Moseroth
R: 'And they departed from Hashmonah, and encamped at Moseroth.' They left Hashmonah and camped at Moseroth. Notice Moseroth. The name may mean bonds or chastisements. Notice Deuteronomy 10. Israel journeyed… to Mosera: there Aaron died. Mosera seems to be the same region as Moseroth, near mount Hor. Notice that place names often carry spiritual echoes. Bonds and chastening fit the wandering years, when God disciplined a generation. Notice Hebrews 12. whom the Lord loveth he chasteneth, and scourgeth every son whom he receiveth. Discipline is a sign of sonship. Notice that God's chastening is never without purpose. It produces the peaceable fruit of righteousness. Notice that Israel was being trained, not abandoned. Today, if you are under God's discipline, do not despise it. He chastens because He loves you, and He is training you for the promise.
S: Israel moved from Hashmonah and camped at Moseroth, a name that may mean bonds or chastenings. The wandering years were a time of God's discipline, yet His chastening was a sign of love and training. If you are under God's discipline, do not despise it, for He chastens because He loves you.
P: Loving discipline. Numbers 33:30: 'encamped at Moseroth.' A name of chastening. Whom the Lord loveth He chasteneth. His discipline trains you for the promise. Do not despise it.

== 33:31
T: Bene-jaakan
R: 'And they departed from Moseroth, and pitched in Bene-jaakan.' They left Moseroth and camped at Bene Jaakan. Notice Bene-jaakan. The sons of Jaakan. A place named after a family, perhaps descendants of Esau. First Chronicles 1 lists Jaakan among the Horites of Seir. Notice that Israel camped among other peoples' places. Their journey passed through others' land and history. Notice that God's people live among neighbors. They are not isolated from the world. Notice Jeremiah 29. seek the peace of the city whither I have caused you to be carried away… and pray unto the LORD for it. Notice that Israel would be a witness by how they lived among others. Deuteronomy 4 says the nations would hear and say, Surely this great nation is a wise and understanding people. Today, live well among your neighbors. Let your life point them to God.
S: Israel moved from Moseroth and camped at Bene-jaakan, named after the sons of Jaakan, possibly Horites of Seir. God's people traveled among others' lands and histories, called to be a witness by how they lived. Live well among your neighbors and let your life point them to God.
P: Among neighbors. Numbers 33:31: 'pitched in Bene-jaakan.' A place named for another family. Seek the peace of the city where God places you. Let your life point others to Him.

== 33:32
T: Hor-hagidgad
R: 'And they removed from Bene-jaakan, and encamped at Hor-hagidgad.' They left Bene Jaakan and camped at Hor Haggidgad. Notice Hor-hagidgad. Hor may mean hole or cave. Perhaps the cave of Gidgad, or a hollow in the land. Deuteronomy 10 calls it Gudgodah. Notice that caves appear often in Scripture as places of refuge. David hid in the cave of Adullam. Elijah heard God's voice at the mouth of a cave. Notice that hollow places can become places of meeting with God. In our lowest, hidden seasons, He speaks. Notice Psalm 142, written when David was in the cave. Thou art my refuge and my portion in the land of the living. Notice that God does not abandon us in hidden places. He is our refuge there. Today, if you are in a hollow season, call on God as David did. He is your refuge and your portion, even in the cave.
S: Israel moved from Bene-jaakan and camped at Hor-hagidgad, a name that may refer to a cave or hollow. Caves in Scripture often became places of refuge and meeting with God, as with David and Elijah. If you are in a hollow season, call on God as David did, for He is your refuge even in the cave.
P: Refuge in the cave. Numbers 33:32: 'encamped at Hor-hagidgad.' A hollow place. David prayed in the cave, Thou art my refuge and my portion. God meets you in hidden seasons.

== 33:35
T: Ezion-gaber

Notice that a wilderness camp later became a place of prosperity. Israel's descendants would build ships where their ancestors pitched tents. Notice that God's plans unfold over generations. Except the LORD build the house, they labour in vain that build it. Today, trust God with the long story. And build only with His blessing.

== 33:36

Notice what happened at Kadesh this time. Miriam died. The people complained for water. Moses struck the rock. Notice that Kadesh means holy. A holy place that witnessed both failure and grace. Notice that God brings us back to places of past failure. Not to shame us, but to let us choose differently. Notice John 21. Jesus asked Peter three times, Lovest thou me? Restoring him after his three denials. Today, if God brings you back to a place of failure, see it as grace. He gives you another chance to trust Him.

P: Back to Kadesh. Numbers 33:36: 'pitched in the wilderness of Zin, which is Kadesh.' Full circle. Jesus restored Peter at a place of failure. God gives another chance to trust Him.
