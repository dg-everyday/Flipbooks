# Psalms 3: Thou, O LORD, art a shield for me

== 3:1
T: How are they increased that trouble me
R: 'LORD, how are they increased that trouble me' Notice LORD. Prayer first. Notice how are they increased. Growing trouble. Notice that trouble me. Personal pain. Notice that many rise up against him. Opposition. Notice that this psalm comes from a hard season in David's life. Notice that his own son had turned on him. Notice that betrayal multiplied his enemies. Notice that David began with God's name. Notice that he did not hide his fear. Notice that he counted his troubles honestly. Notice that honesty is the start of prayer. Notice that numbers do not decide outcomes. Notice that God does. Notice that one with God outweighs many. Notice that Paul asked the same question. Notice that God being for us changes the count. Romans 8 says, 'If God be for us, who can be against us.' Notice God for us. Today, name your troubles honestly before God. He outweighs them all.
S: David cried, LORD, how many are my foes, how many rise up against me. He began with God's name and counted his troubles honestly, for one with God outweighs any number of enemies. Name your troubles before Him, remembering, if God be for us, who can be against us.
P: Many foes. Psalms 3:1: 'LORD, how are they increased that trouble me.' God for us. If God be for us, who can be against us.

== 3:2

Despair spoken. Notice in God. Faith mocked. Notice that Selah follows. A pause. Notice that the cruellest attack was on his hope. Notice that people said God had abandoned him. Notice that such words wound deeply. Notice that they aim at faith itself. Notice that David heard them. Notice that he did not agree. Notice that Selah invites us to stop and think. Notice that other voices do not decide God's help. Notice that the psalmists faced this taunt often. Notice that they kept praying. Notice that God proved the taunt false. Psalm 42 says, 'while they say daily unto me, Where is thy God.' Notice the taunt. Today, refuse the voice that says God cannot help you. Pause and listen to Him instead.
S: Many were saying of David that God would not deliver him, and the psalm pauses with Selah. The cruellest attack aimed at his hope, but other people's words do not decide God's help. Refuse that voice, even when others ask, where is thy God.
P: No help, they said. Psalms 3:2: 'There is no help for him in God.' The taunt. Where is thy God. Trust Him with it today.

== 3:3

R: 'But thou, O LORD, art a shield for me; my glory, and the lifter up of mine head.' Notice but thou. The turn. Notice O LORD. Direct address. Notice art a shield for me. Protection. Notice my glory. Worth. Notice and the lifter up of mine head. Restored dignity. Notice that the psalm turns on the word but. Notice that David answers the taunt with truth. Notice that God surrounds like a shield. Notice that God is his glory when honour is lost. Notice that God lifts the bowed head. Notice that shame bends us down. Notice that God raises our faces. Notice that this is tender. Notice that God told Abram the same. Notice that He is a shield. Notice that He is a reward. Notice that protection and worth come from Him. Genesis 15 says, 'Fear not, Abram: I am thy shield, and thy exceeding great reward.' Notice God as shield. Today, let God lift your head. He is your shield and your glory.
S: David answered the taunts by saying, but You, LORD, are a shield around me, my glory, the One who lifts my head. When honour is lost and shame bends us low, God surrounds us and raises our faces. Let Him lift your head, for He said, fear not, Abram: I am thy shield, and thy exceeding great reward.
P: My shield. Psalms 3:3: 'But thou, O LORD, art a shield for me.' Lifted. I am thy shield. Walk in His ways today.

Notice and he heard me. Answered. Notice out of his holy hill. From His throne. Notice that Selah follows again. Pause. Notice that David prayed out loud. Notice that he was far from Jerusalem. Notice that God still heard from His hill. Notice that distance does not block prayer. Notice that God's throne is never out of reach. Notice that crying out is faith. Notice that Jonah prayed from the deep. Notice that he was further away still. Notice that God heard him too. Notice that no place is beyond God's ear. Jonah 2 says, 'I cried by reason of mine affliction unto the LORD, and he heard me.' Notice heard from the depths. Today, cry out to God wherever you are. Distance does not block His hearing.
S: David said he cried aloud to the LORD and He answered him from His holy hill. Though David was far from Jerusalem, God heard from His throne, for no place is beyond His ear. Cry out wherever you are, as Jonah said, I cried by reason of mine affliction unto the LORD, and He heard me.
P: Heard. Psalms 3:4: 'I cried unto the LORD with my voice, and he heard me.' From the depths. I cried unto the LORD, and he heard me.

== 3:5

R: 'I laid me down and slept; I awaked; for the LORD sustained me.' Notice I laid me down. Rest. Notice and slept. Peace. Notice I awaked. Morning came. Notice for the LORD sustained me. Held all night. Notice that David slept with enemies near. Notice that sleep is an act of trust. Notice that we are helpless asleep. Notice that God keeps watch. Notice that waking is His gift too. Notice that every morning is mercy. Notice that worry steals sleep. Notice that trust gives it back. Notice that God sustains through the night. Notice that the psalmist said He gives sleep. Notice that anxious toil is vain. Notice that rest is a gift to the beloved. If worry keeps you awake night after night, bring it to God and talk with someone you trust. Psalm 127 says, 'for so he giveth his beloved sleep.' Notice sleep as gift. Today, lie down tonight trusting God. He sustains you.
S: David said he lay down and slept, and woke again, because the LORD sustained him. Sleeping with enemies near was an act of trust, and every waking is God's mercy. Rest tonight in His care, for so He giveth His beloved sleep.
P: Slept. Psalms 3:5: 'I laid me down and slept; I awaked.' Gift. For so he giveth his beloved sleep. Let this shape your day.
