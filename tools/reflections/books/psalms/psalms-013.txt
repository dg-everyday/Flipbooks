# Psalms 13: How long wilt thou forget me, O LORD

== 13:1
T: How long wilt thou forget me
R: 'How long wilt thou forget me, O LORD' Notice how long. Four times in two verses. Notice wilt thou forget me. Feeling forgotten. Notice O LORD. Still praying. Notice that he asked how long God would hide His face. Hiddenness. Notice that David felt forgotten by God. Notice that he asked whether it would be forever. Notice that the waiting felt endless. Notice that he repeated how long. Notice that repetition shows deep pain. Notice that God allows this prayer in Scripture. Notice that feeling forgotten is not being forgotten. Notice that Zion once said the same. Notice that God answered tenderly. Notice that He said He could not forget. If you feel God has forgotten you and the waiting seems endless, please tell someone you trust and keep praying. Isaiah 49 says, 'But Zion said, The LORD hath forsaken me, and my Lord hath forgotten me.' Notice God's answer follows. Today, bring your how long to God. Feeling forgotten is not being forgotten.
S: David cried, how long, LORD, will You forget me, forever; how long will You hide Your face from me. Feeling forgotten is not the same as being forgotten, and God answered Zion's identical complaint with tender assurance. If the waiting feels endless, tell someone and keep praying, for when Zion said, the LORD hath forsaken me, God replied that He could not forget.
P: How long. Psalms 13:1: 'How long wilt thou forget me, O LORD.' Answered. Zion said, The LORD hath forsaken me. Trust Him with it today.

== 13:2
T: Having sorrow in my heart daily
R: 'How long shall I take counsel in my soul, having sorrow in my heart daily' Notice how long shall I take counsel. Inner wrestling. Notice in my soul. Alone. Notice having sorrow. Grief. Notice in my heart daily. Every day. Notice that he asked how long his enemy would triumph. Defeat. Notice that David wrestled with his thoughts. Notice that he carried sorrow every day. Notice that daily sorrow is exhausting. Notice that inner arguing does not solve grief. Notice that bringing thoughts to God frees us. Notice that Peter urged casting cares. Notice that we cast all of them. Notice that God cares for us. Notice that we need not carry them alone. If daily sorrow and anxious thoughts are overwhelming you, please share them with God and with someone who can help. 1 Peter 5 says, 'Casting all your care upon him; for he careth for you.' Notice casting cares. Today, stop wrestling alone with your thoughts. Cast them on God.
S: David asked how long he must wrestle with his thoughts and carry sorrow in his heart every day. Daily sorrow and endless inner arguing exhaust us, but bringing our thoughts to God frees us. If anxious thoughts overwhelm you, share them with someone, casting all your care upon Him; for He careth for you.
P: Daily sorrow. Psalms 13:2: 'having sorrow in my heart daily.' Cast it. Casting all your care upon him. Walk in His ways today.

Notice that dim eyes meant fading life. Notice that he feared death. Notice that God gives light to the eyes. Notice that He restores vitality. Notice that Paul prayed for enlightened eyes. Notice that he meant spiritual sight. Notice that hope comes with light. If you are in such darkness that you fear for your life, please reach out today to a trusted person or a crisis line. Ephesians 1 says, 'The eyes of your understanding being enlightened.' Notice enlightened eyes. Today, ask God to light your eyes. Hope comes with His light.
S: David prayed, look on me and answer, LORD my God; give light to my eyes, or I will sleep in death. He moved from complaint to request, asking God to restore the light that was fading from his life. If darkness makes you fear for your life, reach out today, and pray for the eyes of your understanding being enlightened.

== 13:4
T: Lest mine enemy say, I have prevailed
R: 'Lest mine enemy say, I have prevailed against him' Notice lest mine enemy say. Fear of gloating. Notice I have prevailed against him. Defeat claimed. Notice that his foes would rejoice if he fell. Mockery. Notice that David feared his enemies' triumph. Notice that he did not want them to gloat. Notice that his fall would dishonour God. Notice that he prayed for God's reputation too. Notice that the enemy wants to claim victory. Notice that God denies them that. Notice that falling is not final. Notice that Micah answered the gloating enemy. Notice that he said do not rejoice. Notice that he said when I fall I will rise. Notice that he said God is light in darkness. Notice that this is defiant hope. Micah 7 says, 'Rejoice not against me, O mine enemy: when I fall, I shall arise.' Notice rising again. Today, refuse to let your enemy claim the final word. When you fall, you will rise.
S: David prayed that his enemy would not say, I have overcome him, and his foes would not rejoice when he fell. He knew falling is not final and that God denies the enemy its triumph. Answer every gloating voice with defiant hope, rejoice not against me, O mine enemy: when I fall, I shall arise.
P: No gloating. Psalms 13:4: 'Lest mine enemy say, I have prevailed against him.' Rise. When I fall, I shall arise. Let this shape your day.

== 13:5

Notice my heart shall rejoice. Coming joy. Notice in thy salvation. Rescue. Notice that the psalm turns on one word. Notice that nothing outward had changed. Notice that David chose trust. Notice that he leaned on God's mercy. Notice that mercy is God's covenant love. Notice that it does not fail. Notice that joy was expected, not yet felt. Notice that faith anticipates rescue. Notice that the lament became trust. Notice that this pattern runs through the Psalms. Notice that Lamentations made the same turn. Notice that it remembered God's faithfulness. Notice that it said new every morning. Lamentations 3 says, 'They are new every morning: great is thy faithfulness.' Notice faithfulness renewed. Today, choose trust before you feel joy. God's mercy does not fail.
S: David turned, but I trust in Your unfailing mercy; my heart rejoices in Your salvation. Nothing outward had changed yet, but he chose trust in God's covenant love and anticipated joy. Choose trust before you feel it, for His mercies are new every morning: great is Thy faithfulness.
P: But I trust. Psalms 13:5: 'But I have trusted in thy mercy.' New mercies. Great is thy faithfulness. Take it to God in prayer.

== 13:6

R: 'I will sing unto the LORD, because he hath dealt bountifully with me.' Notice I will sing. Praise. Notice unto the LORD. Object. Notice because he hath dealt bountifully. Reason. Notice with me. Personal. Notice that the psalm ends in song. Notice that it began with how long. Notice that six verses moved from despair to praise. Notice that God's past goodness fuelled the song. Notice that David remembered bounty. Notice that memory changes perspective. Notice that the present ache was real. Notice that God's record was real too. Notice that both can be held. Notice that singing is an act of faith. Notice that another psalm told the soul to rest. Notice that the reason was God's bounty. Notice that rest follows remembering. Psalm 116 says, 'Return unto thy rest, O my soul; for the LORD hath dealt bountifully with thee.' Notice rest restored. Today, remember God's bounty and sing. Despair can turn to praise.
S: David ended, I will sing the LORD's praise, for He has been good to me. A psalm that began with how long ended in song, as memory of God's goodness reshaped David's view of the present. Remember His bounty, and say, return unto thy rest, O my soul; for the LORD hath dealt bountifully with thee.
P: Bountiful. Psalms 13:6: 'I will sing unto the LORD, because he hath dealt bountifully with me.' Rest. Return unto thy rest, O my soul.
