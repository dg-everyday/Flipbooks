# Psalms 15: Who shall abide in thy tabernacle

== 15:1
T: Who shall abide in thy tabernacle
R: 'LORD, who shall abide in thy tabernacle' Notice LORD. Asking God. Notice who shall abide. Lasting welcome. Notice in thy tabernacle. God's dwelling. Notice that he asked who may live on God's holy hill. Belonging. Notice that the psalm begins with a question. Notice that it asks who may draw near. Notice that the answer will describe character. Notice that nearness to God shapes how we live. Notice that worship and ethics belong together. Notice that God's guests reflect His holiness. Notice that this is not a list to earn entry. Notice that it describes those God welcomes. Notice that another psalm asks the same. Notice that it asks who may ascend. Notice that it answers clean hands and a pure heart. Notice that grace makes us such people. Notice that Christ opens the door. Psalm 24 says, 'Who shall ascend into the hill of the LORD.' Notice the same question. Today, ask what kind of person dwells near God. Let His nearness shape your life.
S: David asked, LORD, who may dwell in Your sacred tent; who may live on Your holy mountain. The answer describes character, for nearness to God shapes how His guests live, and grace through Christ makes us such people. Let His nearness shape you, as another psalm asks, who shall ascend into the hill of the LORD.
P: Who may dwell. Psalms 15:1: 'LORD, who shall abide in thy tabernacle.' Ascend. Who shall ascend into the hill of the LORD. Trust Him with it today.

== 15:2
T: Speaketh the truth in his heart
R: 'He that walketh uprightly, and worketh righteousness, and speaketh the truth in his heart.' Notice he that walketh uprightly. Integrity. Notice and worketh righteousness. Right deeds. Notice and speaketh the truth. Honesty. Notice in his heart. Inside first. Notice that the answer begins with integrity. Notice that walking describes all of life. Notice that righteousness is practical. Notice that truth begins inside. Notice that honesty with ourselves comes first. Notice that inner truth leads to outer truth. Notice that self-deception is the first lie. Notice that God wants truth in the inward parts. Notice that Paul urged truthful speech. Notice that he said put away lying. Notice that we are members of one another. Notice that lies damage the body. Notice that truth builds it. Ephesians 4 says, 'Wherefore putting away lying, speak every man truth with his neighbour.' Notice truthful speech. Today, be honest with yourself first. Truth in the heart leads to truth in the mouth.
S: The one who may dwell with God walks blamelessly, does what is right and speaks the truth from the heart. Integrity covers all of life, and truthful speech begins with being honest inside, free from self-deception. Start within, then put away lying, and speak every man truth with his neighbour.
P: Heart truth. Psalms 15:2: 'and speaketh the truth in his heart.' Honest. Speak every man truth with his neighbour. Walk in His ways today.

== 15:3
T: He that backbiteth not with his tongue
R: 'He that backbiteth not with his tongue, nor doeth evil to his neighbour, nor taketh up a reproach against his neighbour.' Notice he that backbiteth not. No slander. Notice with his tongue. Speech. Notice nor doeth evil to his neighbour. No harm. Notice nor taketh up a reproach. No gossip spread. Notice against his neighbour. Those near. Notice that the tongue is named first. Notice that slander is serious. Notice that gossip is listed with doing evil. Notice that spreading reproach harms. Notice that taking up means passing it on. Notice that we can refuse to repeat. Notice that neighbours deserve protection. Notice that words can wound like deeds. Notice that James forbade speaking evil. Notice that he called it judging the law. Notice that slander puts us above God. Notice that silence about others can be kindness. James 4 says, 'Speak not evil one of another, brethren.' Notice the command. Today, refuse to repeat a reproach. Protect your neighbour's name.
S: The one who dwells with God does not slander, does no wrong to a neighbour and casts no slur on others. Slander and gossip are listed alongside doing evil, for words can wound as deeply as deeds. Refuse to pass on a reproach, for speak not evil one of another, brethren.
P: No slander. Psalms 15:3: 'He that backbiteth not with his tongue.' Command. Speak not evil one of another. Let this shape your day.

== 15:5
T: He that doeth these things shall never be moved
R: 'He that doeth these things shall never be moved.' Notice that he does not lend money at interest to exploit. Fairness. Notice that he takes no bribe against the innocent. Justice. Notice he that doeth these things. Practice. Notice shall never be moved. Stability. Notice that money is a test of character. Notice that exploitation is forbidden. Notice that bribery is forbidden. Notice that the innocent must be protected. Notice that the psalm ends with a promise. Notice that integrity brings stability. Notice that such people are not shaken. Notice that character is a foundation. Notice that the law forbade exploiting the poor. Notice that loans were to help. Notice that they were not to crush. Notice that generosity reflects God. Exodus 22 says, 'thou shalt not be to him as an usurer.' Notice fair lending. Today, handle money with fairness and integrity. Such a life shall never be moved.
S: The one who dwells with God lends without exploiting the poor and takes no bribe against the innocent; whoever does these things will never be shaken. Money tests character, and fairness and integrity build a life that stands firm. Handle money justly, for God said of the poor, thou shalt not be to him as an usurer.
P: Never moved. Psalms 15:5: 'He that doeth these things shall never be moved.' Fair. Thou shalt not be to him as an usurer.
