R: 'My God, my God, why hast thou forsaken me' Notice my God, my God. Still clinging. Notice why. Anguished question. Notice hast thou forsaken me. Felt abandonment. Notice that he asked why God was so far from helping. Distance. Notice that David wrote this in deep anguish. Notice that he still called God my God. Notice that faith holds on while asking why. Notice that Jesus prayed these words on the cross. Notice that He bore our sin there. Notice that He knows forsakenness from the inside. Notice that no sufferer is beyond His understanding. Notice that the psalm does not end here. Notice that it ends in praise. Notice that the cross was followed by resurrection. If you feel forsaken by God, please tell someone you trust; Christ has been there and you are not alone. Matthew 27 says, 'that is to say, My God, my God, why hast thou forsaken me.' Notice Christ's cry. Today, bring your darkest why to God. Christ has prayed it before you.
S: David cried, my God, my God, why have You forsaken me, why are You so far from saving me. Jesus prayed these very words on the cross, entering the deepest darkness so that no sufferer is beyond His understanding. If you feel forsaken, tell someone you trust, and remember Christ's cry, my God, my God, why hast Thou forsaken Me.
