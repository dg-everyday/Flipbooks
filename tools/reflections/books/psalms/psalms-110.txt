# Psalms 110: A priest for ever after the order of Melchizedek

== 110:1
T: Sit thou at my right hand
R: 'The LORD said unto my Lord, Sit thou at my right hand, until I make thine enemies thy footstool.' Notice the LORD said. God spoke. Notice unto my Lord. David's Lord. Notice sit thou at my right hand. Place of honour. Notice until I make. God acts. Notice thine enemies thy footstool. Defeated. Notice that this is the most quoted psalm in the New Testament. Notice that David called someone Lord. Notice that Jesus asked how David's son could be David's Lord. Notice that the answer is Christ. Notice that He is both. Notice that He sits at God's right hand. Notice that He reigns now. Notice that Peter preached this. Notice that Jesus was raised. Notice that He was exalted. Notice that He was made Lord and Christ. Acts 2 says, 'that God hath made that same Jesus, whom ye have crucified, both Lord and Christ.' Notice Lord and Christ. Today, honour Jesus as Lord. He sits at God's right hand.
S: David wrote, the LORD says to my Lord, sit at My right hand until I make Your enemies a footstool for Your feet. This most quoted psalm in the New Testament points to Christ, who is both David's son and David's Lord. Honour Him, for God hath made that same Jesus, whom ye have crucified, both Lord and Christ.
P: Right hand. Psalms 110:1: 'Sit thou at my right hand.' Lord and Christ. God hath made that same Jesus both Lord and Christ.

== 110:2
T: Rule thou in the midst of thine enemies
R: 'The LORD shall send the rod of thy strength out of Zion: rule thou in the midst of thine enemies.' Notice the LORD shall send. God extends. Notice the rod of thy strength. Mighty sceptre. Notice out of Zion. From Jerusalem. Notice rule thou. Reign. Notice in the midst of thine enemies. Among foes. Notice that the King rules among opposition. Notice that His reign does not wait for peace. Notice that it is already active. Notice that it spreads from Zion. Notice that the gospel went out from Jerusalem. Notice that it spread to the world. Notice that His rule is spiritual. Notice that Isaiah foresaw this. Notice that the law would go forth. Notice that it would be from Zion. Notice that the word would come from Jerusalem. Isaiah 2 says, 'for out of Zion shall go forth the law, and the word of the LORD from Jerusalem.' Notice the outgoing word. Today, trust Christ's reign even amid opposition. His word goes forth.
S: David wrote, the LORD will extend Your mighty sceptre from Zion, saying, rule in the midst of Your enemies. Christ reigns even among opposition, and His sceptre is the gospel that went out from Jerusalem to the world. Trust His reign, for out of Zion shall go forth the law, and the word of the LORD from Jerusalem.
P: Rule. Psalms 110:2: 'rule thou in the midst of thine enemies.' Out of Zion. Out of Zion shall go forth the law. Trust Him with it today.

Notice that holiness is beautiful. Notice that the verse mentions the dew of youth. Notice that renewal is fresh. Notice that willing service is the fruit of grace. Notice that Paul urged this. Notice that we present our bodies. Notice that it is a living sacrifice. Notice that it is holy. Notice that it is reasonable. Romans 12 says, 'that ye present your bodies a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable unto God' Notice willing offering. Today, offer yourself willingly to Christ. His people serve freely.
S: David wrote, Your troops will be willing on Your day of battle, arrayed in holy splendour, with the dew of youth. Christ's people serve Him freely, not by force, and holiness is beautiful. Offer yourself willingly, that ye present your bodies a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable unto God.
P: Willing. Psalms 110:3: 'Thy people shall be willing in the day of thy power.' Living sacrifice. Present your bodies a living sacrifice. Walk in His ways today.

== 110:4
T: A priest for ever after the order of Melchizedek
R: 'The LORD hath sworn, and will not repent, Thou art a priest for ever after the order of Melchizedek.' Notice the LORD hath sworn. Oath. Notice and will not repent. Will not change. Notice thou art a priest. Mediator. Notice for ever. Eternal. Notice after the order of Melchizedek. A different line. Notice that the King is also a Priest. Notice that this was unusual in Israel. Notice that kings were from Judah. Notice that priests were from Levi. Notice that Melchizedek was both. Notice that Christ is both. Notice that His priesthood never ends. Notice that Hebrews explains this. Notice that He was made priest by oath. Notice that He is not replaced. Notice that He lives forever. Notice that He saves completely. Hebrews 7 says, 'Thou art a priest for ever after the order of Melchisedec' Notice the eternal priest. Today, come to God through Christ your priest. His priesthood never ends.
S: David wrote, the LORD has sworn and will not change His mind, You are a priest forever, in the order of Melchizedek. Christ is both King and Priest, and by God's oath His priesthood never ends. Come to God through Him, for He is a priest for ever after the order of Melchisedec.
P: Priest for ever. Psalms 110:4: 'Thou art a priest for ever after the order of Melchizedek.' Melchisedec. Thou art a priest for ever after the order of Melchisedec.

== 110:5
T: The Lord at thy right hand
R: 'The Lord at thy right hand' Notice the Lord. Sovereign. Notice at thy right hand. Beside. Notice that the Lord stands beside the King. Notice that He will crush kings. Notice that this pictures final judgment. Notice that it is ancient royal imagery. Notice that it is not a call for Christians to fight. Notice that judgment belongs to God. Notice that He will judge rulers. Notice that no power escapes Him. Notice that this is comfort to the oppressed. Notice that Revelation shows the end. Notice that the Lamb is King of kings. Notice that He is Lord of lords. Notice that He overcomes. Notice that His people are with Him. Revelation 17 says, 'for he is Lord of lords, and King of kings' Notice the final King. Today, trust that Christ will judge every power. Leave judgment to Him.
S: David wrote, the Lord is at Your right hand; He will crush kings on the day of His wrath. This ancient royal imagery pictures final judgment, which belongs to God and is not a call for Christians to fight. Trust Him, for He is Lord of lords, and King of kings.
P: Right hand. Psalms 110:5: 'The Lord at thy right hand.' King of kings. For he is Lord of lords, and King of kings.

Notice that it pictures complete victory. Notice that it is recorded, not a model. Notice that Christ's judgment is righteous. Notice that it comes at the end. Notice that it is His, not ours. Notice that He judges with fairness. Notice that He judges the nations. Notice that Isaiah described His judgment. Notice that He judges the poor rightly. Notice that He is fair to the meek. Notice that righteousness is His belt. Notice that His judgment is good news for the oppressed. Isaiah 11 says, 'But with righteousness shall he judge the poor' Notice righteous judgment. Today, trust Christ's fair judgment. It is good news for the poor.

Notice that this pictures victory after struggle. Notice that Christ was humbled first. Notice that then He was exalted. Notice that suffering came before glory. Notice that Paul described this. Notice that Christ humbled Himself. Notice that He obeyed to death. Notice that God highly exalted Him. Notice that every knee will bow. Philippians 2 says, 'Wherefore God also hath highly exalted him' Notice exalted Christ. Today, trust that humbling leads to lifting. God exalts in His time.
S: David wrote, He will drink from a brook along the way, and so He will lift His head high. The King is refreshed and presses on to victory, as Christ was humbled before He was exalted. Trust God's timing, for wherefore God also hath highly exalted Him.
P: Lift up. Psalms 110:7: 'therefore shall he lift up the head.' Exalted. Wherefore God also hath highly exalted him. Take it to God in prayer.
