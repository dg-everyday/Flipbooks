# February 2027: Love & Faithful Relationships

== Feb 01
R: Peter ends a long section of practical teaching with a simple summary of how God's people should treat one another: 'be ye all of one mind, having compassion one of another, love as brethren, be pitiful, be courteous.' To be pitiful, in the old sense of the word, means to be tenderhearted. To be courteous means to be humble and kind in the way we speak and act. None of this is complicated, yet all of it is costly. It asks us to slow down, to notice how others are feeling, and to treat people like family even when they are difficult. Love is not just a warm feeling. It is a daily practice of compassion, gentleness, and respect. This month, as we reflect on love and relationships, start here. Choose one person today and treat them with intentional tenderness. A patient word, a kind gesture, or simple courtesy can become a powerful act of love.
S: Peter calls believers to be united, compassionate, tenderhearted, and courteous toward one another. Love is a daily practice of kindness and respect, not just a feeling. Choose one person today to treat with intentional tenderness.
P: Love is not complicated, but it is costly. 1 Peter 3:8 calls us to have 'compassion one of another,' to be tenderhearted and courteous. Who could use a little extra kindness from you today? Small acts of love make a big difference.

== Feb 02
R: When Paul wrote his final letter to Timothy, he began with deep affection: 'To Timothy, my dearly beloved son: Grace, mercy, and peace, from God the Father and Christ Jesus our Lord.' Timothy was not his son by birth, but by faith. Paul had mentored him, traveled with him, and poured his life into him. Now, near the end of his own life, Paul wanted Timothy to receive the three gifts he valued most: grace for his failures, mercy for his weaknesses, and peace for his anxious heart. That is what love looks like in spiritual friendship. It does not just give advice; it blesses. It wants the very best of God for the other person. Who has been a Paul to you? And who might God be calling you to love like a Timothy? Today, speak a blessing over someone you care about. Tell them they are dearly loved, and pray that God would fill their life with grace, mercy, and peace.
S: Paul greeted Timothy as his dearly beloved son in the faith and blessed him with grace, mercy, and peace. Loving spiritual friendships want the very best of God for one another. Speak a blessing over someone you care about today.
P: Who has been a spiritual mentor to you? In 2 Timothy 1:2, Paul calls Timothy 'my dearly beloved son' and blesses him with grace, mercy, and peace. Today, send a blessing to someone who has shaped your faith, or someone you are helping to grow.

== Feb 03
R: Paul closes his letter to the Ephesians with a blessing: 'Peace be to the brethren, and love with faith, from God the Father and the Lord Jesus Christ.' Notice where these gifts come from. Peace, love, and faith are not things we manufacture by trying harder. They flow from God Himself. We often try to build loving relationships on our own strength, and we quickly run out of patience, energy, and grace. But Paul points us to the source. When our hearts are filled with God's peace, we become peacemakers. When we receive His love, we have love to give. When faith grows in us, we can trust Him with the people we cannot fix or control. Love joined with faith is a powerful combination. It keeps loving even when the results are slow, because it trusts that God is at work. Today, before you try to love others, receive His love first. Then let it flow through you to those around you.
S: Paul blesses the believers with peace and love joined with faith, all flowing from God. We cannot manufacture these gifts on our own, but we can receive them from Him. Let God fill you first, then let His love flow to others.
P: You cannot give what you have not received. Ephesians 6:23 reminds us that peace, love, and faith come 'from God the Father and the Lord Jesus Christ.' Start today by receiving His love, then share it freely with everyone you meet.

== Feb 04
R: Even the great apostle Paul needed help. He wrote, 'I beseech you, brethren … that ye strive together with me in your prayers to God for me.' He was facing danger and uncertainty, and he did not pretend to be strong enough on his own. He asked his friends to pray. And notice the word strive. Prayer, for Paul, was not a casual afterthought. It was a shared effort, like teammates pulling together on the same rope. He made his request 'for the love of the Spirit,' because love moves us to carry each other's burdens before God. One of the most loving things you can do for someone is pray for them, faithfully and specifically. And one of the most humble things you can do is ask others to pray for you. Today, reach out to a friend and ask how you can pray for them. Then, if you are carrying something heavy, be brave enough to ask for prayer too.
S: Paul asked his friends to strive together with him in prayer out of the love of the Spirit. Praying for one another is one of the deepest ways to show love. Ask someone how you can pray for them, and let others pray for you too.
P: Even Paul needed people to pray for him. In Romans 15:30, he asked his friends to 'strive together with me in your prayers to God for me.' Who can you pray for today? And who can you ask to pray for you? Drop a prayer request below if you would like support.

== Feb 05
R: Jesus once looked around a dinner table and noticed who had been invited: friends, relatives, wealthy neighbors. People who could return the favor. So He said, 'When thou makest a dinner or a supper, call not thy friends, nor thy brethren … lest they also bid thee again.' Jesus was not forbidding dinner with family. He was challenging a love that keeps score. It is easy to be generous with people who will repay us. It is far more Christlike to welcome those who never can: the lonely, the poor, the overlooked. In the next verses, Jesus promises that such kindness will be rewarded by God Himself. That changes the direction of our hearts. We stop looking to people for our reward and start looking to God. Love becomes a quiet offering to Him. Today, think of someone who rarely gets invited. Find a way to include them, and let your kindness be a prayer offered to the Father who sees.
S: Jesus challenged His host to invite those who could not repay rather than those who would return the favor. True love does not keep score but looks to God for its reward. Include someone today who is often overlooked.
P: Real love does not keep score. In Luke 14:12, Jesus challenged a host to think beyond friends and rich neighbors who could repay him. Who in your life rarely gets invited? Make room at your table, and let your kindness be an offering to God.

You do not need a platform or a title to tell your story. You simply need to share what God has done and how He has shown you compassion. == Feb 07
R: Peter describes the Christian life like a staircase, each step building on the one before. Faith leads to virtue, virtue to knowledge, and on it climbs until it reaches the top: 'And to godliness brotherly kindness; and to brotherly kindness charity.' Charity here means love, the selfless, giving love of God. It is the crown of spiritual growth. Notice what comes just before it: brotherly kindness, the warm affection of family. Love is learned in ordinary relationships before it reaches its fullest form. You practice it at the kitchen table, in the office, in the long conversations and the small irritations. Godliness that does not lead to kindness is incomplete. The goal of all our growing is to love. If you have been focused on knowing more or doing more, pause today and ask a simple question: am I becoming more loving? Take one step up that staircase. Show warm kindness to someone close to you, and let it grow into love.

In his day, as in ours, people often honored the wealthy and overlooked the poor. But God does not measure worth by bank accounts. Many who have little in this world are rich in faith, trusting God daily in ways the comfortable seldom need to. They are heirs of a kingdom that no market can shake. This verse is a gentle correction for anyone tempted to show favoritism. It is also a deep encouragement for anyone who feels poor, overlooked, or small. In God's eyes, you are not less. You are loved, chosen, and promised an inheritance. Today, look at people the way God does. Honor those the world overlooks. And if you are the one who feels overlooked, know that heaven sees you as rich.

Yet in the middle of his distress, he lifted his eyes to who God is: 'But thou, O Lord, art a God full of compassion, and gracious, longsuffering, and plenteous in mercy and truth.' It is defined by the cross, where Jesus gave Himself completely for people who did not deserve it. And John immediately draws a conclusion: 'and we ought to lay down our lives for the brethren.' Love sets aside its own agenda to meet the need of another. It listens when it would rather speak. It serves when it would rather rest. That kind of love is impossible without first receiving the love of Christ. Today, let His sacrifice fill your heart. R: Paul describes a wardrobe for the people of God: 'Put on therefore, as the elect of God, holy and beloved, bowels of mercies, kindness, humbleness of mind, meekness, longsuffering.' The phrase bowels of mercies sounds strange today, but it means a deep, heartfelt compassion that moves you from the inside. Notice the order. Before Paul tells us what to wear, he reminds us who we are: chosen, holy, and beloved. We do not put on kindness to earn God's love. We put it on because we already have it. Like clothing, these qualities are chosen each morning. Some days, kindness fits easily. Other days, we have to deliberately reach for patience and humility. But the more we wear them, the more natural they become. Today, before you step out the door, take a moment to dress your heart. Put on compassion, kindness, humility, gentleness, and patience. Then go out into the world as one who is dearly loved.

== Feb 15
R: Paul makes a remarkable claim: 'For all the law is fulfilled in one word, even in this; Thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself.' All of God's commands about how we treat each other, honesty, faithfulness, generosity, justice, are summed up in love. If we truly loved our neighbors as ourselves, we would not lie to them, steal from them, gossip about them, or ignore their needs. Love is not a loophole that lets us skip obedience. It is the heart of obedience. It asks us to treat others with the same care, patience, and attention we naturally give ourselves. That is a high standard. We usually make excuses for our own failures but judge others harshly. Love reverses that. Today, think of someone you find difficult. Ask yourself how you would want to be treated if you were in their place. Then, with God's help, give them that same grace. That is how the whole law is fulfilled.
S: Paul teaches that the whole law concerning others is fulfilled in loving your neighbor as yourself. Love is the heart of obedience, not a way around it. Give someone the same grace and care you would want for yourself today.
P: All of God's commands about how we treat others come down to one word: love. Galatians 5:14 says, 'Thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself.' Think of someone who is hard to love, and give them the grace you would want for yourself today.

R: Elizabeth had waited a lifetime for a child. In her old age, God answered her prayers, and she gave birth to a son, John. Then something lovely happened: 'And her neighbours and her cousins heard how the Lord had shewed great mercy upon her; and they rejoiced with her.' Her joy became their joy. The whole community celebrated God's mercy together. There is a special kind of love in rejoicing with others. It is easy to weep with those who weep, but sometimes harder to celebrate when someone receives a blessing we are still waiting for. Envy can creep in quietly. Yet God calls us to share each other's joys as fully as we share each other's sorrows. Today, think of someone who has recently received good news. Celebrate with them sincerely. Send a note, say a prayer of thanks, or simply tell them how happy you are for them. Shared joy multiplies, and it honors the mercy of God.
S: When God showed great mercy to Elizabeth, her neighbors and relatives rejoiced with her. Celebrating the blessings of others is a beautiful form of love. Rejoice sincerely with someone who has received good news.

== Feb 25
R: James calls it the royal law: 'If ye fulfil the royal law according to the scripture, Thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself, ye do well.' It is royal because it comes from the King, and because it is the highest law of His kingdom. James was writing to a church that had started showing favoritism, giving the best seats to the rich and pushing the poor aside. He reminded them that love does not play favorites. It treats every person with dignity, whether they arrive in fine clothes or worn ones. We may not assign seats in church, but we can still show favoritism in quieter ways: whom we greet warmly, whom we listen to, whom we overlook. The royal law calls us higher. Today, notice how you respond to different kinds of people. Ask God to help you show the same warmth and respect to everyone, because everyone is someone He loves. When you live this way, you do well.
S: James calls loving your neighbor as yourself the royal law of God's kingdom. This love refuses to show favoritism and treats every person with dignity. Show the same warmth and respect to everyone you meet today.
P: Everyone deserves a warm welcome. James 2:8 calls loving your neighbor 'the royal law.' It means no favorites, no one overlooked, and dignity for every person. Who can you welcome warmly today?

== Feb 26
R: Freedom is one of the great gifts of the gospel. Paul writes, 'For, brethren, ye have been called unto liberty.' Through Christ, we are free from the burden of earning God's favor, free from guilt and fear. But Paul immediately adds a caution: 'only use not liberty for an occasion to the flesh, but by love serve one another.' Freedom is not an excuse to live for ourselves. It is an opportunity to love more freely. That is a surprising twist. The free person in Christ chooses to serve, not because they must, but because they want to. Love turns freedom outward. Think about how you are using the freedom God has given you. Is it making you more self-centered, or more generous? Today, use your freedom to serve someone. Help with a task, offer your time, or meet a practical need. You will discover that serving in love is not a loss of freedom. It is freedom at its very best.
S: Paul reminds us that we are called to freedom, but that freedom should be used to serve one another in love. True liberty turns us outward toward others. Use your freedom today to serve someone with a willing heart.
P: Freedom is best used for love. Galatians 5:13 says we are 'called unto liberty' but should 'by love serve one another.' How will you use your freedom today? Find someone to serve with a glad heart.

== Feb 27
R: Peter calls believers to a love that is both sincere and strong: 'see that ye love one another with a pure heart fervently.' The love he describes is unfeigned, not pretend, not polite on the outside while cold on the inside. It is pure, with no hidden motives. And it is fervent, a word that means stretched out, intense, wholehearted. This kind of love does not come naturally. Peter says it flows from souls purified by obeying the truth through the Spirit. In other words, as God changes our hearts, our love for others grows deeper and more genuine. Many of us know how to be nice. But God invites us to something richer: love that is real, warm, and persistent. As February draws to a close, ask God to purify your heart and deepen your love. Think of someone you love only halfway. Pray for them by name, and look for a way to love them sincerely and fervently today.
S: Peter calls believers to love one another sincerely, with pure hearts and fervent intensity. This kind of love grows as God purifies our hearts by His truth and Spirit. Ask God to deepen your love for someone today.
P: God invites us beyond being nice to loving deeply. 1 Peter 1:22 says, 'love one another with a pure heart fervently.' Who could use some sincere, wholehearted love from you today? Pray for them by name and reach out.

== Feb 28
R: This verse is one of the most beautiful descriptions of God in all of Scripture: 'The LORD is gracious, and full of compassion; slow to anger, and of great mercy.' Each phrase is worth savoring. He is gracious, giving us what we do not deserve. He is full of compassion, His heart moved by our pain. He is slow to anger, not quick to condemn, but patient with our failures. And He is of great mercy, holding back the judgment we might expect and offering kindness instead. This is the God who loves you. Not a harsh judge waiting for you to fail, but a gracious Father who delights to show mercy. As this month of love comes to a close, let this picture of God settle into your heart. Then reflect it to others. Be gracious, compassionate, slow to anger, and full of mercy. The world desperately needs to see the love of God, and it often sees it first in His people.
S: The psalmist describes the LORD as gracious, compassionate, slow to anger, and great in mercy. This is the heart of the God who loves us. Let His character settle into your heart and shape how you treat others.
P: This is who God is. Psalm 145:8 says, 'The LORD is gracious, and full of compassion; slow to anger, and of great mercy.' As this month of love closes, rest in His kindness and reflect it to everyone around you.
