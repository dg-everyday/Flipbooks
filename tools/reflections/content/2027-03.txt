# March 2027: Faith & Trust

== Mar 01
R: Jesus asks a probing question about how we handle money: 'If therefore ye have not been faithful in the unrighteous mammon, who will commit to your trust the true riches?' Mammon means worldly wealth, the kind that comes and goes. Jesus is saying that our everyday faithfulness with ordinary things reveals whether we are ready for greater things. How we spend, save, give, and handle what is entrusted to us is not separate from our spiritual life. It is a training ground for it. Living by faith is not only about grand moments of trust. It shows up in paying bills honestly, giving generously, and refusing to let money become our master. The true riches Jesus speaks of are the treasures of His kingdom: deeper fellowship with God, spiritual responsibility, and eternal reward. Today, look at your finances as an act of faith. Ask God to make you faithful in small things, and trust Him to entrust you with what truly lasts.
S: Jesus teaches that faithfulness with worldly wealth shows whether we can be trusted with true riches. Our handling of money is part of our walk of faith. Be faithful in small things today and trust God with what truly lasts.
P: Faith shows up in everyday choices, even in our wallets. In Luke 16:11, Jesus asks, 'who will commit to your trust the true riches?' Be faithful with what you have today, and trust God with what truly lasts.

== Mar 02
R: Paul remembered the Thessalonians with gratitude, and he named three things that made their faith beautiful: 'your work of faith, and labour of love, and patience of hope in our Lord Jesus Christ.' Notice how each virtue produces something. Faith works. Love labors. Hope endures with patience. These were not people who simply believed the right things. Their beliefs had hands and feet. Their faith moved them to action, their love moved them to sacrifice, and their hope kept them steady through persecution. Faith that never acts is like a seed that never grows. Love that never labors is only sentiment. Hope that gives up at the first trouble is only wishful thinking. But when all three are rooted in Jesus, they produce a life that others remember with thanks. Today, ask yourself how your faith is working, how your love is laboring, and how your hope is enduring. Then take one practical step that puts your faith into loving action.
S: Paul gave thanks for the Thessalonians because their faith worked, their love labored, and their hope endured. True faith always shows itself in action. Take one practical step today that puts your faith and love to work.
P: Faith works. Love labors. Hope endures. 1 Thessalonians 1:3 celebrates the 'work of faith, and labour of love, and patience of hope' in believers. How will your faith show up in action today?

== Mar 03
R: Paul has spent the opening chapters of Romans showing that every one of us falls short. Then comes the good news: 'Even the righteousness of God which is by faith of Jesus Christ unto all and upon all them that believe: for there is no difference.' No difference. That phrase cuts both ways. We are all equally in need of grace, and we are all equally welcome to receive it. There is no special class of people who can earn God's approval, and no group too far gone to be saved. The righteousness we could never achieve is offered to us as a gift through faith in Jesus. This levels every proud heart and lifts every discouraged one. You do not have to compare yourself with others to know where you stand with God. If you trust in Jesus, His righteousness covers you completely. Today, rest in that gift. And look at others with fresh eyes, remembering that there is no difference at the foot of the cross.
S: Paul announces that the righteousness of God comes through faith in Jesus to all who believe, with no difference between people. We are all equally in need of grace and equally welcome to receive it. Rest in this gift and see others through the same grace.

== Mar 04
R: This verse begins with a hard truth: 'the scripture hath concluded all under sin.' Like a prison door closing, God's Word shows that none of us can free ourselves by our own efforts. But the purpose of that hard truth is not despair. It is hope. Paul continues, 'that the promise by faith of Jesus Christ might be given to them that believe.' The bad news makes room for the good news. Only when we realize we cannot save ourselves do we become ready to receive a Savior. Many people spend their lives trying to prove they are good enough. They feel the constant pressure to earn approval from God and others. But faith lets you set down that impossible burden. The promise is given, not earned. It comes to those who believe. Today, if you feel trapped by your failures, hear this verse as an invitation. You do not need to break out on your own. Trust Jesus, and receive the promise He freely gives.
S: Scripture shows that all people are confined under sin, unable to free themselves. This hard truth prepares us to receive God's promise through faith in Jesus. Set down the burden of earning and simply trust Him today.

== Mar 05
R: James paints a tender picture of a church caring for its sick. The elders come, they pray, they anoint with oil, and James promises: 'the prayer of faith shall save the sick, and the Lord shall raise him up; and if he have committed sins, they shall be forgiven him.' Notice that healing and forgiveness appear side by side. God cares about the whole person: body, heart, and soul. Sometimes we carry physical pain. Sometimes we carry the heavier weight of guilt. James assures us that God meets both needs with compassion. And He often does it through community. The sick person is not left to suffer alone; others come and pray. Is there someone in your life who is unwell or burdened today? Visit them, call them, or pray with them in faith. And if you are the one who is hurting, do not hide. Invite others to pray for you. The Lord who raises up is also the Lord who forgives.

R: The writer of Hebrews compares Moses, a faithful servant in God's house, with Jesus, the Son who rules over it. Then comes a surprising line: 'whose house are we.' We are God's house, the very place where He lives among His people. What a privilege. And the verse continues with a call: 'if we hold fast the confidence and the rejoicing of the hope firm unto the end.' Our confidence is not in ourselves. It is in the character of Christ, the faithful Son who never fails. Because He is trustworthy, we can hold on with boldness and even joy. Some seasons test that confidence. Doubts whisper, troubles mount, and hope feels thin. That is when we remember who holds the house together. It is not our strength but His faithfulness. Today, choose to hold fast. Rejoice in the hope you have, even if it feels small. The Son who rules over God's house will keep you to the end.

== Mar 07
R: Peter wrote to believers who were suffering for their faith. He did not promise them that the pain would end quickly. Instead, he gave them a way to walk through it: 'let them that suffer according to the will of God commit the keeping of their souls to him in well doing, as unto a faithful Creator.' To commit something is to hand it over for safekeeping, like depositing a treasure with someone you trust. Peter invites us to entrust our very souls to God. And he reminds us of who God is: a faithful Creator. The One who made you knows how to keep you. He has not lost control of the world He formed. Meanwhile, we keep doing good. Suffering does not have to make us bitter or idle. It can become an opportunity to trust more deeply and love more faithfully. If you are suffering today, hand your soul to God again. Keep doing what is right, and rest in His faithful care.

The gospel moves forward like a relay race, each runner receiving the baton and handing it to the next. None of us came to faith on our own. Someone taught us, prayed for us, or showed us Jesus. Those who keep learning become the ones who can help others learn. Today, thank God for those who handed the faith to you. Then ask yourself who you are passing it to. A child, a friend, a coworker. R: In the famous chapter on love, Paul describes four things that love does: 'Beareth all things, believeth all things, hopeth all things, endureth all things.' Love bears, quietly carrying burdens and covering faults instead of exposing them. Love believes, choosing to think the best rather than the worst. Love hopes, refusing to give up on people or situations. And love endures, staying faithful through long seasons of difficulty. This is not naive optimism. It is faith in action. It trusts that God is still at work, even in people who seem stuck and situations that seem hopeless. Love like this is only possible because God first loved us this way. He bears with us, believes in His purposes for us, hopes for our good, and endures our wandering hearts. Today, think of someone you are tempted to give up on. Ask God to help you keep bearing, believing, hoping, and enduring. Faith-filled love can see what God is still able to do.

R: The Assyrian army had surrounded Jerusalem, and their spokesman, Rabshakeh, delivered a taunting message to King Hezekiah: 'What confidence is this wherein thou trustest?' It was meant to shake the people's faith. Everything around them looked hopeless. The enemy was mighty, and help seemed far away. Yet Hezekiah did not answer with panic. He took the threat to the temple and spread it before the Lord in prayer. And God delivered Jerusalem in a way no one expected. Today, when doubts taunt you, do what Hezekiah did. Take them straight to God. His faithfulness is the answer to every taunt.
