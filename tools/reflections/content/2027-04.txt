== Apr 01
R: Paul packs the whole gospel into one rich sentence: 'In whom we have redemption through his blood, the forgiveness of sins, according to the riches of his grace.' Redemption means being bought back, set free at a price. In the ancient world, the word was used for freeing a slave. We were held captive by sin and guilt, unable to free ourselves. But Jesus paid the price with His own blood. Now we have forgiveness, not a partial forgiveness, but complete. And notice the measure: according to the riches of His grace. God does not forgive us out of a small, reluctant supply. He forgives from an overflowing treasury that will never run dry. Whatever you have done, it is not too big for the riches of His grace. As we begin this month focused on grace, mercy, and redemption, receive His forgiveness fully. Let go of the chains He has already broken, and walk today in the freedom He purchased for you.
S: Paul declares that in Christ we have redemption through His blood and full forgiveness of sins. This forgiveness flows from the endless riches of God's grace. Let go of the chains Jesus has broken and walk in His freedom today.
P: You have been bought back and set free. Ephesians 1:7 says we have 'redemption through his blood, the forgiveness of sins, according to the riches of his grace.' His grace is not running low. Walk in freedom today.

The God of salvation can deliver even from the heaviest guilt. Honest confession opens the door to His mercy. Today, bring whatever you have been hiding into His light. Ask for His deliverance. And when you feel the weight lift, let your heart sing of His goodness.

== Apr 03
R: At an important meeting in Jerusalem, the early church debated whether Gentile believers needed to keep the Jewish law to be saved. Peter stood up and made it clear: 'But we believe that through the grace of the Lord Jesus Christ we shall be saved, even as they.' Notice the humility in his words. Peter, a Jewish apostle who had walked with Jesus, did not say that Gentiles would be saved like him. He said that he would be saved just like them. Everyone comes to God the same way, through grace alone. This truth tears down walls between people. No one gets to look down on anyone else. Whatever our background, culture, or history, we all stand on the same ground: undeserved grace received by faith. Today, let that truth shape how you see others. Treat every person as a fellow recipient of grace. And rest in knowing that your salvation depends not on your record, but on the grace of Jesus.
S: At the Jerusalem council, Peter declared that everyone, Jew and Gentile alike, is saved through the grace of the Lord Jesus. Grace levels every difference and humbles every proud heart. See every person today as a fellow recipient of grace.

== Apr 05
R: Paul makes a bold argument: 'I do not frustrate the grace of God: for if righteousness come by the law, then Christ is dead in vain.' If we could earn our way to God by keeping rules, then the cross would have been unnecessary. Jesus would have died for nothing. But He did not die in vain. He died because we could never be righteous on our own. To try to earn what Jesus already paid for is to set aside His grace. Many of us slip into this without realizing it. We feel closer to God on days when we perform well and distant on days when we fail. We keep spiritual scorecards. But grace invites us to stop striving and start receiving. Our good works matter, but they are a grateful response to grace, not a payment for it. Today, refuse to frustrate the grace of God. Let go of the scorecard. Rest in what Christ has done. His death was not in vain; it was enough.
S: Paul argues that if righteousness came through keeping the law, Christ would have died for nothing. Trying to earn what Jesus paid for sets aside His grace. Let go of spiritual scorecards and rest in what Christ has done.

R: Paul describes two kingdoms in conflict. One is ruled by sin, and it leads to death. The other is ruled by grace, and it leads to life: 'That as sin hath reigned unto death, even so might grace reign through righteousness unto eternal life by Jesus Christ our Lord.' Sin is a cruel king. It promises freedom but delivers bondage. It ends in death. But grace is a kind and powerful King. It reigns through the righteousness of Jesus, and its rule ends in eternal life. Notice that grace does not merely visit; it reigns. It takes the throne. That means grace is stronger than your failures, stronger than your past, and stronger than the habits that pull you down. Where sin abounded, grace abounds much more. Today, let grace have the throne of your life. When guilt or temptation tries to rule, remember who your King is. You live under the reign of grace, and its kingdom leads to life.

R: This may be one of the most comforting promises in the Bible: 'If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.' Notice the two words used to describe God. He is faithful, meaning He keeps His promise to forgive. He is just, meaning forgiveness is right because Jesus has already paid the penalty. God is not grudgingly letting us off the hook. He is acting in perfect faithfulness and justice. And the cleansing is complete: from all unrighteousness, not just some. Confession is simply agreeing with God about what is true. It is bringing into the light what we would rather hide. That can feel frightening, but this verse turns confession into a doorway of freedom. Today, take some time to be honest with God. Name what is weighing on your conscience. Then receive His forgiveness fully. You do not need to keep carrying what He has already washed away.
