== May 01
R: We often look for wisdom in many places: books, experts, experience, and the advice of friends. These can all be helpful. But Proverbs points us to the true source: 'For the LORD giveth wisdom: out of his mouth cometh knowledge and understanding.' Wisdom is not something we generate on our own. It is a gift that God delights to give. And it comes from His mouth, from what He has spoken. That is why time in His Word is never wasted. When we read Scripture, we are listening to the voice that gives wisdom. As we begin this month focused on wisdom and spiritual growth, consider where you have been looking for direction. Have you been turning to God first, or last? He is not stingy with wisdom. He gives generously to those who ask. Today, open His Word and ask Him for understanding. Then listen. The God who speaks wisdom is ready to share it with you.

== May 02
R: Isaiah looked ahead to a coming King from the family of Jesse, and he described the Spirit who would rest upon Him: 'the spirit of wisdom and understanding, the spirit of counsel and might, the spirit of knowledge and of the fear of the LORD.' This prophecy was fulfilled in Jesus. Every quality of perfect leadership and wisdom rested fully on Him. He always knew what to say, when to be gentle, and when to be firm. He had wisdom for the confused, counsel for the troubled, and might for the weak. And here is the wonderful news: the same Spirit who rested on Jesus now lives in those who belong to Him. We do not receive this Spirit in the same fullness as Christ, but we are not left without help. When we need wisdom, counsel, or strength, the Spirit is near. Today, ask the Holy Spirit to give you wisdom and understanding for whatever you face. You do not have to rely on your own limited insight.
S: Isaiah foretold that the Spirit of wisdom, understanding, counsel, might, and knowledge would rest on the coming King. This was fulfilled in Jesus, and the same Spirit now helps His people. Ask the Holy Spirit for wisdom and understanding in whatever you face today.

== May 03
R: Proverbs opens with its foundational truth: 'The fear of the LORD is the beginning of knowledge: but fools despise wisdom and instruction.' Everything else in the book is built on this. To fear the Lord is to hold Him in awe, to take Him seriously, to give Him first place in your life. That reverence is where true knowledge begins. Without it, we may gather facts, but we will miss the meaning. The verse also gives a warning. Fools despise wisdom and instruction. A fool in Proverbs is not someone with a low IQ. It is someone who refuses to be taught, who thinks they already know best. Pride closes the door to learning. Humility opens it. Today, check the posture of your heart. Are you willing to be taught by God, even when it challenges your opinions? Begin with reverence. Bow before His greatness. And then open yourself to the instruction He gives. That is the beginning of a wise life.
S: Proverbs teaches that reverence for the LORD is the beginning of true knowledge, while fools reject instruction. Humility opens the door to learning, and pride closes it. Begin today with reverence for God and a teachable heart.
P: Wisdom starts with reverence. Proverbs 1:7 says, 'The fear of the LORD is the beginning of knowledge: but fools despise wisdom and instruction.' Stay humble and teachable today. That is where wisdom begins.

== May 04
R: Paul's prayer for the Ephesians shows what he valued most. He did not pray first for their comfort or success. He prayed 'that the God of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Father of glory, may give unto you the spirit of wisdom and revelation in the knowledge of him.' Paul wanted them to know God more deeply. Not just to know about Him, but to know Him personally. And he recognized that this kind of knowledge requires divine help. We need the Spirit to open our eyes and reveal who God is. This is a beautiful prayer to pray for ourselves and for others. How often do we pray for the people we love to know God better? We pray for their health, jobs, and safety, all good things. But the greatest gift we could ask for them is deeper knowledge of God. Today, pray this prayer for yourself. Then pray it by name for someone you love. Ask God to reveal Himself to them.
S: Paul prayed that God would give believers the Spirit of wisdom and revelation to know Him more deeply. Knowing God personally is the greatest gift we can seek for ourselves and others. Pray this prayer today for yourself and someone you love.

== May 05
R: This verse is close to Proverbs 1:7, but it adds something important: 'The fear of the LORD is the beginning of wisdom: and the knowledge of the holy is understanding.' Wisdom begins with reverence, and understanding grows as we come to know the Holy One. In other words, wisdom is not ultimately about techniques or tips for living. It is about a relationship. The more we know God, His character, His ways, His heart, the more we understand life. A person may be very clever and still lack understanding, because they do not know the One who made everything. And a person with little formal education may be deeply wise, because they walk closely with God. This is encouraging. Wisdom is available to anyone willing to seek the Lord. Today, spend time getting to know God better. Read about His character in Scripture. Notice His holiness and His goodness. As you know Him more, you will understand life more clearly.

== May 06
R: Paul longed for the believers in Colosse, many of whom he had never met. He prayed 'That their hearts might be comforted, being knit together in love, and unto all riches of the full assurance of understanding.' Notice how wisdom and love are woven together here. Paul wanted their hearts encouraged, their community knit together like threads in a strong fabric, and their understanding full and confident. True wisdom is not cold or isolated. It grows best in loving community. When we learn together, pray together, and encourage one another, our understanding of God deepens. We see aspects of His truth through each other's eyes. And the goal of it all is to know Christ, in whom the mystery of God is revealed. Today, think about the people you grow alongside. Thank God for them. If you have been trying to grow alone, consider reaching out to join others in studying God's Word. Hearts knit together in love become rich in understanding.

== May 07
R: James asks a direct question: 'Who is a wise man and endued with knowledge among you?' Then he gives a practical answer: 'let him shew out of a good conversation his works with meekness of wisdom.' In the old English of the King James Bible, conversation means way of life. So James is saying that wisdom is proven not by what we know, but by how we live. And it is marked by meekness, a gentle humility. Real wisdom does not show off. It does not need to win every argument or prove how much it knows. Instead, it quietly does good, treats others with kindness, and lives with integrity. Many people talk wisely but live foolishly. James calls us to the opposite. Let your life speak louder than your words. Today, demonstrate wisdom through humble, good works. Help someone without drawing attention to yourself. Respond gently in a tense moment. That is what heavenly wisdom looks like in everyday life.
S: James teaches that true wisdom is shown by a good way of life and humble deeds, not by impressive words. Real wisdom is gentle and does not need to show off. Let your actions speak with quiet humility today.
P: Wisdom is shown, not just spoken. James 3:13 asks, 'Who is a wise man and endued with knowledge among you?' and answers that wisdom shows itself in good works done with meekness. Let your life speak today.

== May 08
R: Revelation 13 describes a beast that opposes God and deceives the world. In the middle of this dark vision comes a call: 'Here is wisdom. Let him that hath understanding count the number of the beast: for it is the number of a man.' People have speculated about this number for centuries. But notice what the verse emphasizes. It is a call for wisdom and discernment. God's people need understanding to recognize evil for what it is, especially when it appears powerful and impressive. And the number, John says, is the number of a man. However frightening evil may look, it is limited. It is human, not divine. It cannot match the power of God. True wisdom does not get lost in fear or fascination with darkness. It sees clearly, stays faithful, and trusts that God reigns over all. Today, ask God for discernment to recognize what is false. And take courage. Every power that opposes God is limited and temporary.

== May 11
R: Stephen described Moses this way: 'And Moses was learned in all the wisdom of the Egyptians, and was mighty in words and in deeds.' Raised in Pharaoh's palace, Moses had the best education in the world. He was capable, articulate, and influential. Yet when he tried to deliver Israel by his own strength, he failed and fled into the desert. It was only after forty years tending sheep in the wilderness that God called him. By then, Moses felt weak and unqualified. He even claimed he was not eloquent. But that was when God's strength could truly work through him. Our skills and training are gifts, and God can use them. But they are not enough on their own. The greatest strength comes when we depend on God rather than ourselves. Today, offer your abilities to God. And if you feel weak or unqualified, take heart. God often does His mightiest work through people who have learned to rely on Him.

These people were always collecting new ideas, always curious, always listening, yet never arriving at truth that changed their lives. Their learning was endless but fruitless. It means applying what we learn, obeying what we understand, and coming to know Jesus, who is the truth. Today, instead of seeking something new, take one truth you already know and put it into practice. Let your learning become living. That is how real growth happens.

== May 13
R: As Jesus walked toward Jerusalem, He saw a fig tree full of leaves. From a distance, it looked healthy and promising. But when He came close, He 'found nothing thereon, but leaves only.' So He spoke a word of judgment, and the tree withered. This was an acted parable. The tree represented religion that looks impressive on the outside but produces no real fruit. Jesus was about to enter a temple full of activity, but lacking true worship and justice. It is a sobering picture for us. We can have all the leaves of faith, church attendance, religious words, outward appearances, and still lack the fruit of love, obedience, and genuine trust. Jesus is not impressed by leaves. He looks for fruit. But here is the hope: He also gives us the life that produces fruit. When we stay connected to Him, real fruit grows. Today, ask Jesus to look closely at your life. Invite Him to grow genuine fruit where there have only been leaves.
S: Jesus found a fig tree with leaves but no fruit and pronounced judgment on it, picturing faith that looks good outwardly but bears no fruit. Jesus looks for genuine fruit, not appearances. Stay connected to Him and ask Him to grow real fruit in your life.
P: Jesus looks past the leaves for fruit. In Matthew 21:19, He found a fig tree with 'nothing thereon, but leaves only.' Faith is more than appearances. Ask Jesus to grow genuine love, trust, and obedience in you today.

== May 14
R: James gives one of the most beautiful descriptions of wisdom in the Bible: 'But the wisdom that is from above is first pure, then peaceable, gentle, and easy to be intreated, full of mercy and good fruits, without partiality, and without hypocrisy.' Read that list slowly. Heavenly wisdom is pure, free from selfish motives. It is peaceable, seeking harmony rather than conflict. It is gentle, not harsh or forceful. It is easy to be intreated, meaning open to reason and willing to listen. It is full of mercy, overflowing with good deeds. It is impartial, treating everyone fairly. And it is sincere, without pretending. This is not the world's idea of wisdom, which often values cleverness, winning, and getting ahead. God's wisdom looks like the character of Jesus. Today, use this verse as a mirror. Which of these qualities do you see in your life? Which ones need to grow? Ask God for the wisdom that comes from above.

Sometimes it is comfort, when truth challenges our habits or opinions. But once we have found truth, we must hold on to it. There will be pressures to trade it away for popularity, convenience, or gain. The world offers many deals that require compromising what we know is right. Proverbs says: do not sell. == May 24
R: John closes his first letter with a statement of certainty: 'And we know that the Son of God is come, and hath given us an understanding, that we may know him that is true.' John was not uncertain about the most important things. Jesus has come. He has given us understanding. And through Him, we can know the true God.
