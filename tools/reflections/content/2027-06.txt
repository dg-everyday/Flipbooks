# June 2027: Courage & Strength

== Jun 01
R: Isaiah pronounces a series of woes on the people of his day, and one of them is surprisingly pointed: 'Woe unto them that are mighty to drink wine, and men of strength to mingle strong drink.' These people were proud of a kind of strength, but it was strength spent in the wrong place. They could drink more than anyone, yet they had no strength to do justice or resist corruption. The next verse says they took bribes and denied justice to the innocent. It is a sobering reminder that not all strength is true strength. We can be strong in ways that do not matter and weak in ways that do. We can boast about endurance in pleasures while lacking the courage to do what is right. As we begin this month on courage and strength, ask God to redirect your strength. Where are you spending your energy? Real strength is found in standing for what is good, protecting the vulnerable, and walking in integrity. That kind of strength comes from God.
S: Isaiah warns against people who were strong in drinking but weak in justice and integrity. Not all strength is true strength; it can be spent in the wrong places. Ask God to redirect your strength toward what is good and right.
P: Real strength is not about how much you can handle; it is about how you stand for what is right. Isaiah 5:22 warns those 'mighty to drink wine' but weak in justice. Where are you spending your strength today?

== Jun 02
R: In John's vision, the elders in heaven fall on their faces and worship: 'We give thee thanks, O Lord God Almighty, which art, and wast, and art to come; because thou hast taken to thee thy great power, and hast reigned.' He has always been present, He is present now, and He will be present forever. His power is not a passing season of strength. It is eternal. When we feel weak, this vision reminds us who is ultimately in control. Kingdoms rise and fall, but God remains Almighty. Every power that resists Him will eventually bow. And His people respond not with fear but with thanksgiving. Today, give thanks to the Lord God Almighty. Whatever feels out of control in your life, remember that He reigns, and His power is greater than anything you face.

== Jun 03
R: Jesus had just healed a boy whom the disciples could not help. The crowd was astonished: 'And they were all amazed at the mighty power of God.' Everyone marveled at what Jesus had done. But in that very moment, Jesus turned to His disciples and spoke about His coming suffering. While the crowd was focused on spectacular power, Jesus pointed to the cross. That is significant. God's mighty power is displayed in miracles, yes. But it is displayed most fully in the cross and resurrection, in self-giving love that conquers sin and death. The disciples wanted a powerful Messiah who would only win. They struggled to understand a Messiah who would suffer. Yet the power of God is perfected in weakness. Today, marvel at the mighty power of God. But also remember that His power often works in ways we do not expect: through humility, sacrifice, and love. Trust His strength, even when it looks different from what you imagined.
S: The crowd was amazed at the mighty power of God after Jesus healed a boy, yet Jesus pointed His disciples to the cross. God's greatest power is displayed in self-giving love. Trust His strength even when it works in unexpected ways.
P: God's power often looks different than we expect. Luke 9:43 says the crowd 'were all amazed at the mighty power of God,' yet Jesus spoke of the cross. His greatest strength is shown in love. Trust Him today.

== Jun 04
R: Looking back on years of ministry, Paul was careful to give credit where it was due. Everything had been accomplished 'Through mighty signs and wonders, by the power of the Spirit of God.' He had traveled across vast regions preaching the gospel, but he knew it was not by his own strength. The Spirit had done the work. This is a freeing truth. We are not called to accomplish God's purposes in our own power. We are called to depend on His Spirit. Paul was gifted, determined, and hardworking, but he never confused his effort with God's power. Perhaps you are facing a task that feels too big for you. A calling, a responsibility, a challenge that stretches you beyond your ability. Remember that the same Spirit who empowered Paul is available to you. You do not have to do it alone. Today, invite the Spirit to work through you. Offer your effort, but trust His power. Then give Him the credit.
S: Paul credited all his ministry to the power of the Spirit of God, not his own strength. We are called to depend on God's Spirit rather than our own effort. Invite the Spirit to work through you in whatever challenge you face today.

== Jun 05
R: Paul reminds us that the battles we face are not fought with ordinary weapons: 'For the weapons of our warfare are not carnal, but mighty through God to the pulling down of strong holds.' A stronghold is a fortified place, hard to break down. In our lives, strongholds can be patterns of thinking, deep fears, lies we believe, or habits that seem impossible to overcome. We often try to fight them with willpower or human strategies, and we grow discouraged when they do not budge. But Paul says our weapons are mighty through God. Prayer, Scripture, truth, faith, and the power of the Spirit can tear down what human strength cannot. This does not mean the battle is instant or easy. Strongholds may come down brick by brick. But they can come down. Today, identify one stronghold in your life. Bring it before God in prayer. Speak His truth over it. Trust that the weapons He gives are strong enough to bring it down.
S: Paul teaches that our spiritual weapons are not worldly but mighty through God to pull down strongholds. Prayer, Scripture, and truth can tear down what willpower alone cannot. Bring one stronghold before God today and trust His power.
P: Some battles cannot be won by willpower alone. 2 Corinthians 10:4 says 'the weapons of our warfare are not carnal, but mighty through God to the pulling down of strong holds.' Pray and speak God's truth over your struggles today.

== Jun 24
R: Paul marveled that God had called him into ministry. He wrote, 'Whereof I was made a minister, according to the gift of the grace of God given unto me by the effectual working of his power.' Paul did not appoint himself. He did not earn his calling. It was a gift of grace. And it was sustained by God's power working effectively in him. Remember, Paul had once persecuted the church. He considered himself the least of all believers. Yet God's grace made him a minister of the gospel. This is good news for anyone who feels unqualified for what God has called them to do. Your calling, whatever it is, is not based on your worthiness but on God's grace. And the power to carry it out is not your own. It is His power, working in you. Today, whatever responsibilities God has given you, at home, at work, at church, receive them as gifts of grace. Then lean on His power to fulfill them.
S: Paul described his calling as a gift of God's grace, carried out by the effective working of God's power. Our callings are not based on our worthiness but on His grace. Receive your responsibilities as gifts and lean on His power today.
P: Feeling unqualified? So did Paul. Ephesians 3:7 says his calling came 'according to the gift of the grace of God given unto me by the effectual working of his power.' Your calling is a gift. Lean on His power today.

== Jun 25
R: This is one of the most quoted verses in the Bible: 'I can do all things through Christ which strengtheneth me.' But its context gives it even deeper meaning. Paul was not talking about achieving every ambition or winning every competition. He was talking about contentment. He had learned to be content in poverty and in plenty, hungry or full. Whatever his circumstances, Christ gave him the strength to endure with peace. That is the true power of this verse. It is not a promise that we will always succeed. It is a promise that, whatever comes, Christ will strengthen us to face it faithfully. In loss, we can endure. In waiting, we can be patient. In abundance, we can stay humble. Today, whatever you are facing, this verse is for you. Not as a slogan for success, but as a lifeline of strength. Christ is with you. He will strengthen you for whatever this day holds.
S: Paul wrote that he could do all things through Christ who strengthened him, in the context of learning contentment in every circumstance. Christ gives strength to endure faithfully, whatever comes. Lean on His strength for whatever this day holds.
P: This verse is about more than success. Philippians 4:13 says, 'I can do all things through Christ which strengtheneth me,' written about contentment in every circumstance. Whatever today brings, He will strengthen you.

== Jun 26
R: John writes about a love so complete that it removes fear: 'Herein is our love made perfect, that we may have boldness in the day of judgment: because as he is, so are we in this world.' When God's love grows to maturity in us, it gives us confidence, even about the day of judgment. Why? Because we are united with Jesus. As He is, so are we. His standing before the Father becomes our standing. That frees us from the fear of punishment. The next verse says perfect love casts out fear. And love made perfect is not only something we receive. It is something we live out. As we love others the way Christ loves us, His love takes deeper root in our lives. Confidence and love grow together. Today, let His perfect love drive out your fears. Then let that same love flow outward in practical action. Show love to someone today, and grow in the confidence of being fully loved by God.
S: John teaches that when God's love is made perfect in us, we can have boldness even on the day of judgment, because we are united with Christ. His love removes fear and flows out through our actions. Let His love drive out fear and move you to love others.

== Jun 27
R: Paul explains that God worked through both Peter and himself, but in different ways: 'For he that wrought effectually in Peter to the apostleship of the circumcision, the same was mighty in me toward the Gentiles.' Peter was called mainly to reach Jewish people. Paul was called to reach Gentiles. Their assignments were different, but the same God empowered both. This is a helpful reminder for anyone who compares their calling with others. Your path may look different from someone else's. Your gifts, opportunities, and audience may differ. But the same God who works mightily in others is working in you. Your assignment is not less important because it is different. God does not use everyone in the same way, but He uses everyone who is willing. Today, embrace the calling God has given you. Stop comparing it to someone else's. Trust that He is working effectively in you, just as He did in Peter and Paul, for His purposes and His glory.
S: Paul explains that the same God worked mightily in both Peter and himself, though their callings were different. Our assignments may differ, but the same God empowers each of us. Embrace your own calling today without comparing it to others.
P: Different callings, same powerful God. Galatians 2:8 says the God who worked through Peter 'the same was mighty in me toward the Gentiles.' Stop comparing your path. God is working mightily in you too.

== Jun 28
R: Paul and his companions had been beaten and imprisoned in Philippi. They had every reason to keep quiet when they reached the next city. But Paul writes, 'we were bold in our God to speak unto you the gospel of God with much contention.' Notice the source of their boldness: bold in our God. Their courage did not come from personality or confidence in themselves. It came from God. And it did not depend on easy circumstances. They spoke with much contention, in the face of opposition. This is courage for today's calling. Most of us will not face prison for our faith. But we do face moments that require courage: speaking up for what is right, sharing our faith, setting boundaries, or doing the hard thing when it would be easier to stay silent. Past hurts can make us hesitate. But Paul shows that courage can rise again. Today, be bold in your God. Ask Him for courage to do what He has called you to do.
S: Despite suffering in Philippi, Paul and his companions were bold in God to share the gospel amid opposition. Their courage came from God, not from personality or easy circumstances. Ask God today for courage to do what He has called you to do.
P: Courage can rise again, even after hurt. 1 Thessalonians 2:2 says that after suffering, 'we were bold in our God to speak unto you the gospel.' Ask God for courage today. Your boldness comes from Him.

== Jun 29
R: Psalm 29 opens with a call to the mighty: 'Give unto the LORD, O ye mighty, give unto the LORD glory and strength.' The psalmist calls even the powerful ones, perhaps angels, perhaps earthly rulers, to recognize that all glory and strength belong to God. It is an invitation to bow. The rest of the psalm describes God's voice thundering over the waters, breaking cedars, and shaking the wilderness. Compared to His power, every human strength looks small. Yet this psalm ends with a beautiful promise: 'The LORD will give strength unto his people.' The God who receives all strength also gives strength. When we acknowledge His greatness, we discover that He shares His power with those who trust Him. Today, give God the glory He deserves. Acknowledge that every strength you have comes from Him. And then receive His strength for whatever you face. The God of thunder is also the God who strengthens His people with peace.
S: Psalm 29 calls the mighty to give glory and strength to the LORD, whose voice thunders over all creation. The God who receives all strength also gives strength to His people. Give Him glory today and receive His strength for what you face.
P: All strength belongs to God, and He shares it with His people. Psalm 29:1 says, 'Give unto the LORD, O ye mighty, give unto the LORD glory and strength.' Honor Him today, and lean on His strength.

== Jun 30
R: As we close this month on courage and strength, Isaiah gives a promise for the weary: 'He giveth power to the faint; and to them that have no might he increaseth strength.' God does not only strengthen the strong. He gives power to the faint, to those who feel they have nothing left. When you are exhausted, discouraged, or overwhelmed, you are exactly the kind of person this promise is for. In the next verses, Isaiah says even young people grow tired, but those who wait on the LORD will renew their strength. They will mount up with wings as eagles, run and not be weary, walk and not faint. Strength is not something we must manufacture. It is something we receive as we wait on God. Today, if you are running on empty, stop and wait on Him. Bring Him your weakness. Rest in His presence. He specializes in giving power to the faint. Let Him renew you for the days ahead.
S: Isaiah promises that God gives power to the faint and increases strength for those who have none. Strength is received as we wait on the LORD. Bring your weariness to Him today and let Him renew your strength.
