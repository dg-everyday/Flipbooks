# July 2027: Peace, Rest & Renewal

== Jul 01
R: Isaiah paints a picture of home that every heart longs for: 'And my people shall dwell in a peaceable habitation, and in sure dwellings, and in quiet resting places.' Peaceable. Sure. Quiet. These words describe more than a building. They describe a life settled in God's care. Isaiah spoke them to a nation surrounded by turmoil, promising that when God's righteous King reigned, His people would finally know security and rest. Many of us live in noisy, hurried, uncertain places. Our homes may be busy, our minds restless, our futures unclear. But God's promise is that He Himself is our dwelling place. In Him, our souls can find a quiet resting place, even when the world outside is loud. As we begin this month of peace, rest, and renewal, ask God to make your heart a peaceable habitation. Create a small space of quiet in your day. Sit with Him. Let His presence settle you. His peace is a home you can return to anytime.
S: Isaiah promises that God's people will dwell in peaceful, secure, and quiet resting places. God Himself is our true dwelling place, even when life is loud. Create a quiet space today and let His presence settle your heart.
P: Your soul needs a quiet place. Isaiah 32:18 promises a 'peaceable habitation, and in sure dwellings, and in quiet resting places.' Take a few quiet minutes with God today and let Him be your home.

== Jul 02
R: When Lazarus died, Jesus told His disciples that their friend was sleeping. They were relieved, thinking he was simply resting. John explains, 'Howbeit Jesus spake of his death: but they thought that he had spoken of taking of rest in sleep.' Jesus used the gentle word sleep to describe death. For those who belong to Him, death is not the end. It is like falling asleep, with the certainty of waking again. Jesus was on His way to raise Lazarus, demonstrating that He has power over the grave. This brings deep comfort to anyone who has lost a loved one in Christ. They are not lost forever. They rest in Him, and one day they will rise. It also brings peace when we think about our own mortality. We do not need to fear the end. In Jesus, even death becomes a kind of rest. Today, rest in His presence and in His promise. The One who calls death sleep is the One who will wake us.
S: Jesus spoke of the death of Lazarus as sleep, and His disciples misunderstood Him. For those who belong to Jesus, death is like a rest from which they will awaken. Rest today in His power over the grave and His promise of resurrection.
P: In Jesus, death is not the end. John 11:13 tells us that when Jesus spoke of sleep, 'Jesus spake of his death.' For those who are His, death is a rest before waking. Hold on to that hope today.

He sees you, hears you, and calls you near. Today, if you feel overlooked or lost in the crowd, take comfort. Jesus has stood still for you. Rise, and come to Him. He is calling your name.

== Jul 04
R: Through Isaiah, God offered His people a gift: 'This is the rest wherewith ye may cause the weary to rest; and this is the refreshing.' He was pointing them to the rest and refreshing found in trusting Him. But the verse ends with sad words: 'yet they would not hear.' They refused the very thing they needed. Instead of resting in God, they kept striving, making alliances, and trusting their own plans. It is possible to be offered rest and still refuse it. Many of us do this. God invites us to slow down, to trust Him, to lay down our burdens. Yet we keep running, worrying, and carrying everything ourselves. We say we are too busy to rest. But God's rest is not laziness. It is the deep refreshment of trusting Him. Today, do not be like those who would not hear. Listen to His invitation. Choose a moment to stop, breathe, and trust. Receive the rest and refreshing He freely offers.

== Jul 05
R: Jesus offers one of the most beautiful invitations in Scripture: 'Take my yoke upon you, and learn of me; for I am meek and lowly in heart: and ye shall find rest unto your souls.' A yoke was a wooden frame that joined two animals so they could work together. Jesus is not promising a life without work. He is promising a life of shared work, where He carries the weight with us. And His yoke fits well. It is shaped by His gentle and humble heart. Many of us are wearing heavy yokes: the yoke of perfectionism, people-pleasing, fear, or trying to earn God's approval. These burdens wear us down. Jesus invites us to exchange them for His. When we walk with Him, learning from His gentle ways, we find rest for our souls, even in the middle of busy days. Today, identify a burden that has been weighing you down. Lay it at His feet. Take His yoke instead, and learn to walk in step with Him.
S: Jesus invites us to take His yoke, learn from His gentle and humble heart, and find rest for our souls. His yoke means sharing life's work with Him rather than carrying it alone. Exchange your heavy burdens for His gentle yoke today.

R: Proverbs gives a warning that may seem surprising in a month about rest: 'Slothfulness casteth into a deep sleep; and an idle soul shall suffer hunger.' There is a difference between rest and laziness. God's rest refreshes and renews us for faithful living. Slothfulness, on the other hand, slowly drains life away. It is a kind of spiritual drowsiness, where we drift without purpose and stop caring about what matters. And it leaves the soul hungry. This proverb helps us see that true renewal is not the same as doing nothing. Our souls are renewed not only by stillness but by meaningful engagement with God and His purposes. Rest prepares us for good work, and good work makes rest sweet. Today, examine whether you are resting or simply drifting. If you have grown spiritually sleepy, ask God to wake you up. Feed your soul with His Word. Take one purposeful step. Let rest renew you for a life that is fully awake to God.
