== Nov 01
R: David begins this psalm with an urgent plea: 'Hear my prayer, O LORD, give ear to my supplications: in thy faithfulness answer me, and in thy righteousness.' Notice what David appeals to. Not his own goodness, not his track record, but God's faithfulness and righteousness. David knew he did not deserve an answer. In the very next verse he says that no one living is righteous before God. His hope rested entirely on God's character. This is the foundation of living by faith in prayer. We do not come to God because we have earned a hearing. We come because He is faithful. He keeps His promises. He is righteous, always doing what is right. That gives us confidence. As we begin this month focused on prayer and God's presence, bring your requests to Him on the basis of who He is. You do not need perfect words or a perfect record. You need a faithful God, and you have one. Pray today, trusting His faithfulness.

== Nov 02
R: Cornelius was a Roman centurion, a Gentile, an outsider to God's covenant people. Yet he prayed faithfully and gave generously to the poor. One afternoon, an angel appeared to him and said, 'Cornelius, thy prayer is heard, and thine alms are had in remembrance in the sight of God.' God had been listening all along. Every prayer, every act of generosity had been noticed and remembered. And God answered by sending Peter to share the gospel with Cornelius and his household. This story reminds us that God sees and hears people we might overlook. He is not limited by our categories of who is in or out. Cornelius was seeking, and God responded. It also encourages anyone who has been praying faithfully without seeing results. Your prayers are not forgotten. They are remembered in the sight of God. Today, keep turning toward God in prayer. Keep giving generously. Trust that He hears, and that He will answer in His perfect time.

== Nov 03
R: King Hezekiah was gravely ill, and the prophet Isaiah told him he would die. Hezekiah turned his face to the wall and wept bitterly in prayer. Then God sent Isaiah back with a new message: 'Thus saith the LORD, the God of David thy father, I have heard thy prayer, I have seen thy tears: behold, I will add unto thy days fifteen years.' I have heard. I have seen. God was not indifferent to Hezekiah's pain. He noticed every tear. And He responded with mercy. Not every prayer for healing is answered the way we hope. But this story shows that our prayers truly matter to God. He hears our words and sees our tears. Prayer is not a ritual that changes nothing. It is real conversation with a God who responds. Today, if you are carrying something heavy, turn toward God as Hezekiah did. Pour out your heart honestly. He hears your prayer and sees your tears. You are not crying into empty air.

It does not stop praying for those who resist or reject God. It keeps bringing them before the Lord with longing. Perhaps you have family members, friends, or neighbors who do not know Jesus. Our prayers are part of how He works. Today, write down the names of people you long to see come to faith. Pray for them by name. Let your heart's desire become your persistent prayer. God hears, and He is able to save.

With righteous anger, He overturned their tables and declared, 'It is written, My house shall be called the house of prayer; but ye have made it a den of thieves.' Today, ask Him to clear away whatever is crowding out your time with God. Make room for prayer. Let your heart become a place where you meet Him, not a marketplace of distraction.

Today, before you speak to God, take time to listen. Read a passage of Scripture slowly. Sit in silence for a few moments. Hear Him.
