# January 2028: Seeking God First

== Jan 01
R: The first morning of a new year has a particular freshness to it. The calendar is clean, the pages are unwritten, and for a moment everything feels possible. Jeremiah wrote this verse at a very different kind of morning. Jerusalem lay in ruins, and he sat among the ashes of everything he had loved. Yet in the middle of his grief he remembered something that changed his outlook: 'They are new every morning: great is thy faithfulness.' God's mercies do not run on last year's supply. They are renewed with every sunrise. Whatever you carry into this year, whether regret, weariness, or uncertainty, you do not have to face it with yesterday's strength. There is fresh mercy waiting for you today, and there will be fresh mercy tomorrow. Begin this year by looking up rather than back. The God who carried you through every morning of last year has not changed. His faithfulness is great, and it is new again today.
S: Jeremiah wrote of God's new mercies while sitting among the ruins of Jerusalem. God's compassion is not a store that runs low; it is renewed every morning. Begin the new year by trusting His faithfulness for today.
P: A new year, and new mercies to match. Lamentations 3:23 says God's mercies 'are new every morning: great is thy faithfulness.' Whatever you carry into 2028, there is fresh grace waiting for you today. What are you trusting God for this year?

== Jan 02
R: Most of us start a new year with a list. We want to exercise more, spend less, read more, and worry less. Lists are not wrong, but Jesus offers a better starting point: 'But seek ye first the kingdom of God, and his righteousness; and all these things shall be added unto you.' He spoke these words to people who were anxious about food and clothing, the most basic needs of life. He did not say those needs were unimportant. He said they belonged in their proper place. When God's kingdom comes first, everything else finds its order. Our priorities settle, our worries shrink, and our hearts become steady. Seeking first means giving God the first thought of the morning, the first say in our decisions, and the first claim on our loyalty. Before you finalize your plans for this year, put Him at the top of the list. Then trust Him with everything below it. He knows what you need.
S: Jesus told anxious listeners to seek God's kingdom and righteousness first. When God comes first, every other need finds its proper place. Put Him at the top of your plans this year and trust Him with the rest.
P: Before the resolutions, a priority. Matthew 6:33 says, 'seek ye first the kingdom of God, and his righteousness; and all these things shall be added unto you.' Put God first on your list this year, and trust Him with everything that follows.

== Jan 03
R: God spoke these words to people living far from home. The Israelites had been carried away to Babylon, and many of them wondered whether God had forgotten them. Through Jeremiah, He gave them a promise: 'And ye shall seek me, and find me, when ye shall search for me with all your heart.' Notice the condition. God is not hiding from us, but He does invite wholehearted seeking. A half-hearted search rarely finds anything of value. We look for lost keys with determination. We search for answers to problems with focus and energy. God invites that same earnestness in our pursuit of Him. Wholehearted seeking might look like turning off a screen to pray, opening the Bible when you would rather scroll, or asking honest questions instead of drifting. The promise is that you will find Him. Not a distant idea of God, but God Himself. Bring your whole heart to Him today, and expect to be found.
S: God promised the exiles in Babylon that they would find Him when they searched with all their heart. He is not hiding, but He invites a wholehearted search. Bring Him your full attention today and trust the promise that you will find Him.
P: God is not hard to find for a heart that truly looks. Jeremiah 29:13 says, 'ye shall seek me, and find me, when ye shall search for me with all your heart.' What is one way you can seek Him with your whole heart today?

== Jan 04
R: If you could ask God for only one thing, what would it be? David answered that question in Psalm 27: 'One thing have I desired of the LORD, that will I seek after; that I may dwell in the house of the LORD all the days of my life.' David was a king with armies, wealth, and enemies to defeat. He could have asked for victory, security, or a longer reign. Instead, his deepest desire was to be near God and to behold His beauty. That single desire simplified his life. When we want many things equally, we are pulled in many directions. When one desire rises above the rest, the others begin to fall into line. Being close to God is not a reward for finishing everything else. It is the center that holds everything else together. Today, let your many wants become one. Ask to know God more, to see His beauty, and to stay near Him all your days.
S: David, a king with every reason to ask for power, said his one desire was to dwell near God and behold His beauty. One great desire brings order to all the smaller ones. Make nearness to God the center of your life today.
P: If you could ask God for one thing, what would it be? In Psalm 27:4 David wrote, 'One thing have I desired of the LORD, that will I seek after.' His answer was simply to be near God. Let that become your one thing today.

== Jan 05
R: David wrote Psalm 63 in the wilderness of Judah, a dry and dusty place where water was scarce. He knew real thirst, the kind that makes the mouth parched and the body weak. And he used that experience to describe his longing for God: 'O God, thou art my God; early will I seek thee: my soul thirsteth for thee.' Thirst is not a polite preference. It is an urgent need that will not go away until it is satisfied. David recognized that his soul had the same kind of need. Nothing else could quench it but God Himself. Sometimes we try to satisfy our soul's thirst with busyness, entertainment, approval, or possessions. They may help for a moment, but the dryness returns. Only God truly satisfies. Notice also that David sought God early. He did not wait until the day was spent. Bring your thirst to God this morning. He is the living water your soul has been looking for.
S: In the dry wilderness, David compared his longing for God to physical thirst. The soul has a need that only God can satisfy. Seek Him early today and let Him refresh your dry places.
P: Your soul was made to thirst for God. Psalm 63:1 says, 'O God, thou art my God; early will I seek thee: my soul thirsteth for thee.' Nothing else truly satisfies. Bring your thirst to Him this morning.

== Jan 06
R: Moses spoke these words to a people who had not yet entered the Promised Land. Looking ahead, he warned them that they might one day turn away from God and end up scattered among other nations. But even in that warning, he left the door open: 'But if from thence thou shalt seek the LORD thy God, thou shalt find him.' From thence. From wherever you have ended up. From the far country of your own choices. From the place you never expected to be. God does not wait for us to fix everything before we come back. He invites us to seek Him from right where we are. Perhaps you feel far from God today. Maybe you drifted slowly, or maybe you walked away on purpose. Either way, the promise stands. Seek Him with all your heart and soul, and you will find Him. No distance is too great for a God who wants to be found.
S: Moses told Israel that even if they were scattered far away, they could seek God from there and find Him. God does not require us to fix everything before we return. Seek Him today from right where you are.

== Jan 07
R: Picture a deer in a dry season, searching for a stream. It is not wandering casually. It is driven by need, moving from place to place until it finds water. The psalmist used that picture to describe his soul: 'As the hart panteth after the water brooks, so panteth my soul after thee, O God.' This psalm was written by someone who felt far from God. He remembered better days, when he had walked with the crowds to worship. Now he was downcast and longing. His honesty is a gift to us. Spiritual longing is not a sign that something is wrong with your faith. Often it is a sign that your faith is alive. A heart that pants for God is a heart that knows where real refreshment is found. If you feel dry today, do not hide it. Tell God you long for Him. Then go to the stream. Read His Word, pray, and wait for Him to refresh your soul.
S: The psalmist compared his longing for God to a deer searching for water in a dry land. Spiritual longing is often a sign of living faith, not failing faith. Bring your thirst honestly to God today and let Him refresh you.

== Jan 08
R: Our minds can be restless places. Thoughts race ahead to tomorrow's problems, replay yesterday's conversations, and invent worries that may never happen. Isaiah offers a different way: 'Thou wilt keep him in perfect peace, whose mind is stayed on thee: because he trusteth in thee.' The word stayed means propped up, leaning, supported. A mind stayed on God is a mind that rests its full weight on Him. It is not a mind that never thinks about problems, but a mind that keeps returning to Him in the middle of them. Perfect peace is not the absence of trouble. It is the steady calm of a heart that trusts. This peace does not depend on how well your day goes. It depends on where your thoughts are anchored. Today, when your mind begins to spin, gently bring it back. Remind yourself who God is and what He has promised. Lean your whole weight on Him.
S: Isaiah promised perfect peace to the one whose mind is stayed, or leaning, on God. Peace does not come from the absence of trouble but from trust anchored in Him. When your thoughts begin to spin today, bring them back to Him.

== Jan 09
R: The apostle Paul had plenty in his past that could have held him back. Before he followed Christ, he persecuted believers. After he followed Christ, he suffered beatings, shipwrecks, and prison. He also had remarkable successes. Yet he wrote, 'this one thing I do, forgetting those things which are behind, and reaching forth unto those things which are before.' Paul was not pretending the past did not happen. He was refusing to let it steer his future. Both failures and achievements can become anchors. Old guilt can whisper that you are disqualified. Old victories can tempt you to coast. Paul let go of both. He leaned forward like a runner straining toward the finish. As this year unfolds, what from the past is still pulling at you? Bring it to God. Receive His forgiveness for what was wrong and give Him thanks for what was good. Then turn your face forward. There is more ahead than behind.
S: Paul, who had both failures and successes in his past, chose to forget what was behind and reach for what was ahead. Neither old guilt nor old victories should steer our future. Give your past to God today and lean forward into what He has prepared.
P: The past is a teacher, not a master. Philippians 3:13 says, 'this one thing I do, forgetting those things which are behind, and reaching forth unto those things which are before.' What do you need to release so you can move forward?

== Jan 10
R: Paul continued his thought with a picture from the athletic games of his day: 'I press toward the mark for the prize of the high calling of God in Christ Jesus.' A runner in a race does not wander. Eyes fixed on the finish line, every step is purposeful. Paul saw his whole life that way. He was not drifting through the years. He was pressing toward something. The prize he sought was not a trophy or a title. It was the high calling of God, the full purpose God had for him in Christ. That calling was worth every ounce of effort. We can easily drift through a year without direction. Days blur together, and we look up in December wondering where the time went. Pressing toward the mark means living on purpose. It means asking each day what God is calling you toward and taking steps in that direction. Keep your eyes on the prize today. Run with purpose.
S: Paul described his life as a runner pressing toward the finish line. The prize was God's high calling in Christ, worth every effort. Live on purpose today and take a step toward what God is calling you to.

== Jan 12
R: Moses wrote Psalm 90, and it is one of the oldest prayers in the Bible. He had watched a whole generation pass away in the wilderness, and he knew how short human life is. So he prayed, 'So teach us to number our days, that we may apply our hearts unto wisdom.' Numbering our days does not mean living in fear of death. It means living with an honest awareness that our time is limited and valuable. When we forget that, we waste days on things that do not matter and postpone the things that do. When we remember it, we become wiser. We say the kind word now. We forgive sooner. We spend more time with God and with the people we love. A new year is a good time to pray this prayer. Ask God to help you see your days clearly. Then invest them in what lasts. Each day is a gift worth spending well.
S: Moses, who had seen a generation pass, asked God to teach him to number his days. An honest view of life's shortness leads to a wiser heart. Ask God to help you spend today on what truly lasts.

== Jan 13
R: At the start of a year, many of us have plans: projects to begin, goals to reach, problems to solve. Proverbs gives simple advice about all of it: 'Commit thy works unto the LORD, and thy thoughts shall be established.' The word commit carries the idea of rolling something onto someone else. Picture a heavy load you can no longer carry, rolled onto stronger shoulders. That is what God invites you to do with your work. Committing your works to the Lord means bringing your plans to Him before you start, asking for His direction, and trusting Him with the outcome. It does not mean you stop working. It means you stop carrying the weight alone. And the promise is that your thoughts will be established. Your mind becomes steady. The anxious second-guessing quiets down. Before you dive into today's work, pause and roll it onto Him. Then work with a settled heart.

One of his conclusions was this: 'Commit thy way unto the LORD; trust also in him; and he shall bring it to pass.' Yesterday we thought about committing our works. Committing without trusting is like giving someone your keys and then grabbing them back. True commitment lets go. And the promise is beautiful: He shall bring it to pass. God is able to accomplish what we cannot. Commit your way to Him today and rest in His faithfulness.

It is easy to read it backward, as if God were promising to hand us whatever we want if we act religious enough. But the order matters. Delight comes first. When we truly delight in God, enjoying Him, treasuring Him, finding our gladness in Him, our desires begin to change. We start wanting what He wants. Our hearts align with His. Then the desires He fulfills are the very desires He has shaped. Delighting in God is not a duty to get through. It is a joy to discover. It might mean noticing His goodness in creation, savoring a verse that speaks to you, or simply resting in the knowledge that you are loved. Look for something to delight in about God today. Let Him become your joy, and watch your desires become more like His.

Jesus had taught in the synagogue, cast out an unclean spirit, healed Peter's mother-in-law, and then, as evening came, healed many sick people while the whole city gathered at the door. He must have been exhausted. Yet the next morning we read, 'rising up a great while before day, he went out, and departed into a solitary place, and there prayed.' == Jan 17
R: David had a morning habit: 'My voice shalt thou hear in the morning, O LORD; in the morning will I direct my prayer unto thee, and will look up.' Notice the three parts of his prayer. First, he spoke to God. Second, he directed his prayer, arranging it carefully the way a priest laid out a sacrifice on the altar. Third, he looked up, watching expectantly for God's answer. Many of us do the first part and skip the rest. We say a quick prayer and then rush on, never looking for what God will do. David prayed with expectation. He believed God heard and would respond. That changed how he walked through the rest of his day. He was looking for God's hand. Try David's pattern this morning. Speak honestly to God. Bring your requests with care. Then look up. Go through your day watching for His answers, His help, and His presence. You may be surprised at how much you notice.
S: David brought his prayers to God in the morning and then looked up, expecting an answer. Prayer becomes richer when we watch for God's response. Pray with expectation today and look for His hand at work.
P: Pray, then look up. Psalm 5:3 says, 'in the morning will I direct my prayer unto thee, and will look up.' Bring God your requests this morning and spend the day watching for His answers.
