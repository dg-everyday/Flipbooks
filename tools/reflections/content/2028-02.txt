# February 2028: Kindness, Friendship & Family

== Feb 01
R: Some friendships last only as long as things are easy. When life gets complicated, those friends quietly disappear. Proverbs describes something better: 'A friend loveth at all times, and a brother is born for adversity.' At all times. In seasons of success and seasons of loss. When you are fun to be around and when you are struggling. A true friend does not keep a schedule of availability based on convenience. And the second half of the verse adds a striking thought. A brother is born for adversity. Hard times reveal what relationships are made of. They are the very moments real friendship was designed for. Think of the people who stayed with you through a difficult season. Give thanks to God for them, and maybe tell them how much it meant. Then ask yourself whether there is someone going through adversity right now who needs a friend who loves at all times. You may be the answer to their prayer.
S: Proverbs describes a friend who loves at all times and a brother born for hard seasons. True friendship is revealed when life becomes difficult. Thank God for faithful friends today, and be one to someone who is struggling.
P: Real friends show up in hard times. Proverbs 17:17 says, 'A friend loveth at all times, and a brother is born for adversity.' Who stood by you in a difficult season? Who needs you to stand by them now?

== Feb 02
R: Loneliness is one of the quiet struggles of our time. Many people long for close friends but are unsure how to find them. Proverbs offers practical wisdom: 'A man that hath friends must shew himself friendly.' Friendship usually begins with someone taking the first step. A greeting, an invitation, a question, a word of encouragement. Waiting for others to reach out can leave us waiting a long time. Being friendly is not about having a big personality. It is about showing genuine interest in others. Then the verse lifts our eyes higher: 'there is a friend that sticketh closer than a brother.' Some human friendships reach that level of closeness, and they are treasures. But ultimately this points us to the faithfulness of God, who never leaves us. Even when human friends are few, we are not friendless. Today, take a small step of friendliness toward someone. And rest in the One who sticks closer than any brother.
S: Proverbs teaches that people who want friends must show themselves friendly. It also speaks of a friend who sticks closer than a brother. Take a step of friendliness today, and rest in God's faithful presence.
P: Friendship often starts with a first step. Proverbs 18:24 says, 'A man that hath friends must shew himself friendly: and there is a friend that sticketh closer than a brother.' Reach out to someone today.

== Feb 03
R: King Solomon had wealth, power, and wisdom, and he spent much of Ecclesiastes asking what truly matters in life. One of his conclusions was simple: 'Two are better than one; because they have a good reward for their labour.' Work done together often accomplishes more than work done alone. Burdens shared are lighter. Joys shared are fuller. Solomon had seen people who worked endlessly by themselves, piling up riches with no one to share them, and he called that vanity. Life was not designed to be lived in isolation. God made us for relationship, with Him and with each other. Independence is often praised, but there is wisdom in partnership. Perhaps there is a task you have been trying to carry alone. Could you invite someone to help? Or perhaps someone near you is working hard without support. Could you come alongside them? Two really are better than one. Look for ways to share the work and the reward today.
S: Solomon observed that two people working together accomplish more and enjoy more than one working alone. God designed us for partnership, not isolation. Look for a way to share a task or come alongside someone today.

== Feb 04
R: Solomon continued with a vivid picture: 'For if they fall, the one will lift up his fellow: but woe to him that is alone when he falleth; for he hath not another to help him up.' Everyone falls sometimes. We stumble into sin, slip into discouragement, or get knocked down by circumstances beyond our control. The question is not whether we will fall, but whether anyone will be there to help us up. Solomon describes the person who falls alone as one to be pitied. There is no hand reaching down, no voice of encouragement, no one to say, you can get up again. This is why friendship matters so much. It is not only for good times. It is a safety net for hard ones. Today, think of whether you have people who would help you up, and whether you are that person for someone else. If someone you know has fallen, do not stand back. Reach out your hand.
S: Solomon pictured two companions where one lifts the other up after a fall. Those who fall alone have no one to help them rise. Be the friend who reaches out a hand today.

== Feb 05
R: Naomi had lost her husband and both her sons in a foreign land. She decided to return home to Bethlehem and urged her widowed daughters-in-law to go back to their own families. One of them, Orpah, tearfully agreed. But Ruth refused: 'whither thou goest, I will go; and where thou lodgest, I will lodge: thy people shall be my people, and thy God my God.' Ruth had every reason to leave. Naomi had nothing to offer her: no money, no husband, no security. Yet Ruth chose loyalty. She bound her life to Naomi's and to Naomi's God. That decision changed history. Ruth later married Boaz and became the great-grandmother of King David. Loyal love is rare and beautiful. It stays when leaving would be easier. It chooses commitment over comfort. Is there someone in your life who needs that kind of loyalty from you? Stand by them today. Faithful love often matters more than we will ever know.
S: When Naomi had nothing left to offer, Ruth chose to stay with her and follow her God. Loyal love stays when leaving would be easier. Show that kind of faithful love to someone today.

== Feb 06
R: After David defeated Goliath, he stood before King Saul, and Saul's son Jonathan was watching. Something happened in that moment: 'the soul of Jonathan was knit with the soul of David, and Jonathan loved him as his own soul.' It was an unlikely friendship. Jonathan was the prince, the heir to the throne. David was a shepherd boy who would one day take that throne. Jonathan could have seen David as a rival. Instead, he saw a brother. He gave David his robe, his sword, and his bow. Later, he risked his own life to protect David from his father's anger. Knit is a beautiful word. It describes threads woven together so closely they become one fabric. True friendship knits lives together. It is selfless and loyal, and it seeks the good of the other even at personal cost. Thank God today for friends whose lives are woven with yours. And ask Him to make you a Jonathan to someone.

== Feb 07
R: A dull knife can be sharpened by rubbing it against another piece of iron. The process involves friction and sparks, but the result is a blade that works as it should. Proverbs applies that picture to relationships: 'Iron sharpeneth iron; so a man sharpeneth the countenance of his friend.' Good friends make each other better. They challenge our thinking, encourage our growth, and sometimes point out where we have grown dull. That process is not always comfortable. There may be friction. But it is a gift. A friend who only ever agrees with you may be pleasant, but they will not help you grow. Look for friends who love God and love you enough to sharpen you. And be that kind of friend for others. Ask good questions. Share what God is teaching you. Encourage someone to take the next step of faith. Sharpening is a two-way exchange. Both pieces of iron come away better than before.
S: Just as iron sharpens iron, friends sharpen one another. Growth sometimes involves friction, but it makes us better. Seek friends who help you grow, and be that kind of friend to others today.

== Feb 08
R: Not everything that feels kind is kind, and not everything that hurts is harmful. Proverbs puts it sharply: 'Faithful are the wounds of a friend; but the kisses of an enemy are deceitful.' A true friend will sometimes tell you what you do not want to hear. They will point out a blind spot, warn you about a bad decision, or gently confront a sin. Those words can sting. But they come from love. A flatterer, on the other hand, may say only pleasant things while caring nothing for your good. Flattery feels nice but leaves you unchanged. Honest love may hurt for a moment, but it heals. This verse challenges us in two directions. First, will we receive hard truth from those who love us without getting defensive? Second, will we love others enough to speak honestly when needed, with gentleness and care? Today, thank God for friends who are honest with you. Their wounds are faithful.
S: Proverbs teaches that honest words from a friend are better than the flattery of an enemy. Loving truth may sting, but it helps us grow. Receive honest counsel with grace today, and speak the truth in love when needed.

Now He gave them a new title: 'Henceforth I call you not servants; for the servant knoweth not what his lord doeth: but I have called you friends.' A servant does what he is told without knowing the master's plans. A friend is let in on the purpose. Jesus had shared with them everything the Father had shown Him. He had opened His heart. You are not just a servant trying to earn approval. You are a friend of Jesus, invited to know Him and walk with Him. Today, talk to Him as a friend. == Feb 10
R: Paul's letter to the Romans contains a whole chapter on how believers should treat one another. In the middle of it is this instruction: 'Be kindly affectioned one to another with brotherly love; in honour preferring one another.' Kindly affectioned describes the warm care of family members. It is not cold politeness but genuine tenderness. And in honour preferring one another means trying to outdo each other in showing respect. Imagine a community where people compete not to get ahead, but to lift each other up. Where everyone is looking for ways to celebrate others rather than themselves. That is the picture Paul paints. It runs against our natural instinct to seek our own recognition. But it is exactly how Christ treated us. He humbled Himself and put our good above His own. Today, look for a way to honour someone else. Give credit, offer praise, step back so another can shine. Let warm affection guide the way you treat everyone you meet.

But in practice, both halves can be hard. Rejoicing with others can be difficult when their good news highlights our own disappointments. A friend gets the job, the engagement, the healing, the open door we have been praying for. Celebrating sincerely takes humility. Weeping with others can be hard too. It is uncomfortable to sit with someone's pain. We want to fix it, explain it, or move past it quickly. But often what grieving people need most is presence. Someone who will simply weep with them. Love enters into another person's experience. It does not stand at a distance. Today, look around you. Is someone celebrating? Rejoice with them wholeheartedly. Is someone hurting? Sit with them. Let your heart move with theirs.

== Feb 13
R: Paul's famous description of love begins with two qualities: 'Charity suffereth long, and is kind.' The word charity in the King James Version means self-giving love. Suffering long means being patient, especially with people. It means putting up with weaknesses, delays, and irritations without giving up on someone. Kindness is the active side of that patience. It is not merely tolerating others but doing them good. Paul then lists what love does not do. It does not envy. It does not boast. It is not puffed up with pride. These are the habits that quietly poison relationships. Envy resents others' blessings. Boasting draws attention to ourselves. Pride looks down on others. Love lets all of that go. It is patient, kind, content, and humble. No one does this perfectly. But God's love for us is exactly like this, and His Spirit can grow it in us. Today, choose patience with someone who tests you. Then go further and be kind.
S: Paul describes love as patient and kind, without envy, boasting, or pride. These qualities heal relationships while their opposites poison them. Choose patience and active kindness toward someone today.

== Feb 14
R: Words have power. They can calm a storm or start one. Proverbs puts it plainly: 'A soft answer turneth away wrath: but grievous words stir up anger.' We have all experienced both. A tense conversation suddenly cools because someone responds gently. Or a minor disagreement explodes because someone answers harshly. The difference is often a single sentence. A soft answer does not mean a weak answer. It is not agreeing with everything or avoiding the truth. It means speaking with gentleness, calm, and respect, even when the other person is upset. It takes strength to answer softly when we are criticized or misunderstood. Our instinct is to defend ourselves or hit back. But gentle words have a remarkable ability to defuse anger. Today, pay attention to your tone, especially with the people closest to you. When tension rises, take a breath and choose a soft answer. You may be surprised at how quickly the heat goes out of the room.
S: Proverbs teaches that a gentle answer turns away anger, while harsh words stir it up. Answering softly takes strength, not weakness. Choose a gentle response today when tension rises.

== Feb 15
R: Paul's description of love continues with qualities that touch everyday life: love 'Doth not behave itself unseemly, seeketh not her own, is not easily provoked, thinketh no evil.' Love is not rude. It does not demand its own way. It is not touchy or quick to take offence. And it thinks no evil, which means it does not keep a record of wrongs or assume the worst. Think about how much conflict comes from these very things. We act rudely when we are tired. We insist on our way. We are easily irritated. We keep score of every offence and bring up old hurts. Love chooses a different path. It lets small things go. It gives the benefit of the doubt. It assumes good motives rather than bad. None of us love like this by nature, but it is how God loves us. He does not keep a ledger of our failures. Today, release a grudge. Choose to think the best of someone.
S: Paul writes that love is not rude, selfish, easily provoked, or quick to assume evil. It does not keep a record of wrongs. Let go of a grudge today and choose to think the best of someone.

It makes room for their weaknesses. Paul pairs this with lowliness and meekness, because forbearing is easier when we remember our own flaws. Others are putting up with us too. Longsuffering adds endurance to the mix. This is not a one-time effort but a daily choice. Today, think of someone who tests your patience. In Paul's day, fathers had almost total power over their children. Paul limits that power with love. Parents are not to exasperate their children with harshness, unfair demands, constant criticism, or inconsistency. Instead, they are to nurture them. This verse speaks to anyone who has authority over others, whether at home, at work, or in a community. Authority is given to build people up, not to crush them. Today, if you lead others in any way, ask whether your words and actions nurture or provoke. Lead the way God leads you: firmly, patiently, and with love.

R: Proverbs 31 paints a portrait of a capable, godly woman. She works hard, manages her household wisely, cares for the poor, and is respected in her community. Among all her qualities, this one stands out: 'She openeth her mouth with wisdom; and in her tongue is the law of kindness.' Her words are governed by kindness. It is not an occasional habit but a law, a settled rule of her speech. She speaks with wisdom, but never harshly. Her words build up rather than tear down. This is a beautiful goal for anyone. Our homes are shaped by the words spoken in them. Kind words create safety and warmth. Harsh words create fear and distance.
