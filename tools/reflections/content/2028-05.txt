# May 2028: God's Word & Obedience

== May 01
R: In the ancient world, travelling at night was dangerous. Paths were uneven, and there were no streetlights. A traveller might carry a small oil lamp, which cast just enough light to see the next step or two. That is the picture behind this verse: 'Thy word is a lamp unto my feet, and a light unto my path.' Notice what it promises and what it does not. God's Word does not usually light up the whole road for miles ahead. It shows us enough to take the next faithful step. That can be frustrating when we want to see everything at once. But it keeps us close to the One who holds the lamp. This month we will think about God's Word and what it means to obey it. Begin by thanking God that you are not left in the dark. His Word lights the way. Open it today and let it show you your next step.

== May 02
R: Where do you keep your most valuable things? Probably somewhere safe, close at hand, protected from loss. The psalmist treated God's Word that way: 'Thy word have I hid in mine heart, that I might not sin against thee.' To hide God's Word in the heart means more than reading it. It means memorizing it, meditating on it, and treasuring it so deeply that it becomes part of us. And notice the purpose: that I might not sin against thee. Scripture stored in the heart is protection. When temptation comes, there is often no time to look up a verse. But words already hidden in the heart rise up when we need them. Jesus Himself answered temptation in the wilderness by quoting Scripture from memory. Memorizing verses can feel difficult, especially as we get older. But even one verse a week adds up over time. Choose a verse today and begin hiding it in your heart. It will be there when you need it.
S: The psalmist hid God's Word in his heart so that he would not sin against God. Scripture stored in the heart rises up to protect us when temptation comes. Choose a verse today and begin memorizing it.

== May 03
R: Moses had died, and Joshua now faced the enormous task of leading Israel into the Promised Land. God gave him many promises, and one clear instruction: 'This book of the law shall not depart out of thy mouth; but thou shalt meditate therein day and night, that thou mayest observe to do according to all that is written therein.' Before the battles, before the strategy, God told Joshua to stay in His Word. Meditating day and night means keeping Scripture constantly in mind, turning it over, speaking it, and applying it. And the purpose is obedience: that thou mayest observe to do. Meditation is not just thinking about God's Word. It leads to doing it. The verse ends with a promise: then thou shalt make thy way prosperous, and then thou shalt have good success. Real success, in God's eyes, flows from a life shaped by His Word. Whatever challenges you face today, start where Joshua started. Let God's Word fill your mouth and your mind.
S: As Joshua prepared to lead Israel, God told him to meditate on His Word day and night and obey it. True success flows from a life shaped by Scripture. Start today's challenges by letting God's Word fill your mind.

== May 04
R: Psalm 1 opens the whole book of Psalms with a picture of the blessed person. After describing what this person avoids, the psalm reveals what they love: 'But his delight is in the law of the LORD; and in his law doth he meditate day and night.' Notice the word delight. This person does not read God's Word out of mere duty. They enjoy it. It is not a chore to be checked off but a treasure to be savoured. Delight and meditation go together. We think about what we love. If you love someone, your mind returns to them throughout the day. The same is true with God's Word. When we delight in it, we find ourselves thinking about it at odd moments, in the car, in the kitchen, before we fall asleep. If Scripture feels dry to you right now, ask God to give you delight in it. Read slowly. Look for His character on every page. Delight often grows as we linger.
S: Psalm 1 describes the blessed person as one who delights in God's law and meditates on it day and night. When we love God's Word, our minds return to it often. Ask God to grow your delight in Scripture today.

== May 05
R: Psalm 1 continues with a picture of what happens to the person who delights in God's Word: 'And he shall be like a tree planted by the rivers of water, that bringeth forth his fruit in his season; his leaf also shall not wither.' The tree did not plant itself. It was planted, placed there with purpose. And its location makes all the difference. By the rivers, it has a constant supply of water. Its fruit comes in its season. Not all at once, not on demand, but at the right time. Growth in the Christian life works like this. We are planted by God, rooted in His Word, and nourished day by day. Fruit comes gradually and in season. Some seasons are for growing roots, others for bearing fruit. The person rooted in God's Word does not wither when hard times come. Their leaf stays green. If you feel like you are not producing much fruit right now, keep your roots in the river. In His season, fruit will come.
S: Psalm 1 compares the person who delights in God's Word to a tree planted by rivers, bearing fruit in season and never withering. Growth comes gradually as we stay rooted in Scripture. Keep your roots in His Word today and trust Him for fruit in season.

== May 06
R: Paul wrote his second letter to Timothy near the end of his life. He knew he would soon be gone, and he wanted to leave Timothy with what mattered most. One thing was confidence in Scripture: 'All scripture is given by inspiration of God, and is profitable for doctrine, for reproof, for correction, for instruction in righteousness.' Given by inspiration of God means God-breathed. Scripture is not merely human wisdom about God. It comes from God Himself. And because it comes from Him, it is profitable, useful in four ways. Doctrine teaches us what is true. Reproof shows us where we have gone wrong. Correction shows us how to get back on track. Instruction in righteousness trains us to keep walking rightly. Scripture is not only for information. It is for transformation. The very next verse says its goal is to make us complete, thoroughly furnished for every good work. Approach your Bible today knowing that God breathed it. Let it teach, correct, and train you.
S: Paul reminded Timothy that all Scripture is breathed out by God and useful for teaching, reproof, correction, and training in righteousness. Scripture is given not only to inform us but to transform us. Approach your Bible today knowing God breathed it.

== May 07
R: The Bible is not like any other book. Hebrews describes it this way: 'For the word of God is quick, and powerful, and sharper than any twoedged sword.' Quick means living. God's Word is not a dusty record of ancient history. It is alive and active. It speaks into our lives today. It is powerful, able to do what it says. And it is sharper than any sword. A double-edged sword cuts deeply and precisely. God's Word cuts through our excuses and reaches the hidden places of the heart. The verse goes on to say it is 'a discerner of the thoughts and intents of the heart.' Scripture reads us even as we read it. It exposes motives we did not know we had. That can be uncomfortable. But it is the kind of surgery that brings healing. When you open your Bible today, come expecting it to speak. Let it search you. Allow its living words to reach the places that need God's touch.

== May 08
R: Isaiah contrasts two things: the passing beauty of creation and the permanence of God's Word. 'The grass withereth, the flower fadeth: but the word of our God shall stand for ever.' A field of wildflowers can be breathtaking. But within weeks the blooms fade and the grass turns brown. Isaiah says human glory is like that. Kingdoms rise and fall. Fame fades. Even the strongest among us grow old. Ideas that seem permanent in one generation are forgotten in the next. But God's Word stands. It does not wither or fade. It is as true today as when it was first spoken, and it will still be true when everything else has passed. This gives us something firm to build on. In a world of constant change, opinions, and trends, we have a foundation that will not shift. If you feel unsettled by how quickly things change, anchor yourself in what endures. Read God's Word today. It will stand when everything else falls.
S: Isaiah contrasts fading grass and flowers with the eternal Word of God. Human glory passes, but God's Word stands for ever. Anchor yourself today in the Word that never changes.

== May 09
R: After forty days of fasting in the wilderness, Jesus was hungry. The tempter came and suggested He turn stones into bread. It seemed like a small thing. He was hungry, and He had the power. But Jesus answered, 'It is written, Man shall not live by bread alone, but by every word that proceedeth out of the mouth of God.' Jesus was quoting Deuteronomy, where Moses reminded Israel how God had fed them with manna in the wilderness. The lesson then and now is the same. Physical food sustains the body, but we are more than bodies. Our souls need nourishment too, and that comes from God's Word. We are careful to eat every day. We would never go a week without food. Yet many of us go days without feeding our souls. Jesus shows us that God's Word is not optional. It is our daily bread. Make sure your soul eats today. Open the Scriptures and let God feed you.

== May 10
R: It is possible to hear a great message, nod in agreement, and walk away unchanged. James warns against exactly that: 'But be ye doers of the word, and not hearers only, deceiving your own selves.' Hearing is good. We need to hear God's Word. But hearing alone can trick us into thinking we are growing when we are not. James compares it to someone who looks in a mirror, sees a smudge on their face, and walks away without washing it. What was the point of looking? God's Word is meant to be done. It shows us what to change, and then it calls us to act. The deception James warns about is subtle. We can feel spiritual because we know a lot of verses or listen to a lot of teaching. But knowledge without obedience is self-deception. The real test is what we do. Think about something God has shown you recently in His Word. Have you acted on it? Take one step of obedience today.

To call Jesus Lord is to say He has authority over our lives. Yet it is possible to say the word without living it. We can use the right language, sing the right songs, and still go our own way when it comes to everyday choices. Jesus noticed that gap and named it. His question is not meant to condemn but to awaken. It invites honest self-examination. Where are we calling Him Lord with our lips while ignoring Him with our lives? Perhaps in how we handle money, how we speak about others, how we use our time, or how we treat people who are difficult. Calling Jesus Lord is easy. Following Him as Lord is harder, but far better. Let His question search you today. Then bring your actions into line with your words.

'Therefore whosoever heareth these sayings of mine, and doeth them, I will liken him unto a wise man, which built his house upon a rock.' The wise builder did two things: he heard and he did. The foolish builder heard but did not do. From the outside, the two houses may have looked the same on sunny days. But the storm revealed the difference. Storms come to everyone. Rain, floods, and winds beat on every life. What matters is what lies underneath. Obedience to Jesus' words is like digging down to bedrock. It takes effort, and much of it is unseen. But when the storm hits, it holds. Look at your foundation today. Are you building on hearing alone, or on hearing and doing?

== May 13
R: Jesus spoke these words to His closest friends: 'If ye love me, keep my commandments.' He connected love and obedience in a single sentence. Love for Jesus is not just a warm feeling. It shows itself in how we live. And obedience is not cold rule-keeping. It flows from love. Think about how love works in human relationships. If you love someone, you want to please them. You care about what matters to them. You listen to their wishes and honour them. The same is true with Jesus. When we love Him, we want to do what He says, not out of fear, but out of affection. This also means obedience is not a way of earning His love. We already have it. We obey because we are loved and because we love Him in return. If obedience feels like a burden, the answer may be to spend time with Jesus and let your love for Him grow. Love makes obedience a joy rather than a weight.

When Samuel confronted him, Saul claimed he had saved them to sacrifice to God. Samuel's answer cut through the excuse: 'Behold, to obey is better than sacrifice, and to hearken than the fat of rams.' Saul thought he could substitute a religious gesture for obedience. We can fall into the same trap. We may give money, serve at events, or sing loudly, while ignoring what God has clearly told us to do. Those offerings cannot replace obedience. God is not looking for impressive gestures. He is looking for listening hearts. What has He asked of you? Doing that is better than any sacrifice.
