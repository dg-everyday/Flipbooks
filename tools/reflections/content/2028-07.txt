== Jul 01
R: The people of Jerusalem had gathered to hear God's law read aloud. As Ezra read and the Levites explained, the people understood how far they had fallen short, and they began to weep. But Nehemiah stopped them. He told them to go and celebrate, to eat and drink and share with those who had nothing. Then he gave the reason: 'neither be ye sorry; for the joy of the LORD is your strength.' There is a time for sorrow over sin. But God did not want His people to stay in grief. Having heard His Word, they were to rejoice in His goodness. The joy of the Lord is not shallow happiness. It is a deep gladness rooted in who God is and what He has done. And it is strength. Joy gives us energy to keep going, courage to face hard days, and resilience when trouble comes. This month we will think about joy, praise, and worship. Begin by letting God's joy become your strength.

== Jul 02
R: Where is joy found? People search for it in many places: possessions, relationships, achievements, travel, and entertainment. Some of these bring moments of happiness. But David discovered where full joy lives: 'Thou wilt shew me the path of life: in thy presence is fulness of joy; at thy right hand there are pleasures for evermore.' Fulness of joy. Not partial, not temporary, but complete. And it is found in God's presence. The pleasures at His right hand are for evermore. They do not fade or disappoint. Every other joy is a small stream. God Himself is the ocean. This does not mean earthly joys are wrong. They are gifts from Him. But they were never meant to carry the full weight of our happiness. Only God can do that. If you have been chasing joy and finding it slipping away, turn toward His presence. Spend time with Him. Let Him show you the path of life. Full joy is waiting there.

== Jul 03
R: Psalm 100 is a short song of praise, and it gives a surprising instruction about serving God: 'Serve the LORD with gladness: come before his presence with singing.' Many people think of serving God as a heavy duty, something we do out of obligation. But the psalmist says to serve with gladness. Joy and service belong together. Why? Because we serve a good God who has been kind to us. We serve out of gratitude, not fear. We serve because we love Him. And our service is not to a harsh master but to a loving Father. The psalm continues by reminding us that He made us, we are His people, and the sheep of His pasture. That is reason for gladness. Serving with gladness changes everything. It turns chores into worship and duties into delights. Whatever work is in front of you today, whether at home, at work, or in your community, do it gladly as service to the Lord. Let joy fill your work.

== Jul 04
R: Psalm 100 continues with directions for approaching God: 'Enter into his gates with thanksgiving, and into his courts with praise: be thankful unto him, and bless his name.' The picture is of worshippers coming to the temple, passing through the gates and into the courts. They did not walk in casually. They came with thanks and praise on their lips. Thanksgiving and praise are the doorway into God's presence. When we begin with gratitude, our hearts open. We remember His goodness. Our worries shrink in the light of His faithfulness. Many of us approach God with a list of requests. That is not wrong. He invites us to bring our needs. But this psalm teaches us to begin with thanks. Before you ask for anything today, thank Him for something. Then praise Him for who He is. You may find that your requests look different after you have entered through the gates of thanksgiving. Bless His name today.

R: This verse is often sung, often quoted, and easy to say. But its original setting gives it depth. Psalm 118 celebrates God's rescue. The psalmist had been surrounded by enemies and pushed hard, nearly falling. The stone the builders rejected had become the cornerstone. In other words, what people threw away, God made central. Then comes the joyful declaration: 'This is the day which the LORD hath made; we will rejoice and be glad in it.' Every day is a day the Lord has made. Not just the good ones. Not just the days when everything goes right. This day, the one you are living right now, is His. He made it, and He is present in it. Rejoicing in it is a choice. It does not depend on everything being perfect. It depends on recognizing whose day it is. Whatever today holds, it belongs to God. Choose to rejoice and be glad in it.

== Jul 06
R: Paul wrote the letter to the Philippians from prison. He did not know whether he would live or die. Yet joy runs through the whole letter, and here he says it twice: 'Rejoice in the Lord alway: and again I say, Rejoice.' Paul repeats it because he knows it is hard to believe. Rejoice always? Even in prison? Even in uncertainty? Yes, because the joy he speaks of is in the Lord. It is not joy in circumstances. Circumstances change. Joy in the Lord does not. Paul could not rejoice in his chains, but he could rejoice in Christ. He could rejoice that the gospel was spreading, that God was faithful, and that nothing could separate him from God's love. This kind of joy is a decision and a discipline. It turns our attention from what is wrong to who God is. What would it look like for you to rejoice in the Lord today, even if your circumstances are hard? Paul says it again: rejoice.

== Jul 07
R: This is one of the shortest verses in the Bible. Just two words: 'Rejoice evermore.' It comes in a list of brief instructions near the end of Paul's first letter to the Thessalonians. Pray without ceasing. In every thing give thanks. Quench not the Spirit. Each command is short, but each one reaches into every part of life. Evermore means always, continually, without stopping. It is not occasional joy, saved for special events. It is a steady undercurrent of gladness that runs beneath everything. How is that possible? Not by pretending. Not by forcing a smile. It comes from remembering what God has done and who He is, again and again. The Thessalonian believers were facing persecution. Paul was not writing to people with easy lives. Yet he called them to rejoice evermore, because their joy was anchored in Christ. Today, let joy be your background music. Return to it throughout the day. Rejoice evermore.
S: Paul's short command to rejoice evermore calls for a steady undercurrent of gladness in every part of life. This joy comes from remembering who God is, not from easy circumstances. Return to joy again and again throughout today.

== Jul 08
R: David wrote Psalm 34 after a frightening escape. He had pretended to be insane before a Philistine king to save his life. It was not a proud moment. Yet when he was safe, he did not dwell on his fear or embarrassment. He began with praise: 'I will bless the LORD at all times: his praise shall continually be in my mouth.' At all times. In good times and hard times. When things go well and when they fall apart. David decided that praise would be his constant response. Continually in my mouth. It would not be occasional but habitual. This is a choice that shapes a life. Complaining can become a habit, and so can praise. What fills our mouths often fills our hearts. When we make a habit of blessing God, we begin to see His goodness everywhere. Today, try to make praise continual. Thank Him for small things. Bless Him in ordinary moments. Let His praise be in your mouth all day.

== Jul 09
R: Praise is personal, but it is also meant to be shared. David followed his own declaration of praise with an invitation: 'O magnify the LORD with me, and let us exalt his name together.' To magnify means to make large. A magnifying glass does not make something bigger than it is. It helps us see how big it really is. When we magnify the Lord, we are not adding to His greatness. We are helping ourselves and others see it more clearly. And David wants company. Let us exalt his name together. There is something powerful about shared praise. When we worship alongside others, our faith is strengthened. We hear what God has done in their lives. We are reminded that we are not alone. Joy multiplies when it is shared. Today, invite someone to magnify the Lord with you. Share something God has done. Ask what He has done for them. Exalt His name together.

Not necessarily a polished performance, but a glad sound. God is not looking for perfect voices. He delights in joyful hearts. Many people feel shy about singing. But this psalm is not about talent. It is about gladness overflowing into sound. And notice the reason for the joy: the rock of our salvation. A rock is solid, unshakable, and dependable. Our salvation rests on God, who does not move or change. That is worth making noise about. Throughout Scripture, God's people sang. They sang at the Red Sea, in the temple, in prison cells, and on the road. Singing lifts the heart and focuses the mind on God. Today, whether in the car, at home, or under your breath, sing to the Lord. Make a joyful noise to the Rock of your salvation.

== Jul 11
R: The first verses of Psalm 95 are loud and joyful, with singing and shouting. But then the tone shifts: 'O come, let us worship and bow down: let us kneel before the LORD our maker.' Worship has more than one posture. Sometimes it is exuberant celebration. Sometimes it is quiet reverence. Bowing and kneeling are postures of humility. They acknowledge that God is great and we are small. He is the Maker, and we are what He made. Kneeling before Him is not humiliation. It is recognition of who He truly is. There is freedom in this. When we bow before God, we stop trying to be in control. We release the burden of running the universe. We rest in His authority. Whether or not you physically kneel, you can bow your heart today. Take a quiet moment to acknowledge His greatness. Let reverence fill your heart. He is your Maker, and He is worthy of your worship.
S: Psalm 95 moves from joyful singing to quiet reverence, inviting us to bow and kneel before the Lord our Maker. Bowing acknowledges His greatness and releases our need for control. Take a quiet moment today to bow your heart before Him.

== Jul 12
R: Psalm 96 calls the whole earth to praise God. In the middle of it is this invitation: 'O worship the LORD in the beauty of holiness: fear before him, all the earth.' The beauty of holiness. Holiness is not dull or grim. It is beautiful. God's holiness, His perfect purity and goodness, is the most beautiful thing in existence. And when we worship Him, we are drawn into that beauty. Some think of holiness as a list of rules. But true holiness is the radiant goodness of God's character. It is love without selfishness, truth without falsehood, and justice without corruption. When we glimpse it, we are moved to worship. The verse also says, fear before him. This is not terror but awe, the trembling wonder of standing before Someone infinitely greater. Beauty and awe belong together in worship. Today, spend a few moments reflecting on God's holiness. Let its beauty draw you to worship.

Sometimes our souls need to be told what to do. Our emotions may be flat. Our minds may be distracted. Our hearts may feel heavy. So David commands his soul to praise. This is not hypocrisy. It is leadership of oneself. And he wants all of himself involved: all that is within me. Not just his lips, but his mind, his heart, his emotions, and his will. Whole-person worship. Many people wait until they feel like praising God. But feelings often follow actions. When we choose to bless the Lord, our hearts often warm along the way. Today, if your soul feels sluggish, speak to it as David did. Tell your soul to bless the Lord. Then bring everything within you into His praise.

R: David continued his instruction to his soul: 'Bless the LORD, O my soul, and forget not all his benefits.' Forgetfulness is one of the great enemies of praise. We are quick to remember our problems and slow to remember God's blessings. A single bad day can erase the memory of a hundred good ones. So David reminds himself to remember. And then he lists the benefits: God forgives all our iniquities, heals our diseases, redeems our life from destruction, crowns us with lovingkindness and tender mercies, and satisfies our mouths with good things. Each one is a reason for praise. One helpful practice is to make your own list. Write down the ways God has blessed you. Forgiveness, provision, protection, answered prayers, people He has placed in your life, and moments when He showed up. When you see them written out, praise comes more easily. Today, forget not. Count His benefits, and bless Him for each one.

R: Jesus was talking with a Samaritan woman at a well. She raised a long-standing debate: where should people worship? Samaritans said on their mountain. Jews said in Jerusalem. Jesus answered with something deeper: 'God is a Spirit: and they that worship him must worship him in spirit and in truth.' True worship is not limited to a particular place. Because God is Spirit, He can be worshipped anywhere. What matters is not the location but the heart. In spirit means from the inside, with genuine heart engagement, not just outward forms. In truth means according to who God really is, as He has revealed Himself. Both are needed. Spirit without truth can become emotion without substance. Truth without spirit can become cold and empty. Jesus invites us to worship with both our hearts and our minds fully engaged. You can worship God today wherever you are, in a kitchen, an office, or a hospital room. Bring Him your heart, and bring Him the truth.

R: The Israelites had just walked through the Red Sea on dry ground. They watched the waters crash down on Pharaoh's army. They were free. And the first thing they did was sing.
