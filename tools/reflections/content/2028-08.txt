# August 2028: Generosity & Good Works

== Aug 01
R: Paul was saying goodbye to the leaders of the church in Ephesus. He knew he would likely never see them again. As he reminded them how he had lived among them, working with his own hands to support himself and others, he quoted words of Jesus not recorded anywhere else: 'It is more blessed to give than to receive.' Our culture often teaches the opposite. Happiness is found in getting more. But Jesus turns that upside down. Real blessing is found in giving. Those who have experienced generosity know this is true. There is a joy in giving that receiving cannot match. It frees us from the grip of possessions. It connects us to others. It reflects the heart of God, the greatest Giver of all. Paul also mentions supporting the weak. Giving is not just about money. It includes time, attention, help, and care for those who cannot repay. This month we will think about generosity and good works. Begin by discovering the blessing of giving.
S: Paul reminded the Ephesian leaders of Jesus' words that it is more blessed to give than to receive. Real blessing is found not in getting more but in giving, especially to the weak. Discover the joy of giving in some way today.
P: The happiest people are often the most generous. Acts 20:35 reminds us of Jesus' words, 'It is more blessed to give than to receive.' Look for a way to give today.

== Aug 02
R: Paul was collecting a gift for poor believers in Jerusalem, and he wrote to the Corinthians about how to give. His instruction was clear: 'Every man according as he purposeth in his heart, so let him give; not grudgingly, or of necessity: for God loveth a cheerful giver.' Notice three things. First, giving is personal: as he purposeth in his heart. It is a decision each person makes before God. Second, it should not be grudging or forced. Giving that comes from pressure or guilt misses the point. Third, God loves a cheerful giver. The word cheerful has the sense of glad and joyful. God is not mainly interested in the size of our gifts. He cares about the heart behind them. A small gift given gladly delights Him more than a large one given with a frown. Cheerful giving comes from understanding how much God has given us. When we remember His generosity, our own giving becomes a joy. Give gladly today.
S: Paul taught that each person should give as they decide in their heart, not grudgingly or under pressure, because God loves a cheerful giver. God cares more about the heart than the size of the gift. Give gladly today, remembering how generous He has been to you.
P: God loves a glad giver. 2 Corinthians 9:7 says, 'so let him give; not grudgingly, or of necessity: for God loveth a cheerful giver.' Let your giving come from a joyful heart today.

== Aug 03
R: Every farmer knows a basic truth. If you plant a few seeds, you get a small harvest. If you plant many, you get a large one. Paul applies that principle to generosity: 'He which soweth sparingly shall reap also sparingly; and he which soweth bountifully shall reap also bountifully.' This is not a formula for getting rich. Paul is not saying that if you give money, God will automatically give you more money. The harvest he describes is broader. A few verses later, he says God will make all grace abound toward you, so that you will have enough for every good work. The return on generosity includes joy, gratitude, deeper faith, strengthened relationships, and the ability to give even more. Generous people tend to live generous lives in every way. Stingy people tend to live small lives. What kind of sowing are you doing? Not only with money, but with kindness, time, and encouragement. Sow bountifully today.
S: Paul compares generosity to sowing seed: those who sow sparingly reap sparingly, and those who sow bountifully reap bountifully. The harvest includes grace, joy, and the ability to give more. Sow generously today with your time, kindness, and resources.
P: What are you sowing? 2 Corinthians 9:6 says, 'he which soweth bountifully shall reap also bountifully.' Be generous with your time, kindness, and resources today.

== Aug 04
R: Jesus used a picture from the marketplace. When buying grain, a generous seller would fill the container, press it down to make room, shake it to settle it, and keep pouring until it ran over the top. Jesus said that is how giving comes back to us: 'Give, and it shall be given unto you; good measure, pressed down, and shaken together, and running over.' Then He added a principle: 'For with the same measure that ye mete withal it shall be measured to you again.' This verse comes right after Jesus' teaching on judging and forgiving. So it applies to more than money. The measure we use for mercy, forgiveness, kindness, and generosity tends to return to us. Those who are generous with grace find grace overflowing in their lives. Those who are stingy with others often find life feels stingy too. God is not stingy. He gives in overflowing measure. Today, let your measure toward others be generous, pressed down, and running over.
S: Jesus described giving that returns in overflowing measure, pressed down and running over, and said the measure we use will be measured back to us. This applies to mercy and kindness as well as money. Be generous in every way today.
P: Overflowing generosity. Luke 6:38 says, 'Give, and it shall be given unto you; good measure, pressed down, and shaken together, and running over.' Let your measure be generous today.

== Aug 05
R: Proverbs gives a picture of refreshment: 'The liberal soul shall be made fat: and he that watereth shall be watered also himself.' Liberal here means generous. To be made fat in that culture meant to prosper, to be well nourished and satisfied. The generous person is not drained by giving. They are enriched. And the second half paints a lovely image. The one who waters others will himself be watered. Imagine someone carrying water to thirsty plants on a hot day. As they work, they are splashed and cooled themselves. Refreshing others refreshes us. Many people discover this. When they are feeling low, helping someone else lifts their own spirits. When they are worried about their own needs, giving to others reminds them that God provides. Generosity breaks the grip of self-focus. If you feel dry today, try watering someone else. Encourage them, help them, or meet a need. You may find yourself refreshed in the process.

== Aug 06
R: Proverbs makes an astonishing claim about helping the poor: 'He that hath pity upon the poor lendeth unto the LORD; and that which he hath given will he pay him again.' When we help someone in need, we are not just giving to them. We are lending to God. The Creator of the universe takes it personally. He considers Himself indebted. And God always repays His debts. This changes how we see generosity toward the poor. It is not wasted. It is not lost. It is an investment in God's own hands. And He is the most faithful debtor there is. His repayment may not always come in the form we expect. It may come as provision, peace, joy, or eternal reward. But it will come. Jesus later said something similar: whatever we do for the least of these, we do for Him. Today, look for someone in need. Show them compassion. And know that you are lending to the Lord.
S: Proverbs teaches that showing pity to the poor is lending to the Lord, and He will repay. God takes our kindness to the needy personally. Show compassion to someone in need today, knowing it is never wasted.

== Aug 07
R: In ancient Israel, the first part of every harvest was set aside for God. Before the farmer used any of the crop for himself, he brought the firstfruits as an offering. Proverbs teaches this principle: 'Honour the LORD with thy substance, and with the firstfruits of all thine increase.' Firstfruits means the first and best, not the leftovers. Giving God our firstfruits is a statement of trust. It says, God comes first, and I believe He will provide for the rest. It is easy to give God what is left over after everything else is paid. But that often means very little is left. Putting Him first changes the order of our priorities. This principle applies beyond money. We can give God the first part of our day in prayer, the first moments of a decision in seeking His will, and our best energy in serving Him. Today, think about what it would look like to give God your firstfruits. Honour Him with your best.
S: Proverbs calls us to honour the Lord with our substance and the firstfruits of our increase. Giving God the first and best, not the leftovers, is a statement of trust. Consider today how you can honour God with your best.

And notice the phrase: when it is in the power of thine hand. If you can do good now, do it. Is there something good you have been meaning to do? A payment, a thank-you, a visit, or a helping hand? == Aug 09
R: In Malachi's day, God's people had become careless in their giving. They were holding back what they owed, and the work of God's house suffered. So God issued a remarkable challenge: 'prove me now herewith, saith the LORD of hosts, if I will not open you the windows of heaven, and pour you out a blessing, that there shall not be room enough to receive it.' This is one of the few places in Scripture where God invites His people to test Him. Prove me. Try Me. See if I will not be faithful. God was not asking for their money because He needed it. He was inviting them to trust Him. Holding back came from fear that there would not be enough. God answered that fear with a promise of overflowing blessing. Generosity is ultimately a matter of trust. Do we believe God will provide if we give? The windows of heaven are not opened by stinginess. Trust Him with your giving today.

== Aug 10
R: Jesus sat near the temple treasury, watching people give. Many wealthy people put in large amounts. Then a poor widow came and dropped in two tiny copper coins, worth almost nothing. Jesus called His disciples over and said, 'Verily I say unto you, That this poor widow hath cast more in, than all they which have cast into the treasury.' How could two small coins be more than all the large gifts? Jesus explained: the rich gave out of their abundance, but she gave out of her poverty. She gave all she had to live on. God does not measure gifts by their size. He measures them by their sacrifice and the heart behind them. The rich gave what they would not miss. The widow gave what she could not spare. This story encourages anyone who feels their gift is too small to matter. In God's eyes, it may be the greatest gift of all. Give what you can today, with your whole heart.
S: Jesus said a poor widow's two small coins were more than all the large gifts, because she gave all she had. God measures gifts by sacrifice and heart, not size. Give what you can today with your whole heart.

== Aug 11
R: In Jesus' day, some people made a show of their giving. They wanted everyone to see how generous they were. Jesus said they already had their reward: the applause of people. Then He gave a different instruction: 'But when thou doest alms, let not thy left hand know what thy right hand doeth.' It is a vivid picture. Give so quietly that even your other hand does not notice. Do not keep a mental record of your generosity. Do not draw attention to it. Simply give and move on. Why? Because the motive matters. If we give to be seen, we have already received what we wanted. But if we give quietly, out of love for God and others, our Father who sees in secret will reward us. Social media can make this harder. It is tempting to share every good deed. But there is a special joy in doing good that no one else knows about. Today, do something generous in secret. Let it be just between you and God.
S: Jesus taught His followers to give so quietly that the left hand does not know what the right hand is doing. Giving to be seen receives only human applause, but secret giving is rewarded by God. Do something generous in secret today.

== Aug 12
R: Just a few chapters before warning against showy giving, Jesus said something that may seem to contradict it: 'Let your light so shine before men, that they may see your good works, and glorify your Father which is in heaven.' Should our good works be hidden or seen? The answer is in the motive and the goal. When we give to be praised ourselves, Jesus says hide it. But our lives as a whole should shine in a way that points people to God. The difference is where the glory goes. Showy giving draws attention to us. Shining light draws attention to the Father. A light is not meant to be admired for itself. It is meant to help people see. When our kindness, honesty, and generosity are visible in daily life, others notice. And when done with humility, they may wonder where it comes from. That is when they glorify God. Today, let your light shine through ordinary good works, and let the glory go to Him.
S: Jesus told His followers to let their light shine so others would see their good works and glorify the Father. Our lives should point people to God, not to ourselves. Let your light shine through ordinary good works today, and give Him the glory.

To those on His right, He says, I was hungry and you fed me, thirsty and you gave me drink, a stranger and you took me in. They are puzzled. When did we do that? And the King answers, 'Inasmuch as ye have done it unto one of the least of these my brethren, ye have done it unto me.' Jesus identifies with the least. The hungry, the thirsty, the stranger, the sick, and the prisoner. When we serve them, we serve Him. This changes how we see every person in need. The homeless man, the lonely neighbour, the struggling family, the person in the hospital bed. In serving them, we are serving Christ. Notice that the righteous did not even realize it. Their kindness was not calculated. It was simply who they had become. Look for the least today. Serve them as if you are serving Jesus. Because you are.

Then he adds this: 'For we are his workmanship, created in Christ Jesus unto good works, which God hath before ordained that we should walk in them.' Good works are not the root of salvation. And He designed you with a purpose. Even more, He has prepared good works in advance for you to do. Before you were born, God had good things in mind for you to accomplish. That is a remarkable thought. Your life is not random. There are specific good works with your name on them, waiting for you to walk in them. Today, ask God to show you the good works He has prepared for you. Then step into them with joy.

After summarizing the gospel of grace, he said, 'these things I will that thou affirm constantly, that they which have believed in God might be careful to maintain good works.' Careful to maintain. The phrase suggests giving attention, taking thought, being intentional. Good works do not happen automatically. They need thought and planning. They make a real difference in people's lives. Notice the connection: those who have believed in God. Faith leads to action. Those who truly trust God become people who do good. It is not optional decoration on the Christian life. It is part of its fabric. Today, be careful to maintain good works. Think ahead about how you can bless someone, and then follow through.

Like a dressmaker's pattern or a craftsman's template, it shows others what to do. Titus was a leader, and people would be watching him. His life needed to match his teaching. Paul adds that Titus should show uncorruptness, gravity, and sincerity in his teaching, meaning purity, seriousness, and genuineness. Every one of us is a pattern for someone. Whether we realize it or not, we are showing people what faith looks like. That is a sobering thought, but also an opportunity. Our good works can inspire others to do the same. Today, think about who might be watching you. Live in a way that you would be glad for them to copy. Be a pattern of good works.

R: Paul gave Timothy a message for wealthy believers: 'Charge them that are rich in this world, that they be not highminded, nor trust in uncertain riches, but in the living God, who giveth us richly all things to enjoy.' Notice that Paul does not condemn wealth itself. He warns about two dangers that come with it. First, pride: being highminded, thinking we are better because we have more. Second, misplaced trust: relying on riches for security. Riches are uncertain. Markets change. Possessions break. Savings can disappear. But the living God is certain. And notice how Paul describes Him: who giveth us richly all things to enjoy. God is not stingy. He gives generously and wants us to enjoy His gifts. The point is not to despise what we have, but to hold it with open hands and keep our trust in the Giver, not the gifts. By global standards, many of us are rich in this world. Let this charge speak to you today.

Rather than seeing this as a reason to give up, Moses saw it as a reason for constant generosity: 'therefore I command thee, saying, Thou shalt open thine hand wide unto thy brother, to thy poor, and to thy needy, in thy land.' Moses mentions thy brother, thy poor, thy needy, in thy land. Start with those near you. Open your hand to someone in need today. Let generosity be a habit, not an occasional event.

R: When Paul encouraged the Corinthians to give generously, he pointed to the greatest example of generosity there is: 'For ye know the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ, that, though he was rich, yet for your sakes he became poor, that ye through his poverty might be rich.' Jesus was rich beyond measure. He shared the glory of heaven with the Father. Yet He left it all behind. He was born in humble circumstances, lived without a home of His own, and died with nothing. When we understand what Christ gave up for us, our own giving becomes a grateful response. Reflect on His generosity today. Let it inspire yours.

R: During a severe drought and famine, God sent the prophet Elijah to a widow in Zarephath. She was gathering sticks to make one last meal for herself and her son before they died of hunger. Elijah asked her to make him a small cake first. It was an enormous request. But Elijah promised that her flour and oil would not run out until the rain returned. The verse tells us what happened: 'And she went and did according to the saying of Elijah: and she, and he, and her house, did eat many days.' She gave from her last handful, and God provided day after day. The flour never ran out. The oil never failed. This story shows that generosity is not always about giving from abundance. Sometimes it means giving from scarcity, trusting God to provide. The widow's faith was rewarded, not with a single feast, but with daily provision. If you feel you have too little to give, remember her. God can stretch what you offer.
S: During a famine, a widow gave her last bit of food to Elijah, and God made her flour and oil last for many days. Generosity sometimes means giving from scarcity, trusting God to provide. Remember today that God can stretch whatever you offer.

R: Jesus ended His teaching about possessions with a short sentence that reaches deep: 'For where your treasure is, there will your heart be also.' We often think our hearts direct our money. But Jesus says it also works the other way. Where we put our treasure, our hearts follow. If we invest heavily in earthly things, our hearts become attached to them. If we invest in God's kingdom, through generosity, good works, and care for others, our hearts are drawn toward heaven. This means generosity is not just a result of a changed heart. It is also a way to change our hearts. When we give, our affections shift. We begin to care more about the people and purposes we invest in. This month we have thought about generosity and good works. End it by looking at where your treasure is. Your bank statement and calendar may reveal more than you expect. Then decide where you want your heart to be, and invest your treasure there.
S: Jesus said that where our treasure is, our heart will be also. Generosity not only flows from a changed heart but also helps change it. Look honestly at where your treasure is today and invest it where you want your heart to be.
