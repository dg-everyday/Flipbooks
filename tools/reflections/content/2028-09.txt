# September 2028: Patience & Waiting on God

== Sep 01
R: Waiting is one of the hardest things we are asked to do. We wait for test results, for answers to prayer, for doors to open, for relationships to heal. And while we wait, our hearts can grow anxious or discouraged. David knew this. He ended Psalm 27 with a word for anyone in a waiting season: 'Wait on the LORD: be of good courage, and he shall strengthen thine heart: wait, I say, on the LORD.' He says wait twice, at the beginning and the end. It is as if he knows we need to hear it more than once. Between those two commands is a promise. As we wait with courage, God strengthens our hearts. Waiting on the Lord is not passive. It takes courage to keep trusting when nothing seems to be happening. But God meets us in the waiting with strength. This month we will think about patience and waiting on God. Begin with David's double encouragement: wait on the Lord.
S: David urges us twice to wait on the Lord and promises that God will strengthen our hearts as we take courage. Waiting takes courage, and God meets us with strength. Whatever you are waiting for today, wait on the Lord.
P: Waiting takes courage, and God gives strength. Psalm 27:14 says, 'Wait on the LORD: be of good courage, and he shall strengthen thine heart: wait, I say, on the LORD.'

== Sep 02
R: It is hard to wait patiently when others seem to get ahead by doing wrong. David addressed this frustration in Psalm 37: 'Rest in the LORD, and wait patiently for him: fret not thyself because of him who prospereth in his way.' Fret means to burn, to become heated with worry or resentment. When we see dishonest people succeed, we can start to fret. Why are they prospering while I am struggling? Is it worth doing right? David's answer is to rest in the Lord. Not to stop caring about justice, but to trust that God sees and will act in His time. Wicked plans may succeed for a while, but they do not last. The rest of the psalm promises that evildoers will fade like grass, while those who wait on the Lord will inherit the earth. Fretting drains our energy and steals our peace. Resting restores both. Is something causing you to fret today? Bring it to God. Then rest in Him and wait patiently.
S: David told those frustrated by the success of the wicked to rest in the Lord and wait patiently instead of fretting. Fretting drains us, but resting in God restores peace. Bring whatever is making you fret to God today and rest in Him.
P: Do not burn with worry. Psalm 37:7 says, 'Rest in the LORD, and wait patiently for him: fret not thyself.' Trust that God sees and will act in His time.

== Sep 03
R: David's testimony in Psalm 40 begins with waiting: 'I waited patiently for the LORD; and he inclined unto me, and heard my cry.' The words waited patiently in Hebrew are literally waiting I waited, a way of emphasizing how long and hard the waiting was. This was not a quick answer. David waited and waited. But then something beautiful happened. God inclined unto him. To incline means to bend down, to lean in close. It is the picture of someone stooping to hear a small child, or a parent bending over a crib. God bent down to listen to David's cry. The waiting was long, but God was not distant. And when the time came, He leaned in close. If you have been waiting a long time, take heart from David's story. God has not stopped listening. He is not too high or too busy to bend down to you. Keep crying out. Keep waiting. He will incline His ear.
S: David waited patiently for the Lord, and God bent down to hear his cry. Even long waiting does not mean God is distant. Keep crying out to Him today; He will lean in to hear you.
P: God bends down to listen. Psalm 40:1 says, 'I waited patiently for the LORD; and he inclined unto me, and heard my cry.' Keep waiting and keep praying. He hears you.

== Sep 04
R: In the middle of Lamentations, a book of grief over the destruction of Jerusalem, Jeremiah writes words of surprising hope: 'The LORD is good unto them that wait for him, to the soul that seeketh him.' He was surrounded by ruins. Everything familiar was gone. Yet he declared that God is good to those who wait. This does not mean God is only good when things go our way. It means His goodness is experienced in a special way by those who keep waiting on Him through hard times. Waiting and seeking go together here. Waiting is not sitting idle. It is actively looking to God, seeking Him while we wait for His answer. And the promise is that we will find Him good. Not just His gifts, but Himself. Sometimes we discover God's goodness most deeply in the waiting room. We learn that He is enough even before the answer comes. Wait for Him today. Seek Him while you wait. You will find Him good.
S: In the middle of grief, Jeremiah declared that the Lord is good to those who wait for Him and seek Him. We often discover God's goodness most deeply while waiting. Seek Him today as you wait, and find Him good.
P: God is good to those who wait. Lamentations 3:25 says, 'The LORD is good unto them that wait for him, to the soul that seeketh him.' Seek Him in the waiting today.

== Sep 05
R: Jeremiah continues his thought: 'It is good that a man should both hope and quietly wait for the salvation of the LORD.' Two things are good: hoping and quietly waiting. Hope looks forward with confidence that God will act. Quiet waiting is the posture of the heart while we hope. It is not frantic, not complaining, not demanding. It is calm trust. This is not easy. When we are in pain or uncertainty, our instinct is to be loud. We want to fix, push, demand, or worry out loud. Quiet waiting takes a different kind of strength. It means resting our case with God and trusting His timing. Quietness is not the same as silence. Jeremiah himself cried out to God throughout this book. Quiet waiting can include honest prayer. But underneath the prayer is a settled heart that knows God will bring salvation in His time. Today, practice quiet waiting. Hope in God, and let your heart be still.
S: Jeremiah says it is good both to hope and to wait quietly for the Lord's salvation. Quiet waiting is calm trust, not frantic demanding. Practice quiet, hopeful waiting today.
P: Hope with a quiet heart. Lamentations 3:26 says, 'It is good that a man should both hope and quietly wait for the salvation of the LORD.' Let your heart be still today.

== Sep 06
R: Isaiah prayed with longing for God to come down and act. And in the middle of that prayer, he wrote this: 'since the beginning of the world men have not heard, nor perceived by the ear, neither hath the eye seen, O God, beside thee, what he hath prepared for him that waiteth for him.' No one has ever seen a God like ours. And no one can imagine what He has prepared for those who wait for Him. Paul later echoed this verse, applying it to the wonders of God's plan revealed in Christ. Waiting can make us feel like we are missing out. Others seem to move ahead while we stand still. But Isaiah reminds us that God is preparing something for those who wait. It is beyond what eyes have seen or ears have heard. Our waiting is not wasted time. It is the time during which God is preparing. If you are in a waiting season, trust that something good is being prepared for you.
S: Isaiah declared that no eye has seen or ear heard what God has prepared for those who wait for Him. Waiting is not wasted time but the season when God prepares. Trust today that God is preparing something good for you.

== Sep 07
R: Habakkuk had asked God hard questions. Why was there so much injustice? Why did God seem silent? God answered, but His answer involved waiting: 'For the vision is yet for an appointed time, but at the end it shall speak, and not lie: though it tarry, wait for it; because it will surely come, it will not tarry.' God's plans have an appointed time. They are not random or forgotten. They are scheduled in His wisdom. And notice the apparent contradiction: though it tarry, it will not tarry. From our perspective, it may seem slow. But from God's perspective, it is right on time. It will not be one moment late. This is a word for anyone holding on to a promise. God has not forgotten. The vision will speak. It will come true. But it will come at the appointed time, not necessarily our preferred time. Write down what you are trusting God for. Then wait for it. It will surely come.
S: God told Habakkuk that His vision had an appointed time and, though it seemed to tarry, it would surely come and not be late. God's plans are never forgotten, only timed in His wisdom. Hold on to His promises today and wait for His appointed time.

== Sep 08
R: Ecclesiastes 3 contains a famous poem about time: a time to be born and a time to die, a time to plant and a time to pluck up, a time to weep and a time to laugh. It begins with this verse: 'To every thing there is a season, and a time to every purpose under the heaven.' Life moves in seasons. No season lasts forever. Joyful seasons end, and so do painful ones. Planting is followed by harvest. Weeping gives way to laughing. Recognizing this brings both humility and hope. Humility, because we cannot hold on to good seasons forever. Hope, because hard seasons will not last forever either. It also helps us accept where we are. If you are in a season of planting, do not expect harvest yet. If you are in a season of rest, do not feel guilty for not being busy. Each season has its purpose under heaven. Ask God today what season you are in. Then embrace it, trusting Him with its timing.
S: Solomon wrote that there is a season and a time for every purpose under heaven. Life moves in seasons, and none of them last forever. Ask God what season you are in today and embrace it, trusting Him with its timing.

== Sep 09
R: After listing all the seasons of life, Solomon makes this observation: 'He hath made every thing beautiful in his time.' Not everything is beautiful right now. Some seasons are painful, confusing, or dull. But in His time, God makes them beautiful. Like a tapestry that looks like a tangle of threads from the back, our lives may seem messy up close. But God is weaving something beautiful, and in His time we will see it. Solomon adds that God has set the world, or eternity, in our hearts. We long for meaning beyond the present moment. We sense there is more. Yet we cannot fully see God's work from beginning to end. We see only a piece. That is why trust is needed. We cannot see the whole picture yet. But God can, and He is making it beautiful. If your life feels like a tangle right now, trust the Weaver. In His time, beauty will appear.

== Sep 10
R: James turns to the farmer as an example of patience: 'Behold, the husbandman waiteth for the precious fruit of the earth, and hath long patience for it, until he receive the early and latter rain.' A farmer cannot rush a harvest. He prepares the soil, plants the seed, and then waits. In Israel, the early rain came in autumn, softening the ground for planting. The latter rain came in spring, helping the crops mature before harvest. The farmer depended on both, and he could control neither. He simply waited, working faithfully, trusting the rains would come. James connects this to waiting for the coming of the Lord. But the principle applies to all of life. Some things cannot be hurried. Growth takes time. Character takes time. Answers to prayer often take time. Like the farmer, we do our part and then trust God for what only He can provide. Be patient today. The rain will come in its season.
S: James points to the farmer, who waits patiently for the early and latter rain before the harvest. Some things cannot be hurried, and we must trust God for what only He can provide. Do your part faithfully today and wait patiently for His rain.

== Sep 11
R: After describing the patient farmer, James applies it directly: 'Be ye also patient; stablish your hearts: for the coming of the Lord draweth nigh.' Stablish means to strengthen, to make firm. A heart that is not established is easily shaken. It swings with every piece of news, every disappointment, every delay. An established heart is anchored. It stays steady even when circumstances change. How do we establish our hearts? By grounding them in what is certain. James points to the coming of the Lord. Whatever else happens, Christ will return. That hope is fixed. When we keep it in view, our hearts become steady. The present struggles, however hard, are temporary. The Lord draws near. This truth also puts our waiting in perspective. We are not waiting aimlessly. We are waiting for Someone. And He is coming. Today, ask God to establish your heart. Anchor it in His promises, especially the promise of His return.
S: James urges believers to be patient and establish their hearts, because the coming of the Lord draws near. An established heart stays steady when circumstances change. Anchor your heart today in the certain hope of Christ's return.

== Sep 12
R: In a long list of instructions for Christian living, Paul places three short phrases side by side: 'Rejoicing in hope; patient in tribulation; continuing instant in prayer.' They fit together like three strands of a rope. Rejoicing in hope looks forward. We rejoice now because of what God has promised for the future. Patient in tribulation endures the present. When trouble comes, we hold steady rather than giving up. Continuing instant in prayer keeps us connected. Instant means constant, persistent, always ready. Prayer is what makes the other two possible. Hope without prayer can fade. Patience without prayer can turn into grim endurance. But prayer keeps both alive. It keeps us talking to the One in whom we hope and from whom we draw strength. If you are in a hard season, take these three phrases as your plan for today. Look ahead with joy. Hold steady in the trouble. And keep praying.

The list continues with meekness and temperance in the next verse. Notice that it is fruit, singular. These qualities grow together as one harvest in a life surrendered to God. And notice longsuffering in the middle of the list. Longsuffering means patience, especially patience with people. It is the ability to endure irritation, offence, and delay without losing our temper or giving up on others. This kind of patience is not a personality trait some people are born with. It is fruit, grown by the Spirit. Fruit grows gradually. You cannot force an apple to ripen overnight. In the same way, patience develops over time as we stay connected to God. If you struggle with impatience, do not just try harder. Stay close to God. Ask God's Spirit to ripen this fruit in you.

New projects, new relationships, and new ventures all feel full of promise. But the real value is in the end, in finishing well. Many things are started; fewer are completed. And finishing often requires patience. The second half connects patience and humility. The proud in spirit want things done their way and on their schedule. They are quick to push, quick to complain, and slow to listen. The patient in spirit can wait. They trust the process. They do not need to be in control of every timeline. Pride and impatience often go together. So do humility and patience. If you find yourself frustrated with delays today, ask whether pride is part of it. Let humility grow patience in you, and keep going until the end.

== Sep 15
R: Proverbs often contrasts the wise and the foolish. Here it does so through anger: 'He that is slow to wrath is of great understanding: but he that is hasty of spirit exalteth folly.' Being slow to anger is a sign of great understanding. Why? Because understanding sees more of the situation. It considers other perspectives, possible explanations, and long-term consequences. It does not react to the first impression. A hasty spirit, on the other hand, exalts folly. It puts foolishness on display for everyone to see. Quick tempers lead to words we regret, decisions we cannot undo, and relationships damaged in a moment. Being slow to wrath does not mean never feeling anger. It means not being ruled by it. It means pausing before reacting, giving time for understanding to catch up with emotion. A simple practice can help: when you feel anger rising, pause and breathe. Ask God for understanding. Then respond, rather than react.
S: Proverbs teaches that being slow to anger shows great understanding, while a hasty spirit puts folly on display. Understanding sees more of the situation before reacting. Pause before you respond to anger today and ask God for understanding.
P: Slow anger shows deep understanding. Proverbs 14:29 says, 'He that is slow to wrath is of great understanding.' Pause, breathe, and pray before you respond today.

Mastering them takes daily discipline and God's help. Many powerful people have conquered nations but could not conquer their own tempers. Their lack of self-control undid their achievements. The person who rules their spirit has a strength that lasts. Self-control is part of the fruit of the Spirit. It is not about suppressing every feeling. It is about not letting feelings take the throne. Where do you most need to rule your spirit? Your words, your reactions, your appetites, your thoughts? Ask God for help there today. That victory is greater than taking a city.

== Sep 17
R: James gives a short formula for healthy communication: 'let every man be swift to hear, slow to speak, slow to wrath.' Most of us do the opposite. We are quick to speak, slow to listen, and quick to get angry. We formulate our reply while the other person is still talking. We interrupt. We assume we know what they mean. James calls us to reverse the order. Be swift to hear. Listen carefully, fully, with genuine interest. Be slow to speak. Think before you answer. Be slow to wrath. Do not let anger take over. These three go together. When we truly listen, we often understand more and react less. When we pause before speaking, our words are wiser. And when we are slow to anger, conflicts cool rather than escalate. The next verse explains why: the wrath of man worketh not the righteousness of God. Our anger rarely accomplishes God's purposes. Practise James's formula in your conversations today. Listen first.
S: James urges us to be quick to listen, slow to speak, and slow to anger. Most of us do the opposite, but reversing the order brings wisdom and peace. Practise listening first in your conversations today.
P: Listen more. Speak less. James 1:19 says, 'let every man be swift to hear, slow to speak, slow to wrath.' Try it in every conversation today.

== Sep 24
R: Micah looked around and saw corruption everywhere. Leaders took bribes. Friends could not be trusted. Even family members turned against each other. It would have been easy to despair. But Micah made a decision: 'Therefore I will look unto the LORD; I will wait for the God of my salvation: my God will hear me.' Three firm statements. I will look. I will wait. My God will hear.
