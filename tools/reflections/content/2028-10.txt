# October 2028: Integrity & Holy Living

== Oct 01
R: The people in Micah's day wanted to know how to please God. Should they bring burnt offerings? Thousands of rams? Rivers of oil? Even their firstborn children? They assumed God wanted bigger and more costly religious gestures. Micah's answer was surprisingly simple: 'He hath shewed thee, O man, what is good; and what doth the LORD require of thee, but to do justly, and to love mercy, and to walk humbly with thy God?' Three things. Do justly: treat people fairly and honestly. Love mercy: not just show mercy occasionally, but love it, delight in kindness. Walk humbly with thy God: live in daily, humble dependence on Him. None of these require wealth or status. Anyone can do them. But they touch every part of life: how we work, how we treat people, and how we relate to God. This month we will think about integrity and holy living. Micah's three requirements are a good place to start. Live them today.
S: When people asked what God required, Micah answered: do justly, love mercy, and walk humbly with God. God desires a life of integrity more than religious gestures. Live out these three requirements in ordinary ways today.
P: What does God require? Micah 6:8 says, 'to do justly, and to love mercy, and to walk humbly with thy God.' Simple to say, life-changing to live. Practise all three today.

== Oct 02
R: There is a freedom that comes with honesty. Proverbs describes it this way: 'He that walketh uprightly walketh surely: but he that perverteth his ways shall be known.' To walk uprightly means to live with integrity, the same in private as in public. And such a person walks surely, with security and confidence. They do not have to keep track of lies. They do not fear being found out. They can look people in the eye. Their footing is solid. The person who perverts their ways lives differently. They must constantly cover their tracks. They worry about exposure. And Proverbs says they shall be known. Sooner or later, crooked paths are revealed. Secrets have a way of coming to light. Integrity is not always the easy path in the moment. Sometimes honesty costs us. But over the long run, it is the path of security and peace. Where are you tempted to cut corners? Choose the upright path today. Walk surely.
S: Proverbs teaches that those who walk with integrity walk securely, while those who take crooked paths will be exposed. Honesty brings freedom and confidence. Choose the upright path today, even where it costs you.
P: Integrity gives you solid footing. Proverbs 10:9 says, 'He that walketh uprightly walketh surely.' No hiding, no fear of being found out. Walk uprightly today.

== Oct 03
R: Life is full of decisions, and some are complicated. How do we know which way to go? Proverbs offers a guide: 'The integrity of the upright shall guide them.' Integrity is not just a quality we have. It is a compass that directs us. When we are committed to honesty and righteousness, many decisions become clearer. We do not have to agonize over whether to lie on a form, take credit for someone else's work, or cut a corner that no one will notice. Integrity has already answered those questions. It rules certain options out before we even consider them. The second half of the verse gives the contrast: 'the perverseness of transgressors shall destroy them.' Crookedness eventually brings ruin. Lies multiply. Compromises lead to bigger compromises. What seemed like a shortcut becomes a trap. Let integrity be your guide today. When a decision comes, ask not only what is easiest or most profitable, but what is upright. Follow that.
S: Proverbs teaches that integrity guides the upright, while crookedness destroys those who practise it. A commitment to honesty makes many decisions clearer. Let integrity guide your choices today.
P: Integrity is a compass. Proverbs 11:3 says, 'The integrity of the upright shall guide them.' When you face a decision today, ask what is upright, and follow that.

== Oct 04
R: Our choices do not affect only us. They ripple into the lives of those who come after us. Proverbs says, 'The just man walketh in his integrity: his children are blessed after him.' A parent who lives with integrity leaves a legacy far more valuable than money. Children watch how their parents handle honesty, work, conflict, and faith. They absorb those values. Integrity becomes part of the family inheritance. Many people can trace their faith or character back to a parent or grandparent who lived rightly. They remember a father who kept his word, a mother who told the truth when it was hard, or a grandparent who prayed faithfully. Those lives continue to bless generations. This is also true for those without children of their own. Students, nieces, nephews, younger coworkers, and neighbours all watch how we live. What legacy are you building? Every honest choice today is an investment in someone else's tomorrow. Walk in your integrity.
S: Proverbs teaches that a just person who walks in integrity leaves blessing for their children. Integrity becomes a legacy that shapes generations. Remember today that your honest choices are an investment in others' futures.

== Oct 05
R: Psalm 15 begins with a question: Lord, who shall abide in thy tabernacle? Who may dwell with God? The answer describes a person of integrity, starting with this: 'He that walketh uprightly, and worketh righteousness, and speaketh the truth in his heart.' Notice the last phrase: speaketh the truth in his heart. Truth is not only about what we say out loud. It begins inside. It means being honest with ourselves and with God. It means not deceiving ourselves about our motives, our sins, or our needs. Many people speak truthfully to others while telling themselves comfortable lies. I am not that bad. It was not my fault. I can handle this on my own. Honesty in the heart is the foundation of all other honesty. When we are truthful inside, truthfulness flows out in our words and actions. Today, take a few moments to be completely honest with God about your heart. Let truth begin on the inside.
S: Psalm 15 describes the person who may dwell with God as one who walks uprightly and speaks the truth in their heart. Honesty begins inside, with ourselves and with God. Take time today to be completely honest with God about your heart.

== Oct 06
R: Psalm 24 asks who may ascend the hill of the Lord and stand in His holy place. The answer: 'He that hath clean hands, and a pure heart; who hath not lifted up his soul unto vanity, nor sworn deceitfully.' Clean hands refer to our actions. What we do. Pure heart refers to our motives. Why we do it. God cares about both. It is possible to do the right things for the wrong reasons, or to have good intentions while doing wrong things. God desires integrity in both. Not lifting up the soul unto vanity means not giving our devotion to empty things, to idols of any kind. Not swearing deceitfully means keeping our word. No one meets this standard perfectly. That is why we need a Saviour. But through Christ, we are cleansed and given new hearts. And by His Spirit, we grow in holiness. Ask God today to clean your hands and purify your heart. Let both your actions and motives honour Him.
S: Psalm 24 describes those who may stand before God as having clean hands, a pure heart, and honest words. God cares about both our actions and our motives. Ask Him today to clean your hands and purify your heart.

== Oct 07
R: David wrote Psalm 51 after his terrible sin with Bathsheba was exposed. He had committed adultery and arranged the death of her husband. When the prophet Nathan confronted him, David broke. He did not make excuses. He poured out his heart in confession. And in the middle of the psalm, he prayed, 'Create in me a clean heart, O God; and renew a right spirit within me.' The word create is the same word used in Genesis when God created the heavens and the earth. David knew he could not fix his own heart. He needed God to do something new, something only the Creator could do. He did not ask for a cleaned-up version of his old heart. He asked for a new one. Here is hope for anyone who feels their heart is too far gone. God does not just patch us up. He creates new hearts. Pray David's prayer today. Ask God to create something clean and new in you.
S: After his great sin, David asked God to create a clean heart in him and renew a right spirit. He knew only God, the Creator, could make his heart new. Pray this prayer today and ask God to create something new in you.

== Oct 08
R: Psalm 139 is a long meditation on how completely God knows us. He knows when we sit and when we rise. He knows our thoughts before we think them. He formed us in the womb. There is nowhere we can go to escape His presence. And at the end of the psalm, David invites God to do what He already does: 'Search me, O God, and know my heart: try me, and know my thoughts.' This is a brave prayer. Most of us would rather keep certain parts of our hearts hidden. We prefer not to look too closely at our motives, our anxieties, or our secret sins. But David opens the door. He asks God to search every corner. Why? Because God's searching is not to condemn but to heal. The Great Physician examines us to find what needs treatment. We cannot fix what we will not face. Pray David's prayer slowly today. Invite God to search your heart and know your thoughts.

== Oct 09
R: David's prayer continues: 'And see if there be any wicked way in me, and lead me in the way everlasting.' He asks God to find anything wrong, any wicked way, any path that leads away from God. This is not someone defending himself. It is someone who wants to be corrected. Then David asks for direction: lead me in the way everlasting. Searching is not the end goal. Being led is. God does not expose our faults just to make us feel bad. He reveals them so He can lead us onto a better path. The way everlasting is the path that lasts, the road that leads to life. This two-part prayer is a pattern for spiritual growth. First, let God show you what is wrong. Second, let Him lead you to what is right. Both require humility. Both require trust. If God has been showing you something that needs to change, do not resist. Let Him lead you in the way everlasting.
S: David asked God to see if there was any wicked way in him and to lead him in the way everlasting. God reveals our faults so He can lead us onto a better path. Let Him show you what needs to change today, and follow where He leads.

== Oct 10
R: Psalm 19 begins with the heavens declaring God's glory and moves to the perfection of God's Word. Then it ends with a personal prayer: 'Let the words of my mouth, and the meditation of my heart, be acceptable in thy sight, O LORD, my strength, and my redeemer.' Words of my mouth: what we say. Meditation of my heart: what we think about. Both matter to God. Words often reveal what has been growing in our hearts. If our thoughts are bitter, our words will eventually be bitter too. If our thoughts are filled with God's truth, our words will reflect it. David wanted both to be acceptable in God's sight. Not just in the sight of others. It is possible to say the right things in public while harbouring wrong thoughts in private. Integrity means our inner life and outer words match, and both please God. This verse makes a wonderful prayer to begin any day. Say it before you speak to anyone else today.

== Oct 11
R: In the Sermon on the Mount, Jesus pronounced blessings on unlikely people: the poor in spirit, the mourners, the meek. Then He said, 'Blessed are the pure in heart: for they shall see God.' Purity of heart means single-mindedness. A pure heart is not divided. It does not chase God with one hand and the world with the other. It wants God above all. It is undiluted, sincere, and wholehearted. The religious leaders of Jesus' day focused on outward purity: washing hands, avoiding unclean foods, following rituals. Jesus pointed to the heart. That is where true purity begins. And the reward is wonderful: they shall see God. Not just in eternity, though that is included, but even now. A pure heart perceives God's presence more clearly. When our hearts are cluttered with competing desires, it is hard to see Him. When our hearts are focused, He becomes clearer. Ask God today for a pure heart. Ask Him to remove whatever divides your devotion.
S: Jesus said the pure in heart are blessed, for they shall see God. Purity of heart means single-minded devotion, not divided between God and the world. Ask God today to remove whatever divides your heart.

== Oct 12
R: In Jesus' day, people swore oaths to back up their promises. They swore by heaven, by earth, by Jerusalem, or by their own heads. Some even used clever loopholes, swearing by things that did not technically bind them. Jesus cut through all of it: 'But let your communication be, Yea, yea; Nay, nay: for whatsoever is more than these cometh of evil.' In other words, just say yes or no, and mean it. A person of integrity does not need to swear or add dramatic promises to be believed. Their word is enough. If they say yes, they will do it. If they say no, they mean it. This applies to everyday life. Do we keep our commitments? Do we show up when we said we would? Do we follow through on small promises? Or do people learn not to count on our words? Being reliable is a form of holiness. Today, let your yes be yes. Keep every promise, large and small.
S: Jesus taught that our communication should simply be yes or no, without needing oaths to be believed. A person of integrity is reliable in their word. Keep every promise today, large and small.

== Oct 13
R: Peter wrote to scattered believers living among people who did not share their faith. He urged them to live distinctively: 'But as he which hath called you is holy, so be ye holy in all manner of conversation.' In the King James Version, conversation means conduct, the whole way we live. Not just our words, but our behaviour, habits, and choices. Holiness is not only for church or prayer time. It reaches into every part of life: how we work, how we spend money, what we watch, how we drive, how we treat people who cannot benefit us. The reason is simple. The One who called us is holy. Children tend to resemble their parents. As God's children, we are called to reflect His character. Holiness is not about being odd or self-righteous. It is about being set apart for God, living in a way that shows we belong to Him. Think of one area of your life you have kept separate from God. Invite His holiness in today.

== Oct 14
R: After eleven chapters explaining the mercies of God, Paul turns to how we should respond: 'I beseech you therefore, brethren, by the mercies of God, that ye present your bodies a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable unto God, which is your reasonable service.' The word therefore points back to everything God has done. Because of His mercy, our response is to give ourselves completely to Him. In the Old Testament, sacrifices were killed and placed on the altar. Paul calls for a living sacrifice. Not a one-time offering, but a daily surrender of our whole selves. Our bodies, which include our actions, words, time, and energy, are offered to God. The problem with a living sacrifice, someone has said, is that it keeps crawling off the altar. That is why it must be renewed daily. Paul calls this reasonable service, the logical response to God's mercy. When we understand how much He has given us, offering ourselves back is the only thing that makes sense. Present yourself to Him today.
S: Paul urges believers, in view of God's mercies, to present their bodies as a living sacrifice to God. This is a daily surrender of our whole selves, the reasonable response to His mercy. Offer yourself to God again today.

== Oct 15
R: Right after calling us to be living sacrifices, Paul explains how that works: 'And be not conformed to this world: but be ye transformed by the renewing of your mind.' Conformed means pressed into a mould. The world constantly pushes us to think like it, value what it values, and live as it lives. Without realizing it, we can be shaped by its ideas about success, beauty, pleasure, and identity. Paul says resist the mould. Instead, be transformed. The word is metamorphosis, the same word used for a caterpillar becoming a butterfly. Real change from the inside out. And it happens through the renewing of your mind. As we fill our minds with God's truth, our thinking changes. As our thinking changes, our lives change. The result is that we can prove, or discover by experience, what God's will is: good, acceptable, and perfect. What is shaping your mind these days? Feed it with God's Word today. Let Him transform you from the inside out.

== Oct 16
R: What we think about shapes who we become. Paul knew this, so he gave the Philippians a filter for their thoughts: 'whatsoever things are true, whatsoever things are honest, whatsoever things are just, whatsoever things are pure, whatsoever things are lovely, whatsoever things are of good report; if there be any virtue, and if there be any praise, think on these things.' Eight qualities. True, not false. Honest, worthy of respect. Just, right and fair. Pure, clean and wholesome. Lovely, beautiful and pleasing. Of good report, admirable. Virtuous. Praiseworthy. This is not about ignoring problems or pretending everything is fine. It is about choosing what we dwell on. Our minds will fill with something. If we do not choose good things, other things will rush in: worry, bitterness, envy, impurity. Today, run your thoughts through Paul's filter. When something does not pass, replace it. Think on these things, and watch your heart follow.

== Oct 17
R: Daniel was a young man taken captive to Babylon. He was chosen to be trained in the king's court, given a new name, and offered food from the king's own table. Much of it would have violated God's law or been offered to idols. It would have been easy to go along. He was far from home, in a powerful empire, with his future in the king's hands. But 'Daniel purposed in his heart that he would not defile himself.' He made a decision before the pressure peaked. He settled it in his heart. And then he acted wisely. He did not rebel or make a scene. He respectfully asked for an alternative, a ten-day test of vegetables and water. God gave him favour, and the test succeeded. Integrity often begins with a decision made in advance. When we decide beforehand what we will and will not do, we are much stronger when temptation comes. What do you need to purpose in your heart today?

== Oct 18
R: Joseph was a slave in Egypt, serving in the house of Potiphar, an officer of Pharaoh. He worked so faithfully that Potiphar put him in charge of everything. Then Potiphar's wife began to pressure Joseph day after day to sleep with her. Joseph refused: 'There is none greater in this house than I; neither hath he kept back any thing from me but thee, because thou art his wife: how then can I do this great wickedness, and sin against God?' Joseph saw the situation clearly. It would betray his master's trust. But more than that, it would be sin against God. That was the deciding factor. He was far from his family, and no one in Egypt would likely have known. But God would know. Joseph's integrity cost him dearly. He was falsely accused and thrown into prison. But God was with him there too. Integrity may cost us in the short term. But it honours God, and He does not forget. Stand firm today.

He could have admitted to wrongs he did not commit just to end the argument. But he refused: 'God forbid that I should justify you: till I die I will not remove mine integrity from me.' Job would not lie, even to make his situation easier. He would not agree with something false, even under pressure from people he respected. His integrity was more important than his comfort or reputation. This takes great courage. Sometimes the pressure to compromise comes from those close to us. Friends, family, or coworkers may urge us to say what is not true, to go along with something wrong, or to take blame we do not deserve. Integrity means holding fast to the truth even then. He decided ahead of time not to look at women with lust. This is ancient wisdom that speaks powerfully today. We are surrounded by images on screens, billboards, and phones. Many of them are designed to capture our attention and stir our desires. Without a deliberate decision, our eyes will wander. Job shows that purity is not passive. It requires a commitment and boundaries. It is not about shame but about protecting the heart. What covenant might you need to make with your eyes? The heart in Scripture is not just the seat of emotions. It is the centre of the whole person: thoughts, desires, will, and choices. And Proverbs says everything in life flows from it. It means noticing bitterness, envy, or lust before they take root. It means filling our hearts with God's Word and presence. Guarding the heart is not a one-time task but a daily practice. == Oct 23
R: Few verses are as direct as this one: 'Lying lips are abomination to the LORD: but they that deal truly are his delight.' Abomination is a strong word. It means something God finds deeply offensive. Lying is not a small matter to Him. God is truth itself, and lies contradict His nature. But the second half of the verse is just as strong, in a positive direction. Those who deal truly are His delight. God takes pleasure in honest people. When we tell the truth, especially when it is hard, we bring Him joy. Lying can creep in subtly. Exaggerating a story. Making excuses. Telling people what they want to hear. Shading the facts to look better. These may seem harmless, but they chip away at integrity. Dealing truly means being honest in word and in action. It means keeping our dealings fair and our words reliable. Today, make it your aim to be someone God delights in. Deal truly in everything.

== Oct 24
R: Paul gives a practical instruction in Ephesians 4: 'Wherefore putting away lying, speak every man truth with his neighbour: for we are members one of another.' The reason he gives is interesting. Not just that lying is wrong, though it is. But because we are members one of another. We are connected, like parts of a body. Imagine if your eyes lied to your hands about where an object was. Or if your nerves sent false signals to your brain. The body would not function. It would hurt itself. In the same way, when believers lie to each other, the whole body suffers. Trust breaks down. Relationships are damaged. Unity is lost. Truth-telling is not just a personal virtue. It is essential for community.
