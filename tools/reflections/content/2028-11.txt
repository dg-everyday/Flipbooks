# November 2028: The Character of God

== Nov 01
R: Moses had asked to see God's glory. God placed him in a cleft of the rock, covered him with His hand, and passed by. And as He passed, He proclaimed His own name: 'The LORD, The LORD God, merciful and gracious, longsuffering, and abundant in goodness and truth.' This is how God chose to describe Himself. Not first as powerful, though He is. Not first as holy, though He is. But as merciful, gracious, patient, and full of goodness and truth. These words were spoken right after Israel had worshipped the golden calf. They had broken the covenant almost as soon as they made it. And God responded by revealing His mercy. This description is repeated throughout the Old Testament. Psalms, prophets, and prayers return to it again and again. It became Israel's confession of who God is. This month we will reflect on the character of God. Begin with His own words about Himself. He is merciful and gracious. Rest in that today.

== Nov 02
R: At the burning bush, God called Moses to go to Pharaoh and lead Israel out of Egypt. Moses asked a reasonable question: when the people ask who sent me, what shall I tell them? God answered, 'I AM THAT I AM.' And He added, 'Thus shalt thou say unto the children of Israel, I AM hath sent me unto you.' This name is unlike any other. It speaks of God's self-existence. He simply is. He depends on nothing and no one. He was not created. He does not change. He is the source of all existence, and His being has no beginning and no end. Everything else in the universe depends on something else. Only God simply is. This was exactly what Moses needed. He was facing the most powerful king on earth. But he was sent by the One who is. Whatever you face today, you are not dealing with a God who might be or used to be. He is. Lean on the great I AM.
S: At the burning bush, God revealed His name as I AM THAT I AM. He is self-existent, depending on nothing, without beginning or end. Lean on the great I AM in whatever you face today.
P: God simply is. Exodus 3:14 says, 'And God said unto Moses, I AM THAT I AM.' He depends on nothing, and everything depends on Him. Lean on Him today.

== Nov 03
R: Malachi spoke to a people who had grown careless with God. They questioned His love, cheated in their offerings, and broke faith with one another. They deserved judgment. Yet God said, 'For I am the LORD, I change not; therefore ye sons of Jacob are not consumed.' Their survival depended on God's unchanging nature. If God were fickle, if His promises could be withdrawn on a whim, they would have been destroyed. But God had made a covenant with Jacob's descendants, and He does not change His mind. His faithfulness held even when theirs failed. This is wonderful news. People change. Moods change. Circumstances change. Even our own commitment to God rises and falls. But God's character remains constant. His love does not cool. His promises do not expire. His mercy does not run out. You can build your life on a God who does not change. On your worst day, He is the same as on your best. Rest in His constancy today.
S: God told His careless people that because He does not change, they were not consumed. Their survival depended on His unchanging faithfulness, not theirs. Rest today in a God whose love and promises never change.
P: You can build your life on a God who does not change. Malachi 3:6 says, 'For I am the LORD, I change not; therefore ye sons of Jacob are not consumed.'

== Nov 04
R: Balak, king of Moab, hired the prophet Balaam to curse Israel. But every time Balaam opened his mouth, God turned the curse into a blessing. Frustrated, Balak kept trying different locations, hoping God would change His mind. Balaam finally explained why it would not work: 'God is not a man, that he should lie; neither the son of man, that he should repent: hath he said, and shall he not do it? or hath he spoken, and shall he not make it good?' People lie. People change their minds. People make promises they do not keep. But God is not like us. When He says something, He means it. When He makes a promise, He fulfils it. He does not need to repent, or change course, because He never makes mistakes. This means every promise in Scripture is completely reliable. If God has said it, He will do it. When you doubt whether God will come through, remember this verse. He has spoken, and He will make it good.
S: When a king tried to get Balaam to curse Israel, Balaam explained that God is not a man who lies or changes His mind. Every promise God makes, He keeps. Trust today that what God has spoken, He will do.
P: Every promise God makes, He keeps. Numbers 23:19 says, 'God is not a man, that he should lie … hath he said, and shall he not do it?' Trust His word today.

== Nov 05
R: Near the end of his life, Moses taught Israel a song. It celebrated God's faithfulness and warned against turning away. One of its central lines describes God like this: 'He is the Rock, his work is perfect: for all his ways are judgment: a God of truth and without iniquity, just and right is he.' A rock was a powerful image in the wilderness. It meant stability in shifting sands, shade in the heat, protection from enemies, and even water, as when God brought water from the rock. God is all of these. He is unshakable. And Moses adds more. His work is perfect. Nothing He does is flawed. All His ways are just. He is a God of truth, without any wrongdoing. Just and right is He. In a world where leaders fail and systems are unjust, there is One who is completely right. You can rest your full weight on Him. He will not crumble or shift. He is the Rock.
S: In his final song, Moses described God as the Rock whose work is perfect and whose ways are all just and true. In a shifting world, God is completely stable and right. Rest your full weight on the Rock today.

== Nov 06
R: In the year King Uzziah died, Isaiah saw a vision of the Lord seated on a high throne. The train of His robe filled the temple. Above Him stood seraphim, heavenly beings who covered their faces and feet, and cried to one another, 'Holy, holy, holy, is the LORD of hosts: the whole earth is full of his glory.' Holiness is repeated three times. In Hebrew, repetition was a way of emphasizing. To say holy three times is to say God is supremely, utterly, infinitely holy. He is set apart from everything else, perfect in purity and goodness. Isaiah's response was immediate: Woe is me! I am undone. He saw his own sinfulness in the light of God's holiness. But God sent a seraph with a live coal to touch his lips and take away his sin. Encountering God's holiness humbles us, but it also leads to cleansing. Reflect on His holiness today. Let it humble you, and let His grace cleanse you.
S: Isaiah saw seraphim crying holy, holy, holy around God's throne, and he was humbled by his own sinfulness. God then cleansed him with a live coal. Reflect on God's holiness today, and receive His cleansing grace.

== Nov 07
R: God's people in exile felt forgotten. They complained that God did not see their troubles. Isaiah answered with a series of questions: 'Hast thou not known? hast thou not heard, that the everlasting God, the LORD, the Creator of the ends of the earth, fainteth not, neither is weary?' Everyone gets tired. The strongest people need rest. Even the most dedicated workers burn out. But God never does. He does not faint. He does not grow weary. He never needs a break from caring for you. He never sleeps on the job. His energy and attention are limitless. And the verse adds, 'there is no searching of his understanding.' His wisdom is beyond our comprehension. He understands what we cannot. When you feel unseen, remember that God is never too tired to notice you. When you feel confused, remember that His understanding has no limit. Isaiah goes on to say that this tireless God gives power to the faint. His strength is available to you.

== Nov 08
R: Isaiah 55 is an invitation to come to God, to seek Him while He may be found, and to return to Him so He can have mercy. In the middle of it, God explains why His mercy is so surprising: 'For my thoughts are not your thoughts, neither are your ways my ways, saith the LORD.' In context, this is about God's willingness to forgive. Human beings hold grudges. We keep score. We find it hard to forgive great wrongs. But God thinks differently. His mercy goes far beyond ours. Where we would give up on someone, He extends pardon. This verse is also a reminder of humility. We cannot fully understand God. His thoughts are not like ours. When He acts in ways we do not expect, it is not because He is wrong but because He sees more. We are not the measure of what God should do. If you are struggling to understand His ways right now, let this verse remind you. His thoughts are different. Trust Him anyway.
S: God declared that His thoughts and ways are not like ours, especially in His abundant willingness to forgive. We cannot fully understand Him, and we are not the measure of what He should do. Trust Him today even when His ways surprise you.

== Nov 09
R: God continues His explanation: 'For as the heavens are higher than the earth, so are my ways higher than your ways, and my thoughts than your thoughts.' Look up at the night sky and try to measure the distance to the stars. It is beyond comprehension. That, God says, is the gap between His ways and ours. This is not meant to make God seem distant or cold. In context, it is about the vastness of His mercy and the certainty of His plans. The very next verses compare His word to rain and snow that water the earth and make it fruitful. His word does not return empty. It accomplishes what He sends it to do. So the height of His ways is good news. His plans are bigger than our plans. His wisdom is deeper than our wisdom. His mercy is wider than our mercy. If your own understanding has reached its limit, look up. Remember how high the heavens are. His ways are higher still.

== Nov 10
R: Jeremiah was in prison, and Jerusalem was surrounded by the Babylonian army. The city was about to fall. And God told Jeremiah to buy a field. It seemed absurd. Who buys land in a country about to be conquered? But Jeremiah obeyed, and then he prayed: 'Ah Lord GOD! behold, thou hast made the heaven and the earth by thy great power and stretched out arm, and there is nothing too hard for thee.' The purchase was a sign that God would one day restore His people to the land. Jeremiah could not see how. But he started his prayer with God's power in creation. If God made the heavens and the earth, then nothing is too hard for Him. Not a besieged city. Not a hopeless situation. Not a broken life. What feels impossible to you today? A relationship, a health problem, a financial need, a habit you cannot break? Bring it to the God who made heaven and earth. Nothing is too hard for Him.

== Nov 11
R: Jeremiah 31 is a chapter of hope, written to a people facing exile. In it, God speaks these tender words: 'Yea, I have loved thee with an everlasting love: therefore with lovingkindness have I drawn thee.' Everlasting love. It did not begin when Israel became faithful, and it would not end when they failed. It stretches from eternity past to eternity future. And because of that love, God draws His people to Himself. He does not wait for us to find our way back. He pulls us close with lovingkindness. Many people struggle to believe God loves them. They know about His love in theory, but they feel unworthy, forgotten, or rejected. This verse speaks directly to that. His love is not based on your performance. It is everlasting. It was there before you were born, and it will remain forever. And He is drawing you toward Himself even now. Let these words sink deep into your heart today. You are loved with an everlasting love.

== Nov 12
R: David asks two questions in Psalm 139: 'Whither shall I go from thy spirit? or whither shall I flee from thy presence?' Then he answers them. If I go up to heaven, You are there. If I make my bed in the grave, You are there. If I take the wings of the morning and settle at the far end of the sea, even there Your hand will lead me. There is nowhere beyond God's presence. This can be uncomfortable if we are trying to hide from Him. Jonah tried to run from God and found Him in the belly of a fish. But for those who belong to God, His presence everywhere is a great comfort. You cannot wander so far that He cannot reach you. You cannot fall so low that He is not there. Hospital rooms, lonely apartments, foreign countries, and dark nights are all within His reach. Wherever you are today, God is there. You are never out of His sight or beyond His care.

== Nov 13
R: After reflecting on God's presence everywhere, David turns to how God formed him in his mother's womb. And he bursts into praise: 'I will praise thee; for I am fearfully and wonderfully made: marvellous are thy works; and that my soul knoweth right well.' Fearfully means with awe and reverence. Wonderfully means distinctly, set apart. David marvels at the intricate design of the human body and soul. He sees himself as one of God's marvellous works. Many people struggle to see themselves this way. They focus on their flaws, compare themselves to others, or believe they have little worth. But this verse declares that every person is a careful, intentional work of God. Your existence is not an accident. You were designed with care by a loving Creator. This truth should shape how we see ourselves and others. Every person we meet is fearfully and wonderfully made. Thank God today for how He made you. Then treat others as His marvellous works too.

Yet Psalm 147 says, 'He telleth the number of the stars; he calleth them all by their names.' God knows exactly how many there are. And He knows each one by name. This shows the vastness of His power and knowledge. Nothing in creation is too big for Him to manage or too small for Him to notice. But this verse is not just about astronomy. Look at the verse before it: He heals the broken in heart. The God who names the stars is the same God who binds up wounded hearts. His greatness does not make Him distant. It makes Him able to care for each of us personally. If He knows every star by name, He certainly knows yours. No one is lost in the crowd to Him. He sees you.

== Nov 15
R: Psalm 147 was likely written after the exiles returned to Jerusalem. The people had been scattered, wounded, and grieving. The psalm celebrates God gathering them back. And in the middle of it is this tender verse: 'He healeth the broken in heart, and bindeth up their wounds.' A broken heart is one of the deepest pains a person can experience. Grief, betrayal, rejection, and loss can shatter us inside. And often, no one else can see the wound. But God sees it. And He heals it. The image of binding up wounds is gentle and personal. It is the work of someone who kneels beside the injured, cleans the wound, and wraps it with care. That is how God treats the brokenhearted. He does not rush past pain or demand that we get over it. He tends to it patiently. If your heart is broken today, bring it to Him. Let Him bind up your wounds. He is a gentle healer.

== Nov 16
R: How does God feel about you when you struggle or fail? Psalm 103 answers with a picture from family life: 'Like as a father pitieth his children, so the LORD pitieth them that fear him.' Pitieth means shows compassion. A good father does not look at his struggling child with contempt. He looks with tenderness. When a small child falls, a loving father picks them up. When a child is frightened, he comforts them. When a child fails, he does not reject them. That is how God looks at those who fear Him. And the next verse explains why: 'For he knoweth our frame; he remembereth that we are dust.' God knows our limitations. He knows how weak and frail we are. He does not expect us to be stronger than we are. His compassion is shaped by His understanding of our weakness. Some people had harsh or absent fathers, and this image is hard for them. But God is the Father we always needed. Let Him show you His compassion today.

== Nov 17
R: Moses, who wrote Psalm 90, had seen a lot of change. He had grown up in Pharaoh's palace, fled to the desert, led a nation, and watched a generation die in the wilderness. Against the backdrop of all that change, he wrote, 'Before the mountains were brought forth, or ever thou hadst formed the earth and the world, even from everlasting to everlasting, thou art God.' Mountains seem ancient and permanent to us. But before there were mountains, God was. Before the earth existed, God was. From everlasting in the past to everlasting in the future, He is God. Our lives are short. Moses goes on to compare them to grass that flourishes in the morning and withers by evening. But God is eternal. This truth puts our worries in perspective. The God who existed before the mountains is not threatened by today's problems. And it gives us security. Our short lives are held by an eternal God. Rest today in the One who is from everlasting to everlasting.

R: God tested Abraham by asking him to offer Isaac, the son of promise, on Mount Moriah. Abraham obeyed, trusting God. As they climbed, Isaac asked where the lamb for the offering was. Abraham answered that God would provide Himself a lamb. At the last moment, an angel stopped him, and Abraham saw a ram caught in a thicket. He offered it in place of his son. 'And Abraham called the name of that place Jehovah-jireh.' The name means the Lord will provide, or the Lord will see to it. Abraham did not know how God would provide. But he trusted that He would. Whatever you need today, God sees it. He will provide, perhaps in ways you cannot yet imagine. Trust Jehovah-jireh.

== Nov 23
R: John's first letter contains one of the shortest and most profound statements in the Bible: 'for God is love.' Not just that God loves, though He does. But that love is His very nature. Love is not something God does occasionally. It is who He is. Everything He does flows from love: creating, providing, disciplining, forgiving, and saving. John places this truth next to a challenge: 'He that loveth not knoweth not God.' If God is love, then knowing Him will make us loving. People who claim to know God but have no love for others reveal that they do not really know Him. This does not mean we love perfectly. But it means love should be growing in us, because God's nature is rubbing off on us as we walk with Him. Today, let this simple truth sink in. God is love. His love for you is not a mood that might change. It is His nature. And let His love flow through you to others.
S: John declares that God is love, not just that He loves, but that love is His very nature. Those who truly know Him will grow in love for others. Let the truth that God is love sink in, and let His love flow through you today.
